# Why Positive Thinking Often Fails

## Summary

Based on twenty years of research, Gabriele Oettingen demonstrates that positive fantasies about future success paradoxically reduce motivation and achievement. This analysis synthesizes her findings on why conventional positive thinking advice fails and how mental contrasting offers a more effective alternative.

## Key facts

- Merely dreaming about the future makes people less likely to realize their dreams because the pleasurable act of fantasizing lets people fulfill wishes in their minds, sapping energy to perform the hard work of real life. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p9]
- Positive fantasies create a relaxed physiological state (lowered blood pressure) that is incompatible with the energized state needed for action. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p43]
- When people positively fantasize, their minds are fooled into thinking they have already attained the goal, reducing perceived need for action. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p49]
- Positive fantasies skew information processing, causing people to seek information confirming the fantasy and avoid information about obstacles. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p52]
- Mental contrasting reverses these effects by connecting the positive fantasy with the reality of obstacles, energizing action only when goals are achievable. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p10]

## Inferences

- Inference: The research suggests that 'Dream it. Wish it. Do it.' should be revised to 'Dream it. See the obstacles. Do it.' to reflect the importance of incorporating reality into positive visioning.
