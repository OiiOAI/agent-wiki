# The Sense and Mechanism of Parapraxes

## Summary

This analysis addresses how parapraxes (slips of the tongue, forgetting, mislaying) arise from the interaction of two intentions—a disturbed intention and a disturbing intention—which has been repressed or 'forced back' from conscious expression. The disturbing intention finds expression against the speaker's will, either by altering, mingling with, or replacing the intended expression. This mechanism explains why the speaker often denies or is unaware of the disturbing purpose.

## Key facts

- Parapraxes arise when one intention interferes with another; the disturbed intention is unmistakable but the disturbing one requires investigation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4833]
- The disturbing intention may contradict, correct, or supplement the disturbed one, or it may have no content relationship but be connected through associative links. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4846]
- The essential condition for a parapraxis is that the disturbing intention must have been suppressed or rejected before it can manifest as a disturbance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4848]
- Psychoanalysis requires accepting the 'strange hypothesis' that intentions can find expression of which the person himself knows nothing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4848]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that the speaker often denies the disturbing intention (as in the 'hiccough' example) indicates that the intention has been 'forced back' to an unconscious level where it can operate but cannot be consciously acknowledged.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all parapraxes have a sense, though Freud regards this as probably the case; some slips of the tongue may occur on a purely physiological basis.
