# Why Intuition Gets Lost in Translation

## Summary

This analysis examines why experts struggle to communicate their intuitive knowledge to others. Key barriers include: experts don't always know what they know; they find it hard to explain patterns, mental models, and action scripts; and they may resist sharing knowledge due to fear of criticism or lack of time. Effective transfer requires specific techniques like cognitive task analysis and coaching.

## Key facts

- As people become more expert, they find it harder to explain how they performed a task because their knowledge has become intuitive patterns rather than verbalizable rules. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p262]
- When people don't know how to transmit intuitive decision-making skills, they tend to give speeches expounding theories or make up procedures even if they don't follow them. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p263]
- Cognitive task analysis works better when asking about specific incidents rather than general questions like 'How do you know?'. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p265]
- Doug Harrington's landing signal officer saved his career by asking Doug to describe his strategy, which led Doug to discover the problem himself rather than being told. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p269]

## Inferences

- Inference: The pattern of 'intuitive landing' where experts can't verbalize their knowledge suggests that much expertise may exist as tacit knowledge that resists explicit transfer through traditional teaching methods.
