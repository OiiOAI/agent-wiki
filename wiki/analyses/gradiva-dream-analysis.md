# The Psychology of Dream Formation in Jensen's Gradiva

## Summary

Freud applied his theory of dream interpretation to the dreams in Jensen's 'Gradiva', demonstrating that the manifest content of Hanold's dreams concealed latent thoughts involving repressed memories of his childhood friend Zoe Bertgang. The dreams represented compromises between repressed erotic wishes and the censorship of resistance. Hanold's first dream showed him that Zoe (disguised as 'Gradiva') was living contemporaneously with him, while his second dream revealed Zoe's intentions through symbolic displacement (lizard-catching representing man-catching).

## Key facts

- Hanold's first dream showed Gradiva in Pompeii, but the latent thought was that the girl he was looking for was living in his town contemporaneously with him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2946]
- The displacement in the dream transformed 'Gradiva in the present' into the dreamer transported into the past at Pompeii. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2947]
- Hanold's second dream contained a play on words: 'somewhere in the sun' referred both to where he met Zoe's father and to the Albergo del Sole where Zoe was staying.
