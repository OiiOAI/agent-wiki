# Why Empathic States Don't Always Lead to Action

## Summary

Empathic states often fail to produce compassionate action because intense vicarious pain shifts focus to alleviating one's own distress rather than the other's. The anterior cingulate cortex monitors one's own physiological state, so when 'feeling someone's pain' becomes self-focused (how would I feel?), the dominant motivation becomes reducing personal discomfort. Additionally, cognitive load, stress, hunger, and social exclusion all reduce prosocial behavior by shifting priorities to self-care. The Buddhist monk approach—compassion without empathic arousal—activates different neural circuits (dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and mesolimbic dopamine) that promote sustained prosociality without the vicarious exhaustion.

## Key facts

- Feeling someone else's pain intensely makes one's primary concern alleviating one's own distress, leading to helping less and instead looking out for number one. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p556]
- Heart rate increases (indicating anxious arousal) predict less prosocial behavior, while heart rate decreases predict helping—the latter can 'hear' the sound of someone else's need rather than the distressed pounding in their own chest. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p556]
- Stress, working via glucocorticoids, reduces empathy; blocking glucocorticoid secretion makes both mice and humans more empathic toward strangers. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p556]
- When Matthieu Ricard focused on feeling others' pain empathically, his amygdala activated and it became 'intolerable' and 'emotionally exhausting'; when focusing on compassion without empathic arousal, his amygdala was silent and mesolimbic dopamine activated. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p557]

## Inferences

- Inference: Compassion training (focusing on warmth and care) produces dlPFC activation and coupling with dopaminergic regions, generating positive emotions and greater prosociality than empathy training (focusing on feeling the other's pain).
