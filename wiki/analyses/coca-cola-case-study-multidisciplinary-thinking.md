# The Coca-Cola Business Plan: A Case Study in Multidisciplinary Thinking

## Summary

This case study demonstrates how elementary ideas from psychology, physics, and other disciplines can be combined to solve extreme-scale business problems. The solution uses Pavlovian conditioning, operant conditioning, social proof, and lollapalooza effects to build a $2 trillion business from a $2 million investment.

## Key facts

- The Glotz problem posed in 1884: turn $2 million into $2 trillion in 150 years by creating a non-alcoholic beverage business called Coca-Cola, with half the equity going to the foundation. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p302]
- The business plan requires creating a strong trademark (to avoid generic commodity selling), universal appeal (harnessing elemental forces), and worldwide expansion starting from Atlanta. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p303]
- The essence of the business is creating and maintaining conditioned reflexes through operant conditioning (maximizing rewards) and Pavlovian conditioning (associating the brand with things consumers like). [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p306]
- The plan achieves lollapalooza results through a combination of Pavlovian conditioning, powerful social proof effects, and a wonderful-tasting, energy-giving, stimulating, desirably cold beverage causing operant conditioning. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p309]
- In reality, Coca-Cola Company followed so much of the plan that by 2006 it was worth about $125 billion and would only need to grow at 8% per year to reach $2 trillion by 2034. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p313]
- The 'New Coke' fiasco demonstrated that Coca-Cola's executives did not understand elementary psychology well enough to predict and avoid dangerously threatening their company. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p314]

## Inferences
