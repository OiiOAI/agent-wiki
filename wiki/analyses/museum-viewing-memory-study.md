# Museum Viewing and Memory Study

## Summary

A study conducted at the British Museum by Carlos Pedreira and Joaquín Navajas using a movable eye tracker found that visitors, over the course of a few minutes in a museum room, looked at approximately 50 objects for more than one second. However, when asked what they had seen after leaving the room, they could remember only about 5 objects. This striking discrepancy between visual attention and subsequent memory raises important questions about the limited capacity of human memory and the selective nature of visual encoding.

- After leaving the room, visitors were asked what they had seen and could remember only five or so objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 90% reduction from objects viewed (50) to objects remembered (5) suggests that sustained visual attention does not guarantee encoding into long-term memory, indicating distinct neural mechanisms for attention and memory consolidation.
