# Parallels Between Obsessional Neurosis and Religious Practices

## Summary

Freud identified structural similarities between obsessional neurosis and religious practices, including: ceremonial actions with anxiety upon neglect, stereotyped ritual forms, unconscious motivation underlying apparently meaningful actions, and the role of guilt and repression. However, religious ceremonials have recognized symbolic meaning while neurotic ones appear senseless until psychoanalytic interpretation reveals their significance. Both represent compromises between drive impulses and repressing agencies.

## Key facts

- Religious ceremonials share with neurotic ceremonials the conscience anxiety brought on by their neglect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2999]
- The sharpest difference between neurotic and religious ceremonial disappears when one penetrates to the true meaning of obsessive actions through psychoanalytic technique. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2999]
- Freud described religion as a universal obsessional neurosis and obsessional neurosis as an individual religiosity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3007]
