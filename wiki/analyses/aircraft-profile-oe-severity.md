# Aircraft Profile and Operational Error Severity

## Summary

Analysis of aircraft profiles during operational errors reveals that while most OEs occur when at least one aircraft is in level flight with another climbing or descending, the 'all level' aircraft profile is paradoxically associated with the greatest likelihood of moderate severity errors (43% overall). Flight level also relates to severity, with more errors being moderately severe at FL 290 or less than above FL 290.

## Key facts

- Most OEs occurred when aircraft were level and others were descending (45%) or climbing (31%). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p101]
- The greatest likelihood for moderate severity occurred when all aircraft were in level flight: 43% overall, 53% at FL 290 or less, and 29% at FL 290 or more. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p101]
- Moderately severe errors were more likely to occur at or below FL 290 than at higher flight levels. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p108]

## Inferences

- Inference: The counterintuitive finding that all-level profiles have higher severity may result from reduced attention to aircraft in level flight, as controllers may focus monitoring efforts on aircraft in transition phases.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Tests of significance were not conducted for aircraft profile data due to low expected values in some cells, so these findings are descriptive only.
