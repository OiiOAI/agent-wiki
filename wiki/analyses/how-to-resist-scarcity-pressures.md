# How to Resist Scarcity Pressures

## Summary

Cialdini outlines a two-stage response to scarcity pressures: First, use the physical arousal that scarcity creates as an internal warning signal to stop and calm down, since emotional reactions suppress cognitive analysis. Second, clarify whether you want the item for possession value or functional utility—if you want it for its utility (to eat, drive, etc.), remember that scarce items do not function any better than plentiful ones, as demonstrated by the cookie study showing scarce cookies were not rated as better-tasting.

## Key facts

- Scarcity pressures produce physical agitation, rising emotions, narrowed focus, and retreating cognitive processes. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p238]
- Robert Wood commented on his Poseidon experience: 'You get caught up in the mania of the thing, the acceleration of it. Logic goes right out the window.'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p238]
- Knowing the causes of scarcity pressure may not protect against them because knowledge is cognitive and cognitive processes are suppressed by emotional reactions to scarcity. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p239]
- The arousal itself can serve as a warning signal—tune into visceral reactions to flag the presence of scarcity tactics. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p239]
- In the cookie study, scarce cookies were rated as significantly more desirable but were not rated as any better-tasting than abundant cookies. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p240]
- The joy is not in experiencing a scarce commodity but in possessing it—these must not be confused. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p240]

## Inferences

- Inference: The two-stage defense strategy works because it addresses both the emotional and cognitive components of scarcity influence separately.
