# Dostoevsky and Parricide: Freud's Psychoanalytic Interpretation

## Summary

Freud's extended essay analyzes Dostoevsky as a paradigmatic case of neurotic pathology centered on the Oedipus complex. Through examination of Dostoevsky's life and works, particularly The Brothers Karamazov, Freud argues that the writer's so-called epilepsy was actually a hysterical identification with his father, serving as self-punishment for unconscious parricidal impulses. The essay traces the development of the superego from paternal identification, explores the connection between gambling addiction and childhood onanistic compulsion, and situates Dostoevsky's literary achievement within the context of the universal Oedipal theme found in Sophocles' Oedipus Rex and Shakespeare's Hamlet.

## Key facts

- Freud identifies castration anxiety as the mechanism that represses hatred of the father in the Oedipus complex, while simultaneously love for the father undergoes repression treated as an internal drive danger. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6714]
- Dostoevsky possessed a strong innate bisexual disposition, which Freud considers one of the preconditions or reinforcements of neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6714]
- The superego develops from identification with the father and, if the father was hard and cruel, the superego takes on those attributes, becoming sadistic while the ego becomes masochistic. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6715]
- Dostoevsky's epileptic attacks represented hysterical symptoms serving as father identification permitted by the superego as punishment: 'You wanted to kill your father in order to be your father yourself. Now you are your father, but a dead father.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6715]
- Freud states that a great need for punishment develops in the ego, which offers itself as a victim to Fate and finds satisfaction in ill-treatment by the superego, since every punishment is ultimately castration. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6715]
- The formula Freud proposes for Dostoevsky is: 'a person with a specially strong innate bisexual disposition, who can defend himself with special intensity against dependence on a specially severe father.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6715]
- Dostoevsky's gambling addiction was a method of self-punishment; the compulsive repetition of gambling represents a repetition of the old compulsion to masturbate, linked to fear of the father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6720]
- Freud argues that it is a fact that large groups of criminals want to be punished; their superego demands it and saves itself the necessity for inflicting the punishment itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6716]
- Three masterpieces deal with parricide as their subject: Sophocles' Oedipus Rex, Shakespeare's Hamlet, and Dostoevsky's The Brothers Karamazov, with sexual rivalry for a woman as the motive. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6717]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud connects the bliss experienced during the aura of Dostoevsky's attacks to a 'record of the triumph and sense of liberation felt on hearing the news of the death,' analogous to the triumph and mourning found in the ceremony of the totem meal described in Totem and Taboo.
- Inference: Freud's claim that gambling represents a repetition of onanistic compulsion is based on Stefan Zweig's story 'Vierundzwanzig Stunden aus dem Leben einer Frau,' which Freud interprets as representing a puberty wish-fantasy where the mother initiates the son into sexual life to save him from masturbation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Dostoevsky was free from his attacks in Siberia, which would substantiate Freud's view that they were punishment, cannot be proved.
