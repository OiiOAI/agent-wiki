# 800-Gram Count Assessment

## Summary

The 800-Gram Count is a self-assessment for tracking daily fruit and vegetable intake. The assessment involves weighing all sanctioned produce consumed throughout one day, tallying grams, and comparing the total to the 800-gram target.

## Key facts

- A kitchen scale is recommended for accurate measurement. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1491-L1494]
- The test should be conducted on a typical eating day, preferably when not eating out. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1499-L1502]
- Tracking begins with the first food consumed in the morning and ends with the last food at night. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1506-L1508]
- A score of 800 or more grams per day indicates success. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1524-L1527]
- Counted items include raw, cooked, frozen, or canned fruits and vegetables; fruits in smoothies, salsas, and soups; tofu; beans; tomato sauce without oil or sugar; potatoes; corn; edamame; peas; avocados; pickles; fermented vegetables like kimchi; olives; and fruit compotes without sugar. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1511-L1520]
- Items that do not count: dried fruits, nondairy milks, juice, jellies, jams, fried vegetables, grains, all flours, vegetable-based pastas, nuts, seeds, and popcorn. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1511-L1520]

## Inferences

- Inference: The assessment uses a binary pass/fail system: either you reach 800 grams or you do not, with no intermediate scoring.
- Inference: Retesting should continue daily until the person has a comfortable understanding of what constitutes 800 grams.
