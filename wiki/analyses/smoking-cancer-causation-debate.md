# The Smoking-Cancer Causation Debate

## Summary

Chapter 5 of The Book of Why examines a pivotal moment in scientific history when statisticians faced the challenge of determining whether smoking causes lung cancer. Unable to rely on their preferred method—the randomized controlled trial—researchers struggled to reach consensus on both the answer and the fundamental question itself. The debate illustrates why adequate causal language and methodology are essential for answering critical public health questions.

## Key facts

- The smoking-cancer causation debate was discussed in Chapter 5 of The Book of Why as a seminal moment in the history of causation and science. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L247-L247]
- Statisticians were unable to use randomized controlled trials to investigate whether smoking causes lung cancer. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L247-L247]
- Scientists disagreed on both the answer to whether smoking causes cancer and on how to formulate the causal question itself. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L247-L247]
- Millions of lives were lost or shortened because scientists lacked adequate causal language and methodology. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L247-L247]
