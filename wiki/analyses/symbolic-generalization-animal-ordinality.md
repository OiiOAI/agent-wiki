# Symbolic Generalization in Animal Ordinality Learning

## Summary

Analysis of whether ordinality and transitivity learning in non-human animals (chimpanzees, rhesus monkeys, and rats) demonstrates genuine symbolic generalization or merely indexical associative learning. The chapter concludes that current experimental evidence does not support symbolic generalization, as all studies approach a performance ceiling at quantities exceeding approximately 5.

## Key facts

- Boysen et al. (1995, 1996) conducted experiments investigating numerical abilities in chimpanzees, including studies on ordinality and transitivity learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p527]
- Hauser et al. (1995) demonstrated ordinality learning ability in wild rhesus monkeys. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p527]
- Eichenbaum et al. (1996) demonstrated transitive associations in rats using an ABC paradigm, with generalization possible across at least 5 ordered stimuli (ABCDE) to novel transitive relationships like BC. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p527]
- Ordinality and transitivity are described as respectively the token-object and token-token associative components of numerical symbols. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p527]
- All experimental results to date approach a ceiling in abilities when quantity exceeds approximately 5. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p527]
- The performance ceiling at quantity 5 suggests there is no symbolic generalization occurring in these animal experiments. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p527]

## Inferences

- Inference: The observation that animal studies show a ceiling at quantity 5 suggests that ordinal discrimination of quantity does not necessarily imply symbol use, distinguishing mere indexical association from genuine symbolic representation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether any of the chimpanzee or rat experiments actually accomplish the step from associative learning to genuine symbolic number concepts remains uncertain.
- Uncertain: Whether animals could generalize from known ordinal associations to novel transitive relationships—a test that would demonstrate symbolic generalization—has not been shown as of the time of writing.
