# Cynefin and Theory of Constraints Comparison

## Summary

Steve Holt's analysis concludes that Cynefin and Theory of Constraints are aligned rather than adversarial. While they use different terminology, both frameworks share similar philosophical roots and can be mutually reinforcing. TOC provides problem-solving tools while Cynefin provides sensemaking orientation.

## Key facts

- Eli Goldratt and Dave Snowden share similarities: both were physicist-philosophers, both ran software companies, both enjoyed argument and debate, and both used religion-based teaching stories. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p213]
- Goldratt's TOC definition of constraint aligns most closely with Cynefin's fixed and governing constraints, not enabling constraints. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p217]
- Goldratt's 'Inherent Simplicity' concept means finding heuristics that enable systems to be managed easily, similar to moving from Complex to Complicated domains in Cynefin. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p220]
- TOC practitioners start with observed issues (UDEs) and use abductive reasoning, testing with safe-to-fail probes, similar to Cynefin's Complex domain approach. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p215]

## Inferences

- Inference: Goldratt's approach to TOC was more aligned with Cynefin than many practitioners realize, given his use of abductive reasoning and experimentation.
