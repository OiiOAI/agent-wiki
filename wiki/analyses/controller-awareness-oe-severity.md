# Relationship Between Controller Awareness and Operational Error Severity

## Summary

Research demonstrates a clear relationship between controller situation awareness of developing errors and operational error severity. When controllers were aware that an error was developing, only 21% of errors were moderately severe, compared to over 50% for errors involving misuse of conflict alert or interfacility coordination where awareness is typically reduced. Horizontal separation (not vertical) was the parameter most affected by awareness levels.

## Key facts

- When controllers were aware the error was developing, 21% of OEs were moderately severe (267 cases). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p100]
- Misuse of displayed data (excluding conflict alert) was associated with 22% moderate severity, suggesting awareness can mitigate error impact. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p100]
- Factors reducing awareness (interfacility coordination at 53.8%, conflict alert misuse at 52%) were associated with greater severity. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p100]
- Greater separation existed for both vertical and horizontal dimensions for controller-aware errors, but only horizontal achieved statistical significance: t(54.69) = -3.06, p < .003. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p113]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistency of the finding across multiple studies that awareness correlates with reduced severity suggests this is a robust relationship that could inform training and procedure development.
