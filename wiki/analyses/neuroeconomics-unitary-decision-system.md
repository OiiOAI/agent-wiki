# Why neuroeconomics findings support a unitary decision-making system

## Summary

This analysis synthesizes evidence from the chapter arguing against multiple selves models. The posterior parietal cortex, traditionally associated with rational processing, predicts preferences under all conditions studied. The basal ganglia, traditionally associated with emotional processing, shows weak associations with emotional dysfunction in disease. Both systems globally participate in valuation rather than being confined to specific emotional or rational functions. The amygdala may influence valuation in ventral striatum but does not support a Freudian multiple-self model.

## Key facts

- LIP activity predicts choices for immediate and delayed rewards, large and small rewards, and high and low probability rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]
- Basal ganglia diseases are only very weakly associated with emotional dysfunction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]
- Dopaminergic forms of learning mediated by basal ganglia do not capture a clear notion of emotionality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]
- Medial prefrontal cortex encodes monetary and primary rewards, preference, expected values, gains and losses, and long-delayed monetary gains—a global involvement in valuation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]

## Inferences

- Inference: The available evidence suggests that existing multiple-self models are largely unsupported by the bulk of existing neurobiological data.
- Inference: Emotions influence decision-making through projections to valuation areas rather than through a separate decision-making system.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The absence of repeated studies showing well-localized neuroscientific correlates for multiple systems should be viewed as an unsolved problem for neuroeconomics rather than as a problem for the idea of multiple systems (Kahneman, p505).
