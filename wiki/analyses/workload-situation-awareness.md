# Workload Effects on Situation Awareness

## Summary

Research examining workload effects on controller situation awareness identifies multiple hypotheses. Awareness may decrease as workload increases due to difficulty maintaining an accurate mental picture. Awareness may also decrease during workload transitions (high to low after busy periods) or during sustained low workload. Additionally, fatigue effects may operate under sustained high workload. Multidimensional scaling analysis grouped facilities into four quadrants based on workload and awareness characteristics.

## Key facts

- Facilities were classified into four categories: high workload/high awareness, high workload/low awareness, low workload/high awareness, and low workload/low awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p109]
- ZAU and ZBW exemplified the low workload/low awareness combination, accounting for 17% of all moderately severe errors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p109]
- ZDV, ZLC, and ZKC exemplified the high workload/high awareness condition. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p104]

## Inferences

- Inference: Reduced SA might occur from different workload-related causes, and the specific mechanism operating during each error may vary by situation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Data were not available to determine which workload-awareness classification was operating during each error analyzed, limiting conclusions about causality.
