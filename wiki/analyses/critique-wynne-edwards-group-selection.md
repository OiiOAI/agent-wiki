# Critique of Wynne-Edwards Group Selection Theory

## Summary

Dawkins argues that Wynne-Edwards's group selection theory of animal birth control is unnecessary because selfish gene theory provides equally valid or better explanations for all the same phenomena. Wynne-Edwards proposed that animals altruistically restrain their birth rates for the good of the group, but this requires group selection (which he invoked explicitly), whereas individual-level selection through kin selection and individual optimization (Lack's theory) can explain clutch size regulation, territorial behavior, dominance hierarchies, and population stability without invoking altruism at the group level.

## Key facts

- Wynne-Edwards's core thesis was that animals regulate their birth rates altruistically for the good of the group, but this requires group selection because normal natural selection cannot evolve lower-than-average reproductive rates in individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p145]
- David Lack's alternative explains clutch size regulation as individual optimization: each female chooses clutch size that maximizes her own number of surviving children, not for group benefit. Individuals who have too many children are penalized because fewer survive—not because the population goes extinct. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p150]
- Overcrowding reducing birth rates is compatible with both theories: Wynne-Edwards says females measure population and adjust births for group benefit; selfish gene theory says females respond to overcrowding as a reliable predictor of future famine to maximize their own selfish reproductive success. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p153]
- Territorial behavior and dominance hierarchies can be explained without the 'ticket to breed' interpretation: outcast red grouse may simply be waiting for a territory-holder to die (wait-and-hope strategy) rather than 'accepting' that they shouldn't breed—the selfish gene explanation is sufficient. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p152]
- The evidence for Wynne-Edwards's theory is not good: it consists of examples that could be interpreted his way but could equally well be interpreted on more orthodox selfish gene lines. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p148]

## Inferences

- Inference: The key difference is explanatory mechanism: Wynne-Edwards requires selection between groups (slow, weak force), while selfish gene theory uses selection between individuals within groups (fast, strong force). The latter is generally more parsimonious.
- Inference: Where epideictic displays are concerned, Dawkins concludes Wynne-Edwards may have been right about the phenomenon but wrong about the mechanism—he was correct that population density affects reproduction, but for individual selfish reasons, not group benefit.
