# Negotiation One-Sheet Preparation

## Summary

The One-Sheet is a five-section preparatory document: (1) Goal—set optimistic but reasonable target, write it down, discuss with colleague; (2) Summary—two-sentence summary of known facts; (3) Labels/Accusation Audit—3-5 labels anticipating counterpart accusations; (4) Calibrated Questions—3-5 'What' and 'How' questions for uncovering value and deal killers; (5) Noncash Offers—list of nonmonetary items the counterpart could provide. Voss emphasizes that preparation is not about scripting exact outcomes but having tools ready for adaptive use.

## Key facts

- The One-Sheet has five sections: Goal, Summary, Labels/Accusation Audit, Calibrated Questions, and Noncash Offers. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p218]
- Voss criticizes BATNA-based preparation as setting the upper limit of what negotiators will ask for, leading to 'wimp-win' outcomes; instead, set a high-end goal and remain flexible for better options. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p219]
- The Summary section must describe the situation in a way the counterpart will respond to with 'That's right.'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p220]
- Fill-in-the-blank labels like 'It seems like ____ is valuable to you' can be used quickly without extensive thought. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p221]

## Inferences

- Inference: Voss's emphasis on 'falling to your highest level of preparation' under pressure explains why the One-Sheet focuses on adaptable tools rather than scripted responses.
