# How to Guarantee a Misérable Life

## Summary

Charlie Munger's 1986 Harvard School commencement speech inverted the typical graduation advice by prescribing how to guarantee misery. Drawing from Johnny Carson and adding his own prescriptions, Munger outlined behaviors to avoid: chemical addiction, envy, resentment, unreliability, failure to learn vicariously, surrendering to adversity, and ignoring逆向思考 wisdom.

## Key facts

- Carson's three prescriptions for misery were: ingesting chemicals, envy, and resentment. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p145]
- Munger's first prescription for misery: Be unreliable. Do not faithfully do what you have engaged to do. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p147]
- Munger's second prescription: Learn everything from your own experiences, minimizing vicarious learning from others. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p148]
- Munger's third prescription: Go down and stay down when you get your first, second, or third severe reverse. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p149]
- Munger's fourth prescription: Ignore the rustic's wisdom about avoiding places where you'd die. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p150]
- Johnson said: 'Life is hard enough to swallow without squeezing in the bitter rind of resentment.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p146]
- Epictetus's epitaph reads: 'Here lies Epictetus, a slave, maimed in body, the ultimate in poverty, and favored by the gods.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p149]
