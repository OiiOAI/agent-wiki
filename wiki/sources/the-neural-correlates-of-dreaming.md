# The neural correlates of dreaming

## Summary

This 2017 Nature Neuroscience paper by Siclari et al. investigated the neural correlates of conscious experience during sleep using high-density EEG. The study found that a posterior parieto-occipital cortical 'hot zone' distinguishes between dreaming and no-experience states in both NREM and REM sleep. Decreased low-frequency activity (1-4 Hz) and increased high-frequency activity (20-50 Hz) in this region predicted dream reports. The researchers also demonstrated real-time prediction of dream reports with 87% accuracy and showed that specific dream contents (faces, spatial settings, movement, speech) correlate with activity in corresponding sensory cortical areas.

## Key facts

- Conscious experience fades under normal physiological conditions during sleep; subjects report no experience up to 30% of the time when awakened early in the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p2]
- Up to 70% of NREM sleep awakenings yield dream reports, challenging the traditional view that dreaming is synonymous with REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p2]
- A bilateral parieto-occipital decrease in low-frequency EEG power (1-4 Hz) was associated with dream experience reports compared to no-experience reports in both NREM and REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Experiment 3 achieved 87% total prediction accuracy for distinguishing dreaming from no-experience states in real-time using EEG monitoring of the posterior hot zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p6]
- Dreams containing specific content categories activated corresponding sensory cortical areas: fusiform face area for faces, right posterior parietal cortex for spatial settings, superior temporal sulcus for movement, and Wernicke's area for speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p5]

## Inferences

- Inference: The posterior hot zone likely includes regions supporting multisensory integration necessary for the 'immersive spatio-temporal hallucinations' that characterize dreams, including precuneus, posterior cingulate, and retrosplenial cortices
