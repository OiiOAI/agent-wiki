# Finite and Infinite Games: A Vision of Life as Play and Possibility

## Summary

A philosophical work by James P. Carse that distinguishes between two types of games: finite games, played for the purpose of winning and reaching a definitive conclusion; and infinite games, played for the purpose of continuing the play indefinitely. The book explores how these distinctions apply to culture, society, politics, sexuality, art, language, and the meaning of human existence. Published in 1986 by Ballantine Books.

## Key facts

- The book was written by James P. Carse and first published in 1986 by Ballantine Books, New York. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p6]
- The book's subtitle is 'A Vision of Life as Play and Possibility'. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p1]
- The book is organized into five chapters: (1) There Are at Least Two Kinds of Games, (2) No One Can Play a Game Alone, (3) I Am the Genius of Myself, (4) A Finite Game Occurs Within a World, and (5) Nature Is the Realm of the Unspeakable. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p9]
- Finite games are played to win, to conclude, with audiences who observe players from outside the game. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p146]
- Infinite games are played for the sake of continued play, without audiences in the traditional sense, with players who enter the game freely. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p146]
- The book distinguishes between poiema (a made thing) and poiesis (the act of making), arguing that culture should be understood as poiesis. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p146]
