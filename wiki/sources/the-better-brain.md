# The Better Brain: Overcome Anxiety, Combat Depression, and Reduce ADHD and Stress with Nutrition

## Summary

This book presents research evidence that nutrition—specifically micronutrients (minerals and vitamins) and whole-food dietary patterns—is a fundamental, underrecognized factor in mental health and can serve as an effective primary treatment or adjunct for common psychiatric disorders including depression, anxiety, ADHD, bipolar disorder, and PTSD. Written by two academic scientists, it argues that the current pharmaceutical-dominated paradigm has failed to reduce the prevalence of mental illness, that medical education lacks adequate nutrition training, and that broad-spectrum multinutrient formulas (containing ~30 minerals and vitamins in balance) have shown consistent positive results in peer-reviewed clinical trials. The book covers the science of brain metabolism, the gut microbiome, soil health, and provides practical dietary guidance.

## Key facts

- The book is authored by Bonnie J. Kaplan (PhD, experimental psychology/neurophysiology) and Julia J. Rucklidge (PhD, clinical psychology), who conducted research at the University of Calgary and the University of Canterbury respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p15]
- The authors have no financial ties to any supplement company and no conflicts of interest. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p21]
- Two specific broad-spectrum multinutrient formulas studied in the authors' research are EMP (EMPowerplus, originally called EMPower) and DEN (Daily Essential Nutrients, by Hardy Nutritionals), containing approximately 30 minerals, vitamins, amino acids, and antioxidants. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p17]
- More than fifty peer-reviewed publications have been produced on EMP and DEN, examined and approved by government health regulators in three countries. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p18]
- In every clinical trial using broad-spectrum multinutrients to treat psychiatric symptoms, positive results have been reported; approximately 80% of participants experienced some benefit, with about 50% being much to very much improved. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
- In the SMILES trial, participants had to have previously experienced a major depressive episode and also had to report that they ate a typical unhealthy diet with a high intake of sweets, processed meats, and salty snacks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p113]
- Those in the dietary support group had seven hours of training with a dietitian to learn about a whole foods Mediterranean diet approach. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p113]
- After the SMILES trial, 32 percent of the dietary support group went into remission for their depression compared to 8 percent of the social support group. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p113]
- In the SMILES trial screening, only 15 out of 166 (9 percent) of adults with depression met Australian guidelines for having a good diet. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p114]
