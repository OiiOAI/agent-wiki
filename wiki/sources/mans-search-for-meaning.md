# Man's Search for Meaning

## Summary

Man's Search for Meaning is a memoir and philosophical work by Viktor E. Frankl documenting his experiences as a Holocaust survivor in concentration camps (Auschwitz and affiliated camps), paired with an exposition of his therapeutic method called logotherapy. The book argues that life's primary motivation is meaning, not pleasure or power, and that individuals retain freedom of choice in their attitudes even under extreme suffering.

## Key facts

- The book was written in nine successive days in 1945. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- Frankl originally intended the book to be published anonymously, believing it would never earn its author literary fame. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- The book comprises two parts: an autobiographical account titled 'Experiences in a Concentration Camp' and a theoretical section called 'Logotherapy in a Nutshell'. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- By 1992, the book had nearly one hundred printings in English and had been published in twenty-one other languages. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- English editions alone had sold more than three million copies by 1992. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- Frankl wrote the book to demonstrate that life holds a potential meaning under any conditions, even the most miserable ones. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- Frankl believed his book achieved success because hundreds of thousands of people were reaching for a book dealing with the question of meaning in life. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- The book was first published in German in 1946 as 'A Psychologist Experiences the Concentration Camp' and later as 'Say Yes to Life in Spite of Everything.'. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p94]
- The English translation was first published in 1959 under the title Man's Search for Meaning. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p94]
- The book has sold more than 12 million copies in a total of twenty-four languages. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p94]
- A 1991 Library of Congress/Book-of-the-Month-Club survey named Man's Search for Meaning among the ten most influential books in America. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p94]
- Frankl survived four concentration camps: Theresienstadt, Auschwitz-Birkenau, Kaufering, and Turkheim, part of the Dachau complex. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p95]
- After liberation, Frankl learned his pregnant wife Tilly died in Bergen-Belsen, and his parents and brother died in the camps. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p98]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book's success suggests widespread existential crisis in modern society, as Frankl himself noted that the book's popularity expressed 'the misery of our time' [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9].
