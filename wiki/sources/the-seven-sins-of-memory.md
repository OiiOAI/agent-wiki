# The Seven Sins of Memory: How the Mind Forgets and Remembers

## Summary

A comprehensive exploration of memory failures organized around seven fundamental transgressions: transience, absent-mindedness, blocking, misattribution, suggestibility, bias, and persistence. Schacter argues that each 'sin' represents a by-product of adaptive memory features rather than inherent flaws. The 2021 updated edition incorporates two decades of new research findings and updates at the end of each chapter.

## Inferences

- Inference: Schacter classifies persistence and transience as likely evolutionary adaptations, blocking/absent-mindedness/misattribution/suggestibility as likely spandrels (by-products of useful features), and bias as more culturally variable.
- Inference: The reconsolidation-based treatment findings suggest that propranolol's beneficial effects may involve memory destabilization during reactivation rather than simple blockade of stress hormones.
- Inference: The book synthesizes cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and evolutionary biology to provide a comprehensive account of memory failures.
- Inference: Schacter's framework treats memory not as a perfect recorder but as an adaptive system shaped by evolution, where errors sometimes reflect useful trade-offs.
