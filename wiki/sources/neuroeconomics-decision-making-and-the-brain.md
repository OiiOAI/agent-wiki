## Inferences

- Inference: The textbook demonstrates strong connections between reinforcement learning theory and neural implementations of reward processing.
- Inference: The chapters show how economic models of altruism and public goods can be tested using neural evidence.
- Inference: The convergence of evidence from perceptual decision tasks (random dot motion), foraging tasks (matching law), and competitive games (matching pennies) on similar computational architectures (softmax action selection, reinforcement learning valuation) suggests common neural mechanisms for choice across diverse domains.
- Inference: The chapter represents a synthesis of extensive work on the primate mechanism for choice, identifying areas of economic theory most relevant to neurobiology.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Several features of the model remain unspecified, including critical pathways between valuation and choice areas and the systems that decide when to choose.
