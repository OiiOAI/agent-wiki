# Nonviolent Communication: A Language of Life

## Summary

Nonviolent Communication: A Language of Life (3rd edition, 2015) by Marshall B. Rosenberg presents a methodology for compassionate communication structured around four components: observations, feelings, needs, and requests. The book addresses how language and communication patterns can block compassion, and provides practical guidance for expressing oneself honestly while receiving others empathically. It covers applications across intimate relationships, families, schools, workplaces, therapy, conflict resolution, and international mediation.

## Key facts

- Nonviolent Communication is founded on language and communication skills that strengthen our ability to remain human, even under trying conditions. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p28]
- The four components of NVC are: 1) observations, 2) feelings, 3) needs, and 4) requests. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p31]
- NVC guides us in reframing how we express ourselves and hear others, replacing habitual automatic reactions with conscious responses based on awareness of what we are perceiving, feeling, and wanting. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p28]
- The book is published by PuddleDancer Press (3rd edition, September 2015) and includes chapters on communication blocking compassion, observing without evaluating, identifying feelings, taking responsibility for feelings, making requests, receiving empathically, conflict resolution, and expressing appreciation. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p14-17]
- The foreword by Deepak Chopra connects NVC to the concept of Ahimsa, noting that Rosenberg grasped both action and consciousness levels of nonviolence. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p20]
- NVC is described as having nothing new integrated into it—all has been known for centuries—and serves as a reminder about how humans were meant to relate to one another. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p28]
- Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella recommended the book to his leadership team as part of building empathy in corporate culture. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p6]
- NVC consists of two parts: expressing honestly and receiving empathically. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p134]
- Empathy is described as a respectful understanding of what others are experiencing, requiring emptying our mind and listening with our whole being. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p134]
- We need empathy to give empathy; when unable to empathize, we should give ourselves emergency first aid empathy, scream nonviolently, or take time out. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p147-p149]
- Self-judgments, like all judgments, are tragic expressions of unmet needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p178]
- NVC mourning is connecting with feelings and unmet needs stimulated by past actions we regret. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p180]
- We are never angry because of what others say or do; the cause of anger lies in our thinking—in thoughts of blame and judgment. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p194]
- The four steps to expressing anger are: stop and breathe, identify judgmental thoughts, connect with needs, and express feelings and unmet needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p209]
- Creating a connection between people is the most important element in conflict resolution. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p216]
- NVC differs from traditional mediation by concentrating on identifying the needs of both parties before seeking strategies to fulfill those needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p247]
- The protective use of force prevents injury or injustice without passing judgment on the person or behavior, while the punitive use of force causes individuals to suffer for perceived misdeeds. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p249]
- Punitive action tends to generate resentment and hostility and to reinforce resistance to the very behavior being sought. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p250]
- NVC helps resolve internal conflicts by translating negative internal messages into feelings and needs, which can free people from depression. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p265]
- Appreciation expressed in NVC has three components: the actions contributing to well-being, the needs fulfilled, and the pleasureful feelings from those needs being met. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p279]
