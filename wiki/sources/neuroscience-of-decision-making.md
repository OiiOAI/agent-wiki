# Neuroscience of Decision Making

## Summary

This edited volume explores the intersection of behavioral decision research and neuroscience, providing a coherent framework for understanding key themes in judgment and decision making research. Published in 2011 by Psychology Press, the book covers five sections: theoretical models of decision making, risk and uncertainty, reward and loss processing, cooperation and trust, and goal-directed decision making. It features contributions from leading researchers examining how brain systems underlie choice behavior.

## Inferences

- Inference: The book integrates cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and computational modeling to provide a unified account of decision-making processes.
- Inference: The various chapters suggest that different neural substrates underlie different aspects of decision making: emotion/somatic signals (Ch 3), uncertainty/salience signals (Ch 4), heuristic/analytic conflicts (Ch 5), and reward learning (Ch 6).
- Inference: The striatum performs distinct functions across its subdivisions, with ventral regions supporting reward anticipation and dorsal regions mediating action-outcome learning and habits.
- Inference: The caudate nucleus integrates contextual information with dopaminergic signals to guide motivated behavior based on goal relevance.
- Inference: Reward and punishment processing deficits in depression may reflect underlying mesolimbic dopaminergic system dysfunction.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact boundaries between heuristic and analytic neural systems remain uncertain, with heuristic processing covering a wide region of the lateral temporal lobe.
- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms causing anhedonia in depression remain unclear, particularly whether deficits are more tied to reward anticipation versus consumption.
- Uncertain: The extent to which the ventral striatum specifically codes reward versus general salience remains debated.
