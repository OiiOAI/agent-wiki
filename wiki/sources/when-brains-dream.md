# When Brains Dream: Exploring the Science and Mystery of Sleep

## Summary

A neuroscience book by Antonio Zadra and Robert Stickgold published by W.W. Norton & Company, exploring the science and mystery of sleep and dreams.

## Key facts

- The book is titled 'When Brains Dream: Exploring the Science and Mystery of Sleep'. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p2]
- The authors are Antonio Zadra and Robert Stickgold. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p2]
- The book was published by W.W. Norton & Company, Independent Publishers Since 1923. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p2]
- The book is dedicated to early dream explorers and to all who have wondered why we dream, where dreams come from, and what meanings they may hold. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p3]
- The book proposes the NEXTUP model (Network Exploration to Understand Possibilities) as an explanation for why humans dream. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p9]
- The authors state that dreams can predict the future in a way that waking consciousness cannot. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p9]
- The book challenges the idea that dreams occur only in REM sleep, citing evidence that dream reports are obtained from N1, N2, N3, and REM stages. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
- The authors argue that for every two hours awake, the brain needs approximately one hour of sleep to process the meaning and significance of new information. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p20]
- Humans dream during all sleep stages, not just REM sleep, spending more than six hours out of eight in various dream states. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p97]
- The average adult recalls only about four to six dreams a month, despite dreaming extensively throughout the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p97]
- The function of dreaming takes place 'live' while the dream is unfolding, not dependent on remembering dreams upon awakening. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p97]
- NEXTUP proposes that dreaming extracts new knowledge from existing memories through the discovery and strengthening of previously unexplored weak associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p112]
- During REM sleep, priming produced by strongly related words drops by 90 percent while that produced by weak primes increases more than twofold. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p116]
- Serotonin release is completely blocked during REM sleep, which may shift the brain's bias toward assigning more value to weak associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p122]
- Dreams contain convincing sensory experiences embedded in narrative stories with moment-to-moment but not beginning-to-end continuity. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p158]
- Nightmares affect 10 to 30 percent of adults at least once a month, with approximately 85 percent reporting at least one nightmare per year. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p168]
- Creative breakthroughs in dreams are rare, but up to a third of participants can solve problems with personal meaning over a week using dream incubation. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p187]
- The book contains 15 chapters plus an epilogue covering topics from early dream explorers through contemporary dream science. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p327]
- Chapter structure includes: early explorers (Ch. 2), Freud's theories (Ch. 3), neuroscience methods (Ch. 4), sleep functions (Ch. 5), animal dreams (Ch. 6), why we dream (Ch. 7), the NEXTUP model (Ch. 8), dream content (Ch. 9-10), creativity (Ch. 11), therapy applications (Ch. 12), PTSD and nightmares (Ch. 13), lucid dreaming (Ch. 14), and paranormal claims (Ch. 15). [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p327]
- The book includes an appendix presenting the NEXTUP model of how and why we dream. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p327]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book synthesizes findings from sleep laboratories, neuroimaging studies, and historical scholarship on dreams to build its theoretical framework.
- Inference: The authors argue that dream interpretation as commonly practiced cannot be the evolutionary function of dreaming, as most dreams are never interpreted.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neurobiological mechanisms by which NEXTUP operates remain to be fully elucidated.
