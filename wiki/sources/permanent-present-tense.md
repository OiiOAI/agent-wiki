# Permanent Present Tense: The Unforgettable Life of the Amnesic Patient, H.M.

## Summary

Permanent Present Tense is a neuroscience book by Suzanne Corkin documenting the life and case of Henry Molaison (H.M.), an amnesic patient who underwent bilateral medial temporal-lobe resection in 1953 to treat epilepsy. The surgery successfully controlled his seizures but resulted in severe, permanent amnesia that robbed him of the ability to form new long-term memories. Corkin studied Henry for 46 years, from 1962 until his death in 2008, using his case to revolutionize understanding of memory systems, including the distinction between short-term and long-term memory, and declarative versus nondeclarative memory.
