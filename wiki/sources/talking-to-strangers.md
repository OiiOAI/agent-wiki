# Talking to Strangers

## Summary

Talking to Strangers is a 2019 book by Malcolm Gladwell that examines why humans are so poor at judging strangers, using historical and contemporary case studies including Sandra Bland, Fidel Castro's spies, Neville Chamberlain's negotiations with Hitler, Bernie Madoff, and Jerry Sandusky. The book argues that defaulting to truth is a rational human behavior with deep evolutionary roots.

## Key facts

- The book was published in 2019 by Little, Brown and Company. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p4]
- The book is divided into five parts: Spies and Diplomats, Default to Truth, Transparency, Lessons, and Coupling. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p5]
- Gladwell uses the Sandra Bland traffic stop incident as a central case study throughout the book's introduction. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p9]
- The central question is why humans are so bad at understanding strangers, despite being forced to interact with them constantly in modern society. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p1]
- The Sandra Bland case serves as the book's frame, with Gladwell returning to it repeatedly to illustrate how multiple failures combined to produce her death. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p192-194]
