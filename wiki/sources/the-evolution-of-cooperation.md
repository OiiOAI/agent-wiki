# The Evolution of Cooperation

## Summary

A book by Robert Axelrod that explores how cooperation can evolve even in populations of selfish individuals through analysis of the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma. The book spawned a substantial research industry across multiple disciplines including political science, economics, biology, sociology, and computer science. Richard Dawkins wrote the foreword to the new edition, describing it as 'believable optimism' grounded in Darwinian principles.

## Key facts

- The book was first published in 1984 by Basic Books. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p8]
- A revised edition was published in 2006. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p8]
- Richard Dawkins wrote the foreword to the new edition. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p1]
- An earlier version of Chapter 5 appeared in Science in 1981 and won the Newcomb-Cleveland prize of the American Association for the Advancement of Science. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p2]
- The book's core concept is the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma tournament, where computer programs competed to play the game. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p2]
- By 1988, Axelrod and Douglas Dion had compiled an annotated bibliography listing more than 250 works directly inspired by the book. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p4]
- Research inspired by the book spans politics and law, economics, sociology and anthropology, biological applications, evolutionary theory, computer science, and more. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p4]
- The book was not originally published in Britain; it became available there after Richard Dawkins recommended it to Penguin Books in 1989. [raw/books/strategy/The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod.pdf#p3]
