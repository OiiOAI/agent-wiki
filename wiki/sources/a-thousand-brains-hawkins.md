# A Thousand Brains: A New Theory of Intelligence

## Summary

This book presents Jeff Hawkins' Thousand Brains Theory of intelligence, which proposes that the neocortex contains approximately 150,000 cortical columns, each of which learns a complete model of the world using reference frames. The book covers Hawkins' journey from studying electrical engineering to founding Numenta, his discovery of how neurons make predictions via dendrite spikes, and his insight that reference frames are the missing framework for understanding cortical function. It explores implications for machine intelligence, consciousness, and humanity's future.

## Key facts

- The neocortex occupies about 70 percent of the volume of a human brain and is responsible for all capabilities associated with intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p16]
- A human neocortex contains approximately 150,000 cortical columns, each about one square millimeter wide. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p36]
- Jeff Hawkins co-founded Palm Computing in 1992 and later created some of the first handheld computers and smartphones. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p21]
- Numenta was founded to develop a theory of how the neocortex works and apply brain principles to machine learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p22]
- In 2010, Hawkins' team discovered that dendrite spikes represent predictions inside neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p56]
- In 2016, Hawkins realized that reference frames are the missing ingredient in understanding cortical columns. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p59]
- Grid cells and place cells exist not only in the old brain (hippocampus and entorhinal cortex) but also in the neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p67]
- The neocortex contains about 150,000 cortical columns, each capable of learning models of the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p225]
- The neocortex learns models using reference frames, similar to how grid cells and place cells work in the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p225]
- The book divides the brain into the old brain (30%) and the new brain or neocortex (70%). [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p208]
- False beliefs persist due to three factors: they cannot be directly experienced, contrary evidence must be ignored, and they prescribe behaviors for viral spread. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p182]
- The combination of the old brain and viral false beliefs represents an existential risk greater than the presumed threat of AI. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p225]
- The book proposes that the neocortex uses reference frames to learn models of objects and that each cortical column is a complete sensory-motor system capable of recognizing complete objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p96]
- The author argues that current AI approaches based on deep learning cannot achieve true intelligence because they lack continuous learning, embodiment, multiple models, and reference frames. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p116]
- The book addresses consciousness as a physical phenomenon tied to the brain's modeling activity and memory formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Thousand Brains Theory suggests that what we perceive as a unified reality is actually a democratic consensus among thousands of semi-autonomous cortical columns, each building its own model of the world.
- Inference: The book argues that true machine intelligence requires learning models of the world similar to the neocortex, rather than just pattern matching.
- Inference: Hawkins proposes that knowledge preservation may be more important than gene preservation for humanity's legacy.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact number of cortical columns in humans may vary from the stated 150,000; Hawkins acknowledges this is a simplification.
- Uncertain: The detailed mechanism by which neurons in the neocortex create reference frames is still being worked out.
