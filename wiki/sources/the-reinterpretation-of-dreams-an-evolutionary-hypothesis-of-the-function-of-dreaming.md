# The Reinterpretation of Dreams: An Evolutionary Hypothesis of the Function of Dreaming

## Summary

This paper by Antti Revonsuo presents the Threat Simulation Theory of dreaming, arguing that the biological function of dreaming is to simulate threatening events and rehearse threat perception and threat avoidance skills. The theory is grounded in evolutionary psychology and positions dreaming as an adaptive mechanism that evolved in the ancestral human environment where threats to survival were frequent. The author presents six empirically testable propositions supported by evidence from normative dream content, children's dreams, recurrent dreams, nightmares, post-traumatic dreams, and the dreams of hunter-gatherers.

## Key facts

- The paper argues that dream consciousness is not a random by-product of REM sleep but an organized and selective simulation of the perceptual world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p1]
- The Threat Simulation Theory posits six propositions: (1) Dream consciousness is an organized and selective simulation; (2) It is specialized in simulating threatening events; (3) Only real threatening events fully activate the system; (4) Threat simulations are perceptually and behaviorally realistic rehearsals; (5) Realistic rehearsal leads to enhanced performance regardless of explicit memory; (6) The ancestral environment included frequent dangerous events that activated these mechanisms.
