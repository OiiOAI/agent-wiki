# Memory, Sleep, and Dreaming: Experiencing Consolidation

## Summary

This article reviews evidence that sleep plays a critical role in consolidating memories, transforming them by integrating new information into existing networks while retaining the essential gist. The authors argue that subjective dream reports provide a window into memory consolidation processes occurring in the sleeping brain, and present experimental evidence linking dream content to both recent experience and enhanced memory performance.

## Key facts

- Postlearning sleep is beneficial for human memory performance across verbal learning, procedural skill learning, emotional memory, and spatial navigation tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p1]
- Animal research demonstrates that presleep experience is replayed on a cellular level during postlearning sleep, with neural reactivation most consistently observed during NREM sleep following hippocampal sharp-wave ripple burst events. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p2]
- Sleep transforms memories rather than veridically strengthening them, facilitating integration into cortical networks and extraction of meaning from experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p3]
- NREM dreams are more likely to contain episodic memory sources than REM dreams, whereas REM dreams feature more intense emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p4]
- Recent memories are most strongly related to dream content early in the sleep phase, with this association decreasing over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p4]
- Only intermittent fragments of recent episodic memories appear in sleep mentation, intermingled with remote and semantically related material, rather than faithful replay. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p4]
- In studies of 299 home-collected dream reports, 51% were judged to contain at least one dream element bearing strong similarity to a recent waking experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p5]
- After playing the video game Tetris, 64% of participants reported unambiguous game-related images in at least one sleep onset report. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p6]
- After training on a downhill skiing arcade game, 30% of 386 sleep onset mentation reports directly incorporated the game. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p6]
- Dreaming of a spatial learning task is associated with enhanced subsequent navigation performance, whereas thinking of the task during wakefulness is unrelated to later performance. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p9]
- Sleep participants who referred to the maze task in their reports improved 10-fold more at retest than sleep participants who gave no task-related reports (P = .0003). [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p10]

## Inferences

- Inference: The McClelland et al. complementary learning systems theory proposes that sleep facilitates memory consolidation by alternating reactivation of new and old memories during different sleep stages, allowing integration without interference.
- Inference: The finding that dreaming of a task predicts enhanced memory consolidation suggests dream content may serve as a behavioral marker of successful offline memory processing.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neurophysiological mechanisms linking conscious dream experience to memory consolidation remain under investigation.
- Uncertain: The relationship between REM sleep reactivation and dreaming is less clearly established than for NREM sleep.
