# Getting to Yes: Negotiating an Agreement Without Giving In

## Summary

This book presents the method of 'principled negotiation' developed at the Harvard Negotiation Project. It offers an alternative to positional bargaining by focusing on four core principles: separating people from the problem, focusing on interests rather than positions, inventing options for mutual gain, and insisting on objective criteria. The method is designed to produce wise agreements efficiently while maintaining or improving relationships between parties.

## Key facts

- The book was authored by Roger Fisher and William Ury, with Bruce Patton joining as editor and co-author for the second edition. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p1]
- The authors developed the principled negotiation method at the Harvard Negotiation Project at Harvard Law School. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p10]
- The book identifies four principles of principled negotiation: separate the people from the problem, focus on interests not positions, invent options for mutual gain, and insist on using objective criteria. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p10]
- Principled negotiation contrasts with positional bargaining by deciding issues on their merits rather than through a haggling process focused on what each side says it will and won't do. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p6]
- The method is described as 'hard on the merits, soft on the people' and uses no tricks or posturing. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p6]
- No method can guarantee success if all the leverage lies on the other side; negotiation methods can only protect against bad agreements and maximize the value of any agreement reached. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p50]
- Principled negotiation can produce outcomes as good as or better than any other negotiation strategy while being more efficient and less damaging to relationships. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p71]
- The book argues that the first thing you are trying to win is a better way to negotiate—a way that avoids choosing between getting what you deserve and being decent. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p72]
