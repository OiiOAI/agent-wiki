# Mind in Life: Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of Mind

## Summary

This book explores the deep continuity between life and mind, arguing that mind emerges from the self-organizing features of life. The author brings together biology, phenomenological philosophy, and neuroscience to address the explanatory gap between consciousness and nature. The work elaborates the enactive approach, which posits that cognitive beings actively generate and maintain their own cognitive domains through autonomous agency and structural coupling with the environment.

## Key facts

- The central thesis is that there is a deep continuity between life and mind: where there is life there is mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p8]
- The self-organizing features of mind are an enriched version of the self-organizing features of life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p8]
- The autopoietic organization of biological life already implies cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p8]
- Mental life is bodily life and is situated in the world; roots of mental life lie in body and environment, not simply the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p8]
- The book draws from three main sources: biology, phenomenological philosophy, and psychology and neuroscience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p8]
- The book was originally co-authored with Francisco Varela as a follow-up to The Embodied Mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p10]
- The theory of autopoiesis addresses the organization of the living by focusing on the single biological individual—the living cell—as a self-producing unity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p102]
- An autopoietic machine is organized as a network of processes of production that continuously regenerate and realize the network of processes that produced them, and constitute it as a concrete unity in space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p104]
- Viruses are not autopoietic because they do not produce from within themselves their own components; these are produced by the host cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p107]
- The individual human subject is the enculturated bodily subject; the knowing and feeling subject is not the brain in the head or even the brain plus the body, but the socially and culturally situated person. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p397]
- Thompson reassesses his earlier critical view of Husserl, concluding that Husserl was not a methodological solipsist, was greatly concerned with intersubjective and embodied aspects of experience, and that his theory of intentionality was not a representational theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p398]
- Thompson argues that Dreyfus's interpretation of Husserl as a representationalist and protocognitivist is mistaken and should be replaced with a thorough reassessment of Husserl's work. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p398]
- The book develops the 'enactive approach,' arguing for deep continuity between life and mind including conscious mentality, grounded in autopoiesis and structural coupling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p162]
- Thompson argues against genocentrism and adaptationism, proposing instead 'enactive evolution' based on autopoietic and developmental systems perspectives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p197]
- The book argues the 'hard problem' of consciousness results from Cartesian categories that separate consciousness from life; a better formulation is the 'body-body problem' asking how living subjectivity emerges from living being. ## Inferences

- Inference: The book's aim is to enlarge and enrich philosophical and scientific resources for addressing the explanatory gap, rather than to close it reductively.
- Inference: Thompson's approach involves mutually illuminating phenomenological analyses of experience with scientific analyses of life and mind.
- Inference: The appendix on Husserl demonstrates Thompson's intellectual development from The Embodied Mind (1991) to Mind in Life (2007), showing a more favorable assessment of Husserlian phenomenology for cognitive science.
