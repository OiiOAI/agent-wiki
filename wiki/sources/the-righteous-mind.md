# The Righteous Mind: Why Good People Are Divided by Politics and Religion

## Summary

A 2012 book by social psychologist Jonathan Haidt exploring the moral foundations of human reasoning, arguing that moral judgments arise from intuitive processes and that political and religious divisions stem from differing moral value systems across cultures.

## Key facts

- The book is authored by Jonathan Haidt. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p4]
- It was published in 2012 by Pantheon Books, a division of Random House, Inc., in New York. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p4]
- The full title is 'The Righteous Mind: Why Good People Are Divided by Politics and Religion'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p4]
- The Library of Congress classification places it under Ethics, Social psychology, Political psychology, and Psychology, Religious. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p4]
- The book is dedicated to Harold Haidt, the author's father. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p5]
- The book's central question, posed in the introduction, asks why it is so hard for people to get along, given that 'we are all stuck here for a while' on Earth together. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p9]
- Haidt argues that morality is 'the extraordinary human capacity that made civilization possible' by enabling large cooperative groups beyond kinship. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p10]
- The book is organized into three parts corresponding to three principles of moral psychology: Part I covers intuitions first/strategic reasoning second; Part II covers the moral foundations beyond harm and fairness; Part III covers morality binding and blinding. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p12-13]
- Moral Foundations Theory proposes six psychological systems comprising universal foundations of moral matrices. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p203]
- Liberals rely primarily on three foundations (Care/harm, Liberty/oppression, Fairness/cheating), while conservatives use all six. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p205]
- The book introduces the 'hive hypothesis,' arguing humans are '90 percent chimp and 10 percent bee.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p242]
- Haidt defends group selection as a valid evolutionary mechanism, presenting four exhibits: major transitions, shared intentionality, gene-culture coevolution, and fast evolution. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p220]
- The book argues that religion functions as a 'team sport' that binds groups together through shared practices and beliefs. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p268]
- The book argues that moral intuition drives moral judgment, with reasoning serving to justify intuitions rather than discover truth. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p389]
- Haidt proposes that humans are '90 percent chimp and 10 percent bee,' having evolved both individualistic primate nature and groupish eusocial capacities. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p394]
- The book develops 'Durkheimian utilitarianism' as a moral framework that incorporates group solidarity alongside individual welfare. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p415]
- Haidt identifies five moral foundations (Care, Fairness, Loyalty, Authority, Sanctity) that vary in emphasis between liberals and conservatives. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p409]
- In lab experiments with invisibility and plausible deniability, most people cheat only up to the point where they can find justification preserving their belief in their own honesty. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p100]
- Motivated reasoning follows a 'Can I believe it?' pattern when we want to believe something, versus 'Must I believe it?' when we don't want to believe. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p101]
- Self-interest is a weak predictor of policy preferences; rather, people care about their groups—racial, regional, religious, or political. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p102]
- Drew Westen's fMRI study found that threatening information about one's own candidate activates emotion-related brain areas, with no increase in dlPFC activity (cool reasoning). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p104]
- Moral philosophers (tested by Eric Schwitzgebel) are no more virtuous than other professors across measures including charity, voting, and library book returns. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p105]
- WEIRD people (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) are statistical outliers on many psychological measures. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p112]
- Shweder's three ethics are: autonomy (individuals with rights), community (members of larger entities with duties), and divinity (temporary vessels with divine souls). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p115]
- The five moral foundations are: Care/harm, Fairness/cheating, Loyalty/betrayal, Authority/subversion, and Sanctity/degradation. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p142]
- Liberals rely primarily on Care and Fairness foundations; conservatives use all five foundations more equally. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p177]
- A sixth foundation, Liberty/oppression, was added to account for proportionality fairness and reverse dominance hierarchies. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p190]
