# The Feeling of What Happens: Body and Emotion in the Making of Consciousness

## Summary

This book by Antonio Damasio explores the biological basis of consciousness and its relationship to emotion and feeling. Damasio argues that consciousness emerges from the brain's representation of the body and that emotions are essential regulatory devices evolved for survival. The book distinguishes between core consciousness (simple, momentary awareness) and extended consciousness (elaborate self-awareness tied to memory and identity), proposing the proto-self as the nonconscious foundation from which both emerge.

## Key facts

- Consciousness consists of two intimately related problems: how the brain engenders mental images of objects, and how the brain engenders a sense of self in the act of knowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p9]
- Consciousness and mind are closely tied to external behaviors observable by third persons, and both are correlated with the functions of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p13]
- The book proposes that homeostasis—coordinated physiological reactions maintaining steady internal states—is key to understanding consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p40]
- The triangulation of mind, behavior, and brain has allowed advances in understanding vision, memory, and language, with similar approaches applied to consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p13]
- The book proposes that consciousness has at least two distinct kinds: core consciousness (simple, foundational) and extended consciousness (complex, dependent on memory and language). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p77]
- Wakefulness and consciousness can be decoupled: dream sleep shows consciousness without wakefulness; akinetic mutism shows wakefulness without consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p88]
- Core consciousness requires only a brief, short-term memory (about a fraction of a second), not extensive autobiographical memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p94]
- The proto-self is a nonconscious neural collection that maps the body's current state moment by moment, serving as the biological substrate for self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p153]
- Core consciousness occurs when second-order neural maps represent the relationship between an object and the modified proto-self, producing a feeling of knowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p168]
- Consciousness is founded on the body's representation in the brain—structures involved in body regulation are the same structures involved in consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p313]
- The brain structures most closely related to consciousness are phylogenetically older and located near the brain's midline, not on its external surface. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p276]
- Consciousness is valuable because it links the world of automatic regulation with the world of imagination, enabling novel adaptive responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p303]
- The book proposes that core consciousness provides a sense of self in the moment, while extended consciousness draws upon autobiographical memory accumulated over a lifetime. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
- Damasio argues that consciousness has its origins in the representation of the organism rather than the representation of objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
- Multiple personality disorder patients have multiple autobiographical selves but share one core consciousness and one core self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
- The core self is closely tied to the proto-self, which is based on representations of one singular body in its singular brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
