# The Matrix (Red Pill vs Blue Pill)

## Summary

The Matrix metaphor represents the choice between comfortable illusion (blue pill) and uncomfortable truth (red pill). Like the film's character Neo, people who choose truthseeking give up comfortable beliefs—accepting that favorable outcomes may result from luck rather than skill, and that unfavorable outcomes may reflect their own control. This trade-off must be freely chosen to be productive and sustainable.

## Key facts

- Like the matrix built to be a more comfortable version of the world, our brains have evolved to make our version more comfortable: beliefs are nearly always correct; favorable outcomes are the result of skill; unfavorable outcomes are beyond control; and we compare favorably with peers. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1130-L1140]
- Living in the matrix is comfortable, as is the natural way we process information to protect our self-image. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1140-L1145]
- Choosing to exit the matrix means asserting that striving for a more objective representation of the world, even if uncomfortable at times, will make us happier and more successful in the long run. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1145-L1150]
- Unlike David Letterman's approach, Morpheus didn't force people out of the matrix against their will—he asked Neo to make the choice freely. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1155-L1160]
