# Gene Editing

## Summary

Gene editing technology allows precise modification of DNA molecules, with potential benefits including eliminating inherited diseases. Hawkins argues it could also enable human survival on Mars by modifying genomes for low-gravity environments, or reduce aggressive behavior to create more cooperative societies. He presents this as a choice between guided evolution and leaving our future to unguided natural selection.

## Key facts

- Gene editing technology can eliminate inherited diseases but can also design new life-forms or modify children's DNA for traits like athleticism or attractiveness. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p215]
- If humans cannot live on Mars due to low gravity, gene editing could modify the human genome to allow permanent habitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p215]
- Gene editing could potentially eliminate aggressive behavior and make people more altruistic, which might be desirable for space colonies or any situation where careless violence could kill entire communities. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p216]
- Hawkins argues that opposing gene editing on principle chooses a future in the best interests of existing genes or viral false beliefs rather than human survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p217]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hawkins frames gene editing as a choice between unguided evolution and using intelligence to take control of humanity's future.
