# Long-Loop Reflex

## Summary

Long-loop reflexes are reflex responses mediated through the motor cortex and other supraspinal structures, as opposed to spinal reflex pathways. They have longer latencies than spinal reflexes and are important for regulating contractions in distal muscles requiring precise voluntary control.

## Key facts

- A brief stretch of a thumb muscle produces two discrete components: the M1 response generated by the monosynaptic connection of muscle spindle afferents to spinal motor neurons, and the M2 response controlled by a pathway that loops through the motor cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p856]
- In patients with Klippel-Feil syndrome the M2 response is evoked bilaterally because neurons in the motor cortex activate motor neurons on both sides of the spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p856]
- Long-loop reflexes via the cortex are of primary importance in regulating contractions in distal muscles, whereas subcortical reflex pathways are largely responsible for regulating proximal muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p857]
