# Experience as Matching Process

## Summary

Rather than building knowledge from scratch, the preconfigured brain matches incoming experience with preexisting neuronal trajectories. Meaning is attached to neuronal patterns through action-based grounding, comparing self-generated actions with sensory feedback via corollary discharge. Novel situations are matched to highest-probability preexisting trajectories, while discrepancies trigger construction of new combinations.

## Key facts

- The preconfigured brain provides a scaffold that allows it to make guesses about consequences of body actions and filter which aspects of the world are worth attending. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p343]
- Only through actions can neurons relate their responses to sensory inputs to something else supported by corollary discharge mechanism, allowing sensory signals to acquire meaning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p345]
- The brain always instantly compares relationships rather than identifying explicit features; there is no such thing as unknown for the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p367]
- Every new situation has elements of familiarity that can activate one of the pre-existing neuronal trajectories, so familiarity and novelty need each other to exist. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p367]

## Inferences

- Inference: A novel corridor in a maze can be navigated by concatenating neuronal trajectories of two already-explored corridors, illustrating how new combinations are built from existing trajectories.
- Inference: This framework predicts that sequential neuronal dynamics should exist before experience, fitting observations that firing rates and synaptic strengths are preserved across brain states.
