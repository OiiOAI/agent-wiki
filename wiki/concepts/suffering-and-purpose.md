# Suffering and Purpose

## Summary

Frankl argues that suffering is an ineradicable part of life, and that finding meaning in suffering is essential to human existence. He contends that unavoidable suffering can be transformed into achievement when approached with dignity, and that the way one accepts fate and suffering determines whether one is worthy of one's suffering.

## Key facts

- Frankl wrote that 'suffering is an ineradicable part of life, even as fate and death. Without suffering and death human life cannot be complete'. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p56]
- Frankl stated that the way in which a man accepts his fate and all the suffering it entails gives him ample opportunity to add a deeper meaning to his life. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p57]
- Frankl wrote of a young dying woman who said to a chestnut tree, 'I am here—I am here—I am life, eternal life'. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p58]
- Frankl quoted Rilke: 'Wie viel ist aufzuleiden!' (How much suffering there is to get through!). [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p64]
- Frankl argued that finding meaning in suffering allowed prisoners to refuse to minimize the camp's tortures by ignoring them or entertaining artificial optimism. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p64]
- Frankl observed that a man who becomes conscious of responsibility toward another person will never be able to throw away his life. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p65]
