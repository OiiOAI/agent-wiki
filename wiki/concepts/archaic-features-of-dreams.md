# Archaic Features of Dreams

## Summary

Dreams exhibit archaic features because the dream-work, under the influence of dream censorship, transposes latent thoughts into a mode of expression that harks back to superseded states of intellectual development—picture language, symbolic connections, and conditions existing before thought language developed.

## Key facts

- The dream-work translates thoughts into a primitive form of expression similar to picture language and symbolic connections. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5003]
- Symbolic connections which the individual has never acquired by learning may be regarded as phylogenetic heritage from human prehistory. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5003]
- The regression of the dream-work is not only formal but material, reviving characteristics of primitive mental life including old dominance of the ego and initial impulses of sexual life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5014]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud distinguishes between individual prehistory (childhood) and phylogenetic prehistory as two kinds of archaic features to which dream life leads back.
- Inference: The unconscious is recognized as a particular realm with its own wishful impulses, mode of expression, and mechanisms not in force elsewhere.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether symbolic connections derive entirely from phylogenetic heritage or also from individual experience cannot be definitively separated in this discussion.
