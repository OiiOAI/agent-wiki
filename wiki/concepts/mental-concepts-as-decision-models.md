# Mental Concepts as Decision-Models

## Summary

Mental concepts are described as decision-models used to improve capacity for independent action. They represent observed reality and must be internally consistent and match-up with reality. The process involves forming concepts of observed reality, changing these concepts as reality changes, and using them to determine the precise nature of actions needed to be compatible with the basic goal of survival.

## Key facts

- Mental concepts are used as decision-models for improving capacity for independent action. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p444]
- To make timely decisions, one must form mental concepts of observed reality and be able to change these concepts as reality appears to change. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p445]
- The emerging pattern of ideas and interactions must be internally consistent and match-up with reality. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p447]
- To check internal consistency, one attempts to trace back to original constituents used in the creative induction—if directions cannot be reversed, ideas do not go together without contradiction. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p447]
- When a concept becomes a coherent pattern with little or no appeal to alternative ideas, effort turns inward toward fine-tuning ideas to improve generality and precision of match with reality. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p447]
