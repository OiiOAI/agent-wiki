# Psychological Stability of Conceptions of Justice

## Summary

A conception of justice is stable when the public recognition of its realization by the social system tends to bring about the corresponding sense of justice. Rawls argues that the two principles of justice are more stable than the principle of utility because when they are satisfied, everyone benefits and all acquire inclinations to uphold the scheme. By contrast, the principle of utility may demand that some sacrifice advantages for others, requiring difficult identification with broader interests.

## Key facts

- A conception of justice is stable when the public recognition of its realization by the social system tends to bring about the corresponding sense of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p169]
- When the two principles are satisfied, each person's liberties are secured and there is a sense defined by the difference principle in which everyone is benefited by social cooperation, so all acquire inclinations to uphold the scheme. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p169]
- The principle of utility threatens instability unless sympathy and benevolence can be widely and intensely cultivated, since it asks citizens to accept lower prospects over the whole course of life for the sake of others. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p170]
