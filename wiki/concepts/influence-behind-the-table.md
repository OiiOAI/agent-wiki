# Influence Behind the Table

## Summary

Level II or 'behind the table' players are parties not directly involved in negotiations but who can help implement agreements they like or block ones they dislike. Successful negotiation requires identifying and influencing these hidden decision makers through questions about team impact and buy-in.

## Key facts

- There is always a team on the other side, and if you are not influencing those behind the table, you are vulnerable. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p142]
- A deal was lost when the division head killed a training deal that the CEO and HR head had fully supported, demonstrating the danger of ignoring behind-the-table players. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p151]
- Questions like 'How does this affect the rest of your team?' and 'How on board are the people not on this call?' can identify behind-the-table influencers. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p151]
- The Dos Palmas failure partially stemmed from not accounting for internal Philippine military coordination problems. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p127]
