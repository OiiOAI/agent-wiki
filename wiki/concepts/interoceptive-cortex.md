# Interoceptive cortex

## Summary

Interoceptive cortex, particularly the anterior insular (AI) cortex, plays a crucial role in processing internal bodily states and is central to feeling states and empathy. Based on anatomical observations by Bud Craig, the insula maps the body's internal state through thalamic nuclei, sensorimotor cortices, and posterior dorsal insula, with modality-specific sensory representations re-represented in the anterior insula and then transferred via callosal pathways to the right AI for subjective feeling awareness.

## Key facts

- The insular cortex processes a variety of information about internal bodily states including pain, taste, hunger, thirst, and arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p249]
- Bud Craig developed a detailed anatomical model suggesting that the body's internal state is first mapped by afferents to thalamic nuclei, sensorimotor cortices, and posterior dorsal insula. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p249]
- In humans, the modality-specific sensory representation of the body's physiological condition in the posterior insula is re-represented in the anterior insula on the same side, then transferred via callosal pathway to the right AI for subjective feelings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p249]
- Critchley's research with subjects with pure autonomic failure (PAF) showed enhanced activity in right AI during autonomic arousal, suggesting sensitivity to autonomic feedback absent in PAF individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p249]
- Critchley et al. (2004) demonstrated that activity and size of right AI were positively associated with the degree to which participants were aware of their own heartbeat. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p249]
- Singer et al. (2004) proposed that cortical re-representations in AI of bodily states have a dual function: forming subjective representations of feelings and serving as the visceral correlate of prospective empathic simulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p249]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dual-function model of AI suggests it serves both self-awareness and other-understanding through shared visceral representations.
- Inference: The involvement of AI in both interoception and empathy suggests a common neural substrate for understanding one's own and others' feelings.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the right AI exclusively processes subjective feelings or whether both hemispheres contribute to different aspects of interoceptive awareness requires further investigation.
