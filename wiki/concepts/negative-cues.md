# Negative Cues

## Summary

Negative cues are key events that fail to occur in a situation. Unlike novices, who cannot recognize their significance because they lack experience with typical scenarios, experts readily detect these absences. Experience enables the formation of expectancies; without expectancies, one cannot notice when they are violated. The concept is illustrated through examples including a research team calmly handling proposals (where a novice missed the absence of the usual frenzy) and Sherlock Holmes solving a case by noting that a dog did not bark.

## Key facts

- Novices struggle to form expectancies, making them unable to recognize when key events do not occur. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Negative cues are described as 'missing events'—key events that fail to happen. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experts notice missing events immediately because they have formed expectancies based on experience. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- A new researcher did not notice the absence of frenzy when ten proposals went out smoothly, because she lacked the prior experience of a chaotic proposal period. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- In Sherlock Holmes' 'Silver Blaze,' the crucial clue was that a dog did not bark when a murderer passed by, indicating the dog recognized the intruder. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
