# Hormone

## Summary

Hormones are chemical messengers released from secretory cells (including neurons) in glands that enter the bloodstream and influence cells throughout the body with receptors for them. Unlike neurotransmitter signaling (milliseconds), hormonal effects emerge over hours to days.

## Key facts

- A hormone is a chemical messenger released from secretory cells (including neurons) that enters the bloodstream and can affect any cells with appropriate receptors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p724]
- While neurotransmitter signaling occurs in milliseconds, hormonal effects emerge over hours to days and can persist much longer. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p724]
- Most hormones are part of a neuroendocrine axis: hypothalamic hormone → pituitary hormone → peripheral gland hormone (e.g., CRH → ACTH → cortisol). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p725]
- Hormones can change protein activity, turn genes on or off, alter metabolism, cause cell growth or atrophy, division or cell death. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p725]
