# System Archetype

## Summary

System archetypes are common structures that produce characteristic behaviors across many different types of systems. They include patterns like tragedy of the commons, eroding goals, policy resistance, and addiction. These archetypes, responsible for some of the most intransigent problems, can be transformed with systems understanding to produce more desirable behaviors.

## Key facts

- Systems thinkers call common structures that produce characteristic behaviors 'archetypes,' which were originally called 'system traps.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p22]
- The limits-to-growth archetype occurs when a reinforcing loop driving growth eventually encounters balancing loops that constrain that growth in a finite environment. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p81]
- In physical, exponentially growing systems, there must be at least one reinforcing loop driving growth and at least one balancing loop constraining growth, because no physical system can grow forever in a finite environment. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p81]
- Nonrenewable resources are stock-limited—the faster the extraction rate, the shorter the lifetime. Renewable resources are flow-limited—they can support extraction indefinitely only at a finite flow rate equal to their regeneration rate. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p94]
