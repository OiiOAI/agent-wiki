# Motivation

## Summary

Motivation is an emotional state that drives action but is unreliable because it fluctuates based on feelings, health, energy levels, and external factors. The book argues that motivation should not be the primary strategy for habit formation because habits require consistent repetition, which motivation cannot guarantee. However, motivation is valuable as a bonus when it naturally arises after taking action.

## Key facts

- Motivation is described as unreliable because it's based on feelings, which are fluid and unpredictable due to events, blood sugar, depression, chemical fluctuations, hormones, health, and external stimuli. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p41]
- The author states that if getting motivated is your strategy, you can't build habits, because habits require consistent repetition. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p41]
- Motivation is unreliable because you must be most motivated to exercise more than you want chips and TV, and you'll only succeed sometimes. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p42]
- The 'law of decreasing enthusiasm' describes how initial excitement about a behavior decreases as it becomes habitual, making motivation an unreliable long-term strategy. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p44]
- Jeremey Dean's book 'Making Habits, Breaking Habits' notes that a habit 'doesn't just fly under the radar cognitively; it also does so emotionally' and performing a habit is 'curiously emotionless.'. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p44]
- Motivation becomes more reliable when you use willpower first; motivation comes fast when you take action first. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p46]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book recommends treating motivation as a bonus that may arise during or after action, rather than a prerequisite for starting.
