# Lollapalooza Tendency

## Summary

The tendency for multiple psychological tendencies acting in confluence to produce extreme effects—either extremely good or extremely bad outcomes. Munger considers this one of the most important concepts in understanding human behavior, noting that when several biases combine, their combined effect far exceeds the sum of individual tendencies.

## Key facts

- Munger lists Lollapalooza Tendency as Tendency Twenty-Five, the final and concluding psychological tendency in his framework. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p471]
- Munger describes Lollapalooza effects as the tendency to get extreme outcomes from confluence of psychological tendencies. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p471]
- In analyzing Milgram's obedience experiment, Munger notes that about six powerful psychological tendencies acting in confluence brought about the extreme experimental result. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p589]
- Lollapalooza Tendency is the tendency to get extreme consequences from confluences of psychological tendencies acting in favor of a particular outcome. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p596]
- This tendency was not in any coherent fashion in the psychology texts examined by Munger, yet it dominates life. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p597]
- It accounts for the extreme result in the Milgram experiment. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p597]
- It accounts for the extreme success of some cults that bring pressure from many psychological tendencies to bear at the same time on conversion targets. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p597]
- Some minds targeted by cults 'simply snap into zombiedom' under cult pressure, which one cult calls 'snapping.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p597]
- Milgram's experiment involved about six powerful psychological tendencies acting in confluence to bring about his extreme experimental result, yet the psychology professoriate did not immediately recognize this. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p593]
- Some minds targeted by cults simply snap into zombiedom under cult pressure; cults use the term 'snapping' for this conversion phenomenon. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p599]
- Munger suggests that Availability-Misweighing Tendency and Envy/Jealousy Tendency may have contributed to early psychology professors adopting an over-restricted approach to psychology. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p600]

## Inferences

- Inference: Lollapalooza effects explain why certain situations produce catastrophic failures or spectacular successes that seem disproportionate to any single cause—multiple psychological tendencies reinforce each other.
