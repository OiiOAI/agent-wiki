# Neuroactive peptide processing

## Summary

Neuroactive peptides are synthesized as polyprotein precursors in the cell body and undergo proteolytic processing in the secretory pathway. Multiple peptides are often encoded by a single mRNA and cleaved from one polyprotein precursor. Processing occurs at basic amino acid residues (lysine and arginine) by serine proteases, and different neurons processing the same polyprotein can produce different peptide products.

## Key facts

- Neuroactive peptides are derived from secretory proteins synthesized on rough endoplasmic reticulum, processed in the Golgi apparatus, and packaged in large dense-core vesicles transported to axon terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p349]
- Polyproteins can serve as a mechanism for amplification by providing more than one copy of the same peptide from one precursor, and for diversity by producing several distinct peptides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p350]
- Processing of polyproteins to neuroactive peptides occurs by limited proteolytic cleavage catalyzed by proteases at basic residues (lysine and arginine) within the substrate protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p350]
- Example: POMC (proopiomelanocortin) is processed differently in different lobes of the pituitary gland, yielding α-MSH and γ-MSH in the intermediate lobe but not the anterior lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p350]
