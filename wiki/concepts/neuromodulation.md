# Neuromodulation

## Summary

Neuromodulation refers to neurons that modulate the signaling of other neurons, typically by forming synapses onto axon terminals rather than dendritic spines. These modulatory neurons can inhibit or facilitate the release of neurotransmitter from the target neuron's terminals.

## Key facts

- In neuromodulation, a neuron sends axon terminals to synapse onto the axon terminal of another neuron, rather than its dendritic spine. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p711]
- A neuromodulatory neuron releasing GABA onto a target neuron's axon terminal can inhibit action potentials before they reach the terminal and trigger neurotransmitter release. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p711]
- Neuron A in an axoaxonic synapse releases inhibitory neurotransmitter that prevents the action potential in neuron B from reaching its terminal. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p711]
