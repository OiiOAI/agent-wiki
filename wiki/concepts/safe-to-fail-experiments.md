# Safe-to-Fail Experiments

## Summary

Safe-to-fail experiments are a core method in the Complex domain of the Cynefin Framework. Rather than implementing large-scale change programs, multiple small experiments are run in parallel, monitored, and amplified if successful or dampened if unsuccessful. This approach accounts for the Hawthorne Effect and allows systems to evolve naturally rather than through imposed change.

## Key facts

- Running multiple safe-to-fail experiments in parallel is recommended to account for the Hawthorne Effect. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p286]
- Not focusing on one sole change program allows several small experiments to play out, and the system evolves towards a natural disposition rather than one imposed from an idealized standpoint. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p286]
- If a safe-to-fail experiment goes well, it should be amplified; if it takes an undesirable turn, it should be dampened. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p287]
- Safe-to-fail experiments are part of the probe-sense-respond approach in the Complex domain. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p287]
