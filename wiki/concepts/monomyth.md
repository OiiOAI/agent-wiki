# The Monomyth

## Summary

The monomyth is Campbell's term for the common narrative structure underlying hero myths from cultures worldwide. A hero ventures from common day into a region of supernatural wonder, encounters fabulous forces, wins a decisive victory, and returns bearing gifts. The three-part structure of separation, initiation, and return appears in myths from Prometheus to Buddha to modern narratives.

## Key facts

- A hero ventures forth from the world of common day into a region of supernatural wonder: fabulous forces are there encountered and a decisive victory is won: the hero comes back from this mysterious adventure with the power to bestow boons on his fellow man. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p29]
- The standard path of the mythological adventure of the hero is a magnification of the formula represented in the rites of passage: separation—initiation—return. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p29]
- Prometheus, Jason, Aeneas, Buddha, and Moses all follow variations of the monomyth pattern. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p29-32]
- Whether the hero be ridiculous or sublime, Greek or barbarian, gentile or Jew, his journey varies little in essential plan. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p34]
- Dream is the personalized myth, myth the depersonalized dream; both myth and dream are symbolic in the same general way of the dynamics of the psyche. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p21]
