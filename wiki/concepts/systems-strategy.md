# Systems Strategy

## Summary

In 'The E-Myth Revisited,' Gerber defines Systems Strategy as the integration of three kinds of systems—Hard Systems, Soft Systems, and Information Systems—to create a business that works without constant owner involvement. The strategy is presented as essential for bridging the gap between working in the business and working on it.

## Key facts

- A system is defined as 'a set of things, actions, ideas, and information that interact with each other, and in so doing, alter other systems.'. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p240]
- Systems Strategy encompasses the Innovation, Quantification, and Orchestration of Hard, Soft, and Information Systems in the business. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p241]
- The purpose of a system is to free the owner to do the things they want to do rather than being trapped in tactical work. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p244]
- Conflict between desired outcomes and existing conditions creates the essential conditions for innovation and the birth of a system. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p243]
