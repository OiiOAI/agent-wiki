# Sector Complexity in Air Traffic Control

## Summary

Sector complexity in ATC is measured through facility estimates using weighted factors (departures, arrivals, radar vectored arrivals, en route aircraft, emergencies, special flights, coordinations) and a 16-factor survey covering traffic characteristics, coordination requirements, weather effects, and operational conditions. Research examines how sector complexity relates to operational errors and controller situation awareness.

## Key facts

- A facility average complexity estimate is calculated using 7 weighted factors: departures, arrivals, radar vectored arrivals, en route aircraft requiring control actions, en route aircraft not requiring actions, emergencies, and special flights. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p112]
- The 16-factor survey includes: climbing/descending traffic, aircraft mix, intersecting flight paths, multiple functions, required procedures, military flights, coordination required, airline hubbing effects, weather factors, complex routings, restricted areas, sector airspace size, longitudinal sequencing, radio/radar coverage, frequency congestion, and average traffic volume. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p112]
- Both global complexity measures significantly differed between zero-error sectors and high error sectors (4+ errors), with F(2,45) = 5.45, p < .007 for facility estimates and F(2,45) = 3.12, p < .05 for the 16 CF total. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p114]
- Frequency congestion and weather were the two individual complexity factors that significantly differed across error categories (p < .004 and p < .03 respectively). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p114]
- There was a significant correlation between total OEs in a sector and number of OEs without awareness: r = 0.84, p < .001. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p114]

## Inferences

- Inference: Controllers in high-error sectors demonstrate less awareness of developing errors, suggesting that sector complexity may lead to reduced situation awareness and higher error incidence.
