# Autonomy Support in Health Care

## Summary

Research applying Self-Determination Theory to health behavior change shows that autonomy support from health-care providers significantly affects patient outcomes. Patients who perceived their providers as autonomy supportive developed more autonomous reasons for participating in treatment programs, which predicted better adherence to medication, greater weight loss, and maintenance of these changes over time. The approach contrasts with traditional compliance models by emphasizing patient responsibility, choice, and understanding rather than control and scare tactics.

## Key facts

- Patients whose reasons for entering weight-loss or alcohol treatment programs were more autonomous attended more regularly, lost more weight, and maintained losses at two-year follow-up. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p174]
- People with addictions may find it harder to change, but can overcome addictions when they are really ready—when they accept responsibility for their behavior and health. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p174]
- When health-care providers were perceived as more autonomy supportive, patients reported more autonomous reasons for adhering to program guidelines, which predicted maintained outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p179]
- The biopsychosocial approach emphasizes building partnerships between health-care providers and patients, recognizing that social and psychological processes are integral to well-being.
