# Reciprocation Tendency

## Summary

The automatic tendency of humans to reciprocate both favors and disfavors, present also in apes, monkeys, dogs, and social insects. This tendency facilitates group cooperation and commercial trade but can be exploited by 'compliance practitioners.' Cialdini's zoo experiment demonstrated that triggering this tendency through small concessions dramatically increases compliance.

## Key facts

- Cialdini's experiment showed that asking strangers to supervise juvenile delinquents for two years (with 100% rejection) could be tripled to 50% acceptance by first asking a smaller favor (two hours per week) before the zoo request. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p527]
- Munger credits Reciprocation Tendency as a partial cause of the Watergate scandal, where the attorney general's subconscious response to a subordinate's concession led to agreement to the burglary. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p528]
- Sam Walton wouldn't let purchasing agents accept even a hot dog from vendors, recognizing that Reciprocation Tendency operates subconsciously. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p525]
- An automobile salesman giving a customer coffee and a comfortable seat is likely to trick the customer into parting with an extra $500 through Reciprocation Tendency. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p524]

## Inferences

- Inference: Reciprocation Tendency explains why commercial trade was such a fundamental driver of civilization—innate psychology enabled trust between strangers that formal legal systems alone could not.
