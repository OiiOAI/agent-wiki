# Surface Layout Perception

## Summary

Surface layout perception is the direct perception of the arrangement of surfaces relative to the ground and to one another. Gibson proposed that there is no perception of 'space' without perception of a continuous background surface—the ground. The theory asserts that depth perception as traditionally conceived is a myth; what is perceived is the layout of surfaces including their slants, depths, and arrangements.

## Key facts

- The ground theory asserts that the world consists of a basic surface with adjoining surfaces, not of bodies in empty air; even the space of an airplane pilot is determined by the ground and horizon. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p172]
- The optical information for layout consists of invariants such as the horizon ratio (objects of the same height are bisected by the horizon in the same ratio) and equal amounts of texture for equal amounts of terrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p186]
- The 'visual cliff' experiments showed that terrestrial animals perceive the edge of a surface of support not as abstract depth but as a negative affordance—a 'falling-off place' dangerous for locomotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p181]
- The terrestrial horizon is an invariant feature of all ambient arrays, stationary even when all other structure changes; it is perpendicular to gravity and at 'eye level' relative to all terrestrial objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p187]
- Experiments on bisecting stretches of ground showed that observers could accurately match distances without attending to visual angles—the invariant ratio of texture elements was picked up unawares. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p185]

## Inferences

- Inference: Size constancy does not 'break down' with distance because size is not perceived via retinal image size alone but via invariant ratios that remain constant regardless of distance.
- Inference: The distinction between 2D and 3D vision is a myth—both are cases of perceiving layout, with front-facing layout (wall) and slanting layout (tunnel) being unitary features.
