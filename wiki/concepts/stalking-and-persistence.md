# Stalking and Persistence

## Summary

Unwanted persistent pursuit that escalates through predictable stages. The book documents cases including Edward Taylor's stalking of radio personality Jim Hicklin (which ended in murder) and various cases of persistent letter writers. Key concepts include the 'engage and enrage' principle—responding to pursuers fuels escalation—and the importance of context over content in evaluating threats.

## Key facts

- Edward Taylor was visited by private detectives and then police, escalating his pursuit of Jim Hicklin; he eventually purchased a gun and murdered Hicklin and his wife on a cruise ship. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p96]
- Anne Murray's stalker violated court orders over 200 times in his first months of freedom despite six years in prison. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p91]
- The 'engage and enrage' principle: responding to pursuers fuels escalation; no contact is often the most effective management strategy. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p88]
- Direct threats to public figures are less predictive of violence than inappropriate communications like love-sickness, exaggerated adoration, or themes of rejection. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p82]
- Anonymous threats to public figures portend less danger than accredited threats; those who sign their names are more likely to pursue an encounter. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p83]
