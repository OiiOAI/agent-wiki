# Gremlins

## Summary

Gremlins are destructive, unhelpful beliefs or automatic programmes stored in the Computer that are removable. They typically develop after age eight through experience, education, or interpretation of events, and can be replaced with helpful Autopilots through conscious effort.

## Key facts

- Gremlins are unhelpful or destructive beliefs and automatic programmes that are removable from the Computer. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p58]
- Gremlins typically occur after age eight and are soft-wired, meaning they can be identified and removed. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p58]
- A common Gremlin is the unrealistic expectation that 'I should always be on time,' which creates stress when late. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p59]
- Another common Gremlin is the belief that 'I must always win to prove myself,' which makes competitive activities intense and unpleasant. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p59]
- Gremlins often contain rigid words like 'must' and 'should,' which evoke judgment and guilt rather than possibility. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p72]
- The word 'SHOULD' is identified as a giant destructive Gremlin that can be replaced by 'COULD' for healthier thinking. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p72]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gremlins are often planted by the Chimp through emotional interpretation of experiences, but the Human can also inadvertently create them through logical misinterpretation.
