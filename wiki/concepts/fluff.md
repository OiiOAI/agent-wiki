# Fluff

## Summary

Fluff is a hallmark of bad strategy—superficial restatement of the obvious combined with a generous sprinkling of buzzwords that masquerades as expertise, thought, and analysis. It uses 'Sunday words' (inflated and unnecessarily abstruse) to create the illusion of high-level thinking.

## Key facts

- A hallmark of true expertise and insight is making a complex subject understandable; a hallmark of mediocrity and bad strategy is unnecessary complexity masking an absence of substance. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p26]
- An example of fluff: 'Our fundamental strategy is one of customer-centric intermediation'—where 'intermediation' simply means accepting deposits and lending them (being a bank) and 'customer-centric' has no operational meaning. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p25]
- Fluff originated in the academic world and, more recently, in the information technology industry. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p25]
