# The Meaning of Human Freedom

## Summary

Freedom as defined by Deci encompasses multiple levels: political freedom (freedom from external coercion), interpersonal freedom (autonomy support versus control in social contexts), and psychological freedom (being governed by a true self rather than by introjects and rigid inner structures).

## Key facts

- Human freedom means to be truly autonomous, to act in a way that is not bounded by introjects, rigid inner structures, paralyzing self-criticisms, or the push to defy limiting forces. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p203]
- Being free means to feel volitional and to be governed in one's actions by a true self. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p203]
- Social contexts play a role in creating internal pressures such as introjected values and regulations with which people limit their own freedom. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p204]
- The American system offers substantial freedom to pursue desired outcomes, but paradoxically people's freedom often ends up being limited by the pursuit of these outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p204]
- Freedom is a characteristic of an individual's psychological functioning at any given moment and must be practiced on a moment-to-moment basis. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p205]
- Being free does not mean doing your own thing at the expense of others; rather, it involves concern for others and respect for the environment as manifestations of human connectedness. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: Elena Bonner's choice to return to internal exile in the Soviet Union rather than remain in Western freedom demonstrates that psychological freedom can exist within political unfreedom, and vice versa.
