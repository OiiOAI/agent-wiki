# Body ownership

## Summary

Body ownership is the sense that one's body belongs to oneself. Research using the rubber hand illusion and full body illusions demonstrates that body ownership is a perceptual inference, a controlled hallucination that can be manipulated experimentally.

## Key facts

- The rubber hand illusion shows that synchronous stroking of a fake hand and real hand leads to the rubber hand being experienced as part of one's body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p152]
- Body ownership in the rubber hand illusion occurs because simultaneous visual and tactile signals provide sensory evidence that the rubber hand is part of the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p152]
- Full body illusions show that the subjective ownership of the entire body and the location of the first-person perspective can be manipulated through virtual reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p153]
- When people are shown their own body from behind via head-mounted display and see it stroked synchronously with actual touch, they feel the virtual body is their own. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p153]
- Body ownership illusions are weaker than classic visual illusions — people do not become fully convinced the fake body is theirs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p157]

## Inferences

- Inference: The malleability of body ownership through experimental manipulation suggests it is a perceptual construction, not a direct perception of bodily reality.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The role of hypnotic suggestibility in body ownership illusions complicates interpretation of experimental results.
