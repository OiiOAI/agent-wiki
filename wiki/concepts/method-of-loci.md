# Method of Loci

## Summary

The method of loci is a well-known memory technique where items to be remembered are placed at different locations in a familiar mental map (like a house). To recall them, one mentally 'walks through' the house, bringing each item to mind in sequence. This technique provides evidence that knowledge is stored in reference frames and that recall is a form of movement through those reference frames.

## Key facts

- The method of loci involves placing items to remember at locations in a familiar house, then mentally walking through to recall them. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p81]
- The success of the method demonstrates that recalling information is easier when items are assigned to locations in a familiar reference frame. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p81]
- The method shows that recall is achieved by mental movement through a previously learned reference frame. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p81]
- This supports the premise that all knowledge is stored in reference frames and that thinking is a form of movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p81]
- Simonides invented the method of loci after the roof collapse at a banquet, when he identified crushed guests by remembering where each had been seated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p70]
- The method works by visualizing a familiar place and mentally placing objects to remember at specific locations within it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p70]
- Effective use requires vivid, eye-catching visual images, ideally with emotional content or unusual features. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p82]
- Peter of Ravenna reported using the method to memorize canon law, 200 speeches by Cicero, 300 philosophical sayings, and 20,000 legal points. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p87]
- The method of loci originates from a legend about Simonides of Ceos, who was called out of a banquet just before the roof collapsed, allowing him to identify the dead by remembering where each guest had been seated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]
- A 1970s study showed that subjects using the method of loci remembered 72 percent of words on average, compared to only 28 percent for those using other memorization methods. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]
- An earlier study demonstrated that the method of loci enabled subjects to remember more than 95 percent of a list of 40 to 50 words after seeing them only once. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]
- The technique requires forming vivid and specific mental images of both the places used and the items to be remembered. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]

## Inferences

- Inference: The method of loci works because it exploits the brain's natural navigation systems, suggesting these systems evolved for spatial memory but were co-opted for general knowledge storage.
- Inference: The method of loci effectiveness likely stems from the brain's excellent spatial memory and the cognitive effort required to create vivid associations, which engages deeper processing.
