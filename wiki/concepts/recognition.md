# Recognition (Thymos)

## Summary

Recognition, or thymos, refers to the fundamental human desire to have one's dignity recognized and evaluated at its proper worth by other human beings. Fukuyama argues this drive is so deep and fundamental that it is one of the chief motors of the entire human historical process. In modern times, the struggle for recognition has shifted from the military arena to the economic realm, where it has the socially beneficial effect of creating wealth. Beyond subsistence levels, economic activity is frequently undertaken for the sake of recognition rather than merely satisfying material needs.

## Key facts

- Every human being seeks to have his or her dignity recognized at its proper worth by other human beings. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p16]
- The desire for recognition is one of the chief motors of the entire human historical process. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p16]
- The struggle for recognition has shifted from the military arena to the economic realm, creating wealth rather than destroying it. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p16]
- Work and money are more important as sources of identity, status, and dignity than as means of satisfying material needs. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p16]
