# Empowerment Promise

## Summary

An empowerment promise is a foundational instructional technique where the instructor tells students what they will be able to do after listening to instruction. The promise creates engagement and focus, and the instructor should conclude by noting delivery on that promise.

## Key facts

- When instructing, you should tell students what they will be able to do after they listen or read. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p51]
- The beginning and end of a class are the most important parts. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p174]
- Once you have delivered on a promise, you should say so explicitly because students may not infer it. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p54]
- Stories are like flight simulators for real life, and instructors should start chapters with stories. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p52]
- The book uses What you need to know sections to note delivery on empowerment promises. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p54]
- The empowerment promise must be delivered within the first five minutes of a lecture. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p187]
- Empowerment can come from solving problems, seeing problems in a new light, learning to make money, or gaining conversation topics. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p187]
- An outline on a blackboard tells the audience that you are about to start and serves as a vehicle for the empowerment promise. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p187]
- A system demonstration can express an empowerment promise by showing something impressive that will be understood by the audience. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p188]
