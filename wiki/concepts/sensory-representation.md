# Sensory Representation

## Summary

Sensory representation refers to how the brain encodes and processes sensory information from the environment. Kandel describes the groundbreaking work of Mountcastle (somatosensory cortex columns), Hubel and Wiesel (visual cortex orientation selectivity), and others showing that sensory systems deconstruct information through specialized pathways before reconstructing it into coherent perceptions. The binding problem addresses how these separate representations are unified.

## Key facts

- Wade Marshall discovered in 1936 that the somatosensory cortex of the cat contains a map of the body surface; each part is represented in proportion to its importance in sensory perception, not its size. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p309]
- Vernon Mountcastle found that individual neurons in the somatosensory cortex respond to signals from only a limited area of the skin called the receptive field and that tactile sensation is organized into columns dedicated to specific submodalities. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p310]
- Hubel and Wiesel discovered that once visual signals reach the cortex, cells respond not to small spots of light but to linear contours and edges, with each cell responding to a specific orientation. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p311]
- The primary visual cortex gives rise to two parallel pathways: the 'what' pathway carrying information about form and the 'where' pathway carrying information about movement and location. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p313]
- Mountcastle stated: 'Afferent nerve fibers are not high-fidelity recorders... The central neuron is a story-teller with regard to the nerve fibers, and it is never completely trustworthy, allowing distortions of quality and measure... Sensation is an abstraction, not a replication, of the real world.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p312]
