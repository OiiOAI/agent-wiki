# The Focusing Question

## Summary

The Focusing Question is the core practical tool of The ONE Thing methodology: 'What is the ONE Thing I can do such that by doing it everything else will be easier or unnecessary?' It combines big-picture strategic thinking with small-focus tactical action, driving users to identify the single most leveraged activity in any situation.

## Key facts

- The Focusing Question is: 'What is the ONE Thing I can do, such that by doing it, everything else will be easier or unnecessary?'. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p88]
- The question has three parts: sparks focused action, provides a criterion ('such that'), and identifies ultimate leverage ('everything else will be easier or unnecessary'). [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p88]
- Andrew Carnegie advised concentrating energy, thought, and capital exclusively on one business and leading in it. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p83]
- Research shows asking questions improves learning and performance by as much as 150 percent. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p85]
- The Focusing Question is: What is the ONE Thing I can do right now such that by doing it everything else will be easier or unnecessary?. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p110]
- The question combines big-picture thinking with small-focus action. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p106]
- The Focusing Question should be applied across all time horizons from someday to right now. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p117]
- Forming a new habit takes on average 66 days according to research cited in the book. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p178]
- Andrew Carnegie reportedly had the Focusing Question inscribed above his office door. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p102]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Focusing Question can be applied at multiple scales: long-term life purpose ('Big-Picture Question') and immediate daily tasks ('Small-Focus Question').
