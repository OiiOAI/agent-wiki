# Hemineglect

## Summary

Hemineglect is a neurological syndrome, typically caused by stroke damage to the right parietal lobe, in which patients behave as if the left side of their world does not exist. Patients may eat from only one side of a plate, shave one side of their face, or deny ownership of a limb on the affected side.

## Key facts

- Hemineglect occurs with lesions in the right parietal lobe, causing patients to neglect the left side of space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p295]
- Patients with hemineglect often do not recognize they have a problem, a condition called anosognosia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p295]
- Information presented to the neglected field can be processed nonconsciously even when patients verbally deny seeing it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p296]
- The right hemisphere attends to the entire visual field while the left hemisphere attends only to the right field, explaining why right parietal damage causes left neglect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p301]
- Hemineglect is typically caused by vascular disorders (strokes) affecting the right side of the brain, in an area corresponding broadly to area 7a in the macaque brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p178]
- In severe cases, patients may deny that their own left limbs belong to them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p178]
- Patients with hemineglect may fail to pick up food from the left side of their plate or only draw the right side of a clock or face. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p178]
- Hemineglect can affect visual imagination, as demonstrated by patients who when asked to imagine being at one end of Milan's main square described mainly buildings on their right side, but when asked to imagine being at the opposite end described mainly the other side. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hemineglect demonstrates that consciousness and attention are separate systems, and that the autobiographical self is derived from conscious musings.
- Inference: Hemineglect affecting both perception and imagination suggests the damage disrupts a general attentional system rather than just sensory processing.
