# Chronic Pain

## Summary

Chronic pain is pain that persists long after the normal healing period, affecting an estimated 10 percent of the world's population. Predictive processing theory explains chronic pain as arising from precision-weighted interactions between predictions and current bodily signals, rather than as a simple response to tissue damage. The theory provides a framework for understanding how expectations and attention can create, amplify, or entirely generate pain experiences.

## Key facts

- It is estimated that up to 10 percent of the world's population experiences chronic pain, and in the UK alone between one third and one half of the population experiences chronic pain according to a 2016 meta-analysis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p43]
- In 2016 a third category of pain called 'nociplastic pain' was added, defined as pain arising from abnormal processing of pain signals without clear evidence of tissue damage or recognized systemic pathology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p44]
- Professor Irene Tracey and colleagues at Oxford showed that expectations of pain activated key neural circuits relating to the experience of painfulness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p44]
- Simple verbal manipulations such as a dentist describing the feeling as a 'gentle tickle' alter the way patients experience the effects of the drill, though effects depend not just on words but also on the patient's level of confidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p45]
- On the first Global Day Against Pain in 2004, it was declared that 'chronic and recurrent pain is a specific health care problem, a disease in its own right.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p62]

## Inferences
