# Problem Resolution

## Summary

Problem resolution involves applying second-order change techniques. The authors discovered that spontaneous change often provides insights into effective intervention—change frequently occurs through minor, fortuitous events, acts of desperation, or seemingly illogical actions that actually modify the attempted solution itself rather than the problem directly. The four principles of second-order change are: (a) apply change to the solution rather than the problem; (b) accept that effective change often appears weird and uncommonsensical; (c) focus on what is happening now rather than why it happened; (d) reframe the situation to lift it out of paradox.
