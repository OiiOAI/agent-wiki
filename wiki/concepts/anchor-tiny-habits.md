# Anchor (Tiny Habits)

## Summary

An Anchor (technically called an Action Prompt) is a behavior you already do reliably that serves as a reminder to perform a new Tiny Habit. The term 'anchor' reflects how new habits are attached to something solid and reliable in your existing routine. Anchors work by leveraging behavior sequencing—identifying what comes 'after' an existing habit so the new habit naturally follows.

## Key facts

- An Anchor is an existing routine that reminds you to do a new Tiny Behavior. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p129]
- The key insight is finding what behavior a new habit should come 'after'—behavior sequencing. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p128]
- Example recipe: 'After I flush the toilet, I will do two push-ups.'. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p129]
- Morning routines are typically the most predictable and therefore the most fertile soil for Anchors. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p130]
- The Trailing Edge is the last action in a behavior sequence, which can serve as a more precise Anchor (e.g., 'After I turn off the water' rather than 'After I put my breakfast dishes in the sink'). [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p136]
- Fuzzy Anchors like 'after I eat breakfast' work better when made specific to the Trailing Edge, such as 'After I start the dishwasher.'. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p137]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since Anchors are behaviors already embedded in daily life, they require no additional time, effort, or external tools to function as prompts.
