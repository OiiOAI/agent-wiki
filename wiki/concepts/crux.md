# Crux

## Summary

The crux is the most important part of a set of challenges that is addressable—having a good chance of being solved by coherent action. Rumelt defines it as the outcome of a three-part strategic skill: judgment about which issues are truly important, judgment about the difficulties of dealing with these issues, and the ability to focus and avoid spreading resources too thinly. The term is borrowed from rock climbing, where the crux is the toughest part of a boulder problem that requires solving the puzzle rather than just applying strength.

## Key facts

- The term crux was adopted from rock climbing, where it denotes the toughest part of a boulder problem that cannot be overcome by strength alone. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p9]
- The crux represents a three-part strategic skill: judgment about which issues are important, judgment about difficulties, and the ability to focus. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p11]
- A crux is both critical and addressable—it can be surmounted with reasonable surety through focused effort. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p15]
- Finding the crux narrows attention to a critical issue where a commitment to action can surmount the most critical obstacles. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p33]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of crux enables strategists to break through complexity by focusing on solvable problems rather than attempting to address all challenges simultaneously.
