# Diet quality and mental health correlation

## Summary

Numerous studies worldwide show that dietary patterns correlate strongly with mental health outcomes. A 'traditional' dietary pattern of vegetables, fruit, meat, fish, and whole grains is associated with lower odds for depression and anxiety disorders, while a Western diet of processed foods, fried foods, refined grains, sugary products, and beer is associated with more mental health symptoms.

## Key facts

- A study of over a thousand Australian women showed that a 'traditional' dietary pattern of vegetables, fruit, meat, fish, and whole grains was associated with lower odds for depression and anxiety disorders. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p105]
- A Western diet of processed or fried foods, refined grains, sugary products, and beer was associated with more mental health symptoms in the same Australian study. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p105]
- Low seafood consumption is associated with a sixty-five-times higher lifetime risk for depression, a fifty times higher risk for postpartum depression, and a thirty times higher risk for bipolar disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p107]
- Over 30 percent of the average adult caloric intake in the United States is from low-nutrient-density foods. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p106]
- More than three-quarters of the energy in products purchased by Americans from 2000 to 2012 came from moderately and highly processed foods. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p106]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation between diet quality and mental health has been replicated across multiple cultures and study designs, suggesting a robust relationship.
