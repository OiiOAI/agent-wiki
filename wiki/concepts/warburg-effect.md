# Warburg Effect

## Summary

The Warburg effect describes how cancer cells consume glucose at up to forty times the rate of healthy tissues but use inefficient anaerobic glycolysis despite having oxygen available. Discovered by Otto Warburg in the 1920s, this allows cancer cells to generate building blocks for rapid cell division rather than maximizing energy production. This metabolic vulnerability is being targeted by PI3K inhibitors and dietary interventions.

## Key facts

- Otto Warburg discovered in the 1920s that cancer cells devour glucose at up to forty times the rate of healthy tissues. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p157]
- Cancer cells use anaerobic glycolysis despite having plenty of oxygen available, producing only 2 ATP per glucose vs. 36 ATP in normal aerobic respiration. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p157]
- The Warburg effect generates many by-product molecules (like lactate) used as building blocks for rapid cell division, making the energy yield the 'by-product'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p158]
- Warburg was awarded the Nobel Prize in 1931 for his discovery of a crucial enzyme in the electron transport chain. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p157]
- Lew Cantley discovered that PI3K enzymes fuel the Warburg effect by speeding up glucose uptake into cells via the insulin/IGF-1 pathway. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p159]
