# Body-brain integration

## Summary

The body and brain form an indissociable organism integrated by mutually targeted biochemical and neural circuits. There are two principal routes of interconnection: sensory and motor peripheral nerves, and the bloodstream carrying chemical signals. The brain receives signals from the body, from itself, and from the environment to achieve suitable accommodations between organism and environment.

## Key facts

- The brain and body are indissociably integrated by mutually targeted biochemical and neural circuits with two principal routes: peripheral nerves and the bloodstream. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p107]
- Nearly every part of the body sends signals to the brain via peripheral nerves, entering at the level of spinal cord or brain stem and carried to somatosensory cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p108]
- The brain acts on the body through autonomic and musculoskeletal nervous systems, and by manufacturing chemical substances released into the bloodstream. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p108]
- From an evolutionary perspective, if there had been no body, there would have been no brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p110]
