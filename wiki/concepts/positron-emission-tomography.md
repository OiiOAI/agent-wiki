# Positron Emission Tomography (PET)

## Summary

PET scanning visualizes dynamic biochemical changes in the living human brain by monitoring patterns of radioactive emissions following injection of positron-emitting molecules. It has revealed brain activity changes during emotional and cognitive processes.

## Key facts

- PET scans employ mathematical triangulation procedures to computationally reconstruct images of brain activity from radioactive emissions, depicting differential isotope densities in brain subregions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p54]
- PET has verified that Parkinson's disease involves deficits of dopamine activity in basal ganglia and that schizophrenics have excessive dopamine receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p54]
- Obsessive-compulsive disorder is characterized by excessive activity in frontal lobe areas, visible in PET scans of affected individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p54]
- PET scanning has shown that people prone to panic attacks exhibit overactivity in right parahippocampal regions, and that feelings of happiness reduce brain activity while sadness increases it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p56]
