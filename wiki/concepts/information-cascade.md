# Information Cascade

## Summary

An information cascade occurs when individuals rationally ignore their own private information and follow the behavior of others, leading groups to fall prey to collective misinformation despite no individual acting irrationally. First formally analyzed by Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer, and Welch, cascades form when public information (others' actions) appears more reliable than private information (one's own judgment), even when that public information is itself based on flawed premises. This provides a rational theory of market bubbles, fads, and herd behavior without requiring irrationality or malfeasance.

## Key facts

- Sushil Bikhchandani, David Hirshleifer, and Ivo Welch demonstrated that rationally behaving agents can fall prey to effectively infinite misinformation through information cascades. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L2945-L2948]
- In information cascades, individuals follow predecessors blindly independent of their own information signals, making their action uninformative to all later decision makers. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L2975-L2978]
- The 2010 Amazon book pricing incident resulted in The Making of a Fly selling for $23,698,655.93 due to two sellers algorithmically setting prices as constant fractions of each other. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L2990-L2993]
- The May 6, 2010 'flash crash' saw several S&P 500 companies briefly priced above $100,000 or below $0.01, wiping out nearly $1 trillion in value. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L2998-L3000]

## Inferences

- Inference: ormation cascades explain how economic bubbles can form and collapse without any individual being at fault—the game itself can produce irrational collective outcomes from rational individual actions.
