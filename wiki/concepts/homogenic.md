# Homogenic (genetic similarity concept)

## Summary

Panksepp introduced the term 'homogenic' to describe genetic similarity on the level of function, implying a common genetic source for intrinsic brain functions across species. This term contrasts with phenogenic (environmentally influenced traits) and helps conceptualize how similar neurodynamic processes may underlie emotions across mammalian species.

## Key facts

- Panksepp coined 'homogenic' to denote genetic similarity on the level of function for intrinsic brain processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p181]
- A similar term 'logogenic' was proposed for innate perceptual sensitivities ingrained by direct genetic influences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p181]

## Inferences

- Inference: These neologisms were created to provide conceptual language for discussing cross-species neurobiological homologies in emotional brain systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: These terms appear to be Panksepp's proposed neologisms and may not have achieved widespread adoption in the field.
