# Transtheoretical Model of Behavior Change

## Summary

Prochaska and DiClemente's model identifies five stages of behavior change: pre-contemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, and maintenance. The model was originally developed to understand addictive behaviors and has been applied to dietary changes. Success requires motivation, readiness, tangible goals, social support, and preparation for setbacks.

## Key facts

- The Transtheoretical Model identifies five steps: pre-contemplation, contemplation, preparation, action, and maintenance. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p243]
- Effective behavior change requires tangible, specific, and realistic goals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p244]
- Support from trusted friends and family helps maintain commitment to intended changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p244]
- Setbacks are expected and should not lead to abandoning goals entirely. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p244]
- For people with depression, getting energy and motivation to change diet may seem insurmountable without initial nutrient supplementation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p244]
