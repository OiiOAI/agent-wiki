# Saccadic control system

## Summary

The saccadic control system is a topographic map circuit in the brain for eye movement control, comprising the lateral intraparietal area (LIP), frontal eye fields (FEF), and superior colliculus (SC). These areas encode RSV signals and project to brainstem circuits that govern actual eye movements through burst neurons. The system serves as the primary model for studying neural choice mechanisms in non-human primates.

## Key facts

- LIP contains a roughly topographic map both of objects in the visual world and the eye movements required to align gaze with those objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- LIP projects to frontal eye fields and superior colliculus, two additional topographic maps broadly similar in function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Frontal eye fields project directly to the superior colliculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Many of these areas are reciprocally connected. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Neurons in LIP, FEF, and SC all show activity related to the probability that a movement will be executed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]

## Inferences

- Inference: The reciprocal connectivity among these areas supports recursive signal propagation through the network while reflecting SV inputs entering at multiple locations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Theories of movement control for oculomotor systems may not fully generalize to skeletomuscular systems, though analogues exist.
