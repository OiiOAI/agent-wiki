# Moderating Role of Experience in Aging

## Summary

Experience significantly moderates age-related cognitive decline through crystallized intelligence, expertise effects, and compensatory mechanisms. Older adults can maintain high performance in domains where extensive practice has automatized operations and established well-developed knowledge structures, potentially overcoming deficits in fluid intelligence.

## Key facts

- Crystallized intelligence (knowledge-based skills) remains stable or shows modest growth throughout much of the adult lifespan, allowing older adults to draw on experience to reduce age differences in performance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p294]
- Expert older adults can outperform novice younger adults and in some situations perform as well as younger experts, as demonstrated in studies of chess, bridge, and medical technology. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p294]
- Experts can perform many domain tasks with little drain on processing resources through accessible knowledge structures, highly automatic operations, and elimination of computational steps. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p294]
- Schema activation and use appears minimally affected by aging, with young and older adults showing similar impacts of schemas on processing. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p300]

## Inferences

- Inference: SA is measured in areas where older adults have considerable expertise, differences between their measured SA and younger adults may be minimal.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Expertise does not always eliminate age effects; limitations may occur when viewing time is restricted or when basic process deficits prevent use of compensatory mechanisms.
