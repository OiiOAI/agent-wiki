# Monogamy

## Summary

Monogamy refers to sexual exclusivity within committed relationships, traditionally viewed as one partner for life but now commonly meaning one partner at a time. Perel explores the tension between cultural insistence on monogamy and the reality of human desire, questioning whether sexual exclusivity is the only valid form of fidelity.

## Key facts

- The Brazilian family therapist Michele Scheinkman states: 'American culture has great tolerance for divorce—where there is a total breakdown of the loyalty bond and painful effects for the whole family—but it is a culture with no tolerance for sexual infidelity.'. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p157]
- Despite a 50 percent divorce rate for first marriages and 65 percent for second marriages, and despite the staggering frequency of affairs, couples remain unflinching in their insistence on monogamy. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p157]
- Historically, monogamy was an externally imposed system of control over women's reproduction—about lineage and property—nothing to do with love. Today it has everything to do with love and is a personal choice for both sexes. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p158]
- The psychologist Erich Fromm points out that people think it is easy to love but hard to find the right person. Once we have found 'the one,' we will need no one else. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p158]
- The feminist psychoanalyst Nancy Chodorow writes: 'This primary tendency, I shall be loved always, everywhere, in every way, my whole body, my whole being—without any criticism, without the slightest effort on my part—is the final aim of all erotic striving.'. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p158]
- Adam Phillips makes the point that 'monogamy is a kind of moral nexus, a keyhole through which we can spy on our preoccupations.'. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p171]
- In an affair, the structure itself—with its secrecy, risk, and barrier to overcome—lends itself to passion in ways that long-term domestic partnership does not. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p162]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel suggests that monogamy as currently practiced may be inherently incompatible with sustained erotic desire, and that redefining fidelity around emotional commitment rather than sexual exclusivity may help couples navigate this tension.
- Inference: Perel implies that the cultural emphasis on monogamy may actually contribute to infidelity by making it a forbidden transgression that heightens its appeal.
