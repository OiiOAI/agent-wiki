# Internet Block Scheduling

## Summary

A strategy for rewiring the brain away from distraction by scheduling specific times for Internet use and avoiding it completely outside these blocks, rather than taking occasional breaks from focus.

## Key facts

- The strategy involves scheduling Internet use in advance and avoiding it completely outside these times until the scheduled moment arrives. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p96]
- The problem with occasional breaks from distraction is that constant switching between low-stimuli and high-stimuli activities teaches the mind to never tolerate absence of novelty. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p96]
- Resisting distraction during offline blocks provides concentration calisthenics that strengthen attention-selecting muscles. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p96]
- Even when stuck during an offline block, you should not immediately abandon it but should either switch to another offline activity or reschedule the next Internet block with at least a five-minute gap. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p97]
- If stuck waiting in line during an offline block, you should simply endure the temporary boredom with only the company of your thoughts. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p98]

## Inferences

- Inference: ternet block scheduling recognizes that the brain can be rewired for distraction and must be actively rewired for focus through deliberate practice of resisting distractions.
