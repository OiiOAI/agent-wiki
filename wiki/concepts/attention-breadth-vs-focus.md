# Focussed vs. Open Attention

## Summary

A fundamental distinction between the attentional modes of the two hemispheres: the left hemisphere exhibits narrow, focused attention for manipulating and using the world, while the right hemisphere exhibits broad, open, vigilant attention for awareness of the environment and social signals. These represent incompatible types of attention that must be deployed simultaneously.

## Key facts

- The right hemisphere is responsible for every type of attention except focussed attention, including vigilance, sustained attention, alertness, and divided attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p64]
- Deficits in focussed attention are more severe with left-hemisphere injury and associated with activity in the left orbitofrontal cortex and basal ganglia. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p64]
- Patients with right-hemisphere damage suffer 'an excessive and more or less permanent narrowing of their attentional window'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p64]
- There is left-hemisphere dominance for local, narrowly focussed attention and right-hemisphere dominance for broad, global, and flexible attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p64]
- Attention is not just another 'function' but has an ontological status prior to functions and things; the kind of attention we bring changes the nature of the world we attend to. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p48]

## Inferences

- Inference: The need to deploy two incompatible attentional modes simultaneously explains why lateralization evolved in vertebrates—segregation prevents interference between modes.
- Inference: Because attention itself creates the world we experience, the attentional mode determines what kind of world comes into being for us.
