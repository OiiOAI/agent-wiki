# Nociceptors

## Summary

Nociceptors respond selectively to stimuli that can damage tissue, signaling impending injury and providing constant reminder of already injured tissue. They fall into two broad classes: Aδ fiber nociceptors producing sharp, pricking pain, and C fiber polymodal nociceptors producing dull, burning pain.

## Key facts

- Aδ mechanical nociceptors respond to sharp objects that penetrate, squeeze, or pinch skin and many also respond to noxious heat capable of burning tissue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p536]
- C fiber polymodal nociceptors respond to mechanical, thermal, and chemical stimuli such as pinch, noxious heat/cold, and irritant chemicals applied to skin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p536]
- Electrical stimulation of C fibers in humans evokes prolonged sensations of burning pain, while Aδ fiber stimulation produces sharp, pricking pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p536]
- In the viscera, nociceptors are activated by distension or swelling, producing sensations of intense pain with diffuse, poorly localized quality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p536]
