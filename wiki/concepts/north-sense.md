# North Sense

## Summary

The North Sense is a small silicon device attached to the chest that delivers a vibration when the user is turned toward magnetic north. It represents an early example of a woven resource—a trusted nonbiological structure delicately and constantly coupled to the user's cognitive system. Users rapidly assimilate its directional information into their cognitive ecology.

## Key facts

- The North Sense is a small silicon device attached to the chest that delivers a short vibration when the user is turned toward magnetic north. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p161]
- Users report varying degrees of anxiety and distress when the device is later removed or becomes inoperative. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p161]
- Over time, the brain learns to rely heavily on signals from such devices as fluidly and automatically as it relies on the deliverances of ordinary senses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p161]

## Inferences

- Inference: The North Sense exemplifies how early versions of deep-weave technologies work—functionality becomes as much part of the user as neural tissue when the merger becomes sufficiently good that the brain simply expects the new capabilities [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p161]
