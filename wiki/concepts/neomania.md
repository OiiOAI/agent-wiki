# Neomania

## Summary

Neomania is the love of the modern for its own sake — a contemporary disease linked to interventionism. It causes people to overestimate new technologies and underestimate ancient ones, leading to fragility through over-optimization. The antidote is the Lindy Effect: technologies that have survived long periods are more likely to survive further time, and 'what has been around for a very, very long time will stick around much longer' than new innovations.

## Key facts

- Neomania is a disease — linked to interventionism — bringing fragility through overvaluation of new over old. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p389]
- Time is the same as disorder; antifragility implies the old is superior to the new, and time will know more about fragilities than human logic. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p389]
- Technothinkers tend to have an 'engineering mind' — autistic tendencies, lack of literary culture, denigration of history. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p395]
- The treadmill effect: people rapidly revert to baseline well-being after 'upgrading' technology and begin hunting for the next iteration. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p405]
- Artisanal items cause fewer treadmill effects and tend to have antifragility; electronic items with on-off switches tend to be fragile. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p406]
- People sit in chairs (ancient Egypt), wear pants (5,000 years old), use forks (Roman times) — most future technology will look like today's, not like science fiction predictions. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p155]
- Nassim Taleb suggests: assume that most technology that has existed for the past fifty years will serve us for another half-century, and recent technology will be passé in a few years. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p155]
- Kubrick's 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) predicted a U.S. colony on the moon by 2000 — Taleb's rule 'whatever has survived for X years will last another X years' would have been more accurate. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p156]

## Inferences

- Inference: Modernist architecture suffers from neomania — it is smooth and unfractal rather than organic and self-similar. Jane Jacobs's opposition to Robert Moses's top-down planning was a fight against irreversibility and the removal of fractal urban vitality.
