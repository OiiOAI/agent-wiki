# Totem Meal

## Summary

A ceremonial ritual hypothesis proposed by William Robertson Smith, suggesting that the periodic killing and communal eating of the totem animal was an important feature of totemic religion. The ritual expressed blood kinship between the god, the sacrificial animal, and the worshippers. Freud adopts this hypothesis and connects it to the primal horde theory.

## Key facts

- Robertson Smith hypothesized that the totemic system included a ceremonial killing and communal eating of the totem animal, whose consumption was forbidden on all other occasions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4238]
- The sacrificial animal was treated as a member of the tribe; the sacrificing community, the god, and the sacrificial animal were of the same blood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4235]
- The holy mystery of sacrificial death is justified by the consideration that only in this way can the sacred cement be procured which creates or keeps alive a living bond of union between the worshippers and their god. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4236]
- The totemic sacrifice was a sacrament and the sacrificial animal was the ancient totem animal, the primitive god himself, by the killing and consuming of which the clansmen renewed and assured their likeness to the god. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4237]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud connects the totem meal to the primal horde theory, proposing that the ritual commemorates the killing of the primal father by his sons.
