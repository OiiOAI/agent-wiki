# Prediction Loop

## Summary

Prediction loops are the fundamental unit of brain operation according to Barrett's framework. The brain constantly makes predictions about sensory input and actions, comparing these predictions (simulations) to actual sensory input from the world. If predictions match reality, they become experience. If they don't match, the brain must resolve prediction errors either by changing predictions or filtering input to fit predictions. This process occurs throughout the brain in billions of parallel loops, generating intrinsic brain activity that creates all perception and action.

## Key facts

- Your brain's prediction loops constantly create simulations of sensations and movements, compare them to actual sensory input, and adjust accordingly. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p79]
- If predictions match sensory input, the predictions are correct and the simulation becomes your experience; if they don't match, the brain must resolve the errors. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p79]
- The brain resolves prediction errors either by being flexible and changing predictions, or by being stubborn and filtering sensory input to be consistent with predictions. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p80]
- Your brain works like a scientist, making predictions (hypotheses), testing them against incoming data (sensory input), and adjusting based on prediction errors. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p81]
