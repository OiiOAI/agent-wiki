# Co-Evolutionary Theory of Language

## Summary

Deacon proposes that languages and human brains co-evolve, but languages adapt much faster (thousands of times faster than brain evolution). Because children are the bottleneck through which languages reproduce, languages evolve toward structures that fit children's learning biases. This explains both language universals and the paradox of easy language acquisition.

## Key facts

- Language evolution proceeds thousands of times faster than brain evolution, so languages do most of the adapting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p117]
- Languages must pass through the 'bottleneck' of children's minds to be reproduced, creating selection pressure for learnability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p117]
- Language structures poorly adapted to children's minds will not persist; less learnable variants are continuously selected against. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p130]
- The 'less is more' effect: children's learning limitations force attention to global grammatical patterns, paradoxically aiding acquisition. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p145]

## Inferences

- Inference: Color term universals (Berlin and Kay) demonstrate this process: weak neural biases, amplified across generations of nongenetic evolution, produce near-universal naming patterns.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all grammatical universals can be explained by co-evolutionary convergence, or whether some require additional mechanisms, is not fully resolved.
