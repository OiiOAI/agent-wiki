# Display Rules

## Summary

Display rules are lessons taught to children about what is and is not an appropriate emotional response to any given situation. In extreme cases, these manifest as directives like 'Buck up! Big boys don't cry,' which signal that uncomfortable emotions are signs of weakness to be avoided. Display rules can also appear in subtler forms, such as dismissing a child's frustration or sugarcoating their distress, and can inadvertently teach children to bottle their emotions rather than acknowledge them.

## Key facts

- Display rules are lessons we teach our kids about what is and is not an appropriate emotional response to any given situation. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p225]
- An extreme display rule might manifest as 'Buck up! Big boys don't cry,' which signals to a child that uncomfortable emotions are signs of weakness. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p225]
- Parents may try to dismiss a child's frustration or sadness by saying 'Oh, he's just tired' or 'She's hungry,' or sugarcoat their distress by saying 'Oh honey, you know you don't really feel that way.'. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p225]
- Even when messages about display rules come from a place of love, they can be counterproductive. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: Display rules are part of the emotional socialization children receive and can interfere with the development of emotional agility if they teach suppression rather than acceptance.
