# Long-Term Potentiation

## Summary

Long-term potentiation (LTP) is a long-lasting increase in synaptic strength induced by high-frequency stimulation, requiring NMDA receptor activation and Ca2+ influx, representing a cellular mechanism for learning and memory.

## Key facts

- LTP was first described in 1973 by Tim Bliss and Terje Lomo in the hippocampus, where high-frequency tetanic stimulation produces long-lasting potentiation of excitatory synaptic transmission. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p286]
- LTP induction requires NMDA receptor activation and the resulting Ca2+ influx into postsynaptic dendritic spines. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p286]
- LTP is blocked by APV, a selective NMDA receptor antagonist, or by intracellular Ca2+ chelation in the postsynaptic cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p286]
- During LTP expression, Ca2+ influx activates CaMKII and PKC, leading to insertion of additional AMPA receptors into the postsynaptic membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p286]
- LTP is input-specific; only those synapses activated during tetanic stimulation are potentiated, as Ca2+ accumulation is largely restricted to individual activated spines. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p284]
- Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) can be induced when presynaptic stimulation precedes postsynaptic firing by a brief interval, providing a physiologically relevant form of LTP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p284]
- LTP at Schaffer collateral synapses requires NMDA receptor activation because the Mg2+ block is relieved only by strong postsynaptic depolarization, acting as a coincidence detector. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1389]
- LTP at mossy fiber synapses is presynaptic and triggered by large Ca2+ influx into presynaptic terminals, activating calcium/calmodulin-dependent adenylyl cyclase and PKA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1389]
- LTP has early and late phases: early LTP lasts 1-3 hours and is mediated by covalent modifications, while late LTP lasts 24+ hours and requires new protein synthesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1395]
- LTP demonstrates three key properties: cooperativity (requires strong input), associativity (weak input paired with strong input undergoes LTP), and synapse specificity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1396]
- Late LTP requires activation of the cAMP-PKA-CREB pathway, leading to new gene transcription and synthesis of proteins for synaptic growth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1369]

## Inferences

- Inference: LTP and related forms of synaptic plasticity provide the cellular substrate for long-term memory storage, as suggested by Donald Hebb's 1949 proposal that synchronized pre- and postsynaptic activity strengthens synaptic connections.
- Inference: The NMDA receptor's requirement for both presynaptic glutamate release AND postsynaptic depolarization fulfills Hebb's coincidence requirement for synaptic modification.
