# Conflict Strategies Workshop

## Summary

The book includes a workshop with six scenarios designed to help readers learn to distinguish between secure and insecure conflict resolution tactics by analyzing how different couples handle disagreements.

## Key facts

- Marcus demonstrates insecure tactics by generalizing the conflict, attacking Daria, and making her sound needy while not addressing her real concern about his potential infidelity on the singles cruise. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p197]
- Daria's insecure reaction is to cave in and apologize for raising the issue, effectively agreeing that her feelings are not important. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p198]
- John's reaction to Ruth's attack about their daughter's math difficulties is classified as secure because he maintains focus on the problem, answers straightforwardly, and shows genuine awareness for his wife's well-being by validating her concern. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p199]
- Steve's insecure tactic is withdrawal—trying to avert confrontation or intimate conversation rather than engaging with Mia's underlying concerns. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p200]
- Todd demonstrates insecure tactics by evading Emma's underlying concern about feeling unattractive, minimizing the importance of her argument, and reverting to withdrawal rather than engagement. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p202]
- Shannon's reaction is classified as secure because she avoids generalizing the conflict, doesn't react defensively, maintains focus on the problem, and responds with a sincere apology. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p204]
