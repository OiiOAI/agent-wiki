# Affect Heuristic

## Summary

The affect heuristic is a mental shortcut where emotional reactions determine risk/benefit assessments. If you like something, you believe risks are smaller and benefits greater. A Harley owner who reads about dangers will subconsciously rate the experience as 'an even greater sense of freedom.'

## Key facts

- The affect heuristic puts risks and benefits on the same sensory thread: if you like something, you believe risks are smaller and benefits greater. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p149]
- Researchers Hirschleifer and Shumway found a correlation between morning sunshine and stock market performance across 26 exchanges from 1982-1997. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p150]
- University of Michigan research showed subjects preferred Chinese characters that followed a smiling face rather than an angry or neutral one, shown for less than 1/100th of a second. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p150]
- Slovic proposed that people use the affect heuristic when they make judgments and choices by consulting their emotions: 'Do I like it? Do I hate it?'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p137]
- In Slovic's experiments, participants shown arguments for a technology's benefits subsequently rated it as lower risk even though no risk information was provided, and vice versa—demonstrating that affect in one domain spills over into another. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p183]
- Members of the British Toxicology Society showed the same pattern of benefits-risks correlation as laypeople: they found little benefit in things they disliked and little risk in things they liked—affect一致性 as a form of associative coherence. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p183]
- Damasio's research showed that people who lack appropriate emotional responses (e.g., from brain damage) are also impaired in making good decisions, suggesting that affect is not just a bias but a necessary guide to judgment. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p182]
- The affect heuristic explains why nuclear power, genetically modified food, and motorcycles are simultaneously judged high-risk and low-benefit: those who dislike them have highly available negative associations. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p137]

## Inferences

- Inference: The affect heuristic is an instance of substitution: the difficult question of what one thinks about a risk is replaced by the easier question of how one feels about it. This is consistent with the broader pattern that System 1 substitutes easy questions for hard ones.
