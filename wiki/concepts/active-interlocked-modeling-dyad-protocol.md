# Active Interlocked Modeling dyad protocol

## Summary

The Active Interlocked Modeling (AIM) dyad protocol, developed by Shebilske, Regian, Arthur, and Jordan (1992), requires trainees to perform each half of a task alternately while being interlocked with a teammate who performs the other half. Despite having only half as much hands-on experience, AIM-dyad trainees performed as well as individual trainees on tests performed alone. The protocol has been successfully adapted at Aer Lingus Irish National Airline. Research suggests trainees enhance their controlled processing skills through this protocol, as reasoning ability compensated for reduced hands-on practice.
