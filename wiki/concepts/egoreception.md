# Egoreception

## Summary

Egoreception refers to the perception of oneself, specified by information that is unique to the observer and cannot be shared. When a point of observation is occupied, the observer's body temporarily conceals portions of the environment in a unique way. The head, body, arms, and hands appear as occluding edges in the field of view, providing information about the observer's own body. This complements exteroception (perception of the environment), with both being picked up concurrently through the same perceptual systems.

## Key facts

- When a point of observation is occupied, there is optical information to specify the observer himself, which cannot be shared by other observers—the body of the observing animal temporarily conceals some portion of the environment in a way unique to that animal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p136]
- The edges of the field of view hide the environment behind them with the head as occluder, and when the field moves there is accretion at the leading edge with deletion at the trailing edge, conforming to the principle of reversible occlusion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p137]
- The nose provides maximum crossed disparity (left-hand edge of right eye's view, right-hand edge of left eye's view), maximum convergence, and maximum motion parallax, constituting the zero of distance-from-here. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p141]
- Egoreception and exteroception are inseparable—the supposedly separate realms of subjective and objective are only poles of attention, not separate realities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p140]

## Inferences

- Inference: The rejection of the doctrine of specific nerve energies (Müller's doctrine) means that egoreception is not localized to specialized proprioceptors; all perceptual systems provide information about the self through their respective sensory channels.
