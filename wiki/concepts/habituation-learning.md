# Habituation

## Summary

Habituation is a form of non-associative learning where an animal learns to ignore a stimulus that is repetitive and inconsequential. In Aplysia, repeated light touches to the siphon produce habituation of the gill-withdrawal reflex, weakening the synaptic connection between sensory and motor neurons.

## Key facts

- Habituation is one of the two forms of non-associative learning Kandel studied in Aplysia, the other being sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p202]
- During habituation, the sensory neuron releases less neurotransmitter, weakening synaptic communication with motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p213]
- Forty stimuli administered consecutively result in habituation lasting only one day, but ten stimuli every day for four days produce habituation lasting weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p203]
- In long-term habituation, the number of presynaptic terminals drops from 1300 to about 850, and active terminals diminish from 500 to about 100. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p225]
