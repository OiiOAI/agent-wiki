# Affective attack

## Summary

Affective attack is a form of aggressive behavior mediated by the RAGE system, characterized by intense emotional (anger-like) responses. Unlike predatory attack, affective attack involves full emotional arousal with autonomic changes, hissing, spitting, and directed rage toward threats. Animals experiencing affective attack can be petted immediately after stimulation ceases, suggesting rapid emotional state changes. This form of aggression is distinguished from predatory attack by its dependence on the medial hypothalamus and PAG, its pharmacological profile, and the fact that it is directed at living targets (conspecifics) rather than prey.

## Key facts

- Three distinct kinds of aggression can be aroused by applying ESB to slightly different brain zones: predatory aggression, angry, rage-like aggression, and perhaps intermale aggression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- During affective attack, the peaceful animal is emotionally transformed, leaping viciously with claws unsheathed, fangs bared, hissing and spitting, with arousal directed at the most salient potential threat. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Within a fraction of a minute after terminating stimulation, the cat was again relaxed and peaceful and could be petted without further retribution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Affective attack sites are more concentrated in the ventrolateral and medial hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Affective attack sites yield escape behaviors, while quiet-biting attack sites also support self-stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- When confronted by conspecifics, no attack is generated from quiet-biting attack sites, while intense attack is still evoked from affective attack sites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Minor tranquilizers such as chlordiazepoxide (Librium) reduce affective attack and increase quiet-biting attack, while psychostimulants can increase affective attack without clearly affecting quiet-biting attack. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Stimulation of the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis facilitates affective attack while suppressing quiet-biting attack. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that during anger the type of available target is not as important as the fact that there is a living target suggests affective attack is directed at removing threats rather than obtaining resources.
- Inference: The finding that with repeated stimulation within colony-living situations animals can ascend in rank within dominance hierarchies suggests that affective attack may serve social competition functions.
