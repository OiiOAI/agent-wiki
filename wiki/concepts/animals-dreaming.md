# Animals and Dreaming

## Summary

Whether non-human animals dream remains scientifically uncertain despite strong circumstantial evidence. Dogs show REM sleep with twitching and running-like leg movements. Rats show brain activity patterns during REM sleep similar to when they learned maze tasks. However, the subjective experience of dreaming cannot be directly verified in animals.

## Key facts

- Dogs spend approximately 36 percent of their sleep time in REM, compared to humans' 20-25 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p93]
- MIT researcher Matt Wilson found that rats show brain activity during REM sleep that appears to be replaying maze-running experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p93]
- Whether animals dream cannot be scientifically proven because dreaming is a subjective, internal experience that animals cannot report. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p93]
- Minimally conscious patients show sleep patterns indistinguishable from fully conscious people, suggesting they may have access to dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p99]
