# Secondary Process

## Summary

Secondary process refers to the mode of psychical functioning that operates under the influence of the cathected ego. In secondary process, psychical activity is moderated and directed by the ego, which inhibits primary processes through side cathexis. This enables reality testing, delayed discharge, and goal-directed thought. The secondary process represents adaptation to the exigencies of life and operates according to the reality principle rather than the pleasure principle.

## Key facts

- Freud defined secondary processes as those 'which are only made possible by a good cathexis of the ego, and which represent a moderation of the foregoing' primary processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p399]
- The precondition of secondary processes 'is a correct employment of the indications of reality, which is only possible when there is inhibition by the ego.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p399]
- Secondary process 'is a repetition of the original ψ passage [of quantity], at a lower level, with smaller quantities,' economizing Qη during thought. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p407]

## Inferences

- Inference: Secondary process thinking allows for the displacement of smaller quantities of cathexis, enabling cognitive operations without full motor discharge.
- Inference: The secondary/primary distinction laid groundwork for Freud's later structural model's division between ego and id.
