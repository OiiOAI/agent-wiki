# Circadian Rhythm

## Summary

Circadian rhythms are 24-hour biological cycles that couple behaviors to the day-night cycle. The molecular basis involves transcriptional oscillators conserved from flies to humans. Key genes include period (per) and clock, which encode transcriptional regulators with PAS domains. The PER protein accumulates during the day and represses CLOCK function at night, generating oscillations. Mutations in these genes can produce long-day, short-day, or arrhythmic phenotypes.

## Key facts

- The period (per) gene in Drosophila affects all circadian behaviors and mutations can produce 19-hour (short-day), 28-hour (long-day), or arrhythmic phenotypes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p87]
- PER protein is a transcriptional regulator that accumulates during the day and represses CLOCK function during the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p87]
- The clock gene in mice encodes a transcriptional activator that, together with PER, controls circadian rhythm in both flies and mice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p89]
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus contains GABAergic neurons that function as the master circadian clock, with individual neurons maintaining close to 24-hour rhythms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1136]
- BMAL1 and CLOCK proteins form a dimer that binds to E-box motifs and activates transcription of Period and Cryptochrome genes; the resulting proteins form a complex that inhibits BMAL1-CLOCK dimers, completing a negative feedback loop. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1136]
- The intrinsic circadian cycle in humans averages 24.1 hours in dim light conditions, requiring daily light exposure to entrain to the 24-hour external day. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1137]
- Melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells signal ambient light levels to the suprachiasmatic nucleus for entrainment, distinct from image-forming visual pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1137]
- Melatonin signaling from the pineal gland reinforces circadian rhythms; exogenous melatonin can entrain circadian rhythms in individuals with non-24-hour sleep-wake rhythm disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1137]
- Circadian rhythm couples behaviors to a 24-hour cycle linked to the rising and setting of the sun. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p98]
- The clock persists even in the absence of light or other environmental influences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p98]
- Seymour Benzer's group identified the period (per) gene through random mutagenesis of Drosophila. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p98]
- Mutations in per affected all circadian behaviors; arrhythmic per mutants lacked all function of the per gene. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p99]
- Long-day per alleles produced 28-hour behavioral cycles, short-day alleles produced 19-hour cycles, demonstrating per is a timekeeper. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p99]
- Joseph Takahashi identified the clock gene in mice through similar genetic screens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p99]
- The molecular mechanism of circadian rhythm involves oscillation of PAS-domain transcriptional regulators and is conserved from flies to mice to humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p99]
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus in the hypothalamus is the master circadian clock of the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1198]
- CLOCK and BMAL1 transcription factors bind to each other and form a heterodimer that enhances transcription of the mouse period (mPer1-3) and cryptochrome genes (mCry1-2). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1198]
- PER and CRY proteins form heterodimers, re-enter the nucleus, and inhibit CLOCK and BMAL1, thus repressing their own transcription. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1198]
- A small number of retinal ganglion cells respond directly to light and project to the suprachiasmatic nucleus to synchronize circadian rhythms with the day-night cycle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1196]
- Without external time cues (zeitgebers), the human endogenous circadian rhythm is approximately 25 hours rather than 24 hours. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1196]

## Inferences

- Inference: Familial advanced sleep-phase syndrome results from mutations in Period genes or casein kinase 1 delta that accelerate the circadian clock cycling.
- Inference: The two-process model of sleep regulation proposes that sleepiness depends on both a homeostatic sleep drive (Process S) that builds during wakefulness and a circadian process (Process C) that creates rhythmic variation in alertness independent of sleep history.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which the suprachiasmatic nucleus coordinates peripheral clocks throughout the body are not fully characterized.
