# Energy Homeostasis

## Summary

Energy homeostasis refers to the maintenance of stable body weight and energy reserves through coordinated behavioral and metabolic mechanisms. The brain regulates long-term energy balance primarily through hypothalamic integration of metabolic signals, with the ventromedial hypothalamus serving as a key sensing area.

## Key facts

- Mammals must maintain constancy of oxygen, carbon dioxide, body water, salt balance, and body temperature through homeostatic mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p91]
- Animals can maintain stable body energy patterns through a variety of behavioral strategies, including single large daily meals or many small meals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p95]
- The VMH contains metabolic integrators that monitor overall body energy status, not merely short-term satiety signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]
- VMH-lesioned animals remain permanently stuck in the lipogenic (energy storage) phase of their daily cycle, leading to obesity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]
- Leptin, a protein derived from adipose tissue, signals body fat status to hypothalamic regulatory systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p98]
- The ob/ob mouse lacks functional leptin, resulting in severe obesity, while the db/db mouse lacks leptin receptors and cannot respond to the signal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p98]
- Local glucose infusions into the VMH decrease daily food intake without affecting immediate meal size, suggesting cumulative metabolic effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p99]
- Body fat stores remain relatively constant over time because calories ingested roughly equal calories expended, with only 0.27% daily mismatch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1082]
- Short-term satiety signals include CCK, GLP-1, and PYY released by intestinal endocrine cells, while ghrelin from the stomach promotes hunger before eating. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1082]
- Leptin, secreted by adipocytes in proportion to fat stores, signals to the arcuate nucleus and informs the brain whether fat stores are adequate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1083]
- POMC neurons release α-MSH which activates MC4R receptors, decreasing hunger; AgRP neurons release GABA and NPY which inhibit MC4R neurons, increasing hunger. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1084]
- Leptin is more effective in defending against low fat stores than in resisting obesity; the defense of energy balance is asymmetric. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1083]

## Inferences

- Inference: The VMH's insulin sensitivity may allow it to serve as a long-term energy integrator by monitoring nutrient storage processes.
- Inference: The close matching between intake and expenditure (only 0.27% daily mismatch) suggests remarkable precision in homeostatic control mechanisms.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise molecular signals that VMH cells use to encode energy status remain incompletely characterized.
