# Life Tasks

## Summary

Life tasks are the three categories of interpersonal relationships that individuals must confront when living as social beings, as proposed by Alfred Adler. These are: the task of work, the task of friendship, and the task of love. Together with parent-child relationships, they encompass the interpersonal challenges that form the basis of human existence. Avoiding these tasks constitutes a 'life-lie.'

## Key facts

- Adler categorized interpersonal relationships into three tasks: 'tasks of work,' 'tasks of friendship,' and 'tasks of love,' collectively called 'life tasks.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p114]
- Work tasks have the lowest interpersonal hurdles because they have a common objective, but work that can be completed without any cooperation is 'unfeasible in principle.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p114-115]
- The task of love is divided into love relationships and parent-child relationships, with the latter being most difficult because separation cannot occur in principle. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p119]
- Adler used the expression 'three social ties' to emphasize the importance of interpersonal relationships in life tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p114]
- Running away from life tasks by making excuses is called 'the life-lie' in Adlerian psychology. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p101]

## Inferences

- Inference: The inability to separate from parent-child relationships (unlike romantic relationships which allow separation through divorce) explains why parent-child dynamics are considered the most challenging life task.
