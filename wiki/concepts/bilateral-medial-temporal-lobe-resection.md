# Bilateral Medial Temporal-lobe Resection

## Summary

Bilateral medial temporal-lobe resection is the surgical procedure performed on Henry Molaison on August 25, 1953, by Dr. William Beecher Scoville. The operation removed brain tissue including the uncus, amygdala, front half of the hippocampus, and entorhinal cortex from both temporal lobes. Scoville described it as a 'frankly experimental operation.' The surgery successfully controlled Henry's epilepsy but caused profound, permanent amnesia. MRI studies later revealed that Scoville had overestimated the extent of removal—he believed it was 8 cm but actual removal was slightly more than half that amount.

## Inferences

- Inference: The catastrophic outcome of Henry's surgery led to the crucial discovery that bilateral damage to medial temporal-lobe structures, particularly the hippocampus, causes amnesia and should be avoided in epilepsy surgery.
