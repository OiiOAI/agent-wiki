# Baroreceptor Reflex

## Summary

The baroreceptor reflex is a homeostatic mechanism that regulates blood pressure by detecting stretch in the walls of the carotid sinus and aortic arch. It provides negative feedback control of the cardiovascular system, preventing orthostatic hypotension and fainting by compensating for rapid hydrostatic effects produced by changes in posture.

## Key facts

- The baroreceptor reflex prevents orthostatic hypotension and fainting by compensating for rapid hydrostatic effects produced by changes in posture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1120]
- When baroreceptors detect decreased stretch from low blood pressure, neurons in the ventrolateral medulla reflexively suppress parasympathetic activity and stimulate sympathetic activity to restore blood pressure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1120]
- The baroreceptor reflex operates as a negative feedback loop with gain; in a healthy individual with reflex gain of 8, systemic blood pressure can be maintained within 10% of its set point. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1121]
- The parasympathetic component of the baroreceptor reflex has a more rapid onset but is briefer than the sympathetic component, making it critical for rapid responses but less important for long-term blood pressure regulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1120]
