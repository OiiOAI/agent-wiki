# Impulse Control Disorders (ICDs) in Parkinson's Disease

## Summary

Impulse control disorders (ICDs), including pathological gambling, compulsive shopping, hypersexuality, and binge eating, are common behavioral complications of dopaminergic medications in Parkinson's disease (PD) patients, affecting approximately 17.1% of those on dopaminergic medications according to the DOMINION study.

## Key facts

- ICDs are associated with dopaminergic medications, particularly D2/3-preferring DA agonists, with odds ratios of 2.72 for DA agonists versus 1.51 for levodopa. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p292]
- Risk factors for ICDs in PD include current smoking, family history of gambling, younger age, being unmarried, depression, anxiety, and higher novelty seeking. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p292]
- Lower striatal dopamine transporter (DAT) levels have been observed in PD patients with ICDs and may predate medication exposure, potentially predicting ICD onset. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p293]
- PD patients with ICDs show enhanced ventral striatal dopamine release in response to reward-related visual stimuli and gambling cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p293]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prevalence of ICDs (17.1%) in medicated PD patients compared to the general population suggests dopaminergic medications significantly alter reward circuitry function.
- Inference: The association between lower DAT levels and ICDs suggests individual differences in dopamine homeostasis may underlie vulnerability to ICDs.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact role of D3 receptors in PD-related ICDs remains unclear, with conflicting findings from different PET imaging studies.
