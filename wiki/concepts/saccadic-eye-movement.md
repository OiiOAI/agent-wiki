# Saccadic Eye Movement

## Summary

Saccades are rapid, highly stereotyped eye movements that move the fovea from one fixation point to another, allowing visual exploration of the environment. They are generated by burst neurons in the brain stem and controlled by the superior colliculus and cortical areas including the frontal eye field.

## Key facts

- Saccades are highly stereotyped with a standard waveform featuring a single smooth increase and decrease of eye velocity, and they occur within a fraction of a second at angular speeds up to 900 degrees per second. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p946]
- The velocity of a saccade is determined only by the distance of the target from the fovea; humans can change amplitude and direction voluntarily but not the speed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p946]
- Horizontal saccades are generated by burst neurons in the paramedian pontine reticular formation that make direct excitatory connections to motor neurons and interneurons in the ipsilateral abducens nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p955]
- Vertical saccades are generated by burst neurons in the rostral interstitial nucleus of the medial longitudinal fasciculus in the mesencephalic reticular formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p957]
- Omnipause neurons in the nucleus of the dorsal raphe fire continuously except around the time of a saccade and inhibit burst neurons; their activity arrests saccades when stimulated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p955]
- Electrical stimulation of the intermediate layers of the superior colliculus evokes saccades into the movement fields of the stimulated neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p959]
