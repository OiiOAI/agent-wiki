# Nighttime Therapy Theory

## Summary

Proposed by Ernest Hartmann, the nighttime therapy theory proposes that dreaming is a form of nighttime therapy that helps weave emotional concerns and traumatic events into existing memory systems within the safety of sleep. Dreams accomplish this by creating connections between new and old memories that are broader and looser than connections made during wakefulness.

## Key facts

- Ernest Hartmann proposed that dreaming is a form of nighttime therapy that helps weave emotional concerns and traumatic events into existing memory systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- According to Hartmann, dreams create connections between new and old memories that are broader and looser than connections made during wakefulness. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Hartmann proposed that emotions are the instigators of dream content and guide the connections made during dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Hartmann introduced the concept of a contextualizing image—a picture-metaphor representing key emotional issues that changes as the dreamer's emotions evolve over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p115]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Hartmann's model is relatively vague in describing the specific mechanisms through which dreams would exert their therapeutic effects
