# Envier Types

## Summary

Greene identifies five common varieties of enviers based on psychoanalyst Melanie Klein's work: (1) The Leveler—uses cynical humor to tear down achievers and deflate pretensions; (2) The Self-entitled Slacker—feels rewards are naturally due without effort, envying those who work hard; (3) The Status Fiend—reduces everything to material comparisons and measures self-worth by status; (4) The Attacher—secretly envies while attaching themselves to the successful, gathering information to harm; (5) The Insecure Master—bosses who secretly doubt their worth and envy subordinates' youth and promise.

## Key facts

- The Leveler cannot recognize or appreciate excellence in almost anyone except the dead, and uses cynicism to disguise envy as indignation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p343]
- The Status Fiend is highly sensitive to differences in rank and will attack underhandedly if one is a peer or colleague rather than clearly superior. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p345]
- The Attacher lacks a clear sense of purpose in life and naturally envies those who act with purpose, partly wishing to harm them. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p347]
- According to Melanie Klein, those prone to envy develop lifelong strategies to mitigate or repress these feelings, but these strategies break down under stress. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p342]
