# Build Half a Product

## Summary

The concept argues for doing fewer things extremely well rather than attempting many things inadequately. The authors recommend cutting ambition in half, treating good ideas that can be done later as future opportunities, and recognizing that things often improve when shortened. They cite writers, musicians, and directors who remove good material to create great finished products, noting that this book was cut from 57,000 to 27,000 words between drafts and was better for it.

## Key facts

- The authors argue you cannot do everything you want to do and do it well, recommending instead to cut ambition in half. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p55]
- The authors state that many great ideas will not seem all that great once perspective is gained, and if truly great, they can be done later. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p55]
- The authors cite that directors cut good scenes to make great movies, musicians drop good tracks for great albums, and writers eliminate good pages for great books. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p55]
- Rework was cut from 57,000 words to about 27,000 words between drafts. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p55]

## Inferences

- Inference: Feature reduction forces difficult prioritization decisions that ultimately strengthen product-market fit.
