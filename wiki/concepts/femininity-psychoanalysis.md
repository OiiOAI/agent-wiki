# Femininity in Psychoanalysis

## Summary

Freud addressed the psychological development of femininity, noting that psychology cannot solve the riddle of femininity. He discussed the pre-Oedipal attachment to the mother, the castration complex and penis envy, the transition to the Oedipus complex through the father, and the development of normal femininity versus masculinity complex and neurosis.

## Key facts

- Psychology cannot solve the riddle of femininity; the explanation must come from elsewhere, when we learn how differentiation into two sexes came about. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6946]
- In the phallic phase, both boys and girls derive pleasurable sensations from their genitals; girls do the same with their clitoris, which serves as the leading erotogenic zone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6948]
- In femininity, the clitoris should wholly or in part hand over its sensitivity to the vagina; this is one of the two extra tasks women must perform in development. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6948]
- Girls must change both their erotogenic zone and their object during development (from mother to father), whereas boys retain both. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6949]
- The girl's libidinal relations to her mother are of very many different kinds, persisting through all three phases of infantile sexuality with characteristics of oral, sadistic-anal, and phallic wishes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6950]
- The turning away from the mother is accompanied by hostility; the attachment to the mother ends in hate, part of which may persist throughout life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6951]
- Girls hold their mother responsible for their lack of a penis and do not forgive her for this disadvantage; this is the specific factor in their turning from the mother. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6954]
- The discovery of her castration is a turning point in a girl's growth, with three possible lines: sexual inhibition or neurosis, masculinity complex, or normal femininity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6955]
- For girls, the wish for a penis eventually may be replaced by the wish for a baby, following an ancient symbolic equivalence, and the girl enters the Oedipus situation as a haven of refuge. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6957]
- In contrast to boys, for whom castration fear compels abandoning the Oedipus complex, the chief motive for surmounting it is lacking in girls; they remain in it longer and demolish it late and incompletely, so the superego formation must suffer. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6958]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud acknowledged that women analysts might have been better able to research femininity without male prejudice, noting that female analysts had begun working on the question.
- Inference: The development of a little girl into a normal woman is more difficult and complicated than a boy's, since it includes two extra tasks (clitoral-vaginal transition and change of object).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The full extent of social customs versus constitutional factors in shaping feminine passivity and masochism.
