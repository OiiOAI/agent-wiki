# Operational Errors in Air Traffic Control

## Summary

Operational Errors (OEs) in Air Traffic Control refer to occurrences where prescribed separation between aircraft is lost. Research analyzing OEs examines relationships between severity (minor, moderate, major), causal factors, controller workload, and situation awareness. Studies use data from FAA Form 7210-3 reports, SATORI recreations, and facility-level analyses to identify factors associated with different error severities.

## Key facts

- Of 1,053 operational errors analyzed, 70% were classified as minor severity and 30% as moderate severity. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p99]
- Over 31/2 years, only 15 of 1,053 OEs had a major severity rating. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p109]
- Average number of aircraft being worked during OEs was approximately 8.5, with a range from 2 to 15. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p105]
- Neither quantitative controller workload nor air traffic complexity rating was found to be related to OE severity. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p106]
- Causal factors more likely to result in moderate severity included misuse of conflict alert (52%), interfacility coordination (53.8%), and readback errors (40.8%). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p100]
- When controllers were aware an error was developing, only 21% of errors were moderately severe versus higher percentages for unaware conditions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p100]

## Inferences

- Inference: Controller awareness of developing errors appears to be a critical factor in mitigating severity, suggesting that interventions improving early detection could reduce moderate and major errors.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The relationship between workload measures and OE severity remains unclear, as quantitative measures did not correlate but cluster-based workload categories were not fully analyzed for severity relationships.
