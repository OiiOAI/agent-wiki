# Lipopolysaccharide

## Summary

Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), also known as bacterial endotoxin, is a component of the membrane of certain bacteria that normally lives within the large intestine. When leaked into circulation due to increased gut permeability, LPS sets off an acute pro-inflammatory response and is directly related to pro-inflammatory cytokine production and increased oxidative stress. LDL particles have docking sites called LPS-binding proteins that allow them to soak up renegade LPS, and a chronically leaky gut may drive LDL levels up.

## Key facts

- Lipopolysaccharide (LPS), also known as bacterial endotoxin, is a normal component of certain gut bacteria that is kept safely in the intestines under normal circumstances. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p172]
- When LPS leaks into circulation, it sets off an acute pro-inflammatory response, causing systemic bacterial invasion signaling. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p249]
- LPS exposure is directly related to pro-inflammatory cytokine production and an increase in oxidative stress. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p249]
