# The Holy Fool

## Summary

The Holy Fool (yurodivy in Russian folklore) is a social archetype—an eccentric, off-putting outsider who nonetheless has access to truth because they are free from social hierarchies and conventions. Gladwell uses this concept to discuss whistleblowers like Harry Markopolos, who do not default to truth and therefore can identify deception that others miss.

## Key facts

- In Russian folklore, the yurodivy or Holy Fool is a social misfit who has access to truth precisely because they are an outcast. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p59]
- Gladwell identifies whistleblowers as modern Holy Fools who sacrifice loyalty to their institutions to expose fraud and deceit. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p60]
- The Little Boy in 'The Emperor's New Clothes' is identified as a Holy Fool archetype who breaks social convention to speak truth. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p60]

## Inferences

- Inference: While Holy Fools are necessary for exposing fraud, Levine argues that everyone cannot be a Holy Fool without destroying the social cooperation that enables functioning societies.
