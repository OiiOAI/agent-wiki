# Prenatal diet and offspring mental health

## Summary

Research shows that a mother's diet during pregnancy can significantly affect her child's mental health and neurodevelopment. Poor diet quality during pregnancy is associated with behavioral problems, hyperactivity, inattention, and aggression in offspring. Severe malnutrition during pregnancy (as seen in historical famines) is linked to increased risk of schizophrenia and depression in children.

## Key facts

- The Dutch Hunger Winter of 1944-45 and a severe famine in China in 1959-61 showed that women malnourished in pregnancy had over twice the risk of producing offspring who went on to develop schizophrenia and depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p110]
- A study of more than 23,000 Norwegian mother-child pairs showed that mothers who ate more processed food during pregnancy were more likely to have children with behavioral problems five years later. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p110]
- A French study of about 1,200 mother-child pairs showed that a typical Western diet during pregnancy increased the chances of the child displaying hyperactivity and inattention at ages three, five, and eight years. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p110]
- A Dutch study of more than 3,000 pregnant women showed that the traditional Dutch Diet was associated with more aggression in their offspring at age six. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p110]
