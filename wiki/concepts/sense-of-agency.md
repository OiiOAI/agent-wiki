# Sense of Agency

## Summary

The sense of agency is the subjective experience of being in control of one's own actions. It is closely linked to beliefs about free will and to moral responsibility. This sense can become detached from actual motor control, as seen in phantom limb experiences and anosognosia for hemiplegia.

## Key facts

- The sense of agency is created from two components: prior expectations and the sensory consequences of the action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1528]
- After limb amputation, patients experience phantom limbs because sensorimotor systems predict the proprioceptive sensations they would feel if they moved an intact limb. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1528]
- After stroke-induced paralysis, some patients believe they can still move the limb despite lack of sensory evidence, a condition called anosognosia for hemiplegia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1528]
- Intentional binding, the subjective experience of action and outcome occurring closer together in time, provides an empirical marker of the sense of agency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1527]
