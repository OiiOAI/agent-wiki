# The Original Position

## Summary

The original position is the appropriate initial status quo in justice as fairness that ensures fundamental agreements reached are fair. It is characterized by the veil of ignorance, where no one knows their place in society, class position, social status, natural assets and abilities, intelligence, strength, conception of the good, or special psychological propensities. The parties are equal, all having the same rights in the procedure for choosing principles. The original position is purely hypothetical; its justification lies in the conditions it embodies being ones we do in fact accept or can be persuaded to accept through philosophical reflection.

## Key facts

- The original position is the appropriate initial status quo which ensures that the fundamental agreements reached in it are fair, yielding the name 'justice as fairness'. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p27]
- Among the essential features is that no one knows their place in society, class position or social status, nor their fortune in the distribution of natural assets and abilities. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The veil of ignorance ensures that no one is advantaged or disadvantaged in the choice of principles by natural chance or social circumstances. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- Since all are similarly situated and no one can design principles to favor their particular condition, the principles of justice are the result of a fair agreement or bargain. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The parties are rational and mutually disinterested: they do not take an interest in one another's interests and their spiritual aims may be opposed. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p24]
- The parties in the original position are equal: all have the same rights in the procedure for choosing principles and each can make proposals and submit reasons. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p29]
- The original position is defined so that it is a status quo in which any agreements reached are fair — the parties are equally represented as moral persons and the outcome is not conditioned by arbitrary contingencies or the relative balance of social forces. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p118]
- The original position is a purely hypothetical situation; nothing resembling it need ever take place, although we can simulate the reflections of the parties by deliberately following the constraints it expresses. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p118]
- The parties assume that they are choosing principles for a public conception of justice; they suppose that everyone will know about these principles. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p130]
- The acceptance of the principles is not conjectured as a psychological law or probability; ideally the acknowledgment of principles is the only choice consistent with the full description of the original position. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p119]
- The original position must be interpreted so that one can at any time adopt its perspective, and it must make no difference when one takes up this viewpoint, or who does so. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p134]

## Inferences

- Inference: The symmetry of everyone's relations to each other in the original position means it is fair between individuals as moral persons with their own ends and capable of a sense of justice.
- Inference: The original position represents the fundamental agreements that would be made by free and equal persons whose relations are fair.
- Inference: Rawls's original position embodies the Kantian idea that principles are acceptable if they could be agreed to by free and equal persons in a fair initial situation.
