# Universal Facial Expressions of Emotion

## Summary

The theory that basic human emotions—anger, sadness, contempt, disgust, surprise, happiness, and fear—are expressed identically on faces across all cultures. Paul Ekman argued this based on studies with the Fore people of Papua New Guinea in the 1960s. However, subsequent research by Sergio Jarillo and Carlos Crivelli, using more rigorous free-choice methodology with the Trobriand Islanders, found that the case for universalism disappears. Lisa Feldman Barrett argues that 'emotions are made and not triggered,' with each person building culturally-specific 'operating instructions' for facial expression.

## Key facts

- Paul Ekman argued that emotion is necessarily expressed on the face and that emotional expressions are universal, based on studies with the Fore tribe in Papua New Guinea using 'forced choice' methodology. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p210]
- Ekman's original data showed that the Fore were only about half as good at correctly identifying 'fear' faces, with 45 percent thinking the surprised face was fearful and 56 percent reading sadness as anger—statistics Gladwell questions as evidence of universalism. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]
- Researchers Sergio Jarillo and Carlos Crivelli replicated Ekman's study but used free-choice methodology with the Trobriand Islanders and found that 'when you redo Ekman's foundational experiment—only this time, carefully and rigorously—the case for universalism disappears.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]
- Lisa Feldman Barrett argues 'emotions are made and not triggered,' stating that each person builds their own culturally-specific set of operating instructions for their face. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]
- Crivelli noted that many universalists were 'born into a world obsessed with human difference' and were 'powerfully drawn to a theory that maintained we are all the same.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]

## Inferences

- Inference: The face may be 'a symbol of how different human beings are, not how similar we are,' creating problems for societies that rely on reading faces to understand strangers.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which Ekman's contributions to understanding emotion remain valuable even if universalism is rejected, since Crivelli noted that 'everyone in the field of human emotion is in some crucial sense standing on his shoulders.'
