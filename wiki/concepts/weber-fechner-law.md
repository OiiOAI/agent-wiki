# Weber-Fechner Law

## Summary

The Weber-Fechner law describes the mathematical relationship between sensory stimulus intensity and perceived magnitude. Llinás invokes this law to argue that qualia operate on a logarithmic geometric basis, dividing sensory experience into discretely perceived events based on constant-ratio progressions.

## Key facts

- The Weber-Fechner law states: s = k·ln A/Ao, where s is sensory experience, k is a proportionality constant, ln is the natural logarithm, A is sensory activation, and Ao is the threshold level of activation. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p219]
- As the amplitude of sensory activation increases, sensory experience increases in a constant ratio, in a geometrical progression based on the value of e (2.17). [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p219]
- Musical pitch perception exemplifies this: an octave corresponds to twice the frequency, two octaves to four times, three octaves to eight times—a logarithmic relationship regardless of starting note. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p219]
- The seven-tone structure of Western music and the seven color bands of a rainbow suggest seven may be a pervasive demarcating number in sensory experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p220]
- Weber concluded that we perceive not the difference between things but the ratio of this difference to the magnitude of things compared, exemplified by needing 110g to detect difference from 100g but 220g to detect from 200g. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p325]
- Fechner calculated mathematically that sensation is a logarithmic function of physical intensity: S = k ln I + C, so when stimulus strength multiplies, sensation adds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p326]
- The Weber-Fechner law applies to vision, hearing, taste, distance perception, time perception, reaction time, intersaccadic intervals, and short-term memory error accumulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p326]
- The log-normal distribution of the brain's mesoscopic connectome and log rule-dominated dynamics may explain the systematic patterns of subjective perception described by Weber-Fechner. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p327]
- Under the Weber-Fechner law, perceived quantities follow a logarithmic rather than linear relationship to actual magnitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- Brain imaging studies show that number estimation activates the parietal cortical area, the same region involved in spatial and geometrical dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- Electrophysiological studies in rhesus monkeys demonstrate that neuronal firing obeys a log rule of quantity representation, conforming to a Gaussian curve when plotted against log number of items. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p373]
- The Weber-Fechner law applies to abstract dimensions including sizes, distances, owned territory, intervals, durations, and symbolic representation of quantities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p355]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Weber-Fechner law's generality suggests common neuronal mechanisms underlie diverse sensory and cognitive domains.
- Inference: Log-normal brain organization supports the logarithmic perceptual scaling described by Weber-Fechner.
