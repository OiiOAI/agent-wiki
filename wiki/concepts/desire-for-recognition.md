# Desire for Recognition

## Summary

The desire for recognition is the universal human wish to be acknowledged by others. Adlerian psychology denies that this desire should be a guiding principle, arguing that living to satisfy others' expectations means living other people's lives. Instead, individuals should recognize that they are not living to satisfy others' expectations, nor should others live to satisfy theirs. This is linked to the concept of separation of tasks.

## Key facts

- Adlerian psychology states: 'There is no need to be recognized by others. Actually, one must not seek recognition.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p114]
- When one seeks recognition and concerns oneself only with being judged by others, one ends up living other people's lives. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p118]
- Adler was critical of reward-and-punishment education because it creates people who think 'If no one is going to praise me, I won't take appropriate action.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p139]
