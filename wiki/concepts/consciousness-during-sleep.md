# Consciousness during sleep

## Summary

Unlike wakefulness where consciousness is continuous, sleep is characterized by alternating presence and absence of conscious experiences. This within-state variation in consciousness allows direct comparison of neural activity between conscious and unconscious states within identical behavioral conditions. The study demonstrates that consciousness during sleep requires localized activation of the posterior cortical hot zone, independent of global EEG patterns characteristic of REM or NREM sleep.

## Key facts

- Consciousness never fades during wakefulness but can fade during sleep; subjects report no experience up to 30% of awakenings early in the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p2]
- The paradoxical occurrence of both presence and absence of experiences within the same behavioral sleep state challenges traditional understanding of neural correlates of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p2]
- Dreaming can occur in two behavioral states that differ radically in global EEG signatures (NREM with slow waves vs. REM with wake-like activation). [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p6]
- Low-frequency EEG activity (1-4 Hz) during sleep reflects neuronal down-states and bistability that prevent stable causal interactions among cortical areas, linked to loss of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]

## Inferences

- Inference: Consciousness during sleep may serve as a valuable model for studying consciousness more broadly, avoiding confounds present in between-state comparisons (wake vs. sleep/anesthesia/coma)
