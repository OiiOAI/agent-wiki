# Nernst Equation

## Summary

The Nernst equation calculates the equilibrium potential for an ion species based on its concentration gradient across the membrane. It defines the voltage at which the chemical and electrical driving forces on an ion are equal and opposite, resulting in no net ion flux.

## Key facts

- The Nernst equation: Ex = (RT/zF) ln([X]o/[X]i), where z is ion valence, R is gas constant, T is temperature, F is Faraday constant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p241]
- At 25°C, Ex = (58 mV/z) log10([X]o/[X]i). For K+ with [K+]i=400mM and [K+]o=20mM: EK = −75 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p241]
- Equilibrium potentials: EK ≈ −75 mV, ENa ≈ +55 mV, ECl ≈ −60 mV in squid giant axon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p241]
- The Nernst equation is: Ex = (RT/zF) ln([X]o/[X]i), where R is the gas constant, T is temperature in Kelvin, z is ion valence, F is the Faraday constant, and [X]o and [X]i are external and internal concentrations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p182]
- At 25°C, the Nernst equation simplifies to: Ex = (58 mV/z) log([X]o/[X]i). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p182]
- For K+ with concentrations [20] outside and [400] inside (squid axon), EK = 58 mV log(20/400) = −75 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p182]
