# Antithetical Meaning of Primal Words

## Summary

A philological-psychoanalytic concept based on Karl Abel's research showing that ancient languages like Egyptian contained words with opposite meanings simultaneously. Freud connected this to the dream-work's tendency to represent contraries indistinguishably, viewing it as evidence of the archaic, regressive character of dream expression.

## Key facts

- Karl Abel demonstrated in 1884 that Egyptian words such as 'ken' meant both 'strong' and 'weak,' representing a primitive stage of language where opposites were undifferentiated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3589]
- Freud argued that the dream-work's tendency to disregard negation and combine contraries mirrors the linguistic practice of primal words. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3588]
- Examples in Indo-European languages include Latin 'altus' meaning both 'high' and 'deep,' and 'sacer' meaning both 'sacred' and 'accursed.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3592]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud connected primitive language characteristics to dream-work mechanisms, suggesting both represent survivals of archaic mental processes.
- Inference: The reversal of sound in words (metathesis) parallels the dream-work's reversal of representational material.
