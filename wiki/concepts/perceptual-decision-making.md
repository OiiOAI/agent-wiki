# Perceptual Decision-Making

## Summary

Perceptual decision-making is the process of selecting one option from alternatives based on evaluation and integration of sensory information. Research across species (rodents, monkeys, humans) has established that this process involves a cascade of neural events where sensory input is encoded by modality-selective areas, then passed to decision centers that compare outputs through gradual evidence accumulation. Multiple factors modulate this process including prestimulus state, reward/punishment, speed-accuracy trade-off, learning, confidence, and neuromodulation.

## Key facts

- Perceptual decisions involve an integrative mechanism whereby sensory evidence supporting alternatives accumulates over time to a preset internal decision boundary. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p137]
- In perceptual decisions, the buildup rate of EEG activity correlates with drift rate estimates from diffusion model analysis and predicts participant choice accuracy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p138]
- A brain region in the posterior portion of the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex uniquely correlates with the difference between outputs of face- and house-responsive regions and predicts behavioral performance in categorization tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p138]
- Prestimulus oscillatory activity in the alpha-frequency band is negatively correlated with discrimination ability, such that higher alpha power predicts decreased performance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p139]
- Punishment levels parametrically affect the buildup rate of decision-related EEG activity, with higher punishment leading to steeper evidence integration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p140]
- In perceptual decision-making, reward and punishment can directly modulate the dynamics of the decision process itself through adjustments to decision boundaries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p141]
- Choice confidence emerges from the process of decision formation, with accumulation rate predictive of confidence on a trial-by-trial basis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p143]
- Human observers optimize perceptual decisions via sequential sampling, but information can be lost during integration when capacity is limited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p152]
- In the Random Dot Motion task, subjects must indicate which of two targets reflects the coherent direction of motion by executing an eye movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p428]
- The random-walk model implements a sequential probability ratio test, which is optimal for the type of inference problem faced in the RDM task. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p429]
- Random-walk models predict a logistic relationship between choice and stimulus coherence, right-skewed reaction time distributions, and longer reaction times for error trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p429]
- Perceptual decision making involves choices between different interpretations of ambiguous sensory stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p414]
- In perceptual decisions, there is uncertainty about the probability of outcomes that must be estimated using sensory information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p416]
- Microstimulation of MT cells encoding for a particular direction of motion biases perceptual judgments in favor of that direction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p415]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perceptual decision-making shares computational principles across sensory modalities and species.
- Inference: The influence of decision modulators (prestimulus state, reward, punishment) can be understood within the framework of reward maximization, where the perceptual system adaptively optimizes behavior.
- Inference: The success of the random-walk model in accounting for both behavioral and neural data suggests that similar accumulator mechanisms may underlie value-based decisions more broadly.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How perceptual decisions are biased by prior information and whether optimal (Bayesian) integration is achieved remains debated.
- Uncertain: The contribution of urgency signals versus collapsing bounds to decision termination is unresolved.
- Uncertain: Current models only apply to two-alternative forced choice tasks; it is not yet clear how to adapt them to more complex decision scenarios with multiple options.
