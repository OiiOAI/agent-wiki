# The Business Development Process

## Summary

The Business Development Process is the systematic approach to building an extraordinary business, consisting of three integrated activities: Innovation (doing new things), Quantification (measuring what is done), and Orchestration (ensuring consistent delivery). This process, developed at E-Myth Worldwide, is designed to transform any small business into an effective organization.

## Key facts

- The Business Development Process is built on three distinct yet integrated activities: Innovation, Quantification, and Orchestration. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p117]
- Innovation is not creativity but doing new things—aiming innovative energies at the way the business does business rather than at the commodity it sells. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p118]
- The entire process by which the business does business is a marketing tool, a mechanism for finding and keeping customers. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p118]
- Innovation, Quantification, and Orchestration are the backbone of every extraordinary business and the essence of the Business Development Process. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p133]
- The Business Development Process is dynamic because the world will not tolerate a stationary object and will eventually destroy whatever you have created. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p133]
- The process enables you to preempt the world's changes, hopefully precede them, anticipate them, and at minimum remain infinitely flexible in relationship to them. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p133]
- The process has seven distinct steps: Primary Aim, Strategic Objective, Organizational Strategy, Management Strategy, People Strategy, Marketing Strategy, and Systems Strategy. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p135]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Business Development Process serves as both a practical business framework and a metaphor for personal transformation.
- Inference: The seven steps flow from personal vision (Primary Aim) through business strategy (Strategic Objective) to implementation across organizational, management, people, marketing, and systems dimensions.
