# Probabilities of Causation

## Summary

Probabilities of causation refer to formal measures for quantifying causal relationships, including PN (Probability of Necessity), PS (Probability of Sufficiency), and PNS (Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency). These concepts enable the evaluation of counterfactual queries in legal and scientific settings.

## Key facts

- The framework for counterfactuals as structural equation derivatives was used to estimate probabilities of causation in legal settings. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3799-L3800]
- PN, PS, and PNS are discussed based on Tian and Pearl (2000) and Pearl (2009, Chapter 9). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3812-L3813]
- Legal questions of attribution and probabilities of causation are discussed at length in Greenland (1999). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3811-L3812]
- Hannart et al. (2016) invoke the ideas of necessary and sufficient probability for attribution of weather and climate-related events. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3859-L3861]
