# Brain Map Dedifferentiation

## Summary

Brain map dedifferentiation occurs when two brain maps meant to be separate merge because their neurons fire together and hence wire together. This 'brain trap' can occur in musicians who use two fingers together frequently, in patients who fuse sexual and aggressive feelings, and in Japanese speakers who cannot distinguish English 'r' and 'l' sounds. Treatment involves relearning how to use the maps separately.

## Key facts

- Merzenich discovered that if a monkey's fingers are sewn together and forced to move at the same time, the maps for them will fuse because their neurons fire together and wire together. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p130]
- When a musician uses two fingers together frequently enough, the maps for the two fingers sometimes fuse, and when the musician tries to move only one finger, the other moves too. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p130]
- This merged map condition is called focal dystonia; the harder the person tries to get out of the brain trap, the deeper he gets into it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p131]
- Japanese people who speak English can't hear the difference between 'r' and 'l' because the two sounds are not differentiated in their brain maps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p131]
- Nancy Byl teaches people who can't control their fingers to redifferentiate their finger maps by relearning how to use their hands the way they did as babies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p131]
- The treatment for fused maps involves stopping the activity that caused the fusion to weaken the merged map, then relearning each movement separately. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: Brain map dedifferentiation explains why some psychological symptoms worsen with effort to change - the individual is inadvertently reinforcing the problematic merged network.
- Inference: The treatment approach of relearning behaviors in simplified, isolated forms may apply to psychological symptoms involving fused emotional brain maps.
