# Precision Optimisation

## Summary

Precision optimisation is the mechanism by which the brain adjusts confidence levels (precision values) in prediction errors. It is the third way to minimise free energy, alongside action and perception. This process is associated with arousal and neuromodulation, and Solms proposes that it constitutes the neural basis of consciousness itself.

## Key facts

- Precision is how the brain represents its degree of confidence in a given source of sensory evidence or predicted action consequences. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p186]
- There are three ways for a self-evidencing system to minimise free energy: action, perception, and precision optimisation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p192]
- Precision optimisation is associated with neuromodulatory mechanisms that generate altered states of consciousness, dreams, and are targeted by psychotropic drugs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p193]
- A precise signal is a 'loud' one with greater chance of updating the generative model. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p185]
- Precision optimisation enables flexible prioritisation of multiple needs on a contextual basis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]
- Precision weightings must be treated as categorical variables differentiated on a qualitative rather than quantitative basis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]
- Only systems that can compartmentalise different error values and flexibly modulate their precision weightings are likely to survive. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]
- Solms predicts that the precision optimisation mechanism constitutes the neural correlate of consciousness in the artificial system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p263]
- Different quantum of externally available energy could vary in relation to thermoregulatory and fatigue thresholds, requiring different optimal precision weightings during different epochs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]

## Inferences

- Inference: Precision optimisation is the statistical-mechanical basis of signal prioritisation—the critical output of the midbrain decision triangle and reticular activating system, bringing the most salient need to affective awareness.
- Inference: Precision optimisation corresponds to what Freud called 'cathectic innervations' and what neuroscientists call neuromodulation, arousal, or post-synaptic gain.
