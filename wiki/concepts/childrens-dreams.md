# Children's Dreams

## Summary

Children's dreams occur typically between the beginning of observable mental activity and the fourth or fifth year. They are characterized as short, clear, coherent, easy to understand, and without distortion. Freud uses them to demonstrate the essential nature of dreams: they are wish fulfillments arising from mental stimuli from the previous day.

## Key facts

- Children's dreams occur typically between the beginning of observable mental activity and the fourth or fifth year, though some children's dreams from ages five to eight show adult-type distortion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4901]
- Children's dreams are short, clear, coherent, easy to understand and unambiguous, and require no interpretative technique. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4901]
- A child's dream is a reaction to an experience of the previous day that has left behind a regret, a longing, or an unfulfilled wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4903]
- Children's dreams produce a direct, undisguised fulfillment of the wish; here the manifest and latent dream coincide. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4903]
- Dreams are not senseless in children; they are intelligible, completely valid mental acts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4902]
- The function of dreams is to get rid of (psychical) stimuli disturbing sleep by the method of hallucinatory satisfaction; Freud calls dreams 'guardians of sleep'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4903-4904]
- Two concurrent purposes operate in dreams: the desire for sleep (constant) and the wish to satisfy a psychical stimulus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4904]
