# Tactical Empathy

## Summary

Tactical Empathy is a negotiation technique combining emotional intelligence with assertive influence to understand and connect with another person's perspective. It involves recognizing the counterpart's feelings and mindset, vocalizing that recognition, and using that understanding to increase influence in subsequent interactions.

## Key facts

- Tactical empathy is defined as 'understanding the feelings and mindset of another in the moment and also hearing what is behind those feelings so you increase your influence'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p49]
- Empathy differs from sympathy; it means understanding another person's perspective without agreeing with their values or beliefs. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p49]
- The relationship between an emotionally intelligent negotiator and their counterpart is essentially therapeutic, duplicating the psychotherapist-patient dynamic. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p47]
- Research at Princeton University found that neural resonance—brain alignment with another person—disappears when people communicate poorly, and good listeners can predict what a speaker is about to say. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p50]
- Tactical empathy was used successfully during a 1998 Harlem standoff where Voss spoke through a door for six hours using the late-night FM DJ voice, resulting in three armed fugitives surrendering without incident. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p48]
