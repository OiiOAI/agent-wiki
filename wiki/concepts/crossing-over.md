# Crossing Over

## Summary

Crossing over is the process during meiosis where bits of paternal and maternal chromosomes physically detach and exchange places with each other. This process of shuffling genetic material means that any one chromosome in a sperm or egg is a mosaic of maternal and paternal genes. Crossing over is crucial to Dawkins' argument because it allows genes on the same chromosome to be inherited together, forming stable cooperating units.

## Key facts

- During meiosis, bits of each paternal chromosome physically detach themselves and exchange places with exactly corresponding bits of the maternal chromosome. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p53]
- Because of crossing over, any one chromosome in a sperm would be a patchwork, a mosaic of maternal genes and paternal genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p54]
- Crossing over does not respect boundaries between cistrons; splits may occur within cistrons as well as between them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p54]
- The shorter a genetic unit is, the less likely it is to be split by any one crossing-over, and the longer it is likely to live in generations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p55]
