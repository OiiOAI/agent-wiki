# The Real Problem

## Summary

The Real Problem is Seth's alternative framing to Chalmers' hard problem of consciousness. Rather than asking why consciousness exists at all, the Real Problem focuses on explaining, predicting, and controlling the phenomenological properties of conscious experience in terms of physical mechanisms in brains and bodies. It accepts that consciousness exists and seeks to explain why particular conscious experiences have the specific qualities they do.

## Key facts

- The Real Problem approach focuses on explaining why particular conscious experiences have the phenomenological properties they have, rather than explaining why consciousness exists at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p30]
- The Real Problem approach requires explaining, predicting, and controlling phenomenological properties in terms of mechanisms in the brain and body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p30]
- The Real Problem does not sweep subjective aspects of consciousness under the carpet, unlike approaches focused on function or behaviour alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p30]
- The Real Problem aligns naturally with a physicalist worldview on the relationship between matter and mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p30]
- The Real Problem approach parallels how biology moved beyond vitalism by focusing on explaining properties of life rather than why life exists. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p35]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Real Problem approach suggests that as explanatory bridges are built from physical mechanisms to phenomenological properties, the intuition of an explanatory gap may fade away.
- Inference: The distinction between Level, Content, and Self in consciousness maps onto the Real Problem's strategy of dividing the 'problem' into distinguishable processes.
