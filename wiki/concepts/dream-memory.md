# Dream Memory

## Summary

Dream memory exhibits distinctive characteristics that differ from waking memory, including hypermnesia for forgotten material, preference for indifferent impressions over significant events, and access to childhood experiences inaccessible in waking life.

## Key facts

- Delboeuf's lizard dream demonstrated that dreams have at their command memories inaccessible in waking life; he dreamed the Latin name 'Asplenium ruta muralis' which he later discovered he had written in a friend's herbarium two years earlier. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1294]
- Maury dreamed of a French town 'Mussidan' and correctly learned from a dream figure that it was in the department of Dordogne, confirmed later in a gazetteer. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1296]
- Freud noted that a striking feature of dream memory is that dreams select indifferent, insignificant material rather than important events from waking life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1300]
- Freud's own dream of a church tower at a station between Salzburg and Reichenhall proved to be from a journey in 1886, demonstrating childhood material surfacing in dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1297]
