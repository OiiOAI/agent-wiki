# Congruence of Right and Good

## Summary

The congruence problem asks whether the standpoint of justice and the standpoint of individual good are compatible—whether it is rational for persons in a well-ordered society to affirm their sense of justice as regulative of their plan of life. Rawls argues that congruence holds in justice as fairness but not in utilitarianism. The main reasons for congruence include: the connection between acting justly and natural attitudes; the Aristotelian Principle making participation in a well-ordered society a great good; the Kantian interpretation where acting justly expresses our nature as free and equal rational beings; and the fact that the desire to express our nature as moral persons can only be fulfilled by acting on principles of justice with first priority.

## Key facts

- The congruence question is whether it is rational for those in a well-ordered society to affirm their sense of justice as regulative of their plan of life. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p500]
- The connection between acting justly and natural attitudes shows that wanting to be fair with our friends is part of these affections as much as the desire to be with them. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p503]
- The Aristotelian Principle implies that participating in the life of a well-ordered society is a great good because it is a social union of social unions realizing to a preeminent degree the various forms of human activity. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p504]
- Acting justly is something we want to do as free and equal rational beings, and the desire to act justly and the desire to express our nature as free moral persons specify the same desire. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p505]
- Utilitarianism is more strict than common sense in demanding the sacrifice of the agent's private interests, making congruence less probable than in the contract theory. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p505]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Rawls acknowledges that in a well-ordered society there may be some persons for whom the affirmation of their sense of justice is not a good, given their particular aims and peculiarities of nature.
