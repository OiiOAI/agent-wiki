# Shifting the Burden to the Intervenor

## Summary

Shifting the burden to the intervenor (addiction or dependence) occurs when a solution to a systemic problem reduces symptoms but does nothing to solve the underlying problem. The intervention undermines the system's own capacity to maintain itself. As capability atrophies, more intervention is needed, creating a destructive reinforcing loop. Breaking addiction is painful but necessary. The best approach is avoiding the trap by strengthening the system's own ability to solve problems rather than taking over.

## Key facts

- The intervention can become a trap if it undermines the original capacity of the system to maintain itself. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p163]
- Examples: Social Security replacing family care for the aged, highways replacing railroads, calculators replacing mental arithmetic, vaccines replacing natural immunity. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p162]
- Addiction is finding a quick and dirty solution to the symptom which prevents or distracts from the harder task of solving the real problem. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p164]
- Breaking addiction may be painful but it allows confronting the real (deteriorated) state and taking necessary actions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p164]
- The best approach: help the system help itself by removing obstacles to natural correction mechanisms. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p165]
- Shifting the burden, dependence, and addiction arise when a solution reduces symptoms but does not solve the underlying problem. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p224]
- If the intervention causes the self-maintaining capacity of the original system to atrophy or erode, a destructive reinforcing feedback loop is set in motion. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p224]
- The system deteriorates; more and more of the solution is required, making the system increasingly dependent on the intervention and less able to maintain its own desired state. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p225]
- The remedy is to avoid getting into the trap: beware of symptom-relieving policies that don't address the real problem; focus on long-term restructuring rather than short-term relief. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p225]
- If you are the intervenor, work to restore or enhance the system's own ability to solve its problems, then remove yourself. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p225]
