# The Shadow

## Summary

The Shadow is the dark, repressed side of personality—containing insecurities, aggressive impulses, and selfish desires—that people conceal beneath their polite social exterior. Coined by Carl Jung, the Shadow operates unconsciously and leaks out in contradictory behavior, emotional outbursts, vehement denials, and projection onto others. Understanding the Shadow is essential for recognizing others' hidden motives and one's own destructive patterns.

## Key facts

- Jung described the Shadow as all the qualities people try to deny about themselves and repress, operating unconsciously with varying thickness depending on depth of repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p299]
- The Shadow becomes disturbed and active in moments of stress, when deep wounds and insecurities are triggered, and tends to emerge more as people age. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p300]
- Signs of Shadow release include: contradictory behavior that belies the constructed front, emotional outbursts where the Shadow speaks at face value, and 'accidental' behavior that fulfills repressed desires. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p302]
- Projection—attributing one's own repressed desires to others—is the most common way of dealing with the Shadow, often expressed as vehement condemnation of the very qualities one possesses. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p303]
- According to Freud, the only way something unpleasant reaches consciousness is through active denial—vehement denial of something often indicates its secret presence in the Shadow. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p302]
- Robert Louis Stevenson captured this dynamic in Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Hyde: 'My devil had long been caged, he came out roaring.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p306]
