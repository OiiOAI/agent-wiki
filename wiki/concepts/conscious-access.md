# Conscious Access

## Summary

Conscious access is the process by which attended information enters our awareness and becomes reportable to others. Dehaene argues this is the central phenomenon for scientific study of consciousness, distinct from vigilance (wakefulness) and attention (selection of information). When we lay eyes on an object, conscious access occurs after roughly half a second of processing, creating a bottleneck through which only one 'chunk' of information can pass at a time.

## Key facts

- Conscious access refers to information that enters awareness and becomes reportable to others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p19]
- Conscious access is deceptively trivial: seeing seems immediate, but an 'avalanche of brain activity' takes nearly half a second before consciousness kicks in. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p21]
- Conscious access creates a narrow bottleneck: at any given time, only about one conscious thought can be processed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p48]
- The term conscious access was coined to focus the scientific study of consciousness on a well-defined phenomenon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p19]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because conscious access is limited to one chunk at a time, all other competing information must wait in an unconscious buffer. [Supporting facts: p19, p48]
