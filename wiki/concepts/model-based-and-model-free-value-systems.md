# Model-based and Model-free Value Systems

## Summary

The brain employs two complementary systems for predicting the value of states and actions: model-based systems that use explicit representations of environmental structure, and model-free systems that cache values directly. These systems trade off computational complexity against learning complexity, with model-based approaches being flexible but computationally demanding, while model-free approaches are efficient but inflexible to motivational changes.

## Key facts

- Model-based systems construct a tree-based model of state-action-environment relationships to guide decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p170]
- Model-free systems cache Q-values representing the overall utility of particular states or actions without reference to a model of transitions or outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p172]
- Model-free cached values do not change with the motivational state of subjects without further learning, which is the defining characteristic of habits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p173]
- Model-based values can change directly when motivational state changes, while model-free cached values require statistically expensive relearning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p173]
- The dorsomedial striatum is associated with model-based processing, while the dorsolateral striatum is involved in representing model-free cached values. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: The competition between model-based and model-free systems may explain why humans sometimes behave inconsistently across situations that should elicit similar responses.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural instantiation of the model-based system for negative values is not completely known.
