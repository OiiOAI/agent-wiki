# Master Coaching

## Summary

Master coaching is a three-dimensional model for effectively teaching intuitive decision-making skills to others. The model emerged from research across music instructors, nurses, executives, and sports coaches, identifying what distinguishes effective coaches from ineffective ones.

## Key facts

- The first dimension is Assessing and Diagnosing: Master coaches pay careful attention to where a person is falling short and try to diagnose why this is happening, rather than jumping in with solutions. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p273]
- The second dimension is Tailoring Instruction: Teaching is more than lecturing; effective coaches have a large repertoire including demonstration with thinking aloud, discussing what could go wrong, letting trainees make mistakes, and asking open-ended questions. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p274]
- The third dimension is Setting the Climate: Master coaches create a respectful attitude rather than a punitive or evaluative one, approaching lessons as collaborations between teacher and student. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p277]
- Barriers to good coaching include being afraid of critiquing others, lack of time, difficulty describing subtle skills, and poor time management. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p270]
- The Navy program demonstrated that coaching skills can be taught and that preparing both coaches and trainees improves the effectiveness of the collaboration. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p281]

## Inferences

- Inference: Master coaching focuses on developing intuition rather than just teaching procedures, because procedures don't tell trainees which procedure to use, whether it's working properly, or how to modify it.
