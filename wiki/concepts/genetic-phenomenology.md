# Genetic Phenomenology

## Summary

Genetic phenomenology investigates how intentional structures and objects emerge through time, in contrast to static phenomenology which takes them as given. It focuses on how experience develops from inarticulate experience without clear subject-object structure. Key themes include the lived body (Leib), time-consciousness, affect, motivation, attention, and habit. Genetic phenomenology distinguishes between active genesis (deliberate productive activity) and passive genesis (involuntary formation of habits, motor patterns, associations, dispositions, motivations, emotions, and memories). At the level of passive synthesis, intentionality functions anonymously and involuntarily through an interplay of affective sensibility, motivation, and attention.

## Key facts

- Genetic phenomenology is concerned with how intentional structures and objects emerge through time; they cannot be taken as given. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p27]
- Genetic phenomenology takes as its point of departure the genesis of intentional experience in time, not the explicit correlational structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p38]
- What we experience is not a fixed given but something emergent out of previous experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p38]
- Genetic phenomenology distinguishes between active genesis (deliberate productive activity) and passive genesis (involuntary formation of habits, etc.). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p39]
- Passive genesis involves the lived body constituting itself and its environment through involuntary formation of habits, associations, and dispositions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p39]
- Husserl states that 'habit is not only our nurse, rather it is the function of consciousness that shapes and constantly further shapes the world.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p42]
