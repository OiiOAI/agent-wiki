# Wildland Firefighters (Best Teams Example)

## Summary

Wildland firefighters are presented as an example of highly effective teams in Klein's research. The U.S. Forest Service uses an incident command structure to manage large-scale fires, assembling teams of 4,000 firefighters from across the country within days. Their effectiveness derives from experience, sharpness, stability, promotion from within, and strong networks.

## Key facts

- Marvin Thordsen observed command staff assemble a team of 4,000 firefighters covering six mountains in Idaho in only a few days. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p289]
- Wildland firefighters get lots of first-hand experience fighting fires every season, unlike the military which does not fight many wars. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p289]
- Some strike team members fight forest fires twelve months a year, working the western states then traveling to Australia and New Zealand for their fire seasons. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p289]
- Core incident command team members work side by side every fire season for decades, providing stability not found in military systems where soldiers rotate assignments. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p290]
- Everyone starts at the bottom and moves up through the ranks, so line crews appreciate that leaders have been in their boots. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p290]
- The command staff met twice a day to make difficult decisions using fragmentary information about terrain, weather, wood characteristics, and bulldozer operator competence. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p290]
