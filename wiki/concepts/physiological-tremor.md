# Physiological Tremor

## Summary

Physiological tremor is an 8-12 Hz rhythmic discontinuity observed in voluntary movements, maintained posture, and supported limbs at rest. This periodic activity represents the reflection at the musculoskeletal level of a descending pulsatile control signal from supraspinal brain structures. The tremor is apparent even at rest and is highly associated with movement onset and direction—movements are not executed continuously but as a discontinuous series of muscle twitches.

## Key facts

- Physiological tremor is an 8-12 Hz periodic discontinuity observed in voluntary movement, maintained posture, and supported limbs at rest. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p36]
- The 8-12 Hz rhythmic discontinuities are not an inherent property of muscle tissue itself but reflect a descending command from the forebrain that is pulsatile in nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p37]
- E. A. Schafer documented in 1886 that voluntary muscular contraction shows rhythmic undulations at 8-12 Hz, representing the rhythm of muscular response to voluntary stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p34]
- The maximum rate for voluntary repetitive movement cannot exceed the rate of physiological tremor; Sherrington noted in 1910 that 'la' cannot be repeated more than 11 times per second. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p35]
