# Cytoskeleton

## Summary

The cytoskeleton determines cell shape and is responsible for the asymmetric distribution of organelles within the cytoplasm. It includes three filamentous structures: microtubules, neurofilaments, and microfilaments, which together account for approximately a quarter of the total protein in the cell.

## Key facts

- The cytoskeleton includes three filamentous structures: microtubules, neurofilaments, and microfilaments, which account for approximately a quarter of the total protein in the cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p125]
- Microtubules form long scaffolds extending from one end of a neuron to the other, constructed of protofilaments made of alternating α- and β-tubulin subunits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p125]
- Neurofilaments are 10 nm in diameter and are the most abundant fibrillar component in axons, related to intermediate filaments of other cell types. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p126]
- Microfilaments are 3-7 nm in diameter and are composed of two strands of polymerized globular actin monomers, concentrated at the cell periphery. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p126]
- MAP2 is present in dendrites but not in axons, whereas tau and MAP3 are present in axons; this difference has implications for Alzheimer's disease pathology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p126]
