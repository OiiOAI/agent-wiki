# Local Protein Synthesis at Synapses

## Summary

Local protein synthesis at synapses is the process by which proteins are translated from messenger RNA directly at synaptic terminals rather than only in the cell body. This mechanism is essential for sustaining long-term synaptic changes and memory storage, with dormant mRNAs being activated at marked synapses by molecules like prion-like CPEB.

## Key facts

- Oswald Steward discovered in the early 1980s that some protein synthesis occurs locally at synapses, in addition to the vast majority taking place in the cell body. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p281]
- One function of local protein synthesis is to sustain the long-term strengthening of synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p281]
- When local protein synthesis was inhibited at a synapse, long-term facilitation began and new terminals grew, but after one day the growth regressed. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p281]
- Dormant messenger RNA molecules become activated only at appropriately marked synapses by prion-like CPEB. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p283]
