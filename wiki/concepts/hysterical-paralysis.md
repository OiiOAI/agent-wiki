# Hysterical Paralysis

## Summary

Hysterical paralysis is a motor paralysis that simulates organic cerebral paralysis but differs from it in important ways. Freud distinguished it from organic representation paralysis by showing it has greater capacity for dissociation and isolation, can be more intense, and follows popular rather than anatomical conceptions of the body.

## Key facts

- Hysteria never simulates periphero-spinal or projection paralyses; hysterical paralyses only share characteristics of organic representation paralyses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p189]
- Hysterical paralysis is characterized by precise limitation and excessive intensity possessed simultaneously, unlike organic cerebral paralysis where these characteristics are not associated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p191]
- In hysteria, the proximal portion of a limb does not enjoy the privilege of exemption that organic hemiplegics have, so the leg must be dragged inertly as a whole. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p190]
- The lesion in hysterical paralysis consists in the inaccessibility of the organ or function to the associations of the conscious ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p197]
- Hysteria is ignorant of the distribution of nerves and has no knowledge of the optic chiasma, consequently not producing hemianopsia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p196]
