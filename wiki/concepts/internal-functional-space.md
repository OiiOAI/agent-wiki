# Internal Functional Space

## Summary

Internal functional space is the conceptual area of transformation between sensory input and motor output within the brain. It must be homomorphic with the external world—preserving conceptual continuity between sensory-derived properties and motor output. The properties of this internal space and the properties of the external world are not the same, yet for useful motor output, there must be continuity of similarity. This space is generated by the intrinsic electrical activity of neurons and their complex connectivity.

## Key facts

- The internal functional space is the area of transformation between sensory input and motor output; it must be homomorphic with the external world to preserve conceptual continuity. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p69]
- The brain acts primarily as a reality emulator rather than a simple translator of sensory input, generating predictive images of events to come that cause creatures to react or behave accordingly. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p60]
- The intrinsic electrical activity of the brain's elements generates internal representations that engender functional states specified in detail but not in context by incoming sensory activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p18]
