# Astrocyte

## Summary

Astrocytes are star-shaped glial cells found in all areas of the brain that play important roles in neuronal function including K+ buffering, neurotransmitter regulation, synapse support, and blood-brain barrier maintenance.

## Key facts

- Astrocytes are star-shaped glia that constitute nearly half the number of brain cells and have large numbers of thin processes that enfold all blood vessels of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p140]
- Astrocytes regulate extracellular K+ by acting as spatial buffers, taking up K+ at sites of neuronal activity and releasing it at distant contacts with blood vessels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p146]
- Astrocytes clear neurotransmitter glutamate from the synaptic cleft via high-affinity transporters, converting it to glutamine which is returned to neurons as a glutamate precursor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p146]
- Astrocytes secrete thrombospondins that promote the formation of new synapses and release neurotrophic factors that promote neuronal development and survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p146]
