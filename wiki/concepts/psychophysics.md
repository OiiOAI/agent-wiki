# Psychophysics

## Summary

Psychophysics is the branch of psychology and neuroscience that quantifies the relationship between physical characteristics of stimuli and the attributes of sensory experience. It uses experimental methods to measure detection thresholds, discrimination, and the mathematical laws relating stimulus magnitude to perceived intensity.

## Key facts

- Stevens's law states that the subjective intensity (I) of a sensation equals a constant K times the stimulus magnitude (S minus threshold S0) raised to a power n: I = K(S - S0)^n. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p387]
- The psychometric function plots the percentage of stimuli detected as a function of stimulus amplitude; by convention, threshold is defined as the stimulus detected on 50% of trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p388]
- Sensory thresholds serve dual purposes: reducing unwanted responses to noise and introducing nonlinearity that aids encoding and processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p388]
- Weber demonstrated that sensitivity to differences in intensity depends on absolute stimulus strength, formalized as ∆S = K·S where K is the just noticeable difference constant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p502]
- Fechner's law (I = K log(S/S₀)) describes the relationship between stimulus strength and sensation intensity, though Fechner's assumption that sensation equals the sum of just noticeable differences was later found incorrect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
- Stevens demonstrated that subjective experience of sensation intensity is best described by a power function rather than logarithmic relationship over extended ranges. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
- Psychometric functions plot the percentage of stimuli detected as a function of stimulus magnitude, with threshold conventionally defined as stimulus detected in 50% of trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
- Stevens introduced four scales of measurement: nominal (named categories), ordinal (ranked), interval (meaningful distances), and ratio scales (where zero has meaning). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p504]
