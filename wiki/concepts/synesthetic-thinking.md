# Synesthetic Thinking

## Summary

Synesthetic thinking refers to the fusion of sensory experiences where one sense triggers impressions in another. In S.'s case, sounds became fused with colors and tastes, each word triggered vivid images, and sensory impressions were not easily separable. This type of thinking both enabled S.'s extraordinary memory and created significant obstacles in understanding language, poetry, and abstract concepts.

## Key facts

- S. experienced sounds that became fused with a sense of color and taste, and each word triggered vivid, inextinguishable images. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p151]
- When S. imagined hearing a piercing sound, he evidenced a cochlear-pupil reflex, demonstrating involuntary physiological responses to imagined sensory experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p152]
- S. experienced taste sensations from words and sounds, reporting that when listening to music he felt the taste on his tongue. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p145]
- When stimulation of S.'s skin with Frey's filaments was applied, instead of a pointed sensation of touch he experienced waves that extended to encompass large areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p153]
