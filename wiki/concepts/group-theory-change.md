# Group Theory (in Context of Change)

## Summary

The authors use Group Theory from mathematics as a framework for understanding the relationship between persistence and change. A group has four properties relevant to this analysis: (a) any combination of members remains within the group, establishing invariance; (b) varying the sequence of operations does not change the outcome; (c) there is an identity member that combined with any other maintains that member's identity (zero change); (d) every member has a reciprocal whose combination produces the identity member. Group Theory cannot provide a model for second-order change (changes that transcend the system) but helps explain why 'more of the same' solutions fail to produce systemic change.

## Key facts

- Group property (a): any combination of group members gives again a group member, maintaining group structure and making it impossible for members to place themselves outside the system. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Group property (b): members can be combined in varying sequences yet the outcome remains the same (commutativity). [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Group property (c): the identity member (zero in addition, one in multiplication) combined with any member gives that member unchanged. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Group property (d): every member has a reciprocal whose combination with it gives the identity member, explaining why opposites often produce no net change within a system. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Groups are invariant only on the first-order change level but open to change on the second-order change level (changes in the rules governing their structure). [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
