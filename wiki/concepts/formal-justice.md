# Formal Justice

## Summary

Formal justice refers to the impartial and consistent administration of laws and institutions, whatever their substantive principles. It means that similar cases are treated similarly, the relevant similarities and differences being those identified by existing norms. The correct rule as defined by institutions is regularly adhered to and properly interpreted by authorities. Formal justice is adherence to principle or obedience to system. While important, formal justice is not a sufficient guarantee of substantive justice, which depends upon the principles in accordance with which the basic structure is framed.

## Key facts

- Formal justice is the impartial and consistent administration of laws and institutions, where similar cases are treated similarly according to existing norms. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p62]
- Formal justice requires that in their administration laws and institutions should apply equally to those belonging to the classes defined by them. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p61]
- Formal justice is adherence to principle, or obedience to system, as Sidgwick emphasized that this sort of equality is implied in the notion of a law or institution as a scheme of general rules. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p61]
- Formal justice is not a sufficient guarantee of substantive justice, which depends upon the principles in accordance with which the basic structure is framed. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p62]
- A slave or caste society could be evenly and consistently administered and still be substantively unjust. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p62]
