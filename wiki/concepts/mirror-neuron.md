# Mirror Neuron

## Summary

Mirror neurons are a class of neurons in the ventral premotor cortex (area F5) and inferior parietal cortex that discharge both when an individual performs a motor act and when they observe a similar act performed by another, providing a mechanism for understanding the actions and intentions of others through direct matching.

## Key facts

- Mirror neurons in area F5 of the ventral premotor cortex discharge both when a monkey performs a motor act and when it observes a similar act performed by another monkey or the experimenter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p940]
- Mirror neurons do not respond when a monkey simply observes an object or observes mimed arm and hand actions without a target object. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p940]
- Many area F5 mirror neurons respond selectively to the sound of specific actions such as ripping paper or cracking open a peanut, even without visual input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p940]
- Some mirror neurons in the rostral intraparietal lobule discharge more strongly when the monkey observes an act with a particular goal, such as grasping food to eat but not simply to move it, suggesting understanding of intentions behind observed actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p941]
- Defects in the human mirror-neuron system may contribute to some symptoms of autism, as autistic children lack the motor system activation during observation of actions that normally developing children exhibit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p941]

## Inferences

- Inference: The direct-matching hypothesis proposes that observation of others' actions activates motor circuits responsible for similar actions by the observer, providing a mechanism for understanding observed actions.
