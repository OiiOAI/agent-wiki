# Splitting of the Ego

## Summary

Splitting of the ego (Spaltung) refers to a phenomenon whereby the ego simultaneously holds two contradictory attitudes toward reality—one acknowledging a distressing reality and another disavowing it. This concept, developed by Freud particularly in relation to fetishism and psychosis, describes how the ego responds to intolerable psychical conflicts by accepting both the demand of reality and the drive's insistence on satisfaction, resulting in a permanent rift that never heals.

## Key facts

- The ego can respond to a conflict between drive demand and reality prohibition by producing two contrary reactions that are both valid and effective simultaneously. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7375]
- The outcome of splitting is a rift in the ego that never heals and increases as time goes on. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7375]
- Splitting of the ego occurs universally in neuroses, where one attitude belongs to the ego and the contrary repressed attitude belongs to the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7359]
- In fetishism, the patient both disavows the perception that females lack a penis and simultaneously recognizes this fact, producing two contrary premises that persist side by side throughout life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7358]
- Disavowals of reality are always half-measures and incomplete attempts at detachment from reality; they are always supplemented by an acknowledgement. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7359]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of ego splitting extends Freud's earlier structural model (id-ego-superego) by showing that the ego itself can be divided rather than functioning as a unified agency.
- Inference: The synthetic function of the ego, which Freud had emphasized, is now recognized as subject to particular conditions and disturbances.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms by which splitting is maintained over time remain inadequately specified in Freud's account.
