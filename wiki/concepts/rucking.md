# Rucking

## Summary

Rucking is the practice of hiking or walking at a fast pace while carrying a loaded pack on the back. It strengthens legs and trunk while providing cardiovascular benefits, and was introduced to Peter Attia by Michael Easter in his book The Comfort Crisis.

## Key facts

- Attia rucks three or four times per week for about an hour, typically with a 50-60 pound pack, climbing several hundred feet over three to four miles. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p265]
- A good goal is to be able to carry one-quarter to one-third of your body weight once you develop enough strength and stamina. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p265]
- Rucking downhill is excellent for eccentric strength and forces controlled descent, protecting knees. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p268]
- Michael Easter states: 'Carrying shaped our species. Our ancestors carried often. It gave them robust functional strength and endurance that was likely very protective.'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p265]
