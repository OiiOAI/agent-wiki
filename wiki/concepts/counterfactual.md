# Counterfactual

## Summary

A counterfactual is a conditional statement expressing what would have happened if some past event had not occurred. First formally articulated in David Hume's 1748 definition of causation, counterfactuals ask: 'If the first object had not been, would the second never have existed?' Counterfactual reasoning allows computation of actual values or probabilities for causal queries, enabling assessment of necessary and sufficient causes in contexts ranging from legal liability (e.g., whether a defendant's action was a necessary cause of injury) to scientific questions (e.g., whether climate change is a sufficient cause of specific weather events).

## Key facts

- Counterfactuals ask whether the effect would have occurred if the cause had not. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L254]
- The counterfactual definition of causation originated with Hume in 1748. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L254]
- Counterfactuals are now computable as actual values or probabilities using causal inference tools. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L257]
- Counterfactual queries can assess necessary and sufficient causes of observed events. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L257]
- Example applications include legal questions about whether a defendant's action was a necessary cause of injury, and scientific questions about whether climate change is a sufficient cause of heat waves. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L257]
- Counterfactuals are defined as derivatives of structural equations. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3799]
- The definition of counterfactuals as derivatives of structural equations was introduced by Balke and Pearl (1994a, 1994b). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3799]
- The framework connecting structural equations counterfactuals and Rubin's potential outcomes is logically equivalent to Lewis's modal logic framework. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3801-L3802]
- Recent books in social science (Morgan and Winship, 2015) and health science (VanderWeele, 2015) are adopting the hybrid graph-counterfactual approach. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3805-L3807]
- Missing-data problems should be viewed in the context of causal modeling according to Mohan and Pearl (2014). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3826-L3828]
