# Infantile Sexual Experiences

## Summary

Freud's theory that at the bottom of every case of hysteria there are one or more occurrences of premature sexual experience belonging to the earliest years of childhood. These experiences serve as the fundamental precondition or disposition for hysteria, creating symptoms but only exercising pathogenic action later when aroused after puberty in the form of unconscious memories.

## Key facts

- Freud proposes that at the bottom of every case of hysteria there are one or more occurrences of premature sexual experience belonging to the earliest years of childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1110]
- These infantile sexual experiences remain without effect to begin with and only exercise a pathogenic action later when they are aroused after puberty in the form of unconscious memories. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1115]
- The experiences that are effective have at first no result; it is their memory that produces the pathogenic effect, not the actual experience itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1118]
- Children cannot find their way to acts of sexual aggression unless they have been seduced previously, suggesting that adults lay the foundation for neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1112]
- The age of eight forms a boundary line for hysteria; after this time, a person who has not had sexual experiences earlier can no longer become disposed to hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1117]

## Inferences

- Inference: The requirement that experiences must occur during a specific developmental period suggests that the psychological apparatus must be at a particular stage of maturation for the experience to become pathogenic when recalled.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: What specifically decides whether memories become conscious or remain unconscious—whether content, timing, or later influences.
