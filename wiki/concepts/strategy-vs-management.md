# Strategy vs. Management

## Summary

The text distinguishes between strategy work and management work. Strategy work defines the goals and objectives to be sought, beginning with recognizing a challenge and understanding difficulties in overcoming it. It produces policies, actions, and objectives. Management work—accomplishing given objectives, also called execution—includes motivating and measuring performance. McNamara's failure in Vietnam illustrates this distinction: he was a skilled manager but his strategy work failed because managing by measuring progress (body counts) didn't work in a war of attrition. The text argues that 'driving results' in the absence of clear strategy is putting the cart before the horse, and that the Balanced Scorecard is a management tool designed to implement or execute a strategy, not to create one.

## Key facts

- Strategy work defines the goals and objectives to be sought and produces policies, actions, and objectives, while management work accomplishes given objectives. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p228]
- McNamara's failure in Vietnam came from applying management by objectives to a situation requiring strategy work, leading into a war of attrition and combat of wills. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p227]
- The Balanced Scorecard divides goals into four categories—financial, customer, internal process, and learning innovation—and is designed to manage or implement a strategy. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p230]
- The DelKha case showed the Balanced Scorecard was useful for managing a business but did not help in redefining the business or building a new business. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: Organizations need both strategy and management work, but confusing one for the other leads to failure.
