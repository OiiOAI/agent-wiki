# Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem

## Summary

Kurt Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem established that within any consistent formal system, there will be sentences that can neither be proved true nor proved false, and that the consistency of a formal system cannot be proved within that system. Boyd incorporated this theorem as evidence that there are fundamental limits to knowledge.

## Key facts

- Gödel established that within any consistent formal system, there will be a sentence that can neither be proved true nor proved false – any consistent system is incomplete. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p98]
- Gödel showed that even though a system is consistent, its consistency cannot be demonstrated within the system – a consistency proof requires appealing to systems outside that system. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p98]
- Boyd devoted two typed pages to Gödel in Destruction and Creation, noting that Gödel indirectly showed that to determine consistency of any new system, one must construct or uncover another system beyond it. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p98]
- Boyd saw Gödel's theorem, along with Heisenberg's uncertainty principle and the Second Law of Thermodynamics, as keys to understanding uncertainty and mismatches as fundamental parts of reality. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p89]
