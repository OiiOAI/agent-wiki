# Big Five personality traits

## Summary

The Big Five (or Five Factor Model) is the most widely accepted contemporary framework for personality classification. The five dimensions are Extraversion (sociability, assertiveness), Neuroticism (emotional volatility, sensitivity to negative emotions), Conscientiousness (self-control, orderliness), Agreeableness (cooperativeness, concern for social harmony), and Openness to Experience (curiosity, aesthetic sensibility). Each reflects multiple subfacets of behavior that correlate across large populations. These traits are moderately heritable (30-60% of variance) but show minimal influence from shared family environment.

## Key facts

- The Big Five dimensions are Extraversion, Neuroticism, Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, and Openness to Experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p230]
- Each trait reflects multiple subfacets that correlate across individuals, suggesting an underlying latent factor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p230]
- Personality traits are moderately heritable (30-60% of variance) but shared family environment explains very little (0-10%). [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p232]
- Big Five traits likely reflect multiple underlying biological parameters (reward sensitivity, threat sensitivity, novelty salience, confidence thresholds, delay discounting) rather than a singular neural substrate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p232]
- Two people may have similar Extraversion scores for quite different underlying reasons based on their profile across many lower-level parameters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: The lack of reliable brain correlates for Big Five traits suggests they are statistical constructs rather than unitary biological entities.
- Inference: Personality traits influence behavioral tendencies but do not determine behavior in any specific situation - actual behavior depends on the particular context and goals.
