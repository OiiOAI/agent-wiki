# Bad Strategy

## Summary

Bad strategy is an identifiable way of thinking and writing about strategy that is long on goals and short on policy or action. It assumes goals are all you need, puts forward strategic objectives that are incoherent, and uses high-sounding words to hide these failings. Bad strategy is not simply the absence of good strategy—it has a life and logic of its own.

## Key facts

- Bad strategy has four major hallmarks: fluff, failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, and bad strategic objectives. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- Fluff is a form of gibberish masquerading as strategic concepts, using inflated and unnecessarily abstruse words to create the illusion of high-level thinking. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- Bad strategy fails to recognize or define the challenge—when you cannot define the challenge, you cannot evaluate a strategy or improve it. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- Many bad strategies are just statements of desire rather than plans for overcoming obstacles. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- Bad strategy may actively avoid analyzing obstacles because a leader believes negative thoughts get in the way. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p15]
