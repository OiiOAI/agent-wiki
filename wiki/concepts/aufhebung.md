# Aufhebung (Hegelian Sublation)

## Summary

McGilchrist uses Hegel's concept of Aufhebung (sublation) to describe how the left hemisphere's contribution should be 'lifted up' by the right hemisphere. The term means something is simultaneously negated, preserved, and elevated. Like Hegel's example of the plant—where the bud is negated by the blossom and the blossom by the fruit, yet all remain necessary moments of the organic whole—the contributions of both hemispheres are preserved and transformed in synthesis rather than simply cancelled. What the left hemisphere offers is not lost but taken up into a higher level of understanding.

## Key facts

- Aufhebung literally means 'lifting up' and refers to how earlier stages of an organic process are superseded but not repudiated. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p288]
- Hegel illustrates Aufhebung with plant development: the bud disappears when the blossom breaks through, yet both are moments of an organic unity. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p289]
- In Aufhebung, the earlier stage is 'taken up into' the succeeding stage and preserved, transformed by a higher level of the process. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p289]
- What is offered by the right hemisphere to the left hemisphere is offered back again and taken up into a synthesis involving both hemispheres. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p291]
