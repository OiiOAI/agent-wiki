# The Symbolic Threshold

## Summary

The symbolic threshold is Deacon's term for the cognitive barrier that prevents nonhuman species from acquiring symbolic communication. Unlike the difficulty of learning complex grammatical rules, which could theoretically be simplified, the symbolic threshold represents an almost impenetrable barrier because it requires a fundamental reorganization of how reference is understood. This threshold is why even simple symbolic languages don't exist in nature, despite animals being capable of complex learned associations.

## Key facts

- The symbolic threshold represents the transition from indexical to symbolic reference, requiring cognitive capabilities beyond what is needed for learned associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p83]
- Even with vastly simplified languages, nonhuman species cannot cross the symbolic threshold because symbolic reference requires a fundamentally different interpretive process. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p41]
- Grammatical rules and syntactic structure are themselves symbolic rules and categories, requiring symbolic competence as a prerequisite. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p43]

## Inferences

- Inference: The symbolic threshold explains why attempts to teach language to nonhuman species have achieved only limited success—animals can learn complex associations but cannot grasp the arbitrary conventional nature of symbolic reference. [Based on: pp.51-52, 70-71]
