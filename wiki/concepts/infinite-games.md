# Infinite Games

## Summary

Infinite games are played for the purpose of continuing the play indefinitely. They have no temporal, spatial, or numerical boundaries and are internally defined. The rules of an infinite game must change during play to prevent anyone from winning and to bring more persons into the play. Infinite players are playful rather than serious, generate time rather than consume it, and play with boundaries rather than within them. They seek strength rather than power, remain open to surprise, and live toward the horizon rather than within fixed boundaries.

## Key facts

- An infinite game is played for the purpose of continuing the play, keeping everyone in play. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p13]
- Infinite players cannot say when their game began, nor do they care, because their game is not bounded by time. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p17]
- There are no spatial or numerical boundaries to an infinite game; anyone who wishes may play an infinite game. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p17]
- The rules of an infinite game must change in the course of play to prevent anyone from winning and to bring as many persons as possible into the play. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p21]
- The rules of an infinite game are like the grammar of a living language, always evolving to guarantee meaningfulness of discourse. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p21]
- Infinite players prepare themselves to be surprised by the future; if surprise is no longer possible, all play ceases. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p32]
- Infinite players die; since the boundaries of death are always part of the play, the infinite player does not die at the end of play but in the course of play. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p40]
