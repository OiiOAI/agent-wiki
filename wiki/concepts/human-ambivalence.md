# Human Ambivalence

## Summary

Human Ambivalence is a fundamental aspect of human nature where emotions are almost never pure and simple. People can feel love and hostility simultaneously, or admiration and envy, or dependence and resentment. This ambivalence begins in childhood—children feel both love and resentment toward parents who are attentive, or love and self-blame toward parents who are neglectful. As adults, the same ambivalence operates toward friends, intimate partners, and especially leaders, whom we unconsciously associate with parental figures. Strong, pure emotions feel frightening because they represent momentary loss of control, so we unconsciously balance them with contrary emotions. This fundamental complexity means we must look beyond simple interpretations of others' feelings and recognize the dual nature underlying all human emotional responses.

## Key facts

- It is a fundamental fact of human nature that emotions are almost always ambivalent, rarely pure and simple—we can feel love and hostility at the same time, or admiration and envy. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p555]
- Ambivalence toward leaders operates because people intuitively recognize the need for leaders to stand above competing interests while also fearing that those in power will abuse their privileges. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p556]
- We unconsciously balance strong emotions because pure emotions represent momentary loss of control and seem to negate our willpower. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p556]
- Children feeling dependent on parents unconsciously balance moments of anger and frustration with feelings of need and love, creating an ambivalent undertone that persists into adulthood. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p555]
- Leaders are rarely aware of underlying ambivalence because people show deference due to their personal fate being in leaders' hands, and they cannot afford to show true feelings. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p557]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept explains why people often cannot articulate why they suddenly lose respect for a leader—the ambivalence was always there, suppressed beneath surface expressions of loyalty.
