# Ticker Watching

## Summary

Ticker watching is the tendency to obsessively monitor short-term fluctuations while losing sight of long-term trajectory. Analogized to watching stock tickers moment-by-moment despite having made long-term investments, it causes emotional amplification of minor events and distorted perception of overall performance. The metaphor extends to life satisfaction, where people react to immediate setbacks as if they determine overall happiness.

## Key facts

- The Berkshire Hathaway stock charts illustrate how zooming in on daily fluctuations obscures the long-term upward trajectory, just as momentary setbacks distort perception of overall life happiness. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1630-L1640]
- Casino blackjack experiments demonstrate that emotional responses are path-dependent: winning $100 after losing $1000 feels worse than losing $100 after winning $1000, despite identical endpoints. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1621-L1628]
- The 'zoom lens' effect causes us to treat events that will have little long-term impact on happiness as highly significant, making decision-making reactive. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1640-L1642]
- Mental time travel pulls us out of ticker-watching mode by engaging past and future selves to maintain perspective on what truly matters to overall wellbeing. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1644-L1646]
