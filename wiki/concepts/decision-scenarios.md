# Decision Scenarios

## Summary

Decision scenarios, developed by Royal Dutch/Shell's Pierre Wack, are written and charted mental models designed to change how executives think about decisions. Unlike forecasts that provide answers, decision scenarios describe forces operating in a situation so executives can use their own judgment.

## Key facts

- Decision scenarios were built to describe the forces operating so executives could use their own judgment rather than be told what to do. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p103]
- The ideal number of scenarios was three, with the first corresponding to the mental model held by executives (dubbed the 'three-miracle scenario'), and the others showing different ways of seeing the world. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p103]
- Decision scenarios typically featured only a few variables, usually around three, and a few transitions, rarely more than five or six. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p103]
- Royal Dutch/Shell's scenario planning allowed executives to anticipate the 1973 oil crisis before it occurred, successfully abandoning their incremental strategy. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p103]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Shell scenario approach demonstrates how making mental simulations explicit can change how people respond to information, with executives responding more favorably to decision scenarios than to statistical forecasts.
