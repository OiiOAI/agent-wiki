# Deletion and Accretion of Structure

## Summary

Deletion and accretion refer to the progressive loss or gain of optical structure at occluding edges during occlusion. Deletion occurs when a surface goes behind another, losing structure in the optic array; accretion occurs when a surface emerges, gaining structure. These are the specific optical transitions that specify occlusion and distinguish obstacles from openings.

## Key facts

- Deletion always caused the perception of covering, and accretion always caused the perception of uncovering in Kaplan's experiment—surfaces going out of sight were never seen to go out of existence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p213]
- Loss of structure outside a closed contour during approach specifies an obstacle; gain of structure inside a closed contour during approach specifies an opening. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p251]
- The changing occlusion at an edge, not abrupt increases in texture density, is the effective information for detecting brinks and drop-offs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p251]

## Inferences

- Inference: This optical mechanism explains how the visual system distinguishes things from holes, surfaces from apertures—something the gestalt figure-ground phenomenon misleadingly obscures.
