# Temporal-Difference Prediction Error Theory of Dopamine

## Summary

The temporal-difference (TD) prediction error theory of dopamine posits that phasic firing of dopaminergic neurons in the ventral tegmental area (VTA) encodes a reward prediction error signal computed from diverse afferent inputs including the medial prefrontal cortex, nucleus accumbens shell, ventral pallidum, central nucleus of the amygdala, lateral hypothalamus, habenula, cholinergic pedunculopontine nucleus, serotoninergic raphe, and noradrenergic locus coeruleus. This signal is theoretically appropriate for controlling learning of both predictions and reward-optimizing actions.

## Key facts

- Dopaminergic neurons combine diverse afferents (including inputs from medial prefrontal cortex, nucleus accumbens shell, ventral pallidum, central nucleus of amygdala, lateral hypothalamus, habenula, cholinergic pedunculopontine nucleus, serotoninergic raphe, and noradrenergic locus coeruleus) to compute a temporal-difference reward-prediction error. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p328]
- Dopamine provides target areas with a neural signal theoretically appropriate for controlling learning of both predictions and reward-optimizing actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p328]
- Dopamine signal in VTA to ventral striatal and frontal target areas is used to train predictions (as in the critic), while similar signal in substantia nigra pars compacta to dorsal striatal target areas is used to learn an action-selection policy (as in the actor).
