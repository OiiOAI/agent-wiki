# Systems Thinking

## Summary

Systems thinking is an interdisciplinary approach to understanding phenomena by examining how components interact within wholes, rather than analyzing isolated parts. Emerging from biology and Gestalt psychology in the early 20th century, systems thinking challenges reductionist approaches by emphasizing emergent properties, circular causality, feedback loops, and the relationship between organization and environment. Key figures include Ludwig von Bertalanffy, who formulated General Systems Theory, and Fritjof Capra, who documented the paradigm shift from mechanical to systemic worldviews.

## Key facts

- Systems thinking emerged from the recognition that entities called 'systems'—consisting of parts in interaction—cannot be understood by analysis because the properties of parts are not intrinsic but only understandable within the larger whole. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p112]
- Bertalanffy noted that analytical procedure depends on two conditions: non-existent or weak interaction between parts, and linear relationships describing part behavior—conditions not fulfilled in systems. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p112]
- According to systems view, the essential properties of an organism or living system are properties of the whole which none of the parts have; they arise from interactions and relationships among parts. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p111]
- Capra identified five criteria of new-paradigm thinking: (1) relationship between part and whole, (2) shift from structure to process, (3) shift from objective to epistemic science, (4) shift to networks as knowledge metaphor, (5) shift from truth to approximate description. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p126]
- The term 'organized complexity' was introduced to describe phenomena exhibiting properties at each level of complexity that do not exist at lower levels. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p111]
- Boyd adopted the key insight that living systems are essentially open systems and need to remain open to exist and grow, employing the concept of 'organic whole.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p127]

## Inferences

- Inference: Systems thinking represented a fundamental paradigm shift from Cartesian/mechanistic to holistic/organic worldviews in 20th-century science.
- Inference: Boyd's OODA loop and strategic theory directly reflect systems thinking principles, particularly the emphasis on feedback, adaptation, and environmental interaction.
