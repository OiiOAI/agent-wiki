# The Moon of Carrots

## Summary

The Moon of Carrots represents the use of rewards (carrots) rather than punishment (sticks) to increase chances of success. Chimps enjoy eating carrots and these need to be big, while Humans work best with recognition and appreciation. Types of carrots include rewards (material things), celebrations (recognising effort), recognition (praise from the right person), encouragement (looking at benefits of achieving), and support (from the right person or people). Sticks include threats, punishment, and attacks. Beating yourself up and guilt, blame, and regret can become destructive sticks. In a civilised society the stick is replaced with carrots, benchmarks, and consequences.

## Key facts

- Chimps enjoy eating carrots but these need to be big otherwise the Chimp will not move; Humans work best when they receive recognition and appreciation for what they have done. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p176]
- Chimps work with violence, threats, and sticks; Humans work with compassion, encouragement, carrots, and consequences—using a stick on yourself is more destructive than others doing it to you. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p176]
- Once you have recognised guilt, blame, or regret and shown remorse and made amends, these negative emotions become a stick you beat yourself with for no good purpose and will destroy any chance of happiness or constructive living. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p178]
- An abusive reminder stick occurs when someone who has clearly shown remorse and change is repeatedly reminded of their previous misdemeavours by another person—this is a form of passive aggression and abuse. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p178]
- To bring out the best in yourself you need to make sure you are getting plenty of carrots; most Chimps within us do need recognition from the right person and if deprived of this they can become bitter, angry, and upset. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p177]
