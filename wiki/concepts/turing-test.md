# Turing Test

## Summary

The Turing test is a behaviourist test of intelligence requiring an observer to determine via verbal interaction whether they are communicating with a person or a machine. Solms critiques it as unsuitable for testing consciousness because it assesses intelligence and behaviour rather than subjective experience, and demonstrates its inadequacy using the ELIZA chatbot example.

## Key facts

- The Turing test requires interaction via computer monitor and a decision based on verbal statements alone whether the system has a mind; passing requires 30% of judges after five minutes to think they are interacting with a real mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p261]
- Solms argues the main problem with the Turing test is that it is a behaviourist test of intelligence, not a test of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p261]
- ELIZA is a simple chatbot simulating a psychotherapist that many people believe is a real person and find helpful, demonstrating that intelligent behaviour can be indistinguishable from human behaviour without consciousness being present. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p261]
- Solms notes the positive aspect of the Turing test is that it circumvents prejudice about whether 'mere machines' can be conscious. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p262]
- The problem of philosophical zombies shows that appearances can be misleading when it comes to consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p261]
- In a Turing test the examiner types questions on a keyboard and gets answers—from a person and a computer, each in another room—on a monitor; if the examiner cannot distinguish the computer from the person, the computer has passed the test. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p143]
- Turing proposed that asking whether a machine is able to think is analogous to asking whether it can replicate human behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p143]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The validity of Turing test results is debatable as they may depend not just on the complexity of the computer's algorithms but also on the ability of examiners to pose the right questions.
