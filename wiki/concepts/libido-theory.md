# Libido Theory

## Summary

Libido theory posits that libido is a quantitatively variable force serving as a measure of processes and transformations in sexual excitation. It is distinguished from other psychical energy by a qualitative character, suggesting a special chemistry of the sexual function. Ego libido becomes object libido when cathecting sexual objects, and when withdrawn from objects it becomes narcissistic ego libido again. The theory aims to express neurotic and psychotic phenomena in terms of the economics of libido.

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's libido theory served as the foundation for understanding all neurotic disorders, from 'actual' neuroses to severe psychoses
- Inference: The theory implies that pathogenic effects are primarily a prerogative of libidinal drives rather than ego drives
- Inference: The recognition that self-preservative drives are libidinal does not necessarily prove there are no other drives operating in the ego.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges uncertainty about whether the ego drives themselves may go astray as a primary fact in severe psychoses
