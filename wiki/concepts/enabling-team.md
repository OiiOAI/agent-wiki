# Enabling team

## Summary

An enabling team is composed of specialists in a given technical or product domain who help bridge capability gaps for stream-aligned teams. These teams cross-cut to stream-aligned teams and have the bandwidth to research, try out options, and make informed suggestions on tooling, practices, and ecosystem choices. Enabling teams have a strongly collaborative nature and actively avoid becoming 'ivory towers' of knowledge. Their end goal is to increase the autonomy of stream-aligned teams by growing their capabilities.

## Key facts

- Enabling teams are composed of specialists in a given technical or product domain who help bridge capability gaps for stream-aligned teams. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Enabling teams cross-cut to stream-aligned teams and have the required bandwidth to research, try out options, and make informed suggestions on adequate tooling, practices, frameworks, and ecosystem choices. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- The end goal of an enabling team is to increase the autonomy of stream-aligned teams by growing their capabilities with a focus on their problems first, not the solutions per se. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- An enabling team should plan for its own extinction from the very first day to avoid other teams becoming dependent. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Jutta Eckstein refers to enabling teams as 'Technical Consulting Teams' that provide guidance, not execution. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Stream-aligned teams should expect to work with enabling teams only for short periods of time (weeks or months) before the new skills are embedded. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]

## Inferences

- Inference: Enabling teams differ from communities of practice (CoP) in that members work on enabling activities full-time rather than meeting weekly or monthly.
