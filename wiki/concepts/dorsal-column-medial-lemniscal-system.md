# Dorsal Column–Medial Lemniscal System

## Summary

The dorsal column–medial lemniscal system transmits tactile and proprioceptive information from the body to the thalamus and cerebral cortex. Large myelinated Aα and Aβ fibers ascend ipsilaterally in the dorsal columns to the gracile and cuneate nuclei, then cross and ascend in the medial lemniscus.

## Key facts

- The dorsal column contains about one million fibers as it enters the brain stem, with 90% being axons of dorsal root ganglion cells transmitting tactile and proprioceptive information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p542]
- Below T7 the dorsal column is called the gracile fascicle; above T7 it divides into gracile and cuneate fascicles, terminating in anatomically distinct nuclei in the caudal brain stem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p542]
- Axons of dorsal column nuclei form the medial lemniscus, which crosses the midline in the medulla and terminates in the lateral and medial ventral posterior nuclei of the thalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p542]
- Cutaneous information enters the lateral and medial ventral posterior nuclei; proprioceptive information enters the superior ventral posterior nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p542]
