# Burning Bridges and Building New Ones

## Summary

A DBT distress tolerance strategy for addiction recovery that involves permanently eliminating access to addictive substances, people, and environments while simultaneously creating new supports and alternative behaviors. Part of the DCBA skills cluster.

## Key facts

- Burning bridges involves listing all things in one's life that make addiction possible and erasing or getting rid of them, including tempting people, websites, and contact information. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p409]
- Building new ones involves listing all the things that would make addiction impossible and actively implementing them as new behavioral supports. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p409]
- Strategies for battling addiction urges include keeping new imagery in mind when urges hit, looking at moving images, surrounding oneself with new smells, and urge-surfing. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p409]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'burning bridges' metaphor emphasizes the irreversible nature of the commitment, suggesting that going back to addiction should become physically and socially impossible.
