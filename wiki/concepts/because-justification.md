# Because Justification

## Summary

The word 'because' creates justification even when unnecessary. Ellen Langer's library experiment found that 'May I use the Xerox machine?' succeeded 60% of the time, but 'because I need to make copies' succeeded 93% — even though everyone was waiting to make copies.

## Key facts

- Ellen Langer found that 'Excuse me, I have five pages. May I use the Xerox machine?' succeeded 60% of the time, but adding 'because' increased success to 93-94% even with meaningless reasons. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p121]
- Using the simple validation 'because' is sufficient; a sign announcing 'We're renovating the highway for you' is redundant yet calms frustrated drivers. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p122]
