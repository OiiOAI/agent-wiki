# Observer Effect

## Summary

The observer effect (also known as audience effect) refers to the phenomenon where the mere presence of other people increases an individual's tendency to behave prosocially. People donate more and share more benefits when they know their decision is observed by others. This effect is mediated by reputation processing, where individuals are concerned with how they appear to others.

## Key facts

- The mere presence of other people increases an individual's tendency to behave prosocially, a phenomenon called the 'observer effect' (also known as 'audience effect'). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p200]
- People donate more money when they know their decision is observed by other people. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p200]
- Participants were more willing to share benefits in economic games if they believed that other people were able to know the participants' decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p200]
- The ventral striatum is commonly activated by both the concrete reward of money and the more abstract social reward of good reputation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p201]
- Children with autism spectrum disorders showed no increase in prosocial behavior when being watched, indicating insensitivity to social reputation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p202]
