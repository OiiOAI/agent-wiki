# Motivation Wave

## Summary

The Motivation Wave describes the predictable pattern where motivation spikes temporarily (often after a resolution or inspiring event) and then crashes down. This phenomenon explains why people overestimate their future motivation—during high-motivation states, people feel capable of doing difficult things and make ambitious plans, but motivation is unsustainable. The wave affects everything from New Year's resolutions to online courses (where less than 10% of enrollees finish) to health products that get abandoned after initial enthusiasm.

## Key facts

- The Motivation Wave is described as temporary surges in motivation that eventually crash down. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p58]
- Studies show that less than 10% of students complete online courses despite initial motivation. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p59]
- Motivation fluctuates day to day and even minute to minute, with predictable shifts like willpower decreasing from morning to evening. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p60]
- The Motivation Wave is listed as one of the Models in the Behavior Design framework. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p314]
- David Sobel suggested the name 'Motivation Wave' at one of Fogg's boot camps. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p311]
