# Spatial Summation

## Summary

Spatial summation is the process by which postsynaptic potentials produced by different presynaptic neurons at different locations on the postsynaptic cell are added together. The extent of summation depends on the length constant of the neuron, which determines how much the synaptic potential decreases as it spreads passively from the synapse to the trigger zone.

## Key facts

- The length constant of the cell determines the degree to which the EPSP decreases as it spreads passively from a synapse along the dendrite to the cell body and axon initial segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- In cells with a longer length constant, signals spread to the trigger zone with minimal decrement; in cells with a short length constant, the signals decay rapidly with distance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- Neurons with a large length constant are more likely to be brought to threshold by inputs arising from different sites than are neurons with a short length constant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- The depolarization produced by one synapse is almost never sufficient to trigger an action potential at the trigger zone, so inputs from many presynaptic neurons acting at different sites must be added together in spatial summation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
