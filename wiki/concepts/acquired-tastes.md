# Acquired Tastes

## Summary

Acquired tastes are by definition learned, unlike innate tastes such as milk, water, or sweets which are immediately perceived as pleasant. Acquired tastes are initially experienced with indifference or dislike but later become pleasant. Many delicacies that people pay dearly for must be 'developed' and were initially disgusting to the individual. The neuroplastic brain, once rewired, develops a new nature that is every bit as biological as the original.

## Key facts

- Acquired tastes are initially experienced with indifference or dislike but later become pleasant, unlike innate tastes which are immediately perceived as pleasant. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p110]
- Many delicacies that people pay dearly for, that they must 'develop a taste for,' are the very foods that disgusted them as children. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p110]
- In Elizabethan times lovers were so enamored of body odors that women kept peeled apples in their armpits to give to lovers as 'love apples.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p111]
- Cow urine, naturally repugnant to most, is used by the Masai tribe in East Africa as a hair lotion because of the cow's importance in their culture. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p111]
- Many tastes we think 'natural' are acquired through learning and become 'second nature' to us, indistinguishable from original nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of acquired tastes extends beyond food to include sexual preferences, which can be learned and become 'second nature.'
- Inference: Our neuroplastic brains, once rewired, develop new natures every bit as biological as original ones, making it impossible to distinguish second nature from original nature.
