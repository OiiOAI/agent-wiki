# Human Freedom in Relation to Nature

## Summary

Carse argues that human freedom is not freedom over nature but the freedom to be natural—to answer the spontaneity of nature with our own spontaneity. Though we are free to be natural, we are not free by nature; we are free by culture, by history. The paradox is that the more deeply a culture respects the indifference of nature, the more creatively it will call upon its own spontaneity in response. The contradiction is that the more vigorously we attempt to force nature's agreement with our designs, the more subject we are to its indifference. Nature has no outside and no inside; it cannot be used for or against itself. The use of agricultural poisons will arrest the spontaneity of living entities but it is not an unnatural act—nature has not been changed.
