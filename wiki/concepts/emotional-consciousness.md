# Emotional Consciousness

## Summary

Emotional consciousness, including feelings of fear and anxiety, is constructed in the brain from non-emotional ingredients assembled in working memory. Fear requires awareness of a present threat, while anxiety concerns threats that are uncertain or future-oriented. The nonconscious defensive state produced by the amygdala becomes a conscious feeling only when its consequences are monitored and interpreted through memory-based schemas. Autonoetic fear (fear as a personal self-aware experience) may be unique to humans.

## Key facts

- Emotional experiences like fear and anxiety are psychologically constructed states assembled from non-emotional ingredients in working memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p319]
- Fear requires: (1) a sensory representation of a threat, (2) amygdala activation of defensive circuits, (3) attention/working memory, (4) semantic memory about the threat, (5) episodic memories of past threats, and (6) arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p319]
- The key difference between fear and anxiety is that fear involves a threat that is present, while anxiety involves threats that are not present or may never occur. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p324]
- The amygdala creates a nonconscious defensive state; conscious feeling of fear requires that the brain monitor the consequences of this state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p322]
- Feelings are not simply emotions happening to us; they require mental work including interpretation, pattern completion, and labeling using cognitive schemas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p318]
- Autonoetic fear (fear as a personal state experienced by the self) is likely unique to humans and may not exist in other animals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p323]
- Emotion schemas are stored in semantic and episodic memory as emotional concepts and are used to categorize situations as threatening or safe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p318]
- The labeling process of emotional states is imprecise and depends on individual learning and interpretation, so each person may use emotion terms slightly differently. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p318]
- Anxiety can be initiated by external stimuli that predict harm but not exactly when it will occur, or by stimuli weakly associated with danger, or by memories and thoughts independent of external events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p324]
- The human brain's compulsion to organize experiences into categories depends on natural language, enabling symbolic representation of fear or anxiety without actual exposure to threat stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p323]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because subcortical defensive circuits mature earlier than cortical circuits, infants can react 'emotionally' before they can actually feel emotion.
- Inference: Existential anxieties about death or leading a meaningful life may not depend on survival circuits but rather on abstract concepts in autonoetic consciousness.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise boundary between noetic and autonoetic emotional experiences in humans remains unclear.
- Uncertain: Whether some animals can experience noetic fear (factual awareness of threat) without autonoetic fear (self-aware fear) cannot be definitively determined without language.
