# Drug addiction

## Summary

Drug addiction is defined as compulsive drug use despite significantly negative consequences. Addictive drugs recruit the brain's reward circuitry, particularly the dopaminergic projection from the ventral tegmental area to the nucleus accumbens. All addictive drugs increase synaptic dopamine in this pathway, mimicking and overwhelming the influence of natural stimuli and motivational states.

## Key facts

- Addiction is defined as compulsive drug use despite significantly negative consequences; the addicted person loses control over drug use. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1156]
- All addictive drugs increase synaptic dopaminergic transmission in the pathway from the ventral tegmental area to the nucleus accumbens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1156]
- Even after long periods of abstinence, exposure to drug-related cues may trigger intense drug urges and relapse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1156]
- Dopamine may act as a learning signal rather than a pleasure signal; drug consumption signals 'better than expected' to the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1161]
- Relapse risk can persist for years after drug use has stopped and withdrawal symptoms have resolved. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1161]
