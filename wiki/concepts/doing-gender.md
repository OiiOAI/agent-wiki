# Doing Gender

## Summary

'Doing gender,' formulated by West and Zimmerman (1987), is the ethnomethodological theory that gender is not an attribute of individuals but an accomplished achievement of ongoing social interaction. It distinguishes among sex (biological identification), sex category (social identification), and gender (behavior meeting social expectations). People 'do gender' by acting in ways considered appropriate to their sex category designations, subject to the principle of accountability—people are held responsible for behaving in expected or comprehensible ways. The theory recognizes that people also 'do difference' by acting to distinguish themselves as masculine not feminine, or vice versa.

- West and Zimmerman's 'Doing Gender' (1987) distinguishes among sex (biological identification), sex category (social identification), and gender (behavior meeting social expectations for being male or female). [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p363]
- Ethnomethodology posits that gender does not inhere in the person but is achieved in interaction in the situation, subject to the principle of accountability. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p363]
- People in interactions hold each other 'accountable' for behaving in ways that are expected or useful or understandable, managing conduct in anticipation of how others might describe it. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p363]
- West and Fenstermaker recognize that part of doing gender involves 'doing difference'—acting to distinguish oneself as masculine not feminine or feminine not masculine. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p364]
- Butler's concept of gender performativity parallels 'doing gender' but differs in beginning from an attempt to deconstruct the category 'woman' and in questioning the existence of a unified, basically rational self. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p364]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'doing gender' perspective has gained additional attention through resonance with Judith Butler's thesis of performativity, though ethnomethodology assumes a unified self while Butler questions this assumption.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Some critics argue the approach is too voluntaristic and does not adequately explain where the standards for accountability come from.
