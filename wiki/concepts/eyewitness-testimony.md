# Eyewitness Testimony

## Summary

Eyewitness testimony refers to accounts given by people of events they have witnessed. Research by Elizabeth Loftus demonstrates that eyewitness memories are highly susceptible to reconstruction through leading questions and language, resulting in documented cases of wrongful conviction.

## Key facts

- Elizabeth Loftus and John Palmer demonstrated that language influences memory reconstruction in their study of automobile destruction, showing interaction between language and memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]
- The Jennifer Thompson and Ronald Cotton case exemplifies how eyewitness identification can lead to wrongful conviction when memory is reconstructed through questioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]
- Victims may say 'That's the closest' at an initial lineup but later become 'absolutely positive' due to memory reconstruction, as seen in the Steve Titus case. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]

## Inferences

- Inference: Eyewitness testimony unreliability has led to advocacy for changes in lineup procedures and wrongful conviction legislation.
