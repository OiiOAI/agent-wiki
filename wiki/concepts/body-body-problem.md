# Body-body problem

## Summary

The body-body problem is Thompson's reformulation of the hard problem of consciousness. Rather than asking how subjective experience relates to physical processes (in the Cartesian sense), it asks how living subjectivity emerges from living being — where living being already possesses interiority that escapes objectivism. The distinction is between the body as physical object (Körper) and the body as lived subject (Leib), with the gap being between seeing the body as living versus as lived, rather than between mental and physical as radical ontologies.

## Key facts

- The body-body problem asks how living subjectivity emerges from living being, where living being is understood as already possessing interiority — the interiority of selfhood and sense-making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p227]
- Unlike the hard problem's absolute gap, the body-body problem has no common factor missing because both poles are within the single typology of embodiment — living being and lived being. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p228]
- The lived body is the living body performing; our lived body is a performance of our living body, something our body enacts in living. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p228]
- The hard problem is not so much hard as impossible: the Cartesian chasm between consciousness and life is a philosophical construction built on sedimented problematic thinking going back to Descartes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p217]
- The body-body problem concerns the relation between one's body as one subjectively lives it and one's body as an organism in the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p243]
- The gap is no longer between two radically different ontologies but between two types within one typology of embodiment (subjectively lived body and living body). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p243]
- The gap is no longer absolute because in order to formulate it we need to make common reference to life or living being. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p243]

## Inferences

- Inference: Life or living being is already beyond the gap between internal and external; a purely external view of structure and function is inadequate for life because living being embodies immanent purposiveness.
- Inference: The body-body problem encompasses both the relation between self and world and the relation between self and body, since one's living body is part of the world and one's body as lived is part of one's sense of self.
