# Mindless Conformity

## Summary

Mindless conformity refers to the automatic acceptance of rules, authority, and social norms without conscious questioning or awareness. Langer argues that this tendency—rooted in our desire for certainty—unnecessarily limits free will and prevents us from recognizing alternative possibilities.

## Key facts

- William James believed science had failed by abandoning unusual phenomena and tried to keep an open mind to possibilities that scientists typically prejudged as impossible. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p175]
- The Asch conformity experiment demonstrated that people often conform to obviously wrong answers given by planted accomplices rather than point out the error. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p176]
- Langer states: 'Accepting certainty is the most flagrant instance of unnecessarily limiting our free will. As soon as we think we know something, we stop considering other, potentially better alternatives.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p176]
- Scientific evidence can only yield probabilities, but too often we convert them into absolutes, making it hard to question basic assumptions. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p177]
- If we think we know, we have no doubt; if we have no doubt, we have no choice. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p178]

## Inferences

- Inference: Langer suggests that accepting the status quo inherently limits innovation, because once we believe something is certain, we stop searching for alternatives.
