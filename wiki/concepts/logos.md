# The Heraclitean Logos

## Summary

For Heraclitus, logos (from λέγω, 'to say' or 'to reason') is the ultimate reason, cause, meaning, or deep structure of the world. Unlike later philosophy where logos becomes a power behind appearances, Heraclitus saw logos as a 'phenomenal property' evidenced and experienced in reasoned thought and responses to the world. Crucially, logos is 'shared' and reciprocal, not achieved through 'private,' isolated thought processes. McGilchrist views the Heraclitean logos as aligned with right-hemisphere understanding.

## Key facts

- Heraclitus held that logos is not some power lying behind appearances, but a 'phenomenal property' evidenced in reasoned thought and responses to the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p375]
- Heraclitus saw logos as 'shared,' reciprocal, and perhaps even reciprocally coming into being, rather than something achieved through 'private' isolated thought processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p377]
- Appreciating the coming together of opposites, wherein all opposing principles are reconciled, was the essence of sophia (wisdom) for Heraclitus. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p375]
