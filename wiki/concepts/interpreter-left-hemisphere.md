# The Interpreter (Left-Hemisphere)

## Summary

The interpreter is a left-hemisphere cognitive system that generates explanations for behaviors and events, even when it lacks sufficient information. Discovered through split-brain research, it creates coherent narratives to explain why things happen, often filling in gaps with plausible stories rather than admitting ignorance.

## Key facts

- The interpreter was discovered when split-brain patients were shown different images to each hemisphere and the left hemisphere (with language) had to explain choices made by the disconnected right hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p302]
- The left hemisphere constructs theories and seeks patterns even in random events, whereas the right hemisphere uses simpler frequency-matching strategies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p301]
- The interpreter explains behaviors generated by the right hemisphere even when the left hemisphere has no information about what triggered those behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p302]
- The interpreter generates spurious explanations for physiological arousal when no obvious explanation is available, as demonstrated by the Schachter and Singer epinephrine experiment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p303]

## Inferences

- Inference: The interpreter's drive to create explanations may be the basis for human belief in dualism and the sense of a unified self.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which the interpreter generates narratives remain incompletely characterized.
