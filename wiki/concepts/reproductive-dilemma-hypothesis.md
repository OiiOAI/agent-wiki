# The Reproductive Dilemma Hypothesis

## Summary

The reproductive dilemma hypothesis proposes that symbolic communication evolved as a solution to an unprecedented social-reproductive problem in hominid evolution: the need for exclusive sexual relationships and reliable provisioning in the context of cooperative group hunting and scavenging. Males who provision females and offspring require paternity certainty, but group living and male absence during hunting creates opportunities for cuckoldry. Symbolic communication—specifically marriage as a social contract—provided the only viable solution to this evolutionary instability.

## Key facts

- Males must hunt cooperatively to be successful hunters, females cannot hunt because of ongoing reproductive burdens, yet hunted meat must reach those least able to gain access to it directly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p428]
- A reproductive balance must be struck so that most males and females have roughly equal probability of access to reproduction over the course of a lifetime for cooperative provisioning to be a stable strategy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p430]
- Sexual access is a prescription for future behaviors, and no index or memory of past behaviors can represent this—this information can only be given expression symbolically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p431]
- Marriage establishes alliances including promises and obligations between kin groups; it is the regulation of reproductive relationships by symbolic means and is essentially universal in human societies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p433]

## Inferences

- Inference: Symbolic culture was a response to a reproductive problem that only symbols could solve: the imperative of representing a social contract.
- Inference: The near synchrony of first brain size increase, first stone tools, and reduction in sexual dimorphism indicates a fundamental restructuring of hominid adaptation linking feeding ecology, social structure, and representational abilities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact timeline and sequence of how these interdependent changes co-evolved.
