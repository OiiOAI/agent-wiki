# Finite Games

## Summary

Finite games are played for the purpose of winning, requiring a definitive ending when one player is declared the winner. They are bounded by time, space, and number, with fixed rules that cannot change during play. Players must be selected for participation, and winning yields titles that are timeless and publicly recognized. The core dynamic involves theatrical self-presentation and the accumulation of power through competitive success.

## Key facts

- A finite game is played for the purpose of winning, and must come to a definitive end when someone has won. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p13]
- Finite games have temporal, spatial, and numerical boundaries to which all players must agree. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p14]
- The rules of a finite game must be published prior to play and agreed upon by players before the game begins; the agreement of players constitutes the ultimate validation of those rules. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p19-20]
- The rules of a finite game may not change during play, else a different game is being played. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p20]
- What one wins in a finite game is a title, which is the acknowledgment of others that one has been the winner of a particular game. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p34]
- Power is a concept belonging only to finite play, and is determined by the outcome of a completed game; one does not win by being powerful, one wins to be powerful. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p46]
- Finite players are serious and play within boundaries; they aim to be powerful and to consume time. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p3]

## Inferences

- Inference: Finite games require self-veiling because players must intentionally forget the voluntary nature of their play, else competitive effort will desert them.
