# Social Hierarchy

## Summary

Social hierarchies are ranking systems that formalize unequal access to resources. While found in many social species, humans have uniquely complex hierarchies including socioeconomic status, multiple simultaneous ranking systems, and occasional leader selection based on conceptions of the 'common good.' Lower social status correlates with worse health through psychological stress mechanisms.

## Key facts

- Humans belong to multiple, simultaneous hierarchies and can have very different ranks in each—inviting rationalization about why the hierarchy where we excel 'really counts'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p444]
- Lower socioeconomic status correlates with worse health, explained primarily by the psychological stress of low SES rather than healthcare access or lifestyle factors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p454]
- Subjective SES predicts health as accurately as objective SES—the stress of feeling poor, not just being poor, drives health disparities. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p454]
- Higher income inequality in a community correlates with steeper health gradients and lower social capital (trust and efficacy). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p454]
- Dunbar's number (~150) predicts the average size of human social groups based on neocortex-to-group-size ratios across primates. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p443]
- Humans uniquely invent socioeconomic status—the most caustic form of rank-based subjugation ever devised in the primate world. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p455]

## Inferences

- Inference: The health gradient across SES is fundamentally about the psychological stress of knowing one's place in a visible hierarchy—health reflects shame as much as material deprivation.
