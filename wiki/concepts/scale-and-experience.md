# Scale and Experience

## Summary

Scale and experience are mechanisms for achieving efficiency, but beyond certain points other factors become decisive. Samsung produced 295 million smartphones in 2019 versus Apple at 197 million, but Samsung sells 150 different models while Apple sells only 3, so Apple gets 66% of global phone profit versus Samsung's 17%. The Boeing B-17 production study showed labor hours fell from 140,000 to 15,000 over production run, but careful research rejected scale economies as the cause—instead, improved organization of work, reduction in rework, and just-in-time production were key. BCG's Bruce Henderson popularized the 'experience curve,' showing costs fell 20% for each doubling of cumulative output, but much of this was industry-wide Moore's Law effects, not proprietary advantages. Texas Instruments discovered this when Taiwanese competitors emerged with equivalent costs. Experience matters most in stable activities with complex processes where continued operation increases efficiency.

## Key facts

- Samsung sold 150 different model phones while Apple sold only 3, resulting in Apple getting 66 percent of global phone profit while Samsung got only 17 percent despite higher volume. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p183]
- Boeing B-17 production labor hours fell from 140,000 to 15,000 per aircraft, but scale economies were rejected as the cause—the changes were in work organization, reduction in rework, and just-in-time production. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p185]
- BCG's experience curve showed unit costs dropping about 20 percent for each doubling of cumulative output, but much of this reduction was Moore's Law applying to the industry as a whole, not individual competitors. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p186]
- Texas Instruments discovered that cost reductions shown on Henderson's curve were not proprietary when Taiwanese competitors emerged with equivalent costs. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p186]

## Inferences

- Inference: Scale advantages are limited when customers desire differentiation, as shown by Toyota's 10% global market share despite significant production scale advantages.
