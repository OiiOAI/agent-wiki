# Bezzle

## Summary

Bezzle is a contraction of 'embezzle' coined by Harvard Economics Professor John Kenneth Galbraith to stand for the increase in undisclosed embezzlement in any period. Galbraith recognized that undisclosed embezzlement has a powerful stimulating effect on spending because the embezzler spends more while the employer spends as before.

## Key facts

- John Kenneth Galbraith coined 'bezzle' to stand for the increase in any period of undisclosed embezzlement, recognizing it has a very powerful stimulating effect on spending. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p351]
- Galbraith did not push his insight further to ask about functional equivalents of bezzle that are large and not promptly self-destructive. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p352]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger extends Galbraith's concept to create 'febezzlement,' arguing that excessive investment management costs function like undisclosed embezzlement but without the self-limiting discovery effect.
