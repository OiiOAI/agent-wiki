# WOOP

## Summary

WOOP is a four-step self-regulation procedure based on mental contrasting developed by Gabriele Oettingen. The acronym stands for Wish (identify a wish), Outcome (imagine the best possible outcome), Obstacle (identify the inner obstacle standing in the way), and Plan (formulate an implementation intention to overcome the obstacle when encountered). WOOP is designed to be easy to learn and apply to both short-term and long-term wishes, and has been scientifically shown to increase energy and direction toward goal achievement.

## Key facts

- WOOP stands for Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, and Plan—four sequential steps. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p10]
- The W step involves identifying the most important wish for a given time period. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p54]
- The O step involves imagining the best possible outcome of fulfilling that wish. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p55]
- The second O step involves identifying the internal obstacle within oneself that holds one back from fulfilling the wish. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p56]
- The P step involves forming an implementation intention: if obstacle X is encountered, then will perform behavior Y. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p10]
- WOOP is described as easy to learn and applicable to short- and long-term wishes. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p10]
- WOOP helped 256 women in a health study exercise nearly twice as much as controls over four months and sustain increased fruit and vegetable consumption over two years. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p124]
- Middle school students taught WOOP showed improvements in grade point averages and school attendance compared to controls. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p134]
- Chronic back pain patients who performed WOOP managed 35 lifts on average versus under 30 for controls after three weeks, and nearly 40 lifts after three months versus declining capacity in controls. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p129]
- Stroke patients who received WOOP reported exercising about 170 minutes per week versus less than 90 minutes for standard treatment controls after one year. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p129]
- College students who used WOOP to reduce binge drinking averaged 1.8 drinking bouts per week versus over 2.5 for control participants. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p131]
- The 'P' in WOOP involves forming if-then plans: 'If obstacle x occurs (when and where), then I will perform behavior y.'. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p115]

## Inferences

- Inference: WOOP apps have been developed to facilitate daily practice (available at woopmylife.org).
- Inference: The technique is described as 'content neutral,' meaning it can be applied to any type of wish or concern.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Long-term exercise effects were not sustained at two-year follow-up, suggesting WOOP may need to be repeated for lasting habit changes.
- Uncertain: Optimal frequency of WOOP practice is not definitively established.
