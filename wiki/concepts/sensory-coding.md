# Sensory Coding

## Summary

Sensory coding is the process by which physical stimuli are represented in the nervous system as trains of action potentials. Each sensory system transforms specific stimulus energy into electrical signals via specialized receptors, with the intensity and temporal properties of stimuli encoded in neural firing patterns.

## Key facts

- Sensory receptors are responsive to a single type of stimulus energy and transduce it into electrical signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p508]
- Receptor specificity allows different sensory modalities—vision, hearing, touch, taste, and smell—to be linked to specific sense organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p500]
- The frequency of action potentials is proportional to the intensity of the stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p513]
- The receptive field of a sensory neuron conveys spatial information about the stimulus location on the receptor surface. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p515]
- Sensory pathways have both serial and parallel components consisting of fiber tracts interrupted by synaptic relays. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p501]
- Sensory systems are recursive; higher centers modify incoming sensory signals by feeding information back to earlier processing stages. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p501]

## Inferences

- Inference: The brain extracts only certain pieces of information from stimuli while ignoring others, then interprets this within the constraints of its intrinsic structure and previous experience, suggesting perception is constructive rather than purely receptive.
- Inference: Colors, tones, smells, and tastes are mental creations constructed by the brain out of sensory experience and do not exist outside the brain.
- Inference: Neural coding is better understood at early stages of sensory processing than at later cognitive stages.
