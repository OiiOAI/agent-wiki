# Strategic Coherence

## Summary

Strategic coherence means that actions and policies do not contradict each other and, in the best cases, work synergistically to create additional power. Examples of coherent strategies include Southwest Airlines' integrated low-cost model and Petzl's quality-focused approach. The space shuttle represents a cautionary tale of incoherent design driven by committee compromises.

## Key facts

- Coherence means actions and policies do not contradict each other, and in the best cases, they work synergistically to create additional power. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p119]
- The space shuttle's two fundamental problems were fabricated cost estimates and design by committee, resulting in an incoherent design. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p122]
- NASA claimed the shuttle could reduce cost per pound of payload to less than $100, but actual cost was about $28,000. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p122]
- The minimum coherence test is that actions should not directly conflict with one another. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p132]

## Inferences

- Inference: Larger organizations cannot achieve the same degree of coherence as niche players but must compensate with greater resource depth.
