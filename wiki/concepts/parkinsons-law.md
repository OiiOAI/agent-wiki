# Parkinson's Law

## Summary

Parkinson's Law states that demand for something expands to match its supply. In business finance, this means that businesses will spend whatever money is available to them. The principle explains why entrepreneurs operating with all revenue in one account never seem to have profit left over—their expenses expand to consume all available resources.

## Key facts

- Parkinson's Law was articulated by C. Northcote Parkinson in 1955. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p54]
- The law explains why expanding roads never reduces traffic congestion long-term because more drivers fill the additional capacity. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p54]
- When supply is scant, two behaviors emerge: obvious frugality and less-obvious extreme innovation to extract maximum value. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p55]
- Michalowicz cites Parkinson's Law: 'If you have ten dollars in your pocket, you will spend ten dollars. As our income increases, Parkinson's Law takes over and we spend every extra penny we earn.'. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p192]
- The author applies Parkinson's Law to explain why lifestyle must be 'locked in' for five years to accumulate wealth. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p192]

## Inferences

- Inference: By removing profit first, entrepreneurs are left with a 'smaller plate' of operating funds, triggering both cost-cutting behaviors and creative problem-solving that improves business efficiency
