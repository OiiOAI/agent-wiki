# Cumulative Prospect Theory

## Summary

Cumulative Prospect Theory (Tversky and Kahneman, 1992) extends Prospect Theory to accommodate decisions under uncertainty and multiple outcomes. It introduces two key innovations: sign-dependent weighting functions (separate functions for gains and losses) and rank-dependent decision weights (applied to cumulative rather than individual probabilities).

## Key facts

- CPT applies separate weighting functions for losses and gains (sign-dependent decision weights). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p147]
- Decision weights in CPT are rank-dependent, applied to cumulative distribution functions rather than single events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p147]
- Rank-dependence prevents violations of stochastic dominance that can arise when non-linear weights are applied directly to multiple outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p147]
