# Addressable Strategic Challenge (ASC)

## Summary

An Addressable Strategic Challenge (ASC) passes the joint filters of critical importance and addressability. The concept emerged from an Intel case study exercise where participants scored challenges on both dimensions. A challenge is important when it strikes at a vital interest of the organization; it is addressable when the organization has the skills and resources to surmount it within a reasonable timeframe. The ASC concept helps strategists narrow focus to challenges where coherent action can succeed.

## Key facts

- ASCs pass the joint filters of critical importance and addressability. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p74]
- Importance is measured by the degree to which a challenge threatens the basis of a company's strategy or its existence. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p75]
- Addressability depends on the skills and resources of the organization and the time span being considered. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p75]
- The Intel study group identified manufacturing and culture challenges as having both high importance and high addressability scores. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p81]

## Inferences

- Inference: ASCs represent where organizational focus should be directed because they combine the most critical issues with achievable solutions.
