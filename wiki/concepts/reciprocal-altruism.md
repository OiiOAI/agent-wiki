# Reciprocal Altruism

## Summary

Reciprocal altruism is cooperation between unrelated individuals based on expectation of future return benefits. Unlike kin selection, it requires cognitive abilities to recognize individuals, remember interactions, evaluate costs and benefits, and detect cheaters who fail to reciprocate.

## Key facts

- Reciprocal altruism requires: individual recognition, memory of favors given, close enough contact for predictable repayment, and ability to evaluate cost equivalence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p94]
- Time lag between favor and repayment creates opportunity for cheating, requiring mechanisms to identify non-reciprocators for the system to survive. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p94]
- Reciprocal altruism is rare in the animal world but reaches heights in humans through gossip networks that identify violators and trustworthy individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p140]
- Reciprocal altruism, the principle of 'You scratch my back, I'll ride on yours', can evolve when individuals benefit from receiving help in return, even if the benefit to the recipient is less than the cost to the altruist. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p203]
- V-formation flying birds may represent a form of delayed reciprocal altruism where individuals take turns as the reluctant leader (who bears the aerodynamic disadvantage of being first), with the benefit of shared turbulence being reciprocated over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p203]
- Reciprocal altruism is most likely to evolve in species with stable social groups where individuals recognize each other, have repeated interactions, and can keep track of who has helped whom—conditions that may have preadapted humans for moral systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p203]
- Reciprocal altruism can evolve in species capable of recognizing and remembering each other as individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p220]
- The theory requires that there be a delay between the giving of a favor and its repayment, creating opportunity for cheating. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p220]
- Three psychological characteristics shaped by natural selection for reciprocal altruism include envy, guilt, gratitude, and sympathy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p225]
- Reciprocal altruism likely played an important role in human evolution given our long memory and capacity for individual recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: The welfare state creates an 'unnatural' reciprocal altruism system that is inherently unstable because it is open to exploitation by selfish individuals who take benefits without returning them—this connects to Dawkins's argument about contraception being necessary if we maintain the welfare state.
- Inference: The psychological mechanisms underlying reciprocal altruism may explain many aspects of human social behavior and morality
- Inference: Money represents a formal token of delayed reciprocal altruism, enabling complex exchanges across time and space
