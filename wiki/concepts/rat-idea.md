# Rat Idea

## Summary

The rat idea was the central symptom of the Rat Man's obsessional neurosis. It originated when a captain described a cruel punishment involving rats boring into a prisoner's anus. Through analysis, Freud demonstrated how this image connected to multiple unconscious meanings: children (via anal eroticism and birth theories), money (via word associations), the patient's father, and hostile impulses toward those he loved.

## Key facts

- The captain's rat punishment story fanned into flame the patient's prematurely suppressed impulses of cruelty, both egoistic and sexual. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3325]
- Rats acquired symbolic meanings including money (via association with 'Raten'), dangerous infections, male sexual organs, children, and dirty animals feeding on excrement. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3325]
- The patient identified with rats because he had been a nasty, dirty little wretch who bit people when in a rage and had been fearfully punished. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3327]
- The rat punishment represented intercourse per anum, connected to the patient's father and the woman he loved. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3325]
- When the solution of the rat idea was reached, the patient's rat delirium disappeared. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3329]
