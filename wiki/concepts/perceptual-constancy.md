# Perceptual Constancy

## Summary

Perceptual constancy refers to the ability to recognize objects as the same under different viewing conditions despite markedly different retinal images. Forms include size constancy (objects perceived as same size at different distances), position constancy (objects recognized regardless of location), form-cue invariance (constancy when cues defining form change), and viewpoint invariance (objects recognized from different angles). These properties are mediated by neurons in the inferior temporal cortex that maintain selectivity across transformations.

## Key facts

- Perceptual constancy allows invariant attributes of objects to be represented independently of other image properties. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p618]
- One of the most striking properties of individual inferior temporal neurons is the invariance of their shape selectivity even to very big changes in stimulus size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p618]
- Many inferior temporal neurons respond similarly to objects regardless of position within their large receptive fields. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p618]
- Many inferior temporal neurons exhibit form-cue invariance, responding similarly to an object regardless of contrast polarity, color, or texture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p618]
- Neurons in posterior face patches are tuned to viewing angle, while neurons in anterior face patches exhibit great robustness to changes in viewpoint. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p618]
- Size constancy allows objects to be perceived as having the same size regardless of distance, despite different retinal image sizes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p678]
- Lesions of the inferior temporal cortex lead to failures of size constancy in monkeys. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p678]
- Position constancy allows objects to be recognized regardless of their location in the visual field, and many inferior temporal neurons respond similarly to stimuli in different positions within their receptive fields. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p678]
- Form-cue invariance refers to the recognition of a form regardless of changes in contrast polarity, color, or texture; many inferior temporal neurons maintain similar responses across such changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p678]
- Viewpoint invariance refers to recognizing three-dimensional objects from different angles; most neurons are tuned for specific viewing angles rather than showing complete viewpoint invariance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p678]

## Inferences

- Inference: Viewpoint invariance may be achieved at a higher stage of cortical processing, such as the prefrontal cortex, through convergent inputs from neurons selective for specific viewpoints.
