# Stochastic Choice Models

## Summary

Stochastic choice models explain why the same person may make different choices when repeatedly faced with identical options. Unlike deterministic utility maximization, these models predict that the frequency of choosing each option reflects the strength of preferences. They decompose decision into evaluation (computing utility) and choice (stochastic selection based on utility differences), enabling identification of cardinal utility from choice frequencies.

## Key facts

- In stochastic choice models, the DM does not always choose the option with the highest utility but is more likely to choose an option the higher its utility compared to alternatives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p37]
- A representation of random choice in stochastic choice models requires both a utility function and an approximate maximization function that together determine choice probabilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p37]
- The frequency of choosing one option over another provides a measure of the strength of preferences, with closer utilities leading to longer response times. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p36]
- To obtain cardinal utility, a specific condition is needed: the strength of preference depends only on the difference in utility, meaning u(x) - u(y) determines choice frequency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p38]

## Inferences

- Inference: Stochastic choice models bridge the gap between deterministic economic theories and actual variable behavior observed in experiments.
- Inference: The connection between response time and choice consistency (distance between utilities) provides a way to measure the psychological difficulty of decisions.
