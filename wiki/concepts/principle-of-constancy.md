# Principle of Constancy

## Summary

The principle of constancy states that the nervous system endeavors to keep constant the sum of excitation in its functional relations. It disposes of accretions of excitation associatively or by motor reaction. Psychical experiences that have failed to find adequate discharge become psychical traumas.

## Key facts

- The nervous system endeavors to keep constant something that may be described as the 'sum of excitation' and puts this precondition of health into effect by disposing associatively of every sensible accretion of excitation or by discharging it by appropriate motor reaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p182]
- A psychical trauma may be defined as any impression which the nervous system has difficulty in disposing of by means of associative thinking or motor reaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p182]
- Impressions become traumatic when they fail to find adequate discharge either because the patient refuses to deal with them for fear of distressing conflicts, or because they are forbidden by modesty or social conditions, or because received in a state where the nervous system was incapable of disposing of them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p182]

## Inferences

- Inference: This principle formed part of the theoretical basis for Freud's later formulations on psychic energy and the pleasure principle.
