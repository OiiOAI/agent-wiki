# Philosophical zombie

## Summary

A thought experiment in philosophy of mind asking whether a perfect physical copy of a person, including all neurons and connections, would be conscious. The experiment explores whether consciousness is purely a product of physical brain activity or requires something more, and leads to the conclusion that materialism implies such a clone should be self-aware and able to feel.

## Key facts

- A philosophical zombie would be a perfect copy of a person created by replicating each neuron and connection in the brain, who behaves identically to the original but the question arises whether this clone is conscious of its own existence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p141]
- If the brain's activity is the substrate of the mind, there is nothing that distinguishes the clone from the original person—the zombie should be self-aware and able to feel. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p141]
- Materialism implies that it does not matter if brain activity occurs in the carbon circuits of organic matter or in silicon networks that make up a computer chip. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p141]
