# Two-Stage Memory Consolidation Model

## Summary

The two-stage model of memory consolidation proposes that memories are formed through two sequential processes. During waking, theta-rhythmic activity from neocortical-entorhinal pathways brings about transient synaptic modifications in the CA3 hippocampal regions where learned information is temporarily held. During offline states, particularly non-REM sleep, sharp wave ripples replay and strengthen these transiently modified synapses repeatedly. This replay allows initially labile memories to become consolidated into more permanent forms over time, resembling how a Polaroid photograph gradually develops into a crisp image.

## Key facts

- During learning, theta-rhythmic activity from the neocortex-entorhinal cortex pathway brings about transient synaptic modifications in CA3 hippocampal regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p207]
- Sharp wave ripples during offline states (especially non-REM sleep) repeat snippets of previously learned information many times, facilitating consolidation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p207]
- The first evidence for replay of learned experience came from Wilson and McNaughton's 1994 study showing that neuron pairs with overlapping place fields continued to fire together during post-exploration sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p208]
- Replay during sharp wave ripples contains accelerated versions of place cell sequences that occurred during exploration. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p209]

## Inferences

- Inference: Manipulations affecting synaptic strength distributions can alter the neuronal membership of spontaneous sharp wave ripple events, suggesting that recently acquired information modifies which neurons participate in replay.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact molecular and cellular mechanisms by which replay during sharp wave ripples strengthens specific synapses remain under investigation.
