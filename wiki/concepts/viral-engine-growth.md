# Viral Engine of Growth

## Summary

The viral engine of growth describes products where awareness spreads from person to person as a side effect of normal product use, without customers intentionally acting as evangelists. The engine is powered by the viral coefficient, which measures how many new customers each new customer brings. A coefficient greater than 1.0 causes exponential growth; below 1.0, the loop fizzles out.

## Key facts

- Hotmail added 'P.S. Get your free e-mail at Hotmail' to every email sent, and within six months had signed up over 1 million customers; eighteen months after launch they sold to Microsoft for $400 million with 12 million subscribers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p197]
- The viral coefficient measures how many new customers will use a product as a consequence of each new customer who signs up, indicating how many friends each customer will bring. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p198]
- A viral loop with a coefficient greater than 1.0 grows exponentially because each person who signs up brings more than one other person on average. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p198]
- Many viral products do not charge customers directly because adding friction to signup impedes the viral process; they rely on indirect revenue sources like advertising. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p199]
