# Individuals vs. Categories

## Summary

The right hemisphere distinguishes individual, unique instances of things, while the left hemisphere processes categories and generic types. This distinction underlies phenomena including Capgras syndrome (failing to recognize a familiar person) and Fregoli syndrome (believing different people are the same person).

## Key facts

- The right hemisphere has the capacity to distinguish specific examples within a category; it stores details to distinguish specific instances, where the left hemisphere presents categories of things. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p80]
- Capgras syndrome (believing a familiar person is an impostor) may occur when fine perceptual changes suggest a wholly different entity, with the significance of the part outweighing the pull of the whole. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p84]
- Fregoli syndrome (believing different people are the same person) results from loss of fine discrimination of individuals, with different individuals lumped together and 're-presented' in a category. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p84]
- The left hemisphere takes a 'God's eye' or invariant view in its representation of objects, where the right hemisphere uses stored 'real world' views to group experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p82]
