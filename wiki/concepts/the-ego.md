# The Ego

## Summary

In the 1895 Project, Freud introduced the concept of the ego as an organization within the ψ (impermeable neuron) system. The ego consists of the totality of cathexes in ψ, distinguished between a permanent component (derived from regularly repeated experiences of satisfaction) and a changing component. The ego exerts inhibitory effects on primary processes through 'side cathexes' and enables secondary process functioning. This early conceptualization prefigures the ego psychology developed in Freud's later structural model (1923).

## Key facts

- Freud defined the ego as 'the totality of the ψ cathexes, at the given time, in which a permanent component is distinguished from a changing one.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p395]
- The ego forms through 'regularly repeated reception of endogenous Qη in certain neurons (of the nucleus) and the facilitating effect proceeding thence.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p395]
- The ego inhibits primary processes through 'side cathexis': 'A side cathexis thus acts as an inhibition of the course of Qη.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p395]
- Freud visualized the ego as 'a network of cathected neurons well facilitated in relation to one another.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p395]
- The ego starts from the system Pcpt., which is its nucleus, and begins by embracing the preconscious which is adjacent to the mnemic residues. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5910]
- The ego is first and foremost a bodily ego; it is not merely a surface entity but is itself the projection of a surface. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5912]
- The ego is to the id like a man on horseback who has to hold in check the superior strength of the horse. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5911]
- A large part of the ego is itself unconscious and behaves dynamically like the repressed unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5904]
- The ego controls the approaches to motility and is the mental agency which supervises all its own constituent processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5904]
- The ego is the seat of anxiety, threatened by dangers from three directions: the external world, the libido of the id, and the severity of the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5941]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ego in the Project functions as what would later be called an 'executive routine' in computational terms, deciding when to permit or inhibit discharge.
- Inference: The 'constant cathexis' of ego neurons resembles what modern neuroscience calls the 'default mode network.'
- Inference: The discovery that part of the ego itself is unconscious was already indicated in Beyond the Pleasure Principle, where Freud stated that 'much of the ego is itself unconscious; only a small part of it is covered by the term preconscious'.
