# Family Romances

## Summary

Family romances refer to neurotic children's imaginative stories that replace their real parents with grander ones of higher social standing. Freud described this as part of normal development involving liberation from parental authority, with both asexual and sexual stages. The romance serves wish-fulfillment while preserving disguised affection for actual parents.

## Key facts

- The liberation of an individual from parental authority is essential to development, though it is painful; neurotics have failed in this task. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3116]
- In the first (asexual) stage, the child replaces both parents or the father alone with grander people of higher social standing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3117]
- Boys are more inclined to feel hostile impulses towards their father than towards their mother and have more intense desire to get free from him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3117]
- When the child learns about sexual differences ('pater semper incertus est, mater certissima'), the family romance exalts the father while not doubting maternal origin. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3118]
- The new aristocratic parents are equipped with attributes derived entirely from real recollections of the actual parents, so the child is not really getting rid of the father but exalting him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3119]
- The child's phantasies express longing for the happy vanished days when the father seemed the noblest and strongest man. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3119]
