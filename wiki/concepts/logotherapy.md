# Logotherapy

## Summary

Logotherapy is a psychotherapeutic method developed by Viktor Frankl that focuses on the human will to meaning rather than pleasure or power as the primary motivation of human existence. The therapy holds that finding meaning in life is the central human motivational force, and that even unavoidable suffering can be transformed into achievement and accomplishment when given meaning.

## Key facts

- Logotherapy boils down to the lesson that life holds a potential meaning under any conditions, even the most miserable ones. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p9]
- Logotherapy is based on the premise that the primary human motivation is not pleasure but meaning. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p56]
- Frankl cited Nietzsche's assertion that 'he who has a why to live for can bear with almost any how' as the guiding motto for logotherapy regarding prisoners. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p63]
- Logotherapy maintains that one can preserve a vestige of spiritual freedom—independence of mind—even in terrible conditions of psychic and physical stress. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p55]
- Frankl argued that suffering itself can be meaningful when one accepts it as one's task; this he called finding the 'why' for one's existence. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p65]
- Logotherapy focuses on the meaning of human existence and man's search for such meaning. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p78]
- According to logotherapy, the striving to find a meaning in one's life is the primary motivational force in man. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p78]
- In logotherapy, the patient is confronted with and reoriented toward the meaning of his life. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p78]
- Logotherapy is less retrospective and less introspective than psychoanalysis, focusing rather on the future, that is to say, on the meanings to be fulfilled by the patient in his future. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p78]
- The logotherapist's role consists of widening and broadening the visual field of the patient so that the whole spectrum of potential meaning becomes conscious and visible to him. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p82]
- Logotherapy tries to make the patient fully aware of his own responsibleness; therefore, it must leave to him the option for what, to what, or to whom he understands himself to be responsible. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p82]

## Inferences

- Inference: Logotherapy differs from Freudian psychoanalysis (which focuses on pleasure) and Adlerian psychology (which focuses on power) by centering on meaning as the fundamental human drive [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p56].
- Inference: Frankl's concept of the 'noölogical dimension' refers to the spiritual dimension of human existence where meaning is pursued—this distinguishes logotherapy from purely psychological approaches [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p56].
- Inference: Logotherapy represents a shift from Freud's 'depth psychology' (focusing on the past and unconscious) to what Frankl called 'height psychology' (focusing on the future and conscious decisions).
