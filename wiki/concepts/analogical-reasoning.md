# Analogical Reasoning

## Summary

Analogical reasoning uses past experiences or examples to understand new situations, make predictions, or solve problems. An analogue is drawn from the same or related domain; a metaphor comes from a markedly different domain. The logic of analogical reasoning is similar to experimental logic: it allows conclusions without knowing all important factors. Effective analogues match on causal variables and can be adjusted for known differences.

## Key facts

- Analogical reasoning is most helpful when there is a good database but not enough information for more rigorous analyses. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p262]
- People do not select analogues just based on similarity; they select analogues that share the same dynamics and causal factors affecting what they are trying to predict. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p252]
- If the difference between analogue and target can be captured as a proportion, the causal factor can be adjusted for; if not reducible to a proportion, the analogue should be rejected. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p252]
