# SnackWell's Phenomenon

## Summary

Named by Michael Pollan, this refers to the difficulty of working backward from an outcome to determine its cause when the relevant information is hidden. In the 1990s, people eating SnackWell's low-fat cookies gained weight, but couldn't easily determine whether to attribute the weight gain to the cookies (skill/decision) or other factors (luck). The phenomenon illustrates why outcomes alone don't tell us what to learn.

## Key facts

- SnackWell's Phenomenon describes people increasing consumption of something that has less of a bad ingredient, not realizing the substitute is also harmful. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L940-L945]
- Nabisco developed SnackWell's cookies by substituting sugar for fat, based on the now-discredited belief that fat, not sugar, caused weight gain. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L935-L940]
- As SnackWell's eaters gained weight, they faced difficulty determining whether to field the outcome as feedback that their belief about health value was wrong, or as bad luck unrelated to their choices. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L946-L952]
- Multiple people could gain weight from different foods (SnackWell's, Oreos, lentils), making it hard for any individual to determine the cause with certainty from the outcome alone. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L954-L959]
