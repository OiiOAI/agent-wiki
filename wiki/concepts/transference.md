# Transference

## Summary

Transference is a phenomenon Freud discovered in which patients unconsciously transfer feelings, perceptions, and ways of relating from important past relationships onto the present—particularly onto the analyst. This allows unconscious procedural memories to be relived rather than remembered, and when pointed out during analysis, underlying neuronal networks can be retranscribed and changed.

## Key facts

- Freud discovered that in mild sensory deprivation created by sitting out of view, patients began to regard him as they had important people in their past, unconsciously reliving past memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p230]
- Patients projected transferences not only onto analysts but onto other people in their lives without awareness, and viewing others in distorted ways often caused difficulties. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p231]
- Freud discovered that transferences of early traumatic scenes could be altered if he pointed out to patients what was happening when the transference was activated and the patient was paying close attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p231]
- Transferences allow underlying neuronal networks and associated memories to be retranscribed and changed through psychoanalytic treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p231]
- Emotions and patterns displayed in relationships are part of the procedural memory system, and when triggered in therapy they give patients the opportunity to look at them and change them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p238]
- Transference refers to the revival of psychological experiences and impulses directed toward the analyst, replacing an earlier person from the patient's past. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2392]
- Freud distinguished between affectionate transferences (supporting recovery) and hostile ones, both of which must be identified and interpreted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2394]
- Dora's transference manifested as replacing Herr K. with Freud in her unconscious, leading to her sudden departure as an act of revenge. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2387]
- Transference cannot be avoided; it is an inevitable necessity of psychoanalytic treatment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2393]
- Freud compared the distinction between new impressions and revised editions: some transferences are exact replacements, others are 'sublimated' revisions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2393]
- Transference multiplies the physician's labors but also becomes the greatest ally if properly detected and explained to the patient. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2394]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Dora case demonstrated that when transference is not recognized in time, patients may terminate treatment prematurely as a form of 'acting out' their unconscious conflicts.
- Inference: Freud concluded that psychoanalytic treatment does not create transference but merely brings it to light, unlike hypnotic suggestion which creates dependent relationships.
- Inference: Since transference love cannot be satisfied without destroying the treatment, the analyst must maintain neutrality and abstinence to preserve the therapeutic frame.
- Inference: The patient's belief in the analyst's communications is a derivative of love—initially it needed no arguments, and only later did arguments become acceptable from someone loved
- Inference: The phenomenon of transference reveals that current relationships are shaped by repetitions of early object relations, suggesting that therapeutic change requires working through these repetitions rather than simply gaining insight.
