# Synapse Formation

## Summary

Synapse formation involves three key processes: (1) axons choosing specific postsynaptic partners, (2) differentiation of the axon into a presynaptic terminal, and (3) differentiation of the target cell into a specialized postsynaptic apparatus. Recognition molecules, activity-dependent mechanisms, and trans-synaptic organizing molecules all contribute to synapse specificity.

## Key facts

- At the neuromuscular junction, motor neurons release agrin which binds LRP4 and activates MuSK, leading to ACh receptor clustering through rapsyn. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1194]
- Neurexin-neuroligin interactions are major synaptic organizing complexes that promote differentiation of pre- and postsynaptic specializations in the central nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1202]
- Nerve and muscle organize each other's differentiation through retrograde (muscle-to-nerve) and anterograde (nerve-to-muscle) signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1190]
- Neural activity sharpens synaptic specificity after initial molecular recognition establishes coarse connectivity patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1187]
