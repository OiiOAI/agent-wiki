# Sympathoadrenal System

## Summary

The sympathoadrenal system and pituitary-adrenal axis are the two complementary physiological systems that support defensive behavior. The sympathoadrenal system responds rapidly (within seconds) to threats, while the pituitary-adrenal system is slower and takes minutes to hours to fully express.

## Key facts

- The sympathoadrenal system involves sympathetic nervous system nerves and adrenaline released from the adrenal medulla, responding within seconds to threats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p93]
- The pituitary-adrenal axis involves release of ACTH from the pituitary, leading to cortisol release from the adrenal cortex, taking minutes to hours. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p93]
- Sympathetic activation increases respiration, raises heart rate, and stimulates conversion of glycogen to glucose for energy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p92]
- Donald Klein proposed a third physiological response—the suffocation alarm—triggered by internal physiological signals such as excess carbon dioxide. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p94]
