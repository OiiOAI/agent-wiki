# Word Presentations and Thing Presentations

## Summary

Freud distinguishes between word presentations (Wortvorstellungen) and thing presentations (Sachvorstellungen or Dingvorstellungen). A thing presentation is an open, non-closed complex of visual, auditory, tactile, and kinaesthetic associations linked to an object, while a word presentation is a closed, determined complex linking auditory images with motor speech images. The conscious presentation of an object combines both, allowing thought processes to become conscious by being linked with word presentations. Unconscious presentations consist of thing presentations alone, which explains why repressed material cannot become conscious without being translated into words.

## Key facts

- A word presentation is a complex presentation consisting of sound image, visual letter image, motor speech image, and motor writing image, which becomes linked to an object presentation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4580]
- The object presentation is one which is not closed and barely capable of being closed, while the word presentation is something closed, even though capable of extension. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4583]
- The word presentation is linked at its sensory end (by its sound images) with the object presentation, which leads to two classes of language disorder: verbal aphasia and asymbolic aphasia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4584]
- Being linked with word presentations is not yet the same thing as becoming conscious, but only makes it possible to become so; it is characteristic of the system Pcs. alone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4572]
