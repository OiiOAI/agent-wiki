# Meaning of Suffering

## Summary

Frankl asserts that we may find meaning in life even when confronted with a hopeless situation, when facing a fate that cannot be changed. What matters is to bear witness to the uniquely human potential at its best, to transform a personal tragedy into a triumph, to turn one's predicament into a human achievement. When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves. Suffering ceases to be suffering at the moment it finds a meaning. Frankl emphasizes that suffering is not necessary to find meaning—meaning is possible even in spite of suffering, but if suffering is avoidable, one should remove its cause.

- We may find meaning in life even when confronted with a hopeless situation, when facing a fate that cannot be changed, by transforming a personal tragedy into a triumph. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p84]
- When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are challenged to change ourselves. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p84]
- Suffering ceases to be suffering at the moment it finds a meaning, such as the meaning of a sacrifice. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p85]
- In no way is suffering necessary to find meaning; meaning is possible even in spite of suffering, provided that the suffering is unavoidable. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p85]
- To suffer unnecessarily is masochistic rather than heroic. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p85]
