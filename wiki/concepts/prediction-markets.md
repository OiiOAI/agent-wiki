# Prediction Markets

## Summary

Prediction markets are platforms where participants bet on the likelihood of future events, creating financial incentives for accuracy. Studies show that betting markets where scientists wager on whether experimental results will replicate are more accurate than traditional peer review alone (71% vs 58% accuracy). Companies including Google, Microsoft, General Electric, Eli Lilly, Pfizer, and Siemens have implemented prediction markets.

## Key facts

- A betting market where scientists wager on the likelihood of experimental results replicating was more accurate than expert opinion alone: 71% versus 58% accuracy. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1455-L1465]
- Companies implementing prediction markets include Google, Microsoft, General Electric, Eli Lilly, Pfizer, and Siemens. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1470-L1475]
- People are more willing to offer their opinion when the goal is to win a bet rather than get along with people in a room. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1475-L1480]
- The study by Anna Dreber and colleagues found that betting markets better predicted replication outcomes compared to traditional peer review. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1455-L1465]
