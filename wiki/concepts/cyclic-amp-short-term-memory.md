# Cyclic AMP and Short-Term Memory

## Summary

Cyclic AMP (cAMP) is a second messenger molecule that plays a crucial role in short-term memory formation. In Aplysia sensory neurons, serotonin released during sensitization activates metabotropic receptors that stimulate adenylyl cyclase to produce cAMP, which activates protein kinase A, leading to enhanced release of glutamate and strengthened synaptic connections.

## Key facts

- Sutherland discovered that the hormone epinephrine produces a brief biochemical change at the cell surface that gives rise to a more enduring change inside cells through cyclic AMP. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p238]
- Metabotropic receptors activate adenylyl cyclase, which makes cyclic AMP; one molecule of chemical messenger produces a thousand molecules of cyclic AMP. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p238]
- Kandel and Schwartz discovered that cyclic AMP second-messenger signaling is turned on by serotonin during sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p241]
- Injecting cyclic AMP directly into a sensory cell dramatically increased the amount of glutamate released, simulating the effect of serotonin. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p242]
- The increase in cyclic AMP lasts about as long as the slow synaptic potential, the increase in synaptic strength, and the enhanced behavioral response. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p241]
