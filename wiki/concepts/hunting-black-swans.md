# Hunting Black Swans

## Summary

Black Swans are incredibly hard to uncover without face-to-face interaction. Voss identifies specific techniques: getting face time (email doesn't allow tone or nonverbal reading), observing unguarded moments (beginning and end of meetings, during interruptions), and recognizing when counterpart behavior 'doesn't make sense'—which often signals hidden constraints, bad information, or undisclosed interests rather than irrationality.

## Key facts

- Black Swans are hard to uncover via email because it gives counterparts time to re-center; face-to-face interaction allows reading nonverbal communication and tone. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p206]
- The first and last few minutes of business meetings often reveal more about the other side than the formal discussion in between—reporters keep recorders running to capture this. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p207]
- When counterparts seem irrational or crazy, they are usually ill-informed, constrained by factors they won't reveal, or have other hidden interests driving their behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p204]
