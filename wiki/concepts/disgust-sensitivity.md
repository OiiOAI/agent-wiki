# Disgust Sensitivity and Political Ideology

## Summary

Sensitivity to visceral disgust predicts social conservatism. People exposed to foul odors or sitting in dirty environments become more socially conservative. Social conservatives have lower disgust thresholds, show larger autonomic responses to negative images, and are less likely to use reappraisal strategies. Leon Kass's 'wisdom of repugnance' argument exemplifies using disgust as a moral guide.

## Key facts

- People become more socially conservative when exposed to a smelly garbage can—the insula activation from disgust generalizes to social judgment. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p466]
- Social conservatives show greater galvanic skin responses (sympathetic arousal) to negative emotional images related to social/purity issues (gay marriage, premarital sex) but not to nonsocial issues (free trade, gun control). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p466]
- When conservatives are instructed to view disturbing images 'in a detached, unemotional way' (reappraisal), they express less conservative political sentiments. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p467]
- Leon Kass, head of Bush's bioethics panel, argued for 'the wisdom of repugnance'—that visceral disgust at things like cloning is 'deep wisdom beyond reason's power to articulate'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p467]

## Inferences

- Inference: The flaw in 'wisdom of repugnance' is that different people are disgusted by different things—whose gag reflex should rule? What was once disgusting (slave equality) is now accepted, suggesting disgust is culturally shaped, not a universal moral compass.
