# Decision-Making in Anxiety Disorders

## Summary

Anxiety disorders are characterized by heightened sensitivity to uncertainty and threat, which manifests across multiple stages of the decision-making process including altered representation of the external world, exaggerated valuation of avoidance, preference for safe actions, and disrupted learning from prediction errors.

## Key facts

- Anxiety disorders are associated with attentional bias toward threat, facilitated attention to danger, and difficulty disengaging attention once captured by threat cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p315]
- Highly trait-anxious individuals show diminished neural representation of expected value in the ventromedial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum during threat conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p317]
- Elevated trait anxiety is associated with steeper discounting of future rewards, possibly due to the uncertainty inherent in waiting for delayed outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p316]
- Dispositional anxiety is associated with larger feedback-related negativity (error-related theta activity), reflecting heightened sensitivity to punishment and contributing to cautious, avoidant behavioral responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p319]

## Inferences

- Inference: Anxious individuals may fail to update learning rates appropriately in volatile environments, causing aversive events to appear more unpredictable and uncontrollable.
- Inference: Avoidance behavior, while adaptive in the short-term, prevents disconfirmation of biased threat expectancies and perpetuates anxiety over the long-term.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether increased interoceptive accuracy in anxious individuals mediates improved Iowa Gambling Task performance remains untested in direct studies.
