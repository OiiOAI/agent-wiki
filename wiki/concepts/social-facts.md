# Social Facts

## Summary

Social facts are forces and structures that are external to, and coercive of, the individual. Durkheim developed this concept as the proper subject matter of sociology. He distinguished between material social facts (such as bureaucracy and law) and nonmaterial social facts (such as culture and social institutions).

## Key facts

- Durkheim conceived of social facts as forces and structures that are external to, and coercive of, the individual. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p47]
- He differentiated between material social facts (bureaucracy, law) and nonmaterial social facts (culture, social institutions). [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p48]
