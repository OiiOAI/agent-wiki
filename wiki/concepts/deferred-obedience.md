# Deferred obedience

## Summary

A psychoanalytic concept describing the psychological process by which prohibitions or commands are internalized and obeyed only after a delay. Freud used this to explain how sons who killed the primal father subsequently forbade themselves from killing the totem (their father's substitute) and renounced the women, creating the two fundamental taboos of totemism.

## Key facts

- Deferred obedience (Nachträglichkeit) refers to the psychological procedure by which prohibitions are revoked and enforced by the individuals themselves after the original transgression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4242]
- After killing the primal father, the sons created the two fundamental taboos of totemism through deferred obedience, prohibiting the killing of the totem and incest. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4242]
- The dead father became stronger than the living one had been; what was previously prevented by his actual existence was thereafter prohibited by the sons themselves through deferred obedience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4242]

## Inferences

- Inference: Deferred obedience represents a shift from external constraint (the living father's power) to internal regulation (the sons' self-imposed prohibitions), marking a crucial step in the development of morality and social organization.
