# The Little Man in the Brain Fallacy

## Summary

This is the fallacy of assuming that the retinal image is something to be 'seen' by a little man in the brain, who would then need his own eye to see it, creating an infinite regress of little men within little men. Gibson identifies this as one of the most seductive fallacies in psychology's history, arising from the temptation to believe the retinal image falls on a kind of screen intended to be looked at.

## Key facts

- The little man in the brain theory conceives the eye as a camera at the end of a nerve cable transmitting the image to the brain, requiring a homunculus to look at this physiological image. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p85]
- If a little man exists in the brain, he would need an eye to see with, creating an infinite series of little men, each within the other, explaining nothing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p85]
- The alternative theory that retinal images are transmitted element by element via optic nerve signals also carries the lurking implication of a little man, since signals must be decoded and interpreted. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p86]

## Inferences

- Inference: Both the image-transmission theory and the signal-transmission theory carry the implication of a mind separate from a body, perpetuating Cartesian dualism.
