# Delays in Feedback Systems

## Summary

Delays are ubiquitous in systems. Every stock is a delay, and most flows have delays—shipping, perception, processing, maturation delays. When decisions are made based on delayed information or responses have long delays, actions will be too much or too little to achieve goals. If action is taken too fast, it may amplify short-term variation unnecessarily. Delays determine how fast systems can react, how accurately they hit targets, and how timely information flows. Overshoots, oscillations, and collapses are always caused by delays.

## Key facts

- Forrester suggested multiplying estimated delay times by three when modeling. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p131]
- Delays between catching a disease and diagnosis range from days to years; pollution emission to ecosystem harm involves years or decades. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p132]
- Delays in changing social norms for family size take at least one generation; retooling a production stream takes 3-8 years. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p132]
- When there are long delays in feedback loops, some sort of foresight is essential—to act only when a problem becomes obvious misses an important opportunity. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p133]
- Overshoots, oscillations, and collapses are always caused by delays. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p133]
