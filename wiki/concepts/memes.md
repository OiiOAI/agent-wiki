# Memes

## Summary

Memes are cultural units that replicate and evolve like genes but through culture rather than biological reproduction. Introduced by Richard Dawkins, the concept explains how ideas spread through populations. Hawkins uses memes to explain how viral false beliefs propagate, noting that memes can have symbiotic relationships with genes, where belief in certain memes leads to behaviors that favor gene propagation.

## Key facts

- A meme is something that replicates and evolves like a gene but through culture, first introduced by biologist Richard Dawkins. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p171]
- The history book example describes mutually supporting memes analogous to how an organism is created by mutually supporting genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p171]
- Memes and genes can evolve in a mutually reinforcing way, where belief in memes leads to behaviors that favor gene replication. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p171]
- Hawkins notes the term 'meme' has been appropriated to represent images on the internet but is using the original definition. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p171]
