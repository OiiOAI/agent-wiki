# Non-linear systems perspective

## Summary

The non-linear systems perspective is an analytical framework that examines international relations and warfare as complex systems characterized by interconnections, interactions, and interdependencies that defy simple linear prediction. Robert Jervis articulates four fundamental characteristics of such systems: it is impossible to do merely one thing; results cannot be predicted from separate actions; strategies depend on the strategies of others; and behavior changes the environment.

## Key facts

- In non-linear complex systems, it is impossible to do merely one thing; results cannot be predicted from separate actions; strategies depend on the strategies of others; and behavior changes the environment. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p166]
- When interconnections are dense, it may be difficult to trace the impact of any change even after the fact, let alone predict it ahead of time, making the system complex and hard to control. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p166]
- Jervis concludes his book with three suggestions for strategies that may overcome or take into account the problems of non-linear systems: constraining the opponent's options, understanding the non-linearity of the environment, and aiming for indirect effects with multiple strategies. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p168]
- Alan Beyerchen argues that Clausewitz understood war is inherently a non-linear phenomenon, perceiving and articulating the nature of war as an energy-consuming phenomenon involving competing and interactive factors. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p170]

## Inferences

- Inference: The non-linear perspective challenges traditional strategic planning by asserting that indirect and delayed effects, unpredictable interactions of variables, learning, and anticipation all contribute to fundamental unpredictability of actions.
- Inference: The non-linear nature of war means that short-term impacts of any action will differ from long-term outcomes, making effectiveness measurement problematical.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Jervis's three strategic suggestions (constraining options, understanding non-linearity, multiple strategies) represent a complete framework or partial response to non-linear complexity.
