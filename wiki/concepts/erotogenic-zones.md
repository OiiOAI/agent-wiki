# Erotogenic Zones

## Summary

Erotogenic zones are parts of the skin or mucous membrane in which stimuli of a certain sort evoke a feeling of pleasure possessing a particular quality. Freud identified predestined erotogenic zones (such as the lips/mouth) and argued that any part of the body can acquire erotogenic susceptibility. These zones serve as the somatic sources of sexual excitation and form the basis for understanding both normal sexual development and perversions.

## Key facts

- An erotogenic zone is a part of the skin or mucous membrane in which stimuli of a certain sort evoke a feeling of pleasure possessing a particular quality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2455]
- The mouth behaves like an erotogenic zone, and stimulation by the warm flow of milk is the cause of the pleasurable sensation in early infancy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2454]
- Erotogenic zones behave in every respect like a portion of the sexual apparatus; in hysteria, these zones become the seat of new sensations and changes in innervation comparable to erection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2441]
- Freud later ascribed the quality of erotogenicity to all parts of the body and to all internal organs. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2456]
- The anal zone has great erotogenic significance, with intestinal disturbances making children 'nervy' and influencing later neurotic symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2458]
- Erotogenic zones are regions of the body with special intensification of susceptibility to stimulus possessed in a certain degree by the whole cutaneous surface. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2471]
- Any part of the skin and any sense organ—probably indeed any organ—can function as an erotogenic zone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2498]
- The leading erotogenic zone in female children is located at the clitoris, homologous to the masculine glans penis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2488]
- The decisive element in sources of sexual excitation is the quality of the stimuli, though intensity is not without importance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2475]
- Erotogenic zones provide pleasure through appropriate stimulation and lead to increased sexual tension, which motivates the continuation of the sexual act. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2479]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of erotogenic zones provides an anatomical basis for understanding how perversions involving non-genital body parts (oral, anal) can be psychologically coherent.
- Inference: The theory explains why conversion symptoms in hysteria often involve organs with erotogenic significance.
