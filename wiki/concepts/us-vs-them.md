# Us vs Them

## Summary

Us vs Them (in-group/out-group dichotomy) is a fundamental human tendency where brains rapidly form categories distinguishing in-group (Us) from out-group (Them). This process occurs with minimal cues (race, gender, arbitrary groupings), activates the amygdala within 50 milliseconds, and is amplified by oxytocin. Thems are perceived as threatening, angry, untrustworthy, disgusting, or primitive and homogeneous. This tendency underlies both our best cooperative behaviors and worst acts of violence and prejudice.

## Key facts

- The brain forms Us/Them dichotomies with stunning speed—50-millisecond exposure to another race activates the amygdala. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p402]
- Oxytocin prompts trust and cooperation toward Us but worsens behavior toward Them—more preemptive aggression and more advocacy of sacrificing Them. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p403]
- Minimal group paradigms show that even arbitrary, flimsy differences (e.g., overestimating dots in a picture) produce in-group biases and cooperation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p404]
- By age three to four, children already group people by race and gender, have more negative views of out-groups, and perceive other-race faces as angrier. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p405]
- Thems are viewed as threatening and angry—whites judge African American faces as angrier than white faces. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p413]
- Disgust serves as an ethnic or out-group marker—pictures of drug addicts or homeless activate the insula, not the amygdala. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p413]
- Essentialism views Them as homogeneous and interchangeable, with a monolithic, immutable essence. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p413]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Us/Them dichotomy is rooted in evolution and served adaptive purposes for group survival and resource competition.
