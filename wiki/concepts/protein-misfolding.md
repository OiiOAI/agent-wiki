# Protein Misfolding in Neurodegenerative Diseases

## Summary

Protein misfolding is a common pathogenic mechanism in adult-onset neurodegenerative diseases. Mutations causing proteins to adopt alternative conformations lead to abnormal protein interactions, impaired protein clearance, and accumulation of toxic aggregates. The cellular response involves chaperone upregulation and ubiquitin-proteasome system activation, but when these mechanisms fail, protein aggregates form and contribute to neuronal dysfunction and death.

## Key facts

- Protein misfolding is a common pathogenic mechanism in adult-onset neurodegenerative diseases including Huntington disease, Parkinson disease, and the spinocerebellar ataxias. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1554]
- Expanded glutamine tracts alter the folding state of native proteins, recruiting protein-folding and degradation machinery, and when overwhelmed, proteins accumulate and form aggregates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1554]
- Overproduction of chaperones such as Hsp70 and Hsp40 reduces protein aggregation and mitigates polyglutamine toxicity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1554]
- Inclusions containing mutant proteins are thought to be part of the cell's attempt to sequester toxic proteins and limit damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1555]
- Protein aggregates in both human patients and mouse models contain ubiquitin and proteasome components, indicating failed protein degradation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1553]
