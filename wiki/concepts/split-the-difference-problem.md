# Split-the-Difference Approach Problem

## Summary

The split-the-difference approach to management is criticized as creating perverse incentives that lead to polarization. When managers are perceived as splitting differences between extreme positions, the best way for subordinates to influence outcomes is to take the most extreme positions possible, knowing they will be averaged out. This creates an arms race where rivals take polarized positions, making splitting increasingly difficult and unsuccessful. Managers unknowingly reward extreme polarization by their approach.

## Key facts

- When a boss tends to split the difference, the best way to influence the boss and get what you want is to take the most extreme position possible, knowing the two opinions will be averaged out. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p210]
- This kind of arms race escalates: rivals are likely to do the same thing, and over time everyone takes the most polarized positions possible, making splitting the difference ever more difficult and ever less successful. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p210]
- Managers have to take responsibility for knowingly or inadvertently creating such incentives, even though it was not their intention to reward extreme polarization. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p210]
- The willy-nilly approach is hard to give any rationale for why one particular problem should be anticipated while another is ignored, making the boss appear capricious or arbitrary with an ulterior motive. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p209]
