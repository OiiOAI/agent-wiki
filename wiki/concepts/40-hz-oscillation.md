# 40 Hz Oscillation

## Summary

The 40 Hz oscillation is proposed as the global signal that binds disparate brain regions into unified conscious experience. This coherent neuronal activity is present during wakefulness and REM sleep but reduced during delta sleep. It reflects thalamocortical resonance and is proposed to implement temporal binding of sensory information into unitary perceptual entities.

## Key facts

- 40 Hz coherent magnetic activity is spontaneously present in awake and REM sleep states but greatly reduced during delta sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p135]
- Auditory stimuli produce well-defined 40 Hz oscillations during wakefulness but no resetting during either delta or REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p135]
- Waking and REM sleep states are electrically very similar with respect to 40 Hz oscillations, yet sensory input is not perceived during REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p136]
- The difference between dreaming and wakefulness is that sensory input during REM sleep is not temporally correlated with ongoing thalamocortical activity and thus not placed into context. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p136]
- Wolf Singer and Charlie Gray reported widespread synchronicity in mammalian cerebral cortex with 40 Hz oscillations binding cortical columns separated by up to 7 mm. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p123]

## Inferences

- Inference: The presence of 40 Hz activity in both wakefulness and REM sleep, but sensory perception only during wakefulness, suggests that temporal correlation with external input—not just the oscillation itself—determines conscious perception.
