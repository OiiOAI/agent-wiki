# Suggestibility in Eyewitness Testimony

## Summary

Suggestibility refers to the tendency to accept false information from external sources and incorporate it into memory. In eyewitness testimony, police interviewing techniques can inadvertently plant false memories or distort genuine ones. Research shows that highly specific questions yield less accurate recall than open-ended questions, with error rates rising from 9% to 49% depending on question type.

## Key facts

- Police interviewing techniques can lead witnesses to provide inaccurate reports and amplify the suggestive effects of misleading information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p161]
- When children were asked open-ended questions such as 'What happened?' they provided accurate details, but specific questions like 'Where did you hurt yourself?' increased inaccurate details from 9% to 49%. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p175]
- Studies show that children's spontaneous recollections tend to be accurate, whereas responses to specific questions are more likely to include distortions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p175]
- When children are interviewed twice and produce details in a second interview not mentioned in the first, the new details are highly likely to be inaccurate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p176]
- Social incentive techniques including praise, rewards, expressions of disappointment, repeated questions, and invitations to pretend dramatically increase false reports in preschoolers—three-year-olds said 'yes' to 81% of misleading questions with social incentives versus 31% without. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p178]

## Inferences

- Inference: Open-ended questioning techniques are more likely to elicit accurate eyewitness accounts than specific or leading questions.
- Inference: Repeated questioning of children about alleged events can create false memories that are difficult to distinguish from genuine recollections.
