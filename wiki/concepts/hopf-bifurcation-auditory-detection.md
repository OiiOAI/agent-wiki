# Hopf Bifurcation in Auditory Detection

## Summary

The Hopf bifurcation model proposes that cochlear amplification operates through active mechanical modules poised on the verge of spontaneous oscillation. This physical principle explains the cardinal features of auditory detection: enhanced sensitivity near characteristic frequency, sharpened frequency selectivity, compressive nonlinearity across stimulus levels, and otoacoustic emissions.

## Key facts

- A Hopf bifurcation occurs when a dynamical system transits from quiescence to spontaneous oscillation as a control parameter varies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p667]
- The normal form equation Λ(dZ/dt) ≈ -Λ(Cc − C − 2ifc)Z − B|Z|²Z + F describes the response of a critical oscillator near the bifurcation point. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p667]
- At the Hopf bifurcation, sensitivity to a low-level stimulus is greatly enhanced near the characteristic frequency but drops rapidly if the stimulus is detuned. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p668]
- The Hopf bifurcation produces compressive growth with a line of slope 1/3 in doubly logarithmic plots, representing a large range of stimulus levels by a narrower range of response amplitudes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p668]
- The generic properties of a Hopf bifurcation account for observations at the level of single hair bundles, basilar membrane, and psychoacoustics without depending on specific molecular details.
