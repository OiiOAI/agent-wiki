# Symbol learning

## Summary

Symbol learning refers to the acquisition of symbolic associations where one associative learning process must be subordinated to another. This is the most critical learning problem faced during symbol acquisition and may be the primary roadblock to symbol learning in nonhuman species. Prefrontal cortex is critical for making the shift away from indexical token-object associations to relying on systematic token-token association patterns to organize reference.

## Key facts

- The most fundamental constraint affecting language evolution derives from learning and memory difficulties, not limited motor abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p274]
- The evolution of vocal abilities might more accurately be seen as a consequence rather than the cause of the evolution of language. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p274]
- The contribution of prefrontal areas to learning involves analysis of higher-order associative relationships where one associative learning process must be subordinated to another. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p286]
- Less effective regulation of competing learning tendencies by the prefrontal cortex is almost certainly the primary roadblock to symbol learning in nonhuman species. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p286]
