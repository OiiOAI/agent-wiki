# Hox Genes

## Summary

Hox genes are a family of homeodomain transcription factors that control anterior-posterior patterning of the body plan and nervous system. In mammals, 39 Hox genes are organized in four chromosomal clusters (HoxA, B, C, D), with gene position within the cluster correlating with expression domain along the anterior-posterior axis. Hox genes specify motor neuron subtype identity in the hindbrain and spinal cord, determining which muscles motor neurons will innervate. Mutations in Hox genes cause transformations of regional identity and disrupt normal connectivity patterns.

## Key facts

- Mammalian Hox genes are organized in four chromosomal clusters, with genes at more 3' positions expressed in more rostral domains and genes at more 5' positions expressed in progressively caudal domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1167]
- Hoxb1 is expressed at high levels in rhombomere 4, where facial motor neurons originate, and mutations in Hoxb1 cause these neurons to acquire trigeminal motor neuron identity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1169]
- Hox6 proteins specify brachial lateral motor column identity while Hox9 proteins specify preganglionic motor column identity; these proteins are mutually repressive. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1168]
- Hox genes control the identity of motor neurons within the lateral motor columns by directing expression of downstream transcription factors and axonal guidance receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1168]
- Hox genes are arranged in four clusters in mammals with 39 genes total; their chromosomal position predicts their rostrocaudal expression domain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1228]
- Hoxb1 is expressed at high levels in rhombomere 4; Hoxb1 mutant mice show facial motor neurons converting to trigeminal-type. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1230]
- Hox6 specifies brachial lateral motor column identity while Hox9 specifies preganglionic motor column; these proteins mutually repress each other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1231]
- Hox proteins control motor pool identity by regulating ephrin receptor expression, which guides axons to specific limb muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1232]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conservation of clustered Hox gene organization from Drosophila to mammals indicates an ancient role for these genes in anterior-posterior body patterning.
