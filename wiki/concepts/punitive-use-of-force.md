# Punitive Use of Force

## Summary

The punitive use of force causes individuals to suffer for their perceived misdeeds, based on the assumption that people commit offenses because they are bad or evil. It includes physical punishment, blaming to discredit, withholding privileges, and withdrawal of caring or respect. Punishment shifts attention from intrinsic values to external consequences and damages self-esteem and goodwill.

## Key facts

- The punitive use of force is based on the assumption that people commit offenses because they are bad or evil and need to be made to repent through suffering. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p249]
- Punitive action is designed to make people suffer enough to see the error of their ways, repent, and change. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p249]
- Punitive action tends to generate resentment and hostility and to reinforce resistance to the very behavior being sought. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p250]
- Punishment includes physical punishment, blaming to discredit another person, withholding privileges such as allowances or driving privileges, and the withdrawal of caring or respect. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p252]
- Fear of punishment focuses attention on consequences rather than on one's own values. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p253]
- Two questions reveal limitations of punishment: what different behavior is wanted, and what reasons are wanted for the person's motivation to comply. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rosenberg notes that children's fear of corporal punishment may obscure their awareness of the compassion underlying parental demands.
