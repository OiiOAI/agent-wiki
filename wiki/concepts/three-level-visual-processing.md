# Three-Level Visual Processing

## Summary

The brain analyzes visual scenes at three hierarchical levels: low-level (local contrast, orientation, color, movement), intermediate-level (contour integration, surface properties, figure-ground segmentation), and high-level (object recognition and identification).

## Key facts

- Low-level processing discriminates simple attributes like local contrast, orientation, color, and disparity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p500]
- Intermediate-level processing parses the visual image into surfaces, global contours, and foreground-background relationships. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p500]
- High-level processing involves object recognition and matching with memories of shapes and meanings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p500]
