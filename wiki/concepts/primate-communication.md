# Primate Communication

## Summary

Research on how nonhuman primates communicate with each other and with humans, including vocalizations, gestures, facial expressions, and learned symbol systems. Studies of vervet monkeys, chimpanzees, and bonobos reveal complex communication systems, though whether these constitute true language remains debated.

## Key facts

- Vervet monkeys have distinct alarm calls for snakes, leopards, and predatory birds, with appropriate responses to each call type. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p69]
- Savage-Rumbaugh's bonobo Kanzi learned lexigrams by observation and can match pictures, objects, and spoken words; he can understand sentences like 'Make the doggie bite the snake' vs. 'Make the snake bite the doggie'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p67]
- Chimpanzees do not appear to adjust their calls to inform ignorant individuals, unlike humans who communicate specifically to share knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p69]

## Inferences

- Inference: The gap between animal communication and human language may lie in intentionality - animals communicate reactively while humans communicate with deliberate intent to share information
