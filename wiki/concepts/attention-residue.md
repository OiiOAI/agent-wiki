# Attention Residue

## Summary

Attention residue is a concept introduced by Sophie Leroy in 2009, describing the phenomenon where when you switch from Task A to Task B, your attention doesn't immediately follow—a residue remains stuck thinking about the original task. This residue is especially thick if work on Task A was unbounded and low intensity before switching. Research shows that people experiencing attention residue demonstrate poor performance on the next task.

## Key facts

- Attention residue occurs when you switch from one task to another and your attention doesn't immediately follow—a residue of your attention remains stuck thinking about the original task. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p26]
- The residue is especially thick if your work on the first task was unbounded and of low intensity before you switched. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p26]
- People experiencing attention residue after switching tasks are likely to demonstrate poor performance on that next task. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p27]
