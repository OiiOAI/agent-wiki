# Synapse Elimination

## Summary

Synapse elimination is a developmental process whereby excess synapses formed early in development are removed, resulting in the refinement of neural circuits. At the neuromuscular junction, multiple motor axons initially innervate each muscle fiber, but all but one input withdraws after birth through a competitive, activity-dependent process. Microglia contribute to synapse elimination by phagocytosing weak synapses, using complement factors (C1q, C3) in an activity-dependent manner.

## Key facts

- In adult mammals, each muscle fiber bears only a single synapse, but at intermediate developmental stages several axons converge on each myotube and form synapses at a common site before all inputs but one are eliminated soon after birth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1252]
- Synapse elimination results from intercellular interactions and appears to be a competitive process, as every muscle fiber ends up with exactly one input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1252]
- Neural activity plays a role in synapse elimination: paralysis of muscle reduces elimination, whereas direct stimulation enhances it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1252]
- Microglia phagocytose synaptic terminals during synapse elimination using complement factors C1q and C3, providing a mechanism for activity-dependent targeting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1253]
- Astrocytes contribute to synapse elimination by secreting TGF-beta, which promotes production of complement factor C1q. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1253]

## Inferences

- Inference: The competitive nature of synapse elimination suggests that the outcome depends on the relative activity levels of competing inputs, with more active axons gaining territory at the expense of less active ones.
- Inference: Dysregulation of microglial pruning may contribute to synaptic loss in neurodegenerative diseases and psychiatric disorders.
