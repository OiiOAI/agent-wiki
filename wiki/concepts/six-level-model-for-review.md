# Six-Level Model for Review

## Summary

The Six-Level Model provides a framework for reviewing work from different perspectives or 'horizons': Ground (current actions), Horizon 1 (current projects), Horizon 2 (areas of focus and accountabilities), Horizon 3 (goals), Horizon 4 (vision), and Horizon 5 (purpose and principles). This model helps prioritize appropriately by understanding that daily work must be grounded in clear higher-level purpose and vision.

## Key facts

- The six levels from bottom to top are: Ground (current actions), Horizon 1 (current projects), Horizon 2 (areas of focus), Horizon 3 (goals), Horizon 4 (vision), and Horizon 5 (purpose and principles). [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p93]
- The Horizon 2 level covers areas of focus and accountabilities—the key areas of life and work within which one wants to achieve results and maintain standards. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p93]
- Horizon 4 (vision) involves projecting three to five years into the future, considering organization strategies, environmental trends, and career transitions. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p94]
- Horizon 5 (purpose and principles) provides the ultimate job description from which all goals, visions, objectives, projects, and actions derive. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p94]
- Minute-to-minute and day-to-day there is no time to think—one needs to have already thought. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p94]
