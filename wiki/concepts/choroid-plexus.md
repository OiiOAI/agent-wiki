# Choroid Plexus

## Summary

The choroid plexuses are vascularized epithelial structures in the cerebral ventricles that secrete cerebrospinal fluid. They are structurally similar to the distal and collecting tubules of the kidney and form the blood-cerebrospinal fluid barrier.

## Key facts

- The choroid plexuses are structurally similar to the distal and collecting tubules of the kidney, using capillary filtration and epithelial secretory mechanisms to maintain the chemical stability of the CSF. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1626]
- The capillaries that traverse the choroid plexuses are freely permeable to plasma solutes, but a barrier exists at the choroid epithelial cells formed by tight junctions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1626]
- In the rat, blood flow per gram of tissue is as much as fivefold higher in the choroid plexus than in cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1627]
- The choroid epithelial cells also synthesize and secrete proteins that may have paracrine effects on the development of neighboring neuroepithelial cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1627]
- CSF has lower concentrations of K+, Ca2+, bicarbonate, and glucose than blood plasma, and CSF is also more acidic (pH 7.33 vs 7.41). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1627]

## Inferences

- Inference: The high vascularity of choroid plexuses supports their role as the primary site of CSF production.
