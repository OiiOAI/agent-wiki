# Feedforward and Feedback Processing

## Summary

Feedforward and feedback are fundamental organizational principles in neural systems. Feedforward connections project from lower to higher processing regions, carrying sensory information upward. Feedback connections descend from higher to lower regions, modulating processing based on context, expectations, and behavioral state. Most brain pathways have both feedforward and feedback connections, forming reciprocal circuits.

## Key facts

- Projections from a lower to a higher processing region are said to be feedforward, while descending projections that modulate spinal reflexes are considered feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p125]
- The motif in which region A projects to region B and, in turn, receives return projections from B is recapitulated throughout the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p125]
- Feedforward projections from the primary visual cortex to secondary and tertiary visual areas originate mainly in layer III and terminate mainly in layer IV of the target cortical area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p135]
- Feedback projections to earlier stages of processing originate from cells in layers V and VI and terminate in layers I, II, and VI. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p135]
- In the lateral geniculate nucleus, synapses formed by feedback axons from the visual cortex are actually greater in number than synapses from the retina, despite the neurons being primarily driven by retinal input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p131]
