# Symbolic Violence

## Summary

Symbolic violence is a concept developed by Pierre Bourdieu describing a form of domination where the dominated accept their own domination as legitimate. It is a 'soft' violence exercised with the complicity of the dominated, practiced indirectly through cultural mechanisms rather than through direct coercion. The educational system is the major institution through which symbolic violence is practiced, with the language and meanings of those in power being imposed on the rest of the population, obscuring domination and getting the dominated to accept their condition.

## Key facts

- Symbolic violence is 'violence which is exercised upon a social agent with his or her complicity.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p436]
- Symbolic violence stands in contrast to more direct forms of social control, being practiced indirectly through cultural mechanisms. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p436]
- The educational system is the major institution through which symbolic violence is practiced on people. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p436]
- Symbolic violence serves to get 'the dominated [to] accept as legitimate their own condition of domination.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p436]
- Bourdieu saw the state as the site of the struggle over the monopoly of symbolic violence. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p436]
