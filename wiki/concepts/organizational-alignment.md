# Organizational Trust: The Principle of Alignment

## Summary

Organizational Trust is established through alignment—ensuring that all structures, systems, processes, policies, and symbols within an organization are in harmony with principles that create trust. Misaligned systems communicate distrust more powerfully than words and create a vicious downward cycle.

## Key facts

- Arthur W. Jones stated 'All organizations are perfectly aligned to get the level of trust they get,' suggesting that low-trust results indicate organizational systems designed for low trust. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p288]
- Leaders can be successful at Self Trust and Relationship Trust levels but fail at Organizational Trust by not designing and aligning systems that promote trust, because trust is hidden in organizations—present in systems and structures rather than visible behaviors. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p288]
- Symbols are powerful manifestations of alignment or misalignment, including tangible objects, systems/processes, behaviors, and legendary stories. They communicate paradigms more clearly than words and override rhetoric. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p290]
- Hewlett-Packard cofounders kept all parts bins and storerooms open as a symbol of trust; when Bill Hewlett found a lock, he broke it open and posted a sign reading 'HP TRUSTS ITS EMPLOYEES.'. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p291]
- Nordstrom's employee handbook consists of only one card with the single rule: 'Use good judgment in all situations,' symbolizing trust and deliberately non-bureaucratic culture. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p291]
- David Packard learned early that distrusting employees caused them to walk off with tools or parts, proving that distrust produces the very behaviors it fears—creating a self-fulfilling prophecy of low trust. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p298]
- When leaders believe people can be trusted, they create systems reflecting that belief, which reinforce trusting behaviors that validate the initial perception—a virtuous, upward cycle. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p298]
