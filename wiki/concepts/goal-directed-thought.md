# Goal-Directed Thought

## Summary

A form of thinking achieved by consciously representing current and desired states and linking these through actions that transform the current state to the desired state. It is tightly linked to prefrontal cortex function and cognitive control, with the dorsolateral PFC and rostrolateral PFC playing specific roles in planning, reasoning, and problem solving.

## Key facts

- The region of the brain most tightly linked to goal-directed thought is the prefrontal cortex, with specific roles for dorsolateral PFC (DLPFC) and rostrolateral PFC (RLPFC) in planning, reasoning, and problem solving. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p353]
- Lesions to the prefrontal cortex lead to profound deficits in goal-directed thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p353]
- Cognitive control allows the PFC to selectively bias currently relevant representations, focusing attention on relevant stimuli while diminishing attention toward competing stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p353]
- Goal-directed thought requires a proportionately higher contribution from lateral PFC and is associated with some of the lowest contributions from the default network. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p359]

## Inferences

- Inference: The opposite patterns of lateral PFC and default network involvement in goal-directed versus spontaneous thought support viewing these as ends of a thought continuum.
- Inference: Cognitive control enables goal-directed thought to keep mental content 'on track' and produce a logically connected train of thought.
