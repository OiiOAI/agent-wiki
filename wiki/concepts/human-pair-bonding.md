# Human Pair Bonding

## Summary

Human pair bonding is characterized by three features absent in other species: both sexes contribute to offspring rearing, adults are bound by long-term exclusive sexual access rights while living in multi-male multi-female groups, and reproduction is regulated by symbolic marriage. This pattern emerged from the evolutionary instability of combining cooperative group hunting with male provisioning of mates and offspring.

## Key facts

- Human pair bonding is rare because it tends to undermine itself: males provisioning offspring require paternity certainty, but group living and male absence during hunting creates cuckoldry opportunities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p419]
- Cooperative hunting and provisioning require groups of females to live and forage cooperatively, but group living is disadvantageous because of proximity of many other sexually active females and their competing demands. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p420]
- Marriage in all societies has reproductive rights and obligations as central content, specifying reproductive status within the wider community and establishing kin group alliances. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p416]

## Inferences

- Inference: The human reproductive pattern of pair bonding within groups represents a unique solution to the evolutionary tension between provisioning and paternity certainty, solved through symbolic social contracts.
