# Split Testing

## Summary

Split testing (also called A/B testing) is an experimental technique where different versions of a product are offered to customers at the same time. By observing the changes in behavior between the two groups, one can make inferences about the impact of the different variations. This technique, pioneered by direct mail advertisers, is incorporated directly into product development in Lean Startups.

## Key facts

- In a split-test experiment, different versions of a product are offered to customers at the same time, allowing inferences about the impact of different variations by observing changes in behavior between groups. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p128]
- Grockit used split testing to discover that lazy registration—a considered industry best practice requiring customers to register upfront before experiencing the service—had no impact on customer behavior compared to immediate access. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p132]
- Split testing often uncovers that features making the product better in engineers' and designers' eyes have no impact on customer behavior. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p129]
- Split testing helps teams refine their understanding of what customers want; Grockit discovered through testing that the key to student engagement was offering a combination of social and solo features. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p129]
- Votizen improved registration from 5% to 17% and activation from 17% to over 90% through split testing new product features, messaging, and design improvements. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p142]
