# Forgetting of Intentions

## Summary

The forgetting of intentions demonstrates that lack of attention does not suffice to explain parapraxes. Normal behavior shows that intentions can be formed and later executed without conscious attention during the interval, as with post-hypnotic suggestion. Forgetting occurs when obscure motives rise against the intention, as in military discipline or love relationships where forgetting signals divided allegiance.

## Key facts

- No group of phenomena is better qualified than forgetting of intentions for demonstrating that lack of attention does not suffice to explain parapraxes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2134]
- Normal behavior after forming an intention coincides fully with experimentally produced behavior of people given post-hypnotic suggestion at long range. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2134]
- In military service, the difference between failure to carry out orders due to forgetting and deliberate evasion is neglected on principle, as forgetting indicates countermotives opposing the order. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2135]
- A proverb reveals popular knowledge that forgetting of intentions is not accidental: 'If one forgets to do a thing once, one will forget to do it many times more.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2141]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that certain domains (military service, love) demand immunity to forgetting implies that in important matters, forgetting signals the wish to treat things as unimportant.
