# Reasonable Person Standard

## Summary

The reasonable person standard is a legal benchmark representing the norms of society against which defendants are measured. Barrett argues this standard is a fiction based on emotion stereotypes and the classical view of human nature. It encompasses stereotyped ideas about emotional expression, feeling, and perception, and is applied inconsistently to defendants based on gender and ethnicity.

## Key facts

- The reasonable person standard is a social contract, a guide to behavior for the average person in a population of diverse individuals, and like all averages, it is a fiction that doesn't apply exactly to any single individual. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p248]
- A legal standard based on emotion stereotypes is especially problematic for equitable treatment of men and women; women are expected to be more emotional and empathic, men more stoic and analytical. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p248]
- When female victims of rape express anger on the witness stand, judges evaluate them negatively; men experiencing similar situations are attributed situational rather than personality-based emotions. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p250]
- In domestic violence cases, men who kill get shorter and lighter sentences than women who kill their intimate partners. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p250]
- African American women who defend themselves against batterers face the 'angry black woman' stereotype and fare worse in court than European American women in similar situations. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p250]

## Inferences

- Inference: Barrett suggests it may be time to 'bury the reasonable person' and conceive some other standard for comparison, given that current standards perpetuate harmful stereotypes.
