# Prediction error minimization

## Summary

Prediction error minimization is the core mechanism by which the brain implements perceptual inference. The brain generates predictions about sensory signals and compares them with actual incoming signals; the differences (prediction errors) are used to update predictions in an iterative process.

## Key facts

- The brain continually generates predictions about sensory signals and compares them with actual sensory signals arriving at the sense organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
- Differences between predicted and actual sensory signals give rise to prediction errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
- Prediction error signals are used to update predictions, ready for the next round of sensory inputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
- Prediction error minimization provides the connection between controlled hallucinations and Bayesian inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
- The brain approximates Bayes' rule by minimizing prediction errors everywhere and all the time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
