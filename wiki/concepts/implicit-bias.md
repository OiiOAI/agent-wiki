# Implicit Bias

## Summary

Implicit bias refers to attitudes or stereotypes that affect understanding, actions, and decisions unconsciously. Growing out of stereotype research and implicit memory studies, implicit bias is measured by tasks like the Implicit Association Test (IAT). Research shows implicit biases can influence behavior even when explicit attitudes are non-prejudiced. Training to reduce implicit bias shows mixed results, though attitudes have shown gradual improvement over time.

## Key facts

- Implicit bias refers to the influence of stereotypes that operate outside conscious awareness, growing out of research on stereotype bias and implicit memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p212]
- The Implicit Association Test (IAT) measures the strength of associations between concepts (e.g., racial groups) and evaluations (e.g., good/bad) by comparing response times to different pairings. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p213]
- A large-scale 2002 IAT study with over 100,000 white respondents revealed white participants showed a large implicit bias favoring whites over Blacks despite expressing no explicit preference on rating scales. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p213]
- Police officers did not show explicit bias to shoot more Black than white targets, but response times revealed an implicit bias: faster decisions for targets fitting racial stereotypes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p214]
- Only half of 18 implicit bias interventions reduced bias on the IAT, and no interventions reduced explicit racial preferences; effects were often weak and short-term, not predicting behavior changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p216]
- Using 4 million IAT results from 2007-2016, both explicit and implicit attitudes regarding race, skin tone, and sexual orientation showed decreasing bias across that decade. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p217]
