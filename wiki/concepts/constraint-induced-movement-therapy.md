# Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy

## Summary

Constraint-induced (CI) movement therapy is a neuroplasticity-based treatment developed by Edward Taub for stroke patients and others with brain damage. The therapy forces patients to use their affected limbs by constraining their good limbs, combined with intensive, shaping-based training (massed practice). The approach is based on the concept of 'learned nonuse,' where patients stop using affected limbs even when recovery is possible. CI therapy has shown that 80% of stroke patients with arm function loss can improve substantially, even years after their stroke. The principles have been extended to treat aphasia, cerebral palsy, and other conditions.

## Key facts

- In CI therapy, patients wear a mitt on their good hand and a sling on their good arm for 90 percent of their waking hours. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- CI therapy sessions involve six hours of training per day for ten to fifteen days straight. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p155]
- Patients do ten to twelve tasks per day, repeating each task ten times. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p155]
- Taub developed exercises working with physiotherapist Jean Crago. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- The therapy always uses the behavioral technique of 'shaping,' taking an incremental approach to all tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- Studies show that 80 percent of stroke patients who have lost arm function can improve substantially with CI therapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p155]
- Even patients who had strokes, on average, more than four years before beginning CI therapy benefited significantly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p155]
- After CI therapy, the brain map for an affected arm doubles in size, returning to normal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]
- The essence of the cure is incremental training or shaping, increasing in difficulty over time, combined with massed practice. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]
- CI principles have been applied to help stroke patients regain speech after damage to Broca's area. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p162]
- A German team found that stroke aphasia patients had a 30 percent increase in communication after only 32 hours of CI language therapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p163]
- CI therapy has been applied to children with cerebral palsy, with extraordinary gains including children beginning to crawl for the first time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p163]

## Inferences

- Inference: The therapy works by overcoming learned nonuse and triggering neuroplastic changes in the brain, allowing adjacent brain regions to take over lost functions.
