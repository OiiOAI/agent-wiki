# Translation of Freud's Einfall as 'Free Association'

## Summary

The translation of Einfall (an idea that suddenly occurs to one) as 'association' has been criticized for losing the concept's essential passivity and unpredictability. Critics argue that 'association' implies deliberate connections while Einfall seems unrelated to everything else happening at that moment—one feels passive in relation to it. The impersonal German verb form 'Es fällt mir ein' (it occurs to me) connects the concept to the unconscious. Strachey himself acknowledged the difficulties, noting in 1957 that 'association' was 'question-begging' but 'hardly to be escaped' for freier Einfall. He recognized there was 'no satisfactory' equivalent for the German word. Steiner (1987) traced the genealogy of the translation and argued that Bettelheim 'magnified Strachey's responsibility out of all proportion' given that Freud implicitly approved the English term.

## Inferences

- Inference: The translation difficulty reflects fundamental differences in how German and English conceptualize spontaneous mental events—German emphasizes the event happening to the person, English emphasizes the person's mental activity.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Freud's use of freie Assoziation as a synonym indicates terminological imprecision or deliberate flexibility remains debated.
