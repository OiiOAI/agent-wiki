# Gating Current

## Summary

Gating currents are small capacitive currents generated by the movement of charged amino acid residues (gating charges) within voltage-gated ion channels during activation. They represent the earliest electrical signature of channel gating, preceding the opening of the ion-conducting pore.

## Key facts

- Gating current (Ig) is produced by outward movement of positively charged residues in S4 regions through the membrane electric field during depolarization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]
- Ig precedes the opening of voltage-gated channels and can be measured when ionic current is blocked (e.g., by tetrodotoxin). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]
- Mutating positively charged lysine and arginine residues in S4 regions progressively reduces gating current, confirming these residues as the source. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]
