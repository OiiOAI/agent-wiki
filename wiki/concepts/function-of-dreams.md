# The Function of Dreams

## Summary

Freud proposes that dreaming has a specific function: it serves to bring back under control of the preconscious the excitation in the unconscious that has been left free, thereby discharging unconscious excitation while preserving the sleep of the preconscious in return for minimal expenditure of waking activity. Dreams act as a compromise between the two systems, fulfilling both the wish to sleep and unconscious wishes. However, when a wish fulfillment jars violently upon the preconscious, the dream fails and becomes a disturber of sleep rather than its guardian.

## Key facts

- Dreaming has the function of bringing under preconscious control the free excitation in the unconscious; it discharges unconscious excitation and preserves sleep in return for small expenditure of waking activity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1815]
- The dream is compatible with sleeping even if it interrupts sleep several times during the night; waking briefly and falling asleep again is like brushing away a fly—one's sleep is maintained. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1813]
- When a wish fulfillment jars too violently upon the preconscious so that sleep cannot continue, the dream breaks off and waking results; the dream then appears as disturber rather than guardian of sleep. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1817]
- Unconscious processes are indestructible; unlike preconscious processes, unconscious paths once laid down never fall into disuse and always conduct excitation to discharge when recathected. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1814]

## Inferences

- Inference: Robert's 'excretion theory' of dreams is essentially confirmed by Freud, though with different premises—the function of making harmful things harmless through dreaming is accepted.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether dreaming has other secondary functions (such as 'thinking ahead' or serving as practice for waking actions) remains debated.
