# Computational Pathology

## Summary

Computational pathology conceptualizes psychopathological conditions as disorders of inference, where the inferential mechanism operates normally but based on a flawed generative model endowed with aberrant prior beliefs. This framework links maladaptive behavior, psychological phenomena, and biological-level abnormalities.

## Key facts

- The hypothesis underlying inferential approaches is that psychopathological conditions may be conceptualized as disorders of inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p191]
- In most computational pathology studies, the inferential mechanism operates normally but based on a flawed generative model with aberrant prior beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p191]
- Aberrant priors may be about states or precisions, or they may be structural priors about the form of the generative model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p194]
- Pathologies covered include addiction, impulsivity, compulsivity, delusions, hallucinations, interpersonal and personality disorders, oculomotor syndromes, prefrontal syndromes, visual neglect, and disorders of interoceptive inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p192-195]
