# Negation (Verneinung)

## Summary

A concept introduced by Freud showing that negation represents intellectual acceptance of repressed content while the repression itself persists. Negation separates intellectual function from affective process, and is the intellectual substitute for repression.

## Key facts

- Negation is a way of taking cognizance of what is repressed; it is already a lifting of the repression, though not an acceptance of what is repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6130]
- With the help of negation, the intellectual function is separated from the affective process. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6130]
- A negative judgement is the intellectual substitute for repression; its 'no' is the hallmark of repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6130]
- The polarity of judgement corresponds to the opposition of two groups of drives: affirmation belongs to Eros, negation to the drive to destruction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6132]
- There is no stronger evidence of successful analysis than when a patient reacts with 'I didn't think that' or 'I didn't think of that'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6133]
