# Incentive-Caused Bias

## Summary

A subconscious cognitive drift toward conclusions that favor the professional's interests rather than the client's, driven by financial and career incentives. Munger identifies this as one of the most powerful and pervasive distortions affecting professional judgment.

## Key facts

- It is not usually the conscious malfeasance of narrow professional advisers that does you in; troubles come from their subconscious bias, impaired cognition due to financial incentives different from yours. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p287]
- Shaw's observation that 'every profession is a conspiracy against the laity' understates the problem—professional defects frequently stem from intertwined subconscious mental tendencies. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p309]
- One partial cure for professional biases is developing multidisciplinary skills to recognize when advice serves the adviser's interests rather than the client's. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p311]
- Federal Express had a system that was always screwed up until they changed from hourly pay to shift-based pay—when all work was done, everyone could go home. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p200]
- Carl Braun required all Braun Company communications to include who, what, where, when, and why—firing employees who didn't include why twice. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p168]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Carnation Company example demonstrates how trademark protection creates incentives for quality control even for products they don't own—parties invest to protect a valuable brand association.
- Inference: The accounting profession's decision not to expense stock options reflected incentive-caused bias, as corporate managers preferred a convention that made their compensation appear less costly.
- Inference: The antidote to incentive-caused bias includes using sound accounting systems, cash registers, and professional skepticism when advisors benefit from certain recommendations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The full extent to which academic psychology has addressed incentive-caused bias, though Munger notes it was largely missing from psychology textbooks he reviewed.
