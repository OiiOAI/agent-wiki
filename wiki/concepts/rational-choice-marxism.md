# Rational Choice Marxism

## Summary

Rational Choice Marxism is a branch of analytical Marxism that applies neoclassical economics concepts—particularly rational choice theory and game theory—to Marxian analysis. It seeks micro-foundations for Marxist theory by assuming actors are rational and seek to maximize their gains, while recognizing structural constraints that do not completely determine choices.

## Key facts

- Jon Elster identifies three interdependencies among actors in game theory: reward depends on choices of all actors, reward depends on reward for all, and choice made by each depends on choices of all. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p211]
- Elster argues that Marx practiced methodological individualism—the doctrine that all social phenomena are in principle explicable in ways involving only individuals, their properties, goals, beliefs, and actions. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p211]
- Roemer moved away from thinking of exploitation as occurring at the point of production and toward exploitation as coercion associated with differential ownership of property. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p211]
- Rational choice Marxism retains an interest in collective action for changing society and accepts that capitalism is an unjust system, even while using mainstream microeconomic approaches. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p210]

## Inferences

- Inference: The shift from labor theory of value to property-ownership-based exploitation allows analytical Marxism to analyze socialist societies as potentially exploitative, broadening the theory's applicability.
