# Consciousness in Infants

## Summary

Research by Dehaene and colleagues demonstrates that infants as young as two months show brain signatures of conscious perception similar to adults. Functional MRI shows awake infants activate a language network including Broca's area and prefrontal cortex when listening to speech, with activity disappearing during sleep. The local-global test reveals conscious responses to auditory novelty with slower processing (1 second latency vs 250-300 ms in adults). Masking experiments show five-month-olds have conscious thresholds around 100 ms compared to 40-50 ms in adults.

## Key facts

- Two-month-old infants show brain activation in Broca's area and prefrontal cortex during speech processing, with activity absent during sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p294]
- Infants show verbal working memory—Broca's area activates more strongly on second exposure to a sentence after 14 seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p294]
- The local-global test produces a global P3 response in two-month-old infants, indicating conscious processing of sound pattern violations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p296]
- Infant conscious responses are 3-4 times slower than adults—one second for frontal response vs 250-300 milliseconds in adults. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p296]
- Five-month-old infants have masking thresholds around 100 ms, dropping to adult values (40-50 ms) by 10-12 months of age. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p298]
- Slow infant processing may reflect lack of myelin insulation on long-distance axons—the workspace is wired but not yet fully myelinated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p300]

## Inferences

- Inference: The architecture of conscious processing is present at birth or early infancy, suggesting consciousness emerges from the same neural substrate throughout development
- Inference: The dramatic slowdown in infants may be analogous to patients recovering from coma—both show adaptive responses but with longer latencies

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether consciousness exists in newborns (hours to weeks old) before the two months tested remains unknown
- Uncertain: The exact timing of when conscious processing first emerges in development is not established
