# Mossy fibers

## Summary

Mossy fibers are one of the two principal afferent inputs to the cerebellum. They originate from cell bodies in the spinal cord and brain stem and carry sensory information from the periphery as well as information from the cerebral cortex. Mossy fibers form excitatory synapses on granule cell dendrites in cerebellar glomeruli and produce brief, small excitatory potentials in Purkinje neurons that generate simple spikes.

## Key facts

- Mossy fibers originate from cell bodies in the spinal cord and brain stem and carry sensory information from the periphery as well as information from the cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Mossy fibers form excitatory synapses on dendrites of granule cells in the granular layer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Each Purkinje neuron is contacted by axons from 200,000 to 1 million granule cells, indicating massive convergence of mossy fiber input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Parallel fibers produce only brief, small excitatory potentials in Purkinje neurons, generating simple spikes that require inputs from many parallel fibers to have a substantial effect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1019]

## Inferences

- Inference: The high convergence ratio suggests mossy fiber input is optimized for encoding the magnitude and duration of peripheral stimuli through firing rate control of simple spikes.
