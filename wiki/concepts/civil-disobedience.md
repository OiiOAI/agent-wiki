# Civil Disobedience

## Summary

Civil disobedience is defined in Rawls's theory of justice as a public, nonviolent, conscientious yet political act contrary to law, usually done with the aim of bringing about a change in the law or policies of the government. It is addressed to the sense of justice of the majority and declares that the principles of social cooperation among free and equal men are not being respected. Civil disobedience falls between legal protest and conscientious refusal, standing at the boundary of fidelity to law.

## Key facts

- Civil disobedience is a public, nonviolent, conscientious yet political act contrary to law. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p329]
- Civil disobedience is addressed to the sense of justice of the majority of the community. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p329]
- Civil disobedience is an appeal to the commonly shared conception of justice that underlies the political order. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p330]
- Civil disobedience is nonviolent because it tries to avoid the use of violence, especially against persons. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p330]
- Civil disobedience expresses disobedience to law within the limits of fidelity to law. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p331]
- Civil disobedience is justified when there are substantial and clear injustices, particularly infringements of the first principle of justice (equal liberty) or blatant violations of fair equality of opportunity. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p336]
- Normal appeals to the political majority must have been made in good faith and must have failed before civil disobedience is justified. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p336]
- The natural duty of justice is the primary basis of political ties to a constitutional regime in civil disobedience. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p339]
