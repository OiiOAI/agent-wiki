# Gamma Oscillations

## Summary

Gamma oscillations are brain waves in the 30-100 Hz range that arise from the temporal interaction between excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic effects, forming a 'tug of war' pattern. The time scale of gamma waves corresponds to the temporal window of spike timing-dependent plasticity and the membrane time constant of cortical pyramidal cells (10-30 ms). This temporal coincidence makes gamma waves the physiological 'frame' for cell assembly organization, defining the window within which upstream neurons can effectively contribute to reader neuron discharge.

## Key facts

- Gamma oscillations arise from the temporal interaction between opposing excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic effects, giving rise to an oscillating tug of war. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p95]
- The time scale of gamma waves (10-30 ms) corresponds to the temporal window of spike timing-dependent plasticity, membrane time constant of cortical pyramidal cells, and fast synaptic signaling mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p95]
- Gamma wave frames (10-30 ms epochs) define the temporal boundaries of cell assemblies, with concatenated gamma assemblies forming 'neuronal words.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p96]

## Inferences

- Inference: The temporal similarities among membrane time constant, fast inhibitory/excitatory signaling, plasticity window, and gamma oscillation period make a strong case for the importance of cell assemblies acting within this time window.
