# Will to Meaning

## Summary

The will to meaning is Frankl's concept that man's search for meaning is the primary motivational force in his life, not a secondary rationalization of instinctual drives. This meaning is unique and specific—it must and can be fulfilled by the person alone. A 1966 French poll found 89% of people admitted they need something to live for, and 61% had someone for whose sake they would die. A Johns Hopkins University survey of 7,948 students found 78% said their first goal was finding purpose and meaning to their life.

## Key facts

- Man's search for meaning is the primary motivation in his life and not a secondary rationalization of instinctual drives. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p79]
- This meaning is unique and specific in that it must and can be fulfilled by him alone; only then does it achieve a significance which will satisfy his own will to meaning. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p79]
- A French poll showed 89% of people admitted that man needs something for the sake of which to live, and 61% conceded there was something or someone in their own lives for whose sake they were even ready to die. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p79]
- A Johns Hopkins University survey of 7,948 students at 48 colleges found 78% said their first goal was finding a purpose and meaning to my life, compared to only 16% who checked making a lot of money as very important.
