# Transaction Cost Economics

## Summary

Transaction cost economics, developed by Ronald Coase and Oliver Williamson, analyzes how firms and markets organize transactions given human limitations (bounded rationality) and behavioral uncertainties (opportunism). The theory explains the existence of firms as governance structures that economize on transaction costs compared to pure market mechanisms.

## Key facts

- Ronald Coase first articulated the nature of the firm in 1937, questioning why economic activity occurs within firms rather than through market contracts. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p423]
- Oliver Williamson developed the transaction cost approach, arguing that bounded rationality and opportunism create market failures that make hierarchical governance structures more efficient than pure markets. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p423]
- Williamson's framework considers why economic agents differ from 'economic man' by having limited computational capacity and unreliable, self-interested behavior. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p423]
