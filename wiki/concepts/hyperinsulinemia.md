# Hyperinsulinemia

## Summary

Hyperinsulinemia is a condition of chronically elevated insulin, caused by insulin resistance. It is described as the canary in the coal mine of metabolic disorder. The body responds to incipient insulin resistance by producing more insulin, which appears successful initially as glucose homeostasis is maintained. However, elevated postprandial insulin is an early warning sign. The author argues that hyperinsulinemia should be treated as a bona fide endocrine disorder, yet Medicine 2.0 typically waits until type 2 diabetes develops before acting.

## Key facts

- Hyperinsulinemia is the condition of chronically elevated insulin, resulting from insulin resistance. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p107]
- The body's first response to incipient insulin resistance is to produce more insulin. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p114]
- Postprandial insulin spike is one of the biggest early warning signs that metabolic dysfunction is developing. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p115]
- The author argues hyperinsulinemia should be treated like any other endocrine disorder (like thyroid hormone or cortisol imbalances), yet Medicine 2.0 waits until type 2 diabetes develops. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p115]
- Doing nothing for hyperinsulinemia until diabetes develops is compared to waiting until Graves' disease causes exophthalmos before treating hyperthyroidism. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p115]
