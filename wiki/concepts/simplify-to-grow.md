# Simplify to Grow

## Summary

Simplify to Grow is the second ingredient of good strategy, emphasizing that organizations should 'weed the garden' of stale activities and business units that don't generate surplus, concentrating resources on business areas that will grow. This works because value creation may not be noticed on too large a base, and different managerial systems are needed for growing versus stable businesses.

## Key facts

- Activities or whole chunks of business are needless when they don't generate a surplus. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p92]
- Resources consumed by needless activities may be money, public controversy, or management attention. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p92]
- If you add $100 million in value to a $100 million company, you will be applauded as a managerial genius; adding the same to ExxonMobil, no one will notice. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p92]
- Concentrating and simplifying the managerial task at the top yields the advantages of focus. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p92]

## Inferences

- Inference: The principle recognizes that managerial bandwidth is limited and that focus creates advantages that scale cannot replicate.
