# Homeotic Genes in Brain Development

## Summary

Homeotic genes are a family of evolutionarily conserved genes that control body segmentation and regional identity during embryonic development. In brain development, Hox genes operate in the brainstem and spinal cord, while Otx and Emx genes operate in the forebrain. These genes encode DNA-binding homeobox domains and are expressed in nested overlapping patterns that correspond to brain regional divisions. Changes in their expression can alter brain/body proportions.

## Key facts

- Hox genes are expressed in serial overlapping patterns along the neural tube in vertebrates, corresponding to segmental organization of the brainstem and spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p189]
- Otx and Emx genes are expressed in the forebrain in nested patterns (Otx2>Otx1>Emx2>Emx1), with Emx1 most restricted to the cerebral cortex region. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p189]
- Human Otx genes can partially compensate for fly gene knockouts, demonstrating remarkable evolutionary conservation of function. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p187]
- Increasing X-Otx2 expression in frog embryos causes enlarged head and brain fractions, while decreasing it causes reduction, suggesting concentration threshold effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p193]
- The expanded human dorsal forebrain structures correspond to the expression domains of Otx and Emx genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p199]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gene duplication in homeotic gene families may provide a mechanism for the human shift in brain proportions, similar to experimental introduction of extra gene product.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Direct evidence linking specific human homeotic gene expression differences to brain structure differences is not yet available.
