# False Beliefs

## Summary

False beliefs occur when the brain's model believes something exists that does not exist in the physical world. They persist due to three basic ingredients: they cannot be directly experienced, contrary evidence must be dismissed, and they prescribe behaviors for viral spread.

## Key facts

- Phantom limbs occur because cortical columns that model the limb remain after removal, causing the sufferer to believe the limb exists in some pose. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p169]
- A flat-Earth model was consistent with human sensations for thousands of years and works well for everyday survival, making it a good practical model despite being factually incorrect. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p169]
- False beliefs require dismissing evidence that contradicts them, such as distrusting pictures from space as fake or explorer accounts as fabricated. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p170]
- The three basic ingredients for false beliefs are: cannot directly experience the truth, must ignore contrary evidence, and prescribe viral behaviors for spreading. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p182]

## Inferences

- Inference: False beliefs persist because the brain's model repair mechanisms can be bypassed when evidence is dismissed or attributed to deception.
