# Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex

## Summary

The vestibulo-ocular reflex (VOR) stabilizes images on the retina during head movements by generating compensatory eye movements. The rotational VOR compensates for head rotation using semicircular canal input, while the translational VOR compensates for linear motion using otolith input, with gains that depend on viewing distance.

## Key facts

- The rotational VOR uses a three-neuron arc connecting each semicircular canal to the appropriate eye muscle through the vestibular nuclei and oculomotor nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p687]
- The VOR operates with approximately 10 ms latency, much faster than visual processing at around 100 ms latency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p687]
- Velocity storage in brainstem networks extends the effective duration of VOR signaling beyond when vestibular nerve discharge returns to baseline. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p688]
- The translational VOR compensatory eye movements scale with target distance, producing larger movements for closer objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p689]
- The cerebellar flocculus and paraflocculus are required for adaptive gain changes in the VOR in response to altered visual conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p691]
- The three-neuron arc connects each semicircular canal to the appropriate eye muscle: vestibular nerve fiber to vestibular nucleus to oculomotor nucleus to extraocular muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p977]
- The flocculus and paraflocculus of the cerebellum are required for adapting VOR gain; mossy fibers carry vestibular, visual, and motor signals while climbing fibers transmit visual error signals for motor learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p982]
- Sustained rotation produces alternating slow and fast eye movements called nystagmus, where the slow phase is the vestibular response and the quick phase resets eye position. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p975]
- The VOR is adaptable; wearing magnifying spectacles that change retinal image motion during head movement causes gradual adjustment of VOR gain over several days. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p981]
