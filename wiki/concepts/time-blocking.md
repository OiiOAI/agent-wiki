# Time Blocking

## Summary

Time blocking is a results-oriented method for viewing and using time by scheduling specific blocks for important activities. The book recommends blocking at least four hours daily for the ONE Thing, scheduling time off first, then the ONE Thing, then planning time. Protecting time blocks requires strategies like building a bunker, storing provisions, sweeping for mines, and enlisting support. The concept is supported by examples from successful people including Stephen King, Paul Graham's maker's schedule, and Jerry Seinfeld's calendar chain method.

## Key facts

- Time blocking is described as productivity's greatest power tool. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p129]
- The recommendation is to block at least four hours daily for the ONE Thing. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p134]
- Time blocking should follow this order: (1) time off, (2) ONE Thing, (3) planning time. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p133]
- Stephen King uses mornings as his prime writing time, with afternoons for naps and evenings for reading and revisions. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p134]
- Paul Graham divides work into maker time (requiring large half-day blocks) and manager time (divided into hours). [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p136]
- Jerry Seinfeld reportedly used a calendar with daily X marks to maintain his joke-writing habit, creating visual momentum. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p137]
- Four strategies to protect time blocks are: build a bunker, store provisions, sweep for mines, and enlist support. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p139]
- Paul Graham created a maker's schedule company culture at Y Combinator by clustering all meetings at the end of the day. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p136]
