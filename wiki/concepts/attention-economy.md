# The Attention Economy

## Summary

The competitive marketplace where companies compete for human attention as a scarce resource that can be sold to advertisers. Under this model, success is measured by 'engagement' — minutes and hours of user attention — creating powerful incentives to design products that maximally capture and hold attention, often at the expense of user wellbeing.

## Key facts

- At Google, success was measured by 'engagement,' defined as 'minutes and hours of eyeballs on the product,' because longer viewing equals more advertising revenue. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p122]
- James Williams noted that in this economy, companies are in 'a race for attention,' with every design choice serving that competitive goal. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p129]
- Tristan Harris testified that tech companies' business model is 'screen time, not life time' — prioritizing metrics that directly harm human flourishing. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p139]

## Inferences

- Inference: The attention economy creates a structural conflict of interest between platform profits and user wellbeing that individual choice mechanisms cannot resolve.
