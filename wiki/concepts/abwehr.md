# Abwehr

## Summary

Abwehr (defense) refers to the elementary psychological endeavor to fend off ideas that can arouse feelings of unpleasure, compared to the flight reflex in the presence of painful stimuli. Freud proposes this defensive trend belongs to a lower psychical agency and may be inhibited by higher agencies.

## Key facts

- Freud regards Abwehrbestreben (defensive trend) as one of the main pillars supporting hysterical symptoms—an elementary endeavor to fend off ideas that can arouse unpleasure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2131]
- The defensive trend may be compared to the flight reflex in the presence of painful stimuli. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2131]
- Freud surmises that the architectonic principle of the mental apparatus lies in a stratification—a building up of superimposed agencies—and the defensive endeavor may belong to a lower psychical agency inhibited by higher agencies. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2131]
- Where things cannot be forgotten on their own account, the defensive trend shifts its target and causes something else at least to be forgotten, something less important that has come into associative connection with the objectionable thing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2131]
