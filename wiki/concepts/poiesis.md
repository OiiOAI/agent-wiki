# Poiesis

## Summary

Poiesis refers to creative, original activity—the work of poets, storytellers, inventors, and artists who bring the unexpected into being. Unlike finite productions (poiema), poiesis is the creative act itself that cannot be abstracted from the maker. Culture as an infinite game is itself a poiesis; all its participants are poietai who make possibilities rather than actualities. Art is not art except as it leads to engendering creativity in others. Society depends on its artists to theatricalize property relations, yet genuine poiesis threatens society by reminding it that society is only a species of culture.

- Poiesis is creative activity; all participants in culture are poietai—inventors, makers, artists, storytellers, mythologists—who are not makers of actualities but makers of possibilities. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p67]
- 'Artists do not create objects, but create by way of objects' (Rank). [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p67]
- Art is not art except as it leads to an engendering creativity in its beholders; whoever takes possession of the objects of art has not taken possession of the art. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p67]
- To separate the poiema from poiesis, the created object from the creative act, is the essence of the theatrical. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p78]
