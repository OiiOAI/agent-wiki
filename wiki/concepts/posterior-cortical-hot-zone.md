# Posterior cortical hot zone

## Summary

A bilateral parieto-occipital brain region encompassing the medial and lateral occipital lobe, extending superiorly to the precuneus and posterior cingulate gyrus. When this region shows decreased low-frequency EEG activity (1-4 Hz) and increased high-frequency activity (20-50 Hz), subjects report dreaming experiences. This region's activation pattern distinguishes conscious experience from unconsciousness during both NREM and REM sleep, irrespective of global EEG state.

## Key facts

- The posterior hot zone includes the medial and lateral occipital lobe extending to the precuneus and posterior cingulate gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Decreased low-frequency activity (1-4 Hz) in the posterior hot zone was associated with dream reports, while increased low-frequency activity was associated with no experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p6]
- High-frequency activity (20-50 Hz) in the posterior hot zone correlated with specific dream contents including perceptions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p4]
- The posterior hot zone topography was nearly identical when comparing NREM and REM sleep states for the dream experience vs. no experience contrast. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p4]
- Real-time EEG monitoring of the posterior hot zone achieved 91.6% accuracy for predicting dream experience and 80.7% for no-experience in NREM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p6]

## Inferences

- Inference: The posterior hot zone may represent the core neural correlate of conscious experiences during sleep rather than the broad fronto-parietal network previously hypothesized
- Inference: The medial surface regions within the posterior hot zone (precuneus, posterior cingulate, retrosplenial cortices) may support the virtual simulation of an environment characteristic of dreams
