# Phallic Stage

## Summary

In 'The Infantile Genital Organization' (1923), Freud introduced the concept of the 'phallic stage' as the final pregenital stage of infantile sexuality. At this stage (approximately ages 3–5), the male child discovers that not all beings possess a penis, leading to the castration complex. The defining characteristic of this organization is that only the male genital—specifically the phallus—comes into psychic account; there is no recognition of female genitality. Freud traced how this stage produces the castration complex, depreciation of women, horror of women, and dispositions toward homosexuality, as well as the basis for the Oedipus complex in males. The stage is superseded by the latency period and only fully resolved at puberty when the genital organization reaches its definitive form.

## Key facts

- Freud introduced the concept of the 'infantile genital organization' in 1923, describing a phase of early childhood sexuality in which only the male genital (phallus) comes into psychic account. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6024]
- At this stage, the child initially assumes all beings possess a penis; the discovery of female genitalia leads to the castration complex—the child believes the female has been punished by loss of the penis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6025]
- Freud cited Ferenczi's derivation of Medusa's head as a symbol of horror rooted in the impression of female genitals devoid of a penis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6026]
- The polarity at the phallic stage is between 'having a penis' and 'being castrated,' not yet between male and female; the male/female polarity only crystallizes at puberty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6027]
