# Parapraxis Mechanism

## Summary

The parapraxis mechanism refers to the psychological process by which unconscious thoughts and repressed impulses achieve expression through minor disturbances in conscious mental functioning. Freud establishes that parapraxes involve compromise formations between the intended mental content and the repressed content seeking expression. The mechanism parallels dream-work, employing condensation, contamination, and displacement through external associations. The determining factor is the 'admissibility to consciousness'—material that is repressed or objectionable finds indirect expression through parapraxes when direct expression is blocked.

## Key facts

- The mechanism of parapraxes corresponds to dream formation, involving condensations, contaminations, and the same situation of unconscious thoughts finding expression as modifications of other thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2240]
- Freud identifies that disturbed speech functions occur more readily and make smaller demands on disturbing forces than disturbances in other psychical activities, due to the compliance of linguistic material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2237]
- The determinant for parapraxis expression lies in the greater or lesser degree to which thoughts bear the character of being 'repressed' or inadmissible to consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2239]
- Among the unconscious impulses expressing themselves through parapraxes, no small part is played by suppressed egoistic, jealous, hostile, and sexual feelings. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2239]

## Inferences

- Inference: The same psychical work seen in parapraxes appears in neurotic symptom formation, suggesting a continuum between everyday parapraxes and psychopathological phenomena.
