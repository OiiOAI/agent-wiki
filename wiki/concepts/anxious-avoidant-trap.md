# Anxious-Avoidant Trap

## Summary

The anxious-avoidant trap describes the dysfunctional dynamic between people with anxious and avoidant attachment styles. Anxious people seek closeness while avoidant people push away, creating a self-reinforcing cycle. Research shows avoidant individuals prefer anxiously attached people and anxious people are more likely to date avoidant men, as each style reaffirms the other's beliefs about relationships.

## Key facts

- Pietromonaco and Carnelley found that avoidant individuals actually prefer anxiously attached people. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p78]
- Simpson showed that anxious women are more likely to date avoidant men. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p78]
- Avoidants' belief that they are strong and independent is confirmed by anxious partners who want more closeness. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p78]
- Anxious types find their perception of wanting more intimacy than their partner can provide is confirmed by avoidant partners. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p78]
- Avoidants end relationships more frequently and 'get over' partners quickly, meaning they are in the dating pool more often. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p80]
- People with anxious attachment cope with threats by activating their attachment system and trying to get close, while avoidant people cope by deactivating and distancing themselves. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p127]
- One partner's activation further reinforces the other's deactivation in a vicious cycle, keeping both in the relationship danger zone. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p127]
- Telltale signs include the roller-coaster effect, emotional counterbalancing, stable instability, fighting about trivial matters that mask intimacy issues, life in the inner circle becoming the enemy, and developing an eerie sense the relationship isn't right. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p129]
- Conflict is often left unresolved because the resolution itself creates too much intimacy, which the avoidant partner wants to avoid. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p131]
- Avoidants feel independent and powerful only to the extent that their partner feels needy and incapable, which is why avoidants rarely date one another. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p129]
- When fights between anxious and avoidant partners escalate, avoidants turn off all attachment-related memories and only remember the worst of their partner. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p132]

## Inferences

- Inference: The gravitational pull between anxious and avoidant individuals explains why anxious people often find themselves in unsatisfying relationships despite wanting security
- Inference: Without intervention, anxious-avoidant relationships tend to worsen over time rather than improve, as each new development in the relationship (marriage, children, illness) manifests these fundamental differences and the gap between partners may widen.
