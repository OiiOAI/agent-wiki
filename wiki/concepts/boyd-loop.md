# Boyd Loop

## Summary

The Boyd Loop (also known as the OODA Loop) is a decision-making framework developed by US Air Force Colonel John Boyd, consisting of four stages: Observation, Orientation, Decision, and Action. Boyd theorized that the combatant who could cycle through these stages faster than an opponent would gain disorientation advantage and win.

## Key facts

- The Boyd Loop consists of four sequential stages: observation, orientation, decision, and action. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p94]
- Boyd taught that if you could go around this loop faster than an opponent, the opponent would become disoriented and confused and you would win. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p94]
- Boyd became known as 'forty-second Boyd' because he would bet other fighter pilots that in training he could get on their tail in forty seconds. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p94]
- In Korea, the F-86 Sabre pilots defeated Mig-15 jets partly because American pilots were trained to be quick, flexible, and very aggressive rather than executing precise maneuvers. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p94]
