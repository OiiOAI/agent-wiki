# The Invisible Gorilla Study

## Summary

The invisible gorilla study by Simons and Chabris demonstrated how mindsets can create blindness to what is otherwise visible. Participants watching a basketball video failed to notice a person in a gorilla suit walking through the scene. Langer's follow-up research found that mindfulness instructions significantly increased the likelihood of noticing the gorilla.

## Key facts

- In the invisible gorilla study, participants watching a video of people playing basketball did not see a person in a gorilla suit walk through the scene. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p187]
- Langer's follow-up study found that participants given mindfulness instructions ('notice how it is the same and how it is different') mostly saw the gorilla, while those without instructions did not. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p187]
- Yanai and Lercher found that participants with strong hypotheses missed what was otherwise in plain sight, demonstrating that 'we tend to find what we're looking for and miss the rest.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p187]
