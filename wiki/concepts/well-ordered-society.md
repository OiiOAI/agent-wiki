# Well-Ordered Society

## Summary

A well-ordered society is one designed to advance the good of its members and effectively regulated by a public conception of justice. Everyone accepts and knows that others accept the same principles of justice, and basic institutions satisfy and are known to satisfy these principles. Such a society's conception of justice is stable.

## Key facts

- A well-ordered society is one designed to advance the good of its members and effectively regulated by a public conception of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p403]
- In a well-ordered society everyone accepts and knows that others accept the same principles of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p403]
- A well-ordered society is regulated by its public conception of justice, implying its members have a strong and normally effective desire to act as principles of justice require. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p404]
- A conception of justice is stable when, given just institutions, participants acquire a sense of justice and desire to maintain them. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p404]
- Stability depends upon a balance of motives: the sense of justice must normally win out against propensities toward injustice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p404]
