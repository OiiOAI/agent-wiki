# PDCA Cycle

## Summary

The PDCA Cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act) was developed by Japanese executives who recast Deming's 1950 Deming Wheel. It emphasizes implementation and compliance through a four-step problem-solving cycle and has remained largely unchanged in the last 40 years.

## Key facts

- Japanese executives recast the Deming Wheel from the 1950 JUSE seminar into the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle, though no one person claims authorship of the translation. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p4]
- The correlation between the Deming Wheel and PDCA maps: Design to Plan, Production to Do, Sales to Check, Research to Action. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p4]
- Dr. Ishikawa redefined the PDCA cycle in 1985 to include determining goals and targets and methods for reaching goals in the planning step, and training and education in the do step. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p5]
- PDCA is used for implementation and compliance and has not changed in the last 40 years. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]
- Deming stated in 1991: 'What you propose is not the Deming Cycle. I don't know the source of the cycle that you propose. How the PDCA ever came into existence I know not.'. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact individuals who translated the Deming Wheel into the PDCA Cycle are unknown, as no one person claimed authorship.
