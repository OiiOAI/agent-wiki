# Synaptic Integration

## Summary

Synaptic integration is the process by which neurons combine excitatory and inhibitory postsynaptic potentials from multiple inputs to generate a coordinated action potential output at the axon initial segment.

## Key facts

- Central neurons receive both excitatory (glutamatergic) and inhibitory (GABAergic/glycinergic) synaptic inputs that summate spatially and temporally at the axon initial segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p273]
- A single excitatory input produces a unitary EPSP of only 0.2-0.4 mV in spinal motor neurons, far below the 10 mV or more required to reach action potential threshold. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p274]
- Excitatory synapses typically target dendritic spines (Gray type I, asymmetric), while inhibitory synapses target dendritic shafts, cell bodies, and axon initial segments (Gray type II, symmetric). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p276]
- Synapses closer to the axon initial segment have greater influence on action potential generation because less charge leaks out during passive propagation to the trigger zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p276]
- Neurons can compensate for remote input location by expressing more glutamate receptors at distal synapses, ensuring more equivalent influence at the initial segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p276]
- Synaptic integration is the cellular process underlying the nervous system's ability to make decisions between competing alternatives, as described by Charles Sherrington. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p279]
- The axon initial segment has the lowest threshold for action potential generation because of its high density of voltage-gated Na+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p279]
- Temporal summation allows consecutive EPSPs from the same presynaptic neuron to add together when inputs arrive faster than the membrane time constant allows decay. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p280]
- Spatial summation allows EPSPs from different presynaptic neurons at different locations to add together when they reach the trigger zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p280]
- The membrane length constant determines how far synaptic potentials spread passively; longer length constants facilitate integration of distributed inputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p280]
- Inhibitory inputs can perform mathematical operations on excitatory inputs: hyperpolarizing inhibition performs subtraction, while shunting inhibition performs division. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p276]
- Synapses are classified by the contacting regions: axosomatic, axodendritic, and axo-axonic. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p283]
- Inhibitory shunting is more effective when initiated at the cell body near the axon hillock because inhibitory actions open Cl- channels that shunt depolarization from spreading excitatory currents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p283]
- Some neurons compensate for cable attenuation by placing more glutamate receptors at distal synapses, ensuring inputs along the dendritic tree have more equivalent strength at the initial segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p283]
- Significant inhibitory input in the brain frequently occurs on the cell body of neurons, allowing it to effectively interrupt excitatory inputs traveling to the trigger zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p283]

## Inferences

- Inference: Synaptic integration represents the fundamental computational operation of neurons, transforming multiple inputs into a single output pattern.
- Inference: Dendritic voltage-gated channels can modulate synaptic integration through amplification or nonlinear processing.
