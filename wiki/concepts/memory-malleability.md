# Memory Malleability

## Summary

The phenomenon demonstrating that memories are not static recordings but are reconstructed each time they are recalled, making them susceptible to modification through suggestion, questioning, and外部 influences.

## Key facts

- Elizabeth Loftus showed that changing a single word in a question ('smashed' vs 'hit') significantly altered witnesses' speed estimates and even whether they remembered seeing broken glass. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p70]
- Frederic Bartlett demonstrated that people reconstruct memories based on their interpretations, remembering schemas rather than exact details, leading to progressive distortion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p68]
- The case of Ronald Cotton demonstrates how eyewitness testimony can be completely wrong despite the witness's complete certainty, as DNA evidence later proved. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p72]
- Jennifer Thompson, despite deliberately trying to memorize her rapist's face during the attack, incorrectly identified Ronald Cotton and could not identify the actual culprit even when faced with him. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p73]
