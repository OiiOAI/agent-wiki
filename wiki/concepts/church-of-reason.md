# Church of Reason

## Summary

Phædrus called the University a 'Church of Reason' to distinguish between the real University (a state of mind, the continuing body of reason itself) and the legal entity (buildings, property, salaries). The real University cannot be located, has no property, and regenerates through centuries via those who carry rational thought. Loss of accreditation merely recognizes that the real University has left the location.

## Key facts

- The real University has no specific location, owns no property, pays no salaries, and receives no material dues; it is a state of mind—continued rational thought through the centuries. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p125]
- The legal entity called a university is a nonprofit corporation, a branch of the state with a specific address that cannot teach or generate new knowledge. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p125]
- The primary goal of the Church of Reason is always Socrates' old goal of truth, revealed by the process of rationality; everything else is subordinate. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p126]
- When a college loses accreditation, the real University—which no legislature can dictate to—simply declares the place no longer 'holy ground' and vanishes from it. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p125]
- Phædrus describes the Church of Reason as being 'founded on their graves' (the ancient Greek rhetoricians), 'supported today by their graves,' and containing 'ghosts' when you 'dig deep into its foundations.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p145]
- The narrator states: 'The crisis is being caused by the inadequacy of existing forms of thought to cope with the situation. It can't be solved by rational means because the rationality itself is the source of the problem.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p142]
- Phædrus compared the needed expansion of reason to Newton's invention of calculus: 'Newton invented a new form of reason. He expanded reason to handle infinitesimal changes and I think what is needed now is a similar expansion of reason to handle technological ugliness.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p143]
- The narrator describes present-day reason as 'an analogue of the flat earth of the medieval period' where going 'too far beyond it you're presumed to fall off, into insanity.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p144]
