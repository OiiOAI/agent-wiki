# Good-Enough Brain

## Summary

The 'good-enough brain' is a conceptual framework describing how the brain makes fast, efficient decisions using a minority of highly active neurons connected by strong synapses, while a larger network of slower neurons enables precision when needed. This represents a tradeoff between speed and accuracy, with the fast-firing 'rich club' providing best-guess solutions under most conditions.

## Key facts

- The good-enough brain is largely prewired and acts quickly via a minority of highly active, bursting neurons connected by fast-conducting axons and strong synapses into a network. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p338]
- The privileged minority of fast-firing neurons is responsible for perhaps half of all spikes and has faster access to the rest of the brain than the remaining majority. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p338]
- Strongly interactive circuits forming the good-enough brain can generalize across situations but with less than perfect fidelity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p338]
- Precision requires deploying a large fraction of slow-firing neurons with plastic properties connected by weaker synapses into a more loosely formed giant network. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p339]
- Just ten strongly task-related neurons can predict 60% accuracy for limb position; adding spikes from ninety more neurons improves prediction by only a few percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p339]
- The good-enough brain makes fast decisions by evaluating relationships among input variables rather than identifying each item separately in complete detail. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p350]

## Inferences

- Inference: The good-enough brain concept parallels Kahneman's System 1 (fast, intuitive) versus System 2 (slow, deliberative), but emphasizes they are two tails of one continuum rather than discrete systems.
