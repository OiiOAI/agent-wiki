# Processing Postulate

## Summary

The Processing Postulate is Crick's speculative proposal that each level of visual processing is coordinated by a single thalamic region. It suggests that consciousness may be composed of multiple 'processing units' or 'awareness units,' each with its own semiglobal representation, characteristic processing time, and form of representation. The thalamus acts as an 'organ of attention' coordinating these units.

## Key facts

- The Processing Postulate states that each level of visual processing is coordinated by a single thalamic region, and any one cortical area is strongly associated with only one part of the thalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p263]
- Cortical area V1 is strongly associated only with the LGN, not with any other part of the thalamus, and may be the seat of a rather fleeting form of visual awareness that does not require an attentional mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p263]
- Crick proposes that each processing unit would have its own semiglobal representation covering several cortical areas, its own characteristic processing time, and its own particular form of representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p264]
- The idea that the thalamus is a key player in consciousness was suggested earlier by Wilder Penfield, and more recently by mathematician David Mumford. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p265]
- James Newman and Bernard Baars suggest that information broadcast to the global workspace comes from the intralaminar nuclei, including the nucleus centralis which is closely associated with the visual system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p265]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Processing Postulate implies that 'consciousness' and 'unconscious' are too broad terms for distinct brain activities; they may need replacement with terms like 'processing unit' or 'awareness unit.'
- Inference: Not enough is yet known about thalamic connections to determine whether the Processing Postulate is true or not.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether each subdivision of the pulvinar is associated with one of Marr's visual processing stages, or whether smaller sub-subdivisions correspond to levels in the visual hierarchy, is unknown.
- Uncertain: The exact nature of how thalamic regions coordinate their associated cortical areas remains speculative.
