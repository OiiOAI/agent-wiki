# Hypnoid States

## Summary

Hypnoid states are conditions resembling hypnosis that play a crucial role in the genesis of hysteria. They are characterized by: (1) withdrawal of ideas from associative contact and correction, (2) amnesia during waking life for experiences occurring in these states, and (3) facilitated conversion of affective excitations into somatic phenomena. They arise through emotional shock, exhaustion, or the introduction of affect into habitual reverie.

## Key facts

- Moebius proposed that the necessary condition for pathogenic operation of ideas is a special frame of mind resembling hypnosis, where an emerging idea meets with no resistance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p191-192]
- The importance of hypnoid states lies in the amnesia that accompanies them and in their power to bring about the splitting of the mind fundamental to major hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p216]
- States filled with emotion and states of fatigue arising from protracted affects are pathogenic, whereas unemotional twilight states are not. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p218]

## Inferences

- Inference: Autohypnosis may develop when affect is introduced into a habitual reverie, creating conditions similar to hypnotic susceptibility where suggested ideas gain unchallenged influence.
