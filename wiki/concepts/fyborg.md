# Fyborg

## Summary

Fyborg (functional cyborg) refers to biological organisms functionally supplemented with technological extensions. The concept, coined by Alexander Chislenko, notes that humans have been fyborgs for thousands of years through use of tools, clothing, and more recently electronic devices. This contrasts with 'cyborgs' which have physical integration of biological and technological structures.

## Key facts

- Chislenko coined the term fyborg and proposed a self-test: Are you dependent on technology to the extent you could not survive without it? Would you reject a lifestyle free of technology?. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p334]
- The first caveman who slapped animal hide across his foot and refused to leave home without it became a fyborg to a limited degree. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p334]
- Manfred Clynes and Nathan Cline coined 'cyborg' in 1960 to describe organisms built for space travel with biochemical, physiological, and electronic modifications. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p344]
- Shoes, clothing, watches, eyeglasses, and cell phones are all examples of functional cyborgization that have been well accepted by society. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p334]
