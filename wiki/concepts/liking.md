# Liking

## Summary

Liking is a principle of compliance stating that we most prefer to say yes to requests from people we know and like. This principle is exploited professionally through friendship as a compliance trigger, exemplified by Tupperware parties where the sales request comes from a friend rather than a stranger. Research indicates that the social bond between hostess and partygoers is twice as likely to determine purchase as preference for the product itself.

## Key facts

- Tupperware Home Parties Corporation arranges for customers to buy from and for a friend rather than an unknown salesperson by providing hostesses with a percentage of sales. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p153]
- The Shaklee Corporation advises salespeople to use the 'endless chain' method, approaching prospects with the name of a friend who suggested the call, claiming it is 'virtually as good as a sale 50 percent made before you enter.'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p154]
