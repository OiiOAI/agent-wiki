# Revision of Dream Theory

## Summary

The 1932 revision of Freud's dream theory reaffirmed core principles while introducing new understanding of the superego's role in dream censorship and addressing difficult cases including traumatic neuroses and infantile trauma dreams. Key advances included experimental confirmation of dream symbolism and a revised understanding of punishment dreams.

## Key facts

- Freud reaffirmed the fundamental distinction between manifest dream content and latent dream thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6850]
- The dream censorship is identified as a function of the superego, which Freud postulates based on observations of delusions of being observed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6856]
- Traumatic neuroses present a challenge to the wish-fulfillment theory of dreams, as patients are regularly taken back to traumatic situations against any apparent wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6869]
- Freud proposes that in traumatic cases, unconscious fixation to trauma allows the upward pressure of traumatic fixation to become active during sleep, causing failure of the dream-work's wish-fulfilling function. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6869]
- Experimental confirmation of dream symbolism was reported through studies by Schrötter (1912) and Betlheim & Hartmann (1924), demonstrating that sexual content emerges in dreams through familiar symbolic substitutions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6863]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's concession that dream function may fail in traumatic cases represents a significant acknowledgment of theoretical limitations, though he maintained that exceptions do not overturn the general rule that dreams are wish fulfillments.
