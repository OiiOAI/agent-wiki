# Serotonin Systems

## Summary

Serotonin (5-HT) is an indoleamine neurotransmitter arising from raphe nuclei in the brain stem midline. It generally inhibits behavior and emotional expression, with widespread projections throughout the brain.

## Key facts

- Serotonin reduces the impact of incoming information and cross-talk between sensory channels, generally inhibiting behavior and emotional expression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p62]
- Serotonin neurons are clustered in raphe nuclei (B1-B9) at the midline of the brain stem, among the most ancient structures in brain evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p64]
- The dorsal raphe nucleus (B7) controls dorsal brain activity in hippocampus and neocortex, while B9 controls ventral activity in hypothalamus, limbic system, and striatum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p64]
- Serotonin synthesis is rate-limited by availability of precursor amino acid tryptophan, so dietary tryptophan can increase brain 5-HT levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p61]
