# Life-Alienating Communication

## Summary

Life-alienating communication refers to forms of language and communication that alienate people from their natural state of compassion, leading them to behave violently and exploitatively toward each other and themselves. Rosenberg identifies four main forms: (1) moralistic judgments that imply wrongness, (2) making comparisons, (3) denial of responsibility for one's own thoughts, feelings, and actions, and (4) communicating desires as demands. These patterns promote violence and prevent compassionate connection.

## Key facts

- Life-alienating communication is identified as specific forms of language that contribute to behaving violently toward each other and ourselves. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p40]
- Moralistic judgments are expressions of our own values and needs when they imply wrongness or badness on the part of people who don't act in harmony with our values, expressed through blame, insults, put-downs, labels, criticism, comparisons, and diagnoses. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p41]
- Psychology professor O.J. Harvey's research at the University of Colorado shows a high correlation between frequent use of classifying and judging words and frequency of violent incidents. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p43]
- Making comparisons is identified as a form of judgment that blocks compassion both for oneself and others. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p44]
- Denial of responsibility obscures awareness that we are each responsible for our own thoughts, feelings, and actions, expressed through phrases like 'have to' and 'you make me feel guilty.'. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p45]
- We deny responsibility for our actions when we attribute their cause to vague impersonal forces, our condition or diagnosis, the actions of others, the dictates of authority, group pressure, institutional policies, gender or social roles, or uncontrollable impulses. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p45-46]
- Life-alienating communication both stems from and supports hierarchical or domination societies, where populations are controlled by a small number of individuals for their benefit. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p49]

## Inferences

- Inference: Life-alienating communication is rooted in views of human nature that stress humans' innate evil and deficiency, requiring education to control undesirable nature.
