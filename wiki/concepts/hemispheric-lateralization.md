# Hemispheric Lateralization

## Summary

The specialization of different cognitive functions to each cerebral hemisphere. McGilchrist argues that the hemispheres differ not in 'what' they process but in 'how' they process—the manner or mode of experience. Functional lateralization is widespread in vertebrates, not unique to humans, suggesting it offers evolutionary advantages.

## Key facts

- Hemispheric lateralization is widespread in vertebrates, appearing far further down the phylogenetic tree than language or handedness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p44]
- The right hemisphere is responsible for every type of attention except focussed attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p64]
- Lateralisation brings evolutionary advantages, particularly in carrying out dual-attention tasks; as one researcher put it, 'asymmetry pays'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p46]
- In the West, about 89 per cent of people are broadly right-handed, and the vast majority of these have speech and semantic language centres in the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p26]
- The hemispheres show consistent differences at multiple levels: structural (size, neuronal count, grey/white matter ratio), neurochemical (different neurotransmitter dependencies), and physiological. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p55]
- The right hemisphere apprehends the world as it really is, with its attention to the individual, the concrete, the implicit, and the metaphoric. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p372]
- The left hemisphere constructs a simplified, abstracted, and fragmented model of the world, prioritizing fixed, determinate, and explicit categories. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p372]
- The left hemisphere acts as the 'emissary' of the right hemisphere, whose proper role is to report back and execute plans, not to rule. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p372]
- The right hemisphere prefers vertical lines and reading from top to bottom; the left hemisphere prefers horizontal lines and linear sequence. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p384]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistency of lateralization across species suggests it serves fundamental adaptive purposes related to incompatible attentional demands rather than being merely contingent.
- Inference: The 'winner takes all' phenomenon means even small initial advantages in one hemisphere become amplified over time through a 'snowball' mechanism.
