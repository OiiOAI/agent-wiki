# Ecological Psychology

## Summary

Ecological psychology is the approach developed by James J. Gibson in his later works, characterized by a rejection of both behaviorism and cognitivism. It emphasizes that vision depends on the eyes in the head on a body supported by the ground, with the brain as merely the central organ of a complete visual system. The approach emphasizes direct perception of the environment rather than indirect, representational theories.

## Key facts

- Termed 'ecological psychology' by Gibson himself. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p2]
- Rejects behaviorism (which Gibson attacked earlier) and cognitivism (which he criticized in his later works). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p2]
- Is increasingly influential on many contemporary post-cognitivist movements in psychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p2]
