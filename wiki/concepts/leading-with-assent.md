# Leading with Assent

## Summary

Leading with assent is a communication technique that validates the other person's perspective before introducing potentially contradictory information. It involves listening for areas of agreement, stating those specifically, and using 'and' instead of 'but' when adding new information. Drawing from improvisational theater's 'yes, and' principle, this approach maintains partnership in truth-seeking and reduces defensiveness, making listeners more receptive to dissent and new ideas.

## Key facts

- Leading with assent means listening for things you agree with, stating those specifically, and following with 'and' instead of 'but'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- 'And' is an offer to contribute; 'but' is a denial and repudiation of what came before. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- In improvisational theater, the first advice is to respond with 'yes, and'—accepting the construct and adding to it. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- When new information is presented as supplementing rather than negating what came before, listeners are more open to it. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
