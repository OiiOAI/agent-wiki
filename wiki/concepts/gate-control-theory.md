# Gate Control Theory of Pain

## Summary

Proposed by Melzack and Wall, this theory states that the relative balance of activity in nociceptive (C fibers) and nonnociceptive (Aβ fibers) afferents regulates transmission of pain signals through inhibitory interneurons in the dorsal horn. Activation of large Aβ fibers closes the gate, suppressing pain transmission.

## Key facts

- Projection neurons in the dorsal horn are excited by C-fiber nociceptors and inhibited by Aβ-fiber-activated interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p535]
- The interaction between large and small fibers occurs at the first convergence site on projection neurons in the dorsal horn. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p535]
- Gate control theory explains the pain relief produced by vibration and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p535]
- The gate-control theory proposes that activation of non-nociceptive sensory neurons closes a gate for central transmission of nociceptive signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p598]
- In the simplest form of the gate-control model, the interaction between large and small fibers occurs at the first possible site of convergence on projection neurons in the dorsal horn of the spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p598]
- Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) activates large-diameter afferent fibers that overlap the area of injury and pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p598]

## Inferences

- Inference: The gate control theory's concept of convergence between sensory modalities explains why behaviors like shaking a hand after injury can provide pain relief - by activating non-nociceptive large-diameter afferents that suppress nociceptive transmission.
