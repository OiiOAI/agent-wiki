# Gate Control Theory of Pain

## Summary

Proposed by Melzack and Wall, this theory states that the relative balance of activity in nociceptive (C fibers) and nonnociceptive (Aβ fibers) afferents regulates transmission of pain signals through inhibitory interneurons in the dorsal horn. Activation of large Aβ fibers closes the gate, suppressing pain transmission.

## Key facts

- Projection neurons in the dorsal horn are excited by C-fiber nociceptors and inhibited by Aβ-fiber-activated interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p535]
- The interaction between large and small fibers occurs at the first convergence site on projection neurons in the dorsal horn. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p535]
- Gate control theory explains the pain relief produced by vibration and transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p535]
