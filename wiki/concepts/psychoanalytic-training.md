# Psychoanalytic Training

## Summary

The specialized education required to practice psychoanalysis. Freud outlined that training should include a personal analysis, theoretical instruction in depth psychology and the unconscious, familiarity with the science of sexual life, and the delicate technique of psychoanalysis including interpretation, fighting resistances, and handling the transference. He argued that medical education does not adequately prepare doctors for analytic work and that analytic training cuts across the field of medical education.

## Key facts

- At the training institutes, candidates themselves are taken into analysis, receive theoretical instruction through lectures, and enjoy supervision when making their first trials with comparatively slight cases. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6436]
- A period of some two years is calculated for training, after which the candidate is only a beginner and not yet a master. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6436]
- Freud argued that analytic training should include elements from the human sciences, psychology, the history of civilization and sociology, as well as from anatomy, biology and the study of evolution. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6458]
- Freud emphasized the analyst's obligation to make himself capable, by a deep-going analysis of his own, of the unprejudiced reception of the analytic material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6428]
- The three training institutes existing in 1926 were in Berlin, Vienna, and London. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6436]
