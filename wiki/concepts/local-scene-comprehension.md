# Local Scene Comprehension

## Summary

Local scene comprehension focuses on a driver's ability to understand the meaning inherent in the nearby traffic, such as identifying potential hazards. In contrast to scene perception and projection (which deals with speeds and distances), local scene comprehension requires comprehending the semantic significance of traffic situations. Scene-interpretation probes and performance probes (hazard detection) are hypothesized to reflect local scene comprehension abilities.

## Key facts

- Local scene comprehension focuses on a driver's ability to understand the meaning inherent in the nearby traffic (e.g., to identify potential hazards). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p262]
- Scene-interpretation probes and performance probes required participants to comprehend the meaning of speed and distance information in order to identify and avoid hazards. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p268]
- Hazard detection was calculated using A' nonparametric signal-detection measure, with participants achieving 86% accuracy (SD = 12) and reliability of .80 in Experiment 3. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p278]
- Scene interpretation questions included: which car was driving erratically, which car was tailgating, which car is most likely to change lanes now, which car is driving fastest (or slowest) now, and which two cars are on a collision course now. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p268]

## Inferences

- Inference: Local scene comprehension involves higher-level cognitive processing beyond basic perception, requiring integration of multiple pieces of information to assess traffic situations.
