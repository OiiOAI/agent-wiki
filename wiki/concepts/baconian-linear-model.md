# The Baconian Linear Model

## Summary

The Baconian Linear Model is the belief that knowledge flows from basic research to academic science, then to technology, and finally to practical applications and economic growth. Taleb argues this model is largely false—the reverse is often true, with practice generating theory rather than theory generating practice.

## Key facts

- The Baconian linear model posits: Academia → Applied Science and Technology → Practice, implying knowledge flows from academic science to practical applications. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p249]
- Taleb states there is no rigorous evidence that the Baconian model is true, and the exact reverse seems true in most examined domains. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p249]
- Academic research appears to cause wealth, but evidence suggests the reverse is true—wealth leads to education, not the other way around. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p260]
- Taiwan had much lower literacy than the Philippines in 1960 but today has ten times the income; Korea had lower literacy than Argentina but now has three times the income. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p260]
- Terence Kealey, a biochemist, argues that universities prospered as a consequence of national wealth, not the cause of it, and that government intervention in funding research decreased private investment. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p285]

## Inferences

- Inference: The model is an epiphenomenon—wealthy countries have both universities and prosperity, but this correlation doesn't establish causation from universities to wealth.
