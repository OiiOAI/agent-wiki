# Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation in Social Neuroscience

## Summary

Transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) and transcranial electrical stimulation (tES, including tDCS) are noninvasive brain stimulation methods used to test causal relationships between neural activity and social behavior. TMS uses magnetic pulses to induce action potentials with good spatial precision, while tDCS induces neuroplastic effects by modulating neuronal sensitivity. These methods have established causal links between DLPFC and TPJ activity and various aspects of social behavior including emotions, cognitions, and behavioral control.

## Key facts

- TMS uses magnetic pulses to induce action potentials in targeted neural populations with good spatial precision (around 1 cm) and can modulate neural function both during and minutes after stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p234]
- tDCS changes the excitability of neurons by means of direct currents, with anodal stimulation generally increasing and cathodal stimulation decreasing neuronal excitability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p235]
- Disruption of the right DLPFC with repetitive TMS increased utilitarian choices to sacrifice one person for the benefit of several others in moral judgment tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p236]
- Disruption of the right temporoparietal junction reduced the role of beliefs in moral judgments, with participants grounding judgments more on consequences than on intentions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p237]
- Anodal tDCS over right LPFC decreased voluntary fairness compliance but increased sanction-induced compliance, while cathodal tDCS had the opposite effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p239]
- Effects of LPFC tDCS on norm compliance were significantly more pronounced in social conditions (interacting with humans) than nonsocial conditions (interacting with computer algorithms), establishing social specificity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p241]

## Inferences

- Inference: The social-specific effects of tDCS on LPFC suggest that this brain region's involvement in behavioral control is specific to social contexts rather than general decision-making.
- Inference: The ability to both enhance and reduce behavior through different tDCS polarities suggests these methods have therapeutic potential for modulating social behavior.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Major shortcomings of TMS include that many protocols may take minutes to apply and may generate distracting noises and sensations.
- Uncertain: Both TMS and tES can typically only be applied to superficial areas of neocortex, precluding routine stimulation of subcortical structures.
