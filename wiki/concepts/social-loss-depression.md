# Social Loss and Depression

## Summary

The major life factor precipitating depression in humans is social loss. The neurobiological cascade of depression begins with activation of brain CRF and the pituitary adrenal stress response, followed by depletion of brain norepinephrine, serotonin, and dopamine reserves. This cascade is initiated by the protest phase of separation (crying) and progresses to the despair phase (depression). Social contact can cure depressive responses, and all antidepressants facilitate synaptic activity of biogenic amine systems.

## Key facts

- The major life factor that precipitates depression in humans is social loss. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- The genesis of many forms of depression can be linked to the neurobiological nature of the primal-loss experience—the despair of children separated from parents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- The cascade of events during separation includes activation of brain CRF and pituitary adrenal stress response, followed by depletion of norepinephrine, serotonin, and dopamine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Social contact is sometimes sufficient to cure depressive responses in young animals after isolation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- All antidepressants facilitate synaptic activity of biogenic amine systems, whether by blockade of synaptic reuptake or inhibition of degradation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Harlow's research showed isolated rhesus monkey babies preferred inanimate 'terry-cloth mothers' over hard wire ones providing only nourishment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Isolated females became poor and abusive mothers, especially toward firstborn, but subsequent offspring received better treatment from learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]

## Inferences

- Inference: The evolutionary function of the despair response may be to conserve limited energy resources and discourage helpless organisms from wandering farther from safety
- Inference: Pets can promote mental health and emotional equilibrium by providing social interaction and warmth
- Inference: The most effective treatment for social-isolation-induced problems was exposure to much younger monkeys that provoked safe and playful social interactions

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Precisely how physiological changes lead to the persistent psychological changes of clinical depression is not yet known
- Uncertain: Whether future CRF-inhibiting and oxytocin-promoting drugs will have antidepressant effects remains to be demonstrated
