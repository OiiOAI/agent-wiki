# The Peter Principle

## Summary

A management principle stating that people are promoted until they reach their level of incompetence and then stay there. Grove provides a detailed description of how the principle operates through alternating 'meets requirements' and 'exceeds requirements' ratings throughout a career.

## Key facts

- The Peter Principle states that when someone is good at their job, they are promoted until they reach their level of incompetence and then stay there. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L2000-L2002]
- An achiever alternates between 'meets requirements' and 'exceeds requirements' ratings throughout their career, settling at 'meets requirements' when no longer promoted. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L2010-L2012]
- If a person who 'exceeds requirements' is not given more work and greater challenges, they will atrophy and performance will return to 'meets requirements' level. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L2018-L2021]
- Grove advocates recycling—putting someone promoted beyond their capability back into the job they did well—rather than forcing them to leave the company. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L2028-L2031]
- Management should support employees facing embarrassment from being recycled, and if done openly, the embarrassment will be short-lived. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L2033-L2035]

## Inferences

- Inference: There is no alternative to the Peter Principle because not promoting high performers leads to atrophy.
- Inference: Recycled employees who regain confidence can become excellent candidates for another promotion later.
