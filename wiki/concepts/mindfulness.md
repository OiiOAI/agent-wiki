# Mindfulness

## Summary

Mindfulness is the overarching theme of DBT and creates distance between thoughts and oneself. It wedges space between a stimulus and a knee-jerk response, allowing calmer and more rational processing. Attia notes that mindfulness helps recognize when suffering stems from thinking about painful past events or worrying about future events rather than present circumstances.

## Key facts

- Mindfulness creates distance between thoughts and oneself, wedging space between a stimulus and a knee-jerk response. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p411]
- When suffering occurs, it is rarely due to a direct present cause; more often it comes from thinking about painful past events or worrying about future events. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p411]
- The Buddha said that 'your worst enemy cannot harm you as much as your own unguarded thoughts'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p411]
- Seneca observed that 'we suffer more often in imagination than in reality'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p411]
- Shakespeare's Hamlet noted 'There is nothing either good or bad, but thinking makes it so'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p411]
- Harvard researchers found that eight weeks of mindfulness training changed brain regions associated with stress, memory, sense of self, and empathy. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p82]
- Mindfulness improves connectivity in brain networks that keep us from being distracted and increases competence. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p82]
- Ellen Langer's research found that musicians who play mindfully produce music audiences like better, and mindful salesmen sell more subscriptions. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p82-83]
- Mindfulness allows observation of the thinker having the thoughts, creating space between thought and action. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p84]
- A study found that most people preferred to give themselves mild electric shocks rather than sit alone with their thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p84]

## Inferences

- Inference: The research suggesting structural brain changes from mindfulness practice implies that emotional agility skills can be developed through practice, not just understood intellectually.
