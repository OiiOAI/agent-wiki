# Dual Structure of Japanese Labor Market

## Summary

The dual structure refers to the division in Japan's labor market between large companies offering lifetime employment and smaller enterprises with more flexible, often precarious employment practices. This structure provides large companies with mechanisms to adjust employment levels.

## Key facts

- Lifetime employment is a privilege only of large companies and is not nearly as widely practiced among small companies. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p419]
- Many big companies can cut their own employment by pushing unneeded workers onto their subsidiaries, where they can be paid less and ultimately be fired. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p419]
- The threat of dropping out of the large-company employment pool is a very live sanction motivating employees to work hard. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p419]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dual structure creates a hierarchical labor market where workers aspire to large company positions as a status marker and employment security guarantee.
