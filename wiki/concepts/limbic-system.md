# Limbic System

## Summary

The limbic system, also called the visceral or old-mammalian brain, is a brain region essential for emotional processing. It contains the amygdala, hippocampus, septal area, preoptic area, hypothalamus, and central gray of the mesencephalon. The concept was elaborated by Paul MacLean as part of his triune brain theory, and while some anatomists debate its precise boundaries, most agree these areas participate importantly in emotional processes.

## Key facts

- The major areas included in the limbic system are the amygdala, hippocampus, septal area, preoptic area, hypothalamus, and central gray of the mesencephalon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- The limbic system is roughly similar in relative size across different mammalian species, being small in reptiles but large in all mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- Limbic epilepsies produce psychomotor fits but rarely cause full-blown convulsions, since their electrical storms tend to be restricted to the visceral parts of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p43]
- The limbic system elaborates ancient family values and other uniquely mammalian emotional tendencies that interact intimately with visceral organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- The limbic system includes hypothalamic and mesencephalic areas with neurochemically coded pathways for control of emotional and motivated behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
- Key visual pathways of the limbic system include the fornix, stria terminalis, ventral amygdalofugal pathway, mamillothalamic tract, habenulopeduncular tract, and medial forebrain bundle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
- The stria terminalis sends descending information from the amygdala to a broad synaptic field from the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis to the ventromedial nucleus of the hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p48]
- The medial forebrain bundle is the area where self-stimulation and stimulus-bound emotive behaviors can be obtained most easily through electrical stimulation of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p48]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Some modern neuroscientists disagree that the limbic system should be considered an anatomically and functionally distinct entity.
- Uncertain: The precise boundaries and complete functional characterization of the limbic system remain debated.
