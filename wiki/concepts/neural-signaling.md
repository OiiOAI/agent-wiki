# Neural Signaling

## Summary

Neural signaling involves four functional components: input (receptor potentials), integration at the trigger zone, conduction via action potentials, and output (neurotransmitter release). Receptor potentials are graded signals proportional to stimulus intensity. If depolarization reaches threshold at the initial segment, an all-or-none action potential is generated that propagates without decay at 1-100 m/s. At synapses, action potentials trigger neurotransmitter release.

## Key facts

- The resting membrane potential of a typical neuron is approximately -65 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p112]
- Receptor potentials are graded in amplitude and duration, proportional to the stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p113]
- Action potentials are all-or-none signals that do not decay during propagation because they are periodically regenerated at nodes of Ranvier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p114]
- The information conveyed by action potentials is encoded in frequency and pattern of firing, not in amplitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p115]
