# Mindful Contagion

## Summary

Mindful contagion is the phenomenon whereby one person's mindfulness can increase the mindfulness of others in their proximity. Research suggests that interacting with mindful individuals leads observers to notice more details, perform better on cognitive tasks, and exhibit more engaged behavior, while interacting with mindless individuals produces the opposite effects.

## Key facts

- Customers who received mindful sales pitches—varying each pitch subtly—described salesmen as charismatic and were more likely to purchase magazines than those hearing mindless repetitive pitches. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p156]
- Children at a summer camp who interacted with mindful interviewers showed higher self-esteem, liked the camp more, were happier, and thought the interviewer liked them more than children interacting with mindless interviewers. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p157]
- When near someone mindful, people were significantly more likely to notice errors in familiar text (24 of 25 participants) compared to those near someone mindless (only 11 of 70 noticed). [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p159]
- Participants in the presence of a mindful experimenter drank half as much alcohol (2 ounces) as those with a mindless experimenter (4 ounces) in a wine-tasting study. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p162]
- Autistic children showed more 'fun behaviors,' more interaction, more collaborative behaviors, and fewer avoidance and stereotyped behaviors when interacting with mindful adults. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p163]
- In the 'Something in the Air' study, participants who entered a room where meditators had been meditating showed faster reaction times than those entering rooms previously occupied by people watching stressful videos or unoccupied rooms. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mindful contagion may explain why caregiver mindfulness correlates with patient functioning—the caregiver's mindfulness may increase the mindfulness of those in their care, benefiting health outcomes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The mechanism for 'Something in the Air' effects is unknown and requires further investigation.
