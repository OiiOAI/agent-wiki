# Goal-Based Concepts

## Summary

Goal-based concepts are mental categories organized around intentions or purposes rather than just perceptual similarities. For example, 'Fish' can be a pet or a dinner depending on the goal. Barrett argues that humans are uniquely equipped to create goal-based concepts, which is essential for forming emotion concepts. Animals like macaques lack the neural wiring for this ability, limiting them to action-based concepts for concrete objects and behaviors.

## Key facts

- Humans construct goal-based concepts; a macaque brain lacks the necessary wiring, which accounts for their smaller affective niche. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p281]
- A human brain is better equipped to create purely mental concepts like 'Wealth,' whereas a chimpanzee brain is better equipped for concepts of actions and concrete objects like 'Eating' and 'Banana.'. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p282]
- Chimpanzees cannot imagine something completely novel, cannot consider the same situation from different points of view, and do not realize that goal-based information resides inside the heads of other creatures. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p285]
- Words don't seem to be intrinsically part of most apes' affective niche, as they are for typical human babies; to apes, words alone are not worth learning. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p283]
