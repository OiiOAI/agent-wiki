# Limits to Compounding

## Summary

The mathematical and economic constraints on how fast wealth can compound over long periods. While short-term returns may be high, the aggregate wealth of the world cannot sustain rates like 10-11% after inflation indefinitely.

## Key facts

- Common stocks have generated returns of ten to eleven percent after inflation for many years, but those returns cannot continue for a very long period because the wealth of the world will compound at no such rate. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p269]
- The $24 real estate investment by the Dutch to buy Manhattan would today, by some estimates, be roughly equivalent to $3 trillion—over 378 years, that's about a seven percent annual compound rate of return. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p270]
- Berkshire Hathaway's future rate of compounding shareholders' wealth is going to go down compared to its past, and its size will be an anchor dragging on performance—this is not an opinion but a promise. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p269]
