# Manifest and Latent Dream Content

## Summary

The distinction between manifest and latent content is fundamental to Freud's dream theory. Manifest content is the apparent story or imagery of the dream as remembered upon waking. Latent content comprises the hidden psychological meaning—dream thoughts, memories, wishes, and conflicts—that analysis reveals. The transformation from latent to manifest content occurs through the dream-work.

## Key facts

- Freud emphasizes that his theory of dreams as wish fulfillments 'is not based on a consideration of the manifest content of dreams but refers to the thoughts which are shown by the work of interpretation to lie behind dreams.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1406]
- The two-content distinction addresses why distressing and unpleasant dreams can still be wish fulfillments at the latent level. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1406]
- The dream of the abandoned supper party demonstrates how manifest content about food and parties concealed latent jealousy and competition with a rival. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1416]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction resolves the apparent contradiction that critics raise against wish-fulfillment theory: distressing dream content is manifest, but the latent meaning behind it remains wish-fulfilling.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud notes that readers and critics frequently overlook this fundamental distinction despite its centrality to the theory.
