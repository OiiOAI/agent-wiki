# Color Vision

## Summary

Color vision is based on the comparison of signals from three types of cone photoreceptors (L, M, and S types sensitive to long, medium, and short wavelengths) and the opponent-process mechanism. The retina and visual cortex transform this into parallel chromatic pathways. Congenital color blindness results from alterations in the genes encoding cone pigments.

## Key facts

- Primates have three kinds of cone photoreceptors distinguished by the range of wavelengths they absorb: L (long-wave), M (medium-wave), and S (short-wave) cones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p632]
- Red light excites L cones more than M cones, while green light excites M cones more, allowing the brain to distinguish spectrum from intensity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p634]
- The opponent-process theory proposes three processes responding in opposite ways: yellow-blue, red-green, and white-black. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p594]
- P-type ganglion cells receive input from single cones, creating small receptive field centers with chromatic opponency between L and M or M and L cones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p594]
- Protanopia and deuteranopia (loss of L or M cones) occur almost exclusively in males with a frequency of about 1% each, transmitted by unaffected women on the X chromosome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p646]
- L and M pigment genes are arranged head-to-tail on the X chromosome and are 96% identical in amino acid sequence, predisposing them to recombination events that cause color blindness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p647]
