# Logical Thinking

## Summary

Logical thinking is the Human's mode of processing information, characterized by basing conclusions on facts, evidence, and truth. This type of thinking is rational, considers multiple perspectives, and maintains context and perspective rather than reacting emotionally to situations.

## Key facts

- Logical thinking is based on facts and truth, with Humans gathering information before making plans and establishing the truth. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p21]
- Logical thinking involves working with evidence and proof while remaining open-minded and able to change position when challenged. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p22]
- Humans keep situations in context and perspective, appreciating that all events come and go. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p22]
- Adult Humans think in shades of grey, accepting that multiple factors may be involved and avoiding harsh judgments. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p22]
- Forms of logical thinking include deducing through steps, working with evidence, following arguments, and employing reasoning. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p23]

## Inferences

- Inference: Logical thinking can be activated by the Chimp through intuitive prompts, though the Human must search for evidence rather than acting on feeling alone.
