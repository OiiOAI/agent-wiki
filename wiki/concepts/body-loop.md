# Body Loop

## Summary

The body loop is the mechanism by which emotional responses produce changes in the body proper (through neural and humoral signals), which are then represented in somatosensory structures to create feelings. The 'as if body loop' is an alternative mechanism where body-related changes are simulated directly in sensory body maps under control of other neural sites (such as prefrontal cortices), bypassing the actual body. The body loop mechanism is of greater importance for real emotional experience than the 'as if' mechanism.

## Key facts

- The body loop uses both humoral signals via the bloodstream and neural signals via nerve pathways to change the body landscape, which is subsequently represented in somatosensory structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p281]
- The 'as if body loop' creates representations of body-related changes directly in sensory body maps under the control of other neural sites, bypassing the body proper to save time and energy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p281]
- Evidence suggests the body loop mechanism is of greater importance for real experience of feelings than the 'as if body loop' mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p295]

## Inferences

- Inference: The superiority of the body loop over the 'as if' mechanism suggests that genuine embodied experience cannot be fully replicated by simulation alone—the actual body contributes something essential to emotional feeling.
