# Scene Perception and Projection

## Summary

Scene perception and projection refers to a driver's ability to perceive the locations and speeds of nearby vehicles and project these into the future. This subprocess of local scene comprehension is hypothesized to involve perceiving and projecting speeds and distances to predict future vehicle positions. Tests measuring scene perception and projection include recall probes (assessing memory for vehicle locations) and time-to-passage probes (assessing ability to judge which vehicle will reach the driver first).

## Key facts

- Scene perception and projection refers to a driver's ability to perceive the locations and speeds of nearby vehicles and project these into the future. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p262]
- Recall probes and time-to-passage probes were hypothesized to assess participants' abilities to perceive speeds and distances and to project speed and distance information into the future so as to predict future vehicle locations. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p268]
- Composite recall error correlated with g/WM (r = -.73, p < .001) and with ASVAB g (r = -.50, p < .003). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p271]
- Time-to-passage probe data were scored in terms of the percentage of trials on which participants answered correctly, achieving 76% accuracy (SD = 11) with reliability of .63 in Experiment 3. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p278]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation of recall error with working memory suggests that maintaining accurate spatial representations of vehicle positions places demands on working memory capacity.
