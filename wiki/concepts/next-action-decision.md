# Next-Action Decision

## Summary

The next-action decision is the core technique of GTD—determining the specific next physical action required to move any commitment forward. When people identify the concrete next action (e.g., 'E-mail Fred for garage info' rather than 'Get a tune-up'), they create options for productive action during any available time window, reducing overwhelm and procrastination.

## Key facts

- Asking 'What's the next action?' forces clarity, accountability, productivity, and empowerment in both personal and organizational contexts. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p309]
- Dean Acheson taught David Allen this technique over thirty years ago, having developed it through executive consulting work. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p310]
- Defining what real doing looks like at the most basic level and organizing trusted reminders are master keys to productivity enhancement. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p313]
- Without a next action, there remains a potentially infinite gap between current reality and what you need to do. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p313]
- Most people wait to make next-action decisions until situations blow up, rather than when things first appear on their radar. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p318]
- Asking 'What's the next action?' undermines the victim mentality by presupposing that change is possible and that something can be done. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p322]

## Inferences

- Inference: The next-action decision technique works because it shifts focus from overwhelming abstract goals to immediately doable physical tasks, which the brain can process without triggering anxiety responses.
- Inference: By requiring specific next actions (who does what, with what tools, where), GTD ensures that action can occur whenever any relevant context window opens, maximizing productive use of fragmented time.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the two-minute rule (do it now if it takes less than two minutes) applies universally across all types of work, or only to routine operational tasks.
