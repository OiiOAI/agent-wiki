# Information Bias

## Summary

Information bias is the delusion that more information guarantees better decisions. After searching through dozens of reviews, the author found the best hotel was the one liked at the start. Additional information wastes time and money and can disadvantage decision-makers.

## Key facts

- Borges' story 'Del rigor en la ciencia' describes a map with scale 1:1, as large as the country itself — an extreme case of information bias where more detail provides no insight. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p135]
- Gerd Gigerenzer found 62% of Chicago students correctly answered which city has more inhabitants (San Diego vs San Antonio), but 100% of Munich students answered correctly because they only knew San Diego. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p136]
- Daniel J. Boorstin wrote: 'The greatest obstacle to discovery is not ignorance – it is the illusion of knowledge.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p136]
