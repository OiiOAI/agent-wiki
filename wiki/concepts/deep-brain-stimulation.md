# Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS)

## Summary

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) is a neurosurgical intervention involving implanted electrodes that deliver electrical pulses to specific brain structures, with STN-DBS being an evidence-based treatment for advanced Parkinson's disease that can affect cognitive and behavioral functions including impulsivity.

## Key facts

- STN-DBS at frequencies above 100 Hz improves motor function in Parkinson's disease and is now an evidence-based treatment for advanced PD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p301]
- STN-DBS impairs performance on the Stroop task, likely reflecting stimulation of associative territories involved in cognitive control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p305]
- STN-DBS compromises medial prefrontal cortex-STN theta-frequency coupling during high-conflict decisions, potentially reducing decision thresholds inappropriately. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p306]
- Behavioral changes after STN-DBS including euphoria, hypomania, mania, and apathy are more frequently reported in case series than randomized controlled trials, suggesting these are relatively rare. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p304]

## Inferences

- Inference: The therapeutic benefit of STN-DBS for motor symptoms alongside its adverse cognitive effects suggests dissociable therapeutic windows for different STN territories.
- Inference: CDs and dopamine dysregulation syndrome often improve after STN-DBS, likely due to reduced dopaminergic medication requirements rather than direct stimulation effects.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise cellular and network mechanisms by which high-frequency electrical stimulation disrupts pathological STN activity remain incompletely understood.
