# The Breaking of the One into the Manifold

## Summary

The crisis where the One divides into the many, splitting the created world into two apparently contradictory planes of being. The primordial parents (sky and earth) must be separated by their children, and the basic paradox of myth emerges: from the source perspective the world is a majestic harmony, but from created forms' perspective it is terrible cacophony of battle and pain.

## Key facts

- The forward roll of the cosmogonic round precipitates the One into the many, creating a rift splitting the world into two planes. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p219]
- In the Maori myth, Tane-mahuta separates Rangi (Sky) and Papa (Earth) by planting his head on earth and pushing the sky up with his feet. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p220]
- In the Babylonian creation, Marduk slays Tiamat and carves her body into the structure of the world. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p222]
- The basic paradox: from the source perspective the flesh was yielded willingly, from the created forms' perspective it was won by mighty arm amid danger and pain. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: This breaking represents the universal pattern of individuation where the ego separates from the whole, setting the stage for all heroic conflict
