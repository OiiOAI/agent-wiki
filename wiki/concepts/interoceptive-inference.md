# Interoceptive inference

## Summary

Interoceptive inference is Seth's application of predictive processing principles to emotion and mood. Just as the brain makes best guesses about external sensory causes, it makes best guesses about internal physiological causes. Emotions and moods are the subjective aspects of interoceptive predictions — controlled hallucinations about the body.

## Key facts

- The brain has no direct access to the causes of interoceptive sensory signals, which lie inside the body — all causes are hidden behind a sensory veil. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p172]
- Emotions and moods are the subjective aspects of predictions about the causes of interoceptive signals — internally driven forms of controlled hallucination. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p172]
- Interoceptive inference solves the problem of emotional range without needing a bright-line distinction between non-cognitive and cognitive processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p172]
- Emotions like fear, anxiety, joy, or regret are every one rooted in top-down perceptual best guessing about the state of the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: teroceptive inference provides a unified account of emotion and body perception, explaining how affective experiences are both physiological and phenomenological.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Experimental testing of interoceptive inference remains challenging due to difficulties in measuring and manipulating interoceptive signals.
