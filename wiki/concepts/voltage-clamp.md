# Voltage Clamp

## Summary

The voltage clamp is an electrophysiological technique that maintains membrane potential at a fixed value using negative feedback, allowing direct measurement of ionic currents flowing through voltage-gated channels. By preventing membrane potential changes, it interrupts the positive feedback between depolarization and channel opening, enabling separation of ionic and capacitive currents.

## Key facts

- The voltage clamp uses negative feedback: membrane potential is measured, compared to a command potential, and current is injected to eliminate any difference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- A small depolarizing step produces capacitive current (instantaneous) followed by leakage current (steady). Larger depolarizations additionally produce time-dependent inward Na+ current followed by outward K+ current. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p263]
- Tetrodotoxin (TTX) selectively blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels, allowing isolation of K+ currents; tetraethylammonium (TEA) blocks K+ channels, allowing isolation of Na+ currents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- The voltage clamp maintains membrane potential at a commanded value by injecting or withdrawing current equal and opposite to the ionic current flowing through voltage-gated channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p202]
- Hodgkin and Huxley used the voltage clamp to separate Na+ and K+ currents by using tetrodotoxin (blocks Na+ channels) and tetraethylammonium (blocks K+ channels). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p204]
- Gating current (Ig) flows when voltage-sensing charges move within channel proteins during activation, prior to channel opening. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p215]
