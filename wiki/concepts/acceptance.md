# Acceptance

## Summary

Acceptance in mindfulness meditation is the practice of letting go of resistance to difficult experiences—whether physical pain, unpleasant emotions, or challenging thoughts. The book distinguishes between two types: Acceptance One (physical relaxation of the body) and Acceptance Two (psychological non-resistance in the mind). Acceptance is presented as having 'the most power to positively impact your sense of wellbeing' among all practices in the book. The core mechanism is captured in the formula P × R = S (Pain times Resistance equals Suffering), suggesting that reducing resistance dramatically decreases suffering even when pain cannot be eliminated.

## Key facts

- Acceptance is defined as letting go of resistance to whatever experience is occurring in the present moment. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p110]
- The book presents acceptance as 'the key to real growth in meditation' and the single most impactful practice for improving life. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p110]
- Acceptance One is experienced as physical relaxation—tension released from the body. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p114]
- Acceptance Two is experienced as psychological non-resistance—absence of mental struggle against the experience. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p114]
- Shinzen Young's formula P × R = S (Pain times Resistance equals Suffering) describes how relief from pain depends on acceptance. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p115]
- Haruki Murakami's quote 'Pain is inevitable. Suffering is optional' summarizes the relationship between pain and acceptance. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p116]

## Inferences

- Inference: The two types of acceptance provide different pathways—someone unable to relax mentally may still achieve physical relaxation, and vice versa, allowing incremental progress.
- Inference: Acceptance does not mean approving of or agreeing with an experience, but rather acknowledging its existence without fighting it.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The claim that Shirley's morphine pump case (page 115) demonstrates reduced suffering through acceptance is presented as anecdotal evidence rather than controlled study.
