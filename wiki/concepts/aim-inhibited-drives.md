# Aim-Inhibited Drives

## Summary

In Freudian libidinal theory, aim-inhibited drives are sexual impulsions that have been diverted from their direct sexual aims toward substitute goals. Freud argues these drives create permanent emotional ties and are especially suited to group formation, unlike directly sexual drives which incur energy loss upon satisfaction.

## Key facts

- Aim-inhibited drives always preserve some of their original sexual aims, as shown by the physical proximity desires of even purely affectionate devotees. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5712]
- Aim-inhibited drives have a functional advantage over directly sexual drives: since they cannot achieve complete satisfaction, they create permanent ties. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5712]
- Directly sexual drives incur a loss of energy each time they are satisfied and must wait for fresh accumulation, allowing the object to change in the interim. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5712]
- Aim-inhibited drives are capable of any degree of admixture with uninhibited drives and can be transformed back into them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5712]
- Directly sexual impulsions are unfavorable to group formation; two people seeking solitude for sexual satisfaction make a demonstration against the herd drive. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5713]
- Neurotics create symptom-formations to replace the great group formations from which they are excluded, recapitulating humanity's institutions in distorted form. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5714]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's discussion of aim-inhibited drives represents a key contribution to libidinal theory, explaining how social bonds and group cohesion function psychologically through redirected sexual energy.
- Inference: The contrast between aim-inhibited and directly sexual drives parallels Freud's broader theoretical distinction between Eros/civilization and the direct expression of sexual instincts.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Freud's claim that aim-inhibited drives create 'permanent ties' has been empirically validated in subsequent psychological research.
