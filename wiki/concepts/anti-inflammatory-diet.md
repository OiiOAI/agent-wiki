# Anti-Inflammatory Diet

## Summary

The anti-inflammatory diet is a dietary pattern characterized by high intake of vegetables, fruits, nuts, good fats, and fish, and low intake of ultra-processed foods and sugary drinks—closely aligned with the Mediterranean diet. It contrasts with the typical Western diet, which is described as pro-inflammatory due to its high content of processed fats and refined carbohydrates and low content of protective nutrients. Excessive chronic inflammation is implicated in depression (via the cytokine hypothesis), cardiovascular disease, and other chronic conditions. Andrew Weil discusses the cytokine hypothesis, proposing that moderating inflammation through diet can help prevent and treat depression.

## Key facts

- The Western diet is described as clearly pro-inflammatory, providing fats and carbohydrates that favor inflammation while lacking protective elements found in vegetables, fruits, herbs, spices (turmeric and ginger), and green tea. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p11]
- The optimal balance of omega-6 to omega-3 fatty acids in the diet should be 1:1 or 2:1, but a typical Western diet has a ratio of approximately 15:1 or higher, associated with cardiovascular disease, excessive inflammation, and many forms of cancer. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p48]
- Government data from the United States, Canada, New Zealand, and Australia show that at least 50% of food consumed is ultra-processed 'stuff'—simple carbs, sugar, salt, trans fats, and artificial coloring—providing calories but not essential nutrients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p27]
- Three randomized clinical trials of adults with depression and poor eating habits showed that dietary improvement education led to dramatic improvements in mood in weeks, with complete remission achieved in many cases. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
