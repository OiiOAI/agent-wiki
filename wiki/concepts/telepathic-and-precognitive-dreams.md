# Telepathic and Precognitive Dreams

## Summary

The authors examine claims of telepathic dreams (communication directly from mind to mind) and precognitive dreams (seeing the future), reviewing historical and experimental research including studies at the Maimonides Dream Laboratory by Montague Ullman and Stanley Krippner. They conclude that while some matches between dreams and target materials exceed chance expectations, numerous factors including probability and memory bias likely explain most apparent cases.

## Key facts

- The Maimonides Dream Laboratory (1962-1972), founded by Montague Ullman, was the first sleep laboratory dedicated to experimental study of dream telepathy. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p248]
- In the Maimonides studies, senders concentrated on randomly selected art prints while receivers slept nearby and reported dreams during REM sleep, with judges matching dream content to target prints. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p249]
- Meta-analyses suggest judges can use dream reports to identify target materials more often than chance, indicating a small but demonstrable effect warranting continued study. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p250]
- The most common explanation for seemingly prophetic dreams involves probability and memory bias—only memorable 'hits' are reported while countless 'misses' are forgotten. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p252]
- The authors conclude that dreams may appear to predict the future through unconscious estimates and guesses based on information the dreamer actually possesses, not through psychic means. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fuzzy nature of dream recall combined with the brain's bias toward finding meaning may lead people to retrospectively match vague dream content to subsequent events.
- Inference: Some apparently precognitive experiences may reflect the brain's normal function of exploring possibilities through weak associations (NEXTUP), including realistic scenario construction based on subtle environmental cues.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The authors acknowledge that some paranormal dream experiences remain difficult to explain through conventional means, though they do not accept telepathy or precognition as valid explanations.
- Uncertain: The scientific community largely rejects paranormal phenomena, with even published empirical support often dismissed without examination of the data.
