# Beast machine theory

## Summary

The beast machine theory proposes that conscious experiences of self and world happen with, through, and because of living bodies. Seth argues that we cannot understand the nature and origin of conscious experiences except in light of our nature as living organisms — directly opposing Descartes' separation of mind and life.

## Inferences

- Inference: The beast machine theory implies that consciousness is not separate from life but emergent from it — mind and life are continuous rather than discontinuous.
- Inference: The beast machine theory extends the controlled hallucination view of perception to the deepest layers of selfhood, revealing the experience of the-self-as-really-existing as one more aspect of perceptual inference.
- Inference: The subjective stability of selfhood emerges from the need to have precise priors about stable bodily states for physiological regulation purposes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: It remains uncertain whether consciousness depends on specifically biological properties that could not be replicated in non-biological systems.
