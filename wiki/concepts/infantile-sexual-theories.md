# Infantile Sexual Theories

## Summary

Freud's account of the characteristic false theories that children construct about sexual matters under the influence of their innate sexual drive components, before receiving factual sexual information. These theories include the belief that all persons possess a penis (blocking recognition of the vagina), the cloacal theory of birth (babies emerge through the anus), and the sadistic theory of intercourse. Each theory contains a fragment of truth arising from the child's own sexual constitution.

## Key facts

- Children produce false sexual theories that are typical and appear in every child whose sexual life is accessible to investigation, arising from the necessities of the child's psycho-sexual constitution rather than from chance impressions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3088]
- The first infantile sexual theory consists in attributing to everyone a penis, since the penis is the leading erotogenic zone and auto-erotic sexual object in childhood, making the boy unable to imagine a person without one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3093]
- The second infantile sexual theory is the cloacal theory of birth, according to which babies emerge from the mother's body like faeces through the anal aperture, a theory valid for many animals and consistent with the child's coprophilic inclinations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3091]
- The third infantile sexual theory arises when children witness parental intercourse: they adopt a sadistic view of coition, interpreting it as something the stronger participant forcibly inflicts on the weaker. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3097]
- The castration complex, first named as such in this paper, results from the child's penis-focused sexuality and the threat of castration administered when the child is caught masturbating, producing deep and persistent effects including horror at female genitalia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3094]
- Girls share their brother's belief in universal penises and develop envy for the penis, expressing wishes to be boys. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3095]
- Children develop theories about marriage, such as that the married couple shows their behinds to each other, urinates in front of each other, or engages in blood-mixing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3098]
- The nuclear complex of a neurosis is brought into being when views the child feels driven to prefer come into opposition with views supported by the authority of grown-ups, producing psychical conflict and dissociation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3092]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's description of the 'woman with a penis' fantasy as leading to homosexuality anticipates his later systematic account of object choice and the role of the castration complex in homosexuality.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The observations on infantile sexual theories apply chiefly to males; Freud notes that observations on females are less fully developed in this account.
