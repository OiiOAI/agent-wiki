# Enactive Approach

## Summary

The enactive approach is a framework in cognitive science introduced by Varela, Thompson, and Rosch (1991) in 'The Embodied Mind.' It holds that living beings are autonomous agents that actively generate and maintain themselves, thereby enacting their own cognitive domains. The nervous system is understood as an autonomous dynamic system that creates meaning rather than processing information computationally. Cognition is conceived as skillful know-how in situated and embodied action, and a cognitive being's world is enacted through autonomous agency and sensorimotor coupling with the environment.

## Key facts

- Living beings are autonomous agents that actively generate and maintain themselves, thereby also enact or bring forth their own cognitive domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
- The nervous system is an autonomous dynamic system that actively generates and maintains its own coherent and meaningful patterns of activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
- The nervous system does not process information in the computationalist sense, but creates meaning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
- Cognition is the exercise of skillful know-how in situated and embodied action; cognitive structures emerge from recurrent sensorimotor patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
- A cognitive being's world is not prespecified and represented internally, but is a relational domain enacted by that being's autonomous agency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
- Experience is not epiphenomenal but central to understanding the mind, requiring careful phenomenological investigation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
- The term 'enaction' comes from the poet Antonio Machado: 'Wanderer the road is your footsteps, nothing else; you lay down a path in walking.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p24]
