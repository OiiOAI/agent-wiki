# Default Network

## Summary

A set of brain regions including the medial prefrontal cortex, anterior and posterior cingulate cortices, precuneus, and posterior parietal lobule that are activated during low-demanding tasks and at rest. This network is associated with spontaneous thought, mind wandering, and internally directed cognition.

## Key facts

- Default network regions lie largely along the midline of the brain and include the medial prefrontal cortex, anterior and posterior cingulate cortices, precuneus, and posterior parietal lobule. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p355]
- The default network is typically deactivated during novel, attention-demanding tasks but becomes activated when attentional demands are low. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p355]
- Subjects who report a higher propensity toward mind wandering show greater default network recruitment as cognitive demands decrease. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p356]
- Default network activation is associated with a state of defocused attention whereby information broadly arising in the external and internal milieu is gathered and evaluated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p355]

## Inferences

- Inference: The default network supports spontaneous thought and mind wandering, which are now recognized as cognitively valuable rather than merely distracting.
- Inference: Activation of the default network during rest may reflect ongoing memory retrieval and consolidation processes.
