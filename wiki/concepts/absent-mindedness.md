# Absent-Mindedness

## Summary

The sin of absent-mindedness involves a breakdown at the interface between attention and memory, where information is either never properly registered or is available but overlooked when needed. Unlike transience, the information is not lost over time—it was never adequately encoded or was not retrieved due to preoccupation with other matters.

## Key facts

- Absent-mindedness involves failure to remember due to preoccupation or attention lapses rather than memory decay over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p16]
- Divided attention during encoding prevents elaborative processing and has drastic effects on subsequent recollection while having little effect on familiarity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p68]
- Practice on various tasks results in a shift from attention-demanding effortful execution to automatic execution with little or no deployment of attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p70]
- Change blindness occurs when people fail to detect changes in scenes or objects because they encode features at a shallow level, recording general gist but few specific details. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p73]
- Prospective memory involves remembering to carry out future intentions and is divided into event-based (triggered by specific events) and time-based (carried out at specific times) tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p76]
- Event-based prospective memory fails when designated cue events fail to trigger recall of intended actions; time-based failures occur when people fail to generate or encounter reminder cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p76]
- Older adults perform almost as well as younger adults on event-based prospective memory tasks but show more noticeable decline on time-based tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p79]
- Time-based tasks can be converted to event-based tasks by generating environmental cues that will be available at the appropriate time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p79]
- The most effective external memory cues are sufficiently informative and available at the exact time an action needs to be performed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p82]
- Taking photos of objects impairs subsequent memory for those objects and their locations compared to simply looking at them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p62]
- Absent-minded forgetting occurs when people are preoccupied with other matters and fail to generate retrieval cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p85]
- Automatic behavior can contribute to absent-minded forgetting when habits take over in changed routines. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p85]
- Approximately 40 children died in hot cars each year since 1998 according to a 2018 National Safety Council report, with 54% attributable to forgetting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p86]
- Mind wandering is related to absent-mindedness because it involves divided attention during encoding that produces forgetting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p89]
- Students engaged in mind wandering about one-third of the time during lectures, and more frequent mind wandering was associated with poorer retention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p90]
- Embedding brief quizzes in video-recorded lectures reduced mind wandering by about half and increased retention of lecture content. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p91]
- Absent-mindedness differs from transience because the information was never properly encoded due to attention lapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p378]
- The frontal lobes of the brain play a critical role in attention and monitoring that prevents absent-minded errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p378]
- Children dying in hot cars represents a tragic example of absent-mindedness where routine overrides intentions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p378]
- Prospective memory failures occur when someone forgets to perform an intended action, such as taking medication at a scheduled time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p378]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between event-based and time-based prospective memory helps explain why different types of forgetting require different interventions.
- Inference: Absent-mindedness may reflect an adaptive trade-off where automatic processing saves cognitive resources but sometimes overrides intentional goals.
