# Neurotrophins

## Summary

Neurotrophins are peptide molecules including NGF, BDNF, and neurotrophin-3 that govern maturation, development, and protection of specific neural systems. They work through Trk receptors and may be deficient in neurodevelopmental disorders.

## Key facts

- Major neurotrophins include nerve growth factor (NGF), brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), and neurotrophins that control specific growth processes in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p66]
- NGF and BDNF can protect cerebellar granule cells and Purkinje cells, neurons deficient in cerebellar tissues of many autistic children. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p66]
- Neurotrophins operate through Trk (tyrosine kinase) receptors: TrkA receives NGF, TrkB receives BDNF and neurotrophin-4, TrkC receives neurotrophin-3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p66]
- Rita Levi-Montalcini received the Nobel Prize for discovering NGF, and BDNF has stronger effects on cholinergic neuron growth than NGF and may prove more effective for Alzheimer's treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p66]
