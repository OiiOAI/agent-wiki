# Thought without language

## Summary

The belief that one cannot think without language is a fallacy of the introspective process. Most mental processes constituting thinking—forming categories, weighing evidence, making decisions, solving problems—occur without language. Many famous scientific discoveries were made without language: Kekulé's benzene ring, Poincaré's Fuchsian functions, Mendeleyev's periodic table. Mathematical thinking takes place in three dimensions and is principally right-hemisphere-mediated.

## Key facts

- We carry out most mental processes that would normally constitute thinking without doing anything consciously, or in language, at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p158]
- Many famous scientific problems were solved without language: Kekulé saw the benzene ring as a snake biting its tail; Poincaré 'felt ideas rise in crowds, collide until pairs interlocked'; the structure of the periodic table came to Mendeleyev in a dream. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p158]
- Einstein wrote that 'the words or the language, as they are written or spoken, do not seem to play any role in the mechanism of my thought'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p158]
- Animals can think and form concepts; any sentient being must be capable of forming concepts and categorising to survive, and studies demonstrate categorical perception is not unique to humans and cannot depend on language. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p159]
- Pigeons can categorise different types of leaves, fish, or people, distinguish a human face in a crowd, and discriminate Monet from Picasso or Bach from Hindemith, without language. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p159]
- Patients who have lost language through stroke demonstrate that complex reasoning and mathematical calculation do not depend on language. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p160]
- Concept formation, together with the ability to see relations between things and events, predates language in human development. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p160]
