# Garden

## Summary

In Carse's philosophy, 'garden' represents an alternative attitude toward nature—not a bounded plot beside a house, but a place of growth and maximized spontaneity that one lives within. Unlike the machine, the garden is grown by an energy originating from within itself. Gardening is a horizonal activity where gardeners are attentive to deep patterns of natural order while aware that much lies beyond their vision. Genuine gardening transforms its workers; one becomes a gardener rather than merely learning to garden. The garden concept underlies Carse's vision of culture as poiesis—encouragement of spontaneity in others, respect for source, and refusal to convert source into resource.
