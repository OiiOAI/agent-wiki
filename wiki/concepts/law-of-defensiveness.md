# Law of Defensiveness

## Summary

The Law of Defensiveness states that people resist change and persuasion because it threatens their sense of autonomy and independence. To influence others effectively, one must make requests seem like the other person's own idea and validate their self-image, never attacking their beliefs or self-esteem.

## Key facts

- When others try to persuade or change us, we become defensive because giving in challenges our need to feel autonomous. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p215]
- Attacking people's beliefs or making them feel insecure about their intelligence only strengthens their defensiveness. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p215]
- Making people feel noble and altruistic is the ultimate lure—they must believe change comes from their own free will. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p215]
- The key strategy is to put focus on others, let them talk, make them feel like stars, and subtly insinuate desires rather than express them directly. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p228]
