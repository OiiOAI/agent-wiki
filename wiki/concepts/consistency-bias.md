# Consistency Bias

## Summary

Consistency bias is the tendency to reconstruct past attitudes, feelings, and beliefs to match current ones. Studies of Ross Perot supporters showed that loyalists, returning supporters, and deserters all rewrote their memories to be consistent with how they felt after the election. In relationships, couples whose feelings changed over time tended to mistakenly remember having always felt the same way. This bias helps reduce cognitive dissonance but can distort memory of the past.

## Key facts

- Studies of Ross Perot supporters showed that loyalists, returning supporters, and deserters rewrote their memories to be consistent with how they felt after the 1992 election, even when this distorted their actual July reactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p187]
- Patients with chronic pain biased to recall past pain as similar to current pain levels—when present pain was high, past pain seemed high; when present pain was low, past pain seemed more benign. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p188]
- People whose views on political issues had changed often recalled past attitudes as highly similar to present ones, with memories of past political views sometimes more closely related to present views than to what people actually believed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p188]
- Married and dating couples whose feelings changed over time tended to mistakenly remember having always felt the same way—only one in five whose feelings had changed recalled accurately. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p190]
- In the ten-year mark of marriages, wives showed a change bias; at the twenty-year mark, they showed a consistency bias—mistakenly recalling feelings from ten years earlier as similar to present ones. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p192]

## Inferences

- Inference: Consistency bias serves to reduce cognitive dissonance by making past beliefs and feelings conform to current ones, providing psychological comfort but at the cost of accuracy.
