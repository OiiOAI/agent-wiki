# Homosexual Current in Hysteria

## Summary

Freud proposed that hysterical patients typically harbor a significant unconscious homosexual current of feeling, which becomes reinforced when heterosexual libido is energetically suppressed. In Dora's case, this manifested as unconscious love for Frau K., underlying her jealousy and the peculiar symptom formations.

## Key facts

- Freud stated he had 'never yet come through a single psychoanalysis of a man or a woman without having to take into account a very considerable current of homosexuality'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2345]
- When sexual libido directed toward men has been energetically suppressed, the libido directed toward women becomes vicariously reinforced and may become conscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2345]
- Dora's jealousy of Frau K. was based on unconscious affection for that woman, which she disguised as rivalry for her father's love. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2347]
- Dora praised Frau K.'s 'adorable white body' in accents more appropriate to a lover than to a defeated rival. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2346]
- The 'gynaeophilic currents' were regarded as typical of the unconscious erotic life of hysterical girls. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2347]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud later acknowledged he should have recognized Dora's homosexual love for Frau K. earlier, as it was the strongest unconscious current in her mental life.
