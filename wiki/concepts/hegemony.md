# Hegemony

## Summary

Hegemony is Gramsci's central concept, referring to cultural leadership exercised by the ruling class. It describes how dominant groups maintain control not primarily through coercion but through winning the consent of the masses through cultural and ideological means.

## Key facts

- Gramsci defined hegemony as cultural leadership exercised by the ruling class, contrasting it with coercion that is 'exercised by legislative or executive powers, or expressed through police intervention.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p172]
- Gramsci emphasized 'hegemony and cultural leadership' over economic and coercive aspects of domination. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p172]
- Gramsci sought to understand how some intellectuals, working on behalf of the capitalists, achieved cultural leadership and the assent of the masses. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p172]
- Through revolution, Gramsci argued it is not enough to gain control of the economy and the state apparatus; it is also necessary to gain cultural leadership over the rest of society. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p172]
- The concept of hegemony was central to the Birmingham school's analysis of how hegemonic ideas operated as mechanisms of social control and were resisted by the lower classes. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p186]
