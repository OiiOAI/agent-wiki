# Information Pickup

## Summary

Information pickup is the process by which an active perceiver extracts information from the ambient optic array. Gibson distinguished information pickup from mere stimulation—perception fails not only for lack of stimulation but also for lack of stimulus information. The visual system is not merely stimulated by light; it is activated by information in the light. This concept is central to Gibson's critique of sensation-based theories.

## Key facts

- The theory of information pickup differs radically from traditional theories of perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p22]
- Optical information pickup entails an activity of the system not heretofore imagined by any visual scientist—the concurrent registering of both persistence and change in the flow of structured stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p22]
- Visual perception can fail for lack of stimulation (in homogeneous ambient darkness) or for lack of stimulus information (in homogeneous ambient light, even with adequate stimulation). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p47]
- Gibson's concept of information is specific to its environmental sources, not Shannon-type information implying a sender and receiver. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p20]
- The perceptual system goes into activity in the presence of stimulus information, not merely stimulation of receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p47]
- Information is conceived as available in the ambient energy flux, not as signals in a bundle of nerve fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p283]
- The state of a perceptual system is altered when it is attuned to information of a certain sort, becoming sensitized so that differences are noticed that were previously not noticed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p275]
- The ecological theory of direct perception implies a new theory of cognition in general, and in turn implies a new theory of noncognitive kinds of awareness including fictions, fantasies, dreams, and hallucinations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p283]
