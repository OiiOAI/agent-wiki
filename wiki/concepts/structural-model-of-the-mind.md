# The Structural Model of the Mind

## Summary

The structural model of the mind is Freud's second topography, introduced in The Ego and the Id, which conceptualizes the psyche as comprising three agencies: the id, ego, and superego. This model superseded the earlier first topography (conscious, preconscious, unconscious systems) by emphasizing functional and structural relationships rather than the criterion of consciousness alone.

## Key facts

- The division of the psychical into what is conscious and what is unconscious is the fundamental premise of psychoanalysis, but consciousness is merely a quality which may be present or absent. - We are obliged to assume that a part of the ego itself may be unconscious and behaves dynamically like the repressed unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5904]
- Consciousness is the surface of the mental apparatus; it is ascribed to a system which is spatially the first one reached from the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5906]
- The repressed merges into the id and is merely a part of it; the repressed is cut off sharply from the ego only by the resistances of repression but can communicate with the ego through the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5910]
- Freud introduced the term 'superego' to describe the agency within the ego that enjoys a degree of autonomy, follows its own intentions, and is independent of the ego for its supply of energy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6897]
- In melancholia, the superego becomes over-severe, abuses the poor ego, humiliates it, threatens it with punishments, and reproaches it for actions in the remotest past. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6897]
- The superego represents the claims of morality, and moral sense of guilt is the expression of the tension between the ego and the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6897]
- The superego takes over the power, function, and methods of the parental agency and proceeds directly from it, particularly linked to the destiny of the Oedipus complex. - Identification is the assimilation of one ego to another, as a result of which the first ego behaves like the second in certain respects; it is a very important form of attachment, probably the very first, and not the same thing as object choice. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6900]
- The superego is the vehicle of the ego ideal by which the ego measures itself, which it emulates, and whose demand for ever greater perfection it strives to fulfil. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6900]
- A child's superego is constructed on the model not of its parents but of its parents' superego; the superego becomes the vehicle of tradition and of all the time-resisting judgements of value which have propagated from generation to generation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6902]
- The id is described as chaos, a cauldron full of seething excitations, open to somatic influences, filled with energy reaching it from the drives, but has no organization and produces no collective will. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6907]
- The id knows no judgements of value, no good and evil, no morality; the pleasure principle dominates all its processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6908]

[raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6908]
- The ego is that portion of the id which was modified by the proximity and influence of the external world, adapted for reception of stimuli and as a protective shield against stimuli. ## Inferences

- Inference: The structural model implies that conflicts within the mind are not simply between conscious and unconscious processes, but between the organized ego and the split-off repressed, and between the ego and the superego. This replaced the earlier view of neuroses arising from a conflict between conscious and unconscious.
- Inference: The three regions (ego, superego, id) do not coincide with the three qualities of consciousness (conscious, preconscious, unconscious) — portions of the ego and superego can themselves be unconscious.
- Inference: Freud's structural model represents a fundamental revision of his earlier topographical model, replacing the division between conscious/preconscious/unconscious systems with functional provinces.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all portions of the superego that are unconscious possess the same primitive characteristics as the id (Freud notes the possibility that portions of the ego and superego can be unconscious without possessing the same primitive and irrational characteristics).
