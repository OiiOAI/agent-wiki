# Self-Respect as a Primary Good

## Summary

Self-respect (or self-esteem) is identified by Rawls as a primary good essential to pursuing one's conception of the good. A person's sense of their own worth is necessary for pursuing their plans with zest and delighting in their fulfillment. Since self-respect normally depends upon the respect of others, the public recognition of the two principles supports men's self-esteem, while a public utilitarian society makes it difficult for men to be confident of their own worth.

## Key facts

- Self-respect is not so much a part of any rational plan of life as the sense that one's plan is worth carrying out. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p171]
- Self-respect normally depends upon the respect of others; unless we feel that our endeavors are honored by them, it is difficult to maintain the conviction that our ends are worth advancing. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p171]
- When society follows the two principles, everyone’s good is included in a scheme of mutual benefit and this public affirmation in institutions supports men’s self-esteem. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-respect is reciprocally self-supporting: those who respect themselves are more likely to respect each other, and conversely, self-contempt leads to contempt of others.
