# Internal Models in Sensorimotor Control

## Summary

Internal models are neural representations of the physical properties of the body and external world that enable prediction and control. Forward models predict behavioral consequences from motor commands using an efference copy. Inverse models calculate motor commands needed to achieve desired outcomes. Observer models combine sensory feedback with motor predictions to estimate current body state while compensating for sensorimotor delays. These models are essential for skilled movement, tool use, and adaptation.

## Key facts

- Forward models predict the consequences of motor commands using an efference copy, enabling anticipation of sensory feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p718]
- Inverse models calculate motor commands that will produce a desired movement by inverting the system dynamics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p717]
- An efference copy of the motor command is passed to a forward model to predict sensory consequences without requiring actual sensory feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p718]
- Observer models combine sensory modalities with motor predictions to optimally estimate current body state while compensating for sensorimotor delays. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p722]
- Sensory prediction error—the discrepancy between predicted and actual sensory feedback—can drive learning of internal models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p723]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cerebellum is thought to implement forward and inverse models for motor prediction and control.
