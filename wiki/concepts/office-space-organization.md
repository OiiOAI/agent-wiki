# Office Space Organization

## Summary

The book presents office space organization as a complement to digital minimalism, based on the psychological principle that a cluttered environment reflects a cluttered mind. It provides specific tips for creating minimalist work spaces.

## Key facts

- Psychologists confirm that a cluttered environment reflects a cluttered mind. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p132]
- The book recommends creating white space on a work desk as big as a piece of A4-sized paper on the dominant side, to be kept clean and clutter-free. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p132]
- The book advises limiting the number of supplies visible on a desk, including paper clips, pens, erasers, and sticky notes. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p133]
- Personal items at work should be limited to one or two items only. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p133]
- All papers, folders, envelopes, and work-related documents should be placed in a single file positioned to the left or right of the computer screen. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p133]
- The monitor should be positioned at the center of the desk, at eye level when seated, and at arm's length from the body. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p133]
