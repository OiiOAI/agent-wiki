# Abstinence (Psychoanalysis)

## Summary

Abstinence in psychoanalysis refers to the analyst's practice of denying the patient satisfaction of cravings and longings, including the erotic transference. Freud formulated this as a fundamental principle: the patient's need and longing should be allowed to persist to serve as forces impelling therapeutic work. The analyst must neither satisfy surrogates nor suppress the patient's desires, but withhold response while maintaining the transference. This preserves the therapeutic potential of the transference and prevents the patient from dissipating libidinal energy in the treatment rather than in real life.

## Key facts

- Abstinence means the patient's need and longing should persist to serve as forces impelling work and change, not be appeased by surrogates. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3884]
- If the patient's craving for love is gratified, she would succeed in acting out rather than remembering, strengthening her propensity to repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3885]
- Returning transference love would be 'a complete defeat for the treatment' as the patient would bring out all her erotic inhibitions without possibility of correction.
