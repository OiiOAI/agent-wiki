# Accountability

## Summary

Accountability means taking business risks under your own name. Society rewards accountability with responsibility, equity, and leverage. While accountability comes with risks like failure and humiliation, in modern society the downside is not that large—people will forgive failures as long as you were honest and made a high-integrity effort.

## Key facts

- Embrace accountability and take business risks under your own name; society will reward you with responsibility, equity, and leverage. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p27]
- Clear accountability is important; without it, you don't have incentives and can't build credibility. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p42]
- In modern society, the downside risk of accountability is not that large. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p43]
- A bet is a form of accountability—if we are in love with our own opinions, it can cost us. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1310-L1315]
- Being in an environment where the challenge of a bet is always looming works to reduce motivated reasoning by changing the frame through which we view disconfirming information. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1315-L1320]
- Accountability improves decision-making even when away from the group because we know in advance that we will have to answer to the group for our decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1320-L1325]
- Imagining how a discussion will go with an accountability group helps us to spot more errors on our own and catch them more quickly. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1330]
