# Membrane Capacitance

## Summary

Membrane capacitance is the ability of the lipid bilayer to store charge separated across the membrane. It slows the time course of electrical signals because changing the membrane potential requires movement of charge onto (or off) the membrane capacitor. The membrane time constant (τ = Rm × Cm) determines how quickly the membrane potential changes in response to current.

## Key facts

- Typical membrane capacitance is approximately 1 μF per cm² of membrane area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p247]
- The membrane time constant τ = Rm × Cm is the time to reach 63% of the final voltage change; typical values range from 20 to 50 ms in neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Because ΔV = ΔQ/C, changing voltage across a capacitor requires time to deposit or remove charge; larger capacitance slows the voltage response to a given current.
