# Warm-Glow Altruism

## Summary

Warm-glow altruism is a model where individuals derive direct utility from the act of giving itself, not just from the resulting level of public good. Proposed by Andreoni (1989, 1990), this impure altruism model explains the widespread occurrence of charitable giving by including the private benefit people receive from personally contributing to charity.

## Key facts

- The warm-glow model assumes preferences u = u(xi, gi, G), where people care about their own contribution gi to the public good in addition to the overall level G. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p308]
- Warm-glow models explain incomplete free-riding, frequent contributions from low-income donors, and incomplete crowding out of private donations by government provision. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p308]
- Neural evidence from Harbaugh et al. (2007) shows that activity in reward centers was higher in voluntary than mandatory transfers, suggesting a warm-glow benefit from being personally responsible for giving. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p313]

## Inferences

- Inference: The warm-glow motive explains why people give even when they could free-ride, since the only way to get the private benefit of warm-glow is to actually give.
- Inference: People seem to get utility not just from outcomes but from taking voluntary action to help others.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The source of the warm-glow preference itself—whether it originates from repeated interactions, signaling, or some other mechanism—is still debated.
