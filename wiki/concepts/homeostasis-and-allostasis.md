# Homeostasis and Allostasis

## Summary

Homeostasis (Greek for 'same steady') is the tendency to maintain viable internal conditions despite external fluctuations. Allostasis extends this concept by highlighting the need to alter fixed points to adapt to changing needs and environments. Predictive processing connects these concepts to the brain's internal predictions—feelings like hunger and tiredness are signals not of present needs but of predicted, impending challenges that left unchecked would lead away from physiological viability.

## Key facts

- Homeostasis derives from Greek words meaning 'same' and 'steady' and implies a tendency to return to a state or to return to a place within a range to maintain viable internal conditions despite external fluctuations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p92]
- The term homeostasis was introduced by American physiologist Walter Bradford Cannon in the 1920s and 1930s, building on the work of nineteenth-century French physiologist Claude Bernard. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p92]
- Allostasis highlights the need to alter fixed points themselves to adapt to changing needs and environments—exemplified by the stress hormone cortisol flooding the bloodstream with glucose to power up cells for predicted actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p93]
- Feelings of hunger and tiredness are for the most part signals not of present needs but of predicted, impending challenges: challenges that left unchecked would lead too far from the safe bounds of physiological viability and health. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p93]
- The interoceptive (inward-looking) sensory system informs the brain about the states of guts, heart, viscera, muscular and air-supply systems, blood sugar level, body temperature, and more. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p93]
