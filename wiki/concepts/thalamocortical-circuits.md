# Thalamocortical Circuits

## Summary

Thalamocortical circuits are reciprocal connections between the thalamus and cortex that are essential for both normal brain functions (including sensory processing and sleep spindles) and pathological states such as primary generalized seizures. The circuitry involves pyramidal cells connected by excitatory synapses with thalamic relay neurons, while GABAergic interneurons in the reticular thalamic nucleus provide inhibitory control. T-type calcium channels and GABAB receptors play important roles in generating oscillatory activity.

## Key facts

- Pyramidal cells in the cortex are reciprocally connected by excitatory synapses with thalamic relay neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1182]
- GABAergic interneurons in the reticular thalamic nucleus are excited by pyramidal cells in the cortex and thalamic relay neurons, and inhibit the thalamic relay cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1182]
- The thalamocortical connections that generate sleep spindles during slow wave sleep are thought to be essential for the generation of primary generalized seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1182]
- Both T-type Ca2+ channels and GABAB receptor-channels play an important role in the generation of oscillatory thalamocortical activity resembling human absence seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1183]
