# Opportunity Cost

## Summary

Opportunity cost is the principle that rational decisions should be based on comparing alternatives. Charlie Munger emphasizes that intelligent people make decisions based on opportunity costs, and he critiques the cost of equity capital concept as an 'amazing mental malfunction' that ignores this basic principle.

## Key facts

- Munger references Mankiw's economics text stating 'intelligent people make decisions based on opportunity costs—in other words, it's your alternatives that matter.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p106]
- Buffett and Munger claim they don't know their cost of capital and are skeptical of the concept taught at business schools. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p106]
- Munger calls the concept of cost of equity capital 'a perfectly amazing mental malfunction.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p107]
- He states it is crazy to say that if you're generating a hundred percent return on capital, you shouldn't invest in something that generates eighty percent. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p107]
- The theory of cost of equity capital is described as something theorists 'went bonkers' on. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p107]
