# Neural Crest Cell Migration

## Summary

Neural crest cells are stem cells at the boundary of the neural tube and epidermal ectoderm that migrate throughout the body to form peripheral nervous system structures. Unlike CNS migration, neural crest cells undergo 'free migration' using chain migration and require epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition, controlled by BMPs, transcription factors (snail, slug, twist), and guidance molecules including ephrins.

## Key facts

- Neural crest cells transform from epithelial to mesenchymal cells soon after induction and migrate to many sites throughout the body to form sensory and autonomic neurons, glia, and nonneural derivatives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]
- Neural crest migration is promoted by BMPs which induce expression of zinc finger proteins snail, slug, and twist that promote epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]
- Neural crest cells pass through the anterior half of each somite but avoid the posterior half, guided by ephrin B proteins that provide repulsive signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1141]
- Differentiation of neural crest derivatives depends on complex interactions between cues received along the migratory journey and intrinsic predispositions that vary along the rostrocaudal axis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]
