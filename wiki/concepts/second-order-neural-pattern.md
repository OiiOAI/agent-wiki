# Second-Order Neural Pattern

## Summary

A second-order neural pattern re-represents both the proto-self and an object in their temporal relationship, thereby representing what is actually happening to the organism. This pattern provides the neural basis for the imaged account that underlies core consciousness. Second-order patterns are generated by cross-signaling among several structures including the cingulate cortex, thalamus, and superior colliculi, rather than by a single brain region.

## Key facts

- Second-order structures must be able to receive signals from sites representing the proto-self and sites representing objects; generate temporally ordered neural patterns describing first-order events; introduce resulting images into the flow of thought; and signal back to object-processing structures to enhance object images. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p148]
- The second-order structures proposed include the superior colliculi, cingulate cortex, thalamus, and some prefrontal cortices, none of which acts alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p180]
- The second-order neural pattern results from ensemble activity of superior colliculi and cingulate under thalamic coordination, with cingulate and thalamus having the major contributions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p181]
- The second-order pattern is not located in a single brain region but emerges from interactions among multiple sites, consistent with general brain organization. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p149]
- Second-order neural patterns represent the relationship between the organism (proto-self) and an object, creating a primordial plot of what is happening to the organism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p266]
- The cingulate cortex, with its somatosensory inputs and connections to sensory channels, is an excellent candidate for generating second-order patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p265]
- Second-order patterns are implicit neural descriptions, not explicit mental narratives. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The enhancement of object images achieved by second-order patterns involves thalamocortical modulation and activation of acetylcholine and monoamine nuclei in the basal forebrain and brain stem.
- Inference: The second-order pattern mechanism suggests that consciousness is fundamentally relational—it emerges from the organism's representation of how it is being affected by something external or internal.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise anatomical sources of the second-order pattern are hypotheses awaiting direct confirmation.
- Uncertain: The mechanisms of fusion and smoothing among multiple second-order maps remain unknown.
