# Decision-Making Critique

## Summary

The Decision-Making Critique is a feedback technique designed to help decision makers review their decisions and improve the quality of their intuitive judgments. It involves examining specific incidents, constructing timelines of key judgments and decisions, and asking focused questions about why decisions were difficult, how situations were interpreted, and what courses of action were selected. The goal is to get process feedback rather than just outcome feedback.

## Key facts

- The Decision-Making Critique helps reflect on how judgments and decisions were made to see what worked well and what should have been done differently. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p85]
- Research is very clear that we learn a great deal from process feedback, such as reflecting on how decisions were made and how patterns could have been spotted more quickly, and we learn much less from outcome feedback alone. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p82]
- The critique format includes constructing a timeline of key judgments and decisions, then examining why difficult decisions were hard, how situations were interpreted, and whether different courses of action should have been considered. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p86]
- Failures are particularly valuable for learning because they grab attention and signal that mental models were not good enough. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p82]
