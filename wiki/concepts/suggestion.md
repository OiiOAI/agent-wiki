# Suggestion

## Summary

Suggestion is the influence one person has on another through appeal rather than argument. Bernheim based his theory of hypnotic phenomena on the thesis that everyone is 'suggestible.' Freud argues that while psychoanalytic treatment also operates through suggestion (specifically through transference), it differs fundamentally from hypnotic suggestion. Hypnotic treatment merely glazes over conflicts without resolving them, while analytic treatment uses suggestion in the service of making the unconscious conscious. The analyst guides the patient's suggestion toward resolving repressions, and crucially, the transference itself is subjected to analysis and dissection, whereas in purely suggestive treatment it is preserved untouched.

- Bernheim maintained that suggestion was the essential element in hypnotism, that hypnosis itself was a result of suggestion—a suggested state. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5200]
- Freud practiced hypnotic treatment for many years and found it rapid and painless but unreliable and lacking in permanence—old ailments returned or were replaced by new ones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5201]
- In hypnosis, the patient's transference may be negative, ambivalent, or protected against, of which the therapist learns nothing; in psychoanalysis, the therapist acts upon the transference itself and can guide it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5203]
- The fundamental distinction between analytic and purely suggestive treatment is that in analysis the transference is subjected to treatment and dissected, while in other suggestive treatments it is carefully preserved and left untouched. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5204]
- Freud defines suggestion as 'a conviction which is not based upon perception and reasoning but upon an erotic tie.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5701]
- Freud protests against the view that suggestion, which explained everything, was itself to be exempt from explanation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5666]
- The hypnotist awakens in the subject a portion of his archaic heritage which had also made him compliant towards his parents. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5700]
- Freud describes hypnosis as a group of two, distinguishing it from groups by limitation of number and from being in love by the absence of directly sexual trends.
