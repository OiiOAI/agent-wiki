# Phantom Limb Syndrome

## Summary

Phantom limb syndrome is a condition in which patients who have had a limb amputated continue to have vivid sensory experiences of the missing limb, including sensations of movement and position. This phenomenon reveals that cortical reorganization following deafferentation creates new connections that the brain learns to interpret as sensations from the lost limb.

## Key facts

- Many patients with amputated limbs continue to feel vivid sensation of the missing limb, feeling it move around and even attempting to shake hands when greeting someone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p431]
- Imaging studies show that after amputation, afferents from adjacent body regions expand into the cortical area that previously represented the lost limb. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p431]
- Phantom limb sensations can be evoked by stimulating body areas adjacent to the amputated region, such as the face or upper arm, because these areas are represented next to the hand area in the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p431]
- The referred sensations experienced from phantom limbs demonstrate that the brain learns to interpret activity in reorganized cortical maps as emanating from the missing body part. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p431]
