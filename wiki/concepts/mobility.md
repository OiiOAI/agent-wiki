# Mobility (Movement Health)

## Summary

Mobility is defined in Built to Move as "the harmonious convergence of all the elements that allow you to move freely and effortlessly through space and life"—including joints, muscles, tendons, ligaments, fascia, nerves, brain, and vasculature. The authors distinguish mobility from exercise, arguing that mobility practices (such as mobilizations and floor-sitting) are foundational to health and should precede or complement exercise programs.

## Key facts

- Mobility refers to the harmonious convergence of all elements that allow free and effortless movement: joints, muscles, tendons, ligaments, fascia, nerves, brain, and vasculature. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L96-L98]
- Mobility is achieved through mobilizations—not exercise—which are techniques that take joints to different places and engage the brain to ingrain new movement patterns. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L225-L230]
- The authors state that achieving good mobility doesn't call for exercise; rather, it's a series of simple activities that enhance capacity for free and easy movement. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L93-L95]
- Mobilizations differ from traditional stretching in that they target multiple aspects of the movement system (connective tissue, joints, nervous system) while stretching is biased toward muscles and works through passive tension. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L240-L245]
