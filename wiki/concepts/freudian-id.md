# Freudian Id

## Summary

A concept introduced by Freud representing the mental region containing the drives and instinctual forces. The id is described as more extensive, imposing, and obscure than the ego. It is the truly mental portion of the psyche and operates according to the pleasure principle, seeking immediate satisfaction of drives regardless of consequences. In the id, contradictions and antitheses persist side by side unconcernedly, and different urges pursue their own purposes independently.

## Key facts

- The id is described as a mental region more extensive, more imposing and more obscure than the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6406]
- The drives fill the id; all the energy in the id originates from bodily needs. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6411]
- In the id there are no conflicts; contradictions and antitheses persist side by side unconcernedly. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6407]
- All that happens in the id is and remains unconscious, and processes in the ego alone can become conscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6409]
- Freud compared the ego to a facade of the id, an external cortical layer modified by the influence of the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6407]
