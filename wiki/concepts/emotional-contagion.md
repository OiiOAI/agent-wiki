# Emotional Contagion

## Summary

Emotional contagion is the tendency to automatically mimic facial expressions, vocalizations, postures, and movements of another person and consequently converge emotionally with them. This automatic process occurs without conscious awareness or deliberate intent, and it serves as a foundation for empathy.

## Key facts

- Newborns 42 minutes to 72 hours old can accurately imitate facial expressions, suggesting imitation is innate rather than learned. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p169]
- Subjects shown 30-millisecond exposures of emotional faces (too fast for conscious recognition) still reacted with corresponding facial muscle activity measured by electromyography. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p171]
- People who are mimicked become more helpful and generous not only toward their mimicker but also toward other people present, suggesting mimicry acts as social glue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p172]
- Neumann and Strack found that subjects automatically mimicked the tone of voice (happy, sad, or neutral) and took on the corresponding mood without conscious awareness of doing so. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p175]
- Infants of depressed mothers show stress reactions including elevated heart rate and cortisol levels during interactions, demonstrating emotional contagion from caregiver to child. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p176]
- Mice can learn specific fear associations vicariously by observing another mouse experiencing fear conditioning, with learning enhanced if mice are related or have mated. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p535]
- A mouse's pain sensitivity is modulated by the pain level of a nearby mouse through social mechanisms—this shared pain only occurs between cagemates. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p536]
- Emotional contagion in humans includes phenomena like one baby crying because another is, or someone catching the emotional fever of a mob plunging into a riot. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p533]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that mimicry increases prosocial behavior toward third parties (not just the mimicker) suggests evolutionary adaptive value for group cohesion.
