# Endocannabinoid

## Summary

Endocannabinoids are lipid-derived signaling molecules that act as transcellular messengers in the nervous system. The two major endocannabinoids, anandamide and 2-arachidonylglycerol (2-AG), are synthesized from arachidonic acid-containing phospholipids and diffuse across membranes to activate CB1 receptors on neighboring neurons, inhibiting neurotransmitter release.

## Key facts

- The two major endocannabinoids are anandamide (arachidonyl-ethanolamide) and 2-arachidonylglycerol (2-AG), both containing an arachidonic acid moiety. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p356]
- CB1 receptors are the most abundant type of G protein-coupled receptor in the brain and are found predominantly on axons and presynaptic terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p356]
- Activation of CB1 receptors inhibits release of several types of neurotransmitters, including both GABA and glutamate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p356]
- Endocannabinoid production is often stimulated in postsynaptic neurons by increases in intracellular Ca2+ that result from postsynaptic excitation; the endocannabinoids then diffuse to presynaptic terminals where they bind CB1 receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p356]
