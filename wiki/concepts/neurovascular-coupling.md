# Neurovascular Coupling

## Summary

Neurovascular coupling is the mechanism linking neuronal activity to changes in blood flow and oxygenation. Active neurons consume oxygen, causing a brief dip in oxygenation, followed by an oversupply of oxygenated blood. This produces the blood oxygen level-dependent (BOLD) contrast measured by fMRI. The timing of the hemodynamic response function peaks 4-5 seconds after neural activity.

## Key facts

- Active neurons consume energy obtained from oxygen in blood, causing blood oxygenation to drop momentarily. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]
- Blood flow to active brain areas increases over the next few seconds, with supply exceeding metabolic demand, resulting in a higher proportion of oxygenated versus deoxygenated blood. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]
- Deoxygenated blood interacts with the magnetic field because unbound iron in hemoglobin creates local field inhomogeneities, whereas oxygenated blood does not. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]
- The temporal profile of the BOLD response (hemodynamic response function) peaks around 4-5 seconds after local neural activity and returns to baseline after 12-15 seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]
- The mechanisms of neurovascular coupling remain incompletely understood, with growing focus on the functional role of astrocytes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: It is unclear whether BOLD signal is more closely tied to individual neuron firing or population activity, and whether increased blood oxygenation reflects local excitation or inhibition.
