# Large-Batch Death Spiral

## Summary

The large-batch death spiral is a self-reinforcing pattern where batch sizes grow over time as teams try to minimize overhead from rework, delays, and interruptions. Unlike manufacturing, there are no physical limits on maximum batch size for product development. Eventually, the batch becomes so large that managers are incentivized to add more features rather than ship, and the company cannot release anything.

## Key facts

- Large batches tend to grow over time because moving the batch forward often results in additional work, rework, delays, and interruptions, giving everyone incentive to do work in ever-larger batches. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p184]
- The large-batch death spiral has no physical limits unlike manufacturing; batch size can keep growing until one batch becomes a 'bet the company' project because the company has taken so long since the last release. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p184]
- When Eric Ries worked at a company in the death spiral, the longer they worked, the more afraid they became of customer reaction, and the more work they completed, the more work they had to do. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p185]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hospital pharmacies delivering big batches of medications once a day cause rework when patient orders change, whereas smaller batches every four hours reduce total workload.
