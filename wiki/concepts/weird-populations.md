# WEIRD Populations

## Summary

Acronym for Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic populations that form the basis of most psychological research. Henrich, Heine, and Norenzayan's 2010 paper 'The Weirdest People in the World?' showed that WEIRD people are statistical outliers on many psychological measures, including moral psychology. WEIRD populations have more independent self-concepts, think more analytically, and rely primarily on the ethic of autonomy, making them unrepresentative of human moral psychology broadly.

## Key facts

- Henrich, Heine, and Norenzayan published 'The Weirdest People in the World?' in 2010 showing WEIRD people are statistical outliers. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p112]
- The WEIRDer you are, the more you perceive a world full of separate objects rather than relationships. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p112]
- Americans are more extreme outliers than Europeans, and within the US, the educated upper middle class is most unusual. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p112]
- WEIRD philosophers have generated individualistic, rule-based, universalist moral systems suited to autonomous individuals. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p113]
- Penn students in Haidt's dissertation were unique in their unwavering devotion to the harm principle and 73% tolerated the 'chicken story'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p112]

## Inferences

- Inference: WEIRD morality is unusually narrow, largely limited to the ethic of autonomy (harm and fairness), while most other societies and non-WEIRD moral matrices include community and divinity concerns
