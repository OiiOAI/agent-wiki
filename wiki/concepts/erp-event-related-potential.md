# Event-Related Potential (ERP)

## Summary

Event-Related Potentials (ERPs) are small brain electrical potentials related to information processing. They are divided into two broad classes: exogenous ERPs, whose characteristics are primarily determined by environmental stimuli (modality, intensity), and endogenous ERPs, determined by cognitive aspects of stimulus processing. ERPs can assess whether environmental stimuli relevant to SA were detected and recognized as important by the operator.

## Key facts

- ERPs are small brain electrical potentials related to information processing, requiring averaging of multiple responses due to their small size relative to background EEG. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- Exogenous ERP characteristics are primarily determined by the nature of environmental stimuli including sensory modality and stimulus intensity. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- Endogenous ERP characteristics are primarily determined by cognitive aspects of stimulus processing. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- Presence of exogenous ERPs to relevant stimuli can determine whether the operator detected the stimuli, while endogenous ERPs indicate recognition of importance to the task. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- If stimuli relevant to good SA are presented to an operator who is cognizant of the task, ERPs would be expected; operators without good SA might miss the importance of stimuli resulting in small or absent ERPs. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The utility of ERPs in complex task situations has been questioned according to Fournier, Wilson, and Swain (1999).
