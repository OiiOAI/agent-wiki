# Sensory Intensity in Dreams

## Summary

Sensory intensity in dreams refers to differences in vividness between particular dream images, ranging from sharpness greater than reality to irritating vagueness. Freud determined that intensity is determined by two independent factors: the expression of wish fulfillment and the amount of condensation expended on an element.

## Key facts

- The sensory intensity of particular dream images is not related to the psychical intensity of corresponding elements in dream thoughts; a complete 'transvaluation of all psychical values' takes place between dream thoughts and the dream. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p330]
- Elements expressing wish fulfillment are represented with special intensity, and the most vivid elements are the starting point of the most numerous trains of thought. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p330]
- The greatest intensity is shown by those elements on whose formation the greatest amount of condensation has been expended. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p330]
- Real sensations experienced during sleep are not distinguished by vividness from elements arising from memories; the factor of reality counts for nothing in determining intensity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p329]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud indicates the problem of varying clarity of whole dreams (as contrasted with vagueness of elements) cannot yet be discussed for reasons that appear later.
