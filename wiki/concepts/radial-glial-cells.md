# Radial Glial Cells

## Summary

Radial glial cells are specialized glial cells that serve dual functions in the developing central nervous system: they act as neural progenitor cells generating neurons and astrocytes, and they provide a scaffolding framework for neuronal migration. Their cell bodies reside in the ventricular zone and extend long processes to the pial surface. During cortical development, newborn neurons migrate along radial glial processes to reach their final positions, following an inside-first, outside-last pattern of cortical layer formation.

## Key facts

- Radial glial cells have cell bodies in the ventricular zone and long processes extending to the pial surface, serving as scaffolds for the radial migration of neurons generated in the ventricular zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1131]
- Radial glial cells serve as progenitor cells that generate both neurons and astrocytes through asymmetric and self-renewing divisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1131]
- Neurons migrate along radial glial fibers with their leading process wrapping around the radial glial shaft and their nucleus translocating within the leading process cytoplasm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1138]
- A single radial glial cell scaffold can support the migration of up to 30 generations of cortical neurons before the radial glial cell differentiates into an astrocyte.
