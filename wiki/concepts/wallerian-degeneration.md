# Wallerian Degeneration

## Summary

Wallerian degeneration is the process by which the distal segment of a transected axon degenerates following injury. It was long thought to be passive, but is now known to be actively regulated by a signaling pathway involving NMNAT enzymes and SARM1. When axons are cut, NMNAT2 levels fall rapidly, disinhibiting SARM1, which then degrades NAD+ and triggers energy collapse and cytoskeletal degradation. The Wlds mutant mouse, which expresses a chimeric NMNAT1 protein, shows dramatically delayed Wallerian degeneration.

## Key facts

- Wallerian degeneration was described by Augustus Waller in 1850 as the degeneration of distal axonal stumps following transection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1285]
- The discovery of the Wlds mutant mouse, in which Wallerian degeneration is slowed approximately 10-fold, revealed that axon degeneration is actively regulated rather than passively resulting from separation from the cell body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1285]
- SARM1 is a key regulator of axon degeneration; its activation triggers NAD+ depletion and energy crisis leading to axon fragmentation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1286]
- NMNAT2 is normally present in axons and maintains NAD+ levels to inhibit SARM1; following axotomy, NMNAT2 degrades rapidly, disinhibiting SARM1 and triggering degeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1286]
- Wallerian degeneration occurs in the distal axonal stump after it separates from the parental cell body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- During Wallerian degeneration, the neuronal membrane breaks down, the cytoskeleton is disassembled, and the axon is phagocytosed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Wallerian degeneration proceeds rapidly and inevitably to completion after it begins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Synaptic transmission fails at severed nerve terminals before physical degeneration of the axon begins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Wallerian degeneration was first described in 1850 by Augustus Waller. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1338]
- Transected axons degenerate because they are separated from the neuronal cell body where most proteins are synthesized. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1338]
- The discovery of Wlds mutant mice proved that Wallerian degeneration is an actively regulated response, not a passive consequence of separation from the cell body. ## Inferences

- Inference: Understanding the SARM1 pathway may lead to treatments for neurodegenerative diseases where axon degeneration precedes neuronal death, such as ALS, Parkinson's, and Alzheimer's diseases.
