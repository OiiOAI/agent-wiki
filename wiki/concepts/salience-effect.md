# Salience Effect

## Summary

The salience effect causes people to overweight prominent, eye-catching features while neglecting hidden or abstract information. This leads to faulty causal attributions and the formation of prejudices, as in the example where a journalist wrongly blamed marijuana for an accident merely because it was found at the scene.

## Key facts

- Salience refers to a prominent feature that catches attention, receiving more weight than it deserves. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p183]
- A journalist who found marijuana at an accident scene falsely concluded it caused the crash, demonstrating salience bias. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p183]
- Kahneman and Tversky found that people place unwarranted emphasis on salient information when forecasting. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p184]
- Salient negative incidents involving ethnic minorities form prejudices even though the majority live lawfully. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p184]
