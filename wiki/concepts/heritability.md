# Heritability

## Summary

Heritability is the extent to which genetic factors account for traits in a population. The strongest evidence comes from twin studies, first used by Francis Galton in 1883. Identical (monozygotic) twins share nearly all genes and are more concordant for neurological and psychiatric traits than fraternal (dizygotic) twins who share on average half their genetic information. Heritability estimates for many neurological, psychiatric, and behavioral traits are around 50%, demonstrating that the environment is also an important factor.

## Key facts

- Heritability estimates for many neurological, psychiatric, and behavioral traits from twin studies are around 50%. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p74]
- Twin studies were first used by Francis Galton in 1883. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p74]
- Heritability is the extent to which genetic factors account for traits in a population. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p91]
- Monozygotic twins develop from a single fertilized egg and share nearly all genes; dizygotic twins develop from two fertilized eggs and share about half their genetic information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p91]
- Identical twins tend to be more concordant for neurological and psychiatric traits than fraternal twins, providing evidence for heritable components. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p91]
- The Minnesota Twin Study examined identical twins separated early in life who shared predispositions for psychiatric disorders despite different environments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p92]
- Heritability estimates for many neurological, psychiatric, and behavioral traits range around 50%, substantially less than 100%. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p92]
- Heritability scores of 40 to 60 percent have been reported for traits related to prosocial behavior, resilience, social responsiveness, political attitudes, aggression, and leadership potential. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p249]
- Human finger number is highly inherited (genes determine the typical 5 fingers) but has low heritability (individual variation is mostly from accidents, not genes). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p251]
- Studying a genetic trait in only one environment artificially inflates heritability by eliminating the ability to see if genes work differently in other environments. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p252]
- Twin and adoption studies overestimate genetic influence due to confounds including the equal environment assumption, chorionic differences, prenatal effects, selective placement, and range restriction. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p246]
- Heritability scores are relevant only to the environments in which traits have been studied; humans live in the most heterogeneous environments of any species. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The confusion between 'inherited trait' and 'high heritability' leads to systematic overestimation of genetic influence on individual differences.
- Inference: Humans' extreme environmental heterogeneity (tundra to desert, nomadic to urban) means heritability estimates for human behavioral traits are particularly unreliable.
