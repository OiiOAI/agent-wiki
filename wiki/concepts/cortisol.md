# Cortisol

## Summary

Cortisol is a master circadian hormone that peaks upon waking, creating a temporary catabolic state. Known as the 'carpe diem' and 'stress' hormone, it is instrumental in liberating energy for use in early daytime hours. Chronic elevation of cortisol from prolonged stress leads to serious physiological consequences including inflammation, elevated blood sugar, insulin resistance, and brain atrophy.

## Key facts

- Cortisol is a master circadian regulator that peaks upon waking, creating a temporary catabolic state in the body and liberating energy as carbs, fat, and amino acids. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p329]
- When insulin and cortisol are both present at the same time after a carbohydrate-rich breakfast, cortisol's fat-burning effect is shut down and it only exerts its catabolic effect on muscles. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p329]
- Chronic stress can cause sustained elevation of cortisol, leading to inflammation, elevated blood sugar, insulin resistance, nutrient deficiencies, increased gut permeability, and more. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p334]
- Deep abdominal fat has four times more cortisol receptors than subcutaneous fat, making visceral fat particularly responsive to cortisol elevation. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p335]
- Chronically elevated cortisol compromises the brain's supply of BDNF and can atrophy the hippocampus, even causing dendrites to recede. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p336]
- A small Thai study of stressed-out medical students found that four days of meditation reduced cortisol by 20 percent. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p336]
- Cortisol is the primary long-acting stress hormone that helps mobilize fuel, cue attention and memory, and prepare the body and brain to battle challenges to equilibrium. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- Cortisol oversees the stockpiling of fuel in the form of fat for future stresses, and this action is crucial to survival. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- At high or unrelenting concentrations, cortisol has a toxic effect on neurons, eroding connections between them and breaking down muscles and nerve cells to provide an immediate fuel source. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]

## Inferences

- Inference: Stress management is critical for maintaining healthy cortisol levels, which directly impacts both metabolic health and brain structure.
