# Commander's Intent

## Summary

Commander's Intent is a statement explaining the purpose behind a plan or order, helping subordinates understand what the commander wants to achieve. It enables team members to improvise and make decisions when plans encounter unexpected conditions, because they understand the reasoning behind the original orders. Research found that only 19-34 percent of Commander's Intent statements effectively communicated intent to subordinates.

## Key facts

- One study found that company commanders' responses matched their battalion commander's expectations only 34 percent of the time when interpreting unexpected developments. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p274]
- Functions of communicating intent include promoting independence, improving team performance by reducing need for clarifications, detecting deviations, catching errors in advance, promoting improvisation, and recognizing opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p273]
- Seven types of information help describe intent: purpose of the task, objective of the task, sequence of steps, rationale for the plan, key decisions that may have to be made, antigoals, and constraints. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p276]
