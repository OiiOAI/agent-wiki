# Repetition Priming

## Summary

Repetition priming is a form of nondeclarative memory where prior exposure to stimuli facilitates subsequent processing. Research demonstrated that Henry showed normal priming on both verbal and nonverbal tasks, proving that priming is spared in amnesia and depends on cortical circuits rather than medial temporal-lobe structures.

## Key facts

- Henry showed normal magnitude of priming on three different test forms administered on three separate occasions using pattern-drawing tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p195]
- Pattern priming in Henry was tied to newly acquired representations of specific target figures, not to preoperatively established memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p195]
- Henry showed normal priming on both perceptual identification and conceptual priming tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p196]
- Perceptual priming depends on circuits in visual areas of the occipital cortex, while conceptual priming depends on temporal and parietal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p197]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between preserved priming and impaired recall/recognition in Henry indicates that separate neural networks support declarative and nondeclarative learning.
