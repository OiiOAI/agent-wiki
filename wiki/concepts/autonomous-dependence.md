# Autonomous Dependence

## Summary

Autonomous dependence refers to healthy emotional bonds with others that are freely chosen rather than coerced. Ryan and Lynch's research on teenagers found that a willing or volitional dependence on parents was essential for teenagers' integrity and well-being, rather than strong independence from them. The concept illustrates that needs for autonomy and relatedness are complementary rather than opposed, and that healthy functioning involves appropriate interdependence rather than isolation.

## Key facts

- Ryan and Lynch found that willing or volitional dependence on parents was essential for teenagers' integrity and well-being, rather than strong independence from them. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p101]
- People have needs for both autonomy and relatedness, so the combination of the two resulting in an optimal amount of dependence should have quite positive correlates. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p101]
- Autonomous dependence is natural, useful, and healthy; it is coerced or controlled dependence that is maladaptive. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p101]
