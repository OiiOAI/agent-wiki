# Stress Inoculation

## Summary

The phenomenon by which mild, controlled stress makes cells and organisms more resilient to future challenges. Like vaccines for the immune system, mild stressors cause biological systems to overcompensate and strengthen themselves. Exercise induces this adaptive stress response, leaving neurons harder for future demands. The key is that stress must be mild enough to allow recovery.

## Key facts

- A 1980s Department of Energy study found nuclear shipyard workers exposed to very low radiation had a 24 percent lower mortality rate than unexposed workers, demonstrating stress inoculation. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p54]
- The author states: 'Stress seems to have an effect on the brain similar to that of vaccines on the immune system. In limited doses, it causes brain cells to overcompensate and thus gird themselves against future demands.'. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p54]
- Exercise controls emotional and physical feelings of stress and works at the cellular level by generating molecular by-products that, under normal circumstances, leave cells hardier for future challenges. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p53]
- The paradigm is described as 'stress and recovery' — a fundamental biology concept where challenges allow systems to strive and grow. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p53]
- The paradox of stress is that 'our wonderful ability to adapt and grow doesn't happen without stress — we can't have the good without a bit of the bad.'. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p64]
