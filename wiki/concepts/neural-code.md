# Neural Code

## Summary

The book critically examines the concept of 'neural coding' in neuroscience. Buzsáki argues that the statement 'the brain codes information' is problematic because when terms are not precisely defined, they convey different meanings to different individuals. He distinguishes between Shannonian information theory (mathematical, content-neutral) and the broader concept of 'information as knowledge' used in neuroscience.

## Key facts

- The book questions the common statement that 'the brain codes information' because precise definitions are often lacking, leading to multiple disguised interpretations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p26]
- From an information theory perspective, answers like 'yes or no' represent only a single bit, but for the receiver they can induce profound and long-lasting brain changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p27]
- Claude Shannon warned that information theory is 'aimed in a very specific direction, not necessarily relevant to such fields as psychology.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p29]
- The author states that 'brains do not process information: they create it,' arguing the brain is a venture-seeking explorer constantly controlling the body to test its hypotheses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p29]
- Coding is defined as an agreement for communication between sender and receiver; without knowing the code, packets carry no inherent information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p29]

## Inferences

- Inference: Understanding brain function may require deciphering the brain's cipher (syntax) rather than just finding correlations between stimuli and neural responses.
