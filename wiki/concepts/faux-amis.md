# Faux Amis

## Summary

McGilchrist identifies 'faux amis' (false friends)—terms that both hemispheres use but understand differently. Like Alice's looking-glass worlds where things superficially look the same but are different, each hemisphere transforms concepts according to its own world. Examples include 'knowledge' and 'truth' (one impersonal and static versus personal and provisional), 'belief' (feeble knowing versus a caring relationship), 'will' (control versus desire/longing), and 'familiarity' and 'newness' (generic familiarity versus lived familiarity). The different understanding of these terms reflects the fundamentally different ontological status of each hemisphere's world.

## Key facts

- The left hemisphere's belief is absence of certainty—a feeble form of knowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p243]
- The right hemisphere's belief is a matter of care—a relationship of responsibility where 'I believe in you' means I stand in a caring relation. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p243]
- For the left hemisphere, truth is coherence—corresponding with itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p274]
- For the right hemisphere, truth is correspondence with something other than itself—being 'true to' something. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p274]
- The left hemisphere's newness is achieved by recombining known elements bizarrely; the right hemisphere's newness is the return from inauthenticity to authenticity through patient openness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p247]
