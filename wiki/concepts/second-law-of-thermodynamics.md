# Second Law of Thermodynamics

## Summary

The Second Law of Thermodynamics states that all observed natural processes generate entropy. High entropy implies low potential for doing work, low capacity for taking action, or high degree of confusion and disorder. This law is cited as indicating that whenever one attempts to do work or take action inside a closed concept-and-match-up-with-reality system, entropy must increase, creating confusion and disorder that cannot be determined within the system itself.

- Entropy is a concept representing the potential for doing work, the capacity for taking action, or the degree of confusion and disorder associated with any physical or information activity. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p451]
