# Rosegger Tailor Dreams

## Summary

Peter Rosegger, the Austrian writer who had been a journeyman tailor in his youth, reported a recurring series of dreams in which he found himself back in his tailor's workshop, working without pay beside his master. These dreams troubled him for years until one night when his master finally dismissed him with the words 'You're no good at tailoring, you can go!' After this dream, the recurring tailor dreams ceased. Freud analyzes these as punishment dreams of a parvenu—the proud author was reminded of his humble origins.

## Key facts

- Rosegger's dream series showed him still haunted by the shadow of an unhappy life from which he had at last escaped; all his enjoyment lay in his daytime existence. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1721]
- The tailor dreams were difficult to reconcile with the dominance of wish fulfillment as the theory would predict. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1721]
- After the dream in which his master dismissed him, Rosegger enjoyed peace and dreamt no more of the tailoring days which had thrown such a long shadow over his later years. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1720]

## Inferences

- Inference: These dreams are punishment dreams in which the author, who had risen from humble beginnings, was reminded by the dream censorship of his earlier inferior status. The satisfaction of being dismissed rather than having to leave voluntarily indicated the triumph of self-criticism over vanity.
