# Organize (CODE Step 2)

## Summary

Organize is the second step of CODE, involving saving information for actionability. Rather than organizing by subject, the author recommends organizing by project — placing notes where they will be useful the soonest. This approach contrasts with traditional methods like the Dewey decimal system and is designed to minimize the cognitive burden of filing while maximizing the likelihood of encountering relevant information at the right moment.

## Key facts

- Twyla Tharp uses 'the box' — a labeled file box for each project — into which she puts notebooks, clippings, CDs, videos, books, and photographs related to that project. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p71-73]
- Tharp's box gives her security to take risks, a way to put projects on hold and revisit them later, and a way to look back at past victories. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p73]
- The author recommends organizing for actionability rather than by subject, saving notes by which project they will support. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p76]
- Most people would save a psychology article in a 'Psychology' folder, but this is too broad to be useful — better to save it for a specific project where it's relevant. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p76]

## Inferences

- Inference: Organizing by project mirrors the 'Hollywood model' of work, where teams assemble for specific projects and disband when complete — an increasingly common structure in knowledge work.
