# Adaptive Organization

## Summary

An adaptive organization is one that automatically adjusts its process and performance to current conditions. Eric Ries defines it as the organizational structure, culture, and discipline needed to handle rapid and often unexpected changes in a startup environment. The key mechanisms include the Five Whys for proportional investment in problem prevention and speed regulators that help teams find their optimal pace of work.

## Key facts

- An adaptive organization automatically adjusts its process and performance to current conditions. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p211]
- Adaptive processes force startups to slow down and invest in preventing the kinds of problems that are currently wasting time; as those preventive efforts pay off, they naturally speed up again. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p213]
- Companies of any size need to manage a portfolio of activities, simultaneously tuning their engine of growth and developing new sources of growth for when that engine inevitably runs its course. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p207]
- The Five Whys approach acts as a natural speed regulator, preventing teams from working too fast and trading quality for time in a way that causes sloppy mistakes. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p217]

## Inferences

- Inference: The need for adaptive organizations arises because when a company grows, the job changes but employees often don't realize they've been transferred to a new role until they're already failing at it.
