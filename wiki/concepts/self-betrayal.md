# Self-Betrayal

## Summary

Self-betrayal is a mechanism in parapraxes where unconscious thoughts break through speech or writing, revealing what the person wished to conceal. Freud presents numerous examples where slips expose hidden wishes, criticisms, or identifications.

## Key facts

- Self-betrayal occurs when wording cancels out intention, exposing inner insincerity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2075]
- Slips of the tongue become a mode of mimetic expression for the expression of something one did not wish to say. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2075]
- The trace of affect following revelation of a slip indicates that some motive contributed to the interference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2071]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-betrayal operates across all parapraxic forms, including slips of the tongue, misreadings, and slips of the pen.
