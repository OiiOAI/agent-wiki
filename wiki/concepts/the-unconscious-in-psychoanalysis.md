# The Unconscious in Psycho-Analysis

## Summary

A 1912 paper where Freud distinguishes three uses of the term 'unconscious': (1) descriptive (latent ideas of which we are not aware), (2) dynamic (ideas with active yet unconscious character, as seen in neuroses), and (3) systematic (designating the system 'The Unconscious' abbreviated Ubw). The paper emphasizes that unconscious ideas can be highly active while remaining cut off from consciousness, and that the laws governing unconscious activity differ from conscious processes, as demonstrated through dream analysis.

## Key facts

- An unconscious conception is one of which we are not aware but whose existence we admit on other proofs or signs. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3958]
- Post-hypnotic suggestion demonstrates that an idea can become active without becoming conscious, revealing unconscious ideas as active. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3958]
- Hysterical phenomena show the mind is full of active yet unconscious ideas, which is the essential fact in psychology of all neuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3959]
- The post-hypnotic suggestion experiment shows ideas can be active and unconscious simultaneously, not only weak and latent. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3959]
- Every psychical act begins as unconscious and may either remain so or develop into consciousness depending on whether it meets resistance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3962]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's threefold distinction (descriptive, dynamic, systematic) foreshadows his later structural model where 'the Unconscious' becomes a system with specific properties.
