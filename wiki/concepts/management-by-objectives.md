# Management by Objectives

## Summary

A management system that answers two questions: Where do I want to go? (the objective) and How will I pace myself to see if I am getting there? (the key results/milestones). MBO focuses on steps 2 and 3 of the planning process and should operate on relatively short time periods for effective feedback.

## Key facts

- A successful MBO system needs to answer only two questions: 'Where do I want to go?' (objective) and 'How will I pace myself to see if I am getting there?' (key results/milestones). [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1220-L1222]
- For feedback to be effective in MBO, it must be received very soon after the activity it is measuring occurs, so objectives should be set for relatively short periods. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1225-L1227]
- A key result must contain very specific wording and dates so that when deadline time arrives, there is no room for ambiguity. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1270-L1272]
- MBO is meant to pace a person with a stopwatch to gauge his own performance; it is not a legal document upon which to base a performance review. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1260-L1262]
- A subordinate can perform well and be rated well even though he missed his specified objective, if he achieved valuable unexpected outcomes. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1257-L1259]
