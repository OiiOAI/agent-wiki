# Active Inference

## Summary

Active Inference is a normative framework for understanding sentient behavior, derived from the free energy principle. It casts perception, action, planning, and learning as problems of (approximate) Bayesian inference under a probabilistic generative model. The central imperative is that living organisms must minimize the surprise of their sensory observations—operationalized as minimization of variational free energy—via both perception (updating beliefs) and action (changing the world). Planning is treated as inference, scored by expected free energy. The framework offers two complementary roads: the low road (starting from the Bayesian brain) and the high road (starting from first principles of self-organization and the free energy principle).

## Key facts

- Active Inference treats perception as unconscious inference, in keeping with Helmholtz's (1866) notion of perception as unconscious inference and the modern Bayesian brain hypothesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p23]
- Under Active Inference, both perception and action minimize the same quantity—variational free energy—complementarily: perception updates beliefs to reduce divergence, while action changes observations to maximize evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p30]
- Active Inference extends the inferential approach to action through the concept that agents engage in self-evidencing—acting to obtain sensory data consistent with their internal model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p46]
- Active Inference posits that living organisms must minimize the surprise of their sensory observations to survive, where surprise is defined as the negative log probability of observations under a generative model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p46]
- Active Inference accommodates planning through expected free energy, which scores alternative policies (action sequences) by their expected future free energy, thereby balancing exploration and exploitation automatically. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p9]
- Expected free energy can be decomposed into information gain (epistemic value) and pragmatic value, which together dissolve the classic exploit-explore dilemma in behavioral psychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p33]
- Active Inference inherits from a free energy principle that equates existence with self-evidencing, where even non-sentient systems minimize free energy simply by virtue of their existence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p7]
- The two roads to Active Inference are: the high road (starting from how organisms persist and act adaptively, motivating Active Inference normatively) and the low road (starting from the Bayesian brain, motivating it as a variational approximation). [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p15]
- Active Inference unifies perception and action under one imperative—minimizing variational free energy—whereas traditional frameworks treat them separately. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p8]
- Under Active Inference, learning is another way to minimize free energy, operating at a slower timescale than perception but sharing the same inferential nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p8]
- Markov blankets provide a formal separation between an adaptive system's internal states and external states of the environment, with sensory and active states mediating all interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p42]
- Active Inference reconciles enactive theories (self-organization), cybernetic theories (goal-directed purposive behavior), and predictive theories (model-based control) under a unified variational framework. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p57]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because variational free energy is defined as an upper bound on surprise, minimizing it guarantees a reduction in surprise but does not guarantee exact Bayesian inference. This means Active Inference agents achieve approximate Bayesian optimality.
