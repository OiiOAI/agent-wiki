# Exercise and Academic Performance

## Summary

Research demonstrating correlations between physical fitness and academic achievement. Studies from California Department of Education with over one million students show fit students score significantly higher on academic tests. Exercise primes the brain for learning by improving mood, focus, and motivation, while also triggering neurobiological changes including BDNF elevation and neurogenesis that strengthen the brain's capacity to form memories.

## Key facts

- A Virginia Tech study showed that cutting gym class and allocating more time to math, science, and reading did not improve test scores. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p22]
- The California Department of Education correlated FitnessGram scores with Stanford Achievement Test scores for over one million students, finding fit kids scored twice as well academically. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p22]
- Students scoring a six on FitnessGram ranked in the 67th percentile in math and 45th percentile in reading; those passing only one area ranked in the 35th and 21st percentiles. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p22]
- A 2004 panel of 13 researchers reviewed over 850 studies and recommended schoolchildren should participate in one hour or more of moderate to vigorous physical activity daily. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p23]
- Charles Hillman found that fit kids showed more electrical brain activity during attention tasks, indicating more neurons being recruited for attention. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p25]
- Hillman found that 'fit kids slow down and make sure they get that next one right' on tests, demonstrating better executive function related to the prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p25]
- Titusville, Pennsylvania schools saw standardized test scores rise from below state average to 17 percent above in reading and 18 percent above in math after implementing Naperville-style PE in 2000. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p31]
