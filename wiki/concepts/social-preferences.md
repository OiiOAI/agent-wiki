# Social Preferences

## Summary

Social preferences refer to a class of motivational characteristics indicating that individuals care positively or negatively about others' material payoff or well-being. They encompass concerns for fairness, reciprocity, inequity aversion, and efficiency. Social preferences are measured through anonymous one-shot games like the dictator game, ultimatum game, and third-party punishment game.

## Key facts

- Social preferences mean that an individual's utility depends not only on their own material payoff but also on the payoffs of others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p215]
- In the dictator game, where recipients have no choice, dictators give nothing about half the time, indicating limited positive social preferences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p218]
- In the third-party punishment game, 50–60% of impartial third parties punish selfish dictators at personal cost, indicating enforcement of fairness norms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p218]
- Social preferences should be considered one important component of utility functions, traded off against selfish goals: the more costly other-regarding behaviors are, the less likely individuals are to display them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p217]
- There is considerable individual heterogeneity in social preferences: some people show little concern for others while others show strong social preferences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p217]
- Social preferences are preferences that incorporate the welfare of others alongside one's own material payoffs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p210]
- Many individuals are aware of other people's social preferences, and completely egoistic subjects often know that others have social preferences, causing them to change behavior significantly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p210]
- Anticipation of punishment driven by social preferences strongly increases cooperation in public good games and contract enforcement games. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p210]
- Theories of social preferences include inequity aversion (Fehr and Schmidt, 1999), reciprocity-based preferences (Rabin, 1993), and altruism (Andreoni, 1990). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p214]

## Inferences

- Inference: The heterogeneity in social preferences explains why in competitive markets everyone may appear self-interested, while in strategic games most individuals deviate from self-interest.
- Inference: The neural reward circuitry overlap between self-rewards and social rewards (via mutual cooperation, charitable donations, punishment of unfair behavior) suggests social preferences may be encoded in similar neural substrates as primary rewards
- Inference: The discovery that social preference behaviors activate reward circuitry suggests these preferences may function as genuine expressions of preference rather than merely constraints on selfish behavior

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: There is surprisingly little evidence on the intra-personal stability of social preferences over time.
- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanism by which social preferences are weighed against economic self-interest remains to be fully specified
