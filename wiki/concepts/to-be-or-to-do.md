# To Be or to Do

## Summary

The 'To Be or to Do' speech was John Boyd's philosophical statement about career choices delivered to Raymond Leopold. It presented a fork in the road where one path led to compromises, promotions, and membership in the club, while the other led to doing something meaningful for country and self, but with the risk of not advancing. The speech defined Boyd's core values about integrity over advancement.

## Key facts

- Boyd presented the choice as going one way to 'be somebody' with compromises and promotions, versus another way to 'do something'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p282]
- He told Leopold that if he decided to 'do something,' he may not get promoted but would not have to compromise himself. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p282]
- The speech was Boyd's way of laying ground rules and testing Leopold before accepting him as an Acolyte. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p282]

## Inferences

- Inference: The speech encapsulates Boyd's view that career advancement often comes at the cost of integrity and doing meaningful work.
