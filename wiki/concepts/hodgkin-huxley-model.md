# Hodgkin-Huxley Model

## Summary

The Hodgkin-Huxley model is a quantitative mathematical description of the ionic mechanisms underlying the initiation and propagation of the action potential. Developed from voltage-clamp data on the squid giant axon, it describes how voltage-gated Na+ and K+ conductances change over time in response to membrane potential changes.

## Key facts

- Hodgkin and Huxley described ionic currents as INa = gNa × (Vm − ENa) and IK = gK × (Vm − EK), where gNa and gK are voltage- and time-dependent conductances. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p264]
- The model successfully predicted that Na+ channels activate rapidly while K+ channels activate more slowly, and that Na+ channels inactivate during sustained depolarization while K+ channels do not. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p266]
- Calculated action potential waveforms matched experimental recordings, validating the model and confirming that Na+ and K+ conductances alone account for action potential generation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p267]
- The Hodgkin-Huxley model successfully predicted the shape and conduction velocity of the action potential from measurements of Na+ and K+ conductance changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p208]
- The model describes Na+ channels as having activation and inactivation gates, while K+ channels have only activation gates that do not inactivate rapidly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p208]
- According to the model, the action potential threshold occurs when net inward Na+ current exceeds outward K+ and leakage currents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p209]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Hodgkin-Huxley model predicted that voltage-gated channels contain charged gating particles that move through the membrane electric field—an inference later confirmed by gating current measurements.
