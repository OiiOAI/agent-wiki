# Social Connectedness and Happiness

## Summary

The single most consistent finding from fifty years of happiness research is that happiness is best predicted by the breadth and depth of one's social connections, not by wealth, health, or other material factors. This aligns with right hemisphere functions, which are oriented toward interpersonal engagement and contextual understanding.

## Key facts

- Robert Putnam writes in Bowling Alone that 'the breadth and depth of one's social connections' is the best predictor of happiness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p596]
- Depression rates differ by as much as 12-fold between cultures, linked to the degree of stability and interconnectedness within a culture. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p596]
- Rates of psychological disturbance in Mexican immigrants to the USA start low, but increase proportionally with time spent in the US, from 18% for those with less than thirteen years to 49% for those born in the US. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p596]

## Inferences

- Inference: Social connectedness represents a right hemisphere function that provides genuine value, contrasting with the left hemisphere's focus on material acquisition.
