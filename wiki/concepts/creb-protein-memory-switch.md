# CREB Protein and Memory Switch

## Summary

CREB (cAMP Response Element-Binding protein) is a regulatory protein that acts as a molecular switch converting short-term memory to long-term memory. CREB-1 activates gene expression while CREB-2 suppresses it; long-term memory requires activation of CREB-1 and inactivation of CREB-2 by protein kinase A and MAP kinase.

## Key facts

- Protein kinase A can activate CREB, which binds to a promoter called the cyclic AMP response element, switching on genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- In 1990, Dash and Hochner found that CREB is present in Aplysia sensory neurons and is essential to long-term strengthening of synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- Blocking CREB in the nucleus of a sensory neuron prevented long-term but not short-term strengthening of synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- Bartsch found that injecting phosphorylated CREB into the nucleus was sufficient to turn on genes producing long-term facilitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- There are two forms of CREB: CREB-1 that activates gene expression and CREB-2 that suppresses it. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- Repeated stimulation causes protein kinase A to activate CREB-1 and MAP kinase to inactivate CREB-2. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- Tully and Yin discovered in 1995 that CREB proteins are essential for long-term memory in Drosophila, with mutant flies producing more CREB activator showing flashbulb-like memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p278]
