# Excitatory Postsynaptic Potential

## Summary

An excitatory postsynaptic potential (EPSP) is a brief depolarization of the postsynaptic membrane caused by opening of ligand-gated cation channels, bringing the neuron closer to action potential threshold.

## Key facts

- EPSPs at glutamatergic synapses result from opening of AMPA and NMDA receptor-channels permeable to Na+ and K+, with a reversal potential near 0 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p277]
- The end-plate potential at the neuromuscular junction is approximately 75 mV, produced by opening of approximately 200,000 ACh receptor-channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p260]
- EPSPs propagate passively from their site of initiation, decreasing in amplitude and slowing in time course with distance from the synapse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p258]
- In spinal motor neurons, a single sensory neuron produces a unitary EPSP of only 0.2 to 0.4 mV, far below the 10 mV threshold for generating an action potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p262]
- EPSPs are produced by opening of glutamate-gated channels permeable to both Na+ and K+, with a reversal potential near 0 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p264]
- The amplitude of an EPSP decreases with distance from its site of initiation due to passive spread along the membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p244]
- Most EPSPs in the CNS are mediated by ionotropic glutamate receptors (AMPA and NMDA types). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p265]
- Temporal summation of EPSPs occurs when consecutive inputs arrive faster than the membrane time constant allows decay. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p280]

## Inferences

- Inference: The small size of unitary EPSPs in CNS neurons reflects the need for integration of multiple inputs, unlike the neuromuscular junction where a single input is sufficient.
