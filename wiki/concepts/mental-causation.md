# Mental causation

## Summary

Mental causation refers to the problem of how mental states—such as beliefs, desires, and intentions—can cause physical actions in the body. The book argues against both dualism (which posits separate mental and physical substances) and reductive materialism (which reduces thoughts to mere neural patterns without causal efficacy). Instead, a 'cognitive realism' view holds that neural patterns comprising thoughts have causal power solely by virtue of what they mean—based on the criteria configured into the system itself. Thoughts are not immaterial; they are physically instantiated in patterns of neural activity that naturally affect activity in other brain regions.

## Key facts

- The dualist position creates a problem of explaining how the mental and physical realms can interact, which Descartes could not adequately answer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p267]
- Thoughts are not immaterial: they are physically instantiated in patterns of neural activity in various parts of the brain, which can naturally have effects on how activity evolves in other regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p267]
- Although thoughts must be instantiated in some pattern of neural activity, such patterns only have causal power in the system by virtue of what they mean. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p268]
- When we manipulate cognitive representations and consciously work out what to do, that is us deciding—it is not an illusion or epiphenomenon or post-hoc rationalization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p268]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mental causation is a naturalized phenomenon that can be understood without positing any 'ghost in the machine'—the 'ghost' is the machine at work.
- Inference: The meaning of neural patterns drives the unfolding of neural activity, so the low-level physical details are often incidental to understanding causation.
