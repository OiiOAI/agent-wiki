# Evolution of Team Topologies

## Summary

Team topologies should evolve over time as business, organizational, market, technological, and personnel needs change. Organizations must anticipate evolution of team patterns, and different teams within an organization will have different interaction and collaboration needs at any one time. The goal is to move from discovery activities to establish predictable delivery over time.

## Key facts

- Organizations must anticipate the need for evolution of team patterns to meet business, organizational, market, technological, and personnel needs. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2600-L2610]
- The same two teams might need to collaborate closely at some point but be independent six or nine months later. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2610-L2620]
- Interaction modes of different teams should be expected to change regularly depending on what the teams need to achieve. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2750-L2760]
- Initial close collaboration evolves into more limited collaboration on a smaller number of things through discovery, further evolving into X-as-a-Service once established. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2780-L2790]
- Trigger for evolution: Software has grown too large for one team when team size exceeds Dunbar's number of fifteen. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2850-L2870]
- Trigger for evolution: Delivery cadence is becoming slower, shown by decreased velocity or throughput metrics. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2900-L2920]

## Inferences

- Inference: Organizations should treat team topology changes as expected and planned, not as emergency reorganizations.
