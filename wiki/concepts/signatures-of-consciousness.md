# Signatures of Consciousness

## Summary

Four measurable neural markers that indicate the presence of consciousness: (1) late recurrent cortical activation, (2) involvement of frontoparietal networks, (3) nonlinear global ignition above a threshold, and (4) gamma-band synchronization. These signatures are used to detect consciousness in clinical patients and to distinguish conscious from unconscious processing in experiments.

## Key facts

- The first signature involves late cortical activation occurring 300-400ms after stimulus presentation, distinguishing conscious from unconscious processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p117-121]
- The second signature involves the participation of frontoparietal associative cortices, particularly prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p121-125]
- The third signature involves a nonlinear threshold or 'global ignition' where neural activity suddenly spreads widely across the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p130-136]
- The fourth signature involves long-distance synchronization in the gamma band (40Hz) reflecting coherent binding of conscious content. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p136-140]
- Signatures are used to detect consciousness in patients with neurological disorders including vegetative and minimally conscious states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p202-233]
- The P3 wave (P300) is a key electrophysiological signature associated with conscious novelty detection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p124-125]
- Signatures can be used to decode conscious thoughts by examining neuron firing patterns in anterior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p145-149]

## Inferences

- Inference: These four signatures provide objective markers that can supplement clinical assessment of consciousness in patients who cannot communicate verbally
- Inference: The signatures may help distinguish conscious perception from unconscious masked perception in experimental paradigms
