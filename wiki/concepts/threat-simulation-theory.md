# Threat Simulation Theory

## Summary

Threat Simulation Theory (TST), proposed by Finnish philosopher and cognitive neuroscientist Antti Revonsuo in 2000, proposes that dreaming evolved as a mechanism for simulating threatening events and rehearsing possible means of avoiding or surviving them. Empirical support for TST has been mixed, with studies finding that realistic life-threatening events occur infrequently in dreams and that only a small percentage of dreams depicting realistic threats contain effective avoidance responses. In 2016, Revonsuo proposed an alternative Social Simulation Theory, suggesting that dreaming simulates and strengthens social skills, bonds, interactions, and networks.

## Key facts

- Threat Simulation Theory was proposed by Antti Revonsuo in 2000, proposing that dreaming evolved as a mechanism for simulating threatening events and rehearsing avoidance or survival responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p101]
- Studies found that realistic life-threatening events occur infrequently in dreams, with only a small percentage of threatening dreams containing effective avoidance responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p101]
- In a study of 212 adult recurrent dreams, a third did not contain threatening events, and 80 percent of identified threats were fictional or very unlikely to occur in waking life. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p101]
- Successful avoidance responses occurred in fewer than 1 in 5 threatening recurrent dreams, and in fewer than 1 in 25 recurrent dreams overall. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p101]
- In 2016, Revonsuo proposed an alternative Social Simulation Theory, proposing that the function of dreaming is to simulate and strengthen social skills, bonds, interactions, and networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p102]
