# Stuckness

## Summary

Stuckness is the mental state accompanying physical stuckness when working on a motorcycle (or any problem). The narrator argues that stuckness should not be avoided or feared because it is the psychic predecessor of all real understanding. At the moment of stuckness, one is at the cutting edge of the train of knowledge, at the track of reality itself. Traditional scientific method cannot help at this point—only Quality perception can guide where to go next.

## Key facts

- Traditional scientific method is good for seeing where you've been and testing truth, but cannot tell you where you ought to go; creativity, originality, inventiveness, intuition, and imagination are completely outside its domain. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p239]
- The narrator proposes that stuckness is not the worst possible situation but the best—a moment to be cultivated, as Zen Buddhists go to great lengths to induce through koans and meditation. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p243]
- An egoless acceptance of stuckness is identified as a key to understanding Quality in mechanical work and other endeavors. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p244]
- The difference between a good mechanic and a bad one is precisely the ability to select good facts from bad ones on the basis of Quality; good mechanics have to care. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p240]

## Inferences

- Inference: Stuckness reveals the limitations of dualistic subject-object thinking, and overcoming it requires returning to Quality perception.
