# White Belt Mindset

## Summary

The white belt mindset is the concept that mastery requires ongoing willingness to be a beginner, to play the fool, and to approach each moment with openness and emptiness that allows new learning. George Leonard illustrates this through Jigoro Kano's request to be buried in his white belt—signifying that at the moment of death, the ultimate transformation, we are all beginners.

## Key facts

- To be a learner, you must be willing to be a fool—meaning one with the spirit of the medieval fool, the court jester, who bears the number zero signifying the fertile void from which all creation springs. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Ter - George Leonard.pdf#p114]
- The Zen story of the wise man and the tea illustrates that if one's cup is already full, there is no space for anything new. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Ter - George Leonard.pdf#p114]
- Young people sometimes learn faster than older people because they are more willing to let themselves be foolish. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Ter - George Leonard.pdf#p114]
- Abraham Maslow discovered a childlike quality he called 'second naivete' in people who met an unusually high degree of their potential. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Ter - George Leonard.pdf#p115]
- Ashleigh Montagu used the term 'neoteny' to describe geniuses such as Mozart and Einstein. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Ter - George Leonard.pdf#p115]
- Jigoro Kano, founder of judo, requested to be buried in his white belt at the end of his life, showing that at the moment of highest accomplishment, the image of the newest student remains in the master's mirror. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Ter - George Leonard.pdf#p115]
