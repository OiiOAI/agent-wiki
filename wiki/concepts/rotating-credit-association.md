# Rotating Credit Association

## Summary

A rotating credit association is an informal financial mechanism used by ethnic communities to pool savings and provide loans to members. Light documented their use among Chinese and Japanese Americans as a form of social capital that enables entrepreneurship without reliance on formal banking institutions.

## Key facts

- Rotating credit associations were documented by Light (1972) as mechanisms for mobilizing capital within ethnic communities. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p446]
- Peterson noted rotating credit associations in his study of Chinese Americans and Japanese Americans, citing Sowell (1978). [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p446]
- Some rotating credit associations among African-Americans functioned more as rotating consumption associations, with money used for daily needs rather than productive business investment. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p447]
- The informal mutual aid character of rotating credit associations can paradoxically deconcentrate savings and hinder the capital accumulation needed for small business formation. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p447]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rotating credit associations represent a form of social capital that can either enable or constrain entrepreneurship depending on how pooled resources are used

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The effectiveness of rotating credit associations in different ethnic communities varies based on how they are structured and used
