# Partial Awakening Theory of Dreams

## Summary

The prevailing medical theory that dreams result from a partial, incomplete awakening of brain cells during sleep, producing disconnected and irrational mental content.

## Key facts

- According to Binz, products of fatigue accumulated in the albumen of the brain gradually diminish during early morning hours; groups of cells emerge into wakefulness while torpor persists around them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1351]
- Binz concluded that dreams must be characterized as somatic processes, in every case useless and in many cases positively pathological. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1352]
- Maury gave the most elaborate exposition of this view, imagining that waking or sleeping state could be shifted from one anatomical region to another. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1352]

## Inferences

- Inference: This theory leaves no room for assigning any function to dreaming, treating dreams as byproducts of incomplete physiological processes rather than meaningful psychical events.
