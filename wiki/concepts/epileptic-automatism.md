# Epileptic Automatism

## Summary

Epileptic automatism is a condition occurring during or after absence seizures in which consciousness is suspended but automatic behaviors continue. Patients may perform complex actions (walking, opening doors) without awareness, purpose, or memory. The episode ends with sudden return of consciousness and bewilderment. Emotion is absent throughout.

## Key facts

- During absence seizure automatism, patients remain awake with preserved muscle tone but show blank facial expression, suspended movement, and no conscious awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p96]
- Absence automatisms can last from three seconds to tens of seconds, during which patients may walk out of rooms or even buildings in a confused state. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p96]
- Patients have no recollection of events during the seizure or automatism period, cannot recognize observers, and do not know who they are or what they are doing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p97]
- Emotion is missing throughout absence seizures and absence automatisms—no background emotions or specific emotions are exhibited. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p99]
