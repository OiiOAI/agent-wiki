# Social and Emotional Learning

## Summary

Social and Emotional Learning (SEL) refers to school-based programs that teach emotional intelligence skills. Research shows SEL correlates with improved academic performance, better relationships, and reduced behavioral problems. Effective SEL must be systemic, proactive, integrated into curriculum across all grade levels, and culturally responsive.

## Key facts

- A 2018 McGraw-Hill Education survey found that an overwhelming majority of administrators, teachers, and parents believe SEL is just as important as academic learning, yet 65 percent of teachers said they need more time than they currently have. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p178]
- Two meta-analyses demonstrated that systematic processes integrating SEL are the common element among schools reporting improved teacher-student relationships, decreased emotional challenges, and increased academic success. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p186]
- Effective SEL must be systemic rather than piecemeal, requiring buy-in from leadership and bottom, ongoing professional development, and integration into leadership, instruction, faculty meetings, family engagement, hiring procedures, and policies. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p180]
- SEL needs to be culturally responsive, as how people express and regulate emotions varies greatly across cultures and programs developed within Western culture may not address cultural subgroups adequately. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p184]

## Inferences

- Inference: The disconnect between belief in SEL's importance and its actual implementation in teacher training programs suggests systemic barriers beyond individual teacher willingness.
