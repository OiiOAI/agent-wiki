# Pattern Recognition

## Summary

Pattern recognition is the cognitive process by which decision makers identify familiar situations by matching current cues to stored patterns derived from past experience. A pattern is a set of cues that usually occur together, so that recognizing a few cues allows expectation of others. When a pattern is recognized, decision makers gain a sense of what goals are achievable, what to expect next, and what typical responses should be.

## Key facts

- A pattern is a set of cues that usually chunk together so that if you see a few of the cues you can expect to find the others, giving a sense of familiarity. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p40]
- Pattern recognition allows decision makers to recognize situations as familiar by matching them to patterns encountered in the past. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p40]
- Herbert Simon, Nobel laureate, demonstrated that pattern recognition explains how people can make effective decisions without conducting deliberate analysis. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p41]
- The more patterns decision makers learn, the easier it is to match a new situation to patterns in their reservoir. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p40]
