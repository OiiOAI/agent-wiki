# Sexless Marriage

## Summary

Sexless marriage refers to marriages with minimal or absent sexual intimacy. Perel argues that when one partner unilaterally decides there will be no or very little sex, that is not monogamy but 'enforced celibacy.' She examines the emotional toll of sexual deprivation, why people remain in these marriages, and how they may lead to infidelity.

## Key facts

- When one partner has steadfastly been unresponsive to sexual advances for years or decades, while remaining affectionate and close, Perel states that this is not monogamy—it is 'enforced celibacy.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p183]
- Commentators on sexless marriages have decided that fewer than ten times a year might as well be nothing, and fifteen to twenty percent of couples apparently belong in this category. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p184]
- Google searches for 'sexless marriage' outnumber searches related to any other marital issue, according to big data analyst Seth Stephens-Davidowitz reporting in the New York Times. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p185]
- Perel observes that many people have affairs not to exit their marriages, but in order to stay in them, using the third party as 'a fulcrum that helps to keep the couple in balance.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel suggests that the cultural condemnation of infidelity prevents necessary reckoning with how enforced celibacy within marriages constitutes its own form of betrayal.
