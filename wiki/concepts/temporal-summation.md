# Temporal Summation

## Summary

Temporal summation is the process by which consecutive synaptic potentials produced by a single presynaptic neuron are added together in the postsynaptic cell. The extent of summation depends on the membrane time constant, which determines how long a synaptic potential persists before decaying to the resting potential.

## Key facts

- The membrane time constant helps determine the time course of the synaptic potential in response to the EPSC, thereby controlling temporal summation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- Neurons with a large membrane time constant have a greater capacity for temporal summation than do neurons with a shorter time constant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- The longer the time constant, the greater is the likelihood that two consecutive inputs will summate to bring the cell membrane to its threshold for an action potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
