# Corollary Discharge

## Summary

Corollary discharge is a copy of the motor command for eye movements that is fed to the visual system to compensate for the visual effects of saccades, thereby creating a stable representation of the visual world. This concept, first proposed by Hermann von Helmholtz in the 19th century, involves pathways from the superior colliculus through the thalamus to the frontal cortex. It provides information necessary for both perception of target location and accurate visually guided movement.

## Key facts

- Helmholtz postulated that a copy of the motor command for each saccade was fed to the visual system to compensate for eye movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p630]
- The corollary discharge pathway goes from the intermediate layers of the superior colliculus to the medial dorsal nucleus of the thalamus to the frontal eye field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p632]
- Inactivation of the medial dorsal nucleus of the thalamus impairs monkeys' ability to identify where their eyes land after a saccade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p632]
- The corollary discharge provides the vector of the saccade necessary for the monkey to perceive that a target had moved. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p633]
- Corollary discharge begins at least 100 ms before the saccade, while proprioceptive signals reach the lateral intraparietal area about 150 ms after the saccade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p634]
