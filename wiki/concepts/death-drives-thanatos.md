# Death Drives (Thanatos)

## Summary

Death drives (Todestriebe) are hypothesized by Freud in 'Beyond the Pleasure Principle' as a class of drives opposing the life/sexual drives (Eros). They are conceived as conservative forces urging the return of living substance to an inorganic state, expressing the 'inertia inherent in organic life.'

## Key facts

- Freud introduces the concept of death drives as opposing life drives, describing this as a dual-drive theory that extends beyond his earlier distinction between ego drives and sexual drives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5618]
- Death drives are described as conservative, urging restoration of an earlier inorganic state, while life drives work toward combination and preservation of living substance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5616]
- Sadism is tentatively proposed as a death drive displaced onto objects under the influence of narcissistic libido. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5634]

## Inferences

- Inference: The death drive concept emerged partly from clinical observations of the 'compulsion to repeat' in traumatic neuroses and transference, which seemed to override the pleasure principle.
- Inference: Masochism is interpreted as the turning of sadism (a death drive) back upon the ego, representing a regression to an earlier phase.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud himself expresses uncertainty about the death drive hypothesis, stating near the end of the work 'I do not know how far I believe in them' and acknowledging the speculative nature of his arguments.
