# Grand Gesture

## Summary

A deep work strategy that involves leveraging a radical change to your normal environment, coupled with significant investment of effort or money, to increase the perceived importance of a task and reduce procrastination.

## Key facts

- By making a radical environmental change dedicated to supporting a deep work task, you increase the perceived importance of the task, reducing your mind's instinct to procrastinate. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p73]
- J.K. Rowling checked into the Balmoral Hotel in Edinburgh to finish Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, paying over $1,000 per day. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p73]
- Bill Gates took Think Weeks, retreating to a cabin to think deeply without distraction about Microsoft's big issues. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p73]
- Alan Lightman retreats each summer to a tiny island in Maine that lacks Internet and phone service. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p74]
- Peter Shankman booked a round-trip business-class ticket to Tokyo to write a book manuscript in 30 hours, costing $4,000. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p74]
- William Shockley locked himself in a Chicago hotel room until he worked out the junction form of the transistor, which earned him a Nobel Prize. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p74]

## Inferences

- Inference: The psychological mechanism behind grand gestures is that committing seriously to a task by making a significant sacrifice raises the mental priority level, unlocking needed mental resources.
