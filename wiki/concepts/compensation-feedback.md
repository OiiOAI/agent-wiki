# Compensation as Task-Relevant Feedback

## Summary

Money serves different motivational purposes based on Maslow's hierarchy. For high performers, relative compensation (what they get compared to others) matters more than absolute amounts, indicating self-actualization motivation. Performance bonuses should increase as a percentage of total compensation for higher-paid individuals.

## Key facts

- If the absolute amount of a raise is important, the person is likely motivated by physiological or safety/security needs; if the relative amount (compared to others) is important, they are likely motivated by self-actualization. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1942-L1946]
- For highly paid senior managers, the performance bonus should be as high as 50 percent of total compensation, while middle managers should receive 10 to 25 percent. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1958-L1961]
- There are two pure forms of salary administration: experience-only and merit-only, with most companies using a compromise between them. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1974-L1976]
- In experience-only systems, an employee's salary increases with time in position but ultimately must level off because every job has a maximum value. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1978-L1980]
- Merit-based compensation cannot work unless we accept that if someone is going to be first, somebody else has to be last. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1994-L1996]
- Promotions must be based on performance because they communicate a value system to the rest of the company. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1996-L1998]

## Inferences

- Inference: A performance bonus scheme should balance individual performance, team performance, and company financial performance.
