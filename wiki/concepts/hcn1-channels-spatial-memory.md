# HCN1 channels in spatial memory

## Summary

HCN1 channels are hyperpolarization-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated ion channels strongly expressed in the distal dendrites of CA1 neurons, where they are partially open at resting potential. This decreases membrane resistance and reduces EPSP size. Mice lacking HCN1 show enhanced temporal summation of EPSPs and significantly faster learning in the Morris water maze, demonstrating that LTP at perforant path inputs to CA1 contributes to spatial learning.

## Key facts

- HCN1 channels are strongly expressed in the very distal dendrites of CA1 neurons, the site of entorhinal inputs, and are partially open at resting potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1560]
- HCN1 channels decrease membrane resistance (Rm) of the dendrite, reducing the size of the EPSP in response to a given excitatory synaptic current. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1560]
- Mice lacking HCN1 show markedly enhanced temporal summation of EPSPs in distal dendrites due to increased membrane time constant, contributing to a large increase in LTP magnitude at perforant path synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1560]
- Mice lacking HCN1 get smarter, exhibiting significantly faster learning to find the hidden platform in the Morris water maze relative to littermates with normal HCN1 levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1560]
