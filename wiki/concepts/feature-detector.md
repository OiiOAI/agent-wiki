# Feature detector

## Summary

A feature detector is a useful phrase expressing the specificity of neuronal responses, such as the response of many neurons in area V1 to orientation. However, the term has two shortcomings: it implies neurons respond only to the named feature (overlooking other related responses), and it implies the neuron is used by the brain to produce awareness of that feature (which may not be the case). Examples include orientation-selective cells, disparity-sensitive cells, and wavelength-sensitive cells.

## Key facts

- Feature detectors capture the fact that some neurons are sensitive to orientation, some to disparity, some to wavelength, and so on. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p161]
- The first shortcoming of 'feature detector' is that it implies a neuron responds only to the named feature, overlooking that it may also respond to other related features. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p161]
- The second shortcoming is that feature detector implies the neuron is being used by the brain to produce awareness of that feature, which may not be the case. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p161]
