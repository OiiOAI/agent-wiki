# Omnipotence of Thoughts

## Summary

The belief that thoughts and wishes have the power to alter the external world. Freud adopted this term from an obsessional neurotic patient. The concept is central to understanding animistic thinking and magic, and explains the psychological basis of taboo prohibitions.

## Key facts

- The principle governing magic, the technique of the animistic mode of thinking, is the principle of the 'omnipotence of thoughts'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4192]
- Tylor described magic as mistaking an ideal connection for a real one; Frazer stated that men mistook the order of their ideas for the order of nature. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4191]
- Primitive men have an immense belief in the power of their wishes; the basic reason why what they set about by magical means comes to pass is simply that they will it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4191]
- The omnipotence of thoughts survives in neurotics who believe that expressing evil wishes would inevitably lead to their fulfilment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4194]

## Inferences

- Inference: The omnipotence of thoughts may plausibly be brought into relation with narcissism and regarded as an essential component of it.
