# Fatal Unconnectedness

## Summary

Fatal unconnectedness is a term attributed to Alfred North Whitehead describing the condition in academic disciplines where each professor does not know the models of other disciplines, much less try to synthesize them with their own. Munger uses this concept to critique the failure of soft sciences to adopt multidisciplinary approaches.

## Key facts

- Whitehead spoke of 'fatal unconnectedness' of academic disciplines, wherein each professor doesn't even know the models of the other disciplines, much less try to synthesize those disciplines with his own. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p388]
- Munger states that many elite private schools now wisely use multidisciplinary methods in seventh grade science while graduate schools have not yet seen the same light. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p321]

## Inferences

- Inference: The term describes a structural problem in academia where disciplinary silos prevent the cross-pollination of ideas necessary for solving complex real-world problems.
