# Psychological Time

## Summary

Psychological time is the identification with past and future at the expense of the present moment. It creates the false sense that happiness lies somewhere other than now. The book contrasts psychological time with 'clock time'—practical time needed for planning and scheduling. Psychological time is identified as the core delusion that turns situations into personal problems and creates suffering. Unconsciousness, negativity, and suffering all have their roots in psychological time. The solution is not to abandon time entirely but to use clock time while remaining rooted in the timeless Now.

## Key facts

- Psychological time is identification with the past and continuous compulsive projection into the future. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p63]
- Psychological time is distinguished from clock time, which includes learning from the past and setting goals for the future. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p63]
- All negativity is caused by an accumulation of psychological time and denial of the present. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p66]
- Guilt, regret, resentment, grievances, sadness, and bitterness are caused by too much past, not enough presence. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p66]
- The book states that nonforgiveness necessarily implies a heavy burden of psychological time. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p64]
