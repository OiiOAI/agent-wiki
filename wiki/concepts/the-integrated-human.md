# The Integrated Human

## Summary

The Integrated Human refers to individuals who appear comfortable with themselves, able to laugh at their shortcomings, retain spontaneity and authenticity despite socialization. Greene outlines four steps for achieving integration: (1) See the Shadow—look for emphatic traits in yourself, examine emotional outbursts, notice projections onto others, review youthful behavior, and pay attention to dreams; (2) Embrace the Shadow—accept the Shadow completely and integrate it into present personality, exemplified by Abraham Lincoln's embrace of both his cruel and sensitive sides; (3) Explore the Shadow—tap into creative depths through dreams, intuition, and free association, as Einstein did with his theory of relativity; (4) Show the Shadow—consciously display repressed desires and assert yourself more, as Steve Jobs did.

## Key facts

- The four steps for achieving integration are: See the Shadow, Embrace the Shadow, Explore the Shadow, and Show the Shadow. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p314]
- Robert Bly examined his own Shadow and discovered he had 'calculating, manipulative qualities' he had projected onto advertising men and businesspeople, including 'a part of the stockbroker' within himself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p316]
- Abraham Lincoln channeled his aggressive, competitive energy into winning debates and elections, while channeling his sensitivity into empathy for the public and caring about lives lost in war. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p318]
- Einstein based one of his theories of relativity on an image from a dream, and Louis Pasteur made his great discovery through free association of ideas after a laboratory accident. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p318]
