# Misattribution

## Summary

Misattribution involves attributing a memory to the wrong source or time, including déjà vu experiences, cryptomnesia (unconscious plagiarism), and confusing whether something was personally experienced or imagined. Schacter examines the cognitive mechanisms underlying misattribution, including failures of source monitoring, and discusses the 'truth machine' paradigm for distinguishing true from false memories using neuroimaging.

## Key facts

- Déjà vu may result from a mismatch between the familiarity of a current experience and the inability to retrieve the source of that familiarity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p382]
- Cryptomnesia occurs when a person believes they created something original when they actually encountered it previously, constituting unintentional plagiarism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p382]
- Neuroimaging studies have identified patterns of brain activity that can distinguish true from false memories with moderate accuracy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p382]
- Source monitoring failures occur when people cannot distinguish between memories of actual events and memories of imagining or being told about events. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p382]

## Inferences

- Inference: Misattribution errors may reflect the operation of adaptive gist-based processing that prioritizes the content of memories over their source context.
