# Internal Milieu

## Summary

The internal milieu is the chemical environment within which cells live, first named by Claude Bernard in 1865. Its chemical profile is maintained within narrow stable ranges despite large changes in the external environment, making it essential for independent life. The representation of the internal milieu in the brain is a key component of the proto-self and provides stability for selfhood.

## Key facts

- Claude Bernard defined the internal milieu as the fluid within which cells live, noting its chemical profile is usually stable regardless of environmental changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p137]
- The internal milieu is tightly regulated by homeostatic mechanisms that ensure life-threatening deviations do not occur. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p141]
- The hypothalamus maintains a current register of the internal milieu along several dimensions: glucose levels, ion concentrations, water balance, pH, and hormone levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p156]
- The constancy of the internal milieu may serve as a blueprint and anchor for what eventually becomes a self in the mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p136]
