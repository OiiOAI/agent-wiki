# Natural Direct and Indirect Effects

## Summary

Natural direct and indirect effects are key quantities in causal mediation analysis. Natural direct effects capture how the outcome would change if the treatment were changed while the mediator remained at its natural value under the baseline treatment. Natural indirect effects capture how the outcome would change if the mediator were changed while the treatment remained fixed. These concepts were introduced by Robins and Greenland (1992) who deemed them problematic, but later formalized and legitimized by Pearl (2001) through the Mediation Formula.

## Key facts

- Natural direct and indirect effects were first conceptualized by Robins and Greenland in 1992. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3887-L3888]
- Robins and Greenland initially deemed natural direct and indirect effects problematic. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3888]
- Pearl (2001) formalized and legitimized natural direct and indirect effects, leading to the Mediation Formula. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3888-L3889]
- The formalization appeared in Proceedings of the Seventeenth Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3888-L3889]
