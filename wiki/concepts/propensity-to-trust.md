# Propensity to Trust

## Summary

Propensity to trust is the innate human inclination to trust others that individuals are born with. The concept explores how this natural trustfulness can be retained or restored through life experiences, and how extending trust to others can reignite this inner spirit in both the giver and receiver.

## Key facts

- Humans were born with a propensity to trust; as children, most people were naive, innocent, vulnerable, and gullible. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p384]
- Through life experience, many people become less trusting—sometimes with good reason. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p384]
- People can choose to retain or restore their propensity to trust through the key abilities of forgiveness and balanced analysis. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p384]
- Extending trust rekindles the inner spirit of both the giver and receiver of trust. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p385]
- Alexander the Great demonstrated profound trust when he accepted medicine from his physician Philip after receiving a letter warning him of a plot to poison him. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p383]
- Lao Tzu wrote 'No trust given, no trust received,' reflecting the reciprocal nature of trust. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p383]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept suggests that while experience may decrease trust propensity, individuals have agency to consciously choose to trust again.
