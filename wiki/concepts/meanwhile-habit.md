# Meanwhile Habit

## Summary

A Meanwhile Habit is a Tiny Habit that fills a small pocket of waiting time in your existing routine. The term describes those moments when you're waiting for something to happen—water to warm up in the shower, a red light to change, a grocery store line to move—and you can use that waiting period as an Anchor for a new habit. These habits start tiny and stay tiny because the time available is inherently limited.

## Key facts

- Meanwhile Habits use waiting periods as Anchors—for example, waiting for shower water to warm up (about twenty seconds). [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p137]
- Example: 'After I turn on the shower (and while I wait for warm water), I will think of one thing about my body that I'm grateful for.'. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p138]
- Brittany created a Meanwhile Habit: 'After I buckle my seatbelt, I will push play on my audiobook,' which allowed her to listen to at least five books a month. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p138]
- Meanwhile Habits start tiny and stay tiny due to the limited time available, but done consistently they can make a big difference. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p138]
