# Structural Functionalism

## Summary

Structural functionalism was for many years the dominant sociological theory, especially in the work of Talcott Parsons, Robert Merton, and their students. The theory focuses on large-scale social structures and institutions, their interrelationships, and their constraining effects on actors. It is a consensus theory that sees shared norms and values as fundamental to society, focuses on social order based on tacit agreements, and views social change as occurring slowly and orderly. Robert Nisbet called it "without any doubt, the single most significant body of theory in the social sciences in the present [20th] century." However, it declined dramatically in importance in the last three decades. The theory's weaknesses included being unable to deal adequately with social change, being politically conservative, and being incapable of adequately analyzing social conflict.
