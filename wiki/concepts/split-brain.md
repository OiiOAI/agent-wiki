# Split Brain

## Summary

Split brain is a condition resulting from severing the corpus callosum and anterior commissure, performed to relieve certain cases of epilepsy. Roger Sperry and colleagues demonstrated that split-brain patients effectively have two separate brains, with the left hemisphere typically handling speech and language while each hemisphere perceives only its corresponding visual field half.

## Key facts

- Split brain results when the corpus callosum—the large tract of nerve fibers connecting the cerebral cortex on one side of the head to that on the other—is severed completely, together with a smaller fiber tract called the 'anterior commissure.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p169]
- This surgical operation is performed to relieve certain cases of epilepsy that have failed to respond to other treatments. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p169]
- Roger Sperry was awarded a Nobel Prize in 1981 for showing that a cat or monkey whose brain had been split could be taught in such a way that one hemisphere learned one response while the other learned another, or even a conflicting response to the same situation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p170]
- As Sperry put it, 'It is as if the animal had two separate brains.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p170]
- For most right-handed people only the left hemisphere can speak or communicate through writing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p170]
- When the callosum is cut, the left hemisphere sees only the right half of the visual field; the right hemisphere sees the left half. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p170]
- When a picture is flashed into the right (nonspeaking) hemisphere, the left hand can point to and identify unseen objects by touch, but the patient will invent explanations based on what his left (speaking) hemisphere saw—this is called 'confabulation.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p171]
- Early experts mistakenly believed the corpus callosum had no psychological functions; these opinions are now known to be gross errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p170]

## Inferences

- Inference: The brain cannot use the intact intertectal commissure to transmit visual awareness information from one hemisphere to the other, suggesting the superior colliculus is unlikely to be the seat of visual awareness.
