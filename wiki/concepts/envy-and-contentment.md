# Envy and Contentment

## Summary

Charlie Munger views envy as one of the deadly sins and a particularly foolish one because it provides no enjoyment. He advises being satisfied with one's wealth and ignoring when others get richer faster, contrasting this with Stanley Druckenmiller's inability to tolerate others' success in technology stocks.

## Key facts

- Munger states: 'The idea of caring that someone is making money faster than you are is one of the deadly sins. Envy is a really stupid sin because it's the only one you could never possibly have any fun at.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p133]
- Soros couldn't bear to see others make money in the technology sector without him and got 'killed.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p133]
- Druckenmiller's fund suffered large losses because he always had to be the best and couldn't stand others beating him. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p133]
- Munger notes it doesn't bother him at all that others are making money in the tech sector. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p133]
- Johnny Carson's prescription for misery included envy. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p145]
