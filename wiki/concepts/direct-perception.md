# Direct Perception

## Summary

Direct perception is the theory advocated by James J. Gibson, holding that perception occurs directly through the senses without requiring intermediate cognitive representations or processing. This contrasts with cognitivist indirect realism, which holds that we perceive mental representations rather than the world directly.

## Key facts

- Proposed by Gibson as opposed to cognitivist indirect realism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p2]
- Is part of Gibson's direct realism position. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p2]
- Perceiving is an achievement of the individual, not an experience in the theatre of consciousness; it is a keeping-in-touch with the world, an experiencing of things rather than a having of experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p22]
- Direct perception is what one gets from seeing Niagara Falls, as distinguished from seeing a picture of it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p23]
- The act of picking up information is a continuous act, an activity that is ceaseless and unbroken. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p22]
- Perception of the environment can be direct because the change and associated non-change in the optic array is distinct from anything non-real. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p24]
- Perception is not a response to a stimulus but an act of information pickup; perception may or may not occur in the presence of information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p82]
- Unlike sensory awareness which has discoverable stimulus thresholds, perceptual awareness depends on the age of the perceiver, how well they have learned to perceive, and how strongly they are motivated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p82]
- The world is specified in the structure of light that reaches us, but it is entirely up to us to perceive it—the secrets of nature cannot be understood by breaking its code. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p88]
- We cannot explain perception in terms of communication; it is quite the other way around—we cannot convey information about the world unless we have perceived it first. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p88]
- Direct perception is the activity of getting information from the ambient array of light, involving exploratory activity of looking around, getting around, and looking at things. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p170]
- The traditional theory of depth perception was inadequate—tests of depth cues for pilots did not predict flight success, leading Gibson to suspect the whole framework was false. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p172]
- Metzger's 1930 experiment showed that when optical texture is eliminated from entering light (Ganzfeld), observers see 'nothing'—an empty medium rather than 'depth' or 'space'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p174]
- A perfectly clear sheet of glass is invisible because it transmits both light energy and optical information specifying air; people walk through glass doors and deer jump through glass windows. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p176]
- Experiments on equivalent configurations (Ames distorted room, rotating trapezoidal window) do not prove that presuppositions are necessary for perception, since with two eyes and movement, the abnormalities are perceived correctly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p192]

## Inferences

- Inference: Direct perception does not mean perception is infallible; rather, it means the perceiver is in direct contact with environmental information rather than mediated by internal representations.
- Inference: The directness of perception does not mean perception is automatic or infallible; it depends on learning and motivation, suggesting that perceptual learning involves learning to extract information already available in the environment.
- Inference: The direct perception hypothesis implies that animals are capable of extracting invariants from a changing optic array without needing to build up percepts from elementary sensations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Some critics, like Ralph Norman (1980), argued that Gibson's theory lacks an account of the process of pickup, suggesting Gibson thought optical structure is automatically picked up if present.
- Uncertain: Whether direct perception operates fully for all environmental affordances or whether some high-order invariants require extensive learning to extract.
