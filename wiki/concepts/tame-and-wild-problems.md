# Tame Problems and Wild Problems

## Summary

Tame problems (Type I) are those with clear definitions, predefined goals, and established rules for solving them, found in the Clear and Complicated domains. Wild problems (Type II complex and Type III wicked) are ill-defined with no clear solutions, cannot be bounded, and consist of conditions that change in real-time. Reductionist approaches fail with wild problems.

## Key facts

- Tame problems are Type I problems where there is consensus on the problem and solution, including examples like calculating waste in a process and solving basic mathematical equations. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p387]
- Type II problems (complex problems) have consensus on the problem's definition but no consensus on how to solve the problem, with examples including cancer research, economic disparity, and terrorism. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p387]
- Type III problems (wicked problems) are problems in which neither the problem nor the solution can be agreed upon, with examples including climate change and pandemics. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p387]
- The practice of divide and conquer (reductionism) does not work with wild problems and eliminates complexity, resulting in outcomes that fail when reintroduced to the wild. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p388]
- West Churchman stated that attempting to tame wicked problems is 'morally wrong' when done partially: 'Whoever attempts to tame a part of a wicked problem, but not the whole, is morally wrong.'. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p388]
- The fable of blind men and the hippopotamus contrasts elephants (tame) with hippopotami (wild/aggressive), where each requires a different approach. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p387]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wild problems require holistic approaches that include the wildness of the problem rather than attempting to reduce or tame them
- Inference: SenseMaker and narrative approaches are particularly suited for addressing wild and wicked problems
