# Parallel Processing in Vision

## Summary

Vision employs parallel processing pathways from the retina onward, with distinct channels dedicated to different visual features (achromatic contrast, red-green, blue-yellow) that remain anatomically segregated through the LGN and into cortical areas, eventually diverging into dorsal (where/how) and ventral (what) processing streams.

## Key facts

- Parallel processing starts in the retina with distinct ganglion cell types analyzing different local features: achromatic contrast (parasol cells), red-green opponent information (midget cells), and blue-yellow information (bistratified cells). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p549]
- The LGN in primates consists of six primary layers: four parvocellular and two magnocellular, each paired with a koniocellular layer, maintaining anatomical segregation of parallel channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p549]
- Parvocellular layers receive input from midget retinal ganglion cells (~70% of primate retinal input), carrying red-green opponent information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p549]
- Magnocellular layers receive achromatic contrast information from parasol ganglion cells (~10% of retinal input). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p549]
- Koniocellular layers receive blue-yellow information from bistratified ganglion cells (~8% of retinal input). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p549]
- Visual information is processed along two major cortical pathways: a ventral pathway into the temporal lobe carrying 'what' information (object recognition), and a dorsal pathway into the parietal lobe carrying 'where/how' information (visually guided movement). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p551]
- The pathways are interconnected so that information is shared; for example, movement information from the dorsal pathway contributes to object recognition through kinematic cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]
- Connections between cortical areas are reciprocal—each area sends information back to the areas from which it receives input, providing top-down cognitive influences including attention and expectation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]

## Inferences

- Inference: The preservation of parallel channels through multiple processing stages suggests that different visual attributes are analyzed by dedicated neural circuits rather than by a single integrated processor.
