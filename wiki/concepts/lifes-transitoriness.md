# Life's Transitoriness

## Summary

Frankl argues that the transitoriness of existence does not make life meaningless but constitutes our responsibleness. Potentialities are transitory, but once actualized they become realities delivered into the past, wherein they are rescued and preserved from transitoriness. Nothing is irretrievably lost but everything irrevocably stored. Having been is the surest kind of being. We must decide what will be the monument of our existence with each choice being condemned or actualized, becoming an immortal footprint in the sands of time.

## Key facts

- The transitoriness of our existence in no way makes it meaningless but does constitute our responsibleness; man constantly makes his choice concerning the mass of present potentialities. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p89]
- As soon as potentialities are actualized, they are rendered realities at that very moment and saved and delivered into the past, wherein they are rescued and preserved from transitoriness. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p89]
- Nothing can be undone, and nothing can be done away with; having been is the surest kind of being. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p89]
