# Splitting of the Mind

## Summary

Breuer introduced the concept of 'splitting of the mind' (Spaltung) to describe the division of psychical activity into conscious and unconscious parts in hysterical patients. This differs from 'splitting of consciousness' in that the field of ideational psychical activity does not coincide with potential consciousness—the latter is more restricted. In severe hysteria, a large complex of ideas admissible to consciousness exists side by side with a smaller complex of ideas that are not. The split-off portion of psychical activity is inaccessible to perception by the self-consciousness of the waking individual. Breuer argued this represents a fundamental phenomenon of hysteria, present to a rudimentary degree in every major case.

## Key facts

- In hysterical patients, the field of ideational psychical activity does not coincide with potential consciousness, which is more restricted than the former. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p743]
- The splitting of psychical activity which is striking in 'double conscience' is present to a rudimentary degree in every major hysteria, and the liability to such dissociation is the basic phenomenon of this neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p744]
- Janet's findings describe a splitting not merely of psychical activity but of consciousness, with the split-off portion containing all psychical functions including self-consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p746]
- Breuer distinguished between his own concept of 'splitting of the mind' and Janet's concept of 'splitting of consciousness', arguing that in his view the field of ideational activity exceeds potential consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p743]

## Inferences

- Inference: Breuer's distinction between splitting of the mind versus splitting of consciousness reflects a theoretical difference regarding the scope of unconscious ideation—Breuer believed unconscious ideas could exist without necessarily forming a secondary conscious system.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The relationship between Breuer's 'splitting of the mind' and Freud's later concept of repression (Verdrängung) remains partially unclear in this text.
