# Valence (Affective)

## Summary

Valence is the hedonic dimension of affect, referring to whether a feeling is positive (pleasurable) or negative (unpleasurable). Solms argues that valence is inherently subjective and qualitative—it cannot be reduced to objective rewarding/punishing stimuli. Multiple competing needs generate different valenced states that must be evaluated on separate categorical scales rather than summated into a single metric.

## Key facts

- Valence refers to the hedonic dimension of affects, meaning they feel good or bad to us. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p178]
- Valence is inherently subjective and qualitative, not residing in stimuli themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p178]
- Multiple needs cannot be reduced to a single common denominator; each must be satisfied in its own right on separate scales. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: Solms' view that valence is subjective implies that what one person finds thrilling, another may find terrifying, supporting his critique of behaviourist approaches to emotion.
