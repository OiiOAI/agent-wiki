# Broca's Area

## Summary

Broca's area is a region in the left lateral frontal cortex (inferior left frontal gyrus, Brodmann's areas 44 and 45) involved in speech production and grammatical processing. Damage to Broca's area and surrounding structures produces Broca aphasia, characterized by nonfluent, effortful speech, intact comprehension for simple sentences, and difficulty with complex grammatical structures.

## Key facts

- Broca's area is located in the inferior left frontal gyrus (Brodmann's areas 44 and 45) and is involved in speech production and grammatical processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1416]
- Broca aphasia results from damage to Broca's area, surrounding frontal fields, underlying white matter, insula, basal ganglia, and a small portion of the anterior superior temporal gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1416]
- Broca's area is part of a neural network involved in both the assembly of phonemes into words and the assembly of words into sentences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1416-1417]
- In 1861, Paul Broca demonstrated that damage to the lower portion of the left frontal cortex could produce a profound disturbance of the ability to speak, calling this 'aphemia' to emphasize its motoric character. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p304]
- Broca's area can roughly be identified with the ventral premotor and prefrontal areas and is adjacent to the mouth-tongue-larynx region of the motor cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p305]
- Broca's area is not a true language area but rather an area that language most intensely utilizes due to its location adjacent to motor cortex for vocalization. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p311]
