# Thermoreceptors

## Summary

Thermoreceptors detect changes in skin temperature through TRP ion channels activated at specific temperature thresholds. Six types cover cold to hot ranges: TRPA1 (<17°C), TRPM8 (8-28°C), TRPV4 (>27°C), TRPV3 (>31-39°C), TRPV1 (>43°C), and TRPV2 (>52°C).

## Key facts

- TRP ion channels are membrane proteins with six transmembrane domains that show sharp increases in cation conductance when their thermal threshold is exceeded. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p537]
- Normal skin temperature is approximately 32°C (90°F); humans are normally unaware of slow temperature changes within the 31°-36°C range. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p537]
- Cold receptors (TRPM8) are approximately 100 times more sensitive to sudden drops in skin temperature than gradual changes, allowing detection of distant drafts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p537]
- TRPV1 and TRPV2 receptors respond to temperatures exceeding 45°C and mediate sensations of burning pain through heat nociceptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p537]
- TRP receptors also bind chemical ligands: capsaicin activates TRPV1 (producing burning sensation from chili peppers), menthol activates TRPM8 (producing cooling sensation). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p537]

## Inferences

- Inference: The low threshold for detecting sudden warming (about 0.1°C) compared to the high threshold for detecting sudden cooling suggests asymmetric neural processing of thermal stimuli.
