# Evenly Suspended Attention

## Summary

Evenly suspended attention is Freud's recommended attitude for the psychoanalyst during sessions. The analyst should maintain equal notice to all material without consciously directing attention or selecting from what is heard, thereby avoiding the distortion that comes from following expectations or inclinations. This is the necessary counterpart to the fundamental rule demanded of patients. Freud described it as turning one's unconscious 'like a receptive organ towards the transmitting unconscious of the patient,' comparable to a telephone receiver converting electrical oscillations back into sound waves.

## Key facts

- Evenly suspended attention rejects the use of special expedients such as taking notes, which introduces detrimental selection and ties up mental activity better used in interpreting. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3826]
- Directing attention deliberately leads to selecting material according to expectations or inclinations, risking finding only what one already knows or falsifying perception. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3826]
- The rule for the doctor is to 'withhold all conscious influences from his capacity to attend, and give himself over completely to his unconscious memory.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3826]
