# The Left Hemisphere as Sleepwalker

## Summary

McGilchrist argues that the popular assumption that the right hemisphere might be like a zombie or sleepwalker is inverted. The zombie state—characterised by dissociation, amnesia, identity disturbances, depersonalisation and derealisation—is actually associated with right-hemisphere deficit. In fact, hypnosis (a dissociative state) involves left-hemisphere predominance, not right. The left hemisphere, with its denial, conformism, and willingness to disregard evidence, better fits the description of a sleepwalker.

## Key facts

- The dissociative state is characterised by fragmentation of experience, amnesia for autobiographical information, identity disturbances, depersonalisation and derealisation, which are associated with right-hemisphere deficit. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p331]
- Subjects with right-hemisphere damage report becoming 'insensible automata, puppets, or mere spectators, devoid of feelings and cut off from the surrounding world'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p331]
- Hypnosis involves left-sided activation, not right-hemisphere release as commonly supposed, with enhanced focal concentration and suspension of peripheral awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p332]
- Ramachandran found the left hemisphere to be 'a conformist, largely indifferent to discrepancies, whereas the right hemisphere is the opposite: highly sensitive to perturbation'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p330]

## Inferences

- Inference: the story of the Western world is one of increasing left-hemisphere domination, we would expect not insight but 'a sort of insouciant optimism, the sleepwalker whistling a happy tune as he ambles towards the abyss'.
