# Hysterical Symbol Formation

## Summary

Hysterical symbol formation is a pathological displacement process where an innocent idea (A) takes the place of a repressed idea (B), acquiring its affective significance. Unlike normal symbol formation where the symbol's importance is consciously recognized (as when a soldier sacrifices himself for his flag), the hysteric is unaware that weeping at A is due to the association A–B, and B plays no part in conscious psychical life. The symbol entirely replaces the thing. This process involves primary processes of Qἠ displacement.

## Key facts

- Normal symbol formation: a soldier sacrifices himself for a flag because it has become the symbol of his fatherland, and no-one thinks that neurotic. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p350]
- In hysterical symbol formation, the hysteric who weeps at A is quite unaware that he is doing so on account of the association A–B, and B itself plays no part at all in his psychical life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p350]
- The pathological process is one of displacement, such as occurs in dreams—a primary process. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p351]
- Whatever should evoke B from outside or by association is instead replaced by A entering consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p350]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hysterical symbol formation represents a failure of secondary process binding, where primary displacement takes over
- Inference: The displacement is driven by the ego's defensive attempt to avoid the unpleasure associated with B, but achieves only partial success through the compromise of symbol formation
