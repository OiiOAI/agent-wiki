# Empedocles' Dog

## Summary

Empedocles' Dog is a thought experiment from Aristotle's Magna Moralia about why a dog always sleeps on the same tile — there must be some likeness between the dog and that tile. Taleb extends this to argue that surviving human technologies (writing, reading) are like the tile to the dog — a match with deep human nature. If something makes no sense to modern rationalists yet has survived millennia, it will likely outlast those calling for its demise.

## Key facts

- Empedocles explained a dog's preference for one tile by likeness — a natural, biological match confirmed by long recurrent frequentation. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p419]
- Surviving technologies correspond to something deep in human nature — they are the tile to the dog, not rationally designed but naturally selected. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p419]
- If something irrational has been around for a very long time, it will stick around much longer than those who call for its demise. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p420]

## Inferences

- Inference: This connects to via negativa and the Lindy Effect — long survival is evidence of fit with human nature that rational analysis cannot capture. Time reveals secrets that only practice can reveal.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The Magna Moralia attribution to Aristotle is disputed, and the story may be doubly apocryphal.
