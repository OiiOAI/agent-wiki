# Parenthood and Eroticism

## Summary

The transition to parenthood often threatens the erotic connection between partners. Eroticism thrives on unpredictability, spontaneity, and risk—qualities that clash with the routine, predictability, and responsibility required for childrearing. Children's needs are prioritized, leaving couples with less time, energy, money, and privacy. Additionally, American culture tends to desexualize motherhood, leaving mothers sexually invisible. The parent who becomes primary caregiver often undergoes total immersion in children's rhythms, losing erotic self. The challenge is for couples to carve out erotic territory for themselves within family life, maintaining intentional space for desire.

- Eroticism resides in unpredictability, spontaneity, and risk, while family life flourishes in predictability, routine, and regularity. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p117]
- Many couples trace the demise of their erotic life back to the arrival of the first child. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p115]
- The desexualization of the mother is a mainstay of traditionally patriarchal cultures, contributing to sexual invisibility of American mothers. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p130]
