# Self-Organization

## Summary

Self-organization is the capacity of a system to make its own structure more complex through learning, diversifying, and evolving. It is the ability of nature to have diversified millions of species out of a puddle of organic chemicals, or of a society to develop assembly plants and communication networks. Even complex forms of self-organization may arise from relatively simple organizing rules.

## Key facts

- Self-organization is the ability of a system to structure itself, create new structure, learn, diversify, and complexify. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p104]
- Self-organization produces heterogeneity and unpredictability; it requires freedom, experimentation, and a certain amount of disorder. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p104]
- Even complex forms of self-organization may arise from relatively simple organizing rules. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p104]
- Self-organization is such a basic property of living systems that even the most overbearing power structure can never fully kill it. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p103]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Koch snowflake and fractal geometry demonstrate that elaborate structures can be generated by relatively simple rules, suggesting the underlying principle behind biological self-organization.
