# Mechanism Design

## Summary

Mechanism design (sometimes called reverse game theory) asks: given desired behaviors, what rules will produce them? Rather than analyzing what happens under given rules, it designs rules to achieve specific outcomes. The field reveals counterintuitive insights—for instance, that worsening every outcome (death, taxes) can make everyone better off by shifting the equilibrium. Key applications include designing auctions, contracts, and regulations where participants' self-interest aligns with desired social outcomes.

## Key facts

- Mechanism design asks: what rules will give us the behavior we want to see, working backwards from desired outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L2810-L2812]
- The revelation principle by Roger Myerson proves that any game requiring strategically masking the truth can be transformed into a game requiring simple honesty. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L3085-L3087]
- The Vickrey auction is strategy-proof (truthful)—honesty is the dominant strategy with no need for recursive thinking. [raw/books/strategy/Algorithms_to_Live_By.epub#L3025-L3027]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mechanism design shows that seemingly bad rules (death threats, taxes) can paradoxically improve outcomes by changing the payoff structure to make cooperation rational.
