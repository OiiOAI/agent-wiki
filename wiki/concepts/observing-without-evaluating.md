# Observing Without Evaluating

## Summary

Observing without evaluating is the first component of NVC, requiring clear articulation of what is being seen, heard, or touched that affects well-being, without mixing in any evaluation. When observation is combined with evaluation, others are likely to hear criticism and resist the intended message. NVC discourages static generalizations; instead, evaluations should be based on observations specific to time and context.

## Key facts

- When we combine observation with evaluation, people are apt to hear criticism and thus resist whatever we are saying. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p52]
- Indian philosopher J. Krishnamurti remarked that observing without evaluating is the highest form of human intelligence. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p55]
- Semanticist Wendell Johnson pointed out that using static language to express a reality that is ever-changing creates many problems for ourselves. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p52]
- The words 'always,' 'never,' 'ever,' and 'whenever' express observations when used in specific, concrete contexts, but function as evaluations when used as exaggerations. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p57]
- Static evaluations like 'You are always busy' or 'She is never there when she's needed' often provoke defensiveness rather than compassion. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p57]
- Even positive or neutral labels like 'cook' limit perception of the totality of another person's being. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p54]
