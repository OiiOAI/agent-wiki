# Amygdala and Internal State Processing

## Summary

The amygdala plays critical roles in processing internal states including pain, hunger, thirst, and satiety. Henry's bilateral amygdala removal resulted in impaired perception of pain, inability to sense hunger or thirst, and failure to express satiety, demonstrating that the amygdala is essential for appreciating internal bodily states.

## Key facts

- Henry's capacity for pain detection was disproportionately compromised; he never labeled any heat stimulus as painful, even at maximum intensity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p211]
- Other amnesic patients with intact amygdalae showed normal pain perception, indicating the deficit was due to amygdala lesions, not amnesia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p212]
- Henry consistently rated his hunger as 50 on a 0-100 scale regardless of whether he was about to eat or had just finished eating. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p211]
- Henry ate a second identical dinner without recognizing he had just finished eating, demonstrating lack of satiety signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p211]

## Inferences

- Inference: The bilateral resection of Henry's amygdala accounted for his poor appreciation of internal states, independent of his memory deficit.
