# Model-Based Data Analysis

## Summary

Model-based data analysis uses Active Inference formulations to analyze behavioral data from experiments, enabling recovery of the parameters of a subject's generative model (the subjective model) through meta-Bayesian inference. This approach supports computational phenotyping and hypothesis testing about the models brains use.

## Key facts

- The central goal is to recover the parameters of the generative model that a subject's brain uses to produce behavior—the subjective model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p180]
- The objective model (scientist's model of how subjective models produce behavior) is inverted based on observed behavior to draw inferences about subjective model parameters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p180]
- Meta-Bayesian inference involves two inference processes: creatures drawing inferences about their world, and scientists observing behavior to infer the generative model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p181]
- Two related reasons for fitting computational models to behavior are parameter estimation and model comparison. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p180]
