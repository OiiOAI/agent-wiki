# Manichaeism (Political)

## Summary

Manichaeism is a metaphorical framework Haidt uses to describe the tendency of American politics to frame political opponents as forces of absolute good versus absolute evil. Drawing on the third-century Persian religion of Mani, Haidt argues that American political discourse has become increasingly Manichaean since the early 1990s. In this framework, compromise is seen as sin, and bipartisan cooperation becomes difficult. Haidt suggests that structural changes—not pledges of civility—are needed to address this problem.

## Key facts

- America's political class has become far more Manichaean since the early 1990s, first in Washington and then in many state capitals. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p334]
- If you think about politics in a Manichaean way, then compromise is a sin—God and the devil don't issue many bipartisan proclamations, and neither should you. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p334]
- Pledges to be more civil will not work—riders can sign as many pledges as they please, but the pledges are not binding for elephants. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p334]
