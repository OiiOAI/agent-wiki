# Masochism (Economic Problem of)

## Summary

Masochism, particularly in its economic problem, refers to the turning of destructive drives against the ego itself. Freud distinguishes between moral masochism (suffering that gives unconscious pleasure), feminine masochism, and erotogenic masochism. The economic problem concerns how the death drive, when not adequately fused with Eros, turns inward against the ego, creating masochistic phenomena.

## Key facts

- The superego in melancholia has taken possession of the whole of the sadism available in the person concerned; what is holding sway in the superego is a pure culture of the death drive. - In moral masochism the ego does not get the libidinal satisfaction to which it lays claim, but the sense of guilt gives it a substitute for it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5937]
- The superego develops from identifications that are in the nature of desexualization; when such transformation takes place, drive defusion occurs and the erotic component no longer binds the destructiveness. - The ego's struggle against the libido exposes it to the danger of maltreatment and death through the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5941]
