# Adolescent Frontal Cortex Maturation

## Summary

The frontal cortex is the last brain region to fully mature (in terms of synapse number, myelination, and metabolism), not going fully online until the mid-twenties. During adolescence, gray matter volume actually decreases as less optimal synapses are pruned away through competitive elimination—maturation is about a more efficient brain, not more brain. This delayed maturation explains adolescent traits: emotional intensity, novelty seeking, risk taking, peer influence sensitivity, and the peak in violence during late adolescence and early adulthood.

## Inferences

- Inference: The frontal cortex may have evolved to be the last region to mature because it is the part most shaped by experience rather than genes—delayed maturation allows maximum environmental sculpting of the brain region most essential for complex social functioning
- Inference: The finding that London taxi drivers have enlarged posterior hippocampi (used for spatial navigation) demonstrates that brain regions can expand with sustained use, supporting experience-dependent plasticity

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact reasons for the evolutionary selection of delayed frontal cortex maturation remain speculative
