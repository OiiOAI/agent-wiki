# Four-Step Recipe for Active Inference Models

## Summary

The four-step recipe for designing Active Inference models involves: (1) identifying system boundaries using Markov blankets, (2) choosing the appropriate form for the generative model (discrete vs continuous, shallow vs hierarchical, temporal depth), (3) setting up model variables and priors, and (4) setting up the generative process.

## Key facts

- Step 1: Identify system boundaries using Markov blankets—determining what counts as an agent, external environment, and the interface between them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p107]
- Step 2: Choose the form of the generative model—whether discrete or continuous variables, shallow or hierarchical, and whether temporal depth for planning is needed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p110]
- Step 3: Set up variables and priors—which variables to include and which parts are fixed versus learned. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p113]
- Step 4: Set up the generative process—the dynamics external to the agent that determines observations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p119]
