# Competitive Advantage

## Summary

Competitive advantage exists when a business can produce at lower cost than competitors, deliver more perceived value, or both. A competitive advantage becomes interesting when one has insights into ways to increase its value. Wealth increases when competitive advantage increases or when demand for underlying resources increases. Four fronts for progress are: deepening advantages, broadening extent, creating higher demand, and strengthening isolating mechanisms.

## Key facts

- If your business can produce at a lower cost than competitors or deliver more perceived value you have a competitive advantage. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p64]
- A competitive advantage is interesting when one has insights into ways to increase its value—things you can do on your own to increase its value. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p66]
- Wealth increases when competitive advantage increases or when the demand for resources underlying it increases—not by simply having or buying a competitive advantage. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p67]
- Isolating mechanisms inhibit competitors from duplicating your product or the resources underlying your competitive advantage—examples include patents, reputations, network effects, and tacit knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p65]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bay's case demonstrates that having a competitive advantage does not automatically create wealth if the advantage is static—the silver machine problem shows that an advantage that cannot be enhanced yields only ordinary returns on investment.
