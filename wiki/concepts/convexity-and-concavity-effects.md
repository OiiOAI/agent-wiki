# Convexity and Concavity Effects

## Summary

Mathematical properties describing the curvature of responses to interventions or shocks. Convexity refers to responses that accelerate (benefits exceed costs or harms exceed benefits as exposure increases), while concavity describes responses that decelerate. These properties determine fragility and antifragility—concave responses are fragile to volatility while convex responses are antifragile. Book V of Antifragile focuses on these concepts as the 'philosopher's stone' of the book.

## Key facts

- Everything nonlinear in response is either fragile or antifragile to a certain source of randomness—nonlinearity is central to the concept of antifragility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p29]
- Convexity biases and Jensen's inequality show that under uncertainty, the average of nonlinear functions differs from the function of averages, leading to systematic errors in planning. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p16]
- In complex systems, when you double the dose of, say, a medication, you don't get twice the initial effect but rather a lot more or a lot less—demonstrating nonlinear response. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p23]
- Mathematically, concavity is convexity with a negative sign in front of it—sometimes called anticonvexity—mirroring the relationship between fragility and antifragility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p52]
- Book V (The Nonlinear and the Nonlinear) constitutes the central technical section—the 'plumbing' of the book—mapping fragility as nonlinearity through convexity effects. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p37]

## Inferences

- Inference: Convexity and concavity effects provide the mathematical foundation for antifragility theory, explaining why some systems benefit from volatility while others are harmed.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether convexity effects can be reliably measured in practice for complex real-world systems.
