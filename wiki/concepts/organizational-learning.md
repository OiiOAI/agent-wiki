# Organizational Learning

## Summary

Organizational learning theory emerged from systems thinking and cybernetics, applying insights about adaptation, feedback, and knowledge creation to organizations. Single-loop learning involves adjusting actions based on feedback while maintaining existing assumptions; double-loop learning also questions and modifies underlying mental models and norms. Key concepts include cognitive maps (mental representations guiding action), experiential learning cycles, and the distinction between mechanistic/organic organizational metaphors. Organizations viewed as organisms or learning brains require loose structures, broad information sources, and willingness to embrace contradictions for effective learning.

## Key facts

- Single-loop learning adjusts actions based on feedback; double-loop learning (or generative learning) also modifies underlying mental models and assumptions, providing wider choices and new decision rules. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p120]
- Cognitive maps are 'internal images or other mental representations of spatial relationships (or other kinds of knowledge), which allow one to choose alternative paths towards one's goals,' formed through socialization, culture, and experience. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p120]
- Garwin defined a learning organization as 'an organization skilled at creating, acquiring, interpreting, transferring, and retaining knowledge, and at purposefully modifying its behavior to reflect new knowledge and insights.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p125]
- Organizational learning facilitators include: broad base of contributors; diverse perspectives; quick feedback; brainstorming forums; dialectic debate; willingness to try new approaches even with failures. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p125]
- Boyd's OODA loop includes double feedback loops that allow monitoring of goal relevance, response repertoire, and institutional memory adequacy—key features of organizational learning. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p124]
- Bennis noted that organizational effectiveness 'has to be made…on the basis of the processes through which the organization approaches problems. The measure for health is flexibility, the freedom to learn through experience.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p125]
