# Implicit Association Test

## Summary

The Implicit Association Test (IAT) is a psychological measure that reveals unconscious biases by measuring response times when pairing concepts. For example, people unconsciously pause when asked to pair positive words with images of out-group members or negative words with in-group members. The test demonstrates automatic, unconscious prejudice without conscious awareness or intent.

## Key facts

- The IAT measures unconscious bias by requiring subjects to categorize faces and words quickly, with response time delays revealing implicit associations. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p403]
- The test is automatic—subjects are not consciously prejudiced but are processing words and images, unconsciously pausing when linking negative concepts with in-groups. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p403]
- The IAT assesses whether people make subconscious associations between concepts by measuring the time it takes to sort images and concepts; people who associate 'white' with 'good' and 'Black' with 'bad' are slower when asked to sort the opposite associations. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p17]
- Langer and Ngnoumen's study asked participants to generate novel psychological categories (e.g., how social each person seemed, whether smiling) for sorting photos, reducing implicit racial bias on the IAT by half. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p17-18]
- After the mindful labeling intervention, white participants spent much more time listening to stories of people who were not like them, showing increased empathy. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p18]
