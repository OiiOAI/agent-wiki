# Omnipotence of Wishes

## Summary

Obsessional neurotics characteristically ascribe omnipotence to their thoughts, feelings, and wishes, whether good or evil. Freud encountered this conviction in multiple patients and argues it is not delusional but rather reflects the overwhelming nature of their unconscious love and hatred. The patient's hatred was in truth overpowering—it was precisely these affects that created the obsessional thoughts. Because a large part of their internal, mental effects escaped conscious knowledge, patients overestimate the effects of their hostile feelings upon the external world. Freud later recognized this omnipotence of wishes as an essential element in the mental life of primitive people, discussed in Totem and Taboo (1912-13a).

## Inferences

- Inference: The belief in omnipotence reflects the actual overwhelming intensity of unconscious affects that created the obsessional thoughts, rather than a frank megalomania.
