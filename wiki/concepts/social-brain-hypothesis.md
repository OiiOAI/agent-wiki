# Social Brain Hypothesis

## Summary

The social brain hypothesis stipulates that the need to optimize behavior within complex social environments largely constrained the evolution of the primate brain. First outlined by Alison Jolly in lemurs and popularized by Humphrey, Byrne and Whiten, and Dunbar, the theory provides correlational evidence for coevolution of social complexity and brain development markers.

## Key facts

- The social brain hypothesis stipulates that the need to optimize behavior within complex social environments largely constrained the evolution of the primate brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p206]
- The theory was first outlined in the pioneering study of Alison Jolly in lemurs, and popularized by Humphrey, Byrne and Whiten, and Dunbar. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p206]
- In evolved animals, other group members constitute stochastically behaving entities guided by hidden mental states, requiring very elaborated computations for predicting their actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p206]
- The possibility that 'social life provided the evolutionary context of primate intelligence' is key to understanding the importance of social dominance for domain-general human cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p206]
- Nicholas Humphrey (1976) proposed that higher intellectual faculties of primates evolved as an adaptation to complexities of social living. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p100]
- Richard Byrne and Andrew Whiten named this the Machiavellian intelligence hypothesis, suggesting an 'arms race' of increasing social skill selected for brain expansion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p100]
- Dunbar concluded that ecological factors like predator risk drove the need for larger social groups, and the complexities of these groups then drove brain size expansion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p102]
- Five aspects of social behavior correlate with neocortex size: social group size, grooming clique size, male mating strategy skill, tactical deception frequency, and social play frequency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p102]
