# Defensive Behavior Trio

## Summary

The defensive behavior trio—freezing, fleeing, and fighting—represents the classic antipredation options available to animals. Freezing is typically the first response when a threat is detected, as it reduces detection by predators. Fleeing is employed when the threat is closer, and fighting is used as a last resort when escape is not possible.

## Key facts

- The classic antipredation options are freezing, fleeing, and fighting—often summarized as the 'freeze-flight-fight' response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p88]
- Freezing is the optimal default response when a predator is detected at moderate distances because motion is an important cue used by predators. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p88]
- According to predatory imminence theory, freezing has the lowest threshold and is activated first, then fleeing or fighting as threat proximity increases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p97]
- Other defensive options include playing dead (tonic immobility), defensive burying, making loud noises, and relying on safety in numbers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p89]
