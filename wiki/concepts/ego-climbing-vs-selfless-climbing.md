# Ego-Climbing vs. Selfless Climbing

## Summary

A key metaphor in the book contrasting two approaches to any endeavor, particularly climbing mountains (and by extension, living life and pursuing understanding). Ego-climbing involves trying to prove oneself, treating the goal as external and distant, while selfless climbing involves complete presence to the immediate moment, treating each step as an end in itself. The metaphor illustrates Phædrus's failure at a Himalayan pilgrimage: he had greater physical strength but lacked the spiritual submission that enabled other pilgrims to reach Mount Kailas.

## Key facts

- The narrator states: 'Mountains should be climbed with as little effort as possible and without desire. The reality of your own nature should determine the speed.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p173]
- Ego-climbing is described as being 'like an instrument that's out of adjustment. He puts his foot down an instant too soon or too late. He's likely to miss a beautiful passage of sunlight through the trees.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p179]
- Phædrus's account of failing the Mount Kailas pilgrimage states he 'had the physical strength but that physical strength wasn't enough' and was 'trying to use the mountain for his own purposes.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p178]
- The narrator observes that Chris's struggle on the mountain is due to ego-climbing: 'When you try to climb a mountain to prove how big you are, you almost never make it.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: The metaphor extends to teaching: Phædrus's methods were effective partly because they encouraged students toward selfless engagement with Quality rather than grade-seeking.
