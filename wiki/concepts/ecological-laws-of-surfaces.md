# Ecological Laws of Surfaces

## Summary

The ecological laws of surfaces are a set of principles describing the characteristic properties of surfaces that are perceptually relevant. These laws replace purely geometrical descriptions with properties that have behavioral significance. They include: persistence and layout, resistance to deformation, resistance to disintegration, characteristic texture (pigment and layout), characteristic shape, illumination conditions, absorption characteristics, reflectance, and spectral reflectance (color).

## Key facts

- All persisting substances have surfaces, and all surfaces have a layout. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p50]
- Any surface has resistance to deformation depending on the viscosity of the substance. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p50]
- Any surface has characteristic texture depending on its composition, generally having both a layout texture and a pigment texture. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p50]
- A surface has characteristic spectral reflectance distribution of different wavelengths, which constitutes different colors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p50]
- A surface may be strongly or weakly illuminated, in light or in shade, and illuminated surfaces may absorb either much or little of the illumination falling on them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p50]

## Inferences

- Inference: These laws provide the foundation for understanding how surfaces can be perceived—their properties specify what substances are made of and how they can be interacted with.
