# Asymmetry of Means

## Summary

The left hemisphere point of view inevitably dominates because it is most accessible—closest to the self-aware, self-inspecting intellect. The means of argument (language, logic, linearity) are all ultimately under left-hemisphere control. The left hemisphere has most syntax and most of the lexicon, making it 'very powerful in constructing an argument' while the right hemisphere 'hasn't got a voice anyway'.

## Key facts

- The left hemisphere dominates because it is most accessible: closest to the self-aware, self-inspecting intellect. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p321]
- The means of argument—language, logic, and linearity—are all ultimately under left-hemisphere control. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p321]
- The left hemisphere has most syntax and most of the lexicon, making it 'very powerful in constructing an argument' while the right hemisphere 'hasn't got a voice anyway'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p322]
- The left hemisphere builds systems over time, elaborating its own workings into systematic thought which gives it permanence and solidity, and these have become instantiated in the external world around us. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p321]

## Inferences

- Inference: The predominance of the left hemisphere may result from a deficit in the right hemisphere.
