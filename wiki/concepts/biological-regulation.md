# Biological regulation

## Summary

Biological regulation refers to the collection of biological processes that maintain the integrity of cells and tissues throughout an organism's structure for survival. It depends on innate neural circuits in the brain stem and hypothalamus that control reflexes, drives, and instincts. These circuits contain dispositional representations that, when activated, set in motion responses for maintaining respiration, feeding, and other essential functions.

## Key facts

- Biological regulation depends on a collection of processes that maintain the integrity of cells and tissues throughout the organism's structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p134]
- The innate neural patterns most critical for survival are maintained in circuits of the brain stem and hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p138]
- The hypothalamus is a key player in regulation of endocrine glands and immune system function, maintaining the internal milieu within suitable ranges for survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p138]
- Biological regulation, survival, and mind are intimately interwoven in biological tissue using chemical and electrical signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p143]
