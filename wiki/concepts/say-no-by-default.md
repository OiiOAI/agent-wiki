# Say No by Default

## Summary

The concept of 'saying no by default' advocates starting with a presumption against new requests, features, or changes rather than automatically accommodating them. The authors argue that yes is easy but leads to accumulated commitments that dilute focus, while no maintains priorities and keeps products coherent. They recommend using no to get priorities straight and cite ING Direct as an example of a successful bank that built its growth by refusing customer requests for credit cards, online brokerage, and large deposits.

## Key facts

- The authors recommend starting with no rather than yes because yes creates accumulated commitments while no maintains priorities. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p113]
- ING Direct built the fastest-growing bank in America by saying no to customer requests for credit cards, online brokerage, and accounts with million-dollar deposits. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p113]
- The authors argue that making a few vocal customers happy at the expense of product coherence is not worth it. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p113]
- When saying no, the authors recommend being polite, explaining your point of view, and potentially recommending a competitor if you think there's a better solution. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p114]

## Inferences

- Inference: Consistent refusal of requests that don't fit the core product can actually strengthen brand identity and attract customers who share those values.
