# Winner's Curse

## Summary

The Winner's Curse is the tendency for the winner of an auction to pay more than the true value of the item, making them the economic loser. This occurs because the bidder with the highest estimate often has the least information or is most emotionally invested in winning.

## Key facts

- The winner's curse suggests the winner of an auction often turns out to be the loser; companies that emerged as winning bidders from Texas oilfield auctions systematically paid too much. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p86]
- More than half of all acquisitions destroy value according to a McKinsey study, demonstrating the winner's curse in corporate mergers and acquisitions. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p86]
- Warren Buffett's advice about auctions: 'Don't go.' If unavoidable, set a maximum price and deduct 20% to offset the winner's curse. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p87]
