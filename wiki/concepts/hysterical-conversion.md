# Hysterical Conversion

## Summary

Hysterical conversion is the psychical mechanism by which an affective idea or excitation is transformed into somatic phenomena, such as pain, paralysis, or other physical symptoms. The theory posits that when an incompatible idea cannot be consciously accepted, the associated psychical energy is converted into physical symptoms, thereby sparing the patient mental pain while producing genuine bodily dysfunction.

## Key facts

- Conversion transforms affective excitation into somatic phenomena, allowing the patient to escape an intolerable mental condition at the cost of physical illness and psychical abnormality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p148]
- The hysterical symptom is not created by the neurosis but merely used, increased, and maintained by it; genuine organically-founded pain often serves as the basis for hysterical conversion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p155]
- Conversion can result equally from fresh excitations and from recollected ones, explaining why symptoms may appear only after memory reproduction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p174]
- The determining process (recollection of trauma) continues to operate for years as a directly releasing cause, not indirectly through intermediate causal links. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p221]

## Inferences

- Inference: Conversion is facilitated by simultaneity between somatic sensation and psychical affect, by verbal expressions that enable symbolization, and by weakened resistances in particular neural pathways due to constitutional, developmental, or illness-related factors.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neurological mechanism by which conversion is brought about remains unexplained; Breuer acknowledges he can give no hint of how conversion is physiologically achieved.
