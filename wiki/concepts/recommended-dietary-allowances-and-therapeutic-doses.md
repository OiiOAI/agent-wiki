# Recommended Dietary Allowances and Therapeutic Doses

## Summary

RDAs were developed to identify minimum intake to prevent nutritional deficiency diseases, set for 97.5 percent of healthy individuals. They do not identify what is required for optimal brain function. Research suggests therapeutic doses often need to exceed RDAs for mental health benefits, but remain below Tolerable Upper Intake Levels.

## Key facts

- RDAs are defined as intake sufficient for 97.5 percent of healthy individuals to prevent deficiency diseases. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p254]
- RDAs identify minimum intake to avoid scurvy (10mg vitamin C) but not optimal brain functioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p254]
- The Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) is often 10 times higher than RDA, providing a therapeutic window. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p255]
- RDAs and ULs are established based on single-nutrient intake and may be less relevant when nutrients are consumed in combination. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: The therapeutic window between RDA and UL suggests most people can safely supplement above minimum requirements.
