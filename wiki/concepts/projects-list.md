# Projects List

## Summary

A Projects list is an inventory of all desired outcomes requiring more than one action step to complete. This list serves as a placeholder for all open loops and the key driver for weekly review of progress.

## Key facts

- A project is defined as any outcome you are committed to achieving that will take more than one action step to complete. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p187]
- Right now you probably have between thirty and a hundred projects. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p187]
- The Projects list ensures placeholders exist for all open loops, not to reflect priorities. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p187]
- Creating and maintaining one list of all projects can be a profound experience as you discover how many commitments you have. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p210]
- Definition: A project is any desired outcome requiring more than one action to achieve. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p212]
- The Projects list serves as a master inventory rather than a daily prioritizing guideline. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p216]
- The real value of the Projects list lies in the complete review it provides at least once a week. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p212]
- Common subsorts include Personal/Professional division, Delegated Projects, and Specific Types of Projects. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p217]

## Inferences
