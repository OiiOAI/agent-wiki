# Purposive Ideas in Dream Interpretation

## Summary

Freud defends his method of dream interpretation against objections that following chains of free association is arbitrary. He argues that when conscious purposive ideas are abandoned, unknown (unconscious) purposive ideas take charge and determine the course of involuntary ideas. The apparent 'superficial' associations in dream interpretation are substitutes by displacement for suppressed deeper connections. Superficial associations replace deep ones when the censorship makes normal connecting paths impassable, analogous to traffic using inconvenient footpaths when main roads are blocked.

## Key facts

- When we abandon purposive ideas, unknown purposive ideas take charge and thereafter determine the course of the involuntary ideas. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1769]
- The real reason for the prevalence of superficial associations is not the abandonment of purposive ideas but the pressure of the censorship. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1771]
- Whenever one psychical element is linked with another by an objectionable or superficial association, there is also a legitimate and deeper link between them which is subjected to the resistance of the censorship. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1771]

## Inferences

- Inference: The technique of psychoanalysis relies on these two theorems: that unconscious purposive ideas govern associations when conscious purposive ideas are abandoned, and that superficial associations are substitutes for suppressed deeper ones. Patients cannot truly abandon purposive ideas related to their treatment.
