# Dynamic Co-Emergence

## Summary

Dynamic co-emergence is a conception of emergence in which part and whole co-emerge and mutually specify each other. It involves three propositions: (1) nonlinear dynamics generate emergent processes, (2) global-to-local (downward) determinative influence exists from emergent processes to component dynamics, and (3) emergent processes exhibit 'relational holism' not exhaustively determined by intrinsic properties of components. This view contrasts with classical reductionism and avoids the incoherence Kim finds in reflexive downward causation by conceptualizing influence not as efficient causation but as global-to-local structuring influence.

## Key facts

- Dynamic co-emergence means that part and whole co-emerge and mutually specify each other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p414]
- Emergent processes occur in networks whose coupled elements have nonlinear interactions, which are nonadditive or nonproportional. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p403]
- In complex systems, global patterns both arise from local interactions and govern or constrain those interactions through circular or reciprocal causality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p407]
- Downward causation corresponds to the second-order contextual constraint of a system's organization restricting the degrees of freedom of the system's components. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p409]
