# Weak Associations

## Summary

Weak associations refer to loosely or indirectly related concepts in memory networks. Research demonstrates that during REM sleep, the brain preferentially activates weak associations rather than strong ones, a finding central to the NEXTUP theory explaining dream function, content, and bizarreness.

## Key facts

- During REM sleep, the brain preferentially activates weak associations rather than strong ones, as shown by semantic priming studies where weak primes produced eight times more priming. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p112]
- NEXTUP proposes that the brain starts with a new memory from the day and searches for weakly associated memories from any time in the dreamer's past. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p109]
- Weak associations being incorporated into dream narratives explains why so many dreams lack transparent connections to daily events and why dream content seems bizarre. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p112]
- The brain searches more widely during dreaming than during wakefulness, going through less obvious associations and exploring potentially useful hidden connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p112]
