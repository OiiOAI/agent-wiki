# Decision-Making Exercises

## Summary

Decision-Making Exercises (DMXs) are paper-and-pencil scenario-based training tools designed to build intuitive decision-making skills. They present participants with realistic dilemmas containing uncertainty and challenge them to develop plans of action. DMXs serve as simulated experiences that help build the pattern recognition abilities essential to intuition, particularly when real-life practice opportunities are scarce.

## Key facts

- Decision-Making Exercises are simple thought exercises, usually involving paper-and-pencil scenarios, that capture the essence of typical difficult decisions. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p68]
- Well-designed DMXs can be surprisingly effective at capturing the essence of tough decisions without the costs of more complicated simulations. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p68]
- DMXs can be used to reveal the limits of mental models, build familiarity within teams, fill gaps in experience base, teach ways to handle uncertainty, and identify leverage points for constructing new options. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p75]
- DMXs are best played in small groups of six to eight people rather than in solitaire mode to create productive pressure and learning opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p77]
