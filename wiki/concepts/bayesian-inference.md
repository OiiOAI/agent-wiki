# Bayesian inference

## Summary

Bayesian inference is a mathematical framework for reasoning under uncertainty, formalized by Thomas Bayes. In Seth's account, the brain uses Bayesian inference to make optimal 'best guesses' about the causes of sensory signals by combining prior beliefs with incoming likelihoods to form posterior probabilities.

## Key facts

- Bayesian inference is a form of abductive reasoning — finding the best explanation for a set of observations when they are incomplete, uncertain, or ambiguous. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p100]
- Bayes' rule combines priors and likelihoods: the posterior is the prior multiplied by the likelihood, divided by a second prior on the data. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p101]
- Priors are probabilities of something being the case before new data arrives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p101]
- Likelihoods formalize reasoning from causes to effects: given a hypothesis, what is the probability of the observed data?. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p101]
- Posteriors represent updated beliefs after combining priors and likelihoods. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p101]
- The brain approximates Bayes' rule through prediction error minimization — minimizing prediction errors everywhere and all the time implements Bayesian inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]

## Inferences
