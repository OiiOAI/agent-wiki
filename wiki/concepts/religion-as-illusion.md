# Religion as Illusion

## Summary

Freud's psychoanalytic interpretation of religious belief as a collective human illusion. Religious doctrines are teachings that are not precipitates of experience or end results of thinking, but fulfillments of the oldest, strongest, and most urgent wishes of mankind. These include the need for a protective father (divine providence), justice (moral world order), and immortality (afterlife). Freud distinguishes this from mere error: unlike errors, illusions are characterized by wish-fulfillment as motivation. Religious doctrines cannot be proved or refuted, but they exercise the strongest influence on mankind through the force of these ancient wishes. Freud argues that religious commands should be based on rational social necessity rather than divine authority, as relying on God ultimately makes civilization vulnerable when belief declines.

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's analysis implies that as scientific knowledge increases and religious faith declines, civilization must develop secular foundations for its moral demands rather than relying on divine authority
- Inference: Freud's critique anticipates later debates about the relationship between religion and psychology, and his argument about the developmental character of religious belief influenced subsequent psychological study of religion.
