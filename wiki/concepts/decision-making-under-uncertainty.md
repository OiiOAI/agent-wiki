# Decision-Making Under Uncertainty

## Summary

Decision-making under uncertainty refers to choices where outcomes are not fully determined by the decision itself but also depend on chance elements beyond one's control. In Thinking in Bets, Duke contrasts poker (high uncertainty) with chess (no uncertainty) to illustrate how real-world decisions involve both skill and luck. The book argues that distinguishing between decision quality and outcome quality is essential for learning from experience and improving as a decision-maker.

## Key facts

- Duke argues that chess has no uncertainty while poker has great uncertainty, creating a barrier for chess players transitioning to poker. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Many world-class poker players are also world-class backgammon players (Chip Reese, Gus Hansen, Erik Seidel, Dan Harrington), likely due to shared uncertainty elements. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Poker players must navigate luck in the deal of cards; backgammon players must navigate luck in the roll of dice. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Duke cites research on problems with human data processing, including assuming causation when there is only correlation and cherry-picking data to confirm preferred narratives. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- The book examines bookmakers' odds-setting before Brexit and the confusion that follows when declaring someone 'right' or 'wrong' about predictions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Nate Silver's FiveThirtyEight gave approximately 60%–70% probability for a Clinton victory in 2016, leading to criticism when Trump won despite being the underdog. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
