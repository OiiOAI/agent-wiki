# The Apeiron

## Summary

The apeiron (Greek: ἄπειρον) is the 'unbounded' or 'indefinite' originary principle posited by Anaximander. It is undefined, unlimited, without beginning or end, and serves as an eternal source from which things arise and to which they return. Crucially, the apeiron must be able to contain opposing principles without their mutual annihilation—the only candidate capable of fulfilling this condition. McGilchrist interprets the apeiron as an insight into the right-hemisphere world.

## Key facts

- Anaximander posited the apeiron as the 'unbounded' or 'indefinite' originary principle from which all things arise and to which they return. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p373]
- The apeiron literally means undefined or unlimited, carrying the suggestion of something that cannot be qualified and must be approached apophatically. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p373]
- Opposing principles within the apeiron balance one another, and their giving and taking creates all things, 'paying retribution for their injustice according to the ordinance of time.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p373]
- McGilchrist sees the apeiron as an insight into the right-hemisphere world: the primacy of what is neither definite nor finite, and the nature of the arch as process rather than thing. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p373]
