# Process Over Product

## Summary

A philosophy advocating that creatives should share their day-to-day process—their methods, influences, works-in-progress, and behind-the-scenes work—rather than waiting to share only finished products. This openness creates a unique bond with audiences who want to see how creative work is made.

## Key facts

- Kleon distinguishes between 'the artwork' (finished piece) and 'the art work' (the day-to-day stuff behind the scenes). [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p36]
- Traditionally, artists are trained to regard their creative process as something to keep private, as articulated in Art and Fear by David Bayles and Ted Orland: 'To all viewers but yourself, what matters is the product: the finished artwork.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p36]
- The Internet enables artists to share whatever they want, whenever they want, at almost no cost, deciding how much of their process to reveal. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p39]
- Designers Dan Provost and Tom Gerhardt are quoted: 'People really do want to see how the sausage gets made. By putting things out there, consistently, you can form a relationship with your customers. It allows them to see the person behind the products.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p41]
- Kleon advocates becoming a 'documentarian' of your work: starting a work journal, keeping a scrapbook, taking photographs of work at different stages, shooting video of yourself working. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p44]
