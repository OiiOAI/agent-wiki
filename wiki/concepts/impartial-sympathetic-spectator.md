# Impartial Sympathetic Spectator

## Summary

The Impartial Sympathetic Spectator is a concept from Hume and Adam Smith used to define rightness: something is right if an ideally rational and impartial spectator would approve of it from a general point of view with all relevant knowledge. Rawls contrasts this with the contractarian approach, noting that while classical utilitarianism derives from this concept (the spectator's approvals follow the classical principle of utility), justice as fairness provides an alternative interpretation of impartiality from the standpoint of the litigants themselves.

## Key facts

- An impartial sympathetic spectator is a person who takes up a general perspective where his own interests are not at stake, possessing all requisite information and powers of reasoning, equally responsive to the desires and satisfactions of everyone affected. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p177]
- The classical view results in impersonality, the conflation of all desires into one system of desire; the one person of the classical doctrine is identical with the impartial sympathetic spectator. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p178]
- The fault of the utilitarian doctrine is that it mistakes impersonality for impartiality; impartiality should be defined from the standpoint of the litigants themselves who must choose principles in an original position of equality. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p180]
