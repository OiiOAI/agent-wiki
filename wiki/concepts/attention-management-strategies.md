# Attention management strategies

## Summary

Attention management strategies are central to understanding how experts respond to expected, unexpected, and novel events. Throughout skill acquisition, a human's expectations provide a framework for attention management. Early in training, there is relatively little information about the task and all events are new, showing marked inefficiencies. As skill develops, expectations become embodied in a mental model. Four changes relevant to attention management occur: automatic detection and correction of familiar events; anticipatory behaviors based on temporal associations; metaattention behaviors when expectations are violated; and knowledge-based behavior for novel complex situations requiring controlled processes.

- Detection of possible events can reach the level of automatic control and become strongly associated with corrective actions, executed before reaching conscious awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p325]
- Strong temporal association between event A and event B can lead an experienced operator to begin searching for event B the moment event A is detected, manifesting as anticipatory behaviors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p326]
- Controlled processes are invoked as metaattention behaviors when event A is associated with numerous dangerous, unpredictable events, leading to increased vigilance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p326]
- Attention management skills are most crucial when unexpected events exceed the capacity of automatic processes and controlled processes must quickly take over the task. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p326]
