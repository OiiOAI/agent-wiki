# Two Currents in Love

## Summary

Freud's theory that normal love requires fusion of an affectionate current (originating in childhood attachments to family) and a sensual current (emerging at puberty). Failure of these currents to combine produces psychical impotence and the need to debase sexual objects.

## Key facts

- The affectionate current is older, formed in early childhood based on self-preservative drive interests directed toward family members. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3613]
- The sensual current follows the earlier paths at puberty, cathexing objects on the model of infantile choices but encountering the incest barrier. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3614]
- Where they love they do not desire, and where they desire they cannot love—this division characterizes the behavior of those in whom the currents failed to fuse. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3615]
