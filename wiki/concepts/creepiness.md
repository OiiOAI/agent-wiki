# Creepiness

## Summary

The book defines creepiness as behaving in a way that makes a woman feel sexually insecure. The author argues creepiness results from a disconnect between stated intentions and actual behavior.

## Key facts

- Creepiness is defined as behaving in a way that makes a woman feel insecure sexually. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p191]
- The further you get out of line with your intentions, the more distrustful she will be of your actions and words, and the more insecure she will feel and the creepier you become. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p191]
- The author states there's no such thing as a man who is adored by women who isn't also creepy some of the time, and advises men to give themselves permission to be creepy. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: Creepiness is framed as an unavoidable risk of expressing sexuality openly, and accepting this risk paradoxically reduces the likelihood of being perceived as creepy.
