# The Seven Sins of Memory

## Summary

Daniel Schacter classified memory imperfections into seven categories termed 'the seven sins of memory': transience (fading of memories over time), absent-mindedness (failure to attend at encoding or retrieval), blocking (temporary inaccessibility of stored information), misattribution (assigning memory to wrong time/place/person), suggestibility (incorporation of external suggestions into memory), bias (distortions reflecting current knowledge and beliefs), and persistence (obsessive remembering of unwanted information). Some of these imperfections may have adaptive value.

## Key facts

- Absent-mindedness and blocking are sins of omission where information is inaccessible; misattribution, suggestibility, and bias are sins of commission where wrong memory is present; transience and persistence represent too little or too much memory retention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1509]
- Tip-of-the-tongue states engage the anterior cingulate and right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex during attempts to resolve memory blocks, reflecting conscious efforts to distinguish correct from incorrect information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1509]
- Emotional memory persistence correlates with amygdala activity during encoding; Cahill and colleagues found that recall of emotional story components was highly correlated with amygdala activity during initial presentation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1510]

## Inferences

- Inference: The case of Shereshevski, who could not forget any detail, illustrates that memory imperfections like transience and blocking may have adaptive value by preventing cognitive overload with useless details.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Little is known about the neural bases of suggestibility and bias in memory.
