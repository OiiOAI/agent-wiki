# One-on-One Meeting

## Summary

The one-on-one meeting is a regular scheduled meeting between a supervisor and subordinate that serves as the principal medium for their business relationship. Its main purposes are mutual teaching and exchange of information. The meeting should be the subordinate's meeting with its agenda set by them, allowing the supervisor to coach while learning about the subordinate's work and concerns.

## Key facts

- The main purposes of one-on-ones are mutual teaching and exchange of information between supervisor and subordinate. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L673-L674]
- One-on-ones should be the subordinate's meeting with its agenda and tone set by them, requiring the subordinate to prepare an outline in advance. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L693-L696]
- The frequency of one-on-ones should depend on the task-relevant maturity of the subordinate, with more frequent meetings for inexperienced subordinates. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L678-L681]
- One-on-ones have enormous leverage: 90 minutes of supervisor time can enhance a subordinate's work quality for two weeks or 80+ hours. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L719-L721]
- The supervisor's role is to facilitate the subordinate's expression by applying 'Grove's Principle of Didactic Management': asking one more question. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L702-L704]
- A 'hold' file where both parties accumulate issues for discussion helps batch problems and minimizes ad hoc interruptions. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L708-L710]

## Inferences

- Inference: The one-on-one concept emphasizes that supervision should serve the subordinate's development rather than merely satisfying the manager's information needs.
- Inference: The practice of requiring written outlines develops the subordinate's analytical and communication skills.
