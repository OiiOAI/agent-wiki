# Experienced Well-Being

## Summary

Experienced well-being refers to the quality of moment-to-moment emotional experience, as measured by methods like the Day Reconstruction Method (DRM) and experience sampling. Research shows that experienced well-being is determined primarily by situational factors, health, and social contact, with income having no effect on experienced well-being above approximately $75,000 despite large effects on life satisfaction.

## Key facts

- The Day Reconstruction Method (DRM) reconstructs the previous day by breaking it into episodes and asking participants to rate emotions experienced during each, enabling duration-weighted measurement of experienced well-being. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p508]
- The U-index measures the proportion of time spent in an unpleasant emotional state, with American women spending 19% of time unpleasant versus 16% for French women and 14% for Danish women. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p509]
- A small fraction of the population experiences a large fraction of all suffering—both because of health or temperament and because of personal tragedies. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p510]
- Income above approximately $75,000 in high-cost areas has zero effect on experienced well-being, though it continues to increase life satisfaction—a finding confirmed in a 2010 analysis of over 450,000 Gallup responses. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p513]
- Poor health has a much larger impact on experienced well-being than on life satisfaction, while education has a larger effect on life satisfaction than on experienced well-being. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p513]
- The best predictor of how people feel on a given day is whether they had contact with friends or family, making 'happiness is the experience of spending time with people you love' a defensible empirical claim. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p512]

## Inferences

- Inference: Life satisfaction and experienced well-being are different measures that respond to different factors, so neither should be treated as a proxy for the other in policy or personal decisions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the $75,000 saturation point applies equally to all cost-of-living areas and whether it has changed since the 2010 study.
