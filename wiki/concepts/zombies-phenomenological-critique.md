# Zombies (phenomenological critique)

## Summary

Thompson offers a phenomenological critique of the zombie thought experiment used in philosophy of mind. A zombie is conceived as physically and biologically identical to a conscious human being but lacking all conscious experience. Thompson argues the scenario requires strong assumptions — that bodily experience is not necessary for or constitutive of the relevant behavior — that are themselves deeply problematic given phenomenological analyses showing that prereflective bodily experience (kinesthesis) is constitutive of perception.

## Key facts

- The zombie scenario requires assuming that exactly the same behavior can happen with and without sentience, and that sentience is strictly internal while behavior is entirely external — both assumptions are deeply problematic. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p223]
- Husserl's analysis shows that every visual or tactile perception is functionally linked to sensing of bodily movements (kinesthesis); perceptual continuity depends on this linkage, making bodily self-experience constitutive of ordinary perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p224]
- The zombie scenario seems conceivable mainly to those under the sway of the Cartesian concept of consciousness who have not carefully considered the phenomenology of bodily experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p225]
- If zombiehood is possible, then it is possible that one's spouse, child, or loved ones are zombies — this expresses at its most extreme the Cartesian problem of other minds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p226]

## Inferences

- Inference: Our understanding of what it is to be a conscious subject is intersubjectively constituted through empathy — the sensorimotor and affective coupling of our lived bodies and mutual imaginative exchange.
