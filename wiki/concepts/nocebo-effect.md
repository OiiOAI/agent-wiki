# Nocebo Effect

## Summary

The nocebo effect is the inverse of the placebo effect, occurring when expectations and predictions cause people to experience unwanted symptoms rather than relief. For example, if a doctor warns that a completely innocent cream will cause intense itching, patients may experience that itching as a direct consequence of their expectations. This creates a self-perpetuating cycle where warnings about side effects can increase their apparent incidence.

## Key facts

- Nocebo effects are the inverse of placebo effects and occur when expectations and predictions cause us to experience unwanted symptoms rather than relief. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p48]
- If a doctor applies a 100 percent innocent cream but warns the patient will experience intense and unpleasant itching sensations, the patient may experience intense itching as a direct consequence of their expectations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p48]
- Warnings about side effects can actually bring about the unwelcome effects they describe, creating a self-perpetuating cycle where further warnings entrench expectations of the ill effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p48]
- The nocebo effect is when expecting pain causes the brain to simulate pain and therefore feel it. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p228]
- When you are expecting pain, your brain regions that process nociception change their activity, simulating pain before it occurs. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p228]
- Both placebos and nocebos involve chemical changes including opioids that work like morphine, codeine, and heroin. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p228]
- Abiola Keller showed it is not stress that is harmful but the perception that stress is harmful—adults who believed stress was harmful were more likely to die earlier. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p107]
- Participants who were told they had a 'tiring' gene had less endurance and poorer lung capacity regardless of actual genetics. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p111]
- Just believing one has a cold without virus exposure increased odds of developing cold symptoms—38% of those primed to have a cold developed one versus only 5% of controls. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p135]
- Actively imagining having a cold led to significantly more cold-like symptoms than passive belief, but passive belief was more likely to produce reported cold a week later. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p135]

## Inferences

- Inference: The nocebo effect demonstrates that pain is constructed by predictions, just as emotions are constructed.
- Inference: Elevated IgA levels in participants who developed colds from expectation alone suggest their bodies were fighting off a 'cold' even without virus exposure, possibly activating dormant viruses.
