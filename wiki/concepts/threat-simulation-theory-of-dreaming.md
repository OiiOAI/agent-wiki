# Threat Simulation Theory of Dreaming

## Summary

The Threat Simulation Theory, proposed by Antti Revonsuo in 2000, is an evolutionary hypothesis that the biological function of dreaming is to simulate threatening events and rehearse threat perception and threat avoidance. The theory argues that in the ancestral human environment where life was short and full of threats, a dream-production mechanism that selects threatening waking events and simulates them repeatedly in various combinations would have been valuable for developing and maintaining threat-avoidance skills, thereby increasing reproductive success.

## Key facts

- The theory states that dreaming simulates threatening events to rehearse threat perception and threat avoidance, which would have been biologically adaptive in the ancestral environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p1]
- According to the theory, the dream-production mechanism tends to select threatening waking events and simulate them over and over again in various combinations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p1]
- The theory identifies six empirically testable propositions covering organized simulation, specialization in threats, full activation by real threats, perceptual/behavioral realism, implicit learning benefits, and ancestral selection pressures. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p2]
- The theory is supported by evidence from normative dream content, children's dreams, recurrent dreams, nightmares, post-traumatic dreams, and dreams of hunter-gatherers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p1]

## Inferences

- Inference: The theory treats phenomenal dream experience as a natural biological phenomenon best understood from the combined viewpoints of psychology, evolutionary biology, and cognitive neuroscience.
- Inference: The theory predicts that threat simulations should be most frequent and realistic when triggered by ecologically valid cues from real threatening experiences.
