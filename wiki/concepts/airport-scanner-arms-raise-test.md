# Airport Scanner Arms-Raise Test

## Summary

A shoulder mobility assessment test where a person lies face down with arms extended forward holding a pole, then raises their arms overhead while keeping their forehead and belly in contact with the floor. The test measures shoulder flexion and is designed to reveal how much tension a person holds in their shoulders.

## Key facts

- The test is performed lying face down with arms extended straight in front, holding a PVC pipe or similar object between thumbs and forefingers. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1277-L1285]
- The thumbs should point toward the ceiling throughout the movement. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1283-L1285]
- The forehead and belly must remain in contact with the floor while raising the arms. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1285-L1287]
- The arms must stay straight (parallel) and thumbs up during the test. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1285-L1287]
- The arms should be held at the highest point for five inhales and exhales. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1287-L1288]
- Bending the elbows and holding breath should be avoided during the test. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1287-L1289]
- If unable to lift arms from the floor, the person is significantly below where they should be. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1291-L1294]
- Lifting 1 to 2 inches off the floor indicates the position can be accessed but not yet owned, with limited stamina. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1295-L1297]
- Lifting 2 or more inches off the floor indicates good shoulder flexion and lack of shoulder flexion problems. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1297-L1299]
- The recommended retest frequency is once per week. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1301-L1303]

## Inferences

- Inference: The test is named after airport security scanners because they provide a public setting where people raising their arms overhead can be easily observed.
