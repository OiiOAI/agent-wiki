# Sodium-Potassium Pump

## Summary

The Na+-K+ pump is a P-type ATPase that actively transports Na+ out of and K+ into the cell using ATP hydrolysis. It maintains the ionic gradients essential for resting membrane potential and prevents gradual dissipation of these gradients due to passive ion flux through resting channels. The pump is electrogenic, transporting net positive charge outward.

## Key facts

- The Na+-K+ pump hydrolyzes one ATP molecule to extrude three Na+ ions and import two K+ ions per cycle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p243]
- The pump is electrogenic because unequal Na+ and K+ flux generates net outward current, making the resting potential slightly more negative than diffusion mechanisms alone would produce. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p243]
- The Na+-K+ pump is inhibited by ouabain and digitalis plant alkaloids, important in treating heart failure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p243]
- The Na+-K+ pump hydrolyzes one ATP molecule to extrude three Na+ ions and bring in two K+ ions per cycle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p184]
- The pump is electrogenic because its unequal Na+ and K+ flux generates a net outward ionic current, making the resting potential slightly more negative than diffusion alone would produce. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p184]
- The Na+-K+ pump is a P-type ATPase with 10 transmembrane α-helices in its α-subunit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p185]
- The Na+-K+ pump is inhibited by ouabain and digitalis, which are important in treating heart failure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p185]
- Ion flux through channels (~10⁷ to 10⁸ ions/second) is more than 10,000 times faster than pump operation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p183]
