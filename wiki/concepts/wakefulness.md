# Wakefulness

## Summary

Wakefulness is the state in which the brain and mind are 'on' and images of the organism's interior and environment are being formed. While wakefulness and consciousness tend to go together, they can be decoupled: dream sleep shows consciousness without wakefulness, and akinetic mutism or absence seizures show wakefulness without consciousness.

## Key facts

- Wakefulness permits the brain to form images of surroundings, but consciousness may still be absent in the wakeful state. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p89]
- When wakefulness is removed (dreamless sleep, anesthesia, coma), consciousness is also removed, but the reverse coupling can be broken. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p89]
- Wakefulness is best observed in the transition from sleep to wakefulness, as exemplified by Winnie in Beckett's Happy Days. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p89]
