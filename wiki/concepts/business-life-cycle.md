# Business Life Cycle (Infancy, Adolescence, Maturity)

## Summary

Gerber describes three phases of small business growth. Infancy is the Technician's Phase where the owner and business are identical; it ends when the owner realizes the business cannot continue as it has. Adolescence begins when the owner hires help, leading to Management by Abdication rather than Delegation. Maturity is achieved when the business operates on an Entrepreneurial Perspective—ideally from the beginning.

## Key facts

- In Infancy, the owner and business are one and the same; removing the owner would make the business disappear. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p41]
- Infancy ends when the owner realizes the business cannot continue to run the way it has been and must change. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p44]
- Adolescence begins when the owner decides to get help, typically hiring technical help first. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p49]
- The owner's Management by Abdication rather than Delegation causes deterioration as the business grows. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p52]
- Beyond the Comfort Zone, businesses can either get small again, go for broke, or achieve Adolescent Survival. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p53]

## Inferences

- Inference: Businesses that survive Adolescence typically do so through extreme personal sacrifice and eventual burnout rather than through sustainable systems
