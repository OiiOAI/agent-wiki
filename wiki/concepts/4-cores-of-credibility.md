# 4 Cores of Credibility

## Summary

The 4 Cores of Credibility are the foundational elements that make an individual believable and trustworthy: Integrity and Intent (dealing with character), and Capabilities and Results (dealing with competence). Covey emphasizes that all four cores are necessary for credibility—missing any one undermines the ability to inspire trust.

## Key facts

- Core 1—Integrity: Being honest, congruent, and walking one's talk; having the courage to act in accordance with values. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p80]
- Core 2—Intent: Having straightforward motives and agendas based on mutual benefit; genuinely caring about others' well-being. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p80]
- Core 3—Capabilities: The abilities that inspire confidence—talents, skills, knowledge, and style used to produce results. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p80]
- Core 4—Results: Track record, performance, and getting the right things done; accomplishing what is expected. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p80]
- Covey uses a tree metaphor: Integrity is the root, Intent is the trunk, Capabilities are the branches, and Results are the fruits. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p81]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between character (integrity, intent) and competence (capabilities, results) suggests different development pathways for each core.
