# Graphic Thinking

## Summary

S.'s characteristic mode of cognition described as 'speculative' by him—meaning he could actually see what others only imagined. He solved problems through visual manipulation of mental images rather than abstract reasoning, allowing him to find contradictions in texts, devise practical solutions, and work through mathematical problems by visualizing concrete scenarios. However, this concrete visual approach also limited his ability to handle abstraction.

## Key facts

- S. characterized his thinking as 'speculative'—meaning he could see with his mind what others only dimly imagined, with images so palpable they verged on reality. - S. detected contradictions in Chekhov's stories that the author himself had failed to notice—for example, noticing a character wearing a 'greatcoat' in one sentence and asking to have his 'coat' removed later. - S. solved the classic 'bookworm' problem instantly by visualizing two books on a shelf, recognizing the worm only traveled through bindings, not pages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p110]
- S. solved complex mathematical problems by visualizing scenarios—for example, representing bread loaves as cubes divided among people to solve sharing problems.
