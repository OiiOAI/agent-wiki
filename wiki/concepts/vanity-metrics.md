# Vanity Metrics

## Summary

Vanity metrics are traditional numbers used to judge startups, such as gross number of customers or total revenue, that give the rosiest possible picture but do not indicate whether a company is on pace to build a sustainable business. They create the illusion of progress and are contrasted with actionable metrics that demonstrate clear cause and effect.

## Key facts

- Vanity metrics give the rosiest possible picture; a traditional hockey stick graph showing total registered users and paying customers can make it appear a product development team is making great progress even when they are not. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p121]
- Grockit's progress was being measured by vanity metrics: the total number of customers and total number of questions answered, which gave the team the sensation of forward motion even though the company was making little progress. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p127]
- Vanity metrics prey on a weakness of the human mind: when numbers go up, people assume the improvement was caused by their actions. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p133]
- When vanity metrics go down, it results in blaming others, leading departments to believe they are constantly improving while being sabotaged by other departments. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p134]
