# Basal Ganglia

## Summary

The basal ganglia are ancient subcortical structures that control motor functions and are key components of emotional processing. They receive massive cortical input and are divided into dorsal striatum (caudate-putamen) and ventral striatum (nucleus accumbens), each with distinct inputs and outputs.

## Key facts

- The basal ganglia receive their most massive input from the neocortex, though this input is not obligatory for normal striatal function in simple mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
- The overall functions of the basal ganglia are under the control of ascending brain dopamine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p48]
- Dopamine systems ascend into the striatum dividing into the nigrostriatal system (substantia nigra to dorsal striatum) and mesolimbic/mesocortical pathways (ventral tegmental area to ventral striatum and frontal cortex). [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p48]
- Decorticate animals continue to exhibit their instinctive behavior patterns, demonstrating basal ganglia can function independently of neocortical input in lower mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
