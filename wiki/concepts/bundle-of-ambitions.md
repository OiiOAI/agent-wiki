# Bundle of Ambitions

## Summary

The bundle of ambitions concept challenges the common assumption that individuals and organizations have one or two primary driving goals. In reality, most have multiple intentions, visions of the future, and desires that may conflict with one another. Forging purpose out of this bundle is part of solving gnarly challenges. The concept is illustrated through examples including Rumelt's own aspirations at age twenty-five and Reed Hastings's many desires for Netflix.

## Key facts

- The idea that a person or organization has one or two primary driving goals is a fantasy invented by economists and management thinkers. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p27]
- Most people and organizations have multiple intentions, visions, and desires that may conflict and cannot all be satisfied at once. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p27]
- Effective strategy emerges from exploration of challenges, ambitions, resources, and competition rather than starting from fixed goals. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p28]
- The clash of competing ambitions within an organization can create a null set where no feasible action satisfies all desires. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p72]

## Inferences

- Inference: Strategy often requires choosing among values and ambitions, as not all elements of a bundle can be accomplished simultaneously.
