# Mechanoreceptors

## Summary

Mechanoreceptors sense physical deformation of tissue through stretch-sensitive ion channels in their membranes. The glabrous skin of the hand contains four types: Meissner corpuscles (RA1), Merkel cells (SA1), Pacinian corpuscles (RA2), and Ruffini endings (SA2), each with distinct response properties.

## Key facts

- Mechanoreceptor ion channels can be activated directly through lipid tension in the plasma membrane or indirectly through second-messenger pathways involving distinct force sensor proteins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p532]
- Slowly adapting (SA) receptors continue firing during steady pressure on the skin, while rapidly adapting (RA) receptors respond only to motion and vibration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p534]
- Merkel cells (SA1) signal pressure and texture, distinguish edges and points, and are critical for reading Braille. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p534]
- Meissner corpuscles (RA1) detect initial contact, object slippage, and low-frequency vibration; Pacinian corpuscles (RA2) detect high-frequency vibration through their large onion-like capsule. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p534]
- Ruffini endings (SA2) respond more to skin stretch than indentation, signaling shape of grasped objects and finger joint movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p534]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hair follicle afferents serve a function similar to Meissner corpuscles in hairy skin, detecting hair movement rather than static pressure.
