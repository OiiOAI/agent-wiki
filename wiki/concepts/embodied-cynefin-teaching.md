# Embodied Cynefin Teaching

## Summary

Embodied Cynefin teaching uses physical exercises and body-based activities to help learners understand the framework's domains experientially before analytical explanation. Developed by Chris Corrigan and others, this approach draws on improv theater, martial arts, and Augusto Boal's Theater of the Oppressed.

## Key facts

- Chris Corrigan developed three embodied exercises: organizing by height alone (Clear), by multiple dimensions (Complicated), and the Sun/Moon eclipse game (Complex). [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p162]
- The teaching protocol includes: state challenge, let group work it out, then debrief with specific questions. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p161]
- Keith Johnston created a rope-based physical Cynefin Framework in Adelaide in 2015. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p173]
- Key teaching principles: separate experience from sense-making, repeat instructions twice only, be sparing with debrief questions, avoid Cynefin language during exercises. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p168]

## Inferences

- Inference: Embodied teaching leverages the principle that the body knows things before the mind articulates them.
