# Productivity

## Summary

Productivity is defined as the output divided by the labor required to generate it. The book identifies two ways to increase productivity: by performing work activities faster (increasing the rate), or by increasing the leverage of activities (changing what work is done). The goal is to 'work smarter, not harder' by arranging work flows to maximize output per activity.

## Key facts

- Productivity is defined as output divided by the labor required to generate the output. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L602-L603]
- Productivity can be increased by performing work activities at a higher rate, which increases output per hour. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L606-L608]
- Productivity can also be increased by changing the nature of work to increase the ratio of output to activity, described as 'working smarter, not harder.'. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L610-L612]
- Leverage is the output generated by a specific type of work activity, with high-leverage activities generating more output per unit of effort. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L614-L616]
- Work simplification involves creating flow charts, counting steps, and targeting 30-50 percent reduction in steps by questioning why each step exists. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L624-L629]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between output and activity is crucial—many administrative tasks can be activities without producing valuable output.
- Inference: Productivity improvement requires first defining what output actually means for a given role or function.
