# Anxiety Hysteria

## Summary

Anxiety hysteria (or anxiety neurosis) is a neurotic disorder characterized by free-floating anxiety that becomes attached to specific objects or situations, forming phobias. Freud proposed this term to describe conditions where repressed libido is converted into anxiety rather than being converted into somatic symptoms as in conversion hysteria. The condition tends to develop progressively into a phobia, restricting the patient's activities as a protective measure against anxiety-producing situations.

## Key facts

- Freud classified Little Hans's condition as anxiety hysteria, where repressed libido is converted into anxiety rather than somatic symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3228]
- Anxiety hysterias are the most common psychoneurotic disorders and are par excellence the neuroses of childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3229]
- In anxiety hysteria, the liberated anxiety is not converted into somatic symptoms but remains in the mental sphere, though attached to specific objects. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3228]
- A characteristic of anxiety hysterias is that they tend to develop more and more into phobias, restricting the patient's activities. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3229]
- Anxiety hysterias may be combined with conversion hysteria in any proportion, ranging from pure conversion hysteria to simple anxiety hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3228]
- The treatment of anxiety hysteria by violent means—depriving patients of their defenses—is impossible and potentially dangerous. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3230]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Little Hans case demonstrated that phobias in children are not simple irrational fears but complex structures containing displaced unconscious material related to the Oedipal situation.
