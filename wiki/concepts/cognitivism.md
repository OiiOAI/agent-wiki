# Cognitivism

## Summary

Cognitivism was the dominant approach in cognitive science from the 1950s to 1970s, based on the 'cognitive revolution' against behaviorism. It conceptualizes the mind as a digital computer, with cognition as information processing. The brain is seen as a 'physical symbol system' and mental processes as symbolic manipulation. Cognitivism went hand in hand with functionalism, holding that embodiment is irrelevant to mind—what matters is software, not hardware. It created an explanatory gap between subpersonal computational cognition and subjective mental phenomena, essentially banishing consciousness from the science of mind.

## Key facts

- Cognitivism emerged from the cognitive revolution against behaviorism in the 1950s, centered on the computer model of mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p16]
- According to cognitivism, the brain is a 'physical symbol system' and mental processes are symbol manipulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p16]
- Cognitivism made meaning scientifically acceptable but at the price of banishing consciousness from the science of mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p17]
- Mental processes were taken to be entirely nonconscious 'subpersonal routines' inaccessible to personal awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p17]
- Ray Jackendoff identified three mind-body problems created by cognitivism: phenomenological, computational, and mind-mind problems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p18]
- Cognitivism inherited its consciousness taboo directly from behaviorism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p17]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cognitivist separation of cognition and consciousness perpetuated Cartesian dualism in materialist form, creating a new 'mind-mind problem.'
