# Psychological Bends

## Summary

Frankl uses the analogy of the 'bends' (caisson disease) to describe the psychological danger faced by liberated prisoners. Just as a diver leaving a pressurized chamber suddenly risks physical harm, a person suddenly freed from prolonged mental pressure can suffer damage to his moral and spiritual health. This explained why some prisoners, upon liberation, could not escape the brutality they had witnessed in camps and used their new freedom licentiously and ruthlessly.

## Key facts

- A man who has suddenly been liberated from mental pressure can suffer damage to his moral and spiritual health, analogous to the physical danger the caisson worker faces leaving his diver's chamber suddenly. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p73]
- During the psychological phase after liberation, people with primitive natures could not escape the influences of the brutality which had surrounded them in camp life and thought they could use their freedom licentiously and ruthlessly.
