# James-Lange theory

## Summary

The James-Lange theory, proposed in the late 18th century by William James and Carl Lange, posits that changes in bodily responses are a necessary condition for emotional experience to arise. The theory suggests we feel our hearts beating when we fall in love or experience fear, and we feel our stomachs constricting under stress. This foundational theory underlies modern interoceptive models of emotion.

- According to this theory, emotions cannot be experienced in the absence of bodily feelings—we feel our hearts beating when we fall in love or experience fear.
