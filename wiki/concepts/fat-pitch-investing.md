# Fat Pitch

## Summary

Fat Pitch is Munger's baseball metaphor (adapted from Ted Williams' hitting technique) for investment opportunities that are 'slow, straight, and right in the middle of your sweet spot.' Just as Williams insisted on swinging only at balls in his 'best' cells of the strike zone, Munger waits for exceptional opportunities before committing significant capital, then swings hard.

## Key facts

- Ted Williams divided the strike zone into seventy-seven cells and would only swing at balls in his 'best' cells, accepting strikeouts to avoid reducing his chances of success. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p61]
- Munger uses the analogy that securities investors can watch business propositions thrown at them but should only act when they find a 'fat pitch that is slow, straight, and right in the middle of your sweet spot.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p61]
- Munger notes two harmful behaviors: swinging too often (both individuals and professionals) and discovering a fat pitch but being unable to swing with full capital weight. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p62]
- Buffett teaches that investors should have only 20 punches representing all investments in a lifetime, forcing careful consideration and loading up on what they truly thought about. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p62]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fat pitch strategy implies that patience and selectivity in investing, rather than constant activity, are essential to long-term success.
