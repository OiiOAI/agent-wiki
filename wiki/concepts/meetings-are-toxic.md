# Meetings Are Toxic

## Summary

The authors argue that meetings are among the worst productivity killers because they are usually about abstract words rather than real work, convey minimal information per minute, drift off-topic easily, require preparation people rarely have, often have vague agendas, frequently include people wasting time, and procreate into more meetings. They calculate that a one-hour meeting with ten people actually costs ten to fifteen hours of productivity when accounting for mental switching costs. The solution is to minimize meetings, keep them small, have clear agendas, meet at the problem site, and end with solutions and assigned responsibility.

## Key facts

- The authors identify meetings as one of the worst interruptions to productivity, listing problems including focus on abstract concepts, minimal information transfer, easy topic drift, and lack of preparation. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p84]
- A one-hour meeting with ten people actually costs ten to fifteen hours of productivity when including mental switching costs. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p84]
- The authors recommend that if a meeting is necessary, it should have a timer, include as few people as possible, have a clear agenda, begin with a specific problem, meet at the site of the problem, and end with a solution and assigned responsibility. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p85]

## Inferences

- Inference: The high cost calculation suggests that meetings should be evaluated as investments with quantifiable returns rather than assumed to be necessary coordination mechanisms.
