# Counterwish Dreams

## Summary

Counterwish dreams are a category of dreams whose manifest content appears to express the opposite of a desire—frustration, failure, or the death of loved ones. Freud explains these through two principles: the wish to be proven wrong (often in therapeutic contexts), and masochistic tendencies that transform unpleasure into satisfaction.

## Key facts

- Counterwish dreams are traced to two principles: the wish that Freud may be wrong (common in resistant patients), and masochistic inversion of aggressive impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1426]
- The young man who dreamt he was arrested for infanticide after intercourse with a married woman was expressing reassurance through disguise: the wish that no child resulted from the act. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1424]
- A patient's masochistic dream of being chided by her brother, of men caressing homsexually, and of her brother selling the business represented inverted self-punishment wishes.
