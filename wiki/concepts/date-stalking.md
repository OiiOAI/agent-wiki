# Date Stalking

## Summary

Unwanted romantic pursuit by someone the victim has dated or met, distinct from pursuit by strangers. The book analyzes warning signs during dating, the failure of 'letting him down easy,' and strategies for escaping unwanted pursuit without escalation.

## Key facts

- Stalking is described as a crime of power, control, and intimidation similar to date rape, enforcing the cruelest rule that women are not allowed to decide who will be in their lives. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p133]
- The axiom presented is: 'Men who cannot let go choose women who cannot say no.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p135]
- Warning signs during dating include: researching the victim's background, leaving multiple messages before even meeting, hyper-attentiveness, offering unsolicited help, projecting emotions or commitments not present, whirlwind pace placing commitment issues on the agenda prematurely, jealousy, isolating from friends. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p138]
- The book recommends one explicit, unconditional rejection followed by absolute no contact, as any response is viewed by the pursuer as progress. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p134]
- Conditional rejections (e.g., 'I'm not ready for a relationship right now') are not true rejections because they give the pursuer something to challenge. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p134]
- Changing phone numbers is not recommended; instead, getting a second line and leaving the old number with an answering machine allows documentation of calls while keeping the stalker unaware of the new number. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p135]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book argues that persistence only proves persistence, not love, and that a relentless pursuer indicates a troubled individual rather than a devoted one.
- Inference: Cultural messages from movies like 'The Graduate' model persistence as a romantic strategy, which may normalize stalking behavior.
