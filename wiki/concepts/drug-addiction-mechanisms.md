# Drug Addiction Mechanisms

## Summary

Drug addiction is a chronic syndrome characterized by compulsive drug seeking and consumption despite negative consequences. The risk for addiction is roughly 50% genetic. Drugs of abuse are chemically diverse but converge on the brain's reward circuitry by increasing dopaminergic neurotransmission. Repeated drug exposure induces lasting adaptations including tolerance, sensitization, and dependence/withdrawal. Molecular adaptations include upregulation of cAMP-CREB pathways and induction of ΔFosB transcription factor, leading to synaptic and circuit changes that mediate aspects of the addicted state.

## Key facts

- Drug addiction risk is roughly 50% genetic, reflecting combined actions of hundreds of genetic variations with small individual effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1098]
- Tolerance refers to diminished drug effects after repeated administration; sensitization is escalating effects; dependence is an adaptive state unmasked during withdrawal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1098]
- Upregulation of the cAMP-CREB pathway is a molecular mechanism underlying tolerance and dependence; when drug is removed, the upregulated pathway causes withdrawal symptoms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1100]
- ΔFosB accumulates in nucleus accumbens neurons after repeated drug exposure due to its unusual stability, mediating reward sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1100]
- Repeated drug exposure causes both synaptic plasticity (LTD/LTP-like changes at glutamatergic synapses) and whole-cell plasticity (altered intrinsic excitability). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1101]

## Inferences

- Inference: The convergence of diverse drugs on common dopaminergic reward pathways explains why chemically different substances produce similar addictive syndromes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific genes conferring addiction risk remain largely unknown despite the established 50% heritability.
