# Craftsman Approach to Tool Selection

## Summary

A philosophy of selecting network tools based on whether their positive impacts substantially outweigh their negative impacts on core factors determining success and happiness, in contrast to the any-benefit approach.

## Key facts

- The craftsman approach requires identifying core factors determining success and happiness, then adopting tools only if positive impacts substantially outweigh negatives. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p112]
- This approach opposes the any-benefit mind-set, which identifies any potential positive impact as justification for using a tool. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p112]
- Malcolm Gladwell, Michael Lewis, and George Packer don't use Twitter because its benefits don't substantially outweigh negatives for their specific circumstances. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p113]
- Michael Lewis worries that increased accessibility will sap his energy and reduce his ability to research and write. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p114]

## Inferences

- Inference: Network tools should be evaluated with the same level of sophistication and skepticism that skilled laborers apply to their tools throughout history.
