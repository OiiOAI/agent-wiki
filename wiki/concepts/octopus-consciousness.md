# Octopus Consciousness

## Summary

Octopus consciousness represents a uniquely alien form of awareness due to the independent evolution of cephalopods from vertebrates 600 million years ago. Octopuses have distributed nervous systems with most neurons in their arms, can change color despite being color-blind, and may not experience body ownership in the human sense. Their remarkable intelligence includes observational learning, problem-solving, and tool use, but intelligence is not a reliable indicator of consciousness.

## Key facts

- The most recent common ancestor of humans and octopuses lived about 600 million years ago—octopus minds are an independently created evolutionary experiment, 'as close to the mind of an alien as we are likely to encounter.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p224]
- Peter Godfrey-Smith wrote, 'If we want to understand other minds, the minds of cephalopods are the most other of all.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p224]
- Octopuses have about 500 million neurons with approximately three-fifths located in their arms rather than their central brain, which has about 40 anatomically distinct lobes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p224]
- Octopus brains lack myelin, making their nervous system more distributed and less integrated than mammalian nervous systems of similar size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p224]
- Octopuses are color-blind but can match the color, shape, and texture of their surroundings using chromatophores controlled by the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p226]
- Octopus chromatophore control is thought to be 'open-loop'—the brain might not even know what its skin is doing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p226]
- Octopus arms behave like semi-autonomous animals; a severed octopus arm can execute complex action sequences like grasping food for some time after separation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p227]
- Octopuses use taste-based self-recognition through chemicals secreted in their skin to distinguish self from other, even though they don't necessarily know where their body is in space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p227]
- RNA sequences in octopuses can undergo significant editing before translation into protein, possibly related to their impressive cognitive abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: What it is like to be an octopus may not include experience of body ownership in anything like the human sense, because octopuses may not even bother maintaining unified perception of what is part of their body.
- Inference: There might even be 'something it is like to be an octopus arm.'
- Inference: Octopus consciousness may be more distributed and less integrated, perhaps without a single 'center.'
