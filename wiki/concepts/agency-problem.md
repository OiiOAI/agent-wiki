# Agency Problem

## Summary

The agency problem refers to the asymmetry where one party gets the upside while a different party bears the downside—making that party personally antifragile at others' expense. Taleb identifies this as the central problem of modernity, manifesting across banking (bankers get bonuses from risks that taxpayers absorb), corporations (managers with stock options profit from volatility while shareholders lose), pharmaceuticals (companies profit from treating the healthy while hidden harms emerge decades later), and academia (researchers get publications while society absorbs wrong predictions). The legal system cannot catch these transfers because consequences are delayed and causal chains invisible. The only true mitigator is skin in the game.

## Key facts

- The worst problem of modernity lies in the malignant transfer of fragility and antifragility from one party to the other, with one getting the benefits and the other (unwittingly) getting the harm. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p467]
- The U.S. stock market has cost retirees more than three trillion dollars in losses over the past dozen years compared to money market funds, while managers enriched themselves by approximately four hundred billion dollars through stock option asymmetry. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p491]
- Banks have lost more than they ever made in their history, yet managers were paid billions in compensation—taxpayers took the downside, bankers got the upside. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p491]
- Alan Blinder, former Federal Reserve vice chairman, was selling a product that allowed wealthy investors to circumvent deposit insurance limits while taxpayers bore the risk. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p510]
- Pharmaceutical companies cannot gain if you avoid sugar; gym equipment manufacturers cannot profit if you walk on rocks—corporations must produce 'growth in revenues' for security analysts, driving via positiva instead of via negativa. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p495]
- Publicly listed corporations have no sense of honor, shame, pity, or generosity—they merely obey the balance sheet and the metric demands of security analysts. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p498]
- The agency problem occurs when a manager is not the true owner, following strategies that cosmically seem sound but secretly benefit them at the expense of true owners or society. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p530]
- The agency problem is easy to hide risks, leading to fragility, and also affects politicians and academics. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p530]
- Skin in the game (Captain and Ship Rule) removes the agency problem and lack of doxastic commitment. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p530]

## Inferences

- Inference: Taleb's proposal that companies 'too big to fail' should not pay anyone more than corresponding civil servants would force companies to stay small enough to avoid bailout eligibility, aligning incentives with society.
- Inference: The transfer of antifragility explains why large corporations systematically fail over time while managers profit during the process—corporations collapse but managers have already extracted value through options.
