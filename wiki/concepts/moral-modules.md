# Moral Modules

## Summary

Moral modules are hypothesized innate cognitive systems that generate automatic moral intuitions. Haidt and Joseph identified five universal modules: reciprocity, suffering/harm, hierarchy, in-group/out-group coalitions, and purity. Each produces specific moral emotions and can be triggered by specific environmental inputs.

## Key facts

- The reciprocity module produces emotions of gratitude, anger, contempt, and guilt, generating virtues of fairness, justice, trustworthiness, and patience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p140]
- The suffering module produces sympathy, compassion, and empathy, generating virtues of compassion and kindness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p142]
- The hierarchy module produces emotions of guilt, shame, embarrassment, and respect, generating virtues of respect, loyalty, and obedience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p143]
- The in-group/out-group coalition module generates emotions including compassion, contempt, anger, guilt, and shame related to group membership, producing virtues of trust, cooperation, loyalty, and heroism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p145]
- The purity module, rooted in disease avoidance, produces disgust and generates virtues of cleanliness, chastity, and purity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p145]
