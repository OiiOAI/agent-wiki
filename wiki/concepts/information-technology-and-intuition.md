# Information Technology and Intuition

## Summary

Information technology can diminish intuition and expertise through multiple mechanisms: disabling existing expertise, slowing the rate of learning, and teaching dysfunctional skills. The damage occurs because technology disconnects users from raw data, limits how they search for information, weakens mental models, hides the story of how data is processed, reduces adaptability by pressuring adherence to prescribed procedures, and makes users passive. Expert weather forecasters exemplified this by refusing to look at computer-generated forecasts until they had reviewed raw data themselves to build their own understanding.

## Key facts

- Information technology can inflict three levels of damage: disabling existing expertise, slowing the rate of learning, and teaching dysfunctional skills that actively interfere with future expertise development. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p307]
- Expert weather forecasters build their own mental models by reviewing raw data themselves and refuse to rely on preanalyzed information because expertise depends on actively searching and building understanding. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p310]
- Information technology can disable expertise by automatically providing processed data rather than letting users work with data themselves, eliminating pattern recognition opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p311]
- Research on weather forecasters showed that after settling on key five to ten pieces of information, additional information didn't help and actually reduced accuracy. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p319]
