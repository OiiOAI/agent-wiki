# Fine Discrimination

## Summary

Fine discrimination is the ability to detect subtle differences that novices cannot perceive even if they try. Wine tasters can distinguish grape types and vintage years; experienced radiologists notice different things in X-rays than students do. These differences cannot be learned simply by being told—they require extensive experience with variety to notice.

## Key facts

- Experts can detect differences that novices cannot see, cannot even force themselves to see; to novices, wines are generic. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p197]
- Fine discrimination is not a fact or insight that can be learned by being told—it takes lots of experience and variety in that experience to notice differences. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p197]
- Radiology conference champions detected when a slide was doctored and hypothesized it was a double exposure, showing the limits of expert discrimination ability. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p197]
