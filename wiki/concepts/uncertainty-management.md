# Uncertainty Management

## Summary

Gary Klein identifies five sources of uncertainty and twelve tactics for managing it. The five sources are: missing information (not having or being unable to locate needed data), unreliable information (distrusting data even when available), conflicting information (inconsistency between trusted data sources), noisy information (difficulty distinguishing relevant from irrelevant data), and confusing information (data that is too complex or allows multiple interpretations). The twelve tactics include delaying, seeking more information, increasing attention, filling gaps with assumptions, building an interpretation, pressing on, shaking the tree, designing decision scenarios, simplifying the plan, preparing for the worst, using incremental decisions, and embracing uncertainty.

## Key facts

- The five sources of uncertainty are missing information, unreliable information, conflicting information, noisy information, and confusing information. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p154]
- These five sources should not be treated as equivalent, as the appropriate response differs for each type. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p154]
- The twelve uncertainty management tactics are: delaying, seeking more information, increasing attention, filling gaps with assumptions, building an interpretation, pressing on, shaking the tree, designing decision scenarios, simplifying the plan, preparing for the worst, using incremental decisions, and embracing the uncertainty. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p155-159]
- Decision makers differ in their tolerance for ambiguity, which affects which tactics feel natural to each person. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p160]
- The average tolerance for ambiguity score range is 44-48 on Stanley Budner's scale. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p162]
