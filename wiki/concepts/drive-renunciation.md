# Drive Renunciation

## Summary

The concept that religious ethics and moral development are founded on the renunciation of instinctual drives, imposed first by parental authority and later internalized as the superego. Freud connects this to the origin of sacredness and holiness, arguing that prohibitions derive from the primal father's will.

## Key facts

- Freud describes how drive renunciation imposed by the superego brings the ego not only unpleasure but also a yield of pleasure—a substitutive satisfaction, making the ego feel elevated and proud. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7279]
- Freud argues that the Mosaic religion developed increasingly into a religion of drive renunciations, with God becoming entirely removed from sexuality and elevated into the ideal of ethical perfection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7281]
- Freud states that what is sacred was originally the prolongation of the will of the primal father—the prohibition against incest being the paradigm case. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7283]
