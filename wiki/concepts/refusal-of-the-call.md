# Refusal of the Call

## Summary

The refusal of the hero's summons converts the adventure into its negative. The subject loses significant action and becomes a victim to be saved. This refusal is essentially a refusal to give up what one takes to be one's own interest, preferring present systems of ideals and goals rather than accepting the necessary deaths and births of transformation.

## Key facts

- Refusal of the summons converts the adventure into its negative. Walled in boredom, hard work, or culture, the subject loses the power of significant affirmative action and becomes a victim to be saved. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p52]
- The refusal is essentially a refusal to give up what one takes to be one's own interest; the future is regarded not in terms of an unremitting series of deaths and births, but as though one's present system of ideals were to be fixed and made secure. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p52]
- King Minos retained the divine bull, refusing the sacrifice that would have signified submission to the will of the god, thereby failing to advance into his life-role with calamitous effect. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p52]
- The myth of Daphne illustrates refusal—fleeing from the god Apollo, she retreats to the image of her parent and finds protection, like the unsuccessful husband whose dream preserves him from mature life. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p53-55]
- Not all who hesitate are lost; the psyche has many secrets in reserve, and sometimes the predicament following refusal proves to be the occasion of a providential revelation. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p55]
