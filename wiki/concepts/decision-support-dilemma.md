# Decision Support Dilemma

## Summary

The decision support dilemma describes the finding that decision support systems often fail to produce expected performance improvements because humans do not operate independently from these systems but are highly influenced by their advice. This results in human and computer functioning as serial rather than parallel systems, where overall reliability can decrease rather than increase. Systems that provide incorrect advice lead to significantly increased errors in human judgment.

## Key facts

- When decision support systems are wrong, people are 30-40% more likely to make an error following the system than if they had received no advice at all. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p200]
- Human operators and decision support systems operate more like serial components than parallel components, meaning combined reliability decreases rather than increases. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p201]
- Critiquing systems that provide advice after operators make decisions showed significantly improved performance with much less susceptibility to biasing than traditional decision support systems. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p202]
- Intelligent target cueing systems that highlight areas of a display lead to significant errors when targets are not in expected areas or when uncued targets are present. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p202]

## Inferences

- Inference: Critiquing systems may achieve better human-automation synergy because they provide input after the operator has made an initial decision, aligning better with a parallel model of interaction.
