# Fairness Foundation (Proportionality)

## Summary

One of the six moral foundations, initially understood through reciprocal altruism theory but revised to focus on proportionality. The Fairness foundation supports intuitions about reciprocal exchange (tit for tat) and proportionality in rewards and punishments. It generates 'righteous anger' when cheaters and free riders exploit cooperative arrangements. The foundation is primarily about proportionality—ensuring people receive outcomes proportional to their contributions—rather than strict equality.

## Key facts

- The Fairness foundation supports righteous anger when anyone cheats, but also generalized concern with cheaters and free riders who 'drink the water' without carrying it. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p201]
- Egalitarianism is rooted more in hatred of domination than love of equality per se. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p200]
- When a few members contribute nothing while others contribute much, most adults do not want benefits distributed equally. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p201]
- The Fairness foundation is about proportionality and the 'law of karma'—making sure people get what they deserve. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p204]
- Conservatives care more about fairness-as-proportionality than liberals, endorsing items like 'employees who work hardest should be paid most.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p204]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between equality and proportionality helps explain why conservatives support meritocratic policies while liberals support redistributive policies—both may be operating within the same Fairness foundation but interpreting proportionality differently.
