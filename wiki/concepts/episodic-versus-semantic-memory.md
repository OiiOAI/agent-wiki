# Episodic versus Semantic Memory Distinction

## Summary

Research with Henry Molaison demonstrated a crucial distinction between episodic and semantic memory. Both types of declarative memory are impaired in anterograde amnesia, but the pattern of impairment differs for pre-existing memories: most details of unique events are lost (episodic/autobiographical memory), but general knowledge of the world is preserved (semantic memory). The hippocampus plays different roles in these two types of memory.

## Key facts

- Remembering unique events (episodic memory) and remembering facts (semantic memory) are both impaired in anterograde amnesia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p350]
- Most details of unique events are lost after medial temporal-lobe damage (episodic, autobiographical memory), but general knowledge of the world is preserved (semantic memory). [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p350]
- The ability to recall and recognize information stored before the onset of amnesia differs depending on whether it is episodic or semantic information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p350]
