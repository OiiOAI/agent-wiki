# Fallacy of the Single Cause

## Summary

The fallacy of the single cause is the tendency to attribute complex outcomes to a single cause, despite Tolstoy's observation that "no one thing is the cause." The 2008 financial crisis resulted from multiple factors: Greenspan's policy, investor stupidity, rating agencies, bad models, greed. The cure is creating networks of potential causes.

## Key facts

- The 2008 financial crisis had multiple causes: Greenspan's loose policy, investor stupidity, rating agencies, corrupt auditors, bad risk models, and greed—none alone, yet all contributed. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p211]
- Tolstoy wrote: "When an apple ripens and falls—no one thing is the cause.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p211]
- The fallacy of single cause enables scapegoating and blame assignment, which goes "hand-in-hand with the exercise of power.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p212]
