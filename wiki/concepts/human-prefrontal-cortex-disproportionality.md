# Human Prefrontal Cortex Disproportionality

## Summary

Human prefrontal cortex is estimated to be approximately two times greater than predicted by nonhuman primate allometric trends. This finding is based on analyses of primate data sets collected by architectonic neuroanatomists, controlling for part/whole and extrapolation effects. Assessments of gyrification patterns by Armstrong, Zilles, and colleagues also found prefrontal region to be a significant outlier with respect to primate trends. However, some MRI-based analyses have suggested equal proportions between apes and humans, with disagreement depending on methods of analysis, control for species variance, identification of architectonic boundaries, and control for part/whole errors.

## Key facts

- Deacon's assessments converge on a value that is roughly two times greater than predicted by nonhuman primate trends for prefrontal cortex proportion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p517]
- Assessments of the degree of gyrification in human and other primate brains by Armstrong, Zilles, and colleagues (1989, 1995a, 1995b) found that the prefrontal region in the human brain is a significant outlier with respect to primate trends. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p518]
- These findings are in agreement with many qualitative assessments of neuroanatomists for over a century and are consistent with the predictions of developmental parcellation processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p518]
- Semendeferi et al. (1997) suggested that frontal cortex is of equal proportions in ape and human brains based on MRI reconstructions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p517]
- Deacon argues that the identification of disproportion depends on how the analysis controls for species variance, identification of architectonic boundaries across species, and control for part/whole errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p517]

## Inferences

- Inference: When analytical artifacts are controlled for (species variance, architectonic homology, part/whole errors), the different data sets do not produce conflicting results and human prefrontal cortex is found to be divergent from nonhuman trends.
- Inference: The twofold increase above the trend represents a reliable estimate of human prefrontal disproportionality based on multiple converging lines of evidence.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: More current and complete data sets would be valuable for settling lingering questions and providing more detailed assessment of differences between primate and nonprimate prefrontal areas.
