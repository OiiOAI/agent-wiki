# Psychology of Superstition

## Summary

Freud develops a psychological theory of superstition arguing that superstitious beliefs arise from unconscious knowledge of the motivation behind one's own parapraxes, projected outward onto external events. The superstitious person lacks awareness of the true (internal) causes of their chance actions and therefore attributes meaning to external coincidences. This displacement transforms internal psychical determination into external supernatural causation. Superstition is closely related to paranoia, where both conditions involve the projection of unconscious mental processes onto the external world.

## Key facts

- Freud distinguishes himself from the superstitious person: he does not believe external events without mental participation can predict the future, but believes his own unintentional mental manifestations can reveal unconscious material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2223]
- Freud states that unconscious ignorance of the motivation of chance actions, which presses for recognition, forces allocation of this motivation to the external world through displacement. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2224]
- Freud proposes that much of mythological and religious thinking represents psychology projected into the external world—the 'endopsychic perception' of unconscious factors reflected in supernatural constructions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2224]
- Freud connects superstition in obsessional neurotics to suppressed hostile and cruel impulses—the expectation of trouble serves as punishment for unconscious wicked wishes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2225]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mythological view of the world, including concepts of paradise, God, good and evil, and immortality, may be explained as psychology projected outward, transformable back into the psychology of the unconscious.
