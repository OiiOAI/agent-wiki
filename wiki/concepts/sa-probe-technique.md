# SA Probe Assessment Technique

## Summary

The SA probe assessment technique uses psychophysiological measures to identify specific times when other SA measures should be applied. Because physiological data are continuously available, changes in operator physiology (such as increased heart rate or EEG changes) can serve as markers indicating that something important occurred. These physiological markers can be used to time subjective queries or debrief discussions about SA, and can also be applied online to initiate inquiries about noteworthy events.

## Key facts

- Transitory changes in heart rate during flight testing have been used as a debriefing tool where increases in heart rate were used to focus discussion on significant episodes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p182]
- The same strategy can determine when best to query operators about their SA during a mission. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p182]
- Sudden changes in physiological measures can serve as markers indicating that something of importance happened to the operator, whether external (information from displays) or internal (realized critical situation). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p182]
- This activity may be used online to initiate an inquiry to determine whether anything noteworthy had been detected. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p182]

## Inferences

- Inference: The probe technique may be especially valuable for understanding SA in complex real-world environments where critical events are unpredictable.
