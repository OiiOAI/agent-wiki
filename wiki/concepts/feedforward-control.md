# Feedforward Control

## Summary

Feedforward control generates motor commands without using sensory feedback, based on an internal model of the system's dynamics. Also called open-loop or ballistic control, it is essential for rapid movements that are too fast for feedback correction due to sensorimotor delays of approximately 200 ms.

## Key facts

- Feedforward commands are generated without regard to the consequences and are termed open-loop because the sensorimotor loop is not completed by sensory feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p805]
- The combined sensorimotor loop delay is approximately 200 ms for a response to a visual stimulus, meaning rapid movements such as eye saccades lasting less than 100 ms cannot use sensory feedback to guide them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p805]
- Open-loop control is advantageous for rapid movements and for systems with simple, well-understood dynamics like the vestibulo-ocular reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p807]
- A disadvantage of open-loop control is that any movement errors caused by inaccuracies in planning or execution will not be corrected. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p806]
