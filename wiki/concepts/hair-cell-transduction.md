# Hair Cell Transduction

## Summary

Hair cells transform mechanical energy into neural signals through mechanoelectrical transduction. Deflection of the hair bundle opens or closes mechanically sensitive ion channels. Displacement toward the tall edge opens channels and depolarizes the cell; displacement toward the short edge closes channels and hyperpolarizes. The tip link, composed of cadherin-23 and protocadherin-15, connects stereocilia and tensions the gating spring. TMC1 and TMC2 proteins form the pore of the transduction channel.

## Key facts

- Hair bundle deflection toward the tall edge opens transduction channels and depolarizes the cell; deflection toward the short edge hyperpolarizes the cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p653]
- The transduction channel has a conductance of approximately 100 pS and is relatively nonselective for cations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p655]
- The transduction channel's pore is approximately 1.3 nm in diameter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p655]
- The upper two-thirds of the tip link consists of cadherin-23, while the lower third consists of protocadherin-15. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p658]
- TMC1 forms at least part of the pore of the transduction channel in cochlear hair cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p660]
