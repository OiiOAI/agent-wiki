# Fixed vs. Growth Mindset

## Summary

Fixed mindset refers to the belief that important qualities such as intelligence and personality are fixed traits that cannot be changed. Growth mindset refers to the belief that these basic qualities are malleable and can be improved through learning and effort. People with fixed mindsets underperform in difficult courses relative to those who believe they can improve. A growth mindset can be developed through practices like praising effort rather than innate ability. Research by Becca Levy at Yale shows that mindsets about aging (viewing it as inevitable decline versus growth) affect health outcomes and longevity.

## Key facts

- Carol Dweck's research distinguishes fixed mindset (qualities are fixed traits) from growth mindset (qualities can be improved through effort). [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p116]
- Children with fixed mindsets about intelligence underperform in difficult courses compared to those who believe they can improve. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p116]
- Just identifying personal values helped protect minority students from absorbing cultural messages that they couldn't perform as well academically. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p104]
- Becca Levy's research found that people with negative views on aging die about 7.5 years earlier than those more open to a positive future. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p118]
- Negative views on aging were associated with twice the risk of heart attack or stroke, even after controlling for known risk factors. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p118]

- Inference: Mindset interventions can produce measurable physiological changes without behavioral modification.
