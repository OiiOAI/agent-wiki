# Hypothalamic-Pituitary-Adrenal Axis

## Summary

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis is the hormonal system that mediates stress responses. CRH neurons in the hypothalamus release CRH, which stimulates ACTH release from the pituitary, leading to cortisol release from the adrenal cortex. In approximately half of people with major depression, this feedback system is impaired and resistant to suppression.

## Key facts

- The HPA axis involves CRH neurons in the paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus that synthesize and release corticotropin-releasing hormone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1555]
- In approximately half of people with major depression, the HPA axis becomes resistant to suppression even by potent synthetic glucocorticoids. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1555]
- Many but not all individuals with major depression exhibit excess synthesis and secretion of cortisol. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1555]
- The HPA axis involves CRF from the hypothalamus stimulating ACTH from the pituitary, which stimulates cortisol release from the adrenal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1459]
- In approximately one-half of people with major depression the HPA axis feedback system is impaired and becomes resistant to suppression by synthetic glucocorticoids like dexamethasone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1460]
- Chronic increase in cortisol may contribute to symptoms of depression, as demonstrated by Cushing disease patients who often experience depression and insomnia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1459]
- Depression and chronic stress share biochemical changes including persistent activation of the HPA axis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1459]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text suggests a vicious cycle in which hippocampal damage could lead to loss of inhibitory control of CRH secretion, producing greater cortisol release and additional hippocampal atrophy, potentially explaining progressive hippocampal volume reduction observed in chronic depression.
