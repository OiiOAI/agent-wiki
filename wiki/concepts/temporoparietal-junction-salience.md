# Temporoparietal Junction in Salience Processing

## Summary

The temporoparietal junction (TPJ) has been identified as a key region for magnitude-based salience processing, with activity patterns correlating with the salience of stimuli predicting rewarding and punishing outcomes. The TPJ is part of a ventral attention network involved in mediating orientation of responses toward behaviorally relevant stimuli, and functional connectivity analyses suggest that TPJ salience signals are linked to activity in the locus coeruleus noradrenergic system.

## Key facts

- Activity patterns in the temporoparietal junction correlate with the salience of stimuli predicting rewarding and punishing outcomes of different magnitudes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p115]
- The TPJ responds to behaviorally relevant stimuli, even if these stimuli are of low visual salience or unattended. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p116]
- Functional connectivity analyses suggest that salience signals in the TPJ are functionally linked to activity in the locus coeruleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p116]
- The TPJ is part of a ventral attention network involved in mediating orientation of responses toward behaviorally relevant stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p116]

## Inferences

- Inference: The coupling between TPJ and locus coeruleus suggests that noradrenergic mechanisms support the TPJ's role in redirecting attention to behaviorally relevant stimuli.
