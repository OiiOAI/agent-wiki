# Myopia for the Future

## Summary

A concept describing how patients with prefrontal lobe damage become excessively concerned with immediate prospects while being unable to properly consider future consequences. Deprived of somatic marker guidance, these patients appear insensitive to the future, similar to individuals under the influence of alcohol or drugs, which also narrow the temporal horizon of decision-making.

## Key facts

- Patients with frontal lobe damage are controlled largely by immediate prospects and appear insensitive to the future, representing a profound exaggeration of a normal basic tendency to prefer immediate over delayed rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p217]
- The concept of myopia for the future has been proposed to explain the behavior of individuals under the influence of alcohol and other drugs, which narrow the panorama of future processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p218]
- Frontal lobe damage compromises not only the repository of knowledge for future-oriented guidance but also the ability to acquire new knowledge of this type. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p218]

## Inferences

- Inference: The inability to maintain somatic markers for future outcomes may explain why frontally damaged patients cannot learn from repeated punishment in the gambling task.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural mechanisms linking somatic marker failure to future myopia remain to be specified.
