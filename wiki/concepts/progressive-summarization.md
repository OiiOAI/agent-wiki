# Progressive Summarization

## Summary

Progressive Summarization is a note-taking technique that involves adding multiple layers of highlighting to notes over time, making them progressively more discoverable. The technique uses bolding and highlighting to distill the essence of notes into increasingly concise forms, allowing users to quickly grasp the main points of a source without rereading the entire original.

## Key facts

- Progressive Summarization uses four layers of highlighting to progressively distill notes. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p106]
- Layer one consists of initial excerpts captured in notes from sources. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p107]
- Layer two involves bolding the main points within a note to enhance discoverability. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p108]
- Layer three involves highlighting only the most interesting and surprising bolded passages. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p109]
- Layer four adds an executive summary at the top of especially valuable notes. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p110]
- Each layer of highlighting should include no more than 10-20 percent of the previous layer. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p124]
- Progressive Summarization is a method for forgetting as much as possible rather than remembering everything. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p123]

## Inferences

- Inference: Progressive Summarization transforms raw notes into increasingly valuable knowledge assets by prioritizing the most important information through visual hierarchy.
