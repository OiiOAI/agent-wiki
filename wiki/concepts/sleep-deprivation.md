# Sleep Deprivation and Attention

## Summary

Chronic sleep deprivation is a major cause of declining attention. Harvard researcher Charles Czeisler and others have demonstrated that being awake for 19 hours produces cognitive impairment equivalent to legal intoxication. Sleep deprivation causes 'attentional blinks' and 'local sleep,' where parts of the brain are asleep while appearing awake.

## Key facts

- 40 percent of Americans are chronically sleep-deprived, getting less than the necessary minimum of 7 hours per night. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p72]
- Being awake for 19 hours straight renders a person as cognitively impaired as someone who is legally drunk. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p72]
- Missing 2 hours of sleep nightly for just 1-2 weeks produces the same impairment as total sleep deprivation. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p75]
- During sleep, cerebrospinal fluid channels open to remove metabolic waste from the brain—the 'brain-cell poop' that accumulates during waking hours. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p78]
- The prefrontal cortex, responsible for judgment, is particularly sensitive to sleep loss and stops utilizing glucose effectively after just one night without sleep. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p78]
- 90 percent of Americans look at a glowing electronic device in the hour before bed, triggering wakefulness hormones at the wrong time. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p82]
- Average people today are exposed to 10 times the amount of artificial light compared to 50 years ago. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p82]
- Adults respond to tiredness by becoming drowsy, while children typically respond by becoming hyperactive, masking their underlying exhaustion. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p73]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sleep deprivation represents a form of attentional pathogenic culture that is systemically induced by modern society.
- Inference: Caffeine merely masks the feeling of sleep deprivation without addressing the underlying cognitive damage.
