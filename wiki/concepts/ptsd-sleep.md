# Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD) and Sleep

## Summary

PTSD can be understood as a disorder of sleep-dependent memory evolution, where traumatic memories fail to be properly processed during sleep. This results in raw, detailed, overwhelming emotional memories that remain intrusive and poorly integrated with broader understanding. A hallmark of PTSD is nightmares that are near-veridical replays of traumatic events—unlike normal dreams that capture gist and emotion rather than exact details.

## Key facts

- PTSD may be viewed as a disorder of sleep-dependent memory evolution, where trauma processing normally occurring during sleep fails to happen. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p90]
- A hallmark of PTSD is nightmares that are near-perfect replays of the traumatic event, which normally never happens in healthy dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p90]
- Sleep normally softens emotional responses when we recall memories, a crucial element of recovering from traumatic events. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p84]
