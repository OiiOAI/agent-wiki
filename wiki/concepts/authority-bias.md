# Authority Bias

## Summary

Authority Bias describes our tendency to defer to the opinions and decisions of authorities, even when these may be wrong or harmful. The chapter references Stanley Milgram's famous 1961 experiment demonstrating that people will administer lethal shocks when instructed by an authority figure.

## Key facts

- Authorities pose two main problems: first, their track records are often sobering; second, we defer to them even when they are wrong. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p30]
- In Milgram's 1961 experiment, more than half of participants went all the way up to maximum voltage of 450V out of sheer obedience to authority. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p30]
- Since the authority bias was discovered in aviation, nearly every airline has instituted 'Crew Resource Management' (CRM) which coaches pilots to challenge each other openly. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p31]
