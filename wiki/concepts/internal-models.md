# Internal Models of Motor Control

## Summary

Internal models are neural circuits that process the relation between hand motion and motor commands, enabling the brain to estimate environmental forces and guide movement. The concept was introduced by Reza Shadmehr and Ferdinando Mussa-Ivaldi in 1994 to explain motor memory formation.

## Key facts

- An inverse model embodies the relation between desired hand motion and motor outputs required to achieve that motion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p169]
- A forward model allows the brain to predict likely outcomes of motor commands and choose those necessary for specific motor tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p169]
- Henry constructed an internal model of the mechanical arm skill that allowed him to estimate forces and counterbalance their effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p174]
- When the mechanical arm's force was removed, Henry's movements showed large errors, indicating he had learned to compensate for the force. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: Henry could build new internal models despite his medial temporal-lobe damage, showing that motor memory formation occurs in circuits outside the hippocampus.
