# Grice's Maxims

## Summary

Grice's maxims are four principles of communication established by British philosopher Paul Grice that govern effective conversation and communication. The maxims cover quality (truthfulness), quantity (completeness), relation (relevance), and manner (clarity). Violations of these maxims can convey unintended implications, such as a short letter implying lack of enthusiasm.

## Key facts

- Paul Grice, a British philosopher, wrote about community expectations in communication and ordinary conversation. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p343]
- The maxim of quality states you should not lie and should not introduce speculations without identifying them as such. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p344]
- The maxim of quantity states you should be complete, supplying the level of detail your community expects. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p344]
- The maxim of relation states you should not clutter communication with distractions. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p344]
- The maxim of manner states you should be clear in your communication. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p344]
- Violating maxims deliberately conveys implication—for example, a too-short letter violates quantity and implies lack of enthusiasm. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p344]
