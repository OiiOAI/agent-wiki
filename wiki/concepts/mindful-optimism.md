# Mindful Optimism

## Summary

Mindful optimism is Langer's proposed attitude toward uncertainty and risk. It involves recognizing that uncertainty is not new—everything has always been uncertain—and choosing to relax rather than worry, since worry is psychologically and physically costly. It is contrasted with defensive pessimism, which keeps people on lookout for the negative and creates chronic stress.

## Key facts

- Mindful optimism involves recognizing that uncertainty is not new and that things can turn out good or bad; worrying beforehand prepares us no better than not worrying. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p47-48]
- Langer proposes that if we relax and things turn out badly, we are stronger to deal with it, and if everything turns out fine, we can continue behaving adaptively. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p48]
- She distinguishes mindful optimism from burying one's head in the sand—it involves implementing useful plans (handwashing, masks during COVID-19) while living fully moment by moment with the expectation that all will be fine. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p48]
- Defensive pessimism is characterized as a losing strategy because 'events are neither good nor bad; it is our thoughts that make them so.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p47]
