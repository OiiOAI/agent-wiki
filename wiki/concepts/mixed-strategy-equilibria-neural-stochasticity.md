# Mixed strategy equilibria and neural stochasticity

## Summary

The Poisson stochasticity of neurons in decision circuits may provide a neural substrate for mixed strategy equilibria. When the relative subjective values (RSVs) of two options are equivalent, the random variation in neural firing may yield stochastic choice behavior. This connects neural noise properties to economic concepts of the trembling hand and mixed strategy equilibria.

## Key facts

- Poisson variance of LIP neurons may serve a useful function by allowing for stochasticity in behavior under conditions where mixed strategy equilibria arise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p499]
- When RSVs of two options are equivalent, the stochastic nature of these neurons may yield mixed-strategy behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p499]
- Neural stochasticity in LIP may be related to economic notions of the trembling hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p499]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mixed strategy equilibria in game theory may be instantiated through Poisson noise in neural decision circuits when options have equal value.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether adjustable inter-neuronal correlations regulate the degree of stochasticity in these populations requires further study.
