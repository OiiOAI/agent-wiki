# Emotional Intelligence

## Summary

Emotional intelligence (EI), viewed through the lens of constructed emotion theory, is about getting your brain to construct the most useful instance of the most useful emotion concept in a given situation. People with a classical view mindset think about EI as detecting emotions accurately or experiencing happiness and avoiding sadness at the right time. In contrast, EI as construction means having a rich set of concepts to choose from. Daniel Goleman argues that higher EI leads to greater success, but science still has no generally accepted definition or measure of EI. The key to EI is gaining new emotion concepts and honing existing ones through experiences like travel, reading, and learning new words.

- Daniel Goleman argues that for star performance in all jobs, emotional competence is twice as important as purely cognitive abilities. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p200]
- Science still has no generally accepted definition or measure of EI. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p200]
- The key to EI is gaining new emotion concepts and honing existing ones. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p201]
- In 1990, psychologists Peter Salovey and John Mayer introduced the first formal theory of emotional intelligence to the scientific literature. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p27]
- Salovey and Mayer defined emotional intelligence as 'the ability to monitor one's own and others' feelings and emotions, to discriminate among them and to use this information to guide one's thinking and actions.'. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p27]
- Emotional intelligence is associated with better relationships, less depression and anxiety among adolescents, higher SAT scores, greater creativity, and better grades. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p60-61]
- Research links emotional intelligence to important health and workplace outcomes including less anxiety, depression, stress, and burnout and greater performance and leadership ability. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p61]
- Emotional intelligence has been found to be a predictor of academic success above grit, a well-known predictor of achievement. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p61]
