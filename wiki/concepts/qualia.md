# Qualia

## Summary

Qualia are the subjective, internal experiences of how sensory inputs are perceived—how seeing feels different from hearing, or why red appears red. In the Thousand Brains Theory, qualia are properties of the brain's model of the world, not properties of the external physical world. Some qualia, like color, may be learned through movement and sensorimotor experience.

## Key facts

- Nerve fibers entering the brain from eyes, ears, and skin look identical and transmit information using identical spikes, yet vision feels different from touch, demonstrating that qualia are created by the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p133]
- The dress illusion demonstrates that qualia are not purely properties of physical reality—identical visual input can result in different perceptions of color depending on the brain's model. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p134]
- The redness of a fire truck is a fabrication of the brain—while related to light frequencies, what we perceive as red is not always the same frequency due to varying lighting conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p134]
- Color qualia may be learned via movement—when we move a green paper and see how light changes, the brain predicts how light will change based on learned surface models. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p134]
- Qualia are the subjective, phenomenal qualities of conscious experience—what it is 'like' to be in a conscious state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p226]
- Nagel argued that being a bat is like being a bat, and humans could never truly comprehend it because experiences and qualia are different. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p226]
- Chalmers distinguished 'easy problems' (how the brain processes information) from the 'hard problem' (why physical processes give rise to subjective experience). [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p227]
- Some philosophers argue mind is not simply what the brain does but ultimately belongs to a nonphysical realm distinct from the physical world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p227]
- Llinás defines qualia as the subjective experience of any type generated by the nervous system, whether pain, color, or musical timbre. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p205]
- Llinás argues that qualia must be primordial in the global organization of nervous system function, not an accidental emergent property limited to higher lifeforms. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p207]
- Penfield's experiments showed that specific sensory experiences could be elicited by electrical stimulation of cortical areas, and that application of barbiturates abolished sensation without changing anatomy. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p209]
- Qualia disappear during non-REM sleep (characterized by slow wave 0.5–4Hz delta activity) and petit mal seizures, indicating specific frequency ranges of brain activity are required for qualia. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p211]
- The Weber-Fechner law (s = k·ln A/Ao) mathematically describes qualia as the logarithm of sensory activation. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p219]
- Llinás proposes qualia represent 'internalized FAPs'—sensory components that produce subjective experience internally rather than overt motor output. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p212]
- Qualia are soluble in local anesthetics and can be disrupted by a 'whack on the head,' indicating they are not transcendent properties but closely tied to biological processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p222]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that different people can perceive the same input differently shows that qualia are constructed internally rather than directly representing external reality.
- Inference: LeDoux takes a physicalist position, assuming brain mechanisms that contribute to consciousness are all that is needed to explain consciousness.
- Inference: The parallel Llinás draws between muscle contraction and qualia—both triggered electrically, both involving summing properties of many cells, both affected by specific drugs—suggests qualia may be understood through the same reductionist approach applied to motor physiology.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which qualia are generated remain to be fully understood, though the theory suggests they emerge from the brain's modeling activity.
- Uncertain: Whether subjective experience can ever be fully explained in physical terms remains philosophically contested.
- Uncertain: Whether qualia exist in invertebrates like octopuses remains debated; Llinás argues the principle of parsimony suggests we should assume they do until proven otherwise.
