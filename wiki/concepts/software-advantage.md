# Software's Competitive Advantage

## Summary

Rumelt argues that software became a sharp source of competitive advantage because microprocessors in everything from PCs to thermostats meant that programming determined device performance. The key advantage is the rapidity of the software development cycle: design errors in hardware can mean months of expensive redesign, while software errors are fixed by typing new instructions and recompiling in minutes or days. This made software the preferred medium except where cutting-edge hardware speed was required.

## Key facts

- The microprocessor could be produced in high volume and specialized with software, allowing many customers to use the same general-purpose chip with proprietary behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p71]
- Andy Grove stated that the Intel 4004 gave Intel its future, and for the first fifteen years they didn't realize it—it was seen as a 'sideshow.'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p71]
- The big difference between hardware and software is the cost of prototyping, upgrading, and especially fixing mistakes—hardware trials are much more costly. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p71]
- If a hardware design doesn't work correctly, it can mean months of expensive redesign; if software doesn't work, a software engineer fixes it by typing new instructions and trying again in minutes or days. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p71]
- Software's advantage comes from the rapidity of the software development cycle—the process of moving from concept to prototype and finding and correcting errors. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p71]
