# Deliberate Practice

## Summary

Deliberate practice is K. Anders Ericsson's theory that expert performance comes from a life-long period of deliberate effort to improve performance in a specific domain. It requires focused attention on a specific skill with feedback for correction. The theory destabilizes myths of innate talent, arguing that differences between expert performers and normal adults reflect deliberate practice, not immutable differences.

## Key facts

- Deliberate practice requires that your attention is focused tightly on a specific skill you're trying to improve. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- Deliberate practice requires receiving feedback so you can correct your approach to keep your attention exactly where it's most productive. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- Ericsson emphasized that diffused attention is almost antithetical to the focused attention required by deliberate practice. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- To learn hard things quickly, you must focus intensely without distraction because deliberate practice cannot exist alongside distraction. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- Anders Ericsson's research on deliberate practice (2008) is cited as the basis for the claim that focused, purposeful improvement is the only serious path to expertise. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p39]
- Reading with a pen in hand forces comprehension checking: if you cannot rewrite something in your own words, you do not understand it. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p54]
- The ability to distinguish the gist from supporting details in texts is a skill that improves only through deliberate practice—selective note-taking provides this practice multiple times per day. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p79]
- Mueller and Oppenheimer (2014) found that students taking handwritten notes during lectures showed significantly better understanding than laptop typists, even after one week. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p75]
- Handwriting forces focus on gist because the writer cannot keep up with everything verbatim, which paradoxically produces deeper understanding. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p75]
- The Dreyfus model of skill acquisition distinguishes five levels: novice, advanced beginner, competent, proficient, expert; following rules only reaches level 3 (competent). [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p65]
- Flyvbjerg's research on expert vs. beginner paramedics showed that teachers systematically confused experts with beginners because teachers focused on rule-following rather than situational judgment. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p65]
- Deliberate practice requires effort and is the only way to genuinely improve understanding and learning. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p82]
- Writing down ideas feels like a detour, but not writing them down is the real waste of time. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p82]
- The attempt to rephrase an argument in our own words confronts us with all the gaps in our understanding. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p82]
- Learning requires thinking to understand and actively retrieving old knowledge to connect it with new ideas. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p84]
- Teachers who prearrange and sort information into modules actually make learning harder because they eliminate the opportunity to build meaningful connections. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p84]
