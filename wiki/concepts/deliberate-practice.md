# Deliberate Practice

## Summary

Deliberate practice is K. Anders Ericsson's theory that expert performance comes from a life-long period of deliberate effort to improve performance in a specific domain. It requires focused attention on a specific skill with feedback for correction. The theory destabilizes myths of innate talent, arguing that differences between expert performers and normal adults reflect deliberate practice, not immutable differences.

## Key facts

- Deliberate practice requires that your attention is focused tightly on a specific skill you're trying to improve. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- Deliberate practice requires receiving feedback so you can correct your approach to keep your attention exactly where it's most productive. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- Ericsson emphasized that diffused attention is almost antithetical to the focused attention required by deliberate practice. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
- To learn hard things quickly, you must focus intensely without distraction because deliberate practice cannot exist alongside distraction. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p23]
