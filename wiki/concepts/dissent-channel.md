# Dissent Channel

## Summary

The Dissent Channel is a formal mechanism at the U.S. State Department, established since the Vietnam War, where employees can have their dissenting views heard and addressed without fear of penalty. It has been credited with policy changes that helped end the Bosnian War and represents an institutional implementation of John Stuart Mill's principle that truth requires hearing all sides. Red teams at the CIA similarly dedicate themselves to arguing against conventional wisdom.

## Key facts

- The State Department has had a formal Dissent Channel since the Vietnam War, where employees can have dissenting views heard without fear of penalty. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1350-L1355]
- The Dissent Channel has been credited with a policy change that helped end the genocidal war in Bosnia. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1350-L1355]
- The CIA created 'red teams' dedicated to arguing against the intelligence community's conventional wisdom and spotting flaws in logic and analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1355-L1360]
- The Dissent Channel represents something hopeful in the nation's decision-making process, allowing dissent regardless of which party controls the administration. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1355-L1360]
- The Dissent Channel is codified in the Department of State's Foreign Affairs Manual, specifically 2 FAM 071-075.1. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1979-L1980]
- There are four awards for constructive dissent associated with the Dissent Channel. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1979-L1982]
- The Dissent Channel has been used in significant policy debates, including during the Obama and Trump administrations. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1979-L1980]
