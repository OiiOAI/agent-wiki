# Lightweight Fighter Program

## Summary

The Lightweight Fighter Program was a U.S. Air Force initiative to develop a small, highly maneuverable, inexpensive fighter aircraft. Secretly championed by John Boyd and the Fighter Mafia, it resulted in prototypes from General Dynamics (YF-16) and Northrop (YF-17). The program was a response to the gold-plated F-15 and represented Boyd's vision of a pure fighter aircraft.

## Key facts

- The lightweight fighter would be a 20,000-pound aircraft, half the weight of the F-15. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p243]
- General Dynamics received $49,000 and Northrop received $100,000 for initial design studies. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p244]
- The Air Force initially called it a 'short-legged' fighter based on absolute fuel amounts, not fuel fraction. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p258]
- The lightweight fighter actually had greater range than the F-15 due to its superior fuel fraction. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p259]
- Secretary of Defense Laird approved the prototype program on April 13, 1972. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p262]
- The YF-16 won the lightweight fighter fly-off in January 1975, with pilots preferring it unanimously due to its ability to perform the 'buttonhook turn'—snapping from one maneuver to another faster than any aircraft previously flown. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p301]
- Boyd computed the Energy-Maneuverability data for both aircraft and planned the fly-off. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p291]
- Schlesinger insisted the lightweight fighter would remain an air-to-air fighter and would not be wired for nuclear weapons delivery as a condition for supporting the program. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p292]
- The Air Force 'missionized' the F-16 by adding approximately 3,000 pounds of electronics, a large ground-mapping radar, and hard points, turning the 'thoroughbred' into a 'draft horse.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p303]
- Boyd fought every change but ultimately turned his back on the F-16 when the Air Force refused to increase wing area from 300 to his preferred 320 square feet. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p304]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's secret knowledge that the lightweight fighter had greater range than the F-15 was held as a 'big stick' for future use against critics.
- Inference: Had Boyd's original version of the F-15 or the modified 'Red Bird' been accepted, the lightweight fighter would never have existed.
