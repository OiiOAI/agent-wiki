# Leverage Points

## Summary

Leverage points are small differences that make large differences—opportunities to create new courses of action, notice problems before obvious signs appear, and identify what is causing difficulties. They serve as starting points for insightful problem solving.

## Key facts

- A leverage point is a focus for building a solution, serving as the starting point for insightful problem solving. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p148]
- Leverage points provide fragmentary action sequences, kernel ideas, and procedures for formulating a solution. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p153]
- Experts have a larger stock of procedures they can use as starting points for building new plans or strategies, while novices are often at a loss about where to begin. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p153]
- Choke points are leverage points that work against us, identifying weaknesses in plans where a single failure could disrupt the entire schedule. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p153]
- In chess, Kasparov demonstrated 'creative desperation'—the drive to search for leverage points—while Deep Blue lacked this sense, searching for best moves even in lost positions. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p152]
- Numbers (parameters) are dead last in leverage—90-99% of attention goes to parameters but there's not much leverage in them. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p178]
- Buffers (stabilizing stocks relative to flows) have high leverage but are usually physical and hard to change. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p179]
- Delays in feedback loops are critical—if they're too short, they cause overreaction; if too long, they cause oscillations or collapse. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p182]
- Self-organization means changing any lower-level aspect—adding structures, loops, or rules—and is the strongest form of system resilience. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p190]
- Goals are higher leverage than self-organization—if the goal is wrong, everything else gets twisted to conform. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p192]
- Paradigms (the mind-set out of which the system arises) are the source of systems—from them come goals, information flows, and structures. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p193]
- The highest leverage point is transcending paradigms—keeping oneself unattached, realizing no paradigm is 'true'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p195]
- Twelve places to intervene in a system are ranked in order of effectiveness: Numbers/Constants/Parameters (12) is least effective, Buffers (11), Stock-and-Flow Structures (10), Delays (9), Balancing Feedback Loops (8), Reinforcing Feedback Loops (7), Information Flows (6), Rules (5), Self-Organization (4), Goals (3), Paradigms (2), and Transcending Paradigms (1) is most effective. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]
- Parameters are the least effective leverage points because they do not change the underlying structure of the system. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]
- Paradigms—the mind-set out of which the system arises—are among the most powerful leverage points because changing them changes the entire system. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]
- Transcending paradigms is listed as the highest-order intervention point, recognizing that even holding paradigms loosely is part of systems thinking. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]

## Inferences

- Inference: Leverage points can be conceptualized as 'affordances' in the ecological psychology sense—the perception of what actions the environment allows—though problem solving involves elaboration, evaluation, and modification rather than purely perceptual processes.
- Inference: Forrester's World model showed that slowing growth is more effective leverage than faster technology or freer prices, because physical capital changes slowly regardless of prices or ideas
