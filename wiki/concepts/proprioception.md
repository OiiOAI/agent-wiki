# Proprioception

## Summary

Proprioception is one of the three main balance systems, involving receptors embedded in muscles, joints, ligaments, and tendons that send information about the body's position and movements to the central nervous system, enabling protective muscle responses in milliseconds.

## Key facts

- Proprioceptors are particularly concentrated in the soles of the feet, and there is significant brain space devoted to processing information from the feet—comparable to the space for hand information. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2455-L2480]
- The time between sensory input and protective muscle movement through proprioception is just milliseconds. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2420-L2445]
- Proprioception helps basketball players dribble without looking down and soccer players execute kicks without falling. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2420-L2445]
- Proprioception has been shown to improve in people who regularly do tai chi, and a 1997 study found older people who exercised had better proprioception than those who did not. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2950-L2970]
- Muscle spindles consist of intrafusal muscle fibers entwined by sensory axons, with stretch-sensitive ion channels in nerve terminals that depolarize in response to muscle stretch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p534]
- Golgi tendon organs located between muscle and tendon measure forces generated during contraction but contribute little to conscious sensations of muscle activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p535]
- Joint receptors play little role in postural sensations; perception of joint angle depends on afferent signals from muscle spindles, efferent motor commands, and skin stretch receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p535]
- Perceived muscle force is mainly related to centrally generated effort rather than actual muscle force, as shown in experiments where muscles are fatigued or partially paralyzed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p535]

## Inferences

- Inference: Proprioception contributes the most to balance at all ages, making it a primary target for balance training interventions.
- Inference: The absence of proprioceptive feedback, as indeafferented patients, results in clumsy, poorly coordinated movements inadequate for complex tasks without visual guidance.
