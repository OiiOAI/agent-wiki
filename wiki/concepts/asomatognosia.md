# Asomatognosia

## Summary

Asomatognosia is a lack of capacity to recognise parts of the embodied self, always associated with right-hemisphere damage, never with left-hemisphere damage. Patients may deny that a limb belongs to them, attribute it to someone else, or experience alien control. The phenomenon can be replicated by selectively anaesthetising the right hemisphere.

## Key facts

- Asomatognosia is a lack of capacity to recognise parts of the embodied self and is always associated with right-hemisphere damage, never with left-hemisphere damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p104]
- A patient believed quite firmly that the paralysed arm belonged to her mother; the process was typically reversed by inhibiting the left hemisphere through vestibular stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p104]
- Asomatognosia can give rise to the belief that the affected part is under alien control; a patient described by Lhermitte showed no concern initially, then reported that an alien hand that 'doesn't belong to me' was placing itself on his chest. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p105]
- The phenomenon can be replicated by selectively anaesthetising the right hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p104]
- Asomatognosia is found in nearly 90 per cent of subjects following any right-hemisphere stroke; not once has it followed left-hemisphere damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p132]
- Asomatognosia involves loss of awareness of the muscle mass in limbs and torso while consciousness of being alive and knowing who one is remains intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p214]
- The condition results from temporary inactivation of somatosensory cortical complex in the right hemisphere, particularly S2 and adjacent SI regions, due to seizure activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p214]
- Internal milieu, visceral, and vestibular signaling continue to provide the foundation for the sense of being, enabling core consciousness to persist. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p215]
- Due to the right somatosensory cortex's dominance in integrating body information for the entire body, the defect pertains to both sides even with asymmetric right-hemisphere damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p215]

## Inferences

- Inference: The reversibility of asomatognosia through vestibular stimulation demonstrates the dynamic interplay between the hemispheres in constituting our sense of embodied self.
- Inference: The distinction between asomatognosia and anosognosia suggests that different levels of body representation contribute differently to the proto-self, with internal milieu and visceral representations being primary.
