# ON and OFF Retinal Ganglion Cells

## Summary

ON and OFF retinal ganglion cells are the two major functional types of ganglion cells that transmit opposite representations of the visual scene to the brain. ON cells fire more rapidly when a bright spot shines on their receptive field center; OFF cells fire more rapidly when a dark spot appears. This arrangement enables rapid communication of both brightening and dimming.

## Key facts

- ON cells fire more rapidly when a bright spot shines on the receptive field center but decrease firing when light covers the surround; OFF cells show the opposite pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p638]
- A bright spot on the center combined with a dark annulus on the surround elicits very strong firing in ON cells; the opposite pattern excites OFF cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p638]
- The ON and OFF pathways are established at the level of bipolar cells: ON bipolar cells use metabotropic glutamate receptors that close cation channels in darkness, while OFF bipolar cells use ionotropic receptors that depolarize in darkness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p642]
- ON and OFF bipolar cell axons terminate in different levels of the inner plexiform layer, with ON cells ending proximally and OFF cells ending distally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p642]

## Inferences

- Inference: The parallel ON and OFF pathways allow the retina to encode both increases and decreases in illumination simultaneously, rather than requiring time to switch states.
