# True Self and False Self

## Summary

The true self represents an individual's authentic identity developed through the integration of experiences and values in alignment with one's inherent interests and potentials. The false self emerges when controlling environments force acceptance of an alien identity, resulting in rigid introjects that dictate behavior rather than authentic values guiding it. Alice Miller's work on 'The Drama of the Gifted Child' informs the discussion of how false self develops as children accept the identity that controlling caretakers want them to have.

## Key facts

- The intrinsic self is a set of potentials, interests, and capabilities that interact with the world, and at any given time, self is the developmental outcome of this dialectical relationship. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p121]
- True self develops as the social world supports the individual's activity and provides noncontingent acceptance and love. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p122]
- False self develops as children accept the identity that controlling caretakers want them to have, in an attempt to please their parents and gain contingent love. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p123]
- Introjection facilitates the emergence of a false self—a set of rigid rules and identities—through which people can lose contact with who they really are. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p123]
