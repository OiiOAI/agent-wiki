# Goal-Directed Task Analysis

## Summary

Goal-Directed Task Analysis (GDTA) is a form of cognitive task analysis used to identify situation awareness requirements. It focuses on the goals the operator must accomplish, the decisions they must make to achieve those goals, and the information requirements needed to make appropriate decisions. The GDTA is technology-agnostic—it determines what dynamic information operators need without focusing on how they currently obtain it. A completed GDTA provides the fundamental foundation for creating systems that support and enhance SA.

## Key facts

- GDTA focuses on goals the operator must accomplish, decisions they must make, and information requirements needed for those decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p83]
- GDTA focuses on dynamic information requirements rather than static system knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p83]
- GDTA is technology-agnostic—it determines what operators need to know without focusing on how they currently obtain information. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p83]
- GDTA seeks to determine what operators would ideally like to know, even if not available with current technology. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p83]
- Developing a GDTA involves interviewing experienced operators to identify goals, decisions, and SA requirements. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p85]
- Begin organizing notes into a preliminary GDTA as soon as possible after the interview while the conversation is still fresh in memory. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p98]
- Number the pages of a GDTA draft to minimize confusion and facilitate discussion during collaborative development sessions. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p98]
- SA requirement levels are not concrete but provide an aid to ensure designers consider not only what information is needed, but also how the information is going to be used. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p98]
- Creating a comprehensive GDTA for a particular job takes extensive time and typically requires 3–10 sessions with subject matter experts. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p98]
- Validate GDTA by distributing copies to as many experts as possible with instructions and a request to identify missing information or errors. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p98]
- Observation of actual or simulated operations allows the interviewer to trace observed actions, statements, and activities back to sections of the GDTA. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p99]
- Goal-Directed Task Analysis (GDTA) is used to identify situation awareness information requirements for commercial airline pilots. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- The top-level mission goal for commercial pilots is to get aircraft from origin to destination safely, legally, with satisfactory comfort and service, on schedule, and efficiently. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- Goal 1.0 involves selecting the best path to destination, broken down into assessing flight plan, determining changes to flight plan, and replanning flight path. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- Goal 2.0 involves executing the desired flight path safely, efficiently, and with ride comfort, including avoiding conflicts and hazardous weather. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- Goal 3.0 involves managing resources effectively, including tailoring operations to colleague competency, keeping everyone informed, configuring aircraft for phases of flight and weather, and minimizing system abnormality impacts. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- Goal 4.0 involves satisfying the customer through providing safety and security and providing customer service. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- The GDTA addresses abnormal situations including bird strikes, runway excursions, fuel mismanagement, unusual attitudes, stalls, overspeed, structural damage, and medical emergencies. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- The GDTA addresses abnormal ATC situations including ATC outage, loss of communication, busted clearances, and aircraft in distress. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p318]
- Goal-Directed Task Analysis identifies major goals of a job class along with major subgoals necessary for meeting each goal. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p164]
- GDTA is based on goals or objectives, not tasks, because goals form the basis for decision making and tasks tend to be technology dependent. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p165]
- SA requirements are defined as dynamic information needs associated with major goals or subgoals, excluding static knowledge such as rules, procedures, and general system knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p164]
- The analysis strives to be technology-free, not addressing how information is acquired since this can vary from person to person, system to system, and time to time. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p165]
- Analyses can take as much as a person-year of effort to complete for a given domain but need not be repeated unless goals and objectives change radically. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p167]
- GDTA has been completed for en route air traffic control, TRACON air traffic control, fighter pilots, bomber pilots, commercial transport pilots, aircraft mechanics, and airway facilities maintenance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p167]

## Inferences

- Inference: The GDTA provides the SA information requirements analysis that forms the basis for SAGAT query development in system evaluation
- Inference: The technology-free nature of GDTA makes the resulting SA requirements analysis applicable across different system implementations, enabling comparison of design alternatives.
- Inference: Because goals and objectives change less frequently than technology, the investment in GDTA provides long-term value for multiple system evaluations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise amount of person-year effort required varies by domain complexity; the text indicates this as a general guideline rather than a precise measure.
