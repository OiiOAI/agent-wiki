# Planum Temporale

## Summary

A component of Wernicke's area in the brain associated with language input comprehension. This region shows significant asymmetry in humans, being larger on the left side than the right, with unique microscopic architecture not found in other primates.

## Key facts

- The planum temporale is larger on the left side than the right in humans, chimpanzees, and rhesus monkeys, but is microscopically unique in the left hemisphere of humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p32]
- In humans, cortical minicolumns of the planum temporale are larger and the area between columns is wider on the left side than the right, while in chimps and rhesus monkeys the columns and intercolumnar spaces are the same size on both sides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p32]
- The increased intercolumnar space in the left planum temporale may indicate additional constituents or a more elaborate local processing architecture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p36]
