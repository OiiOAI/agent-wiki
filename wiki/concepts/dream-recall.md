# Dream Recall

## Summary

Dream recall refers to the ability to remember dreams upon waking. The vast majority of adults (85-90 percent) report dreaming, and studies show most 'non-dreamers' actually do dream but simply don't remember. Dream reports vary in frequency, vividness, and detail depending on sleep stage, time of night, and individual differences.

## Key facts

- The vast majority of adults (85-90 percent) report that they dream. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p97]
- Studies show most people who say they never dream actually do, reporting dreams when awakened from REM sleep in sleep labs. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p97]
- Approximately 0.5 percent of adults may genuinely never remember dreams, though it is unclear if they never dream or cannot remember. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p97]
- The inability to recall a dream does not prove that dreaming did not occur, as supported by delayed recall reports. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p67]
