# Man-with-a-Hammer Syndrome

## Summary

Man-with-a-hammer syndrome is Munger's term for the cognitive bias where people with limited expertise overapply their single tool or method to all problems, ignoring alternatives. The folk saying is: 'To the man with only a hammer, every problem looks pretty much like a nail.'

## Key facts

- Man-with-a-hammer syndrome creates a situation where to the man with only a hammer, every problem looks pretty much like a nail. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p389]
- The antidote for man-with-a-hammer syndrome is having a full kit of tools and using them checklist-style. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p389]
- Munger attributes the origin of 'man-with-a-hammer syndrome' to the folk saying about hammers and nails. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p412]
- Alfred North Whitehead described 'fatal unconnectedness of academic disciplines' as each professor not knowing models of other disciplines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p411]
- Munger calls this approach 'bonkers' and considers it a 'perfectly crazy way to behave.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p411]
- B.F. Skinner lost much of his reputation by overclaiming incentive superpower, behaving like a man-with-a-hammer despite his brilliance. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p504]
- The antidote is having a full kit of tools and using them checklist-style rather than hoping the right tool pops up unaided. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p412]
