# Jack Knetsch

## Summary

Jack Knetsch is a Canadian economist who collaborated with Richard Thaler and Daniel Kahneman on key studies of the endowment effect, fairness norms, and the methodological differences between experimental economics and psychology. His research on trade willingness and fairness demonstrated that the endowment effect could be manipulated by changing whether subjects physically possessed goods before being offered to trade them.

## Key facts

- Knetsch conducted experiments where students received either an expensive pen or a bar of Swiss chocolate and were then offered to exchange. Only about 10% chose to exchange, demonstrating the endowment effect for hedonic goods even in the absence of market context. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p383]
- Knetsch, Thaler, and Kahneman used Vernon Smith's double-blind market method to demonstrate that the endowment effect disappears for goods held 'for exchange' but persists for goods held 'for use,' providing a clean experimental distinction between market behavior and ordinary preference. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p381]
- Knetsch also demonstrated that the endowment effect disappears when participants are given the alternative good before the choice is presented, showing that physical possession itself—not just ownership—creates the psychological reference point that generates loss aversion. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p384]

## Inferences

- Inference: Knetsch's finding that mere physical possession creates an endowment effect suggests that the loss aversion underlying the endowment effect is triggered automatically by the mental representation of the current state as a reference point, not by legal or social ownership claims.
