# Ion Channels

## Summary

Ion channels are multiprotein complexes spanning cell membranes that form pores allowing specific chemical ions to pass. They are regulated either by binding to particular chemicals like neurotransmitters (ligand-gated) or in response to voltage changes across the membrane (voltage-gated). The combined action of these channel types is responsible for synaptic transmission and the generation of electrical action potentials or spikes.

## Key facts

- Ion channels are multiprotein complexes that span the cell membrane, forming a pore through which certain chemical ions can pass. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Ligand-gated channels open in response to binding of specific chemicals like neurotransmitters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Voltage-gated channels open in response to voltage changes across the membrane, creating an amplifying loop that can generate an electrical pulse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Opening voltage-gated channels allows ions to flow down concentration gradients, changing membrane voltage and opening more channels in a cascade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Ion channels have intrinsic mechanisms that shut them very quickly followed by a refractory period during which they cannot open again. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Ion channels have three important properties: they recognize and select specific ions, they open and close in response to specific electrical, mechanical, or chemical signals, and they conduct ions across the membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p152]
- Up to 100 million ions can pass through a single channel each second, comparable to the turnover rate of the fastest enzymes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p152]
- Voltage-gated channels are regulated by changes in membrane potential; ligand-gated channels by chemical transmitters; mechanically gated channels by pressure or stretch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p160]
- The human genome contains nine genes encoding voltage-gated Na+ channels, 10 for Ca+ channels, over 75 for K+ channels, 70 for ligand-gated channels, and more than a dozen for Cl- channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p165]
- K+ channels have a selectivity filter formed by carbonyl oxygen atoms from four amino acid residues per subunit that compensates for the loss of K+ ion waters of hydration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p170]
