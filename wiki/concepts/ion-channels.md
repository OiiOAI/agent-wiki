# Ion Channels

## Summary

Ion channels are multiprotein complexes spanning cell membranes that form pores allowing specific chemical ions to pass. They are regulated either by binding to particular chemicals like neurotransmitters (ligand-gated) or in response to voltage changes across the membrane (voltage-gated). The combined action of these channel types is responsible for synaptic transmission and the generation of electrical action potentials or spikes.

## Key facts

- Ion channels are multiprotein complexes that span the cell membrane, forming a pore through which certain chemical ions can pass. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Ligand-gated channels open in response to binding of specific chemicals like neurotransmitters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Voltage-gated channels open in response to voltage changes across the membrane, creating an amplifying loop that can generate an electrical pulse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Opening voltage-gated channels allows ions to flow down concentration gradients, changing membrane voltage and opening more channels in a cascade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
- Ion channels have intrinsic mechanisms that shut them very quickly followed by a refractory period during which they cannot open again. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p98]
