# Mirror Tracing

## Summary

Mirror tracing is a motor-skill learning task used in memory research where participants trace a figure while viewing it only through a mirror, which reverses the visual image. This task was used by Brenda Milner to demonstrate that Henry Molaison could learn new motor skills despite his profound anterograde amnesia.

## Key facts

- In mirror tracing, a five-pointed star is hidden from direct view by a metal barrier; participants see only its mirror image and must guide their pencil to stay within the star's borders. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p154]
- Henry improved dramatically over three days of mirror-tracing practice, yet on days two and three had no memory of having done the task before. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p154]
- This experiment was the first formal demonstration that a learning deficit in amnesia is restricted to declarative memory while nondeclarative motor learning remains intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p155]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mirror tracing is now a standard test for distinguishing between declarative and nondeclarative memory systems.
