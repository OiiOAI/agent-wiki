# Psychology of Misjudgment

## Summary

The psychology of misjudgment refers to the systematic cognitive errors that humans make in perception and reasoning. Charlie Munger considers this the most practical and important part of psychology, noting that very smart people make totally bonkers mistakes by failing to pay heed to it. The concept encompasses shortcuts, biases, and automatic mental processes that lead to wrong conclusions.

- Munger states: 'The most useful and practical part of psychology—which I personally think can be taught to any intelligent person in a week—is ungodly important.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p163]
- Pascal observed: 'The mind of man at one and the same time is both the glory and the shame of the universe.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p164]
