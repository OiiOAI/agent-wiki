# Prosopagnosia

## Summary

Prosopagnosia is the inability to recognise faces, following right-hemisphere lesions. It reflects the right hemisphere's crucial ability to see the 'configurational' aspects of the whole face, as opposed to the left hemisphere's laborious process of trying to assemble the face from parts.

## Key facts

- An inability to recognise faces is called prosopagnosia, and follows right-hemisphere lesions. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p93]
- In prosopagnosia, the right hemisphere's capacity to see the 'configurational' aspects of the whole is lacking; in its absence the left hemisphere has to rely on the laborious process of trying to put the face together from the parts. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p94]
- A patient reported that faces seemed to him 'strangely flat, white with emphatic dark eyes, as if made from a flat surface, like white oval plates, all alike'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p94]
- Prosopagnosia was linked to right-hemisphere lesions by Sergent and Villemure, who recognised an 'inability to combine the component features into a configurational facial representation that would uniquely define each face'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p94]
- There is a specific defect for recognition of eyes in prosopagnosia; subjects with partial prosopagnosia following right-hemisphere lesion could glean evidence from the mouth area but were quite unable to use information from the eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p95]
- Wigan achieved recognition of prosopagnosia in 1844, but it took another hundred years for it to be given a name; in 1947 Joachim Bodamer described the condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p94]
- Prosopagnosia is the inability to recognize faces, a deficit produced by brain damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p181]
- The affected person often cannot recognize his own face in a photograph or mirror, although he knows it must be his face because it winks when he winks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p181]
- He can usually recognize his wife from her voice or the way she walks, but not just from seeing her face. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p181]
- Unless the damage is extreme, he can describe the features of a face—the eyes, nose, mouth—and how they are related to each other. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p181]
- Damasio showed that patients could not recognize individual members of a class of similar objects—for example, one patient could recognize a car but not whether it was a Ford or Rolls-Royce. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p182]
- Damasio and colleagues discovered that some patients, while they cannot recognize the identity of a face, can recognize the meaning of facial expressions and can also estimate age and gender. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p182]
- These results suggest that different aspects of facial recognition are handled in different parts of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p182]
- Prosopagnosia often accompanies achromatopsia when the latter is bilateral. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p181]

## Inferences

- Inference: Prosopagnosia probably has many varieties because the exact nature of brain damage varies from patient to patient.
- Inference: Prosopagnosia is not a general disorder of memory, since such recognition can be triggered through other sensory channels.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms underlying each type of prosopagnosia case remain to be discovered.
