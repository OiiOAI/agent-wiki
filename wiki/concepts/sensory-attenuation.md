# Sensory Attenuation

## Summary

Sensory attenuation is the phenomenon where, to initiate a new movement, we need to attenuate the precision of proprioceptive predictions. This allows us to maintain the initially false belief that we are moving so that this belief can be realized through action. Technically, this gain is the precision (inverse variance) with which sensory data are predicted. Failures to attenuate may underlie passivity phenomena and catatonic states.

## Key facts

- To initiate a new movement, we need a way to preclude sensory data from updating our expectations, so we can entertain the initially false belief that we are moving. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
- Sensory attenuation is the complement to sensory attention, equipping us with the capacity to ignore certain prediction errors such as those generated by saccadic eye movements (saccadic suppression). [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
- Failures to attenuate are thought to be central to passivity phenomena, catatonic states in schizophrenia, and failure to initiate movements in Parkinson's disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
- Sensory attenuation requires descending motor tracts to predict not just the data but also the precision of those data, decreasing this precision to initiate movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
