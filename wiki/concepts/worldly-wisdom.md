# Worldly Wisdom

## Summary

Worldly wisdom, as Munger defines it, refers to practical knowledge and judgment acquired through the study of multiple disciplines and experience. It goes beyond academic expertise to encompass understanding of human nature, history, and the practical application of ideas across contexts.

## Key facts

- Munger's dedication states: 'Acquire worldly wisdom and adjust your behavior accordingly.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p3]
- One of Munger's talks is titled 'A Lesson on Elementary, Worldly Wisdom as It Relates to Investment Management and Business.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p2]
- Munger juxtaposes historical figures like Ben Franklin with others to illustrate contrasts in worldly wisdom. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p4]
- Modern educational systems deliver too little worldly wisdom in an effective way. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p162]
- Worldly wisdom requires multiple models from multiple disciplines because all wisdom is not found in one academic department. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p164]
- The basic models for worldly wisdom include mathematics (probability, permutations, combinations), accounting, psychology, and microeconomics. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p165]
- The psychology of misjudgment is the most useful and practical part of psychology—though nobody taught it to Munger and he had to learn it one piece at a time. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: The importance of worldly wisdom explains Munger's emphasis on multidisciplinary thinking and his criticism of academic specialization that prevents cross-disciplinary synthesis.
