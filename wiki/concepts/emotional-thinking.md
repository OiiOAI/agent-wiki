# Emotional Thinking

## Summary

Emotional thinking is the Chimp's mode of processing information, characterized by basing conclusions on feelings and impressions rather than facts. This type of thinking tends to be fast, irrational, and often leads to jumping to conclusions, catastrophizing, and paranoid interpretations of events.

## Key facts

- Emotional thinking is based on impressions and feelings rather than facts, with the Chimp making guesses and filling in details with assumptions. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p19]
- The Chimp jumps to an opinion based on feelings and then searches for evidence to back up that opinion, twisting facts to fit. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p19]
- Emotional thinking results in black-and-white thinking without appreciation for shades of grey or alternative interpretations. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p19]
- The Chimp is prone to paranoia, constantly scanning for danger and interpreting neutral situations as threatening. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p20]
- Emotional thinking tends toward catastrophic thinking, overreacting to situations and fueling them with high intensity emotion. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p20]
- Typical Chimp phrases indicating emotional thinking include 'But what if…?' and 'But I feel…'. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p36]

## Inferences

- Inference: Emotional thinking served an evolutionary purpose in the jungle by enabling quick reactions to perceived threats.
