# Postural Control

## Summary

Postural control involves maintaining balance (equilibrium) and body orientation. Balance requires controlling the body's center of mass within the base of support, while orientation involves positioning body segments relative to each other and the environment. Automatic postural responses are coordinated muscle activations triggered by sensory feedback with latencies of 40-120 ms. Multiple sensory modalities (somatosensory, vestibular, visual) are integrated to generate appropriate responses.

## Key facts

- Maintaining balance requires keeping the downward projection of the center of mass within the base of support, defined by points of contact with the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p988]
- Automatic postural responses have characteristic latencies of 40-60 ms in cats and 80-120 ms in humans, longer than stretch reflexes but shorter than voluntary reactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p989]
- Muscles are recruited during postural responses based on equilibrium requirements rather than changes in muscle length; the ankle strategy rotates the body about the ankles, while the hip strategy bends at the hip joints. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p990]
- Anticipatory postural adjustments precede voluntary movements to maintain stability; the magnitude and timing of these adjustments scale with the expected perturbation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p997]
- Large-diameter somatosensory fibers are critical for timing automatic postural responses; their loss delays EMG onset by approximately 20 ms, causing instability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p998]
