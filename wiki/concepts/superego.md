# Superego

## Summary

The superego (Über-Ich) is presented in Civilization and Its Discontents as an internalized agency that serves as the heir to the parental authority. It monitors the ego, imposes demands and prohibitions, and generates the sense of guilt when the ego fails to meet its standards. Freud elaborates that the superego's severity derives partly from the individual's own aggressiveness toward the parental authority, not merely from the authority's severity toward the individual.

## Key facts

- The superego turns into a harsh agency which sets itself over against the ego and, in the form of conscience, puts aggressive demands into action against the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6653]
- The severity of the superego is not merely a continuation of the severity experienced from external authority but represents the individual's own aggressiveness toward that authority. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6658]
- The superego torments the sinful ego with the same feeling of anxiety and is on the watch for opportunities of getting it punished by the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6655]
- Drive renunciation creates conscience, which then demands further drive renunciation in a self-reinforcing cycle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6659]
- The superego functions as an internalized external authority, representing the internalized parental and social demands. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6853]
- The superego serves as heir to parental agency, continuing the role of parents after their direct influence diminishes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6825]
- The superego's functions include conscience, self-observation, and judging and punishing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6894]
- The censorship of dreams is identified as a function of the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6868]
- The superego develops in childhood as children introject parental aggressiveness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6815]
- Through incorporating his father, the all-mighty, all-wise, all-benevolent father of the child's early years becomes an internal mental agency called the ego ideal or superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7028]
- The superego admonishes, censures, represses, and attempts to stem and divert all libidinal wishful impulses which do not meet its demands. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7028]
- In extreme cases, the father with whom the child identifies is equated with the Almighty, with God-the-Father Himself, and the superego demands the impossible from the ego. - The superego is established only after the Oedipus complex has been disposed of and represents its heir. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7360]
- The superego often displays a severity for which no model has been provided by real parents, corresponding instead to the strength of the defense used against the Oedipus complex. ## Inferences

- Inference: The superego represents a reversal of the original relationship between ego and external authority, such that 'if I were the father and you were the child, I should treat you badly.'
- Inference: Conscience and the sense of guilt are fundamentally connected to anxiety, particularly fear of the superego, and can exist unconsciously while still producing significant effects.
- Inference: Freud's introduction of the superego represents a major theoretical advance over the earlier structural model, allowing for better understanding of guilt, moral conflict, and dream censorship.
- Inference: The superego continues the father complex beyond childhood, serving as an internalized authority that critiques and restricts the ego throughout life.
- Inference: The superego continues to play the part of an external world for the ego, although it has become a portion of the internal world.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise relationship between the severity of upbringing and the severity of the superego remains debated, though both constitutional factors and environmental influences contribute.
