# Fourfold Pattern

## Summary

The fourfold pattern describes four combinations of probability level (high vs. low) and outcome valence (gains vs. losses), each producing a distinct decision pattern. High-probability gains elicit risk aversion (certainty effect), high-probability losses elicit risk seeking, low-probability gains elicit risk seeking (lottery effect), and low-probability losses elicit risk aversion (insurance effect). This pattern is considered one of Prospect Theory's core achievements.

## Key facts

- The upper-left cell (high-probability gains) produces risk aversion due to the certainty effect: people prefer a certain smaller gain over a probabilistic larger one, consistent with Bernoulli's observation. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p409]
- The lower-left cell (low-probability gains) produces risk seeking due to the possibility effect: the mere existence of a chance to win a large prize makes lottery tickets attractive despite tiny probabilities. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p410]
- The lower-right cell (low-probability losses) produces risk aversion (insurance effect): people are willing to pay well beyond expected value to eliminate a small probability of a large loss, as in buying insurance or earthquake coverage. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p410]
- The upper-right cell (high-probability losses) produces risk seeking: when facing a likely large loss, people prefer to gamble even when the expected outcome is worse, because the certain loss is especially painful and the hope of avoiding it is attractive. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p368]
- The upper-right cell is where failing businesses double down on obsolete technology, and where nations continue fighting wars after defeat is inevitable—behavior driven by the extreme aversion to certain losses. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p411]

## Inferences

- Inference: legal disputes, the fourfold pattern predicts that plaintiffs with strong cases prefer settlement (risk aversion in gains) while defendants with weak cases prefer to go to trial (risk seeking in losses), giving defendants an inherent negotiating advantage.
