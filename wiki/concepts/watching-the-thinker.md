# Watching the Thinker

## Summary

Watching the thinker is the practice of observing the involuntary stream of thoughts without judgment or identification. By listening to the voice in the head as a witness, one becomes aware of being 'the one who observes'—a conscious presence behind the thinking mind. This creates gaps in the mental stream ('no-mind') that allow stillness and peace to arise. The practice disidentifies consciousness from thought, allowing the 'watcher' or 'presence' to become stronger while mental formations weaken. This is described as the beginning of freedom from the compulsive thinking that characterizes egoic consciousness.

## Key facts

- Watching the thinker means 'listen to the voice in your head, be there as the witnessing presence'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p31]
- When observing the thinker, you feel 'a conscious presence—your deeper self—behind or underneath the thought'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p31]
- The practice creates 'gaps of no-mind' that expand with practice, leading to 'stillness and peace'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p31]
- Watching the thinker withdraws consciousness from mental forms, strengthening the watcher (pure consciousness beyond form). [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p99]
- The Zen master Rinzai would ask students: 'What, at this moment, is lacking?' to draw attention deeply into the Now. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p59]
