# Behavior Change Strategies

## Summary

DBT teaches specific behavior change strategies including reinforcement to increase desired behaviors and extinction and punishment to decrease undesired behaviors. These strategies are based on behavioral psychology principles about how consequences influence behavior.

## Key facts

- Reinforcement (positive or negative) increases the frequency of a behavior by adding something desired or removing something aversive. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p185]
- Extinction stops ongoing reinforcement of behavior, which leads first to a burst of behavior and then to a decrease. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p186]
- Satiation provides relief or what is wanted before the behavior occurs, which reduces motivation and decreases frequency. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p186]
- Punishment decreases behavior by adding an aversive consequence or removing something positive, but must be specific, time-limited, and fit the 'crime'. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p186]
- Shaping reinforces small steps toward the desired behavior, requiring more before reinforcing as new behavior stabilizes. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p185]

## Inferences

- Inference: Effective behavior change requires reinforcing alternative behaviors rather than just punishing or extinguishing unwanted behaviors.
