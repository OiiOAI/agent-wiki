# Delusions of Jealousy

## Summary

Delusions of jealousy are false beliefs about a partner's infidelity that persist despite logical evidence to the contrary. Freud's psychoanalytic investigation reveals these delusions serve unconscious purposes, often involving projection of one's own unfaithful impulses onto the partner as a form of psychological relief or self-punishment.

## Key facts

- A case study presented involves a woman who developed delusions of jealousy after receiving an anonymous letter accusing her husband of infidelity, though she knew the letter was maliciously motivated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5058]
- Analysis revealed the patient herself was unconsciously in love with her son-in-law, and her delusional jealousy served as a mirror image providing relief from her own guilt. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5061]
- The mechanism of displacement operates in generating delusional jealousy, where unconscious wishes find relief through their mirror reflection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5061]

## Inferences

- Inference: Delusions are inaccessible to logical arguments because they are connected to unconscious processes that give them their strength and persistence.
- Inference: The delusion acquired independence from the original precipitating event, having been present as a wish or fear before the anonymous letter.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Why this particular form of consolation (projection onto husband) was chosen rather than other possible mechanisms cannot be fully determined from this case.
