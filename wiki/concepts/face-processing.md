# Face Processing

## Summary

The primate brain contains dedicated systems for face processing. Face-selective neurons are clustered into small areas called face patches, several millimeters in diameter. In macaques, six face-selective areas have been identified in temporal cortex and three in prefrontal cortex. Each face patch is functionally specialized, with posterior patches responding to particular views of faces and anterior patches becoming gradually more selective to identity and less selective for angle of view.

## Key facts

- Face processing might be performed by a dedicated face-processing network spanning from the posterior part of inferior temporal cortex to prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p616]
- Six face-selective areas have been identified in the temporal lobe of macaque monkeys and three in prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p616]
- From posterior to anterior locations within the temporal lobe, face patches become gradually more selective to identity and less selective for angle of view. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p616]
- Dorsal face areas within the temporal lobe exhibit selectivity for natural facial motion that ventral areas lack. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p617]
- Prosopagnosia is a form of associative agnosia resulting from an inability to identify faces as belonging to specific persons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p615]
