# News Illusion

## Summary

The news illusion is the paradox that despite being incredibly well-informed, we know incredibly little of practical value. Dobelli argues news is to the mind what sugar is to the body: appetizing but destructive. News distorts our mental map of real risks, is largely irrelevant to decision-making, and wastes enormous amounts of time.

## Key facts

- Dobelli stopped consuming news for three years, canceling all subscriptions and deleting news apps, and reports missing nothing important. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p215]
- News producers filter out subtle, complex, abstract, and profound stories because they don't capture attention for advertisers. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p215]
- Out of 10,000 news stories consumed yearly, almost no one can name even two that helped them make better life, career, or business decisions. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p216]
- Dobelli calculates that following the 2008 Mumbai terror attacks for one hour by a billion people wasted approximately 2,000 lives' worth of productivity. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p216]
