# Knowledge Engineering

## Summary

Knowledge Engineering treats organizational knowledge as a resource, parallel to how petroleum is treated as a resource. Just as petroleum requires search, evaluation, extraction, processing, and application, so does knowledge. This approach addresses the problem of tacit knowledge held in workers' heads that is never shared, and provides methods for cognitive task analysis to capture and codify expertise.

## Key facts

- The parallel between petroleum and knowledge includes: search (where to drill / where expertise is), evaluate (whether to drill / value of knowledge), extract (drilling rigs / knowledge elicitation), process (refine petroleum / codify knowledge), and apply (heat/power / training and design). [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p211]
- In most organizations, the culture seems to ignore the expertise that already exists, to take it for granted—organizations stop workers from taking equipment but let experts walk out with decades of expertise worth far more. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p210]
- The cost of extracting knowledge can be considerable: interviews, transcriptions, analyses, methods for representing knowledge, and cost of distributing lessons learned. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p212]
