# Intuitive Biology

## Summary

Intuitive biology is a domain-specific cognitive system that allows humans to categorize living things into species-like groups and infer that each species has an underlying causal essence responsible for its appearance and behavior. This ability appears early in development and may be innate.

## Key facts

- Humans intuitively think about plants and animals in special ways different from how they think about objects such as rocks or stars. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p258]
- Children from age three infer that something categorized as animate has an essence that makes it what it is and does not change despite appearance alterations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p263]
- Children believe that changing the innards of a dog makes it no longer a dog, but changing its appearance does not, indicating essentialist thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p258]
- Essentialism appears in all cultures and may take forms such as DNA, a gift from God, or astrological signs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p264]

## Inferences

- Inference: tuitive biology likely evolved because understanding living things' essences had survival advantages.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural substrates of intuitive biology are not fully characterized.
