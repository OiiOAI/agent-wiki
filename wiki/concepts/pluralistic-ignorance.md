# Pluralistic Ignorance

## Summary

Pluralistic ignorance is a phenomenon occurring when individuals in a group each privately reject a norm but go along with it because they incorrectly assume others accept it. In emergency situations, everyone looks to see what everyone else is doing, sees everyone else looking unruffled and failing to act, and concludes that nothing is wrong—despite the danger possibly mounting to the point where a single individual would react.

## Key facts

- Latane and Darley defined pluralistic ignorance as 'a condition in which each person decides that since nobody is concerned, nothing is wrong. Meanwhile, the danger may be mounting to the point where a single individual, uninfluenced by the seeming calm of others, would react.'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p124]
- In a smoke seepage experiment, 75% of lone individuals reported the leak, but only 38% of three-person groups reported it; when two of the three were coached to ignore the smoke, leaks were reported only 10% of the time. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p125]
- The pluralistic ignorance effect is strongest among strangers, because people are unlikely to give off or correctly read expressions of concern when in a grouping of unfamiliar individuals. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p126]
- Pluralistic ignorance occurs when individuals privately disagree with a norm but publicly conform because they believe others accept it. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p256]
- The concept was introduced by Latané and Darley in their award-winning book (1968). [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p256]
- Lee Alexis Wilson, 23, was beaten, strangled, and sexually assaulted near the Art Institute in Chicago during daylight hours. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p257]
- Thousands of persons passed the site where Wilson was attacked; one man heard a scream at 2 PM but did not investigate because no one else seemed to be paying attention. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Chicago murder case demonstrates that pluralistic ignorance can have lethal consequences when individuals collectively fail to act in an emergency because each assumes others will intervene.
