# Exogamy

## Summary

Exogamy refers to the custom prohibiting sexual intercourse and marriage between members of the same totem clan. This institution, linked to totemism, serves to prevent incest and extends prohibitions far beyond actual blood relatives to include all members of the totem group.

## Key facts

- Exogamy is the prohibition of sexual intercourse between members of the same totem clan, and it is strictly enforced with severe penalties including death in many Australian tribes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4122]
- In Australia, the penalty for sexual intercourse with a person of a forbidden clan is death for both parties, though in some cases the woman may only be beaten or speared if she was probably coerced. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4123]
- Exogamy linked with the totem effects more than prevention of incest with a man's mother and sisters—it makes sexual intercourse impossible with all women of his own clan, treating them all as blood relatives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4124]
- Totemic exogamy appears to have been the appropriate means for preventing group incest and became established, persisting long after its original raison d'être had ceased. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4125]

## Inferences

- Inference: The severity of exogamous prohibitions and their universality among totemic peoples suggests that the prevention of incest represents a fundamental psychological and social concern across human cultures.
