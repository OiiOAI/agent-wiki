# Outcome Blindness

## Summary

Outcome blindness is the practice of evaluating decision quality without knowledge of the outcome, preventing 'resulting'—the cognitive bias where we judge decisions as good or bad based solely on whether they turned out well. Pioneered in particle physics and cosmology, the approach has spread to other sciences as a method to enforce disinterestedness. Applied to decision groups, describing situations up to the decision point without revealing the outcome produces higher-fidelity evaluation of decision quality.

## Key facts

- Richard Feynman found that if data analysts knew or could intuit the hypothesis being tested, analysis would be more likely to support that hypothesis. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- According to Robert MacCoun and Saul Perlmutter, outcome-blind analysis has spread to particle physics and cosmology where it is considered essential for trusting results. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- Many expert poker players omit the outcome when seeking advice about their play to promote disinterestedness. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- If the outcome is known, it biases the assessment of decision quality to align with outcome quality. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
