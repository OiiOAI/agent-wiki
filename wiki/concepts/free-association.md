# Free Association

## Summary

Free association is the fundamental technique of psychoanalytic investigation in which the patient or dreamer is asked to report whatever ideas, thoughts, or images come to mind in response to a starting point (such as a dream element or a parapraxis), without exercising critical judgment or selection. The technique is based on the premise that seemingly arbitrary thoughts are in fact determined by unconscious mental processes and can lead to repressed or inaccessible material.

## Key facts

- In free association, the subject is asked to report the first thing that occurs to them without reflection or critical selection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4883]
- Thoughts occurring freely are determined by important internal attitudes not known to us at the moment they operate. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4885]
- The technique of free association was modeled on the method used to investigate parapraxes, where the speaker was asked how they arrived at a wrong word. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4880]
- If the dreamer asserts that nothing occurs to them, urgent pressure should be brought to bear, insisting that something must occur. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4883]
- Free association requires patients to say whatever comes into their head without giving conscious direction to their thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6266]
- The fundamental rule requires patients to report literally everything without critical objections about importance or relevance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6266]
- Free association is not really free—the patient remains under the influence of the analytic situation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6266]
- The method exposes the patient to the least possible compulsion and guarantees no factor in the neurosis structure will be overlooked. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6267]
