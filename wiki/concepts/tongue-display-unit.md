# Tongue Display Unit

## Summary

The Tongue Display Unit (TDU) is a sensory substitution device developed by Paul Bach-y-Rita's team. It consists of a construction hat with an accelerometer that detects head movement in two planes, connected to a thin plastic strip with electrodes placed on the tongue. When the user tilts, electric shocks (described as feeling like champagne bubbles) on different parts of the tongue indicate orientation in space. The device replaces damaged vestibular function by sending balance signals to the brain via the tongue. With use, the brain learns to interpret these signals, and the residual effect—recovery lasting after the device is removed—grows progressively longer.

## Key facts

- The tongue display uses a construction hat with an accelerometer that detects head movement in two planes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p16]
- The device projects a map onto 144 electrodes implanted in a plastic strip placed on the tongue, creating sensations described as champagne bubbles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p16]
- The team has used the device to train over fifty patients to improve their balance and walking, including those with brain trauma, stroke, or Parkinson's disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p36]
- The goal is to make the device small enough to be hidden in the mouth like an orthodontist's mouth retainer. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p17]
