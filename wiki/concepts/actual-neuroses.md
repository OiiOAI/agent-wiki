# Actual Neuroses

## Summary

Freud's theory distinguishing actual neuroses (neurasthenia, anxiety neurosis, and possibly hypochondria proper) from psychoneuroses. Actual neuroses have somatic rather than psychical origins—their symptoms are toxic effects that cannot be analyzed into historical or symbolic meanings. They provide the 'somatic compliance' that psychoneuroses require: excitatory material which is then psychically selected and given a psychical coating. The symptom nucleus (like a grain of sand at the center of a pearl) is formed of somatic sexual manifestation.

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud defends this position against Stekel's inclination to reject the distinction and lump all neuroses together under psychasthenia.
- Inference: The theory suggests that even in psychoneuroses, there is a somatic core that cannot be fully psychoanalyzed.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that analytic treatment can have indirect curative effects on actual symptoms by enabling current noxae to be better tolerated.
- Uncertain: The 'sexual metabolism' or 'chemistry of sexuality' is a term without content; Freud acknowledges knowing nothing about it and cannot decide whether to assume one or two sexual substances [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5146]
