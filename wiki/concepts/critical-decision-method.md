# Critical Decision Method

## Summary

The Critical Decision Method (CDM) is a cognitive task analysis technique developed by Klein, Calderwood, and MacGregor to capture the nature of decision makers' understanding during critical incidents. The method emphasizes nonroutine incidents and their nonroutine aspects to uncover the expertise, cues, and patterns used to identify situational dynamics. CDM uses retrospective accounts and structured interviews with multiple 'sweeps' to probe decision-making activities.

## Key facts

- CDM was developed by Klein, Calderwood, and MacGregor (1989) to understand how experts made difficult decisions, originally applied to fireground commanders. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p75]
- CDM emphasizes nonroutine incidents and their nonroutine aspects because they usually carry the most information about how expertise is used. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p74]
- The CDM interview process involves multiple 'sweeps': first sweep provides initial account, second sweep puts incident on time scale and identifies decision points, third sweep uses probes to examine judgments and decisions depending on expertise, and optional fourth sweep explores what less experienced persons would miss. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p76]
- CDM probes include asking about salient cues, relevant knowledge, goals being pursued, overall understanding, potential courses of action, and hypothetical issues about effects if key features were different. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p76]
- CDM studies of AEGIS naval commanders found virtually every incident included key variables that were context-specific and typically not included in simulations, such as judgments of other officers' competence. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p77]
- CDM applied to NICU nurses identified cues for detecting sepsis in newborns that had not previously been published in medical or nursing literature. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p79]
- CDM avoids problems with introspection by not asking why judgments were made; instead, it emphasizes cues and patterns that were noticed without requiring introspection about inferential processes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p83]

## Inferences

- Inference: The success of CDM in discovering unpublished cues suggests that critical incidents reveal expertise that cannot be obtained from literature or standard training materials.
- Inference: The structured interview approach with multiple sweeps allows for increasingly detailed elicitation of tacit knowledge.
