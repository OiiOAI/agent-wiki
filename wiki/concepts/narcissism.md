# Narcissism

## Summary

Narcissism is presented as a form of overcompensation for neediness. When men find it too difficult to let go of seeking others' approval, some go to the opposite extreme and decide to only care about themselves. The book distinguishes between three categories: needy men (who only care about others' approval, even at their own expense), non-needy men (who look for intersection where both their own needs and their partner's needs are met), and narcissistic men (who only pursue getting their own needs met). Narcissism is characterized by selfishness, aggression, domination, and an inability to accept rejection or wrongdoing. Critically, Manson argues that narcissistic men are still needy — they derive their sense of self from dominating others and receiving their attention, making them paradoxically more invested in others than themselves.

## Key facts

- Non-needy men look for the intersection where they can get both their own needs and their partner's needs met. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p37]
- Narcissistic men overcompensate and decide to only pursue getting their own needs met, often conjuring false beliefs about women's inferiority as justification. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p37]
- The problem with narcissism is that it defines itself by getting what it wants from others — a man who is narcissistic only feels non-needy if he is dominating somebody else. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p39]
- Narcissistic men end up with narcissistic and/or highly needy women, resulting in toxic, superficial relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p39]
- When a woman rejects a narcissistic man, he becomes angry and hurt, blaming her for not seeing his greatness. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p40]
- A non-needy man doesn't try to control what women feel about him — rather he tries to control what he feels about certain women. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p40]
- Narcissism is defined as men who overcompensate for their low self-esteem and lack of confidence by imposing their will and needs onto others unnecessarily. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p268]
- Narcissism is often preoccupied with sleeping with as many women as possible. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p268]
- Narcissistic behavior can come across as confident but is still low-status behavior because it is over-invested in the perceptions of others. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p268]
- Men who are narcissistic experience short-term sexual success but long-term emotional failure. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p268]
- From birth, humans feel a never-ending need for attention that drives narcissistic behavior. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p60]
- Deep narcissists experience a sharp break in early self-development between ages two and five, never constructing a realistic, cohesive sense of self. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p62]
- Deep narcissists' mothers are often either deep narcissists themselves (abandonment) or enmeshers who overinvolve the child (enmeshment). [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p62]
- Deep narcissists see others as extensions of themselves (self-objects), instruments for attention and validation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p64]
- The book identifies four types of deep narcissists: Complete Control Narcissists, Theatrical Narcissists, Narcissistic Couples, and Narcissistic Leaders. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p74]
- Healthy narcissists have stronger, more resilient self-esteem and can turn attention outward toward work or people. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p67]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book changed the term 'False Confidence' to 'narcissism' in revised editions, considering it more precise and comprehensible.
- Inference: The book suggests that self-love and narcissism exist on a spectrum, with healthy self-esteem being essential for developing empathy rather than being opposed to it.
