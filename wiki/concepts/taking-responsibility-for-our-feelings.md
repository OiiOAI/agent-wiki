# Taking Responsibility for Our Feelings

## Summary

The third component of NVC is acknowledging that what others say and do may be the stimulus but never the cause of our feelings. Our feelings result from how we choose to receive what others say and do, combined with our particular needs and expectations. This component moves us toward accepting responsibility for what we do to generate our own feelings, rather than blaming others.

## Key facts

- What others do may be the stimulus of our feelings, but never the cause; our feelings result from how we choose to receive what others say and do. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p86]
- When receiving negative messages, we have four options: 1) blame ourselves, 2) blame others, 3) sense our own feelings and needs, 4) sense others' feelings and needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p86-87]
- Judgments, criticisms, diagnoses, and interpretations of others are all alienated expressions of our own unmet needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p90]
- The more directly we can connect our feelings to our own needs, the easier it is for others to respond compassionately. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p90]
- In developing emotional responsibility, most people experience three stages: emotional slavery (believing ourselves responsible for others' feelings), the obnoxious stage (refusing to care about others' feelings), and emotional liberation (accepting responsibility for our own feelings without being responsible for others'). [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p96-98]
- The book identifies basic human needs including autonomy, celebration, integrity, interdependence, play, spiritual communion, and physical nurturance. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p91-93]
