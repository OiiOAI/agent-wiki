# Controlled Behavior

## Summary

Controlled behavior occurs when people act because they are being pressured, without a sense of personal endorsement. Deci identifies two types: compliance (doing what you are told because you are told to) and defiance (doing the opposite of what is expected just because it is expected). These represent complementary responses to control, and where one dominates the other tendency still exists. Controlled behavior is associated with alienation, diminished intrinsic motivation, and poorer creative performance. Deci argues that controlling social contexts promote automatons—people who comply, engage in instrumental reasoning, and occasionally defy controls.

## Key facts

- Controlled behavior occurs when people act without a sense of personal endorsement; their behavior is not an expression of the self, which has been subjugated to the controls. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p12]
- The two types of controlled behavior are compliance (doing what you are told because you are told) and defiance (doing the opposite of what is expected just because it is expected). [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p13]
- Compliance and defiance exist in an unstable partnership representing complementary responses to control; where there is one, there is also a tendency for the other. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p13]
- Controlling environments that demand, pressure, prod, and cajole people promote automatons—people who engage in instrumental reasoning, comply with demands, and are only half alive. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p83]
- The price of compliance is profound alienation with all its ramifications. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p14]
