# Pursuit of Superiority

## Summary

The pursuit of superiority is Adler's concept describing the universal human desire to improve and escape from a state of helplessness. This is not about being better than others in competition, but rather about taking steps forward on one's own path. It encompasses the desire to learn, grow, and become more capable, driving both individual development and scientific progress.

## Key facts

- Adler called the universal desire to escape from helplessness the 'pursuit of superiority,' which can be understood as 'hoping to improve' or 'pursuing an ideal state.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p83]
- The pursuit of superiority is not about climbing above others but about taking a single step forward on one's own feet. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p94]
- Adler stated: 'The important thing is not what one is born with but what use one makes of that equipment.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p49]
