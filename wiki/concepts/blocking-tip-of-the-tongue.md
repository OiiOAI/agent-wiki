# Blocking (Tip-of-the-Tongue)

## Summary

Blocking refers to the temporary inability to retrieve a known word or name from memory, commonly experienced as the tip-of-the-tongue (TOT) state. Schacter explores why proper names are particularly susceptible to blocking, the role of competing information, and the underlying cognitive and neural mechanisms. The phenomenon reveals the complex retrieval pathways involved in word production.

## Key facts

- Proper names are more susceptible to blocking than common nouns or other types of names, likely due to their arbitrary relationship to the named entity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]
- Tip-of-the-tongue states become more frequent with age, reflecting age-related changes in retrieval processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]
- During a TOT state, partial information often becomes accessible, including the first letter or number of syllables. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]
- Neuroimaging studies show activation in the prefrontal cortex and anterior cingulate during TOT states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]

## Inferences

- Inference: Blocking may serve as a protective mechanism, preventing access to competing or interfering information that could disrupt coherent memory retrieval.
