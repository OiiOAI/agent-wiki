# Insulin Resistance

## Summary

Insulin resistance occurs when cells, initially muscle cells, stop listening to insulin's signals. It begins when fat droplets accumulate in muscle cells, disrupting insulin-dependent transport mechanisms. The pancreas secretes more insulin to compensate, but eventually blood glucose rises. Insulin resistance is associated with up to twelvefold increased cancer risk, fivefold increased Alzheimer's risk, and almost sixfold increased cardiovascular death risk.

## Key facts

- Insulin resistance means cells, initially muscle cells, have stopped listening to insulin's signals. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p106]
- Fat droplets accumulating in muscle cells disrupt the complex network of insulin-dependent transport mechanisms that normally bring glucose into the cell. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p106]
- The pancreas compensates by secreting more insulin to remove excess glucose from the bloodstream and cram it into cells. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p106]
- Insulin resistance is associated with up to twelvefold increased cancer risk, fivefold increased Alzheimer's risk, and almost sixfold increased death from cardiovascular disease. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p114]
- Inactivity accelerates fat-spillover-driven insulin resistance because if a person is not physically active and not consuming energy via muscles, fat accumulates more quickly. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p106]
- When insulin resistance develops, fat also accumulates in the pancreas itself, worsening the ability to mount an insulin response. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p107]
