# Obedience to Authority

## Summary

Obedience to authority is a social influence phenomenon where individuals comply with commands from those in positions of power, even when such commands conflict with personal morals. Stanley Milgram's landmark experiments demonstrated that ordinary people will obey authority figures to the extent of administering potentially lethal shocks, raising profound questions about human social behavior and the Holocaust.

## Key facts

- Milgram began his investigations to understand how German citizens participated in concentration-camp atrocities during Nazi rule. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- Milgram found such extreme obedience in New Haven, Connecticut that he said 'I hardly saw the need of taking the experiment to Germany'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- Milgram's obedience experiments have been replicated with similar results in Holland, Germany, Spain, Italy, Australia, and Jordan. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- After the My Lai trial, 51 percent of Americans surveyed said they too would shoot villagers if so ordered in a similar context. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- The authority principle operates even in monkey colonies with rigid dominance hierarchies, where innovations spread only when taught to dominant animals first. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]

## Inferences

- Inference: The high rates of obedience found across multiple cultures and species suggest this is a fundamental aspect of social organization rather than a cultural aberration
