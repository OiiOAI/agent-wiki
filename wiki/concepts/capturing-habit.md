# Capturing Habit

## Summary

The capturing habit is the practice of externalizing all incomplete items, commitments, and open loops from one's mind into a trusted system. The negative feelings about 'stuff' come from broken agreements with oneself, which can be resolved by either not making agreements, completing them, or renegotiating them.

## Key facts

- The sense of anxiety and guilt does not come from having too much to do; it is the automatic result of breaking agreements with oneself. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p300]
- There are three options for eliminating negative feelings from broken agreements: don't make the agreement, complete the agreement, or renegotiate the agreement. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p300]
- Maintaining an objective and complete inventory of work, regularly reviewed, makes it much easier to say no with integrity. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p301]
- When people notice one receives, processes, and organizes exchanges in an airtight manner, they begin to trust in a unique way. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p298]

## Inferences

- Inference: The capturing habit creates self-confidence in engagement with the world that money cannot buy and noticeably enhances mental well-being and improves quality of communications and relationships.
