# Memory Capacity

## Summary

The limited amount of information the human brain actually stores despite its vast potential capacity. The brain has approximately 100 billion neurons with 10^15 connections, yet stores only about 125 MB of information over a lifetime.

## Key facts

- The human brain has approximately 100 billion neurons (10^11), similar in number to stars in the Milky Way. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p25]
- Each neuron connects to approximately 10,000 others, resulting in about 10^15 connections, roughly the number of grains of sand in a beach 100 meters long. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p25]
- Hopfield network calculations show that given N neurons, only about 0.14N memories can be stored without interference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p26]
- Thomas Landauer estimated that a 70-year-old person stores about 10^9 bits of memory, approximately 125 MB over a lifetime. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p74]
- We transmit approximately 10 million bits per second through the optic nerve, but remember only about 1 bit per second. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p75]

## Inferences

- Inference: The vast difference between the brain's storage capacity and actual memory storage suggests the brain prioritizes meaning extraction over faithful recording.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Landauer's estimates are based on text and image memory; the actual amount may be larger or smaller for other types of memories.
