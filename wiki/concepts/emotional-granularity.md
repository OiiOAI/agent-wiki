# Emotional Granularity

## Summary

Emotional granularity refers to the variation in how individuals use emotion words to describe their feelings. Barrett's early research discovered that some people use fine distinctions between emotions (e.g., 'joy,' 'sadness,' 'fear,' 'disgust,' 'excitement,' 'awe') while others lump together words like 'sad,' 'afraid,' 'anxious,' and 'depressed' to mean 'I feel unpleasant.' Testing over 700 American subjects revealed that people vary tremendously in how they differentiate their emotional experiences, analogous to how some people can distinguish many shades of blue while others call them all 'blue.'

## Key facts

- After testing over seven hundred American subjects, Barrett discovered that people vary tremendously in how they differentiate their emotional experiences. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p15]
- Some subjects experienced sadness and fear as qualitatively different, while others lumped together 'sad,' 'afraid,' 'anxious,' and 'depressed' to mean 'I feel unpleasant.'. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p15]
- Emotional granularity relates to the ability to construct specific instances of emotion categories, with high granularity associated with better emotion regulation and health outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p15]
- People with high emotional granularity may have hundreds of emotion concepts including aggravation, irritation, frustration, hostility, rage, and disgruntlement as distinct from anger. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p125]
- People with moderate granularity might have only the so-called basic emotions, with variations falling under single concepts. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p125]
- People with low emotional granularity have only a few emotion concepts where words like anger, sadness, and fear all correspond to feeling unpleasant. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p126]
- Alexithymia affects about 10% of the world's population, characterized by difficulty experiencing emotion and relying on physical symptoms instead. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p126]
- People with alexithymia are more likely to experience a stomachache where others experience anger, and report perceiving only shouting rather than anger in others. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p126]
- Preciseness in emotion concepts leads to efficiency in neural processing. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p141]
- Emotional granularity is the phenomenon whereby some people construct finer-grained emotional experiences than others. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p201]
- People who exhibit higher emotional granularity go to the doctor less frequently, use medication less frequently, and spend fewer days hospitalized for illness. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p202]
- People with finely distinguished unpleasant feelings were 30 percent more flexible when regulating their emotions. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p203]
- Lower emotional granularity is associated with major depressive disorder, social anxiety disorder, eating disorders, autism spectrum disorders, and borderline personality disorder. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p203]
