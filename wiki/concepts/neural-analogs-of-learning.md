# Neural Analogs of Learning

## Summary

Neural analogs of learning is an approach developed by Eric Kandel to study the cellular mechanisms of learning by simulating Pavlovian learning protocols in isolated neural circuits of Aplysia. By applying different patterns of electrical stimulation modeled on habituation, sensitization, and classical conditioning to specific neural pathways, Kandel showed that synaptic strength could be altered in different ways. This demonstrated that the same synapse can be strengthened or weakened depending on the pattern of activity, providing evidence that synaptic plasticity underlies learning.

## Key facts

- Kandel modeled habituation by applying repeated weak electrical stimuli to a neural pathway leading to cell R2, finding the synaptic response decreased progressively with repetition (homosynaptic depression). [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p179]
- Sensitization was modeled by applying strong stimuli to one pathway after weak stimuli to another, producing heterosynaptic facilitation that enhanced responses for up to thirty minutes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p180]
- Classical conditioning was modeled by pairing a weak stimulus that preceded a strong stimulus, producing a greater enhancement than sensitization alone, critically dependent on timing. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p181]
- These experiments showed that the same synapse can be strengthened or weakened by different patterns of stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p182]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that synaptic plasticity is so readily modifiable suggested that it is built into the molecular architecture of chemical synapses and that the flow of information in neural circuits could be modified by learning.
