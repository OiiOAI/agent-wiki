# Aletheia

## Summary

Heidegger's concept of truth as 'unconcealing' (from Greek aletheia, literally 'un-hiddenness'). Aletheia suggests something that pre-exists our attempts to discover it, is defined by a negative (by what it is not), involves a process of coming into being, has degrees, and is found by removing things rather than by putting them together. This contrasts with truth-as-correctness which is static and unchanging.

## Key facts

- Heidegger related truth to aletheia, literally 'unconcealing' - a concept suggesting something that pre-exists our attempts to 'dis-cover' it. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p218]
- Aletheia implies truth is defined by a negative - by what it is not; and in opposition to something else (unconcealing). [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p218]
- Truth as unconcealing is a progress towards something - the something is in sight but never fully seen; whereas truth as correctness is given as a thing in itself that can in principle be fully known. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p218]
- Heidegger's saying 'in unconcealment dwells hiddenness and safekeeping' draws attention to the simultaneous hiddenness and radiance of truth. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p219]

## Inferences

- Inference: Aletheia corresponds to the right hemisphere's understanding of truth as process and engagement rather than the left hemisphere's understanding of truth as static object.
