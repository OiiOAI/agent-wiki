# P300 Component

## Summary

The P300 is an endogenous component of the Event-Related Potential characterized by a positive deflection appearing 250 to 600 milliseconds after stimulus presentation. Its amplitude is modulated by expectancy, with lower levels of expectancy producing larger P300 amplitudes. The P300 has been used to assess operators' expectancies in various contexts, though it does not indicate whether information was correctly registered relative to the situation.

## Key facts

- The P300 is a positive deflection in the ERP appearing 250 to 600 milliseconds following stimulus presentation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- The cognitive concomitants of P300 are well established, with amplitude changing based on level of expectancy. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- Lower levels of expectancy are associated with larger amplitude P300s. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- Wilson, Swain, and Ullsperger (1998) reported P300 amplitude magnitude is modulated by warning stimuli alerting subjects to the difficulty of succeeding tasks. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
- The P300 does not indicate whether information was correctly registered relative to the situation, though clever experimental design may yield some of this information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p181]
