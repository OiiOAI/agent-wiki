# Phylogenetic Disposition to Neuroses

## Summary

Freud's theory that human dispositions to neuroses were acquired during prehistoric eras, particularly through two major phylogenetic events: the struggles of the Ice Ages (leading to transference neuroses) and the oppression of sons by the primal father (leading to narcissistic neuroses). This phylogenetic acquisition represents what Freud called a 'cultural acquisition' of neurosis.

## Key facts

- Freud posited that dispositions to the three transference neuroses were acquired in the struggle with Ice Age exigencies. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4646]
- During the Ice Age, primal humans faced conflict between self-preservation and desire to procreate due to insufficient food. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4644]
- The conditions of the Ice Age correspond to the conditions of conversion hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4644]
- Fixations underlying narcissistic neuroses originate from father oppression after the Ice Age. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4646]
- Dementia praecox corresponds to the primaeval condition of castrated sons. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4645]
- Paranoia corresponds to the phase of brothers allied in homosexuality against the father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4646]
- Melancholia relates to the alternation of triumph and mourning following the primal father's murder. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4646]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's phylogenetic theory connects specific prehistoric events to specific neuroses: Ice Age conditions to hysteria, father-castration to dementia praecox, fraternal alliances to paranoia, and the primal father's death to melancholia.
- Inference: The theory holds that as the first struggle leads to the patriarchal stage, the second leads to the social stage of civilization.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that 'the extent to which it may throw light on the still unsolved riddles of the neuroses should properly be left to further investigation' (p.4647).
- Uncertain: The mechanisms of hereditary transmission of these acquired dispositions remain obscure, as Freud admits: 'The difficulty basically coincides with one that has been posed earlier, namely, how the brutal father of the Ice Age... reproduced himself' (p.4647).
