# Unity of the Self

## Summary

Rawls presents his account of how the self achieves unity, contrasting his view with dominant-end conceptions. In justice as fairness, the unity of the person is manifest in the coherence of his rational plan of life, founded on the higher-order desire to follow principles of rational choice consistent with a sense of right and justice. Unlike hedonism, where the self becomes one by maximizing pleasure, Rawls holds that the essential unity of the self is already provided by the conception of right. The principles of justice and their realization in social forms define the bounds within which deliberations take place. The self is prior to the ends it affirms; even a dominant end must be chosen from among numerous possibilities.
