# Extended Mind Thesis

## Summary

The Extended Mind thesis, co-developed by Andy Clark and David Chalmers, argues that the machinery of an individual mind is not restricted to the brain or body but can encompass aspects of the material and technological world. Under certain conditions, outward loops involving tools such as calculators, smartphones, or notebooks count as proper parts of the cognitive process.

## Key facts

- Clark and Chalmers argued that true mental circuitry can be spread out across brain, body, and aspects of the material and technological world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p163]
- The parity principle states that if a part of the world functions as a cognitive process which, were it to go on in the head, we would accept as part of the cognitive process, then that part of the world is part of the cognitive process. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p164]
- Otto's notebook functions as part of his extended memory, playing a role similar to Inga's biological memory when both access the address of MoMA. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p165]
- Predictive brains are the biological engines that make deep entwinement possible through their capacity to estimate which actions will reliably reduce uncertainty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p169]

## Inferences

- Inference: Smartphones have become part of our minds in those respects where they play the same role that biological memory used to play [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p166]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the Large Hadron Collider forms part of any individual mind depends on how tightly coupled the resource is to the individual's cognitive processes
