# Deadlines in Negotiation

## Summary

Deadlines in negotiation are often arbitrary and flexible, rarely triggering the negative consequences negotiators fear. Revealing your deadline to counterparts can actually improve outcomes by reducing impasse risk and accelerating real deal-making. Self-imposed deadlines (like kidnappers wanting weekend party money) can be exploited.

## Key facts

- Among hundreds of executives asked if missed deadlines had negative repercussions, only one responded affirmatively, suggesting deadlines are often self-inflicted pressure. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p105]
- Hiding deadlines increases risk of impasse because you concede as deadlines approach while the other side, thinking it has time, holds out. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p107]
- Haitian kidnappers followed a pattern of wanting payment by Friday for weekend partying, allowing negotiators to exploit their self-imposed deadline. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p106]
- Deadlines cut both ways—revealing your deadline to counterparts reduces impasse risk and accelerates deal-making. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p107]
