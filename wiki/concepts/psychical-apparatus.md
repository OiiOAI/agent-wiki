# Psychical Apparatus

## Summary

Freud's concept of a mental apparatus comprising the somatic sexual apparatus and the psychical apparatus, both of which undergo development. The psychical apparatus, as a mental instrument, is central to understanding how deferred effects of infantile sexual experiences produce abnormal reactions. This concept would be elaborated in The Interpretation of Dreams.

## Key facts

- During the interval between infantile impressions and their reproduction, not only the somatic sexual apparatus but the psychical apparatus as well has undergone an important development. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1171]
- The influence of earlier sexual experiences leads to an abnormal psychical reaction because the influence now leads to an abnormal reaction of the psychical apparatus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1171]
- The psychical apparatus is conceived as a compound instrument whose components are called 'agencies' or 'systems' (ψ-systems), possibly arranged in spatial succession like lens systems in a telescope. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1777]
- The apparatus has a sensory end (receiving perceptions) and a motor end (opening to motor activity); psychical processes advance from the perceptual end to the motor end, analogous to a reflex apparatus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1778]
- The Pcpt. system receives perceptual stimuli but retains no trace (no memory), while behind it lies Mnem. systems that transform momentary excitations into permanent traces. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1778]
- The preconscious (Pcs.) holds the key to voluntary movement and allows excitatory processes to enter consciousness when conditions like attention distribution are fulfilled. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1781]
- The unconscious (Ucs.) has no access to consciousness except via the preconscious, through which its excitatory process must submit to modifications. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1781]

## Inferences

- Inference: This is the first appearance of the term 'psychical apparatus' in Freud's published works, marking an important step toward his later metapsychological framework.
- Inference: Memory traces are in themselves unconscious; only perceptions reach consciousness. The capacity to retain modifications and consciousness are mutually exclusive properties of different systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that the hypothesis of systems in spatial order is not strictly necessary—a fixed temporal sequence of excitation through systems could suffice.
