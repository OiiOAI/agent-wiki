# Primal Horde

## Summary

A hypothetical early form of human society described by Darwin, consisting of a violent and jealous father who kept all females for himself and drove away his sons as they grew up. Freud uses this concept, combined with the totem meal hypothesis, to explain the origins of totemism, religion, and moral restrictions.

## Key facts

- According to Darwin's theory of the primal horde, primitive men originally lived in small communities with a jealous oldest male who prevented sexual promiscuity and drove away adolescent sons. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4226]
- Atkinson proposed that the practical consequence of conditions in the primal horde must be exogamy for the young males, who after being expelled would establish similar hordes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4226]
- Freud proposes that one day the brothers who had been driven out came together, killed and devoured their father and so made an end of the patriarchal horde. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4239]
- Freud takes up Darwin's conjecture that the primitive form of human society was that of a horde ruled over despotically by a powerful male. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5696]
- The primal father of the horde loved no-one but himself; he loved others only insofar as they served his needs. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5697]
- Human groups exhibit the familiar picture of an individual of superior strength among a troop of equal companions, corresponding to the primal horde. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5696]
- The primal father's sexual jealousy and intolerance became in the last resort the causes of group psychology. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5698]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud argues that the primal horde theory combined with the totem meal explains how totemism arose from the ambivalent Oedipal attitude toward the father figure.
- Inference: The guilt arising from killing the primal father may be the foundation of all later religious and moral systems.
- Inference: Freud connects the uncanny and coercive characteristics of group formations to their origin from the primal horde.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The primal horde is explicitly described as a hypothesis, 'a Just-So Story,' not a verified historical fact.
