# Feeling of Inferiority

## Summary

A feeling of inferiority is a subjective value judgment about one's own worth, present in everyone. Originating from the German Minderwertigkeitsgefühl (feeling of having less worth), it arises in interpersonal relationships through comparison with others. Adler viewed healthy feelings of inferiority as necessary stimulants for growth, not diseases. The issue is not objective inferiority but subjective interpretation.

## Key facts

- The German word Minderwertigkeitsgefühl means a feeling of having less worth, relating to one's value judgment of oneself. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p79]
- Feelings of inferiority arise entirely through comparing oneself to others—without anyone to compare oneself to, one would not feel inferior. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p80]
- The philosopher who stands 61 inches tall realized that height is not objectively inferior—value depends on the meaning attributed to it. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p79]
- Value is based on a social context, and 'the problem of value in the end brings us back to interpersonal relationships.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p81]
