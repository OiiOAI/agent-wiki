# Habit loop

## Summary

A neurological loop consisting of three parts—cue, routine, and reward—that governs habits. Charles Duhigg's 'golden rule of habit change' states that to change a habit, one must keep the old cue and deliver the old reward but insert a new routine. This framework can be applied to reshape cognitive habits like self-serving bias.

## Key facts

- Habits operate in a loop of three parts: the cue, the routine, and the reward. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1135-L1137]
- In poker, a cue might be winning a hand, the routine taking credit for it, and the reward a boost to ego. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1138-L1141]
- The golden rule of habit change: keep the old cue and deliver the old reward, but insert a new routine. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1142-L1144]
- We can change cognitive habits by substituting what makes us feel good—getting the reward of positive self-narrative from being a good credit-giver, mistake-admitter, and learner rather than from deflecting blame. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1148-L1158]
- Reshaping habit requires time, preparation, practice, and repetition; it starts as a deliberative process and eventually becomes automatic. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1260-L1265]
