# Similarity-Attraction Effect

## Summary

The similarity-attraction effect is the well-documented phenomenon that people tend to like and be attracted to others who are similar to themselves. This principle operates across various dimensions including attitudes, interests, background, and appearance, and is reinforced by the tendency to underestimate how much similarity influences our liking for others.

## Key facts

- The similarity-attraction effect explains why people like others who share their characteristics, attitudes, and interests. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
- Research by LaFrance (1985), Locke and Horowitz (1990), and Woodside and Davenport (1974) demonstrated that mirroring and matching another person's posture and behavior increases liking. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
- People typically underestimate the degree to which similarity affects their liking for another person. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
- Evidence supports that ethnic groups tend to stay with their own in school settings, demonstrating natural in-group preference. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]

## Inferences

- Inference: The underestimation of similarity's influence on liking suggests that people may be unaware of how much their own preferences are shaped by perceived similarity, making it a powerful yet often unnoticed persuasion mechanism.
