# Social Dominance Hierarchy

## Summary

Social dominance refers to relationships wherein the goals of one individual prevail over the goals of another in a systematic manner. Dominance hierarchies have emerged as a major evolutionary force to drive dyadic asymmetries in social groups. The chapter argues that the emergence of dominance relationships is learned incrementally through accumulating positive and negative competitive feedback, treating this as a reinforcement learning problem.

## Key facts

- Social dominance hierarchies influence access to resources and mating partners, constituting a potent biological force binding together social behavior, well-being, and evolutionary success. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p205]
- Social dominance is most often applied to 'learned relationships,' shaped by a history of social victories and defeats within dyads. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p205]
- Dominance hierarchies spontaneously reemerge even when only subordinate or only dominant individuals are put together to form a new social group at each generation, implying that social environment dynamically tunes individuals' brains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p206]
- Dominance relationships are learned progressively by integrating positive and negative competitive feedback associated with specific individuals and other members of the social group. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p209]
- The Rescorla-Wagner learning rule: SDS(t+1) = SDS(t) + α × (R - SDS(t)) tracks the probability of winning in competitive interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p210]
- Transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS) applied over the rostromedial prefrontal cortex (RMPFC) exerted a causal influence over social dominance learning, reflected in higher learning rates associated with victories and lower learning rates associated with defeats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p210]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that dominance hierarchies spontaneously reemerge across generations suggests that the underlying neural mechanisms for detecting and implementing social hierarchies are hardwired but modulated by experience.
- Inference: The computational model using Rescorla-Wagner rule implies that social dominance learning follows similar principles to nonsocial reinforcement learning.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms driving correlations between gray matter volumes and social rank in macaques are unclear as of this writing.
- Uncertain: Whether neural network covarying with learning of social dominance in humans is a cause or a consequence of dominance emergence has been difficult to ascertain, though animal experiments suggest a profound impact.
