# Stepping Out

## Summary

Stepping Out is the second movement in the emotional agility framework. It involves detaching from and observing one's thoughts and emotions to see them for what they are—just thoughts, just emotions. This process creates psychological space between feelings and behavioral responses, allowing individuals to identify difficult feelings as they occur and find more appropriate ways of reacting. The concept draws on mindfulness research and techniques such as journaling to help people gain perspective on their experiences.

## Key facts

- Stepping Out involves detaching from and observing thoughts and emotions to see them as just thoughts, just emotions. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p18]
- This creates Frankl's open, nonjudgmental space between feelings and how we respond to them. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p18]
- James Pennebaker's research found that writing about emotionally charged experiences improved physical and mental well-being, reduced blood pressure, and improved immune function. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p76-77]
- People who developed insight through writing—using phrases like 'I have learned' and 'I now realize'—were those who thrived most. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p78]
- Using third-person self-talk has been shown to be an effective technique for distancing oneself from stress. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p89]
- Mindfulness practices can dissolve fusion between thought and thinker, impulse and action. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p82]
