# Life can be known only by life

## Summary

The proposition that 'life can be known only by life' (Jonas 1966) states that living beings possess firsthand evidence of purposiveness and inwardness because they themselves are living beings who experience their own bodily selfhood. This is a quintessential phenomenological claim: before being scientists, we are first and foremost living beings, and we thus have 'inside knowledge' of life through our own lived experience. Empathy is a precondition of our comprehension of the vital order.

## Key facts

- Jonas proposed that 'life can be known only by life' — that we possess firsthand evidence of purposiveness because we ourselves are living beings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p162]
- From a disembodied analytical standpoint, an organism would be resolved into fleeting physicochemical events, and all features of self-related autonomous unity would appear fictitious. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p162]
- The proposition that life can be known only by life is transcendental in the phenomenological sense: it concerns the conditions for the possibility of knowing life, given that we do actually have biological knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p163]
- Empathy is a precondition of our comprehension of the vital order, encompassing the coupling of our human lived bodies with the bodies of other beings we recognize as living. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p164]

## Inferences

- Inference: The source for the meaning of biological concepts like organism and autopoiesis is the lived body — our original experience of our own bodily existence — not observer-independent physicochemical description.
- Inference: This proposition overturns the uncritical standpoint of objectivism in philosophy and science by tracing the category 'organism' back to its cognitive source in the lived experience of our bodily being.
