# Two-stage model of choice

## Summary

The two-stage model proposes that decision-making consists of a valuation stage and a choice stage. The valuation stage learns and represents SV for actions and goods in the basal ganglia and frontal cortex through dopamine-dependent synaptic plasticity. The choice stage accomplishes selection through winner-takes-all competition in parietal and frontal motor control areas, where RSV signals compete until one option drives collicular burst neurons above threshold.

## Key facts

- The two-stage model divides decision-making into a valuation stage that learns SV and a choice stage that executes selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]
- Valuation involves learning mechanisms distributed in basal ganglia and frontal cortex through synaptic plasticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]
- Choice is accomplished through winner-takes-all competition involving the superior colliculus, orbitofrontal cortex, and related structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]
- Synaptic strengths connecting neurons in valuation areas are set by dopamine-dependent long-term potentiation and long-term depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p497]
- The winner-takes-all operation must be broadly distributed across structures ranging from superior colliculus to orbitofrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p500]

## Inferences

- Inference: The separation of valuation and choice stages explains why valuation-related activity persists throughout a trial in frontal areas but only until decision commitment in parietal areas.
- Inference: The existence of a circuit that 'decides to choose' suggests an additional mechanism controlling when the winner-takes-all process is triggered.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Which specific pathways from medial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum to posterior parietal cortex are critical for choice has not been determined.
- Uncertain: How the goods-based SV encoding in orbitofrontal cortex transforms to action-based encoding in posterior parietal cortex remains unknown.
