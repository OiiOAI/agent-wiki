# Induction and Essentialism in Children

## Summary

Research with children illustrates how the human brain constructs a belief in essences. In experiments, children are shown a red cylinder called a nonsense name like 'blicket' that lights up a machine. They then see a blue square also called 'blicket' and a second red cylinder not called 'blicket.' The child will expect only the blue square to light up the machine, inferring that each 'blicket' contains an unseen causal force. This phenomenon called induction is an efficient way for the brain to extend concepts by ignoring variation, but it also encourages essentialism. As children hear labels for emotions, their brains infer unseen causal forces inside people causing feelings.

## Key facts

- In experiments, children infer that objects called by the same nonsense name contain an unseen causal force that produces the same effect. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p183]
- Induction is an extremely efficient way for the brain to extend concepts by ignoring variation, but it also encourages essentialism. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p183]
- When children hear labels like 'sad' applied to varied behaviors, their brains infer an unseen causal force inside the child causing the feeling. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p183]
