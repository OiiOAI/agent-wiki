# Hormonal Systems and Social Dominance

## Summary

Cortisol (from the HPA axis) and testosterone (from the HPG axis) are the primary hormones involved in social dominance, reciprocally inhibiting each other physiologically and behaviorally. In primates, glucocorticoid levels are often higher in subordinate males during stabilized hierarchies, while higher-ranking males experience higher hormone levels during rank-threatening situations.

## Key facts

- Cortisol and testosterone are end products of two hormonal axes reciprocally inhibiting each other: the HPA axis and the HPG axis, respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p219]
- In primates, glucocorticoid levels are often higher in subordinate males whenever a dominance hierarchy is stabilized, and testosterone levels are generally independent of social rank. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p219]
- Higher-ranking males tend to experience higher testosterone and glucocorticoid levels than lower-ranking males whenever their dominance rank is threatened, such as during social instability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p219]
- Transient fluctuations in cortisol levels seem causally involved in the adaptive memorization of social dominance relationships induced by competitive encounters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p221]

## Inferences

- Inference: The variability in stress responses between high- and low-ranking animals depends on the amount of control exerted over stressor termination and predictability of occurrence.
- Inference: Disruption of cortisol signaling likely leads to imprecise representations of social dominance relationships.
