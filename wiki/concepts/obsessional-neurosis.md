# Obsessional Neurosis

## Summary

Obsessional neurosis is a defence neurosis characterized by compulsive ideas and actions that Freud traced to self-reproaches for pleasurable sexual acts performed during childhood. The condition involves a mechanism of repression followed by the return of repressed memories in distorted, compromise forms.

## Key facts

- Obsessional ideas are invariably transformed self-reproaches that have re-emerged from repression and relate to sexual acts performed with pleasure in childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p169]
- The typical course of obsessional neurosis involves three periods: childhood immorality (with seduction and sexual aggression), repression with symptom formation, and the return of repressed memories as compromise formations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p169-170]
- Secondary defence against obsessional ideas produces protective measures including ceremonials, phobias, collection of objects, and compulsive actions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p173]
- The aetiology of obsessional neurosis involves sexual aggression performed with pleasure, in contrast to hysteria which involves sexual passivity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p169]
- The sufferer from compulsions behaves as if dominated by an unconscious sense of guilt. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3004]
- Obsessional neurosis has as its precondition the repression of a portion of drive activity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2971]
- The mechanism of psychical displacement dominates the mental processes of obsessional neurosis, turning the most trivial matter into something of utmost importance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3007]
- Freud ventured to regard obsessional neurosis as a pathological counterpart of the formation of a religion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3007]
- Obsessional neurosis involves an inventory that includes: an erotic drive and revolt against it; a wish that has become compulsive; a fear that is already compulsive; a distressing affect; and an impulsion toward defensive acts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3288]
- In obsessional neurosis, the precipitating causes are retained in memory but deprived of their affective cathexis, leaving only a colorless ideational content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3312]
- In contrast to hysteria, obsessional neuroses invariably possess the characteristic of premature sexual activity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3289]
- Compulsive acts in obsessional neurosis often appear in two successive stages, where the second neutralizes the first. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3309]
- The conflict in obsessional neurosis is typically between love and hate directed at the same object. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3310]
- Obsessional neurosis is characterized by preoccupation with senseless thoughts, strange impulses, and compulsory actions that give no pleasure but cannot be omitted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4965]
- The obsessional neurotic displays a main characteristic of symptom displacement, replacing one pathological idea with another milder one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4966]
- Patients with obsessional neurosis often show over-conscientiousness and above-average intellectual gifts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4966]
- The sleep ceremonial involves exaggerated precautions with rational screen explanations that cover deeper symbolic meanings. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4970]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud distinguished obsessional neurosis from hysteria based on the passive/active nature of the childhood sexual experience, suggesting different mechanisms of libidinal development underlie each neurosis.
- Inference: Freud connects obsessional symptoms to unconscious wishes seeking discharge, with the symptoms representing compromises between unconscious impulses and defensive operations.
- Inference: The displacement mechanism is especially prominent in obsessional neurosis, allowing substitution of symptoms but not their removal.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether precocious sexual aggressivity always implies prior seduction experience requires further confirmation beyond Freud's initial impression.
- Uncertain: The exact ratio of constitutional versus experiential factors in obsessional neurosis causation is not fully elaborated in this passage.
