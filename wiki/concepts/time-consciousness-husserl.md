# Time-Consciousness (Husserlian)

## Summary

Husserl's account of time-consciousness has a threefold structure comprising primal impression, retention, and protention. Primal impression is directed exclusively toward the currently sounding note with no reference to past or future. Retention is directed toward the just-elapsed phase, still intended as just-past. Protention is directed toward the immediate future in an indefinite, anticipatory way. Their unified operation underlies our experience of the present moment as having temporal width.

## Key facts

- Husserl argues that it would be impossible to experience temporal objects if our consciousness of the present moment were the experience of a punctual or instantaneous now; consciousness of the present moment must be an experience of the present as having temporal width. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p311]
- Primal impression involves no reference to either past or future and by itself is insufficient for the perception of temporal objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p312]
- Retention is directed toward the just-elapsed phase, which though no longer actual, is still being heard in the mode of just-past. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p312]
- Protention is unfilled or indeterminate unlike retention, but involves a sense of anticipation; were the melody to stop abruptly or a wrong note to sound, we would be surprised. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p312]
- Husserl distinguishes retention (primary memory) and protention (primary anticipation) from recollection and expectation (secondary memory and anticipation), which are representational acts that presuppose the work of retention and protention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p312]

## Inferences

- Inference: The threefold structure of primal impression, retention, and protention is invariant and present synchronically as a unified whole throughout the course of experience, though specific experiential contents change moment to moment.
