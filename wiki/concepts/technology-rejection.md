# Technology Rejection

## Summary

An attitude exemplified by John and Sylvia Sutherland, who avoid and resent mechanical technology. They see technology as dehumanizing, alienating, and hostile—a 'death force' that reduces people to 'ants serving strange incomprehensible shapes.' This represents the romantic counterculture's flight from industrial civilization.

## Key facts

- John and Sylvia perceive technology as 'inhuman, mechanical, lifeless, a blind monster, a death force'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p17]
- They speak of 'it' or 'it all' and 'the whole organized bit' and 'the system' as inescapable forces. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p17]
- The narrator argues this attitude is 'self-defeating' because technology cannot be escaped, and Quality may be found in technology as readily as in nature. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p18]

## Inferences

- Inference: The narrator sees technology rejection as a form of self-imposed exile from practical reality, preventing people from caring about and improving their circumstances.
