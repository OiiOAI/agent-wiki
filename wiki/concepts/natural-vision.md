# Natural Vision

## Summary

Natural vision is Gibson's term for unconstrained visual perception, as opposed to laboratory vision. In natural vision, observers look around, walk up to interesting things and move around them to see from all sides, and go from one vista to another. Natural vision is panoramic and persists during locomotion. Gibson contrasted this with 'snapshot vision' (the frozen field of view when the eye is held still) and 'aperture vision' (seeing through a fixed aperture), which are artificial laboratory conditions.

## Key facts

- Natural vision involves looking around, walking up to something interesting and moving around it, and going from one vista to another. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p14]
- The visual system did not evolve for snapshot or aperture vision; the evidence suggests visual awareness is panoramic and persists during long acts of locomotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p15]
- The single frozen field of view provides only impoverished information about the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p15]
- The laboratory must be like life—the controlled displaying of information is vastly more difficult than the controlled applying of stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p16]

## Inferences

- Inference: Laboratory vision research (with head restraints, bite boards, tachistoscopes) studies impoverished conditions that may not reveal how natural vision actually works.
