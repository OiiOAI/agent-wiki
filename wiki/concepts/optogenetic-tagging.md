# Optogenetic Tagging

## Summary

Optogenetic tagging is a technique combining optogenetics with electrophysiology to definitively identify cell types during recording. By expressing light-sensitive proteins (e.g., channelrhodopsin-2) in specific neuron types in transgenic mice, researchers can confirm whether recorded neurons are dopaminergic, GABAergic, or other types based on their responses to light stimulation.

## Key facts

- Optogenetic tagging allows definitive identification of cell types during extracellular recording by expressing ChR2 in specific populations and confirming identity through light-evoked responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p64]
- Using optogenetic tagging, Cohen et al. confirmed that identified dopamine neurons in VTA show phasic activations to reward-predictive cues and reward, consistent with prediction error signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p64]
- Identified VTA GABA neurons show ramping activity from reward-predictive cue onset to expected reward time, supporting the idea that they provide dopamine neurons with an expectation signal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p65]

## Inferences

- Inference: Optogenetic tagging has resolved the problem that traditional electrophysiology could not definitively identify cell types, allowing more precise mapping of circuit functions.
- Inference: The ability to record from molecularly identified neurons has revealed distinct response properties of different cell types within the same brain region.
