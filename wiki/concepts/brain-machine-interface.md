# Brain-Machine Interface

## Summary

Brain-machine interfaces (BMIs) record neural activity reflecting motor intentions and convert this activity into control signals for external devices, enabling severely paralyzed patients to control computers, cursors, and robotic arms by thinking about movements.

## Key facts

- Brain-machine interfaces are now undergoing clinical trials in human neurological patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p909]
- Severely paralyzed patients with multi-electrode arrays in the primary motor cortex can control a cursor on a computer monitor to operate programs, compose messages, track moving targets, and control robotic arms merely by thinking about making corresponding movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p909]
- Brain-machine interfaces include four basic components: implantable electrode arrays, computer algorithms to extract motor intention signals, interfaces to convert signals into control signals for external effectors, and sensory feedback signals to improve performance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p909]
- Subjects can control brain-machine interfaces while engaged in other activities such as looking around or engaging in conversations, demonstrating that much cortical activity converting motor intention into action occurs subconsciously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p909]
- Electrodes in the primary motor cortex provide the best signals for continuous control of the time-varying details of the kinematics and kinetics of the trajectory of a robotic device. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p909]
- Signals from the premotor cortex and posterior parietal cortex may be more appropriate for specifying the overall goals and desired outcome of an action, such as the final target location, without elaborating how to accomplish the goal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p909]

## Inferences

- Inference: A brain-controlled interface using signals from different cortical areas might afford context-dependent control resembling normal voluntary behavior.
