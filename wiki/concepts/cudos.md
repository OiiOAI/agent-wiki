# CUDOS

## Summary

CUDOS is an acronym representing four normative standards for scientific truth-seeking communities developed by sociologist Robert K. Merton in 1942 (refined in 1973): Communism (communal data ownership), Universalism (uniform evidence standards regardless of source), Disinterestedness (vigilance against conflicts of interest), and Organized Skepticism (encouraging dissent and debate). These norms form a framework for designing effective decision groups and evaluating decision quality.

## Key facts

- CUDOS stands for Communism, Universalism, Disinterestedness, and Organized Skepticism as norms for scientific communities. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- The term 'Communism' in CUDOS refers to communal ownership of data, not the political system. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- Universalism means applying uniform standards to claims and evidence regardless of their source. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- Disinterestedness involves vigilance against potential conflicts that can influence evaluation. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- Organized skepticism encourages discussion among groups to promote engagement and dissent. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- When there is drift toward confirmation and away from accuracy, it is likely the result of failing to nurture one of Merton's norms. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
