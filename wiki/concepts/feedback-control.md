# Feedback Control

## Summary

Feedback control uses sensory signals to monitor and correct movement errors during execution. Closed-loop systems compare desired and actual states to generate error signals that shape motor commands. The effectiveness of feedback control depends on the gain of the system and is limited by sensory processing delays.

## Key facts

- Feedback or closed-loop systems monitor action before it is completed by comparing desired and sensed states to generate an error signal that helps shape the motor command. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p807]
- Proportional feedback control involves sensing the error between actual and desired position, with the corrective motor command being proportional to the error size and in a direction to reduce it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p807]
- The sensitivity of feedback control can be modulated by adjusting the gain, but high gain with delays can lead to instability and oscillations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p808]
- Feedback control cannot generate a command in anticipation of an error; it is always driven by an error signal, whereas feedforward control is based only on a desired state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p807]
