# Gene

## Summary

The fundamental unit of heredity, composed of DNA that is passed from one generation to the next. Most genes encode protein products through transcription into mRNA and translation into polypeptide sequences. Genes are located at specific positions (loci) on chromosomes, with humans having approximately 25,000 genes. The brain expresses more genes than any other organ, and diverse neuronal populations express different groups of genes.

## Key facts

- Genes are made of DNA, and it is DNA that is passed on from one generation to the next. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p92]
- DNA consists of two strands with nucleotides adenine (A), guanine (G), thymine (T), and cytosine (C), with A always pairing with T and G with C. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p92]
- Most genes encode protein products generated by translation of mRNA into polypeptide sequences of 20 different amino acids. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p92]
- A typical gene consists of a coding region (exons) separated by noncoding stretches (introns) that are deleted from mRNA before translation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p92]
