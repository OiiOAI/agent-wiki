# A-Type Potassium Channels

## Summary

A-type potassium channels (IA) are voltage-gated K+ channels that undergo rapid inactivation during sustained depolarization. They are expressed in cell bodies and dendrites where they regulate action potential threshold, timing, and backpropagation. Their steady-state inactivation by small depolarizations from rest allows modulation of neuronal excitability by small voltage changes.

## Key facts

- A-type current is formed primarily by Kv4 family α-subunits, which inactivate over milliseconds to tens of milliseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p275]
- A-type channels undergo steady-state inactivation at small depolarizations from rest, allowing modulation of channel availability by small voltage changes around resting potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p275]
- Kv1 channels containing Kv1.4 subunits or auxiliary Kvβ1 subunits also mediate inactivating K+ current, highly expressed in nerve terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p275]
