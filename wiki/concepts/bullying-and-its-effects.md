# Bullying and Its Effects

## Summary

Research shows that about one-third of American children are bullied, with appearance being the top reason. Peer victimization changes brain biology, disrupts stress regulation systems and neurocircuitry, and leads to physical and mental illness, social and academic difficulties. These effects persist into adulthood, affecting employment, relationships, and quality of life.

## Key facts

- Research shows about one-third of American children are bullied at some point, and appearance is the top reason. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p167]
- Peer victimization actually changes the biology of the brain, disrupting the development of the stress regulation system and related neurocircuitry. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p167]
- Bullying effects persist well into adulthood, contributing to problems with employment, relationships, and overall quality of life. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p167]
- Most teachers report feeling unprepared to deal with classroom bullying and are unlikely to interfere in disputes between students. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p171]
- LGBTQ youth experience greater rates of bullying, lower academic achievement, and higher dropout rates than their non-LGBTQ peers. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p170]
