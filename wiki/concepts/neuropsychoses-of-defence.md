# Neuropsychoses of Defence

## Summary

A group of nervous disorders including hysteria, obsessional neurosis, and certain psychotic states, characterized by symptoms arising through the psychical mechanism of unconscious defence against incompatible ideas. Freud later expanded this category to include paranoia.

## Key facts

- The neuropsychoses of defence share the characteristic that their symptoms arise through the mechanism of unconscious defence against ideas distressing to the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p162]
- The group includes hysteria, obsessional neurosis, and certain acute hallucinatory confusional states. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p162]
- Paranoia was subsequently identified as also belonging to this group, proceeding from repression of distressing memories. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p175]
- In each of the defence neuroses, repression has been shown to be the nucleus of the psychical mechanism, and what has been repressed is a sexual experience in childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1087]
- Hysteria effects repression by conversion into somatic innervation, obsessional neurosis by displacement along certain categories of associations, and paranoia by projection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1079]
- Part of the symptoms arise from primary defence—all the delusional ideas characterized by distrust and suspicion concerned with being persecuted by others. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1087]
- Other symptoms are to be described as symptoms of the return of the repressed and bear traces of compromise which alone allows them to enter consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1088]

## Inferences

- Inference: This category represented Freud's attempt to unify various 'functional' nervous disorders under a common psychodynamic framework based on the mechanism of repression.
- Inference: The shared aetiology of infantile sexual experiences across these different neuroses suggests that the form the neurosis takes depends on the specific defensive mechanism employed rather than the nature of the repressed material.
