# Optical Flow

## Summary

Optical flow refers to the pattern of change in the ambient optic array when an observer moves through the environment. The flow radiates outward from a focus of expansion at the horizon, with velocity increasing from the horizon downward. This invariant flow pattern specifies both the observer's locomotion and the unmoving terrain, with the invariants of structure never shifting despite the transformation.

## Key facts

- The flow of the ambient optic array during locomotion radiates from a focus of expansion on the horizon, with increasing rate of flow downward from the horizon. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p149]
- The invariants of structure and texture in the flow never shift—they specify the unmoving terrain, whereas the flow pattern specifies the observer's locomotion with reference to the terrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p147]
- During landing glide, flow vanishes at two limits: the horizon and the point of aim. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p149]
- To steer locomotion, the observer locates invariant features specifying a destination and keeps the focus of optical outflow centered on that item—magnifying the form that specifies the goal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p147]

## Inferences

- Inference: Optical flow is what makes it possible to 'see where one is going' during locomotion, whether flying, driving, or walking—the focus of expansion always specifies the aim point.
