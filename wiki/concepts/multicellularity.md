# Multicellularity

## Summary

Multicellularity arose when cells that were genetically identical remained attached after division rather than separating. This enabled the formation of organisms with division of labor among specialized cell types, transferring fitness from individual cells to the whole organism. True multicellularity differs from aggregative multicellularity (like slime molds) because all cells share the same genome, eliminating evolutionary conflict.

## Key facts

- Multicellular organisms start from a single fertilized egg cell that divides to create millions or billions of cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p76]
- When genetically identical cells come together, they form a truly new individual—a new unified self at a higher level of organization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p75]
- The germ line becomes dedicated to reproduction while the soma forms the body dedicated to ensuring survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p76]
- Multicellularity enabled organisms to become big enough to eat smaller creatures and avoid being eaten. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p76]
