# Unconscious Ideas

## Summary

Unconscious ideas, as discussed by Breuer in Studies on Hysteria, are ideas that remain beneath the threshold of consciousness but continue to exert influence on psychical processes and somatic functions. Breuer argued that the physical process underlying an idea remains the same whether the idea is conscious or unconscious, and that 'unconscious idea' is not a self-contradictory term but rather refers to ideas that have been expelled from association with the ego. Ideas may remain unconscious either because of their relatively small intensity or because they are 'inadmissible to consciousness' (Bewusstseinsunfähig) despite being intense enough to produce powerful somatic effects.

## Key facts

- All intuitive activity is directed by ideas which are to a large extent subconscious, while only the clearest and most intense ideas are perceived by self-consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p742]
- Ideas that are intense enough to cause powerful somatic phenomena and call out appropriate affects but remain outside consciousness may be described as ideas that are 'inadmissible to consciousness' (Bewusstseinsunfähig). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p743]
- When an idea immediately produces lively somatic consequences, the excitation flows off into somatic paths and loses the clarity which would otherwise mark it out in the stream of ideas. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p742]
- Unconscious ideas cannot be influenced or corrected by conscious thought and are often concerned with experiences which have lost their meaning for consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p744]

## Inferences

- Inference: Breuer's concept of unconscious ideas prefigures Freud's later emphasis on the unconscious as a primary determinant of hysterical symptoms.
