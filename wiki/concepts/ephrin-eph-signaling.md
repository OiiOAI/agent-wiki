# Ephrin-Eph Signaling

## Summary

Ephrins and their Eph receptor tyrosine kinases comprise a major axon guidance system. Ephrin-A proteins are GPI-anchored to the membrane while ephrin-B are transmembrane; they bind EphA or EphB receptors respectively. Forward signaling through Eph receptors is typically repulsive. Reverse signaling through ephrins can also occur. In the visual system, counter-gradients of ephrin-A in the tectum and EphA in the retina create the retinotopic map through repellent mechanisms.

## Key facts

- Ephrin-A proteins are membrane-tethered through GPI anchors and typically bind EphA kinases; ephrin-B are transmembrane and bind EphB kinases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1272]
- Ephrin-A5 is the inhibitory cue in posterior tectum that repels temporal retinal axons expressing high EphA, confining them to anterior tectum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1275]
- Ephrin-B expressed by midline glial cells at the optic chiasm deflects EphB-expressing temporal retinal axons ipsilaterally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1274]
- Ephrin gradients in the tectum and Eph receptor gradients in the retina are counter-directed, creating a coordinate system for retinotopic mapping. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1276]
