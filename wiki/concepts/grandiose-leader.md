# The Grandiose Leader

## Summary

Grandiose leaders are individuals with high levels of grandiosity who also possess talent and assertive energy, allowing them to rise to positions of power. Their boldness and confidence attract attention and create a larger-than-life presence that mesmerizes followers. Greene identifies six common illusions grandiose leaders create: 'I am destined' (presenting themselves as fated for greatness), 'I'm the common man/woman' (claiming to embody the masses while actually privileged), 'I will deliver you' (making large vague promises and creating cults through scapegoating), 'I rewrite the rules' (presenting themselves as mavericks who don't need conventional limits), 'I have the golden touch' (creating legends of never failing and deflecting blame), and 'I'm invulnerable' (taking ever-greater risks until a fatal hubristic maneuver ends in collapse.

## Key facts

- Grandiose leaders depend on attention and being adored by the public to validate their high self-opinion. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p382]
- Indira Gandhi positioned herself as 'Mother Indira' embodying the common people despite being educated in Europe and from political royalty. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p383]
- Michael Cimino's Heaven's Gate was one of the greatest disasters in Hollywood history, destroying his career when traditional contracts had served to rein in director grandiosity. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p385]
- Douglas MacArthur deflected blame so effectively that he created the myth of never losing a battle despite actually having lost many. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p386]
- Grandiose leaders often attempt to transfer skills easily—a movie executive tries theme park design, a businessman becomes a nation's leader—with fatal results. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p386]
- The fatal hubristic maneuver for grandiose leaders includes MacArthur's tour after Korea, Mao's Cultural Revolution, and Stan O'Neal's mortgage-backed securities decision. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p387]
- The best response to grandiose leaders is to deflate their sacred image through demystification and publicizing their actual record. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p387]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene suggests that followers share responsibility for grandiose leaders' power—without attention and adoration, such leaders cannot function
