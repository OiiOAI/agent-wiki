# Information Systems

## Summary

Information Systems are defined as systems that provide information about the interaction between Hard and Soft Systems. Examples include inventory control, cash flow forecasting, and sales activity summary reports. In the context of the Power Point Selling System, Information Systems track activity from benchmark to benchmark, revealing conversion rates, identifying where salespeople need help, and enabling calculation of the actual cost of making one sale.

## Key facts

- Information Systems are those that provide information about the interaction between Hard and Soft Systems, including inventory control, cash flow forecasting, and sales activity summary reports. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p241]
- The Information System tracks selling activity from Benchmark to Benchmark, revealing conversion rates between any two benchmarks. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p254]
- Information Systems can identify which salespeople are 'on the system' (using the Selling System verbatim) and which ones are off it. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p254]
- Gerber states that 'information is the glue that holds your Systems Strategy together' and 'tells you when and why you need to change.'. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p255]
