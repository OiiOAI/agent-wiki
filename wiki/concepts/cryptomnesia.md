# Cryptomnesia

## Summary

Cryptomnesia is a form of misattribution where people unknowingly reproduce another person's ideas or words while believing them to be their own original creations. It is the mirror image of false recognition: people misattribute novelty to something that should be familiar. Cryptomnesia results from unconscious priming and can be reduced by requiring people to pay careful attention to the source of their ideas.

## Key facts

- Cryptomnesia occurs when people produce from memory another individual's writings or ideas while unknowingly misattributing these creations to themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p145]
- Carl Jung discovered that Friedrich Nietzsche had taken parts of Also sprach Zarathustra from a story he had read in his youth by Justinus Kerner, concluding Nietzsche had forgotten the source. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p145]
- Cryptomnesia in experiments is attributable to unconscious priming: primed words from group discussions spring to mind easily later, and people fail to recollect they previously heard them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p147]
- Cryptomnesia can be reduced by requiring people to pay careful attention to the source of their ideas and to consider whether an idea is new or related to others' prior suggestions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p147]
