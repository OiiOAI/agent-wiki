# Great Man Theory

## Summary

Freud's psychoanalytic reworking of the concept that great individuals shape history, arguing that their effectiveness derives from fulfilling the masses' unconscious longing for a paternal authority figure. Moses exemplifies this as the great man who created the Jewish people through his person and idea.

## Key facts

- Freud argues that the masses have a powerful need for an authority who can be admired, before whom one bows down, by whom one is ruled—the origin being the longing for the father felt by everyone from childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7273]
- Freud states that the characteristics we equip great men with are paternal characteristics—the decisiveness, strength of will, energy of action, autonomy, and divine unconcern are parts of the picture of a father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7273]
- Freud declares it was Moses who created the Jews through imprinting upon them the trait of being God's chosen people, making them 'holy' and apart from others. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7270]

## Inferences

- Inference: The great man's influence operates through both his personality and the idea he represents—Moses embodied both the paternal authority and the monotheist idea, and possibly introduced traits of his own personality into the character of his God.
