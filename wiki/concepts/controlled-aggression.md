# Controlled Aggression

## Summary

Controlled aggression is the disciplined channeling of one's natural assertive energy into productive pursuits rather than destructive behavior. According to Greene, humans possess a powerful innate energy (willpower, assertiveness) that can manifest positively through ambition, persistence, fearlessness, and constructive anger, or negatively through chronic or passive aggression. The concept emphasizes that this energy cannot be repressed without creating an 'internal saboteur'—a persecutory inner voice that undermines confidence and creativity. The goal is not to eliminate aggression but to harness it productively, like focusing light through a lens.

## Key facts

- Controlled aggression involves channeling one's assertive energy into productive work and patient achievement rather than destructive outbursts. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p606]
- The internal saboteur is a psychological mechanism created when we suppress our assertive energy, causing it to turn inward as self-criticism and self-sabotage. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p623]
- The four positive elements of controlled aggression are: Ambition (specific, achievable goals), Persistence (relentless effort despite setbacks), Fearlessness (recovering boldness in asserting oneself), and Anger (controlled, targeted, reality-based). [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p624]
- Repressing aggressive impulses does not eliminate them but rather thwarts creative energies and dulls positive aspects of assertiveness. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p624]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene suggests that controlled aggression represents a middle path between chronic aggression (excessive outward expression) and repression (turning aggression inward against oneself).
