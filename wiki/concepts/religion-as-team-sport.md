# Religion as Team Sport

## Summary

Haidt's argument, following Durkheim, that religions function primarily to bind people into cohesive moral communities rather than to explain supernatural phenomena. Drawing on David Sloan Wilson's Darwin's Cathedral, the theory proposes that religions evolved as group-level adaptations—helping groups cooperate, suppress free riders, and outcompete other groups. The Durkheimian model emphasizes that religion is fundamentally about doing and belonging, not just believing. Religious rituals, practices, and sacred values create collective effervescence and forge bonds of trust that enable cooperation impossible for isolated individuals.

## Key facts

- Durkheim defined religion as 'a unified system of beliefs and practices relative to sacred things...which unite into one single moral community called a Church.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p269]
- Religious communes survive significantly longer than secular communes (39% vs 6% after 20 years) because rituals work best when sacralized. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p279]
- Putnam and Campbell found that religious belongingness (not believing) predicts neighborliness and charitable behavior. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p289]
- David Sloan Wilson argues that 'religions exist primarily for people to achieve together what they cannot achieve on their own.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p284]

## Inferences
