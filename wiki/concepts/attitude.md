# Attitude

## Summary

Attitude is the psychological lens through which each person perceives and interprets the world. Defined by Carl Jung as 'a readiness of the psyche to act or react in a certain way,' attitudes filter thousands of daily stimuli and determine not only what we see but actively shape what happens to us through self-fulfilling dynamics. Greene distinguishes between constricted (negative, fearful) attitudes and expansive (positive, open) attitudes.

## Key facts

- Jung defined attitude as 'a readiness of the psyche to act or react in a certain way'—a kind of unconscious orientation toward specific stimuli. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p265]
- Two people can experience the same city (Paris) in completely opposite ways based solely on their different attitudes—one feels alienated, the other enchanted. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p264]
- Attitudes have self-fulfilling dynamics: a fearful person makes others defensive, which confirms their belief that the world is hostile. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p267-268]
- William James stated: 'The greatest discovery of my generation is the fact that human beings can alter their lives by altering their attitudes of mind.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p263]
- Chekhov changed his circumstances not by changing Taganrog but by changing his attitude—opening himself to new experiences and possibilities. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p261]

## Inferences

- Inference: Attitudes originate from genetic predispositions, early childhood experiences and attachment patterns, and accumulated life experiences.
