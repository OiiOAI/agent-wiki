# Stimulus Information vs. Stimulation

## Summary

Gibson distinguishes between stimulation (energy absorbed by receptors) and stimulus information (structured patterns in light that specify the environment). This distinction is fundamental to his critique of sensation-based theories. Stimulation can occur without information (as in homogeneous light), and information pickup requires active perceptual activity rather than passive receptor activation.

## Key facts

- In a fog-filled medium, receptors would be stimulated but no image could be formed; the eye would be 'blind' when light entered it as when it did not. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p47]
- The retina is stimulated by light, but the eye (as part of a perceptual system) is activated by stimulus information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p47]
- Gibson found eight separate ways in which the term 'stimulus' was equivocal in modern psychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p49]
- The concept of the stimulus came from physiology where it meant whatever application of energy fires a nerve cell, but this concept was taken over uncritically by psychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p49]

## Inferences

- Inference: The traditional view that retinal inputs are the 'data' for perceptual processing in the brain fundamentally misunderstands the nature of visual perception.
