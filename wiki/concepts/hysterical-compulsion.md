# Hysterical Compulsion

## Summary

A pathological process in hysteria characterized by three features: unintelligibility, inability to be resolved by thought activity, and incongruity of structure. It involves an excessively intense idea (A) that forces its way into consciousness too often and produces inexplicable consequences, while the repressed idea (B) that justifies these effects remains unconscious. The outcome is that the symbol (A) has entirely taken the place of the thing (B), and analysis resolves the compulsion by making the underlying relation intelligible.

## Key facts

- Hysterical compulsion is characterized by: (1) unintelligibility, (2) inability to be resolved by the activity of thought, (3) incongruity in structure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p349]
- Analysis resolves hysterical compulsion immediately once it is explained, demonstrating that these characteristics are essentially one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p349]
- The pathological process involves displacement: something has been added to A which has been subtracted from B, constituting a primary process. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p351]
- The key formula is: A is compulsive, B is repressed (at least from consciousness). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p351]
- For every compulsion there is a corresponding repression, and for every excessive intrusion into consciousness there is a corresponding amnesia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p351]
