# Situation Awareness Errors in Aviation

## Summary

Situation awareness errors in aviation are categorized by Jones and Endsley (1996) using an SA error taxonomy. Level 1 errors (failure to correctly perceive the situation) account for 76.3% of errors, Level 2 errors (failure to correctly comprehend the situation) account for 20.3%, and Level 3 errors (failure to correctly project the situation) account for 3.4%. Common causal factors include information not observed (37.2%), information not available (11.6%), and memory errors (11.1%).

## Key facts

- Loss of Level 1 SA (failure to correctly perceive the situation) accounts for 76.3% of SA errors, with information not observed being the largest single cause at 37.2%. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p360]
- Loss of Level 2 SA (failure to correctly comprehend the situation) accounts for 20.3% of SA errors, with incorrect mental model being the largest cause at 6.4%. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p360]
- Loss of Level 3 SA (failure to correctly project situation) accounts for only 3.4% of SA errors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p360]
- Gibson, Orasanu, Villeda, and Nygren (1997) found workload or distraction (86%), communications or coordination (74%), and improper procedures (54%) were the most common causal factors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p359]
- Consequences of SA loss included altitude deviations (26%), violations of FARs (25%), heading deviations (23%), and traffic conflicts (21%). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p359]
- Dangerous situations resulted from 61% of SA loss cases analyzed. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p359]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since Level 1 errors dominate, training should prioritize attention management and information detection skills
