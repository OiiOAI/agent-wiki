# Talking Cure

## Summary

The 'talking cure' (also called the cathartic method) was Breuer's therapeutic technique for hysteria whereby patients recounted traumatic memories in hypnosis. Symptoms disappeared after describing their first occurrence, as the verbal account allowed abreaction of the emotional charge associated with the experience. This method, developed through Anna O.'s treatment, became foundational to psychoanalysis.

## Key facts

- In the talking cure, patients described symptoms and their first occurrence, and the symptom disappeared after being 'talked away.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p574]
- The procedure involved hypnotizing the patient, having them concentrate on the symptom, then in subsequent hypnosis providing a detailed account of the circumstances. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p574]
- The patient typically recounted experiences in reverse chronological order, beginning with the most recent and least important impressions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p607]
- Anna O. herself named the procedure the 'talking cure' or 'chimney-sweeping.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p575]

## Inferences

- Inference: The therapeutic effect depended on complete telling; incomplete stories produced no therapeutic effect.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the symptom disappearance was solely due to abreaction or partly due to suggestion could not be definitively separated.
