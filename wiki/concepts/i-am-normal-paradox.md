# I Am Normal Paradox

## Summary

The 'I am normal' paradox is the hypothesis that the world should look to others as it looks to us. This causes negotiators to unconsciously project their own style onto the other side. With three negotiation types existing, there's a 66 percent chance your counterpart has a different 'normal' than yours. This projection is one of the most damaging assumptions in negotiations and the Black Swan rule corrects it: treat others the way they need to be treated, not how you want to be treated.

## Key facts

- The 'I am normal' paradox causes negotiators to unconsciously project their own style onto the other side based on the assumption the world looks to others as it looks to them. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p173]
- With three types of negotiators, there's a 66 percent chance your counterpart has a different style than yours—a different 'normal.'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p173]
- The Black Swan rule states: don't treat others the way you want to be treated; treat them the way they need to be treated. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p174]
