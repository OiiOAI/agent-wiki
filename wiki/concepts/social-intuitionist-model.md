# Social Intuitionist Model

## Summary

The social intuitionist model is Haidt's theoretical framework for understanding moral judgment. It posits that moral intuitions arise rapidly and automatically, causing moral judgments, while moral reasoning typically follows after the fact to construct justifications for those judgments. The model is 'social' because it emphasizes that other people constantly influence our intuitions and judgments through casual observation, emotional contagion, and reasoned argument. The model challenges rationalist accounts that portray moral reasoning as a process of discovering truth.

## Key facts

- The model proposes that intuitions come first and strategic reasoning second, summarizing the core finding that moral reasoning usually serves to justify intuitions rather than discover truth. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p12]
- In the model, moral judgment arises from rapid, automatic intuitions (link 1), after which reasoning produces post hoc justifications (link 2). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p62]
- The social nature of the model comes from links where other people influence intuitions (link 3, reasoned persuasion) and trigger new intuitions through emotional appeals (link 4, social persuasion). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p63]
- Haidt published the model in 2001 in an article titled 'The Emotional Dog and Its Rational Tail' in Psychological Review. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p63]

## Inferences

- Inference: The model integrates Hume's philosophical claim that 'reason is, and ought only to be, the slave of the passions' with modern social psychology findings about automatic processing and social influence.
