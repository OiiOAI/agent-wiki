# Sit-and-Rise Test

## Summary

The Sit-and-Rise Test is an assessment that evaluates hip range of motion, leg and core strength, balance, and coordination. A 2014 study published in the European Journal of Preventive Cardiology found that an inability to get up and down off the floor without assistance is associated with greater risk of death in adults aged 51-80. The test involves sitting down and rising up from a cross-legged position, with scoring based on whether support is needed.

## Key facts

- The Sit-and-Rise Test assesses hip range of motion, leg and core strength, balance, and coordination. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L305-L307]
- A 2014 study in the European Journal of Preventive Cardiology found that inability to get up and down off the floor without assistance is associated with greater risk of death in adults aged 51-80. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L302-L305]
- In a 6-year study of 2,002 subjects aged 51-80, 179 (nearly 8%) had died, and researchers determined that higher test scores correlated with improved statistical likelihood of survival. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L302-L305]
- The test is scored from 10 (gold standard, no support needed) with one point deducted for each assist: bracing with a hand on a wall, placing a hand on the ground, touching knee to floor, supporting on legs, or losing balance. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L315-L320]
- Cross-legged sitting externally rotates the hips to their end range and creates a stable platform for sitting, similar to meditation postures. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L420-L423]

## Inferences

- Inference: The test serves as a proxy for overall physical capability and health status in aging populations.
