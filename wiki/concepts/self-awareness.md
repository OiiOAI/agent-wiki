# Self-Awareness

## Summary

Self-awareness is a crucial component of self-control involving comparing oneself to standards. It evolved because it helps self-regulation; animals that could monitor their behavior and adjust to social norms survived better. Self-awareness can be induced by mirrors, cameras, or simply focusing attention inward, leading people to act more consistently with their values.

## Key facts

- When people were placed in front of a mirror or told they were being filmed, they worked harder at tasks, gave more valid answers to questionnaires, and were more consistent with their values. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p107]
- In a Halloween experiment, children were much more likely to take only one piece of candy when a mirror in the room faced forward, even when disguised by costumes. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p108]
- Alcohol reduces self-awareness, causing people to lose self-control, get into more fights, smoke more, eat more, and make more sexual blunders. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p108]
- Self-awareness evolved because it helps self-regulation; without self-awareness, willpower is 'as useless as a cannon commanded by a blind man.'. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p108]
