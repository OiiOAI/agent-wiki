# Joke-Work (Witzarbeit)

## Summary

Freud introduces the term 'joke-work' (Witzarbeit) by analogy with 'dream-work,' referring to the psychical processes involved in constructing jokes, as distinguished from the processes involved in understanding them.

## Key facts

- Freud emphasizes the necessity of not confusing the psychical processes involved in constructing the joke (the 'joke-work' or Witzarbeit) with the psychical processes involved in taking in the joke (the work of understanding). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2700]
- The joke-work uses deviations from normal thinking—displacement and absurdity—as technical methods for producing a joking form of expression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2705]
- Joke-work submits thought material to condensation, displacement, and indirect representation—processes identical to those of dream-work. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2798]
- The hypothesis is that a preconscious thought is given over for a moment to unconscious revision and the outcome is at once grasped by conscious perception. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2799]
- A joke has the characteristic of occurring 'involuntarily'—the joke is there suddenly, ready-clothed in words, without advance conscious knowledge of what one will say. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2800]
- The peculiar brevity of jokes is not merely economy but a mark of the unconscious revision to which the joke thought has been subjected, analogous to condensation in dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2801]
- The infantile is the source of the unconscious; the thought that plunges into the unconscious seeks the ancient dwelling place of its former play with words. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2802]
- The joke-work receives its most powerful stimulus when strong purposes reaching down into the unconscious are present, explaining why neurotic people often fulfil the subjective determinants of jokes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2809]

## Inferences

- Inference: The term 'joke-work' establishes a formal parallel between the processes creating jokes and those creating dreams, suggesting psychoanalytic frameworks may illuminate humor.
- Inference: Freud suggests that joke-work represents a temporary regression to infantile thought processes, where condensation and displacement occur freely without conscious inhibition.
- Inference: The involuntary nature of jokes indicates that the actual construction happens unconsciously, with only the finished product entering consciousness.
- Inference: Neurotic individuals may have heightened aptitude for jokes because their unconscious processes are more accessible or active.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanism by which unconscious revision transforms preconscious thoughts remains speculative, as Freud acknowledges he has no further proof beyond the analogy with dreams.
