# Affective Niche

## Summary

The affective niche is the range of objects and events in an individual's world that cause fluctuations in their body budget and change their affective feelings. Humans have a large affective niche—almost anything can become emotionally significant. Macaques have a much smaller affective niche; the sight of a majestic mountain does not impact their body budget. Animals with small affective niches cannot form emotion concepts because they have insufficient experience with affective variation to learn the necessary categories.

## Key facts

- Humans have a large affective niche; many objects and events from the tiniest insect to the largest mountain cause fluctuations in body budget and change affective feelings. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p280]
- Macaques don't care about as many things as humans; their affective niche is much smaller, and the sight of a majestic mountain doesn't impact their body budget. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p280]
- A human infant receives rewards from caregivers including not just tasty treats but the day-to-day effects of tending to body budget; caregivers become part of the infant's affective niche. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p279]
- Human infants follow a caregiver's gaze to an object, then look back at the caregiver's face, who talks about what the infant is looking at—this shared attention allows humans to regulate each other's body budgets. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p280]
- Other primates do not share attention like humans; a mother macaque may follow her infant's gaze but will not look back and forth inviting the baby to wonder what is in her mind. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p280]

## Inferences

- Inference: The size of the affective niche helps explain the difference between human and animal emotion: animals with small affective niches cannot form emotion concepts because they never experience sufficient affective variation to learn the categories.
