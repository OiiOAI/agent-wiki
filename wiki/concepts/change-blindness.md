# Change blindness

## Summary

Change blindness is the failure to notice changes in visual scenes, especially when changes occur slowly or simultaneously across multiple features. It demonstrates that perceptual experience of change is itself a perceptual inference, not a direct readout of environmental changes.

## Key facts

- Physical change is neither necessary nor sufficient for the perception of change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- In change blindness, the entire lower half of an image can change color slowly without people noticing, even when looking directly at it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- Change blindness only occurs when people have not been primed to expect the color change; actively looking for change makes it easy to see. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- Perception of change is another perceptual inference, another variety of controlled hallucination. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between change in perception and perception of change is crucial — the experience of change is constructed by the brain, not directly given by environmental transformations.
