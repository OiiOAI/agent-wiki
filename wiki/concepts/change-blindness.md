# Change blindness

## Summary

Change blindness is the failure to notice changes in visual scenes, especially when changes occur slowly or simultaneously across multiple features. It demonstrates that perceptual experience of change is itself a perceptual inference, not a direct readout of environmental changes.

## Key facts

- Physical change is neither necessary nor sufficient for the perception of change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- In change blindness, the entire lower half of an image can change color slowly without people noticing, even when looking directly at it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- Change blindness only occurs when people have not been primed to expect the color change; actively looking for change makes it easy to see. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- Perception of change is another perceptual inference, another variety of controlled hallucination. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p130]
- In change blindness paradigms, subjects can fail to notice large changes in pictures they are viewing, even though the difference is obvious when pointed out. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1523]
- When the change involves a face, activity in the fusiform gyrus is elicited whether or not the subject is aware of the change, but conscious perception additionally involves activity in parietal and frontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- In one study, only one-third of observers noticed when a different person emerged from behind a door, despite being directly engaged with that person. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p74]
- In the 'invisible gorilla' study, approximately half of participants failed to notice a person in a gorilla costume walking through a basketball game while counting passes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p74]
- Change blindness is attributable to automatic encoding that does not proceed beyond a general level, resulting in poor recollection of specific details. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p75]
- College students showed higher levels of change blindness when interacting with construction workers versus college-age experimenters, because they encoded the former more generically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p75]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between change in perception and perception of change is crucial — the experience of change is constructed by the brain, not directly given by environmental transformations.
