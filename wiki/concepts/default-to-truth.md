# Default to Truth

## Summary

Default to truth is the tendency to give strangers the benefit of the doubt and assume they are being honest. Gladwell uses the Larry Nassar and Jerry Sandusky cases to illustrate how this deeply rooted human tendency allows predators to operate for years despite warnings, because victims, parents, coaches, and authorities cannot believe someone they trust could be capable of such crimes.

## Key facts

- Default to truth means assuming strangers are honest unless there is compelling evidence otherwise. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p75]
- Nassar had 37,000 child pornography images on his hard drive yet parents defended him. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p75]
- Trinea Gonczar was treated 856 times by Nassar and told a teammate 'He does that to me all the time!'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p76]
- Gonczar said 'I believed in you always until I couldn't anymore'—Gladwell calls this a perfect statement of default to truth. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p76]
- Graham Spanier defaulted to the most innocent explanation ('horsing around') when told about the Sandusky shower incident. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p80]
- Default to truth is not a crime—it is a fundamentally human tendency that society cannot function without. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p81]
- Humans have a universal tendency to assume others are being truthful, making deception detection extremely difficult for most people. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p56]
- Studies show people can correctly identify liars only about 54% of the time—barely better than chance. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p56]
- Even police officers trained in deception detection perform no better than untrained individuals at identifying lies. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p56]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Larry Nassar case demonstrates that default to truth operates even with overwhelming evidence because people cannot imagine their trusted protector as a predator
- Inference: The default to truth helps explain why strangers frequently misunderstand each other and why systems designed to catch deception (like SEC investigations of Madoff) often fail.
