# The Marshmallow Experiment

## Summary

Walter Mischel's marshmallow experiment tested four-year-old children's ability to resist eating one marshmallow to earn a second one 15 minutes later. Long-term follow-up found that children who showed most willpower at age four went on to get better grades, score 210 points higher on the SAT, earn higher salaries, have lower body mass index, and were less likely to abuse drugs. This was remarkable because childhood measures rarely predict adult outcomes.

## Key facts

- Children could eat one marshmallow immediately or wait 15 minutes to get two marshmallows. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p18]
- Children who waited the full 15 minutes scored 210 points higher on the SAT than those who gave in after 30 seconds. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p19]
- The high-willpower children grew up to earn higher salaries and had lower rates of drug abuse. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p19]
- This was remarkable because it is quite rare for anything measured in early childhood to predict adult outcomes at statistically significant levels. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p19]
