# Boyd as Post-Modern Strategist

## Summary

Boyd can be considered the first post-modern strategist based on the close resemblance of both his method and arguments with leading post-modern theorists. His work shares features with constructivism, deconstructionism, and structuration theory, and aligns with postmodern security studies.

## Key facts

- Boyd is considered the first post-modern strategist both in content and in his approach to making strategy, due to similarities with moderate post-modern social theorists. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p296]
- Boyd's concern with epistemology, the centrality of uncertainty, multispectrality, and his deconstructive method of analysis are shared with post-modern theorists. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p296]
- Key figures of postmodernism found inspiration in the same works Boyd studied, including evolutionary biology, quantum mechanics, cybernetics, and chaos theory. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p296]
- Boyd advocates a pluralist methodology for thinking about strategy, consistent with post-modern analysis. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p296]
- The title A Discourse suggests a post-modern view on how Boyd developed his ideas and what his work stands for. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p297]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's post-modern positioning explains his eclectic approach and his influence on contemporary security concepts like Network Centric Warfare and the Revolution in Military Affairs, which share postmodern epistemological assumptions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The characterization of Boyd as a post-modern strategist represents the author's interpretation rather than Boyd's self-identification.
