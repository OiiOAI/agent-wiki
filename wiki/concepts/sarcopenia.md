# Sarcopenia

## Summary

Sarcopenia is the medical term for age-related loss of muscle mass and strength. The body's process of building muscle begins to falter in one's thirties, marked by decreased muscle mass and quality, and accelerates after sixty. Sarcopenia is associated with decreased mobility, increased injury risk, and ultimately loss of independence. It can be staved off through resistance training and sufficient dietary protein intake.

## Key facts

- Sarcopenia is the medical term for age-related muscle loss. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1709-L1710]
- The body's process of building muscle begins to falter when we are in our thirties, marked by loss of muscle mass and strength and decrease in muscle quality. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1711-L1713]
- Sarcopenia accelerates with age and becomes associated with decreased mobility and increased risk of injury. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1713-L1715]
- People who have suffered great muscle loss may lose their independence. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1715-L1716]
- The best way to stave off muscle loss is by actively building muscle through resistance training with weights or rucking. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1718-L1719]
- Even without exercise, getting sufficient dietary protein helps maintain existing muscle. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1720-L1722]
- Ligaments, tendons, connective tissue, and cartilage—all integral to movement—are also built from protein. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1732-L1734]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book suggests that maintaining muscle through adequate protein intake is something everyone can control, unlike age-related decline which is often seen as inevitable.
