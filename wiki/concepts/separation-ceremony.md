# Separation Ceremony

## Summary

A therapeutic ritual created by Esther Perel to help couples mark the end of their marriage with dignity. Like a wedding ceremony marks the beginning of a union, separation ceremonies mark its end. Perel argues that such rituals facilitate transitions, honor what was, and create emotional coherence and narrative continuity. The ceremony may include the reading of goodbye letters that capture cherished memories, responsibilities, and wishes for the future.

## Key facts

- Perel proposes that just as marriage ceremonies mark the beginning of a union, rituals are needed to mark its end. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p286]
- Perel states: 'A marriage is the nexus of an entire life—history, memories, habits, experiences, children, friends, family, celebrations, losses, homes, trips, holidays, treasures, jokes, pictures.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p286]
- Perel believes the ceremony prevents the departing spouse's affair from eclipsing all positive aspects of an otherwise good marriage. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p287]
- In Clive's case, Perel notes that 'guilt was turned into gratitude, denial replaced by memory' through the ceremony. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p288]
