# Modern World-System

## Summary

The modern world-system is Immanuel Wallerstein's core concept, referring to a largely self-contained social system with a division of labor not circumscribed by political or cultural boundaries. It encompasses core areas that dominate and exploit, periphery areas that provide raw materials, and semiperiphery areas, organized around global capitalism.

## Key facts

- Wallerstein defined the world-system as a largely self-contained social system with a set of boundaries and a definable life span, composed internally of a variety of social structures and member groups. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p194]
- The world-system is held together not by consensus but by a variety of forces that are in inherent tension with the potential for tearing the system apart. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p194]
- The core geographical area dominates the capitalist world-economy and exploits the rest, while the periphery provides raw materials and is heavily exploited. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p195]
- Wallerstein argued that capitalism provided a basis for the growth and development of a world-economy without the aid of a unified political structure. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p195]
- Three things were necessary for the rise of the capitalist world-economy: geographical expansion through exploration and colonization, development of different methods of labor control, and development of strong states. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p195]
- The key to capitalism lies in a core dominated by a free labor market for skilled workers and a coercive labor market for less skilled workers in peripheral areas. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p198]
