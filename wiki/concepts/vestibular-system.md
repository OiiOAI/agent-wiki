# Vestibular System

## Summary

The vestibular system, also known as the inner ear, is a labyrinth of ring-shaped canals and tiny organs filled with fluid that detect different types of motion. When the head moves, fluid agitates hair cells that transmit impulses to the brain to maintain equilibrium.

## Key facts

- The vestibular system is composed of ring-shaped canals and tiny organs filled with fluid. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2395-L2410]
- Movement of fluid in these structures agitates minuscule hair cells that transmit impulses to the brain, directing the body to respond to maintain equilibrium. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2395-L2410]
- The vestibular system is one of three main systems involved in the interplay of sensation and reaction for balance. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2385-L2395]
- The three semicircular canals (horizontal, anterior, and posterior) sense head rotations, while the two otolith organs (utricle and saccule) sense linear motion and static orientation relative to gravity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p970]
- Hair cells transduce head motion into neural signals; deflection toward the kinocilium causes depolarization and increased afferent firing, while deflection away causes hyperpolarization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p971]
- The cupula in each semicircular canal is displaced by endolymph flow during head rotation, bending hair cell stereocilia and altering firing rates in vestibular nerve fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p972]
- Otoconia (calcium carbonate particles) embedded in the otolithic membrane allow otolith organs to detect shear forces from gravity and linear acceleration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p973]
