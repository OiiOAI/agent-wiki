# Vestibular System

## Summary

The vestibular system, also known as the inner ear, is a labyrinth of ring-shaped canals and tiny organs filled with fluid that detect different types of motion. When the head moves, fluid agitates hair cells that transmit impulses to the brain to maintain equilibrium.

## Key facts

- The vestibular system is composed of ring-shaped canals and tiny organs filled with fluid. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2395-L2410]
- Movement of fluid in these structures agitates minuscule hair cells that transmit impulses to the brain, directing the body to respond to maintain equilibrium. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2395-L2410]
- The vestibular system is one of three main systems involved in the interplay of sensation and reaction for balance. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2385-L2395]

## Inferences

- Inference: Age-related changes in the inner ear, particularly among impulse-transmitting hair cells, contribute to balance difficulties in older adults.
