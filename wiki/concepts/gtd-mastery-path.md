# GTD Mastery Path

## Summary

GTD mastery is presented as a lifelong practice with three tiers: (1) employing workflow fundamentals—capturing, processing, organizing, reviewing actions; (2) integrated life management—elevated focus on projects, roles, and longer horizons; (3) leveraging skills for creative expression—using freed cognitive space for meaningful work. Like learning an instrument, mastery requires practice and progression through these levels.

## Key facts

- GTD mastery is a lifelong practice similar to learning violin, tennis, or chess—there is no end to how good one can become. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p345]
- Mastery means the demonstrated ability to consistently engage in productive behaviors to achieve clarity, stability, and focus when desired—no matter the challenge. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p345]
- Tier 1 mastery involves basics: in-trays, meetings, e-mail, phone calls, agendas, waiting-fors, reference systems, list management, and Weekly Review. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p349]
- Tier 2 mastery features a complete projects inventory, working map of roles and accountabilities, and integrated total life-management system. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p350]
- Tier 3 mastery involves using freed-up focus for exploring elevated aspects of commitments and leveraging external mind to produce novel value. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p354]
- It can take up to two years to fully integrate the first tier of GTD mastery into one's life and work style. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p349]
- A hallmark of Tier 2 mastery is when pressure and challenges trigger utilization of GTD methodology rather than causing abandonment of it. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p353]

## Inferences

- Inference: The three tiers parallel Maslow's hierarchy—first satisfy basic needs (workflow), then safety and belonging (integrated system), then self-actualization (creative expression).
- Inference: The key transition point between Tier 1 and Tier 2 is when someone stops thinking about the system and starts thinking through the system.
