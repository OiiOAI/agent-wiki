# Recurrent Dreams

## Summary

Recurrent dreams are dreams with the same theme and content that recur over time. About 70% of adults report having had at least one recurrent dream during their life. Approximately 75% are negatively toned, often involving themes of being chased, failing exams, or being unprepared.

## Key facts

- About 70 percent of adults report having had at least one recurrent dream during their life, sometimes dating back to childhood. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p178]
- About 75 percent of recurrent dreams are negatively toned, with negative content categories appearing about ten times more frequently than positive ones. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p178]
- Hostile agents in children's recurrent dreams usually involve fictional or folkloric characters (monsters, witches), while adults typically feature human characters (burglars, strangers, mobs). [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p179]
- Recurrent dreams may represent the dreaming brain returning to established metaphors for concerns when similar experiences get tagged for further processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p179]
- Recurrent dreams and their cessation may be tied to how well we are dealing with emotional concerns and challenges in everyday life. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p199]

## Inferences

- Inference: Recurrent dreams remain unchanged because the dreamer reacts with the same thoughts, behaviors, and emotions each time, preventing resolution.
