# Dendritic Spine

## Summary

Dendritic spines are specialized input zones on dendrites that compartmentalize calcium signaling and are sites of excitatory synaptic input. Their thin necks create diffusion barriers that allow localized calcium accumulation and synaptic plasticity.

## Key facts

- Close to 95% of all excitatory inputs in the brain terminate on dendritic spines, avoiding dendritic shafts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p283]
- The thin necks of spines provide a barrier to diffusion of signaling molecules from the spine head to the dendritic shaft, allowing small Ca2+ currents through NMDA receptors to produce relatively large localized increases in [Ca2+]. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p283]
- When a backpropagating action potential is paired with presynaptic stimulation, the spine Ca2+ signal is greater than the linear sum of individual signals due to Mg2+ expulsion from NMDA receptors, producing supralinearity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p285]
- Spines implement synapse-specific local learning rules that are fundamental for neural networks to store meaningful information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p285]
