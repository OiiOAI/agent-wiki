# Framing

## Summary

Framing is the technique by which the same information presented differently leads to different decisions. We react differently to identical situations depending on how they are presented. Kahneman and Tversky's classic experiment showed that 'Option A saves 200 lives' vs 'Option A kills 400 people' produced opposite choices despite identical expected values.

## Key facts

- Kahneman and Tversky found that 'Option A saves 200 lives' was chosen by the majority, but reframed as 'Option A kills 400 people' produced a complete U-turn in choices. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p101]
- When choosing between '99% fat free' and '1% fat' meat, respondents ranked the first type as healthier even though both were identical. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p101]
- Glossing is a popular type of framing: a tumbling share price becomes 'correction', an overpaid acquisition price is branded 'goodwill', and a problem transforms into an 'opportunity' or 'challenge'. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p102]
