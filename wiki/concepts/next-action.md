# Next Action

## Summary

The Next Action is the next physical, visible activity required to move a project toward completion. It is the critical question in GTD's Clarify phase. Identifying the specific next action removes ambiguity and transforms vague intentions into executable steps. Actions that will take less than two minutes should be done immediately rather than deferred.

## Key facts

- The next action is the next physical, visible activity that needs to be engaged in to move current reality toward completion. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p72]
- If an action will take less than two minutes, it should be done at the moment it is defined—this is the Two-Minute Rule. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p72]
- Things rarely get stuck because of lack of time; they get stuck because what 'doing' would look like and where it happens hasn't been decided. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p52]
- The next-action decision is central to the entire workflow diagram. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p80]
- Only action steps are executable—you cannot 'do' a project, only perform actions related to it. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p79]
