# Group Selection

## Summary

Group selection is the hypothesis that natural selection acts primarily between groups of organisms, favoring traits that benefit the group even at the expense of individuals. Dawkins argues this theory is wrong because within any group of altruists, selfish individuals will always outcompete altruists, leading to the group's degeneration. The book presents individual selection (or gene selection) as the correct alternative.

## Key facts

- Group selection theory suggests that organisms will behave altruistically for the good of the species, such as limiting birth rates to avoid overpopulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p8]
- Dawkins argues that even in a group of altruists, a selfish minority will be more likely to survive and have children who inherit selfish traits, eventually overrunning the altruistic group. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p33]
- Group extinction is a slow process compared with the rapid cut and thrust of individual competition, allowing selfish individuals to prosper at the expense of altruists even while groups decline. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p33]
- The group selection theory now commands little support within professional biologists who understand evolution, despite having great intuitive appeal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p34]
- Konrad Lorenz and Robert Ardrey were cited as proponents of group selection who did not realize their statements contravened orthodox Darwinian theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p34]
- Darwin argued that cohesive tribes would succeed over less cohesive tribes because 'selfish and contentious people will not cohere, and without coherence nothing can be effected.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p213]
- George Williams and Richard Dawkins argued that group selection never actually happened, claiming apparent altruism could always be explained by individual-level selection. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p218]
- Multilevel selection theory posits that natural selection operates simultaneously at individual and group levels, sometimes favoring different traits. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p214]
- Group selection requires mechanisms to suppress free riding within groups, such as gossip, punishment, and shared moral matrices. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p199]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book argues that group selection is an intuitive but logically flawed explanation for altruism, which cannot survive the logic of individual competition.
- Inference: group selection shaped human psychology, it would explain both our capacity for selfishness (individual selection) and our groupish tendencies (group selection).
