# Dynamic Sensorimotor Approach

## Summary

The dynamic sensorimotor approach understands perceptual consciousness through dynamic sensorimotor relations among neural activity, the body, and the world, rather than through the intrinsic properties of neural activity. According to sensorimotor contingency theory, perceptual experience is a skillful activity constituted by the perceiver's implicit practical knowledge of how sensory stimulation varies with movement.

## Key facts

- The dynamic sensorimotor hypothesis states that changes in qualitative expression are to be explained not just in terms of the properties of sensory inputs and of the brain region that receives them, but in terms of dynamic patterns of interdependence between sensory stimulation and embodied activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p251]
- Perceptual experience is not an inner event or state of the brain but a skillful activity constituted in part by the perceiver's implicit, practical knowledge of the way sensory stimulation varies with movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p252]
- 'Cortical dominance' occurs when cortical activation from a new peripheral input source gives rise to experience with a qualitative character normally associated with cortical activity in that area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p251]
- 'Cortical deference' occurs when cortical activity in a given area takes its qualitative expression from the character of its nonstandard or new input source. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p251]
