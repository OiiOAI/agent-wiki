# Semantic Memory

## Summary

Semantic memory is the store of facts, beliefs, and concepts about the world that is not tied to a particular learning experience. Research on Henry Molaison demonstrated that semantic memory becomes consolidated in cortical areas independent of the hippocampus, unlike episodic autobiographical memory.

## Key facts

- Semantic memory is not linked to a particular learning experience; for example, one may not remember when or where they learned that Paris is the capital of France. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p220]
- Henry could recall public events with considerable clarity, including the stock market crash of 1929, Teddy Roosevelt at San Juan Hill, FDR, and World War II. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p220]
- Henry recognized people in 33 of 36 family photos, indicating preserved personal semantic knowledge of his family history. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p221]

## Inferences

- Inference: Semantic memories and episodic autobiographical memories are processed differently, with only semantic memories eventually becoming independent of hippocampal structures.
