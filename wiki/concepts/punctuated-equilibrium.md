# Punctuated Equilibrium

## Summary

Punctuated equilibrium is a theory proposed by Stephen Jay Gould and Niles Eldredge in 1972 challenging the gradualist view of evolution. It argues that evolution is not gradual but occurs in long periods of stasis punctuated by rapid, dramatic changes. This challenged sociobiological thinking by questioning whether every slight behavioral difference has evolutionary consequence during stasis periods.

## Key facts

- Punctuated equilibrium proposes that most of the time nothing happens evolutionarily, and change occurs in intermittent rapid, dramatic lurches. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p388]
- Gould and Eldredge argued that rapid transitions could be triggered by massive selective factors that kill most of a species, leaving only survivors with obscure genetic traits. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p388]
- Punctuated equilibrium challenged sociobiology's gradualism assumption that every smidgen of difference in fitness is evolutionarily consequential. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p389]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The debate between gradualists and punctuationists continues, with evidence supporting both modes of evolutionary change depending on the genes and traits involved.
