# Myelin Sheath

## Summary

The myelin sheath is an insulating material produced by oligodendrocytes in the CNS and Schwann cells in the PNS that allows rapid conduction of electrical signals along axons. Myelin consists of thin sheets of membrane wrapped concentrically around axons. One Schwann cell produces a single myelin sheath for one axon segment, while one oligodendrocyte produces sheaths for up to 30 axons. Myelin has bimolecular layers of lipid interspersed between protein layers, with 70% lipid and 30% protein. The regular lamellar structure enables saltatory conduction, where signals jump from node to node at speeds up to 100 m/s in humans.

## Key facts

- One Schwann cell produces a single myelin sheath for one segment of one axon, whereas one oligodendrocyte produces myelin sheaths for segments of as many as 30 axons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p151]
- Myelin composition is similar to the plasmalemma, consisting of 70% lipid and 30% protein with high concentrations of cholesterol and phospholipid. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p153]
- CNS myelin has two major proteins: myelin basic protein, situated on the cytoplasmic surface of compact myelin, and proteolipid protein, a hydrophobic integral membrane protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p153]
- The density of Na+ channels at nodes of Ranvier is approximately 50 times greater than in myelin-sheathed regions, enabling saltatory conduction at speeds up to 100 m/s in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p152]
- In the peripheral nervous system, myelin contains major proteins P0 and PMP22, and autoimmune reactions to these proteins produce the demyelinating peripheral neuropathy called Guillain-Barré syndrome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p153]
