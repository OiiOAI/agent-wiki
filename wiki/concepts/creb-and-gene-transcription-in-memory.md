# CREB and Gene Transcription in Memory

## Summary

The cAMP-PKA-CREB pathway is a conserved molecular cascade converting short-term synaptic plasticity into long-term memory storage through gene transcription and new protein synthesis. Activation of adenylyl cyclase produces cAMP, which activates PKA; the catalytic subunit translocates to the nucleus where it phosphorylates CREB-1, recruiting CBP acetyltransferase, which modifies chromatin structure and activates transcription of target genes including C/EBP and ubiquitin hydrolase. Degradation of PKA's regulatory subunit creates constitutively active PKA, while C/EBP triggers downstream genes for synaptic growth.

## Key facts

- Phosphorylation of CREB-1 by PKA recruits CREB-binding protein (CBP), which has acetyltransferase activity that acetylates histone proteins, loosening their grip on DNA and enabling transcriptional machinery to access gene promoters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1522]
- CREB-1 activation induces expression of ubiquitin carboxyterminal hydrolase, which degrades approximately 25% of PKA regulatory subunits, resulting in free catalytic subunits that remain active long after cAMP levels return to baseline—a molecular mechanism for persistent kinase activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1523]
- The transcription factor C/EBP, activated by CREB-1, forms homo- and heterodimers that act on downstream genes triggering growth of new synaptic connections supporting long-term memory storage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1523]
