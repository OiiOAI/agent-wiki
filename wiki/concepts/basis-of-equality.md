# Basis of Equality

## Summary

Rawls argues that the capacity for moral personality—being capable of having a conception of one's good and a sense of justice—is the sufficient condition for being entitled to equal justice. This capacity is not at all stringent and refers to a potentiality that is ordinarily realized in due course.

## Key facts

- Moral persons are distinguished by two features: they are capable of having (and are assumed to have) a conception of their good (as expressed by a rational plan of life), and they are capable of having (and are assumed to acquire) a sense of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p448]
- The capacity for moral personality is a sufficient condition for being entitled to equal justice; nothing beyond the essential minimum is required. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p448]
- By giving justice to those who can give justice in return, the principle of reciprocity is fulfilled at the highest level. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p452]
