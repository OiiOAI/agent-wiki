# Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM)

## Summary

Continuous Glucose Monitoring is a medical device that provides real-time blood glucose readings via a subcutaneous sensor, enabling individuals to understand their personal glucose responses to foods, exercise, sleep, and stress. CGM has become an essential tool in metabolic health management, allowing for personalized dietary adjustments rather than relying on generalized nutritional guidelines.

## Key facts

- CGM consists of a microscopic filament sensor implanted in the upper arm, attached to a fingertip-sized transmitter that sends data to the patient's phone in real time. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p331]
- CGM provides continuous, real-time information on blood glucose levels, showing moment-by-moment responses to foods and capturing historical averages and variance. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p331]
- The author recommends keeping average glucose at or below 100 mg/dL, with a standard deviation of less than 15 mg/dL. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p334]
- A 2011 study of 20,000 people found that risk of mortality increased monotonically with average blood glucose levels, even in the nondiabetic range. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p332]
- A 2019 study found that people in the highest quartile of glucose variability had a 2.67 times greater risk of mortality than those in the lowest quartile. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p333]
- CGM costs about $120/month and is currently available by prescription but can be obtained by nondiabetics through online metabolic health start-ups. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p332]
- Sleeping just five to six hours (versus eight hours) accounts for about a 10 to 20 mg/dL jump in peak glucose response. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p337]

## Inferences

- Inference: CGM enables personalized nutrition by revealing individual responses to specific foods, which varies significantly between people.
- Inference: The Hawthorne effect of wearing CGM creates behavior modification that persists even after stopping device use.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Long-term RCTs specifically testing CGM benefits in nondiabetic populations have not yet been published as of the book's writing.
