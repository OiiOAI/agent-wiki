# Neurotransmitter Systems

## Summary

Neurotransmitters are molecules that convey information from one neuron to another at synapses. They include amino acid transmitters (glutamate, GABA), biogenic amines (dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin), neuropeptides, and acetylcholine, each with distinct anatomical distributions and psychological functions.

## Key facts

- Sir Henry Dale and Otto Loewi received the 1936 Nobel Prize for discovering chemical transmission at synapses, identifying acetylcholine as the first neurotransmitter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p59]
- Neurotransmitters are categorized into four groups: amino acids (glutamate, GABA), biogenic amines (DA, NE, 5-HT), neuropeptides, and miscellaneous (ACh). [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p60]
- Julius Axelrod, Ulf von Euler, and Bernard Katz received the 1970 Nobel Prize for discoveries concerning humoral transmitters in nerve terminals and mechanisms for their storage, release, and inactivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p59]
- Rosalyn Yalow received the 1977 Nobel Prize for development of radioimmunoassays of peptide hormones, enabling discovery of brain neuropeptides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p59]
