# Interactive iterative model of skill acquisition

## Summary

The interactive iterative model, proposed by Shebilske, Goettl, and Regian (1999), challenges single cycle models of skill development. Unlike single cycle models that suggest a progressive transition from controlled to automatic processing, the interactive iterative model applies learning phases (controlled, intermediate, and automatic) to interactive task parts rather than whole tasks, applying phases iteratively within components. As skill develops, controlled processes take on more intellectually challenging strategic problems and additional responsibilities of monitoring automatic processes and correcting their failures.

## Key facts

- The interactive iterative model applies learning phases (controlled, intermediate, and automatic) to interactive task parts rather than to whole tasks and applies phases iteratively within components. - As skill develops, controlled processes take on more intellectually challenging strategic problems as automatic processes take over lower order functions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p314]
- Controlled cognitive processes also take on additional responsibilities of monitoring the outcome of automatic cognitive processes and correcting their failures. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p314]
- Single cycle models suggest controlled cognitive processes dominate early in training and become less important later as automatic processes take over. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p314]
