# Constructive Episodic Simulation Hypothesis

## Summary

The constructive episodic simulation hypothesis, proposed by Schacter and Addis, posits that episodic memory's capacity for flexible recombination of past experiences serves an adaptive function in imagining future events. The same recombination processes that enable creative future simulation also contribute to memory errors like source confusion and conjunction errors.

## Key facts

- fMRI studies show that remembering past experiences and imagining future experiences activate highly similar brain networks, including medial temporal lobe, frontal lobe, posterior cingulate, and lateral parietal regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p278]
- Patient KC, with complete episodic memory destruction from brain damage, could not imagine future experiences any better than he could recall past ones, demonstrating shared mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p277]
- Encoding DRM word lists with future-oriented thinking produces higher false recall of non-presented associates, linking future simulation processes to memory errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p279]
- Carpenter's research shows that making correct associative inferences about relationships between episodes increases subsequent misattribution errors for contextual details of those episodes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p280]
