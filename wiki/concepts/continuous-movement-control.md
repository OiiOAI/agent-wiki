# Continuous Movement Control

## Summary

Continuous movement control in Active Inference treats motor commands as predictions. Movement results from fulfillment of proprioceptive predictions by action through spinal reflexes. The equilibrium point hypothesis treats motor control as enacted by reflex arcs drawing limbs toward equilibrium points set by descending motor signals.

## Key facts

- In continuous Active Inference, action is part of the generative process, not the generative model, which only deals with variables influenced by states external to a Markov blanket. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p154]
- A simple point attractor model has dynamics f(x,v) = v - x, where v is the attracting point that x always flows toward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p154]
- Newtonian dynamics recognize that forces change velocity (inducing acceleration), not position: f(x,v) = [x2; (κ/m)(v - x1)] where x1 is position, x2 is velocity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p155]
- Active Inference movement control does not require specification of inverse models that are widely used in other formulations of motor control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p154]
