# Power Dynamics and Eroticism

## Summary

Power imbalances—dominance, submission, aggression, control—are essential components of erotic desire that modern egalitarian relationships try to eliminate. Perel argues that consensual power play in the bedroom is not pathological but a legitimate source of erotic excitement that cannot be reconciled with the ideals of fairness and equality.

## Key facts

- The very dynamics of power and control that are challenging in an emotional relationship become highly desirable when eroticized—dependency, surrender, jealousy, and aggression transform into excitement. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p63]
- Sexual desire and good citizenship don't play by the same rules; egalitarianism can produce boring sex when carried too punctiliously into the bedroom. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p60]
- Only the free can choose to pretend to be prisoners—being able to play with power roles indicates you're no longer controlled by them; play has the potential to disrupt gender categorization. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p64]
- Aggression is the shadow side of love and an intrinsic component of sexuality that cannot be entirely excised from sexual relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p71]

## Inferences

- Inference: The rise of S&M and D/S practices in mainstream culture represents, as Camille Paglia suggests, a collective fantasy that subverts egalitarian values by welcoming dependency and recognizing inequities.
