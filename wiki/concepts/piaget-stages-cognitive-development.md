# Piaget's Stages of Cognitive Development

## Summary

Jean Piaget's stage theory of cognitive development proposes four stages: Sensorimotor (birth to ~2 years) where thought concerns only direct sensory experience and object permanence develops around 8 months; Preoperational (~2-7 years) where symbolic thought and imaginative play emerge but reasoning remains intuitive and prelogical; Concrete Operational (7-12 years) where logical thinking about concrete objects develops but abstract thinking remains difficult; and Formal Operational (adolescence onward) where abstract reasoning and metacognition reach adult levels.

## Key facts

- Piaget's Sensorimotor stage (birth to ~2 years) is characterized by thought concerning only what can be directly sensed and explored, with object permanence developing around 8 months. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p180]
- Piaget's Preoperational stage (~2-7 years) allows symbolic thought and imaginary play but reasoning remains intuitive and prelogical, shown by failure of 'conservation of volume'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p180]
- Piaget's Concrete Operational stage (7-12 years) allows logical thinking about concrete objects but abstract thinking remains difficult, with proverbs interpreted literally. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p181]
- Piaget's Formal Operational stage (adolescence onward) involves approaching adult levels of abstraction, reasoning, and metacognition. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p181]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The assumption that cognitive development occurs in discrete stages rather than continuous progression is debated
- Uncertain: Stage approaches may overweight cognition while underexploring social and emotional influences on development
