# Mean-Variance Model

## Summary

The mean-variance model proposes that decision makers evaluate risky options by trading off expected reward (mean) against risk (variance). This framework, dominant in finance, contrasts with expected utility approaches but can be linked through Taylor series approximations. Neuroimaging studies show separate brain regions encode expected reward and risk variance, which are then integrated in prefrontal cortex for value computation.

## Key facts

- In the mean-variance model, the decision maker trades off between mean (expected reward) and variance (risk), with utility approximately U(R) ≈ U(μ) + U'(μ)σ² where μ is expected return and σ² is variance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p358]
- Brain activation shows separate encoding of expected reward (linear with probability) and risk (quadratic, peaking at 50% probability of winning) in different regions including ventral striatum and insula. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p361]
- Activation in medial prefrontal cortex reflects expected utility, increasing with expected reward for both risk-averse and risk-seeking subjects, but decreasing with risk for risk-averse subjects and increasing for risk-seeking subjects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p361]
