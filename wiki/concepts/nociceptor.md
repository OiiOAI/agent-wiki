# Nociceptor

## Summary

Nociceptors are specialized sensory receptors that detect noxious stimuli capable of causing tissue damage. They are the free nerve endings of primary sensory neurons with cell bodies in dorsal root ganglia or trigeminal ganglia. Three main classes exist: thermal nociceptors (Aδ fibers responding to temperature extremes), mechanical nociceptors (Aδ fibers responding to intense pressure), and polymodal nociceptors (C fibers responding to mechanical, chemical, and thermal stimuli). A fourth class, silent nociceptors, is found in viscera and becomes active during inflammation.

## Key facts

- Thermal nociceptors are activated by extremes in temperature, typically greater than 45 degrees C or less than 5 degrees C. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p583]
- Mechanical nociceptors are activated optimally by intense pressure applied to the skin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p583]
- Polymodal nociceptors can be activated by high-intensity mechanical, chemical, or thermal stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p583]
- First pain is transmitted by Aδ fibers that carry information from damaged thermal and mechanical nociceptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p584]
- Second pain is transmitted by C fibers that convey signals from polymodal nociceptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p584]
- TRPV1 is expressed selectively by nociceptive neurons and mediates pain-producing actions of capsaicin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p584]
- Mutations in the SCN9A gene can result in complete inability to sense pain while preserving normal sensory responses to touch, mild temperature, proprioception, tickle, and pressure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p584]

## Inferences

- Inference: The diversity of nociceptor types and their distinct conduction velocities (Aδ fibers at 5-30 m/s, C fibers less than 1.0 m/s) explain the characteristic temporal pattern of pain perception, with rapid first pain followed by slower second pain.
