# Hard Science Ethos

## Summary

The fundamental organizing ethos of hard science is a framework Munger advocates the soft sciences should adopt more intensely. It consists of four principles: ranking disciplines by fundamentalness; mastering to tested fluency the essential parts of the four-discipline combination (math, physics, chemistry, engineering); never practicing cross-disciplinary absorption without attribution; and when the reductionist approach doesn't work, hypothesizing and testing new principles.

## Key facts

- The fundamental organizing ethos of hard science requires: (1) ranking and using disciplines in order of fundamentalness; (2) mastering to tested fluency the truly essential parts of math, physics, chemistry, and engineering. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p323]
- The ethos requires never practicing cross-disciplinary absorption without attribution and preferring the most fundamental explanation for any phenomenon. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p323]
- Biology, starting 150 years ago with a descriptive mess, has gradually absorbed this ethos with marvelous results. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p323]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger credits his absorption of this ethos early in life as enabling his success in multidisciplinary problem-solving across business, psychology, and other soft fields.
