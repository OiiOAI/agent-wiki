# Orienting Response

## Summary

The orienting response, which Ivan Pavlov called the 'What is it?' reflex, is an orchestrated set of changes in the brain and body in response to something new or surprising. It includes pupil enlargement, blood flow changes, and a release of neuromodulators. This response is evolutionarily valuable for identifying and responding to the unexpected.

## Key facts

- Ivan Pavlov noticed that animals had a characteristic response to changes or new things in the environment, which he called the 'What is it?' reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p132]
- The orienting response includes pupil enlargement, increased brain blood flow, constricted body blood flow, and a brief shot of neuromodulators including dopamine, noradrenaline, and acetylcholine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p133]
- Thomas Grunwald found that electrical activity in the hippocampus spiked in response to surprising noises in healthy subjects, but this response was virtually absent in patients with hippocampal dysfunction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p134]
- Grunwald found that the orienting response is one of the most reliable indicators of a functioning hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p134]
