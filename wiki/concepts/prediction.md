# Prediction

## Summary

The book argues that predicting human behavior, particularly violent behavior, is possible and that everyone is naturally expert at it through their lifelong study of human behavior. The author contends that violence always has purpose and meaning to the perpetrator.

## Key facts

- The author claims humans successfully make high-stakes predictions about behavior of thousands of people daily while navigating traffic. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p17]
- Every thought is preceded by a perception, every impulse by a thought, every action by an impulse, and behavior patterns are detectable. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p17]
- The author states that predicting violent behavior is easier than predicting consumer reactions or jury behavior, contrary to popular belief. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p17]
- The human violence most feared as 'random' and 'senseless' is neither—it always has purpose and meaning to the perpetrator. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p17]
