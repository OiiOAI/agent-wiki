# Stimulus Information

## Summary

Stimulus information refers to the structured aspects of ambient energy that specify external sources, as opposed to stimulus energy itself which merely activates receptors without carrying information about its source. Gibson distinguishes this from the traditional stimulus-response formula, arguing that while stimulus energy can be measured, only stimulation that comes in a structured array and changes over time specifies its external source.

## Key facts

- A stimulus in the strict physiological sense is anything that touches off a receptor or causes a response; it carries no information about its source. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p82]
- A stimulus is temporary—it must begin and end. When it persists, sensory adaptation occurs, meaning a permanent object cannot be specified by a stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p82]
- The presence of stimulus information cannot be said to cause perception; perception is not a response to a stimulus but an act of information pickup. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p82]
- Perceptual awareness, unlike sensory awareness, does not have any discoverable stimulus threshold; it depends on the age, learning, and motivation of the perceiver. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p82]
- Stimulus information is not lost from the environment when it is gained by the observer—there is no conservation of information and it is not limited in amount. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p83]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gibson's concept of stimulus information fundamentally differs from communication-based information theory, where information is transmitted via signals or messages. Instead, information is available in the structure of ambient energy and must be extracted rather than received.
