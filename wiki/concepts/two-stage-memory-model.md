# Two-Stage Memory Model

## Summary

The two-stage model of memory proposes that waking experience (stage 1) followed by sharp wave ripples during offline states (stage 2) enables memory consolidation. Recent revisions suggest the first stage is a selection process matching experience to preexisting sequences rather than creating new patterns.

## Key facts

- The first stage of the two-stage model is a selection process in which existing sequences or their concatenated variants are matched to experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p210]
- The selection-incorporation version of the two-stage model is more economical because only small changes are expected in hippocampal and neocortical networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p210]
- Sharp wave ripples may allow the brain to solidify recently learned material by repeating various aspects of recent experience at a compressed time scale. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p212]
- Replay from the hippocampus occurs during neocortical silence (down states), requiring coordination between hippocampal and cortical activities for reliable message transfer. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p210]

## Inferences

- Inference: The revised two-stage model suggests learning is primarily a selection process—matching experience to preexisting patterns—rather than new construction, explaining how memories can form rapidly from limited experience.
