# Essentialism

## Summary

Essentialism is the philosophical and psychological theory that perceivable things have an embodied unobservable essence that makes them what they are. Children are natural essentialists, believing that animals have an invisible essence independent of appearance.

## Key facts

- Essentialism in some form shows up in all cultures, taking forms such as DNA, gifts from God, or astrological signs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p264]
- Nine-month-old babies already believe in the essence of objects, expecting identical objects to possess the same qualities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p264]
- Essentialism may be adaptive because biologically, animals are similar due to shared evolutionary history, and deeper indicators are more reliable than appearance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p264]
- Vonk and Povinelli found no good evidence that animals use more than perceivable characteristics, suggesting essentialism may be uniquely human. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p265]

## Inferences

- Inference: Essentialism may underlie human dualism, as it leads us to believe there is a non-physical essence separate from the physical body.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact relationship between human essentialism and animal categorization abilities requires further study.
