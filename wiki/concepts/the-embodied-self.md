# The embodied self

## Summary

The right hemisphere is deeply connected to the self as embodied. Only the right parietal lobe has a whole body image, and this body image is not merely a representation but a living image intimately linked to activity in the world—an essentially affective experience. The right hemisphere sees the body as something we 'live', while the left hemisphere sees the body as something we have, a thing in the world, devitalised.

## Key facts

- We know that the left hemisphere carries an image only of the contralateral (right) side of the body—when the right hemisphere is incapacitated, the left part of the individual's body virtually ceases to exist for that person; it is only the right parietal lobe that has a whole body image. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p102]
- Importantly this body image is not just a picture; it is not a representation (as it would be if it were in the left hemisphere), or just the sum of our bodily perceptions, but a living image, intimately linked to activity in the world—an essentially affective experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p102]
- The right hemisphere is responsible for our sense of the body as something we 'live', something that is part of our identity, the phase of intersection between our selves and the world at large. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p103]
- For the left hemisphere, by contrast, the body is something from which we are relatively detached, a thing in the world, like other things (en soi, rather than pour soi), devitalised, a 'corpse'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p103]
- German distinguishes between Leib (the lived body) and Körper (the corpse-body, related to English 'corpse'), referring to the body left when the soul departed; Leib is related to leben/lebendig (English 'live/alive'). [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p103]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between embodied (Leib) and objectified (Körper) selfhood parallels the broader contrast McGilchrist draws between right-hemisphere immediacy and left-hemisphere abstraction.
