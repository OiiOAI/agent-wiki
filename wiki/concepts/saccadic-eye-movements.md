# Saccadic Eye Movements

## Summary

Saccades are rapid, ballistic eye movements that reposition the fovea to a new visual target. They are generated by a neural circuit involving the frontal eye field, parietal cortex, superior colliculus, and brain stem burst generators. Visual neurons in the frontal eye field respond to stimulus appearance, while movement-related neurons fire before saccade onset.

## Key facts

- Movement-related neurons in the frontal eye field form excitatory synapses on neurons in the caudate nucleus that inhibit the substantia nigra pars reticulata, releasing the superior colliculus from inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p962]
- Electrical stimulation of the frontal eye field evokes saccades to the movement fields of the stimulated cells; bilateral stimulation evokes vertical saccades. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p962]
- Bilateral lesions of both the frontal eye field and the superior colliculus permanently compromise the ability to make saccades. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p963]
