# Winner-take-all mechanism in neural choice

## Summary

The winner-take-all mechanism is proposed as the computational process by which neural circuits select a single action from competing options. In the saccadic control system, this occurs when collicular burst neurons are driven above a specific firing-rate threshold, triggering a self-perpetuating burst that persists until movement completion. Inhibitory interconnections in the collicular map prevent simultaneous bursts at multiple locations.

## Key facts

- Collicular burst neurons fire above a specific threshold to trigger movement through a self-perpetuating burst state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Inhibitory interconnections in the collicular map preclude burst-like activity occurring at more than one location at a time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Collicular burst neurons can fire in either a continuous low-frequency state or a fixed high-frequency burst state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]

## Inferences

- Inference: The winner-take-all architecture enables only a single movement to be executed at a time by the collicular system.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How movement selection is triggered in non-reaction time settings remains less understood.
