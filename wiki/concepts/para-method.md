# PARA Method

## Summary

PARA is an organizing system for digital information based on actionability: Projects (short-term efforts with clear outcomes), Areas (long-term responsibilities), Resources (topics of interest for future reference), and Archives (inactive items from other categories). It can be used across any software platform and is designed to minimize time spent organizing while maximizing accessibility of useful information.

## Key facts

- PARA stands for Projects, Areas, Resources, and Archives — the four main categories for organizing all information in one's digital life. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p78]
- Projects are short-term efforts in work or life that are being worked on now, with a beginning and end and a specific clear outcome. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p78-79]
- Areas are long-term responsibilities maintained over time without a definite end date, such as health, finances, or career development. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p79-81]
- Resources are topics or interests that may be useful in the future but are not currently actionable. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p81-82]
- Archives contain inactive items from the previous three categories — completed or canceled projects, abandoned areas, and outdated resources. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p82-83]
- The decision hierarchy for placing notes is: Projects first (most actionable), then Areas, then Resources, then Archives. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p91-92]

## Inferences

- Inference: PARA is designed to mirror how kitchens are organized — Archives are like the freezer, Resources like the pantry, Areas like the refrigerator, and Projects like the pots cooking on the stove — organizing by how soon items will be used rather than by category type.
