# Dehumanization

## Summary

The psychological and rhetorical process of perceiving members of an out-group as less than human, making violence against them psychologically easier. Propagandists exploit the brain's tendency to confuse literal and metaphorical representations, using symbols of revulsion (insects, animals, filth) to trigger visceral disgust responses against real people.

## Key facts

- The Rwandan genocide featured systematic dehumanization of Tutsis, who were referred to only as 'cockroaches' in anti-Tutsi propaganda broadcast via government-controlled radio. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p588]
- Dehumanization employs pseudospeciation—portraying the out-group as a different species—and engages the insular cortex to confuse literal disgust with moral condemnation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p588]
- Forms of dehumanizing metaphors include portraying Thems as rodents, a cancer, as reekingly malodorous, as living in hives of chaos, or as excrement. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p588]

## Inferences

- Inference: The effectiveness of dehumanization in promoting violence suggests that our neural circuits for physical disgust can be hijacked for moral judgment in ways that bypass rational evaluation.
