# Tiny Tweaks Principle

## Summary

The tiny tweaks principle asserts that small shifts over time can dramatically enhance one's ability to thrive. Rather than pursuing revolutionary change through grand gestures, the most effective transformation comes from doing what one can, with what one has, where one is. Each small tweak may appear insignificant individually, but collectively they create profound change—like individual frames in a film that together tell a different story. The principle identifies three broad areas for small changes: mindsets, motivations, and habits.

## Key facts

- Nature favors evolution, not revolution; small shifts over time can dramatically enhance ability to thrive. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p113]
- A degree or two shift in sailing can dramatically change where one winds up across the bay. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p113]
- When approach to problems is too grand, frustration is invited; when aiming for tiny tweaks, cost of failure is small and confidence increases. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p114]
- Three broad areas for making tiny changes are beliefs/mindsets, motivations, and habits. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p114]

- Inference: The principle suggests that sustainable change requires patience and iteration rather than dramatic intervention.
