# Dopamine and Reward Learning

## Summary

Dopamine is a neuromodulator central to motivation for seeking rewards and forming lasting memories of rewarding experiences. Research shows that dopamine is about 'wanting' rather than 'liking'—it energizes us to seek rewards but does not mediate the pleasure of receiving them. Dopamine promotes neural plasticity and helps learn associations between cues, contexts, and actions that lead to rewards.

## Key facts

- Dopamine is central to helping form lasting memories for rewarding experiences, with bursts of activity occurring when experiencing something intensely rewarding. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p103]
- Kent Berridge's research showed that interfering with dopamine reduced animals' ability to work for rewards but had no effect on their ability to enjoy rewards they received. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p103]
- Berridge demonstrated that to the brain, 'wanting' something is not the same as 'liking' it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p103]
- Dopamine promotes neural plasticity and is concentrated in brain areas important for learning how to get rewards: amygdala helps learn about reward cues, hippocampus helps learn about reward contexts, and nucleus accumbens helps learn what actions get rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p104]
- The brain learns only when outcomes do not match expectations—dopamine activity ramps up when expecting a reward, and the degree of mismatch determines the brain's response to the actual reward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p104]
