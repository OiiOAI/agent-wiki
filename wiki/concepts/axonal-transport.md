# Axonal Transport

## Summary

Axonal transport is the mechanism by which proteins, organelles, and other molecules are moved along axons between the cell body and nerve terminals. It includes fast axonal transport (membranous organelles) and slow axonal transport (cytosolic and cytoskeletal proteins).

## Key facts

- Fast axonal transport carries membranous organelles including synaptic vesicle precursors, large dense-core vesicles, mitochondria, and elements of smooth endoplasmic reticulum at rates faster than 400 mm per day. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p132]
- The molecular motors for anterograde fast transport are kinesin and kinesin-related proteins, while retrograde fast transport uses MAP-1C, a microtubule-associated ATPase similar to dyneins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p133]
- Slow axonal transport carries cytosolic and cytoskeletal proteins in the anterograde direction at 0.2 to 2.5 mm per day, including neurofilament subunits and tubulin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p135]
- In 1948 Paul Weiss first demonstrated axonal transport by tying off a sciatic nerve and observing accumulation of axoplasm on the proximal side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p131]
