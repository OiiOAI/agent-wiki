# Regression

## Summary

A concept describing the backward movement of psychical processes, introduced by Breuer in Studies on Hysteria (1895) and developed by Freud throughout his work. Freud later distinguished three types: topographical (movement toward the perceptual end of the psychical apparatus), temporal (return to earlier developmental stages of sexuality or the ego), and formal (reversion to primitive methods of expression). These three forms are considered fundamentally unified, as what is older in time tends to be more primitive in form and closer to the perceptual end.

- Topographical regression, introduced by Breuer and employed in the 'Project', conceives excitation as moving backward toward the perceptual end of the psychical apparatus. - Temporal regression involves return to earlier libidinal objects or to earlier ways of functioning, particularly characteristic of hysteria and obsessional neurosis respectively. - Formal regression occurs where primitive methods of expression and representation take the place of usual ones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p346]
- Freud stated: 'All these three kinds of regression are, however, one at bottom and occur together as a rule; for what is older in time is more primitive in form and in psychical topography lies nearer to the perceptual end.'. - Regression is the most general and striking psychological characteristic of dreaming: a thought is objectified in the dream, represented as a scene, or experienced. ## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of regression provided Freud with a unifying framework for understanding various psychopathological phenomena across different neuroses
- Inference: The later development of fixation points and pregenital organizations allowed for more precise understanding of temporal regression mechanisms
- Inference: Freud's concept of regression connects dream formation with neurotic symptom formation, suggesting both share the mechanism of thoughts being transformed into sensory images through unconscious processes.
- Inference: Regression is the counterpart to fixation, representing a backward movement in psychic development when current satisfactions are blocked.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanical changes that render regression possible during sleep versus daytime are described as conjectural rather than definitively established.
- Uncertain: The localization of regression of the libido in the mental apparatus is unclear; Freud notes it cannot be called a purely psychical process and its most prominent factor is the organic one [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5105]
