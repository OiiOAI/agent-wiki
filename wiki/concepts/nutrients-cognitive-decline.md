# Nutrients for cognitive decline

## Summary

Research on B vitamins and cognitive decline has shown promising results. The VITACOG trial found that seniors with high homocysteine levels who took B vitamins experienced slowed whole-brain atrophy by 53 percent, slowed atrophy of specific brain regions by 90 percent, and slowed or stopped further cognitive decline. Elevated homocysteine is associated with oxidative stress, a risk factor for cognitive decline, dementia, and Alzheimer's disease.

## Key facts

- The VITACOG trial at the University of Oxford found that seniors over age seventy with mild cognitive impairment who took B vitamins (0.8 mg folic acid, 20 mg of vitamin B6, and 0.5 mg of vitamin B12) for two years experienced slowed whole-brain atrophy by 53 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p150]
- Those with high homocysteine levels who were given B vitamins experienced slowed atrophy of specific brain regions by 90 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p150]
- Elevated homocysteine is associated with oxidative stress, a risk factor for cognitive decline, dementia, and Alzheimer's disease in older persons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p149]
