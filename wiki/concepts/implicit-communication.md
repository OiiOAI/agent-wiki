# Implicit Communication (Boyd's Command System)

## Summary

Implicit communication is central to Boyd's command philosophy, allowing units to operate with less information, less external communication, and less explicit direction, thereby reducing response times. It is enabled by shared outlook, trust, and common doctrine.

## Key facts

- Implicit communication can make do with less information, less external communication and explicit direction, which reduces response times. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]
- Implicit communication suffices when everyone understands and is attuned to the organization's purpose or commander's intent. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p288]
- External explicit connections are prone to interruption, and when the command system is based on them, it will generally be slower than one based on implicit communication. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]
- Without the ability of proper communication, isolated and disoriented units send contradicting, incomplete, inconsistent, outdated or no information at all. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's preference for implicit over explicit communication reflects his broader emphasis on adaptability—organizations relying on implicit communication can respond faster because they do not need to wait for detailed orders or complete information.
