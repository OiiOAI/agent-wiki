# Statins and Cognitive Effects

## Summary

Statins are cholesterol-lowering medications that may have unintended effects on the brain. They can reduce the brain's production of cholesterol, which is important for neurotransmitter release and memory function. Lipophilic statins (atorvastatin, lovastatin, simvastatin) can cross the blood-brain barrier more easily and have been associated with cognitive side effects. Statins also lower coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10) levels and nearly double the risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

## Key facts

- Statins can reduce the brain's production of cholesterol, which is important for neurotransmitter release and memory function. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p178]
- Lipophilic statins (atorvastatin, lovastatin, simvastatin) can cross the blood-brain barrier more easily and have been associated with cognitive side effects mimicking dementia in extreme cases. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p178]
- Statins also lower levels of coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10), a nutrient important for brain metabolism that also acts as a fat-soluble antioxidant. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p178]
- A study found that statin users had twice the rate of diabetes after ten years compared to non-users. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p181]
- The NNT (number needed to treat) for statins in at-risk adults with no known cardiac disease ranges from 100 to 150 to prevent one cardiac event, with no effect on death rate. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p180]
