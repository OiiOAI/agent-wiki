# Escalation

## Summary

Escalation occurs when one actor's desired state is determined by trying to surpass another's state, creating a reinforcing feedback loop. The goal is not absolute but relative—to stay ahead of the competitor. Examples include arms races, negative political campaigning, price wars, and increasingly loud advertising. Escalation builds exponentially and can lead to extremes faster than expected. Ways out include unilateral disarmament or negotiating new balancing loops to control competition.

## Key facts

- Escalation comes from a reinforcing loop where actors try to get ahead of each other, not just keep up. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p154]
- The U.S. and Soviet Union exaggerated reports of each other's armaments to justify more armaments of their own. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p154]
- Escalation is exponential and can lead to extremes faster than anyone would believe possible. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p156]
