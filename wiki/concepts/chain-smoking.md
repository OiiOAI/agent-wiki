# Chain-Smoking

## Summary

A working method Kleon describes for maintaining career momentum by never taking breaks between projects, instead using the end of one project to light up the next. The idea is to avoid stalling out by keeping continuous creative momentum rather than waiting for feedback or worrying about what's next.

## Key facts

- Chain-smoking is defined as avoiding stalling out in your career by never losing momentum, using the end of one project to light up the next. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p179]
- The method: Instead of taking breaks between projects, waiting for feedback, and worrying about what's next, use the end of one project to start the next. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p179]
- Charles Eames is quoted: 'We work because it's a chain reaction, each subject leads to the next.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p179]
- Examples of chain-smoking artists: Woody Allen has averaged a film a year for over 40 years because he never takes time off—day he finishes editing is day he starts writing the next script. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p176]
- Bob Pollard of Guided by Voices says he never gets writer's block because he never stops writing. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p176]
- Ernest Hemingway stopped in the middle of a sentence at the end of his day's work so he knew where to start in the morning. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p176]
