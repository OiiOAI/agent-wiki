# Broad-spectrum multinutrients for ADHD

## Summary

Research using many different study designs has consistently shown that broad-spectrum multinutrient supplementation can substantially reduce ADHD symptoms. Controlled trials in both adults and children have demonstrated benefits including improved inattention, emotion regulation, and overall functioning, with minimal side effects compared to stimulant medications.

## Key facts

- Julia's first RCT used the broad-spectrum multinutrient supplement EMPowerplus (EMP) and followed eighty medication-free adults diagnosed with ADHD, who were randomly chosen to receive either multinutrients or a placebo for eight weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p118]
- In the adult ADHD RCT, for those who entered the trial with moderate to severe depression, there was greater improvement in mood for those randomized to the multinutrients compared to those randomized to placebo. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p118]
- In both the adult and child ADHD RCTs, about 80 percent of those taking the broad-spectrum multinutrient formulas were in remission at the one-year follow-up from their ADHD symptoms compared to only 40 percent of those taking medications and 20 percent of those who were taking nothing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p123]
- The primary effect of multinutrients appears to be on the ability to regulate emotions, leading to more stable mood, reduced irritability and aggression, feelings of inner calm, better sleep, and over time better focus and clarity of thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p119]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistency of results across adult and child populations, and the durability of effects at one-year follow-up, suggests that broad-spectrum multinutrients may be a viable alternative or adjunct treatment for ADHD.
