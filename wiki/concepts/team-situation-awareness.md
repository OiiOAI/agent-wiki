# Team situation awareness

## Summary

Team Situation Awareness (SA) refers to the shared and distributed awareness among team members in dynamic work environments. Unlike individual SA, team SA encompasses both the individual SA of each team member and the team processes that facilitate information sharing and coordination. Research has identified specific behavioral indicators of team SA including preparation, communication, leadership, and adaptability. The concept recognizes that effective crew performance depends on crew members actively contributing to each other's awareness and maintaining awareness throughout the flight.

## Key facts

- Team SA is a construct that includes the individual SA of each team member and team processes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p347]
- Endsley (1995) defined team SA as 'the degree to which every team member possesses the situation awareness required for his or her responsibilities'. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p347]
- Schwartz (1990) described team SA as being moderated by the pilot in command who receives information from the entire crew. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p347]
- SA research pinpointed team process skill areas (preparation, communication, leadership, and adaptability) that are most supportive of maintaining SA in a crew situation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p350]
- Research demonstrated the relation between using SA actions in routine flight segments and subsequent decision making. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p350]
- Experienced pilots use more behaviors related to awareness in difficult scenarios than do inexperienced pilots. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p350]
- Training transfer of behaviors associated with SA has been documented (Brannick et al., 1998). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p350]
- 40% of reported incidents in the ASRS database occurred when only one crew member had a problem with SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p353]

## Inferences

- Inference: Team SA training should emphasize the active role crew members must take to maintain awareness rather than passive presence in the cockpit
- Inference: The integration of individual and team-level SA research has led to improved CRM training methodologies
- Inference: Team SA is critical for error reduction because it enables crew members to monitor and compensate for each other's potential awareness gaps
