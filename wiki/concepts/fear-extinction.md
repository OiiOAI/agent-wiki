# Fear Extinction

## Summary

Fear extinction is a neurological process that allows anxiety to be overcome by creating new memories that override fear memories. While the original fear memory cannot be erased, the brain builds parallel neural circuitry through which it learns that anticipated danger does not materialize. This process, called reattribution, involves the prefrontal cortex sending cease-and-desist signals to the amygdala to create a neutral alternative to expected anxiety. Exercise supports fear extinction by providing the neurotransmitters (serotonin, norepinephrine) and neurotrophic factors (BDNF) that strengthen circuits between the prefrontal cortex and amygdala. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) achieves extinction by exposing patients to fear sources in graduated doses; exercise enhances this process by simultaneously engaging the body's arousal response in a safe context.

## Key facts

- In anxiety disorders, the prefrontal cortex area responsible for sending cease-and-desist signals to the amygdala is often smaller than normal, allowing the overexcited amygdala to tag too many situations as dangerous. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p81]
- Research psychiatrist Daniel Pine characterizes anxiety disorders as a learning deficit: patients cannot discriminate between danger and safety, showing nearly the same amygdala activity for benign pictures as for fear-inducing ones. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p82]
- Keith Johnsgard's running-based CBT for agoraphobia has patients sprint toward fear at peak physical arousal without panic, teaching the brain it can survive anxiety symptoms. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p90]
- Neuroscientist Joseph LeDoux showed that signals can be redirected through the basal nucleus of the amygdala (the action pathway) rather than the central nucleus, effectively circumventing fear memory mechanisms. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p90]
