# Mirroring

## Summary

Mirroring (also called isopraxism) is a negotiation technique where you repeat the last three words—or critical one to three words—of what someone has just said. This triggers an unconscious mirroring instinct that makes people feel understood and encourages them to elaborate and connect. It is described as 'the closest one gets to a Jedi mind trick.'

## Key facts

- For the FBI, a 'mirror' is when you repeat the last three words (or the critical one to three words) of what someone has just said. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p36]
- Mirroring follows the biological principle that humans fear what's different and are drawn to what's similar. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p36]
- A study by psychologist Richard Wiseman found that waiters who mirrored customers' orders received 70 percent higher tips than those who used positive reinforcement. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p36]
- The five steps for effective mirroring are: use the late-night FM DJ voice, start with 'I'm sorry,' mirror, maintain silence for at least four seconds, and repeat. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p43]
- Mirroring works because repeating back what people say triggers their mirroring instinct and encourages them to elaborate. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p36]
