# False Causality

## Summary

False Causality is the error of assuming that because two events occurred together or sequentially, one must have caused the other. The author illustrates this with the famous 'storks bring babies' correlation and various medical and business examples where causation is reversed or non-existent.

## Key facts

- Correlation is not causality; sometimes what is presented as the cause turns out to be the effect, and vice versa, and sometimes there is no link at all. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p92]
- The classic example of false causality was the supposed relationship between birth rate and stork pairs in Germany—both declined from 1965 to 1987 and plotted almost identically on a graph. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p92]
- Students get better grades in homes with many books not because books improve grades but because educated parents tend to value education more and have more books. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p92]
