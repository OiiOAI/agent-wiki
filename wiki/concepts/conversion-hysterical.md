# Conversion (Hysterical)

## Summary

Conversion is the theoretical term introduced by Freud to describe the transformation of psychical excitation into chronic somatic symptoms, which is characteristic of hysteria. In the case of Frau Emmy von N., only a small amount of conversion was exhibited, as the excitation remained largely psychical rather than being transformed into somatic symptoms.

## Key facts

- Conversion refers to the transformation of psychical excitation into chronic somatic symptoms characteristic of hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p616]
- Hysterical symptoms are effects and residues of excitations that have acted upon the nervous system as traumas. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p616]
- Residues are not left behind if the original excitation has been discharged by abreaction or thought activity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p616]
