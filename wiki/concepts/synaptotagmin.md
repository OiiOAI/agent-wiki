# Synaptotagmin

## Summary

Synaptotagmin is the major calcium sensor that triggers neurotransmitter release. It binds calcium through its C2 domains and triggers exocytosis when calcium enters the presynaptic terminal during an action potential.

## Key facts

- Synaptotagmin is a synaptic vesicle protein with a single N-terminal transmembrane region and two C2 domains in its cytoplasmic region. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p332]
- The two C2 domains bind a total of five calcium ions, the same number indicated by electrophysiological experiments to trigger release of one quantum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p332]
- Deletion of synaptotagmin in mice, flies, or worms eliminates fast synchronous release triggered by action potentials while leaving slower asynchronous release intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p333]
