# Interests vs. Positions

## Summary

The second principle of principled negotiation is to focus on interests rather than positions. A position is what a party has decided upon, while interests are the underlying needs, desires, concerns, and fears that caused them to decide. For every interest, there are usually multiple possible positions that could satisfy it. Behind opposed positions lie many more shared and compatible interests than conflicting ones, making reconciliation of interests more effective than compromising between positions.

## Key facts

- The basic problem in negotiation lies not in conflicting positions but in the conflict between each side's needs, desires, concerns, and fears—their interests. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p23]
- A position is something decided upon; interests are what caused that decision. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p23]
- Reconciling interests rather than positions works because for every interest there usually exist several possible positions that could satisfy it. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p24]
- Behind opposed positions lie shared and compatible interests as well as conflicting ones, and often the former are more numerous. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p24]
- The most powerful interests are basic human needs including security, economic well-being, sense of belonging, recognition, and control over one's life. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p27]
