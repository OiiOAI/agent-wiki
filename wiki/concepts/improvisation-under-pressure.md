# Improvisation Under Pressure

## Summary

Improvisation under pressure involves modifying plans in real-time when circumstances change suddenly. The book contrasts successful improvisations like Joshua Chamberlain's bayonet charge with failures like the Mann Gulch fire, showing that too much adaptation under stress can be disastrous without shared understanding.

## Key facts

- At Gettysburg, Joshua Chamberlain ordered his men to fix bayonets and charge downhill using a well-practiced wheeling maneuver; he trusted his intuition that the procedure would succeed despite fatigue and fear. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p222]
- In the Mann Gulch fire, Wagner Dodge invented a backfire tactic on the spot that could have saved him, but he couldn't explain it to his team in time; most crew members perished. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p222]
- Dodge was more innovative than Chamberlain but wasn't successful because under time pressure, too much adaptation can lead to disaster; it's better to have well-learned procedures in one's hip pocket. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p223]
- Adaptation carries penalties: every time you change course you open yourself up to unintended consequences you may not have anticipated. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p220]

## Inferences

- Inference: The key difference between successful and unsuccessful improvisation is often whether the improviser can communicate their intent quickly enough for others to follow.
