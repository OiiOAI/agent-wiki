# Language as Manipulation

## Summary

McGilchrist argues that referential language did not originate primarily from a need to communicate, but as a means of mapping and manipulating the world. Language brings precision and fixity - features essential for manipulation. It enables virtual representation of reality, abstracted from the immediate environment, and is particularly effective for manipulating other human beings. The most ancient surviving written texts are bureaucratic records.

## Key facts

- Language may not have originated in a drive to communicate - that came later - but as a means of mapping the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p167]
- Language expands the realm of the 'I-it' relationship rather than serving the 'I-thou' relationship of personal communication. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p168]
- Language brings precision and fixity, two very important features if we are to succeed in manipulating the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p169]
- Language enables the left hemisphere to represent the world 'off-line', a conceptual version distinct from the world of experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p169]
- The most ancient surviving written texts are bureaucratic records. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p168]
- Language is good for manipulating other human beings - we cannot easily hide the truth in non-verbal communication, but we can in words. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p169]

## Inferences

- Inference: Language's role in giving command over the world transformed it from the 'I-thou' world of music (and right hemisphere) to the 'I-it' world of words (and left hemisphere).
