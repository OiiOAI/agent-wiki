# Conscious Ignition

## Summary

Conscious ignition refers to the sudden, discontinuous transition when neural activity jumps from low spontaneous firing to a sustained, synchronized state across the global workspace. Computer simulations by Dehaene and Changeux showed that conscious access corresponds to a phase transition—a physical transformation from one state to another. When a stimulus exceeds a critical threshold, feedback connections cause self-amplification, leading to reverberating activity lasting hundreds of milliseconds that is independent of the original stimulus duration.

## Key facts

- Conscious access corresponds to a phase transition in neural networks, jumping discontinuously from low spontaneous activity to elevated synchronized firing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p231]
- Simulation showed that a brief sensory pulse could lead to sustained reverberating states lasting hundreds of milliseconds regardless of initial stimulus duration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p230]
- The ignition threshold varies with vigilance state—heightened vigilance increases probability that faint sensory inputs trigger global ignition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p232]
- During ignition, spikes become strongly coupled and synchronized among neurons coding for the conscious representation, with prominent gamma-range oscillations (30+ Hz). [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p231]
- Spontaneous brain activity can block external stimuli from triggering ignition, explaining inattentional blindness and the attentional blink. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p235]

## Inferences

- Inference: The phase-transition model explains why conscious perception has a threshold separating subliminal from supraliminal processing
- Inference: The competition between spontaneous activity and external inputs explains why identical stimuli sometimes do and sometimes do not reach consciousness
