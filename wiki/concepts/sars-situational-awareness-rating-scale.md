# SARS (Situational Awareness Rating Scale)

## Summary

SARS (Situational Awareness Rating Scale) is a subjective SA assessment method developed for the tactical air environment. Unlike other subjective scales that measure changeable SA states, SARS treats SA as an innate ability. The scale consists of 31 behavior elements organized into 8 categories, rated on a 6-point scale. Research demonstrated high reliability and correlation between squadron ratings of SA and mission success in simulated air combat, though the scale is closely tied to specific aircraft types and may have limited applicability to other domains.

## Key facts

- SARS defines SA as perceiving what is important in the environment and using that perception to guide behavior selection and performance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p140]
- SARS consists of 31 behavior elements representing 8 categories of mission performance, developed through interviews with experienced F-15 pilots. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p140]
- The 8 SARS categories are: General Traits, Tactical Game Plan, System Operation, Communication, Information Interpretation, Tactical Employment-BVR, Tactical Employment-Visual, and Tactical Employment-General. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p140]
- Subjects rate each SARS element on a 6-point scale with anchors of acceptable and outstanding. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p140]
- SARS demonstrated high reliability estimates, high internal consistency, and interrater reliability for both peer and supervisory ratings. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p141]
- A significant relation was found between squadron ratings of SA and performance in simulated air combat missions using SARS. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p142]
- Unlike other scales, SARS treats SA more as an innate ability rather than a changeable state of knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p142]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation between SARS ratings and mission success suggests that observer-based SA assessment may capture meaningful individual differences in tactical performance.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The scale is closely tied to particular aircraft types and missions, limiting applicability to other domains.
- Uncertain: The usefulness of this scale for evaluating systems rather than individuals is unclear.
