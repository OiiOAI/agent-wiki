# Intention-to-Treat Error

## Summary

The intention-to-treat error categorizes subjects by what they intended to do rather than what they actually did, creating misleading results. "Reckless drivers" completing a journey fastest couldn't have had accidents, making them appear safer. The error also distorts medical studies when sick patients drop out of drug trials.

## Key facts

- Drivers completing a 75-mile journey in under an hour are classified as "reckless," but none could have had accidents—so they appear safer. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p213]
- Companies with large loans appear more profitable only because unprofitable companies don't receive loans and remain in the "equity-only" group. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p213]
- In drug studies, patients who couldn't take medication regularly (due to illness) populate the "irregular intake" group, making the drug appear more effective than it is. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p214]
