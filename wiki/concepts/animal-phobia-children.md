# Animal Phobias in Children

## Summary

Freud's observation that children's animal phobias often represent displaced fear of the father. Analysis of cases like Little Hans (horse phobia) and little Árpád (fowl fetish) revealed that the fear of animals was a substitute for ambivalent feelings toward the father, connected with the Oedipus complex and castration anxiety.

## Key facts

- In children's animal phobias, where the children concerned were boys, their fear related at bottom to their father and had merely been displaced on to the animal. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4228]
- Analysis of Little Hans showed that he was situated in the typical attitude toward his parents which Freud named the 'Oedipus complex' and which he regards as the nuclear complex of the neuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4229]
- The displacement of fear from father to animal cannot bring the emotional conflict to an end; the ambivalence is extended to the substitute object. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4229]
- Freud argues that in these children's phobias some features of totemism reappear, but reversed into their negative form. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4230]

## Inferences

- Inference: The identification of children with their totem animals in these phobias parallels the totemic identification described by Frazer as 'the whole essence of totemism.'
- Inference: Freud proposes substituting the father for the totem animal in the formula for totemism, arguing that this psychological equation explains the origin of totemic prohibitions.
