# Illusion of Attention

## Summary

The illusion of attention is the overconfidence in our ability to notice everything occurring around us. In reality, we focus narrowly and miss unexpected events—sometimes huge ones like a person in a gorilla suit walking through a basketball-passing video. This has serious implications for driving while using cellphones.

## Key facts

- Simons and Chabris' "Monkey Business Illusion" video showed half of viewers failed to notice a person in a gorilla costume walking through and pounding their chest. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p193]
- Drivers using cellphones react as slowly as those under the influence of alcohol or drugs when unexpected events occur. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p193]
- Swissair went bankrupt in 2001 because it was so focused on expansion it overlooked evaporating liquidity—a "gorilla in the room.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p194]
