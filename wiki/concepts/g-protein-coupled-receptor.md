# G Protein-Coupled Receptor

## Summary

G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) are a major family of metabotropic receptors that regulate neuronal excitability, transmitter release, and synaptic function through activation of second-messenger cascades. They consist of a single polypeptide with seven transmembrane segments and couple to intracellular G proteins that activate effectors like adenylyl cyclase, phospholipase C, or directly modulate ion channels.

## Key facts

- G protein-coupled receptors consist of a single polypeptide with seven characteristic membrane-spanning regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p350]
- GPCRs activate G proteins consisting of α-, β-, and γ-subunits; ligand binding promotes exchange of GDP for GTP on the α-subunit, causing it to dissociate from the βγ complex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p349]
- Approximately 3.5% of genes in the human genome code for G protein-coupled receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p350]
- G protein-coupled receptors are activated by many important neurotransmitters including norepinephrine, ACh (muscarinic), GABA (GABAB), certain glutamate and serotonin receptors, dopamine, and neuropeptides.
