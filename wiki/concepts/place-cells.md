# Place Cells

## Summary

Place cells are pyramidal neurons in the hippocampus discovered by John O'Keefe in 1971 that fire action potentials when an animal moves into a particular location in its environment. They register information about space that depends on multiple sensory modalities, forming a cognitive map of external space. Each place cell represents a specific overlapping area, similar to a mosaic, and the spatial map develops within minutes of entering a new environment.

## Key facts

- In 1971, John O'Keefe discovered that neurons in the hippocampus of rats register information not about a single sensory modality but about the space surrounding the animal. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p292]
- O'Keefe showed the hippocampus of rats contains a representation—a map—of external space and that the units of that map are pyramidal cells that process information about place. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p292]
- The pattern of action potentials in place cells is so distinctively related to a particular area of space that O'Keefe referred to them as 'place cells.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p292]
- Damage to the hippocampus severely compromises an animal's ability to learn a task that relies on spatial information, indicating the spatial map plays a central role in spatial cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p292]
- The brain breaks down its surroundings into many small, overlapping areas similar to a mosaic, each represented by activity in specific cells in the hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p319]
- The spatial map develops within minutes of the rat's entrance into a new environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p319]
- Place cells fire when an animal enters a certain location in a specific environment, and different environments activate different populations of place cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
- New place fields form within minutes when an animal enters a new environment and are stable for weeks to months. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
- Place fields in mice lacking the NR1 subunit of the NMDA receptor are larger and fuzzier than those in normal animals, despite the fact that LTP is blocked. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
- Late LTP is required not for the formation but for long-term stabilization of place fields, as demonstrated by mice expressing a persistently active CaMKII that inhibits LTP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1562]
- The formation of stable place fields requires the animal to attend to its environment and the action of dopamine on D1/D5 type receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1563]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery of place cells by O'Keefe and Dostrovsky provided the first evidence for a neural representation of space that allows an animal to move deliberately around the world.
