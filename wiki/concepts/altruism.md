# Altruism

## Summary

Altruism, in the behavioral sense used in The Selfish Gene, is defined as behavior that increases another entity's welfare at the expense of the actor's own welfare. The book addresses the evolutionary puzzle of altruism: if genes are selfish, how can altruistic behavior evolve? Dawkins explains that altruism can arise from selfish genes through mechanisms like kin selection and reciprocal altruism.

## Key facts

- An entity is said to be altruistic if it behaves in such a way as to increase another such entity's welfare at the expense of its own welfare. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p29]
- Welfare is defined as chances of survival, even if the effect on actual life and death prospects is so small as to seem negligible. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p29]
- The definitions of altruism and selfishness are behavioral, not psychological—they concern only the effects of acts on survival prospects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p29]
- Universal love and the welfare of the species as a whole are concepts that do not make evolutionary sense according to the selfish gene theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p28]
- Apparent altruism often turns out on closer inspection to be selfishness in disguise in terms of effects on survival prospects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p29]
