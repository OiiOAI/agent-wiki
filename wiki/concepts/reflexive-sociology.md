# Reflexive Sociology

## Summary

Pierre Bourdieu's reflexive sociology calls for sociology to continually turn back upon itself using its own instruments to understand what it is and what it is doing. Bourdieu argues that sociologists should practice 'epistemological vigilance' and engage in 'socioanalysis' to understand the forces acting on them, enabling them to control the impact of those forces on their work.

## Key facts

- Bourdieu states: 'For me, sociology ought to be meta but always vis-a-vis itself.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p560]
- Bourdieu defines the sociology of sociology as 'a fundamental dimension of sociological epistemology.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p560]
- Bourdieu rejects metatheorizing as an autonomous practice separate from theorizing about and empirically studying the social world. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p561]
- Bourdieu argues that sociologists need to 'avoid being the toy of social forces' through understanding those forces via metatheoretical analysis or 'socioanalysis.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p561]
- Bourdieu practices 'epistemological vigilance' to protect himself from distorting pressures. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p561]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bourdieu's reflexive sociology represents a distinctive approach that refuses to separate metatheorizing from other facets of sociology, arguing for continuous reflexivity during analysis.
