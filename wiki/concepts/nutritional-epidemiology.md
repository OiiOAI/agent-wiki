# Nutritional Epidemiology

## Summary

Nutritional epidemiology studies the relationship between diet and health outcomes through observation of large populations. It has significant limitations in establishing causation due to reliance on food frequency questionnaires, healthy user bias, and small effect sizes that fail Bradford Hill criteria.

## Key facts

- John Ioannidis of Stanford stated: 'Nutritional epidemiology is a scandal. It should just go into the waste bin.'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p308]
- Food frequency questionnaires ask users to recall everything they ate over the last month or year, making the data highly unreliable. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p307]
- The Bradford Hill criteria require effect sizes much larger than those typically seen in nutrition studies to establish causation; smoking shows 1000-2500% increased lung cancer risk versus 17% for red meat and colorectal cancer. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p307]
- David Allison's mouse experiment showed that within the high-calorie group, hungrier mice lived longer because they were healthier to begin with—demonstrating healthy user bias. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p309]
- A JAMA study using Mendelian randomization found that any observed benefit of moderate alcohol consumption completely disappears once other factors are removed, concluding there is no dose of alcohol that is 'healthy.'. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p310]
