# Locked-In Syndrome

## Summary

Locked-in syndrome results from damage to the anterior part of the upper brain stem, destroying motor pathways while preserving consciousness. Patients are conscious and aware but cannot move voluntarily except for vertical eye movements and blinking, which allow communication through coded responses. The syndrome provides powerful evidence that consciousness depends specifically on the posterior brain stem sector, not the anterior sector where motor pathways run.

## Key facts

- Locked-in syndrome results from damage to the anterior (front) part of the upper brain stem, which destroys pathways bringing motor signals to the body while sparing pathways for vertical eye movements and blinking in the posterior sector. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p243]
- Patients with locked-in syndrome retain consciousness, wakefulness, attention, and the ability to understand language, but can only communicate through vertical eye movements and blinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p243]
- The contrast between locked-in syndrome (intact consciousness) and coma (lost consciousness) provides powerful evidence for the anatomical specificity of consciousness-related brain structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p244]
- Locked-in syndrome results from ventral brainstem damage that destroys motor pathways to skeletal muscles while consciousness remains intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p292]
- Locked-in patients do not experience the extreme terror expected from their predicament; they may report a strange tranquility despite being aware of their tragic situation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p292]
- The lack of expected anguish in locked-in patients supports the body loop theory: without the ability to enact emotional responses in the body, the emotional quality of experiences is blunted. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p293]
- Locked-in syndrome is commonly caused by structural damage in the anterior aspect of the pons and midbrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p357]
- Locked-in syndrome can also be caused by severe polyneuropathy, where nerves carrying signals for muscle contraction become dysfunctional, resulting in widespread paralysis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p357]
- The drug curare blocks nicotinic receptors of acetylcholine necessary for nerve fibers to command muscular contraction, resulting in paralysis of voluntary muscles while smooth muscles remain unaffected. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p357]
- In a fully curarized individual, involuntary commands to alter blood vessel caliber or modify viscera can still occur because smooth muscles use muscarinic receptors instead of nicotinic receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p357-358]

## Inferences

- Inference: Locked-in syndrome demonstrates that wakefulness alone is insufficient for consciousness; the specific posterior brain stem structures that remain intact in locked-in but are damaged in coma are essential for consciousness.
- Inference: The locked-in case provides natural evidence that consciousness alone, without body-based emotional grounding, lacks the full affective coloring of normal experience—suffering requires the body's participation in emotion.
