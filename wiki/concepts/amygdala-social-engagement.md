# Amygdala and Social Visual Engagement

## Summary

The amygdala plays a critical role in directing gaze toward ethologically relevant social cues, particularly aspects of the face including eyes and mouth. Research on autism spectrum conditions suggests that the tendency to look at faces is largely determined by genetic factors and may represent a continuum of sociality. Toddlers with autism rarely focus on critical parts of the face, reducing their exposure to social feedback from smiles and grimaces. Patients with bilateral amygdala damage have difficulty judging trustworthiness based on appearance and cannot recognize fearful faces.

## Key facts

- Internally driven seeking of face cues is heritable, with heritability most prominent in gaze toward the eyes and mouth. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p153]
- Autistic children rarely solicit or reciprocate eye contact with caregivers by two to six months of age, and they simply look elsewhere rather than having impaired eye movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p152]
- Monkeys with gaze contact neurons in the amygdala show firing rate increases of several-fold when the recorded monkey makes eye contact with video monkeys, with latencies of 80-140 ms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p155]
- The amygdala's main behavioral function may be to direct gaze to ethologically relevant cues for social communication rather than being the seat of emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p155]
- Patients with bilateral amygdala damage cannot recognize fearful faces but can easily recognize fear expressed via vocal communication. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p153]

## Inferences

- Inference: The disengagement hypothesis suggests that emotions can be conceptualized as disengagement from the autonomous action system, similar to how cognition involves disengagement from the motor action system.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neuronal mechanisms responsible for social visual engagement are not well understood.
