# German Apprenticeship System

## Summary

The German apprenticeship system (Dual System) is a coordinated training approach combining company-based practical training with vocational school instruction. The system is characterized by its governance structure involving works councils and business associations, its compatibility with German bank financing models, and its role in producing skilled workers with portable credentials.

## Key facts

- The German apprenticeship system has been analyzed through David Soskice's work reconciling it with Gary Becker's human capital model. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- German bank financing with its long-term outlook enables employers to invest in apprenticeship training with a longer perspective. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- Works councils and unions monitor in-company training to provide apprentices with guarantees on the quality and marketability of their credentials. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- Fifty-five percent of apprenticeship graduates leave their company after one year, rising to eighty percent after five years. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- In manual trades, only five percent enter Gymnasia and less than two percent complete it, indicating limited advancement pathways in certain sectors of the apprenticeship system. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- The system creates low distrust transaction costs due to the generally close and high-trust relations between company management and works councils. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]

## Inferences

- Inference: The chapter suggests the German apprenticeship system's success depends not just on technical training quality but on the institutional trust infrastructure (works councils, business associations) that validates and monitors training.
