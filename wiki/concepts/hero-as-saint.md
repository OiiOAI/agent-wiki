# Hero as Saint

## Summary

The saint or ascetic is the hero type who goes to the father but to the unmanifest rather than the manifest aspect—taking the step that the Bodhisattva renounced: that from which there is no return. The ego is burnt out; like a dead leaf in a breeze, the body continues to move but the soul has dissolved in the ocean of bliss.

## Key facts

- The saint is endowed with a pure understanding, restraining the self with firmness, turning away from sound and other objects, abandoning love and hatred; dwelling in solitude, eating but little, controlling speech, body, and mind, ever engaged in meditation and concentration, cultivating freedom from passion. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p277]
- Thomas Aquinas, as the result of a mystical experience while celebrating mass, put his pen and ink on the shelf and left the last chapters of his Summa Theologica to be completed by another hand, stating 'such things have been revealed to me that all I have written and taught seems of but small account to me.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p277]
- Beyond life, heroes are beyond the myth also; their legends are rehearsed but the pious sentiments and lessons are necessarily inadequate. They have stepped away from the realm of forms into the hidden; once the hidden profile has been discovered, myth is the penultimate, silence the ultimate word. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p277]

## Inferences

- Inference: The saint represents the ultimate hero achievement—not action in the world but the dissolution of the ego that allows the eternal to manifest through the individual while they still live.
