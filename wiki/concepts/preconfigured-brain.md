# Preconfigured Brain

## Summary

The preconfigured brain hypothesis suggests that the brain starts with evolutionarily preserved, preconfigured internal syntactical rules that can generate a huge repertoire of neuronal patterns. These patterns are initially nonsensical but can acquire meaning through experience. This contrasts with the tabula rasa view by proposing that learning is a 'fitting process' of experience onto preexisting neuronal patterns rather than creating new patterns from scratch.

## Key facts

- The preconfigured brain comes with evolutionarily preserved, preconfigured internal syntactical rules that can generate a huge repertoire of neuronal patterns regarded as initially nonsense neuronal words. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p25]
- Under this hypothesis, learning does not create brand new brain activity patterns from scratch but is a 'fitting process' of experience onto preexisting neuronal patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p25]
- The preconfigured brain is supported by a mechanism the author calls 'high-diversity' organization, where neuronal firing rates, connection strengths, and magnitude of collective behavior vary by three to four orders of magnitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p25]
- Neuroscience has accumulated experimental evidence against the tabula rasa model, including the recognition that much brain activity is self-organized instead of being driven by outside signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p24]
- In the preconfigured brain model, learning is a matching process, and network homeostasis is only minimally and transiently affected by new learning because only rearrangements take place. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p26]

## Inferences

- Inference: The preconfigured brain model may better explain the stability and resilience of brain networks against competing requirements for plasticity and redundancy.
