# Framing Effect

## Summary

A cognitive bias where different descriptions of the same objective choice lead to different decisions. This phenomenon is explained by prospect theory and has been demonstrated in capuchin monkeys through risk preference reversals between gains and losses frames.

## Key facts

- Capuchins showed a reflection effect: when options were framed in terms of gains, they avoided risk (preferred safe experimenter); when framed in terms of losses, they sought risk (preferred risky experimenter). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p89]
- This risk preference reversal is analogous to the pattern observed in humans tested with Tversky and Kahneman's Asian Disease problem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p89]
- A gamble offering a 10% chance to gain $95 and 90% chance to lose $5 is objectively identical to paying $5 for a lottery ticket offering a 10% chance to win $100, yet the second framing attracts more positive responses because losses feel more aversive than foregone gains. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p470]
- In one study, physicians presented with identical surgical outcomes chose surgery 84% of the time when framed as '90% survive one month' but only 50% chose surgery when the same data was framed as '10% die in the first month.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p473]
- In the Asian Disease problem, 72% of respondents chose a sure gain of 200 saved lives when framed as lives saved, but the majority chose the gamble when the same outcomes were framed as 400 deaths versus a probabilistic 600 deaths. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p475]
- Neuroimaging studies show the amygdala (emotion center) activates when choices align with frame-driven emotional responses, while the anterior cingulate (conflict monitor) activates when a person resists the frame's pull, suggesting framing effects are processed by System 1. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p472]
- Richard Thaler noted that if price differences between cash and credit card payments are permitted, retailers prefer to call the difference a 'cash discount' rather than a 'credit card surcharge,' because consumers accept discounts more readily than they accept surcharges. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p470]

## Inferences

- Inference: Framing effects may extend broadly across the animal kingdom and to a variety of taxa, suggesting they are not uniquely human or dependent on cultural factors.
- Inference: Framing effects are nearly impossible to detect without System 2 intervention. People passively accept the frame as given and rarely construct alternative framings, so they remain unaware that their preferences are frame-dependent rather than reality-dependent.
