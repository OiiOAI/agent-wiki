# Framing Effect

## Summary

A cognitive bias where different descriptions of the same objective choice lead to different decisions. This phenomenon is explained by prospect theory and has been demonstrated in capuchin monkeys through risk preference reversals between gains and losses frames.

## Key facts

- Capuchins showed a reflection effect: when options were framed in terms of gains, they avoided risk (preferred safe experimenter); when framed in terms of losses, they sought risk (preferred risky experimenter). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p89]
- This risk preference reversal is analogous to the pattern observed in humans tested with Tversky and Kahneman's Asian Disease problem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p89]

## Inferences

- Inference: Framing effects may extend broadly across the animal kingdom and to a variety of taxa, suggesting they are not uniquely human or dependent on cultural factors.
