# Synesthesia in Memory

## Summary

A condition where stimulation of one sensory pathway leads to automatic experiences in a second pathway. For S., every sound produced immediate experiences of light, color, taste, and touch. This synesthesia was central to his memory system, creating additional sensory 'tags' that guaranteed accurate recall—whenever S. recalled a word, he experienced its unique combination of synesthetic sensations as verification.

## Key facts

- S. possessed marked synesthesia from early childhood, where sounds and words produced colored, tasted, and tactile sensations simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p31]
- When presented with a tone of 30 cycles per second, S. saw a strip of tarnished silver color that narrowed and glistened like steel, while the twilight sound dazzled with silvery gleam. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p32]
- Vowels appeared to S. as simple figures while consonants appeared as splashes, some solid, others scattered—but all retaining distinct form, color, and taste. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p34]
- S. recognized words not only by the images they evoked but by a whole complex of synesthetic feelings the image aroused—taste, weight, and overall sensory experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p37]
- The synesthetic components served as secondary information in recall, providing 'extra' sensory verification that prevented errors in reproduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p37]
- S. heard voices as having distinct colors and textures; he described S.M. Eisenstein's voice as 'a flame with fibers protruding from it.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p33]

## Inferences

- Inference: S.'s synesthesia transformed the phonetic elements of speech into rich sensory experiences, making the sound of words inseparable from their visual and tactile qualities for him.

## Uncertainties
