# Closed Circle

## Summary

A closed circle is a self-referential reasoning system where decisions are based solely on signals from within the system, without external reference or independent analysis. Rumelt applies Gödel's incompleteness theorem metaphorically to human systems, arguing that when executives make decisions based solely on stock market signals, questions about industry fundamentals become 'un-decidable' within that closed logic.

## Key facts

- When executives manage solely based on recent stock market signals, the flow of information becomes a closed circle. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p99]
- Rumelt applies Gödel's incompleteness theorem metaphorically to human systems. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p99]
- In the fiber-optic business, the 'axiom' of market accuracy anchored the circle's center. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p99]
- Within the closed system, 'Are transatlantic cables an overcapacity commodity business?' became an un-decidable proposition. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p99]
- Similar closed circles occur when political leaders create policy based on opinion polls. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p99]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The application of Gödel's incompleteness theorem to social systems is a metaphor, not a mathematical proof.
