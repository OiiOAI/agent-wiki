# Norepinephrine Systems

## Summary

Norepinephrine is a catecholamine neurotransmitter arising from cell groups in the pons and medulla. It promotes sensory arousal, sustains high signal-to-noise ratios in sensory processing, and controls widespread brain activity through the locus coeruleus.

## Key facts

- Norepinephrine promotes sensory arousal and sustains high signal-to-noise ratios in sensory processing areas of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p62]
- The locus coeruleus (A6 cell group) sends inputs to cortex, hypothalamus, cerebellum, lower brain stem, and spinal cord via the dorsal NE pathway, exerting global control over brain activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p63]
- NE neurons contain internal pacemaker mechanisms requiring no incoming influences to maintain spontaneous activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p63]
- Attention deficit disorder in children is partly due to low brain NE activity, and psychostimulants that increase NE can paradoxically reduce hyperactivity by facilitating cortical arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p64]

## Inferences

- Inference: NE dampens background cortical neural activity irrelevant to a given task, increasing the ratio of signal to background noise.
