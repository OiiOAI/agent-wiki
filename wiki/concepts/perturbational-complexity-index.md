# Perturbational Complexity Index

## Summary

The Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI) is a consciousness measurement developed by Marcello Massimini. It uses Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) to stimulate brain activity and EEG to record how this activity spreads. The complexity of the resulting 'echo' pattern is quantified using LZW compression algorithms. Higher PCI values correlate with higher conscious states; the measure can distinguish vegetative from minimally conscious states and detect covert consciousness in brain-injured patients.

## Key facts

- PCI combines TMS to inject pulses of energy into the brain with EEG to record the brain's response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p46]
- Under unconscious states like dreamless sleep and general anaesthesia, the TMS echo is simple and local; during conscious states, the echo ranges widely across the cortex in complex patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p46]
- PCI uses LZW (Lempel-Ziv-Welch) complexity to quantify the algorithmic complexity of the brain's response to TMS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p47]
- A 2013 landmark study showed PCI values correlated well with levels of consciousness impairment in brain-injured patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p47]
- PCI can distinguish between vegetative state and minimally conscious state, and can detect consciousness in locked-in syndrome patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p48]

## Inferences

- Inference: PCI's clinical utility demonstrates that consciousness can be measured without relying solely on behavioural responses.
