# Cognitive Decline in Normal Aging

## Summary

Normal aging involves gradual cognitive decline that varies widely among individuals. Working and long-term memory, visuospatial abilities, and verbal fluency typically decline with age, while vocabulary and information comprehension are often spared. These changes result from structural brain alterations including white matter fragmentation, dendritic arbor shrinkage, neurotransmitter level decreases, and synaptic density reduction, particularly affecting frontal-striatal systems and the temporal lobes.

## Key facts

- Working and long-term declarative memory, visuospatial abilities, and verbal fluency usually decline with old age. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1563]
- Vocabulary, information, and comprehension often show minimal decline in normal individuals well into their 80s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1563]
- Brain weight decreases average 0.2% per year from college age onward and about 0.5% per year in the 70s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1563]
- White matter changes are widespread with age but are especially notable in the prefrontal and temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1565]
- Levels of enzymes synthesizing dopamine, norepinephrine, and acetylcholine decrease with age. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1564]
