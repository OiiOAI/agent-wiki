# Implicit and Explicit Memory

## Summary

Llinás discusses the subdivision of referential memory of the long-term variety into implicit (nondeclarative, unconscious) and explicit (declarative, conscious) types. Studies of amnesic patients, particularly HM, demonstrated these two memory systems are functionally and anatomically separable.

## Key facts

- Explicit memory, also known as declarative or conscious memory, refers to conscious recollection of faces, names of objects, and past experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p189]
- Implicit memory, nondeclarative or nonconscious memory, is the unconscious, unintentional retrieval of memory for performance of a learned activity or skill. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p189]
- Patient HM, studied extensively after medial temporal lobectomy, could not acquire new memories of places, names, events, or people, yet could learn motor skills without conscious recollection. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- After 30 trials over three days, HM could attain and retain a difficult mirror-drawing skill yet had no idea he had ever done the task before. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- Implicit memory includes motor skill learning, perceptual learning (priming), emotional learning such as fear conditioning, and category learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p191]
- Brain regions underlying implicit memory include most prominently the amygdala, whereas explicit memory involves the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p191]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between implicit and explicit memory in HM suggests that motor skill learning uses neural substrates fundamentally different from conscious recollection, supporting modular views of memory systems.
