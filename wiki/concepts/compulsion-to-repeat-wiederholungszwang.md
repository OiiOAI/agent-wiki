# Compulsion to Repeat (Wiederholungszwang)

## Summary

The compulsion to repeat is described by Freud as a fundamental attribute of the unconscious and of drive life, manifesting in childhood play, traumatic neuroses, and psychoanalytic treatment. It operates independently of and can override the pleasure principle, representing the 'driven' or impulsive character of primal drives.

## Key facts

- The compulsion to repeat is identified as a universal attribute of drives and perhaps of organic life generally, operating beyond and sometimes in opposition to the pleasure principle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5602]
- In psychoanalytic treatment, patients repeat repressed childhood experiences in the transference 'as a contemporary experience instead of remembering it as something belonging to the past'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5598]
- Children's play demonstrates repetition of unpleasurable experiences, which may serve the function of mastering traumatic impressions retrospectively. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5594]
- Traumatic dreams in war neuroses repeatedly bring patients back to the accident situation, suggesting the compulsion serves to master the trauma by developing 'the anxiety whose omission was the cause of the traumatic neurosis'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5611]

## Inferences

- Inference: The compulsion to repeat was a key clinical observation that led Freud to posit the existence of death drives, as it seemed to work against pleasure-seeking.
- Inference: The repetition compulsion operates through 'primary psychical processes' characterized by free displacement, condensation, and transfer of cathexes.
