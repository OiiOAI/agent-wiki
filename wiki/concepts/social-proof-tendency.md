# Social-Proof Tendency

## Summary

The automatic tendency to think and do what one observes others around one thinking and doing. Evolution left man with this tendency because followership often works fine (like finding the way to a football game). The tendency is most easily triggered in the presence of puzzlement or stress, and both good and bad behavior become contagious through it.

## Key facts

- Munger describes Serpico Syndrome: a nearly-totally corrupt New York police division joined by Frank Serpico, driven by social proof plus incentives. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p568]
- Oil companies foolishly buying mines were quickly followed by other oil companies doing the same, illustrating Social-Proof Tendency in business. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p565]
- Munger's favorite lesson from Social-Proof Tendency: 'Learn how to ignore the examples from others when they are wrong, because few skills are more worth having.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p571]
- The inaction of a great many bystanders led to the death of Kitty Genovese, illustrating how inaction by others becomes social proof that inaction is the right course. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p569]
- Social-Proof Tendency is most easily triggered in the presence of puzzlement or stress, and particularly when both exist together. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p574]
- Disreputable sales organizations manipulate targets into situations combining isolation and stress, where isolation strengthens the social proof from knaves and people who buy first. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p575]
- Munger's recommended lesson from Social-Proof Tendency is to learn how to ignore examples from others when they are wrong, because few skills are more worth having. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p579]

## Inferences

- Inference: Social-Proof Tendency explains why cults, fraudulent sales operations, and corrupt organizations are so effective—they create isolated, stressed environments where the behavior of planted accomplices becomes irresistible.
- Inference: The Kitty Genovese incident, where bystander inaction contributed to a death, demonstrates how inaction by others creates social proof that inaction is correct.
