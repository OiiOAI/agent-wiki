# Intersubjectivity

## Summary

Intersubjectivity in phenomenology refers to the mutual openness and interrelationship between conscious subjects. Husserl distinguishes three kinds: (1) open intersubjectivity as a structural feature of consciousness whereby objects appear as perceivable by other subjects, (2) face-to-face intersubjectivity as the direct encounter with the other's expressive bodily presence, and (3) generative intersubjectivity involving communally handed-down norms, conventions, and historical traditions. The open intersubjectivity of intentionality means that objects are disclosed as having hidden profiles that imply being perceivable by other possible perceivers. This structural openness precedes any actual encounter with others. At the face-to-face level, empathy enables mutual self and other understanding through processes of bodily coupling, imaginative transposition, and reiterated empathy. Human subjectivity emerges from developmental processes of enculturation within a distributed cognitive web of symbolic culture. The intersubjective perspective transcends any single first-person singular perspective, and self and other bring forth each other reciprocally through empathy. Enculturation shapes the cognitive architecture of the human mind so fundamentally that stripped of culture, humans would not have the cognitive capacities that make them human.
