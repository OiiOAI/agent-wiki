# Dream Interpretation

## Summary

Dream interpretation (Traumdeutung) is Freud's psychoanalytic technique for revealing the latent meaning hidden in the manifest content of dreams. Freud maintained that dreams are the 'royal road' to understanding unconscious mental life, expressing repressed wishes and conflicts through symbolic imagery.

## Key facts

- Freud claimed to have developed a psychological technique that makes it possible to interpret dreams, with every dream revealing itself as a psychical structure with a meaning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1285]
- Freud stated that anyone who fails to explain the origin of dream images can scarcely hope to understand phobias, obsessions or delusions or bring therapeutic influence to bear on them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1273]
- Freud noted he used only his own dreams and those of psychoanalytic patients to illustrate dream interpretation, as dreams from unknown sources were unsuitable for demonstrating the method. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1273]
- Interpreting a dream consists in translating the manifest content of the dream into the latent dream thoughts, undoing the distortion which the dream thoughts have had to submit to from the censorship of the resistance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2947]
- A dream is invariably related to the events of the day before the dream. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2946]
- Anxiety in anxiety dreams corresponds to a sexual affect, a libidinal feeling, and arises out of libido by the process of repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2949]
- Absurdity in dreams is a method of representation employed by the dream-work, indicating that ridicule or contradiction in dream thoughts is expressed through dream absurdity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2965]
- Freud declared that 'the interpretation of dreams is the royal road to a knowledge of the unconscious; it is the securest foundation of psychoanalysis'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3405]
- Dreams of children from eighteen months upwards are perfectly simple and easy to explain as fulfilments of wishes aroused the previous day. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3406]
- Adults' dreams are distorted substitutes for unconscious wish fulfilments, with the distortion being the work of the ego's defensive resistances. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3406]
- Dream-work involves condensation (Verdichtung) and displacement (Verschiebung), processes identical to those transforming repressed complexes into symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3407]
- Dreams are constructed like neurotic symptoms as compromises between the demands of a repressed impulse and the resistance of a censoring force. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6270]
- A dream is the disguised fulfillment of a repressed wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6270]
- Dream work includes condensation, displacement, translation into visual images, and secondary revision. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6271]
- Dreams serve the purpose of fending off external or internal stimuli that would arouse the sleeper. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6270]
- The general function of dreaming is to secure sleep against interruption through a soothing action. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6270]
