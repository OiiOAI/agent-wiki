# Prisoner's Dilemma

## Summary

A classic game theory scenario in which two prisoners held in separate cells without communication face a choice where they can only benefit if both choose cooperation, but neither knows what the other will choose. The dilemma illustrates how rational individual choice can lead to collectively inferior outcomes, and suggests that cultures instilling strong reciprocal obligation facilitate cooperative solutions more easily than cultures legitimizing greater individualism.

## Key facts

- The prisoner's dilemma involves two prisoners held in separate cells unable to communicate who face a choice where they can only benefit if both pick a cooperative option without knowing the other's choice. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p408]
- A culture that inculcates a strong sense of reciprocal obligation among its members would more easily find a solution to the prisoner's dilemma than one that legitimizes greater individualism. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p408]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prisoner's dilemma provides a theoretical framework for understanding collective action problems, suggesting that social trust and norms of reciprocity are essential for achieving cooperative outcomes.
