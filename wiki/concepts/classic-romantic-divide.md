# Classic-Romantic Divide

## Summary

The classic-romantic divide is the fundamental split in ways of understanding the world. The classical (or square) mode is analytical, systematic, concerned with chunks and bits and pieces of things with continuity presumed. The romantic (or hip) mode is intuitive, holistic, concerned only with continuities of things with chunks presumed. Quality is identified as the cleavage term that cleanly splits these two worlds. The narrator suggests understanding Quality can unite rather than divide them.

## Key facts

- The narrator explains: 'Science works with chunks and bits and pieces of things with the continuity presumed, and DeWeese works only with the continuities of things with the chunks and bits and pieces presumed.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p138]
- The narrator describes squareness as 'an inability to see quality before it's been intellectually defined, that is, before it gets all chopped up into words.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p185]
- The narrator identifies Quality as the term that both worlds use and know, 'It was just that the romantic left it alone and appreciated it for what it was and the classic tried to turn it into a set of intellectual building blocks for other purposes.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p189]
