# Perceptron

## Summary

A perceptron is a neural network model consisting of layers of idealized neurons with synaptic connections from each layer to the succeeding one. Introduced by Frank Rosenblatt in the 1950s, perceptrons model how visual perception involves both sequential and parallel processing of information through hierarchical feature detection.

## Key facts

- In a perceptron, neurons are organized in layers with synaptic connections from each layer to the succeeding one, so that information flows in the 'forward' direction from input to output. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1637]
- The computations in a perceptron, as in the visual system, occur through both sequential and parallel processing of information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1637]
- Networks of McCulloch-Pitts neurons can compute any Boolean function, including XOR, by combining AND, OR, and NOT operations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1637]
- The hierarchical perceptron model proposes that visual recognition of an object involves a relatively small number of sequential steps, each consisting of a large number of operations executed in parallel. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1642]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perceptrons lack synaptic loops and therefore have relatively simple dynamics compared to recurrent networks.
- Inference: The Neocognitron and LeNet represent elaborations of the perceptron concept applied to object recognition.
