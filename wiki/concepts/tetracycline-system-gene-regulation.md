# Tetracycline system for gene regulation

## Summary

The tetracycline system allows temporal and spatial regulation of transgene expression in mice. It uses two independent transgenic lines: one expressing the tetracycline transactivator (tTA) under a cell-type-specific promoter, and another containing a gene of interest under control of the tetO promoter. In the absence of doxycycline, tTA activates transcription from tetO; when doxycycline is administered, it binds to tTA and blocks promoter activation, shutting off transgene expression.

## Key facts

- The tetracycline system uses tTA, an engineered protein incorporating a bacterial transcription factor that recognizes the bacterial tetO operon, driven by a cell-type-specific promoter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1558]
- When tTA binds to tetO it activates transcription of the downstream gene of interest; tetracycline or doxycycline binding causes a conformational change that leads to unbinding and blocks transgene expression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1558]
- The tetracycline system can be used to create reversible memory deficits: transgenic mice expressing CaMKII-Asp286 show impaired LTP and cannot learn the Barnes maze task, but these deficits are reversed when doxycycline is administered. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
