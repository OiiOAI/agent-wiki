# Commander's Intent

## Summary

Commander's Intent is a communication technique discovered by Hamlet in Shakespeare's play, where he tells actors exactly what to do but then tells them exactly what not to do, before informing them that their "end" is to hold a "mirror up to nature." This allows actors to successfully improvise the middle. Churchill used this method in WWII, giving vague orders that led to specific innovations like the British Commandos.

## Key facts

- Hamlet gives actors two counterproductive instructions (what to do and what not to do) before rescuing communication by informing them their end is to "hold a mirror up to nature". [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p160]
- Churchill memorized Hamlet's "mirror up to nature" speech and used Commander's Intent when ordering development of "specially trained troops of the hunter class". [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p160]
- The resulting British Commandos developed training innovations not in Churchill's order — mountain hiking, zip lines, rabbit stalking, blindfolded boxing — that worked exactly as he wanted. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p161]
- The Green Berets' three-step formula is: tell the team the goal, explain the why, then close your mouth and let the team invent the middle themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p161]
- Commander's Intent statements serve to let subordinates understand the goals of a mission so they can improvise when plans change. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p227]
- The first facet provides the big picture of why the task is being performed, appearing in 14 of 35 Commander's Intent statements analyzed. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p277]
- The second facet presents an image of what the successfully completed request will look like; it was missing only once in 35 statements reviewed. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p277]
- The third facet is the plan—where commanders often err by providing too many details instead of the big picture. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p277]
- Colonel Hanan Schwartz of the Israeli Defense Forces argued that the plan should be left to the discretion of the unit carrying it out. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p277]
- The fourth facet explains the reasoning behind the plan, allowing subordinates to potentially suggest alternative approaches. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p278]
- The fifth facet prepares people to make key decisions, including contingencies and priorities; found in 13 of 35 statements. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p278]
- The sixth facet refers to antigoals—outcomes the person does not want to happen—added in only 5 of 35 statements. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p278]
- The seventh facet includes constraints and extra information such as terrain and weather observations. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p279]
- Karl Weick described a streamlined five-facet version: here's what I think we face, what we should do, why, what to watch, and now talk to me. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p279]

## Inferences

- Inference: The art of describing intent is to give as little information as needed while ensuring critical considerations are not omitted.
- Inference: The description of intent should reflect the expertise of recipients, stability of the situation, and clarity of the expected outcome.
