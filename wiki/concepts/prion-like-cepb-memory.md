# Prion-like CPEB and Memory Persistence

## Summary

CPEB (cytoplasmic polyadenylation element-binding protein) is a prion-like molecule in Aplysia neurons that maintains long-term memory persistence. Unlike disease-causing prions, this form of CPEB serves a normal physiological function: its self-perpetuating conformational change at activated synapses maintains local protein synthesis needed to sustain newly grown synaptic terminals.

## Key facts

- Kausik Si discovered that CPEB in Aplysia has prion-like properties, with one end of the protein having characteristics of a prion. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p283]
- Prions can fold into two functionally distinct conformations—dominant and recessive—with the dominant form being self-perpetuating. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p283]
- The conversion of CPEB from inactive to active form is controlled by serotonin, the transmitter required for converting short- to long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p285]
- This was the first discovery of a self-propagating form of a prion serving a normal physiological function rather than causing disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p285]
- Self-perpetuation of CPEB allows information to be stored selectively and in perpetuity at one synapse, explaining how memories persist despite constant protein turnover. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p285]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prion-like mechanism of CPEB explains how memories can persist for a lifetime despite constant protein degradation and turnover in neurons.
