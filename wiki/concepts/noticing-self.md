# The Noticing Self

## Summary

The noticing self is a concept describing a part of consciousness that observes, notices, and is always present. It is fundamentally different from the thinking self, which thinks, plans, judges, compares, creates, imagines, visualizes, analyzes, remembers, daydreams, and fantasizes. The noticing self doesn't think; it only notices. It is responsible for focus, attention, and awareness. It notices thoughts but cannot create them. The thinking self comments on experience while the noticing self notices experience directly. The book uses the metaphor of life as a stage show, with the noticing self being the part that can step back and watch, zoom in to any part, or take in the big picture. When thoughts and images appear, the thinking self produces them while the noticing self observes them coming and going. This separation from cognitions is fundamental to mindfulness and unhooking.
