# Proper Mass

## Summary

Proper mass is a concept introduced by Harry Jerison suggesting that evolution provides only as much of each tissue as is optimal given other body systems. Applied to the brain, this implies that brain size should reflect the balance between evolutionary advantages and costs of neural information processing, with average mammalian brain size for a given body weight providing an index of proper mass.

## Key facts

- Jerison argued that the fraction of brain computation dedicated to body maintenance should scale to the 2/3 power of body size because neural representations reflect body surface maps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p167]
- Deacon critiques the proper mass concept, noting that noncognitive metabolic costs must be considered, so average brain scaling does not reflect isometric intellectual capacity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p167]
- Proper mass assessed morphologically cannot be equated to net computational power. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p167]
