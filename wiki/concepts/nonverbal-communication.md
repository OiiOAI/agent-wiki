# Nonverbal Communication

## Summary

Nonverbal communication is described as a 'second language' that humans emit continuously but rarely consciously observe. The text estimates that over 65 percent of human communication is nonverbal, yet people internalize only about 5 percent of this information. This language includes facial expressions, vocal inflections, body tension, nervous gestures, and breathing patterns that reveal true feelings beneath verbal masks.

## Key facts

- Over 65 percent of human communication is estimated to be nonverbal, but people pick up and internalize only about 5 percent of this information. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p105]
- People's real feelings leak out in gestures, tones of voice, facial expressions, and posture because they cannot completely control what they communicate while also concealing it. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p108]
- Microexpressions last less than a second and occur when people are aware of negative feelings and try to suppress them, but they leak out involuntarily. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p115]
- Signs of genuine smiles differ from fake smiles: genuine smiles affect the muscles around the eyes, widening them and often revealing crow's-feet, and pull the cheeks upward. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p119]
- Pupil dilation is a reliable sign that a person is comfortable and likes what they are seeing, as blood rushes to the face during emotional arousal. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p119]
- The voice is harder to consciously modulate than the face, making it an especially honest indicator of genuine feelings. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p119]
- Synchrony—where one person unconsciously mirrors another's posture, leg crossing, head tilt, and eventually breathing patterns—indicates deepening rapport. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p120]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text argues that nonverbal communication requires a different form of knowledge than verbal communication—it must be felt physically rather than simply translated into words.
