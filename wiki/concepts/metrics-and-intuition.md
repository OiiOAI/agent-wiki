# Metrics and Intuition

## Summary

Metrics and intuition serve complementary functions in decision-making. While metrics provide documentation and objective measures, they can also mislead through loss of context and history. Effective decision-makers blend metrics with stories and mental models to fully understand situations.

## Key facts

- Metrics serve functions including setting goals, setting tripwires, spotting trends, sensemaking, regulating performance, ensuring compliance, making comparisons, evaluating performance, promoting fairness, and building stories. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p284]
- Two basic problems with metrics are the loss of history about how data were collected and analyzed, and the loss of context for understanding the metrics. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p287]
- The simpler the metric, the more likely it is to mislead; effective CEOs use a range of metrics rather than a single yardstick. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p290]
- Once we know what a metric is, we can usually find a way to game it, creating perverse incentives with effects opposite to what was desired. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p287]
- Metrics and stories should inform each other; metrics are like puzzle pieces and stories provide the picture on the box showing how pieces fit together. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p293]
- Natural frequencies are easier for people to interpret than probabilities; presenting data as frequencies rather than probabilities significantly improves understanding. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p298]

## Inferences

- Inference: The relationship between metrics and intuition is similar to the analytical-intuitive balance discussed in Chapter 5, requiring neither blind faith in numbers nor dismissal of quantitative data in favor of gut feelings.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The book acknowledges that while blending metrics and stories is ideal, it can be difficult to implement in practice due to time pressure and complexity.
