# Empathy

## Summary

Empathy (Einfühlung) is a unique form of intentionality through which we are directed toward another's experience. Phenomenologists distinguish empathy from mere inference or projection, viewing it as a direct experience of the other as a person—a being whose gestures and actions are expressive of subjective states. Edith Stein identified three levels of empathy: (1) emergence of the other's experience before one as an object, (2) fulfillment through delving into the content and imaginative transposition to the other's perspective, and (3) comprehensive objectification of the explained experience. Four types of empathy can be distinguished: (1) passive coupling of living bodies in perception and action, (2) imaginary transposition to the other's place, (3) mutual understanding of self and other as each being other for the other (reiterated empathy), and (4) moral perception of the other as a being deserving concern and respect. Empathy is based on perceiving the other's bodily presence as animated by its own sensations, expressive of subjective experience, and as another center of spatial orientation. Reiterated empathy—seeing oneself from the other's perspective—enables one to gain a nonegocentric view of one's own lived body and constitutes a basic sense of personal selfhood tied to recognition by others.

- Empathy differs from sympathy or compassion: in empathy one shares the feeling, while in sympathy one feels pity or love for the person but not necessarily the same feeling. ## Inferences

- Inference: The intentionality of consciousness is "intersubjectively open"—objects are disclosed as potentially perceivable by other subjects in principle, even when no others are actually present.
- Inference: Empathy may have motivational and social roles beyond epistemology, as psychopaths who lack empathy but have intact mentalizing abilities demonstrate the functional separation of these systems
- Inference: The empathic response is proposed as a first step in a chain beginning with affect sharing, leading to understanding others' feelings, which then motivates other-regarding concern and helping behavior
- Inference: Empathy and theory of mind represent two distinct capacities relying on different circuitries that usually work together in normal individuals.
- Inference: The automaticity of empathic resonance creates a link between our own and others' needs, desires, and emotions, implying motives are not only self-interested but also automatically other-regarding.
- Inference: Empathy is presented as having healing power, with people transcending psychological pain when they have sufficient contact with someone who can hear them empathically.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which non-human animals possess cognitive empathy remains debated.
