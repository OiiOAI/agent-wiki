# Bifurcated Consciousness

## Summary

Bifurcated consciousness is a concept in feminist sociology describing the division women experience between their own lived experience and the established social typifications available to describe that experience. This 'line of fault' develops between actual, lived, reflected-on experience and the reality of social typifications imposed by a male-dominated culture. The concept, developed from Dorothy Smith's work, suggests that for subordinates, everyday life divides into two realities, leading to the question of how people survive when their own experience does not fit established social typifications.

## Key facts

- Feminist sociologists stress that for women, the most pervasive feature of the cognitive style of everyday life is 'bifurcated consciousness,' developing along 'a line of fault' between lived experience and established social typifications. - Everyday life divides into two realities for subordinates: the reality of actual, lived, reflected-on experience and the reality of social typifications. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p398]
- The concept originates from Dorothy Smith's work on how women experience the disjuncture between their actual experiences and the cultural categories available to describe them. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p398]
