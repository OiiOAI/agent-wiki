# The Two Options for Confidence

## Summary

The chapter presents two options for confidence that decide how confident you will be in every situation. The Chimp option bases confidence on belief in ability to achieve certain levels—if you do not reach those levels, confidence drops and fear increases. The Human option bases confidence on doing your best—if you do your best, you have 100% confidence because you can always guarantee your best effort. With the Human basis, you can hold your head up, smile, and say you could not have done more; you are an adult who can deal with consequences.

## Key facts

- The Chimp bases confidence on belief in ability to reach certain levels it wants to achieve and cannot deal with consequences of not reaching them; the Human bases confidence on doing their best and can always deal with consequences as an adult. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p212]
- If you base confidence on what you can achieve you cannot guarantee it and your Chimp will cause unease seeing the threat of failure; if you base confidence on doing your best you have full control and your Human will take over, seeing life as full of opportunities. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p212]
- The normal state for the Human is confidence because you can always do your best and deal with consequences, therefore there is no fear; the normal state for the Chimp is variable confidence with fear based around consequences and possible failure. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p212]
- Doing your best means that at the time you tried you gave it everything; you cannot do better than your best, so if you base confidence on doing your best, you will always achieve 100% confidence. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p213]
- Not doing your best is when you are dishonest, deliberately lazy, indifferent, or devious, which is different from being disorganised or undisciplined. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p214]
- A common problem causing unhappiness is an unrealistic image of yourself expecting to be perfect; this is a Gremlin and it is impossible to live up to, meaning every move is terrifying and you live with an inadequate self-image. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p215]
- Loss of self-confidence when a relationship goes wrong takes the Chimp about three months to come to terms with through a grieving process; being gentle on yourself during this period is important but watch for self-pity. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p216]
