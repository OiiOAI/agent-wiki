# Mixed Conceptions of Justice

## Summary

Mixed conceptions substitute the standard of utility or other criteria for the second principle of justice while accepting the first principle of equal liberties. Rawls argues these conceptions have intuitionistic features because they lack clear criteria for determining weights between competing ends, and the difference principle may stand in the background controlling the weights expressed in everyday judgments.

## Key facts

- Mixed conceptions are defined by substituting the standard of utility and other criteria for the second principle of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p288]
- All mixed conceptions accept the first principle and recognize the primary place of equal liberties. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p288]
- Mixed conceptions have intuitionistic features because the social minimum and other parameters are not clearly defined. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p289]
- The difference principle may stand in the background controlling the weights expressed in our everyday judgments. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p290]
- The conventions for interpersonal comparisons have unexpected moral consequences and depend upon value judgments. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p295]
