# Nucleus Basalis

## Summary

The nucleus basalis is a part of the brain that, when activated, puts the brain in an extremely plastic state. During the critical period it is continuously turned on by BDNF, allowing effortless learning. In adults it can only be activated by something important, surprising, novel, or through deliberate close attention. Merzenich calls it 'the modulatory control system of plasticity' and believes its atrophy with age contributes to cognitive decline.

## Key facts

- The nucleus basalis, turned on by BDNF during the critical period, allows the brain to focus attention and remember experiences—Merzenich calls it 'a teacher in the brain saying, this is really important.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p90]
- After the critical period, the nucleus basalis can only be activated when something important, surprising, or novel occurs, or if we make the effort to pay close attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p91]
- Merzenich and Kilgard artificially turned on the nucleus basalis in adult rats using microelectrodes and could massively expand brain maps for specific sounds without rewards or attention requirements. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p94]
- In adults with mild cognitive impairment, the acetylcholine produced in the nucleus basalis is not even measurable. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p97]
- The neglect of the nucleus basalis with age leads to 'fuzzy engrams'—representations of sounds or words that are not sharp because neurons don't fire in coordinated, quick ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p96]
