# Existential Vacuum

## Summary

The existential vacuum is Frankl's term for the widespread feeling of total and ultimate meaninglessness of life. It results from a twofold loss: humans have lost animal instincts that provided security, and traditions that buttressed behavior are rapidly diminishing. No instinct tells man what he has to do, and no tradition tells him what he ought to do. This manifests mainly in boredom, and is more prevalent in American students (60%) than European students (25%).

## Key facts

- The existential vacuum is a widespread phenomenon of the twentieth century caused by a twofold loss: the loss of basic animal instincts and the diminishing of traditions which buttressed behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p81]
- No instinct tells man what he has to do, and no tradition tells him what he ought to do; sometimes he does not even know what he wishes to do. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p81]
- A statistical survey showed 25% of European students showed a more-or-less marked degree of existential vacuum, while among American students it was 60%. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p81]
- The existential vacuum manifests itself mainly in a state of boredom, and Schopenhauer said mankind was apparently doomed to vacillate eternally between the two extremes of distress and boredom. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p81]
