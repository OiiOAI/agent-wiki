# False Connection

## Summary

A false connection occurs when a patient creates a causal link between a present mood or symptom and an available conscious idea, rather than recognizing the true unconscious origin. This phenomenon was observed in Frau Emmy von N., who attributed her morning anxiety to concerns about a lift rather than to the real cause (fear of her period returning). The splitting of consciousness facilitates the occurrence of false connections.

## Key facts

- Emmy von N. attributed her morning anxiety to concerns about an unsafe lift, when the true cause was fear of her period returning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p600]
- There is a necessity for bringing psychical phenomena of which one becomes conscious into causal connection with other conscious material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p601]
- When the true causation evades conscious perception, one attempts to make another connection which is believed although false. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p601]
- A split in the content of consciousness greatly facilitates the occurrence of false connections. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p601]
