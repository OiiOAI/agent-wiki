# Aesthetics and Art (Evolutionary Origins)

## Summary

Gazzaniga reviews multiple evolutionary theories for art and aesthetics: Dissanayake's 'making special' theory (art as biological behavior increasing group cohesion), Miller's sexual selection theory (art as fitness indicator like the peacock's tail), Pinker's by-product theory, and Tooby and Cosmides' developmental adaptation theory (art facilitates brain organization). Evidence suggests universal aesthetic preferences (symmetry, fractals) have biological roots.

## Key facts

- Dissanayake proposes art is a biological behavior that increases group cohesion and thus survival advantage, with the fundamental tendency being 'making special'—distinguishing objects/actions through emotional appeal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p224]
- Geoffrey Miller suggests the arts are fitness indicators subject to sexual selection—the more complex and non-functional the artwork, the better it signals genetic quality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p225]
- Steven Pinker initially suggested arts are by-products of brain systems designed for other purposes, like recreational drugs—pleasurable but not adaptive. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p226]
- Tooby and Cosmides argue arts facilitate brain development through aesthetic motivations that guide adaptations toward full capacity, serving an organizational rather than purely functional mode. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p230]
- Symmetry preference is found cross-culturally, in other animals, and plays a role in mate selection, suggesting biological roots in sexual selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p234]
- Humans prefer fractal patterns with a fractal dimension (D) of 1.3, and show lower stress responses when viewing such patterns, suggesting hardwired visual system tuning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p237]

## Inferences

- Inference: The convergence of universal aesthetic preferences across cultures and in other animals suggests these preferences evolved in response to environmental demands rather than being purely cultural constructs.
