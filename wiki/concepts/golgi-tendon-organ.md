# Golgi Tendon Organ

## Summary

The Golgi tendon organ is a force-sensitive sensory receptor located at the muscle-tendon junction that signals muscle tension. Each organ consists of collagen fibers connected in series to muscle fibers, with Ib sensory axons intertwined within the capsule. The organ fires in proportion to muscle force, providing the nervous system with precise information about the contractile state of muscle.

## Key facts

- Golgi tendon organs are located at the junction between skeletal muscle fibers and tendon, consisting of braided collagen fibers connected in series to muscle fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p771]
- Each Golgi tendon organ is innervated by a single Ib axon whose endings become intertwined with collagen fascicles inside the capsule. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p771]
- Golgi tendon organs are most sensitive to changes in muscle tension, with contraction being a particularly potent stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p771]
- The average firing rate of a population of tendon organs in a muscle provides a good index of total muscle force. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p771]
- Ib sensory fibers from tendon organs mediate disynaptic autogenic inhibition of homonymous motor neurons through inhibitory interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p770]
- Ib inhibitory interneurons receive convergent input from tendon organs, muscle spindles, cutaneous receptors, and joint receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p772]
