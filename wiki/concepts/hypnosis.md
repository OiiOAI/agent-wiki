# Hypnosis

## Summary

Hypnosis is a state of suggestibility induced through various techniques. Bernheim viewed suggestion as the essential element in hypnotism, with hypnosis being itself a suggested state. Freud practiced hypnotic treatment for many years before abandoning it because while it was rapid and painless, its results were unreliable and impermanent—symptoms returned or were replaced by others. Freud found hypnosis was essentially 'hackwork' that recalled 'magic, incantations and hocus-pocus.' Psychoanalysis abandoned hypnosis but rediscovered suggestion in the form of transference, though now the therapist guides and analyses the transference rather than leaving it untouched.
