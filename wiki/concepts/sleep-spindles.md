# Sleep Spindles

## Summary

Sleep spindles are 7-15 Hz oscillatory waves lasting 1-2 seconds that characterize stage 2 non-REM sleep. They are generated by interactions between thalamic relay neurons and GABAergic interneurons in the reticular thalamic nucleus. The same thalamocortical circuitry that generates sleep spindles during slow wave sleep is also thought to be essential for generating primary generalized seizures.

## Key facts

- Stage 2 sleep is marked in the EEG by the onset of spindle waves, 7-15 Hz oscillations over a period of 1-2 seconds that resemble a spindle of thread. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1193]
- Sleep spindles and K-complexes reflect slow, synchronized oscillations of neuronal and synaptic activity within the thalamus and cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1193]
- Studies of thalamic brain slices by David McCormick and colleagues indicate that GABAergic interneurons hyperpolarize relay neurons, removing inactivation of T-type Ca2+ channels, leading to an oscillatory response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1183]
- The thalamocortical connections that participate in the generation of sleep spindles are thought to be essential for the generation of primary generalized seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1182]
