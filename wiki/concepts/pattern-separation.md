# Pattern separation

## Summary

Pattern separation and pattern completion are complementary abilities required for optimal memory performance. Pattern separation allows distinction between similar memories, episodes, or spatial configurations, while pattern completion enables retrieval of memories using partial cues. Pattern separation depends on the direct projection from the entorhinal cortex to the dentate gyrus, and pattern completion depends on recurrent connections between CA3 pyramidal cells.

## Key facts

- Pattern separation is the ability to distinguish between two closely related images, episodes, or spatial configurations, while pattern completion uses partial cues to retrieve previously stored memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
- Pattern completion depends on the recurrent connections between CA3 pyramidal cells, as shown by mice with selective loss of LTP at CA3 recurrent synapses that cannot find the hidden platform with fewer spatial cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
- Pattern separation depends on the direct projection from the entorhinal cortex to the dentate gyrus, as shown by mice lacking functional NMDA receptors in dentate gyrus granule neurons that cannot perform pattern separation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
- Adult-born granule neurons in the dentate gyrus are specifically required for pattern separation, and procedures that stimulate neurogenesis enhance pattern separation ability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
