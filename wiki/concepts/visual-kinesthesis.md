# Visual Kinesthesis

## Summary

Visual kinesthesis is the pickup of information about bodily movement through vision, complementing muscle-joint kinesthesis. Discovered during WWII flight training research, it refers to how the flowing ambient optic array specifies locomotion—both the direction of movement (focus of expansion) and the fact of self-movement. Visual kinesthesis operates during passive locomotion (e.g., in a vehicle) where muscle-joint kinesthesis is absent.

## Key facts

- Visual kinesthesis yields the only reliable information about displacement; classical kinesthesis (muscle-joint sensation) is not trustworthy, as demonstrated by a fish in a stream or bird in wind that must exercise muscles merely to stay in place. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p148]
- The focus of out-flow (center of optical expansion) at the horizon specifies the direction of locomotion—this is an optical invariant, not a sensory cue. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p206]
- Visual kinesthesis is the same for passive as for active movement, whereas visual feedback is absent with passive movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p207]
- Three types of movement have unique optical information: head turning (sweeping of field over ambient array), limb movement (protrusion of shapes into field), and locomotion (flow of ambient array). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p150]
- The Lishman and Lee 'gliding room' experiment (1973) demonstrated that an invisibly moving enclosure induces compelling illusion of forward-backward self-movement when floor contact is obscured. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p208]
- Visual kinesthesis specifies locomotion relative to the environment, whereas other kinds of kinesthesis may or may not do so. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p248]
- The control of locomotion in the environment must be visual—walking, bicycling, and driving involve different classical kinestheses but the same visual kinesthesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p248]
- The transposition of the fovea over the sample of the array and the sweeping of the edges of the field of view over the ambient array are propriospecific—they specify eye turning and head turning respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p241]
- Visual kinesthesis allows the bird in wind to perceive when it must fly to stay in place and arrest the flow of the ambient array. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p248]

## Inferences

- Inference: Vision is not purely exteroceptive—it obtains information about both the environment and the self. This challenges the doctrine of special senses as separate channels.
- Inference: Visual kinesthesis provides the proprioceptive complement to exteroception, making the traditional separation between self and world perception artificial.
