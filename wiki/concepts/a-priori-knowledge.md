# A Priori Knowledge

## Summary

Based on Kant's philosophy as discussed in the book, a priori knowledge consists of aspects of reality not supplied immediately by the senses—concepts the mind must contribute, such as time and space. These serve as a screening function for sense data, allowing the world to be intelligible rather than a 'kaleidoscopic jumble.' The book applies this concept to understanding the motorcycle as an a priori construction in the mind, continuously confirmed but never identical to sensory data.

## Key facts

- Kant stated there are aspects of reality not supplied immediately by the senses called a priori, such as time and space, which the mind must supply as it receives sense data. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- The a priori concepts have their origins in human nature, neither caused by the sensed object nor bring it into being, but providing a kind of screening function for what sense data we will accept. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- What we think of as reality is a continuous synthesis of elements from a fixed hierarchy of a priori concepts and the ever-changing data of the senses. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p111]
- The a priori motorcycle has been built up over many years from enormous amounts of sense data and is constantly changing as new sense data come in. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p112]

## Inferences

- Inference: Kant's Copernican revolution in metaphysics parallels Copernicus's astronomical revolution—both involve turning inside out our a priori concepts rather than changing the objective world.
