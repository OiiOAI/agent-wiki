# Dendrite Spikes

## Summary

Dendrite spikes are electrical signals that travel along the branches of neuron dendrites. Discovered around 1990, they begin when a group of about 20 synapses close together on a dendrite branch receive input simultaneously. Rather than being powerful enough to create a full action potential, they raise the neuron's voltage to a 'predictive state,' priming it to fire sooner. Hawkins proposes that dendrite spikes are the neural implementation of predictions.

## Key facts

- Dendrite spikes occur when about 20 synapses situated next to each other on a dendrite branch receive input at the same time. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p54]
- Less than 10 percent of synapses in the neocortex are in the proximal area near the cell body; the other 90 percent are too far away to directly cause a spike. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p54]
- When a dendrite spike reaches the cell body, it raises the voltage but not enough to make the neuron spike, putting it in a predictive state. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p54]
- A dendrite spike means the neuron has recognized a pattern and predicts it should become active, allowing it to spike a little sooner than other neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p54]
- The 2010 discovery that dendrite spikes represent predictions explained why we are unaware of most predictions—they are not sent along axons to other neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p55]
- Unexpected inputs cause more neurons to fire at once because neurons not in predictive state all compete, while predicted inputs only activate predictive-state neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p55]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since dendrite spikes are predictions made within individual neurons, this suggests that much of the brain's computational work occurs at the single-neuron level rather than purely through network interactions.
