# Wish Fulfillment in Dreams

## Summary

The theory that every dream represents the fulfillment of a wish, proposed by Sigmund Freud as the central discovery of dream interpretation. This challenges the prevailing view that dreams are meaningless somatic processes.

## Key facts

- Dreams are not meaningless but represent the fulfillment of wishes rather than 'a fragmentary activity of the brain' as authorities had claimed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1395]
- The dream fulfills wishes that originated from events of the previous evening. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p118]
- Freud's specimen dream of July 23-24, 1895 served as the first dream he could decode to his own satisfaction, demonstrating the wish-fulfillment principle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p106]
- Freud states that 'a dream is a (disguised) fulfillment of a (suppressed or repressed) wish.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1428]
- Simple wish-fulfillment dreams are most common in children, who have not yet developed the complex censorship mechanisms of adults. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1399]
- Freud's own 'thirst dream' demonstrates a 'dream of convenience' where the dream satisfies the wish to drink, preventing the need to wake. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1397]
- Counterwish dreams (Versagenstraum) express the opposite of desired outcomes and are explained by either the wish to be proven wrong or by masochistic tendencies. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1426]
- The interpretation that a married woman's dream of having her period actually announced her missed period reflects a wish to delay the burden of motherhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1398]
- The dream-work consists essentially in the transformation of thoughts into a hallucinatory experience that fulfills a wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4915]
- Anxiety dreams are the open fulfillment of a repressed wish, where the censorship has failed to provide adequate distortion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4918]
- The dream censorship at night is reduced in favor of the single wish to sleep, allowing forbidden wishes to become active. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4920]
- Punishment dreams fulfill the wish of the censoring instance and represent the ego's reaction to repressed impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4921]

## Inferences

- Inference: The wish-fulfillment theory emerged from Freud's clinical work with hysterics and his method of free association.
- Inference: Freud's specimen dream revealed that the dream's apparent meaning was to exculpate him from responsibility for a patient's continued symptoms, fulfilling the wish to not be blamed.
- Inference: Counterwish dreams where the dreamer wishes Freud to be wrong about dream theory are particularly common in patients undergoing treatment, revealing resistance to the psychoanalytic method itself.
- Inference: Freud posits that every dream contains an unconscious wish as its motive force, which is added to the day's residues to enable dream construction.
- Inference: The fairy tale of the three wishes illustrates that when two people are not at one, fulfillment of one person's wish may bring unpleasure to the other—this mirrors the dreamer's relation to their own wishes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The claim that all dreams without exception are wish fulfillments remains theoretically stated but Freud acknowledges he will return to the subject later.
- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges the question of why evil wishes become active precisely at night remains tied to hypotheses about the nature of sleep.
