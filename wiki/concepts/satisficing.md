# Satisficing

## Summary

Satisficing, a concept introduced by Herbert Simon who won the Nobel Prize in economics, describes the decision strategy of selecting the first option that works rather than searching for the optimal option. In the context of naturalistic decision making, satisficing explains why experienced decision makers under time pressure evaluate options one at a time rather than comparing alternatives.

## Key facts

- Herbert Simon identified satisficing as selecting the first option that works, different from optimizing which tries to find the best strategy. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p41]
- Satisficing is more efficient than optimizing, which is hard and takes a long time. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p41]
- The singular evaluation strategy is based on satisficing and makes sense for fireground commanders because of their immense time pressure. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p41]
- Experienced fireground commanders could generate a single course of action from their experience, while novices needed to compare different approaches. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p42]
