# Processing Fluency Theory of Beauty

## Summary

Proposed by Reber, Schwarz, and Winkielman, this theory states that beauty as aesthetic pleasure is a function of the perceiver's processing dynamics—the more fluently perceivers can mentally process an object, the more positive their aesthetic response. The theory rests on four assumptions about ease of processing, positive feelings, value judgment contribution, and moderating factors.

## Key facts

- Processing fluency theory proposes that the ease of mental processing of an object directly influences positive aesthetic response—the easier to process, the more liked. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p218]
- The theory assumes that some objects are processed more easily because they contain hardwired features (like symmetry) that the brain processes quickly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p218]
- Positive feelings from fluent processing contribute to value judgments about beauty unless one questions the informational value of the input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p218]
- Processing fluency can be enhanced by experience, explaining why novel architectural styles may not be liked initially but 'grow on' people over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p219]
- Reber et al. showed that positive emotional responses (measured by zygomaticus major muscle activity) increase before conscious judgment is made, demonstrating pre-conscious positive priming. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p239]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that fluency effects occur pre-consciously suggests beauty judgments have both visceral (automatic) and reflective (conscious) components, aligning with Donald Norman's three levels of beauty.
