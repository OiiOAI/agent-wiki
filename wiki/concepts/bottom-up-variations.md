# Bottom-Up Variations

## Summary

Political volatility that occurs at the local municipal level—petty fights, frictions, and negotiations in running regular affairs. Unlike scalable systems, bottom-up variations remain invariant under scale transformation: multiplying community size by 100 produces markedly different dynamics. Taleb argues that collections of municipalities with provincial enmities aggregate to benign, stable states (exemplified by Switzerland), while large centralized states become fragile.

## Key facts

- Bottom-up variations are political volatility at the municipal level—not scalable (invariant under scale transformation)—so a large state does not behave like a gigantic municipality. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p123]
- Switzerland achieves stability through variations and noise at the local level, just as a cab driver's income shows daily instability but annual stability. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p123]
- On the small local scale, biological responses (shame, eye contact) direct people to avoid causing harm; at large scale, others are abstract items and civil servants' brains lead with spreadsheets rather than emotions. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p123]
- Stalin could not have existed in a municipality—pointing to how scale changes the quality of relationships and accountability. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p124]
- The small (in the aggregate, a collection of small units) is more antifragile than the large; the large is doomed to breaking—a universal mathematical property applying to large corporations, mammals, and administrations. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p124]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bottom-up systems achieve stability through continuous small fluctuations that release pressure gradually, while top-down systems accumulate stress until catastrophic release occurs—explaining why revolutions follow prolonged 'stability'.
