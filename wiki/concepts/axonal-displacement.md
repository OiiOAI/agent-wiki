# Axonal Displacement

## Summary

Axonal displacement is the evolutionary mechanism whereby changes in relative sizes of competing neuronal populations alter the competitive balance for synaptic targets during development. Larger source populations gain a 'voting advantage' in determining which connections persist, causing connections to be displaced from smaller to larger structures. This mechanism explains how quantitative changes in brain size can produce qualitative changes in brain organization without requiring specific genetic rewiring instructions.

## Key facts

- Axonal displacement occurs because the structure sending the greatest number of axons to a target will tend to drive target cell activity more effectively, giving it a competitive advantage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p223]
- Changes in relative sizes of peripheral structures can cascade through many levels of central nervous system organization via displacement effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p225]
- In blind mole rats, reduced visual inputs cause auditory projections to displace visual connections in the thalamus, demonstrating displacement in nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p226]
- Human prefrontal cortex expansion may represent displacement inheritance of space from peripherally constrained structures like motor cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p235]
- The visual cortex appears smaller than predicted in humans because it is appropriately sized for our retina, not because vision became less important. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: Human brain enlargement produced a unique signature of connectional and functional shifts through displacement effects that have no nonhuman primate counterpart.
