# Mind-Body Unity

## Summary

Mind-body unity is Langer's central thesis: the mind and body comprise a single system where every change in a human being is simultaneously a cognitive change (mental) and a hormonal, neural, and/or behavioral change (physical). This challenges the prevailing view of mind-body dualism and suggests that psychology is the most important determinant of health, opening new possibilities for controlling health through mental interventions.

## Key facts

- Langer states that every change in a human being is essentially simultaneously a change at the level of the mind (a cognitive change) as well as the body (a hormonal, neural, and/or behavioral change). [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12]
- She argues that psychology may be the most important determinant of health, and that the goal of her work has been to show the mind is a primary determinant of the body's health and to return control over bodies to individuals. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p13]
- Langer's lab at Harvard focuses on the effects of mind-body unity on health, testing hypotheses such as how thoughts may give people colds, control insulin levels, determine sleep needs, and provide psychological cures for chronic diseases. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12-13]
- Western medicine's dualistic view was strengthened by Descartes and later by Koch's discovery of pathogens causing disease. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p100]
- George Engel's biosocial model recognizes biological, psychological, and social factors interact to cause illness. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p101]
- Israeli scientist Asya Rolls demonstrated that immune responses begin in the brain—inducing abdominal inflammation activated specific neurons, and stimulating those neurons produced the same inflammation. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p102]
- Positive expectations can boost antibacterial and anti-tumor immunity—stimulating pleasure centers in the brain slowed tumor growth. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p102]
- The counterclockwise study (1979) showed elderly men living as if it were 20 years earlier improved vision, joint flexibility, manual dexterity, IQ, gait, and posture. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p103]
- Hotel chambermaids who were told their work was exercise showed significant weight loss, decreased BMI, lower blood pressure, and reduced waist-to-hip ratio compared to controls. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p105]
- A teardrop of joy is biochemically different from a teardrop from cutting an onion. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p102]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since thoughts affect the body simultaneously rather than sequentially, mind-body unity implies that we can make bodily changes by changing our minds without waiting for neuroscientific explanation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neurological 'chain of events' from thought to bodily changes remains unknown.
