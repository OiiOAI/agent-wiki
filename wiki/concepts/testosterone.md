# Testosterone

## Summary

Testosterone is a steroid hormone affecting aggression, dominance, competition, and social behavior. Testosterone levels rise with competition and winning, but also paradoxically support paternal behavior in some contexts. The hormone influences brain regions involved in threat perception and social cognition.

## Key facts

- Testosterone levels rise robustly in 'losers' who nonetheless performed far better than they anticipated, as well as in winners. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p932]
- If testosterone levels don't drop around the time a female gives birth, monogamous male birds and rodents won't act paternally. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p933]
- Fathers have lower testosterone levels than age-matched married men without children, and more involved fathers have lower levels than less involved ones. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p933]
- Conservative individuals are more likely to be skeptical about the validity of someone else's physical pain and more likely to frame it as malingering and dependent manipulation.
