# Periasequeductal Grey

## Summary

The periaqueductal grey (PAG) is a small knot of neurons surrounding the central canal of the midbrain that serves as the final assembly point of all the brain's affective circuitry. It is the main output centre for feelings and emotional behaviours, and lesions to it cause 'staring into empty psycho-affective space'—a state resembling the vegetative state. The PAG must 'choose' what to do next by evaluating competing error signals according to ultimate biological imperatives to survive and reproduce.

## Key facts

- The PAG is the final assembly point of all the affect circuits of the brain, located in the midbrain surrounding the aqueduct of Sylvius. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p126]
- Extensive PAG damage causes 'spectacular deterioration of all conscious activities' and failure to exhibit intentional behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p126]
- PAG lesions cause the smallest absolute destruction of brain tissue to impair the foundation of intentionality itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p126]
- The PAG is the main output centre of all affective circuits, channelling information to the musculoskeletal and visceral effectors that generate 'emotion proper'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p127]
- The PAG functions as 'an interface on salient stimuli between the forebrain and the lower brainstem'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p127]
- The PAG provides 'a massive assembly point of the neural systems that generate emotionality'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p127]
- The rear column of the PAG handles active coping strategies—fight-or-flight, increased blood pressure, non-opioid pain relief; this is where FEAR, RAGE and PANIC/GRIEF circuits terminate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p128]
- The front column of the PAG handles passive coping strategies—freezing with hyporeactivity, sick behaviour, decreased blood pressure, opioid pain relief; LUST, CARE and SEEKING circuits terminate here. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p128]
- The PAG must 'choose' what is to be done next by evaluating residual error signals relayed by the affect systems according to biological imperatives to survive and reproduce. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p128]
- The PAG prioritises behavioral options on the basis not only of current needs but also of current opportunities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p129]
- The adjacent superior colliculi help the PAG render verdicts by providing massively compressed and integrated representations of the exteroceptive world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: The PAG, superior colliculi, and midbrain locomotor region together constitute the brain's 'decision triangle' which makes decisions about what to do next based on feedback from affective circuits together with sensory-motor maps [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p130]
