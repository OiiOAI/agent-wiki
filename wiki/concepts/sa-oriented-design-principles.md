# SA-Oriented Design Principles

## Summary

The book presents eight core principles for designing systems that enhance operator situation awareness. These principles range from organizing information around operator goals (Principle 1) to using information filtering carefully (Principle 8). Key principles include presenting Level 2 information directly to support comprehension, providing assistance for Level 3 SA projections, supporting global SA, and making critical cues for schema activation salient. A case study of FAA Maintenance Control Center redesign illustrates these principles in practice.

## Key facts

- Principle 1: Organize information around goals - Information should be organized in terms of the operator's major goals rather than presenting it in a technology-oriented way based on sensors or systems. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p99]
- Principle 2: Present Level 2 information directly - Directly portraying deviation between current and expected values is better than requiring operators to calculate this information from lower level data. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p99]
- Principle 3: Provide assistance for Level 3 SA projections - System-generated support for projecting future events should directly benefit Level 3 SA, particularly for less experienced operators. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p101]
- Principle 4: Support global SA - A frequent problem for SA occurs when attention is directed to a subset of information and other important elements are not attended to; designs should provide the 'big picture' at all times. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p101]
- Principle 7: Take advantage of parallel processing capabilities - People are more able to process visual and auditory information simultaneously, and tactile information can be processed with either modality as they draw on different cognitive resources. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p102]
- Principle 8: Use information filtering carefully - Computer-driven information filtering approaches can seriously degrade SA by depriving operators of the global awareness they need to be predictive and proactive. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p103]

## Inferences

- Inference: The eight principles were developed based on understanding of factors that affect SA in complex systems as discussed in earlier chapters of the book.
- Inference: The MCC case study demonstrates that information filtering is unnecessary when good organization and integration of data is achieved.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific implementation of each principle may vary depending on the domain and operational context.
