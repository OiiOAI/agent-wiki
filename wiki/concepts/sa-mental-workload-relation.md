# SA and Mental Workload Relation

## Summary

The chapter found that SA and mental workload are distinct constructs that do not show a simple correlation. Interface manipulations designed to improve SA did not necessarily reduce mental workload, suggesting these must be assessed as separate measures.

## Key facts

- Interface manipulations were generally successful in improving SA but did not generally reduce mental workload. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p252]
- The theoretical relation between SA and workload is questionable according to Endsley (1993). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p252]
- Fifteen studies measured both SA and workload, providing data for this comparison. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p251]
- Assertions regarding SA and mental workload must be considered separate assertions and must be assessed by appropriate measures. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p252]

## Inferences

- Inference: mproved SA should not be used as evidence of lower (or higher) mental workload without direct measurement of workload.
