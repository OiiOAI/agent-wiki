# Expressing Anger Fully

## Summary

The NVC approach to anger distinguishes between stimulus (others' behavior) and cause (our own judgmental thoughts). Blaming and punishing are superficial expressions of anger; full expression requires connecting with the needs behind the anger. All anger has a life-serving core—it signals an unmet need.

## Key facts

- We are never angry because of what others say or do; the cause of anger lies in our thinking—in thoughts of blame and judgment. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p194]
- When we shine the light of consciousness on our own feelings and needs rather than analyzing wrongness, we do not feel anger. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p195]
- Anger co-opts our energy by directing it toward punishing people rather than meeting our needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p197]
- Use anger as a wake-up call to realize we have a need that isn't being met. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p197]
- Violence comes from the belief that other people cause our pain and therefore deserve punishment. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p199]
- The four steps to expressing anger are: stop and breathe, identify our judgmental thoughts, connect with our needs, and express our feelings and unmet needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p209]

## Inferences

- Inference: The process emphasizes that before expressing anger, we may need to first empathize with the other person so they can hear us.
