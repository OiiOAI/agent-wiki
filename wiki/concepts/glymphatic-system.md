# Glymphatic System

## Summary

The glymphatic system is a recently discovered waste-clearance system in the brain that pushes cerebrospinal fluid through brain tissue during sleep, providing a nightly cleansing 'power-wash.' This system is particularly active during deep, slow-wave sleep and helps clear amyloid proteins and other metabolic waste products. Poor sleep negatively affects glymphatic activity, linking sleep quality to Alzheimer's disease risk.

## Key facts

- The glymphatic system forcefully pushes cerebrospinal fluid through the brain during sleep, providing a power-wash for the brain every night. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p317]
- During sleep, the channels of the glymphatic system swell up to 60 percent while neurons themselves shrink to make way for cleansing fluid. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p318]
- The glymphatic system is particularly active during the deep, slow-wave phase of sleep. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p318]
- The glymphatic system helps dispose of amyloid proteins that can accumulate and form plaques characteristic of Alzheimer's disease. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p318]
- Poor sleep is associated with greater amounts of amyloid plaque in the brain. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p318]

## Inferences

- Inference: Optimizing sleep duration and quality may directly support the brain's natural waste-clearing mechanisms and potentially reduce Alzheimer's disease risk.
