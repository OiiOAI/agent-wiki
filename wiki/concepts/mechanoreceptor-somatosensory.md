# Mechanoreceptor (Somatosensory)

## Summary

Somatosensory mechanoreceptors are specialized sensory receptors in the skin that detect mechanical stimuli including touch, pressure, and vibration. They are classified into two main types based on their rate of adaptation: slowly adapting (SA) receptors that respond throughout sustained stimulation, and rapidly adapting (RA) receptors that respond only to the onset and offset of stimulation. Type 1 fibers have small receptive fields and terminate in superficial receptors, while Type 2 fibers innervate deeper receptors with larger receptive fields.

## Key facts

- SA1 fibers innervate Merkel cells, which are densely clustered in the center of each papillary ridge in glabrous skin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p553]
- RA1 axons typically innervate 10 to 20 Meissner corpuscles, integrating information from several adjacent papillary ridges. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p553]
- Each Meissner corpuscle is innervated by 2 to 5 RA1 axons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p553]
- SA2 fibers innervate Ruffini endings concentrated at the finger and wrist joints and along skin folds in the palm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p554]
- RA2 fibers terminate in Pacinian corpuscles located in subcutaneous tissue, with each RA2 axon terminating without branching in a single Pacinian corpuscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p554]
- Receptive fields on the fingertips are the smallest on the body, averaging 11 mm2 for SA1 fibers and approximately 25 mm2 for RA1 fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p555]
- RA1 fibers are most sensitive between 2 Hz and 50 Hz, and RA2 fibers between 30 Hz and 500 Hz. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p561]

## Inferences

- Inference: The variation in receptive field sizes across body regions reflects the density of mechanoreceptors, with higher receptor density in areas like fingertips resulting in smaller, more precise receptive fields.
