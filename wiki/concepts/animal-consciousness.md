# Animal Consciousness

## Summary

Animal consciousness is difficult to study scientifically because consciousness is an inner experience that cannot be directly observed. While animals clearly have creature consciousness and complex cognition, whether they have mental state consciousness (phenomenal experience) remains unresolved. Claims about animal consciousness often conflate behavioral analogies with humans with evidence of genuine conscious experience. A conservative scientific approach should assume the nonconscious default condition of the nervous system.

## Key facts

- Animals clearly have creature consciousness (they are alive and respond to their environment) and some use complex cognitive operations to guide behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p275]
- Whether animals have mental state consciousness (conscious experiences) cannot be directly determined without language-based verbal report. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p275]
- Studies claiming episodic-like memory in animals (what, where, when information) may represent complex semantic memory rather than genuine episodic memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p276]
- True episodic memory requires not just what, where, and when representations but also a self-concept: knowledge that an event is happening to YOU. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p278]
- The prefrontal cortex of mice and rats is an impoverished version compared to primates, and even nonhuman primates fail to exhibit all characteristics of human cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p277]
- Language changes the information-processing mode and consciousness potential of the brain, enabling symbolic representation and time travel components of autonoetic consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p279]
- The gold standard of consciousness remains verbal self-report; without language, other animals have no way to tell us what they experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p279]
- Scientists should guard against anthropomorphic assumptions about animal consciousness and keep speculations separate from scientifically based observations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p283]
- The default condition of the nervous system should be assumed to be nonconscious (a-noetic) rather than conscious until evidence demonstrates otherwise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p284]
- Treating circuits that nonconsciously control defense responses as if they give rise to conscious feelings of fear in animals misrepresents what is being studied. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p284]
- LeDoux argues mammals and birds have minds (cognitive capacities) but this doesn't mean they have human-type consciousness with language and reflective self-awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p322]
- The lack of language in nonhuman animals makes it very difficult to scientifically know what, if anything, is on their minds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p316]
- Animals can only respond nonverbally, providing no way to distinguish conscious from nonconscious processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p316]
- Dehaene, Lau, and Kouider stated: 'The human brain is the only physical system that unequivocally possesses consciousness.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p321]
- Anthropomorphic attribution to animals may be pre-programmed by natural selection because it helped predict and control animal behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p310]

## Inferences

- Inference: The time travel component of autonoetic consciousness is conferred or facilitated by the past- and future-tense features of language, suggesting this capacity may be uniquely human.
- Inference: Mammals may have the hardware (hippocampus, prefrontal cortex) and software (attention, working memory) for semantic-like memory and noetic consciousness, but whether these are conscious versus nonconscious is difficult to determine.
- Inference: The default scientific position should be that behavior is controlled nonconsciously until proven otherwise, given the difficulty of demonstrating consciousness without verbal report.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether any nonhuman animal has genuine episodic memory and autonoetic consciousness remains scientifically unresolved.
- Uncertain: The possibility that some animals have noetic conscious states (momentary conscious states about facts) without autonoetic states (self-aware experiences) cannot be ruled out but is difficult to demonstrate.
- Uncertain: Whether apes or other primates have any form of autonoetic consciousness remains debated.
