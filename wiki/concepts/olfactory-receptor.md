# Olfactory Receptor

## Summary

Olfactory receptors are G protein-coupled receptors encoded by a large multigene family that initiates the sense of smell. Each olfactory sensory neuron expresses only one type of receptor, and different odorants are detected by unique combinations of receptors, creating a combinatorial code that vastly expands discriminatory capacity.

## Key facts

- Humans have approximately 350 different odorant receptors, whereas mice have approximately 1,000. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p765]
- Olfactory receptors have seven transmembrane domains typical of G protein-coupled receptors, with variable amino acid sequences especially in transmembrane domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p765]
- Each olfactory sensory neuron expresses only one odorant receptor gene, and neurons with the same receptor are randomly scattered within one of several epithelial zones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p767]
- Each receptor recognizes multiple odorants, and each odorant is detected by a specific combination of different receptors, creating a combinatorial coding system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p767]
- Odorant binding activates a signaling cascade involving Gαolf, adenylyl cyclase III, cAMP, and cyclic nucleotide-gated cation channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p766]
