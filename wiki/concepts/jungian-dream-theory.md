# Jungian Dream Theory

## Summary

Carl Jung's approach to dreams differed fundamentally from Freud's. Jung believed dreams played a vital compensatory role in personality development by presenting unconscious material that needed to be recognized and integrated. He also proposed that dreams could emanate from a collective unconscious expressing itself through archetypes.

## Key facts

- Jung believed dreams present the dreamer with unconscious material including overlooked meanings, undrawn conclusions, and unexpressed affects. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p50]
- Jung proposed the concept of a collective unconscious common to all humankind expressing itself through archetypes observable in myths, fairy tales, rituals, and dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p50]
- Jung believed dreams could have an anticipatory or 'prospective' function, presenting probable future situations and challenges. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p50]
- Unlike Freud's emphasis on dreams as pathological and deceitful, Jung focused on dreaming as a wholesome, natural process with creative and transcendent qualities. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p51]
