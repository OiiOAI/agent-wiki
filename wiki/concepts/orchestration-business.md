# Orchestration (Business)

## Summary

Orchestration is the elimination of discretion or choice at the operating level of a business. Once an innovation has been tested and quantified to work better than the previous method, it should be standardized and repeated consistently every time. Without orchestration, nothing can be planned or anticipated, creating chaos rather than order.

## Key facts

- Orchestration is the elimination of discretion, or choice, at the operating level of your business. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p130]
- Theodore Levitt is quoted: 'Discretion is the enemy of order, standardization, and quality.'. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p131]
- The need for Orchestration is based on the absolutely quantifiable certainty that people will do only one thing predictably—be unpredictable. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p132]
- If you haven't orchestrated it, you don't own it. If you don't own it, you can't depend on it. If you can't depend on it, you haven't got a franchise. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p132]
- Orchestration is doing what you do, saying what you say, looking like you look—being how and who you are—for as long as it works, and changing it when it doesn't. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p133]

## Inferences

- Inference: Orchestration applies to everything from greeting scripts to clothing choices, ensuring consistent customer experience.
- Inference: The Business Development Process is dynamic—once orchestrated, processes must continue to be innovated, quantified, and re-orchestrated as conditions change.
