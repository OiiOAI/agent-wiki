# Dual Reporting

## Summary

A management principle enabling hybrid organizations to function, where employees report to two supervisors: one for mission-oriented priorities and one for functional/technical supervision. This allows knowledge-power people and position-power people to work together effectively. The concept originated from NASA's moon shot coordination efforts.

## Key facts

- Dual reporting enables hybrid organizations to work by allowing employees to have two supervisors: one specifying how the job ought to be done and one monitoring how it is being performed. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1360-L1362]
- The matrix management approach originated from NASA's moon shot, providing means through which various contractors' work could be coordinated. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1350-L1352]
- For dual reporting to work, members must voluntarily surrender individual decision-making to the peer group, depending on trusting the soundness of actions taken by peers. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1370-L1372]
- Trust in dual reporting relates to corporate culture rather than organizational principle—it is a set of values and beliefs about how things are done. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1372-L1374]
- A person can belong to multiple organization charts serving different purposes, like operating the plant on one plane and coordinating across plants on another. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1390-L1392]
