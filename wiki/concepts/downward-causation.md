# Downward Causation

## Summary

Downward causation refers to the influence that emergent global processes exert on their local component dynamics. In complex systems theory, this corresponds to the organizational constraint a system exerts on its components through second-order contextual constraints. Kim's objections to synchronic reflexive downward causation are addressed by noting that it is coherent when conceptualized as global-to-local structuring influence rather than efficient causation. Diachronic downward causation examples Kim provides (rolling wheel, flying bird) fail to capture the nonlinear coupling of self-organizing systems with operational closure.

## Key facts

- Kim argues that 'reflexive downward causation'—the causal influence of a whole on its own microconstituents—is incoherent when understood to happen simultaneously or synchronically. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p414]
- Synchronic reflexive downward causation seems coherent when conceptualized as global-to-local structuring influence rather than efficient causation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p416]
- Kim's examples of diachronic downward causation (rolling wheel, flying bird) do not capture the nonlinear coupling of system components typical of self-organizing systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p417]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between preexisting parts and supervening whole becomes problematic in nondecomposable systems, where components emerge from the whole as much as the whole emerges from the components.
