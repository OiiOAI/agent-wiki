# Cross Bridge Cycle

## Summary

The cross bridge cycle is the mechanism of muscle contraction where myosin heads undergo a sequence of attachment, power stroke, detachment, and reactivation with actin filaments. Each cycle produces a displacement of 5-10 nm and uses ATP, with the cycle continuing as long as calcium and ATP are present.

## Key facts

- The cross bridge cycle is a sequence of detachment, activation, and attachment of myosin heads with actin filaments, where each cycle produces a displacement of approximately 5 to 10 nm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p830]
- The cycle begins with ATP binding to the myosin head, causing detachment from actin, followed by ATP hydrolysis and weak binding to actin; calcium binding to troponin then enables strong attachment and the power stroke. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p830]
- During shortening contractions, myosin heads spend more time near the end of their power stroke where they produce less force, and more time detaching and recocking. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p831]
