# Savant Syndrome

## Summary

A condition where individuals have extraordinary memory or computational abilities but limited reasoning capacity, exemplified by cases like Solomon Shereshevskii and Kim Peek.

## Key facts

- Solomon Shereshevskii could memorize lists of 30-70 numbers effortlessly and repeat them in reverse order years later, but could not understand content or categorize memorized items. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p90]
- Shereshevskii's memory worked through strong synesthesia, where numbers and words triggered visual imagery and multi-sensory associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p91]
- Kim Peek knew zip codes, area codes, and television stations of thousands of US towns, plus vast historical facts, but could not read fiction or grasp imaginative content. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p94]
- Savant abilities resemble computer memory—recording details literally without constructing meaning or understanding. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p95]

## Inferences

- Inference: Savant syndrome illustrates what happens when memory functions without the brain's normal meaning-extraction processes, suggesting that ordinary memory is limited by design to enable understanding.
