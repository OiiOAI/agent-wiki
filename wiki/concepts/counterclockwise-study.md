# Counterclockwise Study

## Summary

The counterclockwise study, conducted by Langer and her Harvard lab over 40 years ago, housed elderly men for one week in a retreat retrofitted to suggest that time had gone backward 20 years. Men were asked to behave as their younger selves, carrying their own luggage up stairs, reading period-appropriate magazines, and watching period-appropriate TV. The results showed measurable improvements in vision, hearing, strength, and even objective appearance, demonstrating mind-body unity.

## Key facts

- Elderly men lived for a week as if they were their younger selves in a retreat retrofitted with magazines, albums, dishes, and VCR tapes from 20 years earlier. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12]
- Men had to carry their own luggage up the front steps even if they had limited mobility, and were asked to behave as younger versions of themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12]
- Results showed that men's vision, hearing, strength, and even objective appearance improved after one week of 'time-machine living.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12-13]
- The findings were so at odds with prevailing views of mind-body dualism that some researchers did not believe them, but Langer says the results elegantly demonstrated mind-body unity. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12-13]
- The men lived in a retreat retrofitted to resemble life 20 years earlier, watching period news broadcasts, TV shows, movies, and listening to era-appropriate jukebox music. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p103]
- The experimental group was asked to talk about events in present tense as if they were happening in real time, while controls discussed the same topics in past tense. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p103]
- Experimental group showed improved vision, joint flexibility, manual dexterity, IQ, gait, and posture, and decreased arthritis symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p103]
- The study was replicated in Italy by Pagnini and Phillips with people living as if it were 1989, again finding improved physical function. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p104]

## Inferences

- Inference: The counterclockwise study provided foundational evidence for mind-body unity, demonstrating that psychological interventions—specifically, changing minds about age—could produce measurable physical changes.
