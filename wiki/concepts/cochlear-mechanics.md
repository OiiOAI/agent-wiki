# Cochlear Mechanics

## Summary

The cochlea performs mechanical frequency analysis through the basilar membrane, which varies in width from approximately 50 micrometers near the base to 500 micrometers near the apex. This creates a gradient of mechanical properties where high-frequency sounds produce maximal membrane motion at the base, and low-frequency sounds produce maximal motion at the apex. The traveling wave on the basilar membrane distributes the energies associated with different frequency components along its length.

## Key facts

- The basilar membrane at the base of the human cochlea is less than one-fifth as broad as at the apex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p650]
- The basilar membrane at the base responds best to the highest audible frequencies, approximately 20 kHz in humans, while the apex responds to frequencies as low as 20 Hz. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p650]
- The arrangement of vibration frequencies along the basilar membrane is an example of a tonotopic map. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p651]
- Each cycle of a sound stimulus evokes a cycle of up-and-down movement of liquid in the cochlea's three chambers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p648]
- The basilar membrane acts as a mechanical frequency analyzer by distributing the energies associated with different frequency components of a stimulus to hair cells arrayed along its length. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p651]
