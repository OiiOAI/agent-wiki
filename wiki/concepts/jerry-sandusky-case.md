# The Jerry Sandusky Case

## Summary

The Jerry Sandusky case involved serial child sexual abuse by former Penn State assistant football coach Jerry Sandusky, discovered in 2001 when graduate assistant Mike McQueary reported seeing Sandusky in a shower with a boy. Gladwell notes the case is 'weird' with significant ambiguity: skeptics like John Ziegler and author Mark Pendergrast argue that some victims may have been motivated by financial settlements and that memory issues affect credibility. Gladwell states he has 'no idea' about Sandusky's guilt but focuses on how the case illustrates problems with default to truth when dealing with strangers.

## Key facts

- John Ziegler argues there was at least a five-week lag between McQueary spotting Sandusky in the shower and his telling Penn State leadership, suggesting McQueary had doubts about what he saw. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p207]
- Ziegler claims that at least some of Sandusky's victims are not credible and may have been attracted by large cash settlements Penn State was offering. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p207]
- Mark Pendergrast argues the case was a 'classic example of a moral panic' and the 'frailty of human memory,' with memories of abuse 'retrieved through therapy and litigation.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p207]
- Elizabeth Loftus, a leading expert on memory, wrote that it is 'amazing that no one else has noticed or written about so many of these things, including all the 'memories' that were retrieved through therapy and litigation.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p207]
- Gladwell states: 'I have no idea' about Sandusky's guilt, noting that the case involves 'a morass of conflicting evidence and speculation and ambiguity.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p207]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gladwell's interest in the case is not Sandusky's guilt per se, but how the case illustrates the danger of 'default to truth'—the tendency to assume strangers are honest—which may have led Penn State officials to dismiss or downplay the initial report.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the conflicting evidence and memory issues in the case ultimately support or undermine the conviction, as Gladwell explicitly declines to adjudicate this question.
