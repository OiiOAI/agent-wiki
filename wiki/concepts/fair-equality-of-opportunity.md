# Fair Equality of Opportunity

## Summary

The principle requiring that offices and positions be open to all under conditions of fair equality of opportunity. It must not be confused with careers open to talents. The reasons for requiring open positions are not solely or even primarily those of efficiency; rather, excluding persons from positions would mean they are unjustly treated even if they benefit from the greater efforts of those allowed to hold them. The principle works together with the difference principle to prevent a meritocratic society.

## Key facts

- The principle of fair equality of opportunity must not be confused with the notion of careers open to talents; its consequences are quite distinct from the liberal interpretation of the two principles taken together. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p86]
- If some places were not open on a basis fair to all, those kept out would be right in feeling unjustly treated even though they benefited from greater efforts of those allowed to hold them. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p86]
- The principle of open positions expresses the conviction that exclusion debars persons from experiencing the realization of self which comes from a skillful and devoted exercise of social duties — depriving them of one of the main forms of human good. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p87]
- The role of the principle of fair opportunity is to insure that the system of cooperation is one of pure procedural justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p90]
- The two principles of justice will not lead to a meritocratic society because the difference principle transforms the aims of society in fundamental respects. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p106]

## Inferences

- Inference: The combination of fair equality of opportunity with the difference principle prevents equality of opportunity from becoming merely a way of releasing men's energies in the pursuit of prosperity while allowing marked disparity between classes.
