# H.M. (Henry Molaison)

## Summary

The most famous amnesic patient in neuroscience history, whose bilateral hippocampus removal in 1953 revealed the critical role of the hippocampus in forming new declarative memories.

## Key facts

- H.M. developed severe amnesia after surgeon William Scoville removed his hippocampus and adjacent structures from both hemispheres in 1953 to treat epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p120]
- After surgery, H.M. could not form new long-term memories, estimating the date as 1953 (two years behind) and his age as 27 (actually 29). [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p121]
- H.M. could still learn motor skills (mirror tracing) without remembering having learned them, demonstrating intact procedural memory despite absent declarative memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p122]
- Brenda Milner's experiments with H.M. revealed the existence of procedural memory independent of the hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p122]
