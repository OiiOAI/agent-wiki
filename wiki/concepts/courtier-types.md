# Courtier Types

## Summary

Seven types of courtiers operate within group courts, each with distinct strategies and dangers: The Intriguer (appears loyal but secretly amasses power with disdain for the boss), The Stirrer (riddled with insecurities, spreads anxiety and rumors), The Gatekeeper (monopolizes access to leaders through adoration), The Shadow Enabler (detects and activates leaders' repressed desires), The Court Jester (uses humor to avoid responsibility), The Mirrorer (reflects back people's moods and ideas to charm them), and The Favorite/Punching Bag (occupying highest and lowest court positions).

## Key facts

- The Intriguer seems intensely loyal and ruthlessly efficient but wears a mask, with disdain for the boss and continual maneuvering to amass more power. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p519]
- Alexander Haig exemplified the Intriguer type, rising by tailoring himself to Henry Kissinger's desires while secretly contemptuous, eventually becoming Nixon's chief of staff. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p519]
- The Stirrer feels deep wells of resentment and envy, infecting the group with doubts and anxieties, stirring up trouble to be at the center of action. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p521]
- The Gatekeeper's goal is gaining exclusive access to leaders by monopolizing information flow, motivated by intense adoration rather than power hunger. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p522]
- The Shadow Enabler detects leaders' repressed desires (such as paranoia or craving for revenge) and provides complicit partners to help bring the Shadow out. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p523]
- Charles Colson exemplified the Shadow Enabler, playing on Nixon's paranoid tendencies and sadistic desires toward enemies. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p523]
- The Court Jester uses humor to avoid responsibility and failure, with 'rebelliousness' never representing a real threat to the status quo. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p524]
- The Mirrorer type is among the most successful courtiers, masters at reflecting back to people their own moods and ideas, making them feel validated without sensing manipulation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p525]
- Frances Perkins exemplified the Mirrorer in FDR's court, adapting to his moods, presenting ideas as stories, and confirming his idealized self-image. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p525]

## Inferences

- Inference: Recognizing courtier types allows individuals to navigate court dynamics defensively while avoiding manipulation and maintaining strategic distance from dangerous players.
