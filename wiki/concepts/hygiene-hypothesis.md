# Hygiene Hypothesis

## Summary

The hygiene hypothesis proposes that our overly sterile modern lives have reduced exposure to infectious agents (notably those that coevolved with us), which normally protect us from immune-related disorders. Research shows that while exposure to pathogens has decreased, rates of both autoimmune and allergic diseases have increased. A study using public sanitation and access to clean drinking water as metrics revealed a striking linear correlation between countries with greater levels of hygiene and increased incidence of Alzheimer's disease.

## Key facts

- The hygiene hypothesis proposes that the absence of certain pathogens results in weakened immune systems, setting the stage for type 1 diabetes, multiple sclerosis, celiac disease, and others. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p243]
- Research shows that while exposure to pathogens has decreased, rates of both autoimmune and allergic diseases have increased. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p243]
- Using public sanitation and access to clean drinking water as metrics, researchers revealed a striking relationship: countries with greater levels of hygiene had increased incidence of Alzheimer's disease in a perfect linear correlation. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p243]
