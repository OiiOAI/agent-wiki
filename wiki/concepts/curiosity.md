# Curiosity

## Summary

Curiosity is an intrinsic drive to seek information, triggered when we discover a discrepancy between what we know and what we would like to know. Neuroscientist George Loewenstein described this as an 'information gap.' Curiosity feels unpleasant like thirst or hunger and motivates action to reduce the gap. The brain's reward circuitry, particularly dopamine circuits, is involved in curiosity-driven learning.

## Key facts

- Psychologist George Loewenstein argued that curiosity is triggered when discovering a discrepancy between what we know and what we would like to know—a space he called an 'information gap.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p138]
- Loewenstein proposed that curiosity doesn't feel good but is an unpleasant state akin to thirst or hunger that compels action for relief. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p138]
- Matthias Gruber and colleagues found that curiosity improves memory for both mundane and interesting information, and that people willing to forgo external rewards in exchange for information activate dopamine circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p141]
- When a question stimulated curiosity enough to get a shot of dopamine to the hippocampus, people could also take in information they weren't particularly motivated to learn. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p141]
