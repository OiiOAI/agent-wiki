# What's Wrong with Economics

## Summary

Munger's nine-part critique of academic economics, covering: fatal unconnectedness leading to man-with-a-hammer syndrome, failure to follow hard science's full-attribution ethos, physics envy, too much macroeconomics emphasis, insufficient synthesis, extreme psychological ignorance, inadequate attention to second-order effects, insufficient attention to febezzlement, and inadequate attention to virtue and vice effects.

## Key facts

- Munger identifies nine major defects in academic economics, starting with 'fatal unconnectedness' of academic disciplines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p411]
- The second defect is economics failing to follow the fundamental full-attribution ethos of hard science. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p414]
- The third weakness is 'physics envy,' which caused adoption of hard-form efficient market theory. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p415]
- Fourth, there is too much emphasis on macroeconomics and not enough on microeconomics. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p418]
- Fifth, there is too little synthesis both within economics and with matter outside traditional economics. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p423]
- Sixth, economics suffers from extreme and counterproductive psychological ignorance. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p433]
- Seventh, economics gives too little attention to second-order and higher-order effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p435]
- Eighth, economics pays insufficient attention to the concept of febezzlement (functional equivalents of embezzlement). [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p442]
- Ninth, economics gives not enough attention to virtue and vice effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p444]
- Munger tested business school students with a simple question about raising prices increasing volume; only one in fifty could name even one instance. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p424]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Medicare cost forecast error of more than 1,000 percent illustrates the failure to account for second and third-order effects.
- Inference: Munger believes economics professors are 'most economical with ideas' and make a few learned in graduate school last a lifetime.
