# Sex Differences in Brain Organization

## Summary

Male and female brains are organized differently during fetal development through the action of sex hormones. Both brain and body initially follow a female developmental plan. Masculinization of the brain occurs when testosterone is converted to estrogen by aromatase, while masculinization of the body occurs when testosterone is converted to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) by 5-alpha-reductase. These two processes are biochemically separate, meaning brain and body can be organized along different gender lines, explaining conditions such as guevedoces in the Dominican Republic where genetic males lacking 5-alpha-reductase are born with female-appearing bodies but develop male brains and male-typical sexuality at puberty.

## Key facts

- The primordial plan for both female and male fetuses in mammals is initially feminine; maleness arises from fetal testosterone secretion that masculine both brain and body, but these processes can be dissociated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p121]
- Brain masculinization is mediated by estrogen converted from testosterone via aromatase, while body masculinization is mediated by dihydrotestosterone (DHT) converted via 5-alpha-reductase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p122]
- The female fetus is protected from maternal estrogens by alpha-fetoprotein, which neutralizes estrogen effects during development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p121]
- Guevedoces in the Dominican Republic lack 5-alpha-reductase, so their bodies cannot convert testosterone to DHT; they are born appearing female but develop male-typical bodies and sexual urges at puberty when testosterone rises. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p123]
- Males have more extensive vasopressinergic circuits while females have more extensive oxytocin circuits in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p109]
- Damage to the preoptic area has more deleterious effects on male sexual behavior than on female behavior, while ventromedial hypothalamic damage compromises female urges more than male. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p121]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because brain and body masculinization are biochemically separate processes, genetic males can have female-typical brains and genetic females can have male-typical brains.
- Inference: Human females exhibit 'concealed estrus' with no obvious advertisement of ovulation, suggesting human sexuality has become strongly dissociated from immediate reproductive concerns and aligned with social bonding.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neuroanatomical details of how fetal hormone exposure translates into adult behavioral differences in humans remain under investigation.
