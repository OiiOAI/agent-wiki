# Tonotopic Organization

## Summary

Tonotopic organization is the systematic arrangement of neurons according to their characteristic frequencies along a spatial gradient. This organization is established in the cochlea based on the mechanical properties of the basilar membrane and is maintained throughout the auditory pathway, appearing in the cochlear nuclei, superior olivary complex, inferior colliculus, and auditory cortex.

## Key facts

- Cochlear nerve fibers terminate in the cochlear nuclei in a tonotopic organization: those encoding low frequencies terminate ventrally, those encoding higher frequencies terminate dorsally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p738]
- Each afferent fiber enters at the nerve root and splits into branches that run anteriorly and posteriorly, imposing a tonotopic map on each subdivision of the ventral cochlear nucleus and on the dorsal cochlear nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p738]
- The central nucleus of the inferior colliculus is tonotopically organized with low frequencies represented dorsolaterally and high frequencies ventromedially. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p745]
- The primary auditory cortex contains a tonotopic representation of characteristic frequencies with neurons tuned to low frequencies at the rostral end and those responsive to high frequencies in the caudal region. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p751]
