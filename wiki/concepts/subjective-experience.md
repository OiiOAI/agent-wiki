# Subjective Experience

## Summary

Subjective experience refers to the 'what it's like' quality of mental states—the fact that life feels like something to a conscious being. The book explores how subjective experience evolved, distinguishing between simple sentience (basic feelings like pain and pleasure) and complex consciousness (unified, integrated experience). The author argues for a transformation view: basic subjective experience may have originated earlier than latecomer views suggest, but was transformed by later evolutionary developments.

## Key facts

- Subjective experience is the most basic phenomenon requiring explanation—the fact that life feels like something to us. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p65]
- The author distinguishes subjective experience (sentience) as a broad category from consciousness as a narrower category within it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p75]
- Bacteria sense the world and act, raising the question of whether subjective experience might extend further than assumed, requiring a boundary to be drawn. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p65]
- The feedback loop between sensing and acting affects how sensory input feels—this is demonstrated by tactile vision substitution systems where users experience visual objects only when they can control the camera. ## Inferences

- Inference: The author argues for a 'transformation view' in which basic subjective experience (pain, thirst, primordial emotions) may have preceded late-arising complex features like working memory and sensory integration.
- Inference: abs, octopuses, and cats all have subjective experience, then consciousness arose at least three separate times in evolution.
