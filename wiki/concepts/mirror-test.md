# Mirror test

## Summary

A test for animal self-awareness devised by Gordon Gallup in 1970. After animals become familiar with mirrors, red dye is applied to parts of their bodies they cannot see directly while asleep. Animals that recognize themselves will repeatedly touch the colored parts. Only a few animals pass, including higher primates, dolphins, and elephants. Humans begin to pass the test at 18 months to 2 years of age.

## Key facts

- The mirror test involves putting an animal briefly to sleep and coloring parts of its eyebrows and ears with red dye; upon waking and seeing its reflection, animals that recognize themselves repeatedly touch the colored parts. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p149]
- The mirror test is passed by higher primates (chimpanzees, gorillas, and orangutans), dolphins, and elephants. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p149]
- Humans begin to recognize themselves in mirrors at between eighteen months and two years of age. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p149]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Failing the mirror test does not disprove self-awareness—an animal might not react to a mark for many reasons, including poor eyesight or lack of interest.
