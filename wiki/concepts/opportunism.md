# Opportunism

## Summary

In transaction cost economics, opportunism refers to the tendency of some economic agents to behave in self-interested ways, potentially through deception or guile, when formal contracts cannot fully specify behaviors. Together with bounded rationality, it explains why certain governance structures emerge to manage contractual relationships.

## Key facts

- Opportunism is defined as a proclivity for at least some economic agents to behave unreliably and engage in self-interested behavior when opportunities arise. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p423]
- Williamson stated that contracting would be feasible if economic agents were completely trustworthy, implying that opportunism creates the need for complex governance structures. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p423]
