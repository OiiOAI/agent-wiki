# Margin of Safety

## Summary

Margin of safety is a value investing principle introduced by Benjamin Graham requiring that investments be purchased at a significant discount to intrinsic value, providing a cushion against errors in calculation or adverse developments.

## Key facts

- Graham's approach involved finding stocks selling at one-third or less of sellout value, providing huge margin of safety. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p191]
- Even with an elderly alcoholic running a stodgy business, a significant excess of real value per share means all kinds of good things can happen. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p191]
- The margin of safety concept was inspired by engineering quality control using elementary mathematics to determine acceptable risk levels. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p161]
- Munger states: 'A great business at a fair price is superior to a fair business at a great price'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p75]
- Buffett states: 'All intelligent investing is value investing—acquiring more than you are paying for'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p79]
- All investment evaluations should begin by measuring risk, especially reputational risk, and incorporate an appropriate margin of safety. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p80]
- Munger advocates avoiding big mistakes and shunning permanent capital loss. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p81]
- Graham advocated buying stocks where the stock price times shares was one-third or less of sellout value, providing a significant excess of real value per share working for you. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p202]
- Graham operated during the 1930s when people were so shell-shocked that obvious bargains existed below working capital per share. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p202]
- Modern accounting is not realistic for margin of safety calculations because when businesses contract, significant assets disappear due to employee obligations. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p203]
- The best part of Graham's system was the concept of 'Mr. Market'—a manic-depressive who offers prices sometimes far below and sometimes far above value, giving investors options. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger and Buffett evolved beyond classic Graham margin of safety by finding 'better businesses'—companies with strong competitive positions that provide qualitative margin of safety even at higher apparent prices.
