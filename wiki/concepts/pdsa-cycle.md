# PDSA Cycle

## Summary

The PDSA Cycle (Plan-Do-Study-Act) is a scientific method for learning and improvement of a product or process, originally developed by Walter Shewhart in 1939 and evolved by W. Edwards Deming from 1986 to 1993. Unlike the Japanese PDCA, PDSA emphasizes learning through prediction and testing hypotheses before implementation.

## Key facts

- Deming reintroduced the Shewhart cycle in 1986, stating it came directly from the 1950 version. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p6]
- In 1993, Deming modified the cycle and called it the 'Shewhart Cycle for Learning and Improvement—the PDSA Cycle,' describing it as a flow diagram for learning and improvement of a product or process. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]
- Deming frequently warned audiences that the Plan-Do-Check-Act version is inaccurate because the English word 'check' means 'to hold back'. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p6]
- In a 1990 letter to Moen, Deming stated to 'be sure to call it PDSA, not the corruption PDCA'. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]
- PDSA is used for testing and implementing changes. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]
- Deming stated in 1980 that the QC Circle Plan-Do-Check-Act 'bears no relation' to his Deming Circle. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]

## Inferences

- Inference: The change from 'Check' to 'Study' in PDSA reflects Deming's emphasis on learning and understanding causes rather than merely verifying results.
- Inference: PDSA is intended for experimentation and learning, while PDCA is intended for implementation and compliance.
