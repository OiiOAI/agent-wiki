# Continuity hypothesis of dreaming

## Summary

The continuity hypothesis holds that there is congruence and continuity between our waking and dreaming lives—that dreams draw upon recent waking experiences, concerns, and emotions. Mary Whiton Calkins first articulated this in 1893, finding that nine out of ten dream reports showed evident connections to elements from the dreamer's waking life.

## Key facts

- Mary Whiton Calkins found that in nine out of ten reports, participants identified connections between their dreams and elements from their waking life. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p33]
- Calkins concluded there is congruence and continuity between waking and dreaming life. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p33]
- The continuity hypothesis remains one of the most widely studied models of dream content. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p33]

## Inferences

- Inference: The continuity hypothesis provides a framework for understanding why dream content reflects waking concerns rather than random stimuli.
