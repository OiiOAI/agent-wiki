# Action Bias

## Summary

Action bias is the tendency to take action even when inaction would be better, especially in ambiguous situations. Goalkeepers dive left or right rather than staying center despite one-third of penalty kicks going down the middle. The bias is accentuated when a situation is new or unclear.

## Key facts

- Soccer penalty kicks go one third of the time to the middle, left, and right of the goal, yet goalkeepers almost never stay standing in the middle. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p103]
- Michael Bar-Eli's study of hundreds of penalty shoot-outs showed goalkeepers dive to the wrong side because it looks more impressive and feels less embarrassing than freezing on the spot. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p103]
- Charlie Munger sums up his investing approach: 'We've got discipline in avoiding just doing any damn thing just because you can't stand inactivity.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p103]
- The action bias exists because in our hunter-gatherer environment, action trumped reflection; it was better to run away once too often than to deliberate and die. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p104]
