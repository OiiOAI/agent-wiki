# Hedonism as a Method of Choice

## Summary

Rawls interprets hedonism not as the claim that pleasure is the sole intrinsic good or that humans only strive for pleasure, but as an attempt to carry through the dominant-end conception of deliberation—to show how rational choice is always possible. The hedonist argues that pleasure, conceived as agreeable feeling, is the only plausible candidate for a dominant end because it is identifiable by introspection and provides a common unit of account. However, Rawls argues hedonism fails: once pleasure is conceived sufficiently definitely for calculation, it is no longer plausible as the sole rational aim; different sorts of agreeable feelings are themselves incomparable; and the problem of plurality of ends arises again within subjective feelings.

## Key facts

- Hedonism as a method of choice tries to carry through the dominant-end conception by arguing that pleasure is the dominant end identifiable by introspection. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p490]
- Once pleasure is conceived in a sufficiently definite way so that its intensity and duration can enter calculations, it is no longer plausible as the sole rational aim. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p492]
- There are different sorts of agreeable feelings themselves incomparable, as well as the quantitative dimensions of intensity and duration, raising the question of how to balance them when they conflict. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p492]
- It is antecedently unlikely that certain kinds of agreeable feeling can define a unit of account the use of which explains the possibility of rational deliberation. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p493]
- The tendency toward hedonism reflects the impossibility of defining an appropriate definite end to be maximized, suggesting that teleological doctrines are radically misconceived. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p494]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rawls suggests that the structural failure of hedonism indicates that teleological theories relate the right and the good in the wrong way, and that the right should be prior to the good.
