# Electromyography

## Summary

Electromyography (EMG) is a clinical diagnostic procedure in which a needle electrode inserted into muscle records extracellular electrical activity of motor units. Key measurements include spontaneous activity at rest, number of motor units under voluntary control, and the duration and amplitude of motor unit action potentials. EMG patterns differ between neurogenic diseases (large, prolonged potentials with reduced recruitment) and myopathies (small, brief potentials with preserved recruitment).

## Key facts

- EMG records extracellular electrical activity from motor units using a needle electrode inserted into muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- In neurogenic disease, EMG shows fibrillations, fasciculations, and giant motor unit potentials with reduced interference pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- In myopathic disease, EMG shows small polyphasic motor unit potentials with full interference pattern but reduced amplitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Fibrillation potentials are spontaneous low-amplitude potentials from single denervated muscle fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Fasciculations are involuntary synchronous contractions of all muscle fibers in a motor unit, visible as flickers under the skin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
