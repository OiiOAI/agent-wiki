# Moral Sentiments

## Summary

Rawls uses 'sentiment' for permanent ordered families of governing dispositions, such as the sense of justice and the love of mankind, and for lasting attachments to particular individuals or associations. Moral sentiments require certain types of explanations that invoke moral concepts and their associated principles.

## Key facts

- A necessary feature of moral feelings, distinguishing them from natural attitudes, is that the person's explanation of his experience invokes a moral concept and its associated principles. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p426]
- Guilt and shame reflect the concern with others and with one's person that must be present in all moral conduct; guilt invokes the concept of right, whereas shame appeals to the concept of goodness. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p429]
- The moral sentiments are a normal feature of human life; we could not do away with them without at the same time eliminating certain natural attitudes such as love and trust, friendship and affection. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p432]
