# JACA Framework

## Summary

A four-element model for predicting violence developed by Gavin de Becker. JACA evaluates Perceived Justification, Perceived Alternatives, Perceived Consequences, and Perceived Ability to determine the likelihood that a person will commit violent acts.

## Key facts

- Justification: Does the person feel justified in using violence? This can range from simple provocation to elaborate manufacturing of justification. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p67]
- Alternatives: Does the person perceive available alternatives to violence that will move them toward their desired outcome? Violence is most likely when no alternatives are perceived. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p67]
- Consequences: How does the person view the consequences of using violence? Violence becomes likely when consequences are perceived as favorable or tolerable. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p68]
- Ability: Does the person believe they can successfully deliver the violence? People with weapons or past success using violence have higher ability appraisals. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p68]
- JACA can be applied to governments as well as individuals—for example, in analyzing why nations go to war. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p68]

## Inferences

- Inference: The JACA framework is applicable across different scales of violence prediction, from individual interpersonal violence to large-scale political violence.
