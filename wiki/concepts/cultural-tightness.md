# Cultural Tightness

## Summary

Cultural tightness refers to societies characterized by autocracy, suppression of dissent, and omnipresence and enforcement of behavior norms. Research shows that tighter cultures correlate with higher population density, more historical food shortages, lower food intake, lower protein and fat levels, environmental degradation, less available farmland or clean water, more pollution, and higher historical incidence of pandemics and communicable diseases.

## Key facts

- Tighter cultures are characterized by autocracy, suppression of dissent, and omnipresence and enforcement of behavior norms. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p312]
- Cultural tightness is predicted by more historical food shortages, lower food intake, and lower levels of protein and fat in the diet. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p312]
- Cultural tightness is predicted by environmental degradation, including less available farmland or clean water and more pollution. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p312]
- Other predictors of cultural tightness include high historical incidence of pandemics, high infant and child mortality rates, and higher cumulative average number of years lost to communicable disease. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p312]
- Cultural tightness was also predicted by a history of floods, droughts, and cyclones. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p313]
