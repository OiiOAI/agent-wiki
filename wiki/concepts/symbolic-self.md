# Symbolic Self

## Summary

Deacon's concept that the self which experiences intentionality, makes moral judgments, and fears death is fundamentally symbolic in nature. This self emerges from symbolic representation processes that allow for self-reflection, perspective-taking, and representation of alternative pasts and futures. It is 'virtual' rather than 'actual' in its mode of reference, existing in a web of relationships extended across individuals and time. This concept resolves Cartesian intuitions about disembodied souls by showing that while the symbolic self is indeed not bounded within a single mind or body, it is not independent of all embodiment—only of any particular embodiment.

## Key facts

- Self-representation in the context of representations of alternative pasts and futures cannot be attained without symbolic representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p486]
- The symbolic self is the locus of accountability in social agreements, the source of empathy, and the origin of rational reflective intentions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p486]
- Deacon cites Vygotsky's observation that this sense of self emerges slowly in children as they mature, gaining perspective-shifting ability and control over nonsocial aspects of self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p487]
- Consciousness of self implicitly includes consciousness of other selves, which can only be represented through virtual reference created by symbols. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p488]
- Deacon describes this as the 'final irony' that virtual, not actual, reference gives rise to the experience of self—'this most undeniably real experience is a virtual reality'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p488]
- The symbolic self is not bounded within a mind or body and derives its existence from outside—from other minds and other times. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p489]
- Deacon connects this to Descartes' insight that only humans have a soul deriving from a realm 'of the nature of language, pure mathematics, or geometry,' suggesting a partial validation of rationalist intuition. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p489]
- The symbolic self is capable of 'true transmigration' though not necessarily as a unified whole, becoming available for regrounding in others' subjective experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p489]

## Inferences

- Inference: The symbolic self's dependence on social and temporal webs suggests that personal identity is inherently intersubjective in a fundamental sense.
- Inference: Deacon implies that the common intuition of free will and agency corresponds to the genuinely emergent properties of symbolic self-representation rather than being an illusion.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanism by which symbolic representation generates the subjective experience of unified selfhood remains unclear.
