# Neural Complexity

## Summary

The human brain's immense neural complexity is presented as the principal reason why neuroscience has not yet achieved comprehensive explanations of mind. Damasio cites statistics on neuron count, synapses, and axonal length to illustrate why understanding brain-mind relationships requires studying interactions across multiple levels rather than individual neurons or local circuits.

## Key facts

- The human brain contains approximately 86 billion neurons in neural circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p279]
- The number of synapses formed among neurons is at least 10 trillion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p279]
- The length of axon cables forming neuron circuits totals approximately several hundred thousand miles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p279]
- Neural firing occurs on a time scale of tens of milliseconds, producing millions of firing patterns per second across diverse distributed brain circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p279]
- Understanding brain-mind relationships requires studying interactions of firing patterns generated by many neuron circuits locally and globally, rather than individual neurons or isolated circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p280]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The neural statistics are described as informal estimates from Charles Stevens of the Salk Institute rather than precise measurements.
