# Attentional Blink

## Summary

The attentional blink is a phenomenon where a second target stimulus becomes invisible when presented within about half a second after the first. It illustrates the temporal limitations of conscious perception: while committing the first letter to memory, consciousness temporarily 'blinks' and fails to perceive subsequent stimuli. This demonstrates that conscious access imposes a narrow bottleneck, and that all other stimuli must wait in an unconscious buffer during this processing period.

## Key facts

- The attentional blink occurs when a second letter is presented within about 500 milliseconds of the first, causing it to be missed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p48]
- During the attentional blink, unconscious stimuli still reach early visual areas and may proceed deeply into the visual system to be classified as targets. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p48]
- The attentional blink and psychological refractory period are related phenomena: conscious perception of the second item is delayed, and the probability of complete blackout increases with delay duration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p49]

## Inferences

- Inference: The attentional blink demonstrates that conscious access requires a 'registering' process that is tightly limited to one chunk of information at a time. [Supporting facts: p48]
