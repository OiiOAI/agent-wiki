# Daydream

## Summary

A daydream (Tagtraum) is a product of the imagination consisting of scenes and events in which the subject's egoistic needs of ambition and power or erotic wishes find satisfaction. Unlike nocturnal dreams, daydreams are recognized as phantasies rather than experienced as hallucinations. They appear in the prepubertal period and persist until maturity. Daydreams share with nocturnal dreams the characteristic of wish fulfillment, and creative writers transform their daydreams into literary works.

## Key facts

- Daydreams are phantasies observable in healthy as well as sick people, appearing in the prepubertal period. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4877]
- The content of daydreams is dominated by ambitions, power, and erotic wishes; in young men the ambitious phantasies are most prominent, in women the erotic ones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4877]
- The creative writer uses his daydreams, with certain remodellings, disguises and omissions, to construct situations for his short stories, novels or plays. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4878]
- The relationship between dreams and daydreams was suggested as a possible clue to understanding the nature of dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4877]
