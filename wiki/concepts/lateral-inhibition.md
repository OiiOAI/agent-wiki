# Lateral Inhibition

## Summary

Lateral inhibition is a neural circuit mechanism where an activated neuron sends inhibitory projections to neighboring neurons, sharpening spatial signals. It is ubiquitous in sensory systems, allowing clear localization of stimuli.

## Key facts

- In lateral inhibition, an activated neuron C sends collateral inhibitory projections to neighboring neurons A and E, causing them to become silent while C fires strongly. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p713]
- Lateral inhibition sharpens spatial signals in sensory systems, such as distinguishing which photoreceptor was stimulated by light. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p714]
- Lateral inhibition also functions in tactile systems, allowing precise localization of skin touch, and in auditory systems for pitch discrimination. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p714]
