# Size-Weight Illusion

## Summary

The size-weight illusion is a perceptual phenomenon where people report that smaller objects feel heavier than larger objects of equal weight. Despite this persistent conscious perception, the sensorimotor system rapidly learns to scale grip and load forces appropriately for the true object weights. This dissociation between perception and action supports the existence of distinct neural pathways for visual perception and visual guidance of action.

## Key facts

- When lifting two objects of different size but equal weight, people report that the smaller object feels heavier despite knowing they are the same weight. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- The size-weight illusion does not weaken with repeated lifting or when subjects are informed that the objects are of equal weight. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- After alternating between lifting large and small equal-weight objects, subjects rapidly learn to scale grip and load forces appropriately for true weight while the perceptual illusion persists. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p725]
- This dissociation demonstrates that the sensorimotor system can operate independently of high-level cognitive centers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p725]
