# Life

## Summary

Life is defined not as a state but as a process—the ongoing activity of maintaining organization against thermodynamic equilibrium. Living things are self-sustaining systems that work to keep themselves organized. The individual atoms and molecules making up a living organism are constantly turned over and exchanged with the environment, but the pattern persists through time.

## Key facts

- Life is not a state but a process—living is an activity that organisms are doing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p26]
- The difference between living and non-living is like the difference between a storm and a calm—life is ongoing dynamic organization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p26]
- The stability of life comes from constant flux—the individual atoms are constantly turning over but the network of chemical reactions is maintained. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p27]
- Cells are self-sustaining as a whole integrated system with enzymes tuned to collectively maintain relations between all elements in the dynamic network. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p27]
- Life is characterized by agency—organisms do things, for reasons, as causal agents in their own right. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p11]
