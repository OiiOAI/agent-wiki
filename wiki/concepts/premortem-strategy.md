# Premortem Strategy

## Summary

The premortem strategy is a mental simulation technique developed by Klein and colleagues to help planners identify potential flaws in their plans before implementation. Planners imagine that months into the future, their plan has failed, and must explain why.

## Key facts

- The premortem strategy asks planners to imagine that their plan has been carried out and has failed, then explain why they think it failed. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p101]
- The exercise takes less than ten minutes to get people to imagine the failure and its most likely causes, with discussion that can continue for an hour. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p102]
- The premortem breaks emotional attachment to the plan's success by challenging planners to demonstrate creativity and competence in identifying likely sources of breakdown. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p101]
- Mitchell, Russo, and Pennington (1989) studied this process as 'prospective hindsight,' finding that people generate more reasons for an event if framed as one that had already occurred. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p105]

## Inferences

- Inference: The premortem technique is complementary to the 'crystal ball' method developed by Cohen, Freeman, and Thompson (1998), as both aim to help people become sensitive to alternate interpretations.
