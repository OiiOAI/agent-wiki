# Epistemic Actions

## Summary

Epistemic actions are actions selected to improve knowledge or information rather than to directly achieve practical goals. In predictive processing terms, both practical and epistemic actions flow from the attempt to minimize expected future prediction error relative to goals.

## Key facts

- Epistemic actions are chosen not because they are of intrinsic value or move us physically closer to a goal, but because they improve the information stream available to the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p150]
- Practical and epistemic actions are determined in exactly the same way, as the predictive brain makes counterfactual predictions about what kinds of futures will result if certain actions are launched. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p154]
- Expert Tetris players sometimes rotate descending shapes to aid identification rather than to fit them into a waiting slot. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p151]
- Expert bartenders use distinctively shaped glasses as environmental cues to help recall and sequence specific drink orders. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p150]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between epistemic and practical components becomes fuzzy in many cases, potentially vanishing entirely in well-practiced routines [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p151]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural mechanisms by which the brain selects the optimal mix of epistemic actions remain to be fully characterized
