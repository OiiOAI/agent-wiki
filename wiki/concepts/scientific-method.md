# Scientific Method

## Summary

The book examines scientific method as both a practical tool for motorcycle maintenance and as a philosophical framework. The narrator describes formal scientific method as involving six categories: statement of the problem, hypotheses, experiments, predicted results, observed results, and conclusions. He notes that scientific method is ultimately about preventing self-deception—ensuring nature hasn't misled us into thinking we know something we don't.

## Key facts

- Scientific method has six categories: (1) statement of the problem, (2) hypotheses as to the cause of the problem, (3) experiments designed to test each hypothesis, (4) predicted results of the experiments, (5) observed results of the experiments and (6) conclusions from the results. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p89]
- The real purpose of scientific method is to make sure Nature hasn't misled you into thinking you know something you don't actually know. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p89]
- Two kinds of logic are used: inductive inferences start with observations and arrive at general conclusions; deductive inferences start with general knowledge and predict a specific observation. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p89]
- The formation of hypotheses is the most mysterious of all the categories of scientific method, as their source is unknown before testing. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p94]
- Galileo Galilei (1564-1642) is considered by many to be the father of modern science, combining designed experiments and mathematics. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p2]
- Francis Bacon (1561-1626) insisted that scientists proceed through inductive reasoning, from observations to axiom to law. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p2]
- Charles Peirce and William James formed the 'Metaphysical Club' in 1872, which became linked with the American philosophy of pragmatism. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p2]
- John Dewey (1859-1952) became a leading proponent of pragmatism, influencing philosophy, education, religion, government, and democracy around the world. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p2]
- C.I. Lewis's 1929 book 'Mind and the World Order' had enormous influence on Walter A. Shewhart and W. Edwards Deming in bringing the scientific method to twentieth century industry. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Shewhart stated that specification, production, and inspection correspond respectively to making a hypothesis, carrying out an experiment, and testing the hypothesis. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- The PDCA and PDSA are only related through the scientific method. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p7]
