# Extrinsic vs. Intrinsic Aspirations

## Summary

Research by Tim Kasser and Richard Ryan identified three extrinsic aspirations (wealth, fame, physical attractiveness) and three intrinsic aspirations (meaningful relationships, personal growth, community contributions). Studies spanning hundreds of subjects found that strong extrinsic aspirations relative to intrinsic ones were associated with poorer mental health including narcissism, anxiety, depression, and poorer social functioning. Having strong intrinsic aspirations was positively associated with well-being. Importantly, even believing one would achieve extrinsic goals did not prevent the negative effects—the type of aspirations held was the critical predictor.

## Key facts

- The three extrinsic aspirations are wealth, fame, and physical attractiveness—outcomes that are instrumental for other ends. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p127]
- The three intrinsic aspirations are having satisfying personal relationships, making contributions to the community, and growing as individuals—outcomes that are satisfying in their own right. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p127]
- When any extrinsic aspiration was very high relative to intrinsic ones, individuals displayed poorer mental health including narcissism, anxiety, depression, and poorer social functioning. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p128]
- Even when people believed strongly they would achieve their extrinsic goals, they still displayed poorer psychological health—the type of aspirations held, not just expectations of achieving them, predicted well-being. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p129]
- Mothers who were controlling and cold had children who placed undue weight on extrinsic aspirations, while warm, involved, and autonomy-supportive mothers had children who desired more intrinsic life outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p130]
