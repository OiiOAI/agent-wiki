# Law of Envy

## Summary

The Law of Envy states that envious people focus outside their boundaries onto what others have, neglecting their own responsibilities. Envy is described as a self-perpetuating cycle that guarantees dissatisfaction and keeps people perpetually insatiable.

## Key facts

- Envy is defined as focusing outside one's boundaries onto what others have or accomplish. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p101]
- The authors identify envy as a direct result of the Fall, noting that Satan envied God and tempted Adam and Eve with the same idea. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p101]
- Envy defines 'good' as 'what I do not possess' and hates the good that one has. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p101]
- The solution to envy is to question oneself rather than envy others, asking what one lacks and what one needs to do about it. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p102]
- Galatians 6:4 is cited: 'Each one should test his own actions. Then he can take pride in himself, without comparing himself to somebody else.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p101]
- Envy is a self-perpetuating cycle where boundaryless people feel empty, look at others' fullness, and feel envious, but this time and energy should be spent on taking responsibility. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p102]

## Inferences

- Inference: The authors connect envy to the Fall narrative, suggesting it is fundamental to human brokenness.
