# VO2 Max

## Summary

VO2 max represents the maximum rate at which a person can utilize oxygen during intense exercise, measured in milliliters of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute (ml/kg/min). It is perhaps the single most powerful marker for longevity, with higher values strongly associated with lower all-cause mortality. The average 45-year-old man has a VO2 max around 40 ml/kg/min, while elite endurance athletes score in the high 60s and above.

## Key facts

- VO2 max is the maximum rate at which a person can utilize oxygen, typically expressed in ml/kg/min. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p227]
- An average 45-year-old man has a VO2 max around 40 ml/kg/min, while an elite endurance athlete scores in the high 60s and above. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p227]
- A person in the bottom quartile of VO2 max is nearly four times likelier to die than someone in the top quartile, and five times likelier than someone with elite-level VO2 max. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p229]
- Poor cardiorespiratory fitness carries a greater relative risk of death (hazard ratio ~4) than smoking (HR ~1.4). [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p229]
- VO2 max declines steeply with age, with activities that are easy in youth becoming difficult or impossible in old age. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p254]
- A 35-year-old man with average fitness (VO2 max in mid-30s) should be able to run at a 10-minute mile pace, but by age 70 only the very fittest 5% can still manage this. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p254]
- VO2 max can be increased through specific training, including both Zone 2 and higher-intensity efforts. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p253]
- VO2 max declines by roughly 10% per decade, and up to 15% per decade after age 50. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p255]
- A 50-year-old woman starting at 32 ml/kg/min can expect a VO2 max of approximately 21 ml/kg/min at age 80, requiring a current VO2 max of 45-49 to maintain hiking capability. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p255]
- Improving VO2 max from the bottom quartile to below average is associated with almost a 50% reduction in all-cause mortality. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p255]
- Sustained training can improve VO2 max by approximately 13% over 8-10 weeks and 17% after 24-52 weeks. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p256]
- Elite octogenarian endurance athletes averaged VO2 max of 38 versus 21 for untrained controls, a difference of more than 80%. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p256]
- Boosting elderly subjects' VO2 max by 6 ml/kg/min (about 25%) was equivalent to subtracting twelve years from their age. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p257]
- VO2 max intervals typically range from 3-8 minutes at maximum sustainable pace, followed by 4 minutes recovery, repeated 4-6 times. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p258]
- Once VO2 max drops below approximately 18 ml/kg/min in men and 15 in women, it begins to threaten ability to live independently. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: The strong dose-dependent relationship between VO2 max and longevity, combined with the large effect size and biological plausibility, suggests a causal relationship even without randomized controlled trials.
- Inference: VO2 max trainability at any age is demonstrated by Robert Marchand, who improved his VO2 max from 31 to 35 ml/kg/min between ages 101 and 103.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: VO2 max improvement potential varies between individuals based on genetics and starting fitness level.
