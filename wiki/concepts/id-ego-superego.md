# Id, Ego, and Superego

## Summary

Freud's structural model of the psyche comprises three agencies. The id is the oldest province, containing inherited drives and operating by primary process; it is inaccessible to direct consciousness. The ego develops from the id's cortical layer, mediating between id demands, superego pressures, and external reality, operating by secondary process. The superego forms through parental influence prolonged into the ego, representing internalized authority and conscience. Both id and superego represent influences of the past, while the ego is determined by individual experience.

## Key facts

- The id contains everything inherited and present at birth, particularly the drives originating from somatic organization; it is the most important portion throughout life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7306]
- The ego performs tasks of self-preservation by becoming aware of stimuli, storing experiences, avoiding excessive stimuli, adapting to moderate ones, and learning to change the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7307]
- The superego forms during childhood as a special agency in which parental influence is prolonged; it includes family, racial, and national traditions as well as demands of the immediate social milieu. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7308]
- The id and superego both represent the influences of the past (heredity versus what is taken from others), while the ego is principally determined by the individual's own accidental and contemporary experiences. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7308]
- The superego takes over the power which the patient's parents exercised over his ego, allowing for potential 'after-education' of the neurotic to correct parental mistakes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7334]

## Inferences

- Inference: The structural model suggests that psychological normality requires harmonious cooperation between the three agencies, with neurosis arising when this coordination fails.
