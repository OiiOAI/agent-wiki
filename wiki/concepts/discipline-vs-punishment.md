# Discipline vs Punishment

## Summary

The authors distinguish discipline (teaching through practice and consequences) from punishment (payment for wrongdoing). Discipline looks forward to help prevent future mistakes; punishment looks backward to pay for past sins. Christ's suffering completed punishment, leaving only discipline for believers.

## Key facts

- Discipline is defined as not payment for wrong but the natural law of God where actions reap consequences; it looks forward. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p181]
- Punishment is payment for wrongdoing; the authors cite Romans 6:23: 'The wages of sin is death.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p181]
- The authors state: 'God is finished punishing us. Punishment ended on the cross for all those who accept Christ as Savior: 'He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree' (1 Peter 2:24).'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p182]
- Discipline is distinguished from punishment by its relationship to time: discipline looks forward to help us not make the same mistakes again. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p182]
- The authors cite Hebrews 12:10: 'God disciplines us for our good, that we may share in his holiness.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p182]
- The freedom of the cross allows practice without fear of judgment: 'Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus' (Romans 8:1). [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p182]

## Inferences

- Inference: The authors connect discipline to the theological concept of progressive sanctification rather than justification.
