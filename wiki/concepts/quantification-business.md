# Quantification (Business)

## Summary

Quantification is the process of measuring the numerical impact of innovations and all business activities. Without quantification, there is no way to know whether an innovation worked. It involves tracking metrics such as customer counts, sales figures, conversion rates, and the specific monetary impact of changes made to the business.

## Key facts

- Without Quantification, there is no way to know whether an Innovation worked. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p128]
- Quantification involves the numbers related to the impact an Innovation makes on the business. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p128]
- The book states that 99 percent of small business owners cannot answer how many selling opportunities they had the day before. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p128]
- To quantify a greeting change innovation, one must count people coming in before and after, purchases made, dollar values, and determine the improvement. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p129]
- Gerber recommends quantifying everything related to how you do business: customer counts, call volumes, product sales by time and day. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: Quantification transforms subjective improvements into objective, measurable business results that can be compared and improved upon.
