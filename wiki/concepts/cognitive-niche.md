# Cognitive Niche

## Summary

The cognitive niche refers to the evolutionary strategy by which humans entered a niche requiring sophisticated cognitive abilities rather than relying on physical weapons like fangs or claws. Gazzaniga argues that this cognitive niche allowed humans to develop unique abilities including fine motor control, language, abstract thinking, imagination, and planning, and that physical changes to the body (free hands, opposable thumbs, larynx) preceded many of these cognitive abilities.

## Key facts

- The author states that 'Homo sapiens entered a cognitive niche' and have done all too well without the fangs and claws that other species possess. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p396]
- According to the text, humans needed free hands and fully opposable thumbs and a larynx before they could acquire many of their unique abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p396]
- The author notes that Neanderthals had a bigger brain than modern humans but did not develop the same advanced artifacts as Homo sapiens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p397]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cognitive niche concept suggests that human intelligence evolved as an adaptation to environmental challenges that could be solved through cognitive rather than physical means.
