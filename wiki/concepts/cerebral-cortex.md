# Cerebral Cortex

## Summary

The cerebral cortex is the heavily wrinkled outer layer of the cerebrum, divided into two hemispheres. Each hemisphere is further divided into four lobes (frontal, parietal, occipital, temporal) with specialized functions. Cognitive operations rely primarily on the cerebral cortex.

## Key facts

- The cerebral cortex is divided into four lobes named for the skull bones that overlie them: frontal, parietal, occipital, and temporal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p13]
- The frontal lobe is largely concerned with short-term memory, planning future actions, and control of movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- The parietal lobe mediates somatic sensation, forming a body image and relating it to extrapersonal space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- The temporal lobe processes hearing, recognition of objects and faces, and through the hippocampus and amygdala, is involved in learning, memory, and emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- The occipital lobe is concerned with vision. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
