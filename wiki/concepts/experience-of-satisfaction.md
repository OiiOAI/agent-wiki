# Experience of Satisfaction

## Summary

The experience of satisfaction is Freud's term in the 1895 Project for the foundational learning event in which an endogenous need (such as hunger) is relieved by external intervention (such as nursing). During this experience, three things occur in the ψ system: (1) lasting discharge removes unpleasure, (2) a mnemic image of the satisfying object becomes cathected, and (3) facilitation forms between the mnemic image and the nuclear neurons. This experience is the prototype for all later wish fulfillment and object relations.

## Key facts

- The experience of satisfaction 'has the most radical results on the development of the individual's functions. For three things occur in the ψ system: (1) a lasting discharge is effected; (2) a cathexis of the neurons which correspond to the perception of an object occurs; (3) at other points of the pallium information arrives of the discharge of the released reflex movement. A facilitation is then formed between these cathexes and the nuclear neurons.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p390]
- Freud noted: 'The initial helplessness of human beings is the primal source of all moral motives,' arising from the infant's dependence on others to perform the specific action. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p390]
- A facilitation forms between mnemic images and nuclear neurons during satisfaction: 'The facilitation, however, is formed in a manner which allows of a deeper insight into the development of ψ.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p391]

## Inferences

- Inference: The experience of satisfaction establishes the neural pathways for all later object relations and wish fulfillment.
- Inference: This concept anticipates attachment theory and object relations theory in emphasizing the role of early caregiver-infant interactions.
