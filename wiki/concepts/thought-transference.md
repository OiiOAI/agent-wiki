# Thought Transference

## Summary

Thought transference is the alleged process by which mental contents—ideas, emotional states, or volitional impulses—are transferred between individuals through empty space without using conventional communication. Freud examined this phenomenon through the analysis of fortune-teller prophecies and analytic case material, finding that interpretations often revealed the secret wishes of those consulted.

## Key facts

- Thought transference claims that mental processes can be transferred between people without using words or signs. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6877]
- Freud reported analyzing cases where fortune-tellers expressed knowledge that could only be explained by thought transference or elaborate fraud. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6879]
- In one case, a fortune-teller's prophecy contained specific numbers that corresponded to the patient's mother's reproductive history, reflecting the patient's unconscious wish to replace her mother with her father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6879]
- Freud concluded that the evidence for thought transference, while not conclusive, showed a 'strong balance of probability' in its favor. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6881]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud suggested that thought transference might represent an archaic form of communication that was replaced during evolution by sensory-based communication, analogous to how instinctive coordination in insect colonies may operate.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Each individual case admits rational alternative explanations, and Freud acknowledges that definitive proof remains elusive.
