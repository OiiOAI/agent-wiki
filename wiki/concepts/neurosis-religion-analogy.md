# Neurosis-Religion Analogy

## Summary

Freud's central argument that religious phenomena parallel neurotic symptoms—both originate from traumatic experiences (in individual or species history), both undergo a latency period, and both exhibit compulsive character that resists logical argument.

## Key facts

- Freud proposes that religious phenomena are only to be understood on the pattern of individual neurotic symptoms—as the return of long-forgotten, important events in primaeval history of the human family. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7227]
- The formula for neurosis development is: early trauma, defence, latency, outbreak of neurotic illness, partial return of the repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7247]
- Freud argues that traditions which have undergone repression and lingered in the unconscious display powerful compulsive effects on their return, analogous to the return of the repressed in neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The analogy suggests that religious dogmas contain a core of 'historical truth' similar to how delusional ideas contain forgotten truths—yet religious phenomena as group phenomena 'escape the curse of isolation' that afflicts individual neurotics.
