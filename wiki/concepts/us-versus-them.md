# Us/Them Dichotomizing

## Summary

Us/Them dichotomizing refers to the automatic human tendency to categorize people into in-groups and out-groups, favoring the former. While rooted in evolutionarily ancient neural circuitry shared with other primates, humans uniquely rationalize this bias with ideology, religion, and cultural narratives. The core of Us/Them is emotional and automatic, with cognition often serving as post-hoc justification.

## Key facts

- The core of Us/Them dichotomizing is emotional and automatic—stereotyping 'isn't a case of lazy, short-cutting cognition. It isn't conscious cognition at all' (Berreby). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p414]
- Humans uniquely kill over ideology, theology, or aesthetics—no other primate does this despite having negative associations about out-group members. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p414]
- The Cagots of France were persecuted for centuries despite having no known distinguishing features—prejudice without any identifiable 'reason'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p414]
- White women show more negative implicit attitudes toward African American men when ovulating, demonstrating that Us/Them intensity is modulated by sex hormones. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p416]
- People belong to multiple, constantly shifting Us/Them dichotomies—race can be 'shoved aside' by gender, age, occupation, or even shirt color. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p420]
- The amygdala responds to other-race faces less when subjects individuate—thinking about what food the person likes rather than categorizing them. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p422]

## Inferences

- Inference: The automaticity of Us/Them is demonstrated by its speed—the amygdala and insula make dichotomies before conscious awareness, or without it entirely. This suggests Us/Them is processed by ancient subcortical systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The evolutionary origins of racial Us/Them are debated—the text notes racial differences emerged relatively recently and hunter-gatherers rarely encountered people of different races, challenging evolutionary psychology explanations.
