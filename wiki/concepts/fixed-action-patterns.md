# Fixed-Action Patterns

## Summary

Fixed-action patterns are rigid, automatically activated behavioral sequences found across animal species, including humans. Ethologists discovered that specific trigger features—often minimal stimuli—activate these preprogrammed responses. The turkey mother's maternal response to 'cheep-cheep' sounds exemplifies how a single stimulus feature can produce an entire behavioral sequence, a phenomenon Cialdini parallels with human automatic responding.

## Key facts

- Fixed-action patterns involve intricate sequences of behavior such as courtship or mating rituals that occur in virtually the same fashion and order every time. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p10]
- The turkey mother will care for any object making the 'cheep-cheep' sound, including a stuffed polecat carrying a sound recorder, but will ignore or kill her own chicks that do not make the sound. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p9]
- A male robin will vigorously attack robin-redbreast feathers placed in its territory while ignoring a perfect stuffed replica of a male robin without red breast feathers. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p11]
- Human behavior exhibits analogous automatic patterns; Langer's experiment showed that the word 'because' followed by any statement triggered compliance even without a genuine reason. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p12]

## Inferences

- Inference: The parallel between animal fixed-action patterns and human compliance responses suggests automaticity is evolutionarily ancient and deeply embedded rather than culturally learned.
- Inference: The effectiveness of 'because' as a compliance trigger implies humans have developed linguistic shortcuts that function similarly to species-specific auditory or visual triggers in animals.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all human compliance behaviors have dedicated evolutionary origins or whether some emerged from cultural learning that became automatized is not clearly established.
