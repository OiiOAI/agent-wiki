# Iatrogenics

## Summary

Harm caused by the healer—the phenomenon where attempts to help or fix systems end up harming them more than helping. Taleb identifies iatrogenics as the most neglected product of modernity, present across multiple domains from medicine to economics. The concept demonstrates that interventions based on incomplete understanding often produce worse outcomes than the conditions they aim to treat.

## Key facts

- Iatrogenics is the harm caused by the healer—among the most neglected products of modernity—present in medicine, policy, economics, and other domains. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p15]
- The medical fragilista overintervenes by denying the body's natural ability to heal and gives medications with potentially very severe side effects. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p27]
- The policy fragilista mistakes the economy for a washing machine that continuously needs fixing and blows it up through well-intentioned intervention. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p27]
- Just as spending a month in bed leads to muscle atrophy, complex systems are weakened, even killed, when deprived of stressors—a form of iatrogenics from overprotection. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p21-22]
- Iatrogenics literally means 'caused by the healer' (iatros being Greek for healer), originally describing medical harm from treatment exceeding benefits. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p151]
- The 1930s tonsillectomy study demonstrated naive interventionism: 389 children examined, 174 recommended for surgery; the remaining 215 examined by second doctors had 99 recommended; examined by third doctors, 52 of the remaining 116 were recommended—all without objective medical necessity. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p150]
- Alan Greenspan's attempt to iron out the boom-bust cycle is identified as the most significant economic iatrogenics of all time, causing risks to accumulate invisibly until they blew up the economy in 2008. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p158]
- The principle 'primun non nocere' (first do no harm) attributed to Hippocrates appeared centuries before the term iatrogenics entered common usage. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p153]
- Ignaz Semmelweis was humiliated and eventually died in an asylum for advocating hand washing to prevent hospital fever, demonstrating institutional resistance to recognizing iatrogenics. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p151]
- The first principle of iatrogenics states that evidence of harm is not required to claim a drug or unnatural procedure is dangerous—harm lies in the future, not the narrowly defined past. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p426]
- Iatrogenics typically follows a treacherous pattern: benefits are small, visible, and immediate, while costs are large, delayed, and hidden. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p427]
- For hypertension treatment, only one person in eighteen benefits when blood pressure is mildly high (5.6%), but one in four benefits when high, and two out of three when severe (72%)—showing convex benefits relative to constant iatrogenics. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p428]
- Taleb catalogs iatrogenic agents including statins, Vioxx, antidepressants, bariatric surgery, cortisone, disinfectants, hormone replacement therapy, hysterectomies, Cesarean births beyond necessity, ear tubes, lobotomies, iron supplementation, whitening of rice and wheat, sunscreen creams, excessive hygiene, and antibiotics. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p434]
- Statins require treating fifty high-risk persons for five years to prevent a single cardiovascular event, with minimal or nonexistent benefits for those not very sick. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p432]
- Back surgery for sciatica shows equivalent results to doing nothing after six years, while every operation carries risks including brain damage from anesthesia, spinal cord injury, and hospital infections. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p434]
- Antibiotics transfer antifragility from the patient's body to the germ by promoting antibiotic-resistant strains. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p435]
- Mammograms administered annually to women over forty do not increase life expectancy and may decrease it, as treating tumors that will not kill the patient shortens life through chemotherapy toxicity. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p450]
- The ancients understood iatrogenics well: Martial wrote 'I did not feel ill, Symmache; now I do (after your ministrations),' and Emperor Hadrian exclaimed as he died that his doctors had killed him. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p441]
- Montaigne cited that ancient Lacedaemonians attributed longevity to 'ignoring medicine.'. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p441]
- Iatrogenics is harm done by the healer, as when the doctor's interventions do more harm than good. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p527]
- Medical iatrogenics occurs when there are small identifiable gains (avoidance of minor discomfort) but exposure to Black Swans with delayed invisible large side effects. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p557]
- Iatrogenics disappears nonlinearly as a function of condition severity—when the patient is very ill, treatment distribution shifts to antifragile. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p559]

## Inferences

- Inference: atrogenics represents the practical failure mode of the fragilista—the gap between intended benefits and actual consequences when complex systems are tampered with by well-meaning but inadequately informed actors.
- Inference: The widespread absence of iatrogenics awareness in economics and policy-making, despite its long recognition in medicine, suggests a systematic blind spot in how societies evaluate intervention.
- Inference: Taleb's iatrogenics framework implies that the standard medical practice of prescribing medication for mildly elevated measurements (like blood pressure) creates a net harm for the general population by treating noise as signal.
- Inference: The nonlinearity principle suggests that pharmaceutical companies' incentive to 'scrape the bottom of the barrel' by seeking disease among healthier populations systematically increases iatrogenic harm.
- Inference: Historical evidence from hospital strikes (where only urgent operations proceed and life expectancy does not drop) supports the via negativa approach of limiting medical intervention.
- Inference: For healthy individuals, medical interventions often carry small probabilities of catastrophic outcomes discounted because unseen, against high probability of mild benefits—making them fragile.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How to distinguish genuine iatrogenic harm from cases where intervention is truly necessary and beneficial.
- Uncertain: Taleb acknowledges that sophisticated doctors are aware of variable measurements and do not prescribe when numbers are not compelling, though he notes not all doctors are sophisticated.
- Uncertain: The claim that 'one needs to treat fifty high risk persons for five years' for statins is presented without citation; the specific statistical claim may require verification.
