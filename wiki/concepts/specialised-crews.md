# Specialised Crews

## Summary

Specialised crews are diverse groups focused on specific functions during crisis, designed to act without extensive preparation. Different crew types serve different purposes: continuity crews (maintain operations), probing crews (lateral thinking for radical innovation), wrecking crews (identify unintended consequences), journaling crews (capture real-time learning), data analytics crews (synthesize weak signals), and healing crews (organisational health).

## Key facts

- Five is a cognitive limit for deep knowledge to manage a crisis, so command teams of less than five are most effective. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p24]
- Fifteen is the natural limit for deep trust, naturally present in informal networks but difficult to create in formal systems. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p24]
- One hundred fifty is a natural acquaintance limit for humans; in a clan there are rarely more than that number. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p24]
- A crew is based on clear roles and identities: securing at least an expert, a naive, and an operational role. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p24]
- The probing crew includes lateral thinkers, strategic designers, user experience designers, artists, business correspondents, and the organisation's most creative members. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p26]
