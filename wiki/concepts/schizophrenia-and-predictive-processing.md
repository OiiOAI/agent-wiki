# Schizophrenia and Predictive Processing

## Summary

Predictive processing may shed light on schizophrenia—a condition involving both hallucinations and delusions. One theory suggests schizophrenia involves the mistaken generation of highly weighted (high-precision) prediction error signals that signify important yet unpredicted sensory information, causing the brain to seek new predictive models. This may explain the distinctive early stages of psychosis where the world begins to look somehow different or strange due to persistent, unresolved prediction errors.

## Key facts

- Peter Chadwick described his own experience of schizophrenia onset as involving what he called a 'step-ladder to the impossible,' ascribing increasing significance to patterns and coincidences until he radically changed his conception of reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p67]
- One theory proposes that schizophrenia involves the mistaken generation of highly weighted (high-precision) prediction error signals that signify important yet unpredicted sensory information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p68]
- The strangeness in early stages of psychosis may reflect persistent, unresolved prediction errors that slowly drive the system to form increasingly radical hypotheses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p68]
- Aberrant prediction errors, even if they play a role in psychosis development, are probably seldom the whole story—there are likely multiple pathways to psychosis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p69]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise role of prediction errors in schizophrenia is still being investigated, and caution is warranted as the research is in early stages.
