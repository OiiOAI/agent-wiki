# Slip of the Pen

## Summary

A slip of the pen is a writing error that reveals unconscious thoughts, analogous to slips of the tongue. Freud provides examples of distorted names, miswritten dates, and prescriptions with unconscious motivations.

## Key facts

- Distorting names is a form of insulting their owners, paralleling hostile slips of the tongue. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2102]
- Slips of the pen occur more readily than slips of the tongue because when writing, inhibitory function of will is directed to bringing articulatory movements into harmony with ideas. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2116]
- Anticipation of what one intends to write can produce errors when expressive movement is retarded. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2116]
- Misprints can be psychologically motivated and may be tendentious, as in political misprints. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2114]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mechanism of slips of the pen involves the same unconscious interference as slips of the tongue, differing only in the medium.
