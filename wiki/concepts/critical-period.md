# Critical Period

## Summary

A critical period is a restricted interval in early postnatal development during which the nervous system exhibits heightened plasticity and is particularly susceptible to modification by experience. Alterations that occur during critical periods are nearly irreversible. Critical periods vary in timing among brain areas and tasks; for example, children with strabismus require eye alignment within the first few postnatal years to develop normal stereoscopic vision, and humans cannot learn a new language without an accent after early adolescence.

## Key facts

- Critical periods are intervals of heightened susceptibility to modification that occur at particular times in development, during which experience has potent and often irreversible effects on neural circuitry. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1258]
- Critical periods vary in timing among brain areas and tasks; sensory processing regions close earlier than those involved in language and higher cognitive functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1277]
- The critical period for ocular dominance plasticity in the visual cortex is regulated by maturation of inhibitory interneurons and the balance between excitation and inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1268]
- The closure of critical periods is associated with myelination and formation of perineuronal nets that create molecular barriers to synaptic remodeling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1270]
- Critical periods can be experimentally 'reopened' in adulthood through manipulations including environmental enrichment, chondroitinase treatment to degrade perineuronal nets, and transplantation of immature inhibitory interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1279]
- Hubel and Wiesel discovered a critical period from the third to eighth week of life when newborn kittens' brains required visual stimulation to develop normally. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p59]
- Language development has a critical period beginning in infancy and ending between 8 years and puberty; second languages learned after this are not processed in the same brain area as native tongue. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p60]
- Discovery of critical periods changed medical practice: children born with cataracts are now sent for corrective surgery during the critical period so brains can form crucial connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p61]
- During the critical period, the learning machinery is 'continuously on,' allowing babies to pick up sounds and words effortlessly through mere exposure. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p89]
- BDNF helps close down the critical period once main neuronal connections are laid down, turning off the nucleus basalis and ending the period of effortless learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p91]
- Merzenich and Kilgard artificially turned on the nucleus basalis in adult rats and could reopen critical-period plasticity, allowing effortless map formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p94]

## Inferences

- Inference: The existence of critical periods suggests that the brain's ability to remodel declines in adulthood, potentially as a trade-off for maintaining the stability of learned skills and memories.
- Inference: Therapeutic interventions based on reopening critical periods may come at a cost, as reducing the constraints on plasticity could compromise existing stable circuits.
