# Know What You Don't Know

## Summary

The wisdom of recognizing the limits of one's own knowledge and being willing to say 'I don't know' rather than offering ill-informed opinions. Munger contrasts this with the 'bee dance' phenomenon where people give incoherent answers when they lack the knowledge to respond properly.

## Key facts

- You must have the confidence to override people with more credentials than you whose cognition is impaired by incentive-caused bias, but you must also recognize when you have no wisdom to add and your best course is to trust some expert. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p271]
- When Jack Welch was asked what Apple did wrong, he answered, 'I don't have any special competence that would enable me to answer that question,' and Munger gives the same answer. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p272]
- A bee that finds nectar straight up (which never happens in ordinary bee life) dances an incoherent dance that gums things up; many people are like that bee—they attempt to answer questions they don't have knowledge to answer. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p272]
- Nobody expects you to know everything about everything; Munger tries to get rid of people who always confidently answer questions about which they don't have real knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p273]
- What could possibly be more useful in life than knowing what you know and what you don't know?. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p271]
