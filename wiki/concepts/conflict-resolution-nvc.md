# Conflict Resolution in NVC

## Summary

NVC conflict resolution prioritizes creating human connection between parties in conflict before addressing issues. The goal is not compromise but meeting everyone's needs fully. Five steps are involved: expressing needs, searching for others' real needs, verifying accurate recognition, providing empathy for mutual hearing, and proposing strategies in positive action language.

## Key facts

- Creating a connection between people is the most important thing in conflict resolution. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p216]
- The objective is not to get the other side to do what they want them to do. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p216]
- NVC aims to meet everyone's needs fully, not to find compromise where everybody gives something up and nobody is satisfied. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p217]
- Needs are the resources life requires to sustain itself; all people have the same needs regardless of nationality, religion, gender, income, or education. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p221]
- Needs contain no reference to anybody taking any particular action; strategies refer to specific actions that specific people may take. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p221]
- When you make the connection, the problem usually solves itself. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p219]
- Maintain respect as a key element in successful conflict resolution. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: tellectual analysis is often received as criticism and can escalate conflicts; focusing on needs rather than analysis resolves conflicts more effectively.
