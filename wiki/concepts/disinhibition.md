# Disinhibition in Aging

## Summary

Disinhibition theory suggests aging is associated with increased inability to inhibit nonselected information, decreasing the efficiency of working memory as irrelevant information occupies precious cognitive capacity. This affects both visual and auditory domains, particularly in tasks requiring selective attention or search.

## Key facts

- Aging is associated with increased inability to inhibit nonselected information, decreasing efficiency of working memory as irrelevant information occupies capacity. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p293]
- Studies of negative priming show older adults experience greater interference when a previously ignored distracter becomes the target. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p293]
- In auditory domains, older adults demonstrate greater susceptibility to interference in cocktail party phenomena where multiple conversations compete for attention. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p294]
- Age effects on selective attention are substantially reduced when the individual does not have to search the environment for information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p294]

## Inferences

- Inference: Disinhibition effects may limit what information older adults can effectively consider during SA formation, particularly in complex environments with multiple competing stimuli.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: There is controversy about whether inhibitory deficits represent a distinct phenomenon or manifestations of other general aging processes like generalized slowing.
