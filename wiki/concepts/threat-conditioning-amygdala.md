# Amygdala-Based Threat Learning

## Summary

Threat learning (fear conditioning) involves the amygdala and allows rapid association between a neutral stimulus and an aversive event. The lateral nucleus of the amygdala receives convergent inputs from conditioned and unconditioned stimuli, and LTP-like mechanisms strengthen these associations.

## Key facts

- The amygdala-based defense system quickly learns about new dangers and can associate a neutral stimulus with a threatening stimulus after a single paired exposure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1379]
- During Pavlovian threat conditioning, synaptic transmission is modified in the lateral amygdala by pairing the conditioned and unconditioned stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1379]
- LTP in the lateral nucleus of the amygdala is triggered by Ca2+ influx through NMDA receptors and L-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1379]
- Threat conditioning requires postsynaptic NMDA receptors and voltage-gated calcium channels in the lateral amygdala, and is enhanced by norepinephrine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1379]
- Damage to the human amygdala impairs implicit threat conditioning but does not affect explicit memory of having been conditioned. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1380]
