# Gnarly Challenges

## Summary

Gnarly challenges are complex strategic problems that lack clear structure. They have four defining characteristics: no clear definition of the problem itself, multiple conflicting ambitions rather than a single goal, alternatives that must be searched for rather than given, and unclear connections between actions and outcomes. Unlike choice challenges or engineering-design challenges, gnarly challenges cannot be solved through deduction or preset frameworks.

## Key facts

- Gnarly problems have no clear definition of the problem itself and studying various concepts may be a large part of strategy creation. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p42]
- Gnarly challenges typically involve a bundle of ambitions—multiple desires, goals, intents, values, fears, and ambitions that may conflict. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p42]
- In gnarly situations, alternatives are not given but must be searched for or imagined. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p42]
- Connections between potential actions and actual outcomes are unclear in gnarly challenges, with multiple interpretations of facts. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p42]
- Most strategic challenges that Rumelt encounters are gnarly, perhaps because companies don't ask for help with easier ones. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p30]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gnarly challenges require the strategist to dig into the nature of the challenge and find the crux—the embedded solvable problem within the complexity.
