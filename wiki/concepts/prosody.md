# Prosody

## Summary

Prosody is defined by Llinás as a generalized form of motor behavior—an outward gesturing of an internal state that conveys the momentary abstraction of emotions or intentions to another organism. It represents the evolutionary foundation of language, predating and underlying human spoken language.

## Key facts

- Prosody is outward expression of momentary internal states that conveys meaning to another creature—it is language but not spoken language. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p229]
- Examples include smiling, laughter, frowning, lifting eyebrows in humans, and defensive posturing, baring teeth, and growling in vertebrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p229]
- Prosody is widespread throughout the animal kingdom and is very old evolutionarily, studied by Darwin in terms of faces and postures representing momentary internal states. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p229]
- Wolf pack language demonstrates sophisticated prosody utilizing vocalization, eye contact, head gesturing, and whole body communication to express socially structured behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p231]
