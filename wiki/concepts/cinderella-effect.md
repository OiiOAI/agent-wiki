# Cinderella Effect

## Summary

The Cinderella effect is the finding by Martin Daly and Margo Wilson that children are far more likely to be abused or killed by a stepparent than by a biological parent. This was framed as parallel to competitive infanticide in nonhuman species, where unrelated males kill offspring to bring females back into estrus. However, the finding has been criticized for not sufficiently controlling for socioeconomic status and for detection bias.

## Key facts

- Daly and Wilson found that a child is far more likely to be abused or killed by a stepparent than by a parent. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p381]
- Critics charge that socioeconomic status was not sufficiently controlled for, as homes with stepparents generally have less income and more economic stress. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p381]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The jury is still out on whether the Cinderella effect reflects adaptive infanticide logic or socioeconomic factors.
