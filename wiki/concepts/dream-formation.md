# Dream Formation

## Summary

Dream formation occurs during sleep, which represents a narcissistic state where the ego withdraws cathexes from external objects. Dreams begin when day residues retain cathexis and become reinforced by unconscious drive impulses. The dream wish then formed pursues a regressive course through the unconscious to perception, transforming preconscious day residues into visual images. This regression to perception produces hallucinated wish fulfillment, which is accepted as real because reality testing has been abandoned with the withdrawal of cathexis from the conscious system.

## Key facts

- Sleep is characterized by an almost complete withdrawal from the surrounding world and a cessation of all interest in it, analogous to an reactivation of intrauterine existence. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4591]
- Dreams are instigated by residues from the previous day that have not submitted to the general withdrawal of cathexes and retain libidinal or other interest. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4593]
- The preconscious dream wish must be sharply distinguished from the day's residues; it may not have existed in waking life and displays the irrational character of everything unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4594]
- In dream formation, the process begun in the Pcs. and reinforced by the Ucs. pursues a backward course through the Ucs. to perception, constituting a topographical regression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4596]
- In dreams, words and speeches in the dream content are not freshly formed but modeled on speeches from the day preceding the dream. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4597]

## Inferences

- Inference: The essential difference between dream-work and schizophrenia is that in schizophrenia the primary process modifies the words themselves, while in dreams it modifies the thing presentations to which words have been taken back.
- Inference: Dreams achieve two results: bringing hidden wishes into consciousness and representing them with the subject's entire belief as fulfilled.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise conditions determining whether a preconscious thought becomes a dream versus a delusion remain unclear.
- Uncertain: Why somnambulism occurs (motor discharge during dreams) while most dreams do not result in motor activity is not fully explained.
