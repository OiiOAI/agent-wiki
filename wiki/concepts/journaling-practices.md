# Journaling Practices

## Summary

Journaling creates precious learning material across the entire crisis. The practice involves starting early, sketching basic shapes, using words for complex concepts, and clustering information visually. Visual journaling provides thick data, synthesises more information in a smaller space, and allows for both quick scanning and detailed recall. The approach is based on Gestalt principles of human perception.

## Key facts

- Journaling creates precious learning material across the entire crisis. Start early and sketch as much as possible. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p16]
- Basic shapes will do; if a concept is too complicated, use words and avoid long paragraphs. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p16]
- Gestalt principles include figure/ground (brain divides elements into background and foreground), focal point (distinct elements are seen first), proximity (clustered elements seen as one entity), and similarity (similar signs grouped together). [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p17]
- Micro learning provides evidence-based protection for key decisions made without full evidence gathered. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p27]
- The 17% principle: 83% of radiologists missed a gorilla in an X-ray; those who saw it changed their minds after talking to the majority. Identifying the 17% in an organisation is important in crisis. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p10]
