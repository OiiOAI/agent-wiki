# The Happiness Trap

## Summary

The Happiness Trap refers to a powerful psychological pattern where people pursue happiness through trying to control inner experiences, but paradoxically create more suffering. People hold unhelpful beliefs about happiness that are widely accepted, creating a vicious cycle where the more they pursue feeling good, the more they suffer. The trap is hidden, so people do not realize they are caught in it.

## Key facts

- The happiness trap is described as a powerful psychological trap where people pursue happiness through trying to control inner experiences, paradoxically creating more suffering. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p10]
- The psychological trap involves misleading ideas about happiness that create a vicious cycle. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p10]
- The happiness trap is described as so well hidden that people do not realize they are caught in it. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p10]
- Research shows that the harder people chase pleasurable feelings and try to avoid uncomfortable ones, the more likely they are to suffer from depression and anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p17]
- The book is based on Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), an evidence-based approach in behavioral psychology. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p202]
- The ACT model is summarized as: Be present, Open up, Do what matters. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p229]
- The technical term for the ability to be present, open up, and do what matters is psychological flexibility, and research shows greater psychological flexibility correlates with greater health, well-being, and happiness.
