# Bias

## Summary

Memory bias refers to the systematic distortion of memories to align with current knowledge, beliefs, and feelings. Schacter identifies several types: consistency bias (remembering past attitudes as similar to present ones), egocentric bias (remembering self-favorably), hindsight bias (seeing past events as more predictable than they were), and the fading affect bias (negative emotions fade faster than positive ones). These biases reveal how memory is reconstructed rather than retrieved verbatim.

## Key facts

- Consistency bias leads people to perceive their past attitudes as more similar to present attitudes than they actually were. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]
- Hindsight bias causes people to overestimate how predictable past events were after learning their outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]
- The fading affect bias describes how negative emotions fade faster than positive emotions in autobiographical memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]
- Egocentric bias leads people to remember their contributions as larger and their mistakes as smaller than others would recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p381]

## Inferences

- Inference: Memory biases may serve self-enhancing functions, promoting psychological well-being and maintaining coherent self-narratives.
