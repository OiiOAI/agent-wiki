# Bounded Rationality

## Summary

Bounded rationality refers to the idea that while an ideal rational agent should fully consider action outcomes, a bounded rational agent must balance costs, effort, and timeliness of computation. Active Inference provides a model of bounded rationality where optimal solutions result from compromises between accuracy and complexity objectives.

## Key facts

- Bounded rationality has been cast in terms of Helmholtz free energy minimization, with energy representing expected value of a choice and entropy representing costs of deliberation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p219]
- What is costly during deliberation is decreasing the entropy of beliefs before a choice to render them more precise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p219]
- Active Inference behavior automatically combines deliberative, perseverative, and habitual components depending on cognitive resources and contextual conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p218]
- Bounded rationality is responsible for the computational limits of organization man, making it impossible to anticipate and specify all future contingencies in contracts. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p423]
- Bounded rationality means people make reasonable decisions based on limited information, not perfect information. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p134]
- Herman Daly calls this the 'invisible foot' of the market, where individual self-interest harms collective welfare. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p134]
- People are 'satisficers'—they attempt to meet their needs sufficiently before moving to the next decision, not optimizers. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p135]
- We misperceive risk, pay too much attention to recent experience, discount the future inappropriately, and filter out information that contradicts our mental models. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p135]
- The bounded rationality of each actor is determined by the information, incentives, goals, and constraints impinging on that actor. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p138]

## Inferences

- Inference: Active Inference provides a unified framework for bounded rationality where the best solution results from compromise between complementary objectives: accuracy and complexity.
- Inference: Since individuals within systems cannot see the whole picture, changing only the actors (without redesigning the system) rarely improves outcomes
- Inference: mproving the information available to actors can quickly change behavior—Meadows cites the example of electric meters in visible vs. basement locations, where visibility alone reduced electricity use by 30%
