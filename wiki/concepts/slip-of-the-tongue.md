# Slip of the Tongue

## Summary

A slip of the tongue (parapraxis in speech) is an unintentional substitution, omission, or distortion of words during speaking, which Freud analyzed as revealing unconscious thoughts. The chunk discusses multiple mechanisms including condensation, contamination, self-betrayal, self-criticism, and identification through name exchange.

## Key facts

- Slips of the tongue often function as self-betrayal, revealing thoughts the speaker did not intend to express. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2075]
- Condensation and contamination are intimate mechanisms that can fuse similar expressions into a slip. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2067]
- Self-criticism and internal opposition can cause a speaker to substitute the opposite of what was intended. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2074]
- Replacing one name with another signifies appreciative feeling which must remain in the background. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2073]
- The trace of affect following revelation of a slip indicates that some motive contributed to the interference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2071]
- Suppressed insulting words can produce slips that give vent to hostile feelings. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2076]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud distinguishes slips of the tongue from mere phonetic errors, arguing that 'contact effects of sounds' are insufficient to explain them without remote psychical influences.
- Inference: Slips can approximate jokes when the speaker has unconscious rather than conscious intention.
