# Mental Contrasting

## Summary

Mental contrasting is a self-regulation technique developed by Gabriele Oettingen that involves first imagining a positive future (wish fulfillment) and then visualizing the personal obstacles that prevent achievement of that wish. Unlike mere positive thinking, mental contrasting creates a cognitive association between the future and reality that energizes action when the goal is deemed achievable, while promoting disengagement when goals are unrealistic. Research demonstrates that mental contrasting increases systolic blood pressure (indicating heightened energy) when expectations of success are high, while decreasing energy when goals appear unattainable.

## Key facts

- Mental contrasting involves first dreaming about a positive future outcome and then visualizing the personal obstacles that stand in the way of that wish. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p10]
- Mental contrasting only energizes action when the person has high expectations of success based on past experience. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p59]
- When expectations of success are low, mental contrasting promotes disengagement from unattainable wishes, freeing energy for more achievable goals. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p59]
- Mental contrasting creates a nonconscious cognitive association between the future wish and the present obstacle, bringing the reality to mind whenever the future is activated. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p85]
- The technique was shown to work in a study with 168 female students at Berlin universities, where mentally contrasting participants with high expectations of success took action sooner on interpersonal concerns. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p59]
- Physiological measurements showed that systolic blood pressure increased after mental contrasting when goals were judged achievable. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p64]
- Mental contrasting creates cognitive associations between obstacles and the instrumental behaviors needed to overcome them without requiring conscious effort. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p91]
- Students who mentally contrasted perceived their present realities more negatively when optimistic about success and less negatively when not optimistic, compared to control groups. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p93]
- Mental contrasting improved children's ability to detect obstacles in chess tasks when pursuing viable wishes and decreased detection for non-viable wishes. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p95]
- Students who mentally contrasted recalled significantly more negative feedback when they had high expectations of success. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p99]
- Only 9-16% of people spontaneously perform mental contrasting without instruction, making it an unnatural but learnable strategy. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p120]
- Mental contrasting works by linking the desired future to present reality, preparing the nonconscious mind to recognize obstacles and respond with instrumental behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p89]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mental contrasting functions as a self-regulation tool by helping people allocate their energy efficiently between pursuing feasible wishes and disengaging from unattainable ones.
- Inference: Mental contrasting differs from simple positive thinking because it incorporates obstacles rather than purely positive fantasies.
- Inference: The technique appears to work by changing perception of reality as an obstacle rather than changing the reality itself.
- Inference: Mental contrasting can lead to disengagement from unfeasible wishes, which is a beneficial self-regulatory outcome.
