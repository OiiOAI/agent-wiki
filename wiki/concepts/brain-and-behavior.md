# The Brain and Behavior

## Summary

Chapter 1 of Principles of Neural Science introduces the fundamental question of how the brain produces behavior and mental processes. It traces the historical debate between localizationist and holistic views, presents evidence for distinct functional brain regions, and establishes that mental processes emerge from interactions between elementary processing units in the brain.

## Key facts

- All behavior is the result of brain function; mental processes are a set of operations carried out by the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p7]
- The human brain contains more than 80 billion individual nerve cells interconnected in neural circuits that construct perceptions, fix attention, guide decisions, and implement actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p3]
- Franz Joseph Gall proposed that the brain contains many specialized organs and that particular regions control specific functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p9]
- Pierre Flourens rejected localization, concluding that all brain regions participate equally in every mental operation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p10]
- Karl Lashley's theory of mass action proposed that it is the full mass of the brain, not regional components, that is crucial for functions like learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p18]
