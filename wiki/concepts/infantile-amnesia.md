# Infantile Amnesia

## Summary

Infantile amnesia refers to the remarkable absence of memory traces from the earliest years of life (up to ages five to eight). Freud observed that from this void of memories, certain well-preserved 'screen memories' stand out that represent important unconscious infantile material through displacement and condensation.

## Key facts

- The earliest years of life do not leave behind traces in memory like later experiences, despite the child's early ability to speak and comprehend complex mental situations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5003]
- Childhood memories that have been retained do not necessarily correspond to important experiences of childhood but often represent commonplace details through condensation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5004]
- Screen memories (Deckerinnerungen) contain forgotten material that can be extracted through thorough analysis, as the important has been replaced by the unimportant. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5004]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud concludes that only what is important remains in memory, but through condensation and displacement, the important is replaced by something appearing unimportant—a mechanism operating in screen memories.
