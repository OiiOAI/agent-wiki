# Vote-Counting Procedure

## Summary

The vote-counting procedure is a meta-analytic technique used when traditional effect size calculations are problematic. In this study, it was applied because of the heavy reliance on within-subject designs in SA research, which complicates standard effect size calculations.

## Key facts

- The vote-counting procedure was used instead of traditional effect size calculations due to the prevalence of within-subject designs in SA research. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p244]
- The vote-counting approach classifies each study outcome as positive (significant result in predicted direction), no effect, or negative (significant result in wrong direction). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p244]
- The overall result for a dependent measure type was positive if the number of positive results exceeded negative results. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p244]
- The vote-counting procedure is described as less powerful and less well quantified than traditional meta-analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p244]
