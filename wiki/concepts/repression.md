# Repression

## Summary

Repression is the psychical mechanism by which incompatible ideas are pushed out of consciousness into the unconscious. In the neuropsychoses of defence, symptoms arise through this process, with subsequent 'return of the repressed' manifesting as obsessions, phobias, or other pathological formations.

## Inferences

- Inference: The psychological precondition for repression appears to be the inverted relation between memory and experience—whereby memories of childhood sexual events become more powerful than the original experiences due to puberty's enhancement of sexual reactivity.
- Inference: Repression requires two factors: one devolving upon the mental apparatus affecting the relation between the two systems, and another introducing organic driving forces into mental life.
- Inference: Dreams and hysterical symptoms share the same psychological mechanisms because both involve repressed material finding expression through compromise formations when unconscious wishful impulses receive reinforcement.
- Inference: Repression requires continuous psychic effort; when the defensive intention weakens, the repressed material returns in symptoms or dreams.
- Inference: Repression differs from simple forgetting because it involves an active, motivated pushing away of material from consciousness, as evidenced by the resistance patients display when the repressed material is approached.
- Inference: The concept of repression evolved from the earlier 'traumatic' formula (1893-1895) which equated neurosis with inability to deal with excessively powerful experiences.
- Inference: The homosexual attitude which came into being during the dream was of such overwhelming intensity that the little boy's ego found itself unable to cope with it and defended itself by the process of repression, drawing in the narcissistic masculinity to assist.
- Inference: Repression by no means always takes the side of masculinity against femininity; there are cases in which masculinity itself has to submit to repression by the ego, challenging Adler's theory of the 'masculine protest'.
- Inference: The incompatibility of Adler's theory with the facts of repression suggests that repression operates on the basis of incompatibility with the ego's current state of development rather than on the basis of the gender of the drive impulse.
- Inference: Repression is a primary defense mechanism that forms the foundation of psychoanalytic understanding of psychopathology.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud notes that a complete psychological theory of repression would need to explain why only sexual content can be repressed.
- Uncertain: Whether sexual and infantile factors are equally required in the theory of dreams as in the theory of psychoneuroses—Freud explicitly left this question open.
