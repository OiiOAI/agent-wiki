# Anxiety

## Summary

Freud revised his theory of anxiety, establishing that the ego is the sole seat of anxiety. Anxiety serves as a signal announcing a situation of danger. The three main species—realistic anxiety (danger from external world), neurotic anxiety (danger from the id's drives), and moral anxiety (danger from the superego)—correspond to the ego's three dependent relations. The discovery that anxiety makes repression rather than being created by it was a significant reversal of earlier theory.

## Key facts

- The ego is the sole seat of anxiety — it alone can produce and feel anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6919]
- Anxiety serves as a signal announcing a situation of danger; the question of what material anxiety is made of loses interest in this light. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6919]
- Realistic anxiety is a reaction to expected injury from outside; neurotic anxiety appears to be pointless but is fear of one's own libido. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6918]
- The surprising result of investigation was that anxiety was there earlier; it was the anxiety that made the repression, not the other way round. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6920]
- Fear of castration is one of the commonest and strongest motives for repression and thus for the formation of neuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6921]
- In women, castration complex takes the form of fear of loss of love, a later prolongation of the infant's anxiety when the mother is absent. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6921]
- A particular determinant of anxiety is allotted to every age of development: psychical helplessness for the ego's early immaturity; loss of object for early childhood; castration for the phallic phase; fear of the superego for latency. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6922]
- The process of repression involves the ego anticipating the drive impulse's satisfaction, reproducing unpleasurable feelings at the beginning of the feared situation, thereby activating the pleasure-unpleasure automatism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6923]
- What is feared is the emergence of a traumatic moment which cannot be dealt with by the normal rules of the pleasure principle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6927]

## Inferences

- Inference: Anxiety originates from a traumatic moment (excessive sum of excitation), and neurotic and realistic anxieties represent signals threatening a repetition of such moments.
