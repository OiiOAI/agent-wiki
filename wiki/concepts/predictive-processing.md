# Predictive Processing

## Summary

Predictive processing (PP) is an emerging framework in philosophy of mind and neuroscience that sees prediction as central to brain and cognition, appealing to concepts of predictive brains and minds. It is closely related to Active Inference and sometimes appeals to specific Active Inference constructs like generative models, predictive coding, and free energy.

## Key facts

- Active Inference creatures are probabilistic inference machines that continuously generate predictions based on their generative models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p204]
- Self-evidencing creatures use predictions to validate hypotheses through predictive coding and revise models through learning, and they enact predictions to guide data gathering. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p204]
- Predictive processing theories have been interpreted in internalist, embodied or action-based, and enactivist and non-representational terms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p205]
- Predictive processing posits that reality as experienced is built from our own predictions rather than passively received from the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p8]
- The brain is constantly trying to guess how things in the world and our own body are most likely to be, given what has been learned from previous encounters. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p14]
- In predictive processing, the current sensory signal is used to refine and correct the process of informed guessing (attempts at prediction) already taking place—predictions do much of the heavy lifting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p9]
- The theory flips the traditional story of perception, with predictions now heavily shaped from deep in the brain reaching down to alter responses at areas closer to sensory organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p10]
- Neuronal connections carrying signals backward (downward) exceed forward connections by a substantial margin—in some places by as much as four to one. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p18]
- The work on predictive processing goes by various names including 'predictive processing,' 'hierarchical predictive coding,' and 'active inference.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p19]

## Inferences

- Inference: Predictive processing provides a unifying framework because it applies to both perception and action—both can be understood as different ways of minimizing prediction errors.
- Inference: The efficiency of the brain's processing is explained by predictive processing: instead of transmitting well-predicted information upward, only deviations from expectations (prediction errors) need to be signaled.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The book notes that the field is still developing and much work remains to be done on the precise mechanisms.
