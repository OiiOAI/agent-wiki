# Predictive Processing

## Summary

Predictive processing (PP) is an emerging framework in philosophy of mind and neuroscience that sees prediction as central to brain and cognition, appealing to concepts of predictive brains and minds. It is closely related to Active Inference and sometimes appeals to specific Active Inference constructs like generative models, predictive coding, and free energy.

## Key facts

- Active Inference creatures are probabilistic inference machines that continuously generate predictions based on their generative models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p204]
- Self-evidencing creatures use predictions to validate hypotheses through predictive coding and revise models through learning, and they enact predictions to guide data gathering. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p204]
- Predictive processing theories have been interpreted in internalist, embodied or action-based, and enactivist and non-representational terms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p205]
