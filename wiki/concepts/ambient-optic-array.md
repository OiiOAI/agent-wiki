# Ambient Optic Array

## Summary

The ambient optic array is the structured light that surrounds any point in an illuminated environment. Unlike radiant light that diverges from sources, ambient light converges to an observation point from all directions. The array has structure because ambient light is different in different directions, specifying the surfaces, textures, and layout of the environment. This structure remains invariant during changes in illumination intensity.

## Key facts

- The ambient optic array is ambient light with structure, having parts, pattern, texture, or configuration. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p45]
- Ambient light cannot be homogeneous or blank if it is to contain information about the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p45]
- The structure of ambient light is based on the environment—specifically on the surfaces that separate substances from the medium. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p45]
- Unstructured ambient light (as in dense fog) cannot specify an environment and provides no information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p45]
- The limiting case of ambient light without structure would arise if air were filled with dense fog where light could not reverberate between surfaces. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p45]
- The ambient optic array at a point of observation is structured light that surrounds a position in the environment, fundamentally different from radiant light leaving a point source. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p89]
- An ambient array must surround the point completely—it must be environing, with the field being unbounded, unlike a picture on a plane surface. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p90]
- The point of observation in ecological optics is a position in ecological space, a place where an observer might be, not an abstract geometrical point. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p90]
- The ambient optic array is structured by the layout of surfaces in the environment, not by an empty medium or fog-filled air. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p109]
- The ambient array has invariant structure specifying persistent environmental features and perspective structure that changes with observer movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ecological approach requires moving beyond the camera analogy—vision is not formed by focused images on a retina but by the pickup of structure from the ambient optic array.
- Inference: The ambient optic array concept rejects the traditional view of vision as image-formation, proposing instead that the visual system registers the invariant structure of ambient light. The eye is not a camera forming and delivering an image.
