# Managing Complexity in System Design

## Summary

Complexity directly works against SA by reducing the ability to perceive needed information and making it difficult to form accurate mental models. The book identifies three layers of complexity: system complexity (number of items, interactions, dynamics, and predictability), operational complexity (portion of system complexity relevant to operational goals), and apparent complexity (cognitive, display, and task complexity). The user's mental model moderates how complexity is perceived.

## Key facts

- System complexity is a function of the number of items, degree of interaction between items, system dynamics, and predictability of changes. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p151]
- Cognitive complexity factors explain four times the variance in performance scores as perceptual complexity factors. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p159]
- Principle 15: Just say no to feature creep - The simplest and most direct way to deal with complexity is to reduce it by questioning the addition of features that may be only infrequently used. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p162]
- Principle 18: Minimize logic branches - 'If-x-then-y' logic is hard to learn; 'If-x-then-y-unless-z' creates even more challenging branching that strains mental model development. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p163]
- Automation frequently increases operational complexity because users typically need to oversee automation, take over when it performs improperly, and interact with it for joint tasks. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p154]
