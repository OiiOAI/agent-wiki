# Fordism

## Summary

Fordism refers to the ideas, principles, and systems spawned by Henry Ford, particularly the development of modern mass-production through the automobile assembly line. It is characterized by mass production of homogeneous products, inflexible technologies, standardized work routines, and deskilling of labor.

## Key facts

- Fordism is characterized by the mass production of homogeneous products using inflexible technologies such as the assembly line. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p192]
- Fordism involves the adoption of standardized work routines (Taylorism) with increases in productivity derived from economies of scale and the deskilling and homogenization of labor. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p192]
- Fordism included the negotiation by unions of uniform wages tied to increases in profits and productivity, and growth of a market for the homogenized products of mass-production industries. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p192]
- Fordism reached its peak and began to decline in the 1970s, especially after the oil crisis of 1973 and the subsequent decline of the American automobile industry. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p192]
- Post-Fordism is characterized by a decline in mass products, growth of interest in more specialized products, more flexible production technologies, and workers with more diverse skills. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p193]
- The shift from Fordism to post-Fordism represents a transition from homogeneity to heterogeneity. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p193]
