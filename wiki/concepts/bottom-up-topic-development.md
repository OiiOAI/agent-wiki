# Bottom-Up Topic Development

## Summary

Bottom-up topic development is the approach of building topics and questions from accumulated notes rather than starting with a predetermined plan. This emerges naturally from the slip-box method, where ideas are tested and developed over time, and only those that prove worthwhile attract follow-up work. It contrasts with brainstorming and top-down planning.

## Key facts

- The process is self-reinforcing: visibly developed clusters attract more ideas and provide more possible connections. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p124]
- When questions arise from the slip-box, they are tried and tested among dozens or hundreds of other possible questions. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p124]
- Good questions are in the sweet spot of being relevant and interesting, not too easy but possible to tackle with available material. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p124]
- Working with the slip-box means we don't need to brainstorm—we can look into our slip-box where ideas have already proved themselves worthy. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p124]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bottom-up development increases openness to new ideas because familiarity with existing ideas enables recognition of their limitations.
