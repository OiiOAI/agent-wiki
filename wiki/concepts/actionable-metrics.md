# Actionable Metrics

## Summary

Actionable metrics demonstrate clear cause and effect, allowing people to learn from their actions. They are contrasted with vanity metrics and must meet three criteria: actionable (demonstrating cause and effect), accessible (understood by all employees), and auditable (credible to employees who can verify the data).

## Key facts

- For a report to be actionable, it must demonstrate clear cause and effect; otherwise it is a vanity metric. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p134]
- Vanity metrics fail the actionable criterion; website hits cannot clearly indicate what actions would increase them. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p134]
- The three A's of metrics are actionable, accessible, and auditable. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p134]
- Cohort-based reports are the gold standard for accessibility because they deal with people and their actions rather than piles of data points. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p135]
- For auditability, employees must be able to test the data by hand in the messy real world by talking to customers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p137]
- At IMVU, reporting data and its infrastructure were considered part of the product itself, owned by the product development team, with reports available to any employee. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p136]
