# Principled Negotiation

## Summary

Principled negotiation is a method of negotiation that decides issues on their merits rather than through a haggling process focused on positions. It involves four principles: (1) separate the people from the problem, (2) focus on interests rather than positions, (3) invent options for mutual gain, and (4) insist on using objective criteria. The method is characterized as 'hard on the merits, soft on the people' and uses no tricks or posturing.

## Key facts

- Principled negotiation decides issues on their merits rather than through a haggling process focused on what each side says it will and won't do. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p6]
- The method looks for mutual gains wherever possible and insists that results be based on fair standards independent of the will of either side. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p6]
- Principled negotiation shows how to obtain what you are entitled to while still being decent, and protects against those who would take advantage of fairness. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p6]
- The method can be used whether there is one issue or several, two parties or many, and applies regardless of whether the other side is more experienced, a hard bargainer, or a friendly one. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p6-7]
- If the other side learns principled negotiation, it does not become more difficult to use—it becomes easier. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p7]
