# Stress and Neural Network Reconfiguration

## Summary

Acute stress triggers time-dependent shifts in large-scale brain networks, with stress-related neuromodulators causing rapid reallocation of neural resources toward the salience network (SN) at the expense of the executive control network (ECN). This biphasic-reciprocal model explains paradoxical findings in stress and cognition research.

## Key facts

- Acute stress triggers nearly immediate neuropeptidergic (corticotropin-releasing factor) and catecholaminergic (norepinephrine, dopamine) responses that generally subside not long after stressor termination. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p374]
- Corticosteroids released through HPA axis activation cross the blood-brain barrier but peak concentrations are not reached until at least 20 minutes after stressor onset. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p374]
- Acute stress shifts locus coeruleus-norepinephrine (LC-NE) activity from phasic mode (selective responsiveness to stimuli) to tonic mode (increased nonselective background activity). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p374]
- The salience network (SN) includes the anterior insula, amygdala, dorsal anterior cingulate cortex, hypothalamus, ventral striatum, and temporoparietal regions, integrating functions related to processing salient stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p373]
- The executive control network (ECN) involves dorsolateral PFC, dorsomedial PFC, and dorsal posterior parietal areas, supporting high-order cognitive functions like complex decision-making and working memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p377]
- Immediately after stress induction, DLPFC activity related to working memory is reduced, while amygdala response to salient stimuli is increased. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p377]
- Hydrocortisone administered 4 hours before testing enhanced working memory performance and DLPFC activation, suggesting genomic corticosteroid effects support executive function recovery. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p379]
- Stress enhances habitual behavior at the expense of goal-directed behavior, with beta-adrenergic blockade (propranolol) blocking stress-induced insensitivity to outcome devaluation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p377]

## Inferences

- Inference: Catecholamines and corticosteroids may actively reverse their effects during recovery to support adjustment of long-term goals, with corticosteroids contributing to normalization of stress response.
- Inference: Regional specificity of corticosteroid effects (enhanced PFC function but opposite effects in ventral hippocampus and amygdala) provides a mechanism for coordinated but opposite changes across large-scale networks.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The distinct roles of noradrenergic versus dopaminergic systems in mediating the central stress response in humans remain to be fully explored.
- Uncertain: Individual differences in stress sensitivity and pathological consequences may result from inability to constrain sympathetic activation by corticosteroids.
