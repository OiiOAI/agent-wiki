# Beating Phantasy

## Summary

A childhood sexual phantasy involving the image of a child being beaten, first emerging between ages 5-6. Freud identified three developmental phases in girls: an initial pre-genital phase expressing jealousy toward rivals; an unconscious masochistic phase representing the Oedipal sense of guilt; and a final conscious phase appearing sadistic but preserving masochistic libidinal satisfaction. The concept illuminates the relationship between perversion, masochism, and the Oedipus complex.

## Key facts

- The first phase of female beating phantasy involves the content 'My father is beating the child whom I hate,' expressing the child's jealousy toward siblings and representing a pre-genital, pre-sexual relationship to the situation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5382]
- The second phase ('I am being beaten by my father') is unconscious, masochistic, and carries both libidinal excitation finding outlet in masturbation, and the child's sense of guilt regarding Oedipal wishes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5383]
- The third phase presents 'I am probably looking on' while children (usually boys) are beaten, representing a substitution that preserves the libidinal charge of the repressed unconscious phantasy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5380]
- The phantasy is invariably cathected with high pleasure and leads to auto-erotic satisfaction, yet the real experience of witnessing beatings at school produces mixed feelings including repugnance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5379]

## Inferences

- Inference: The transition from the second to third phase involves repression of the masochistic content while preserving its libidinal energy, allowing the phantasy to become conscious again in disguised form.
- Inference: Freud views these infantile phantasies as potential foundations for adult perversions if they persist without being sublimated or transformed by reaction-formation.
