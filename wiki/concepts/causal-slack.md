# Causal slack

## Summary

Causal slack is the concept, articulated by philosopher and mathematician George Ellis, that physical indeterminacy creates 'slack' or freedom in physical systems, opening the door for top-down causation. Because low-level physical details and forces are not causally comprehensive - not sufficient to determine how a system will evolve - higher-level features can exert causal influence. This influence is exerted through contextual constraints: the way a system is organized can constrain lower-level components and functionally select among patterns. In living organisms, this organization embodies knowledge, beliefs, goals, and motivations - reasons for doing things.
