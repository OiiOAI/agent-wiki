# Rational Choice Theory

## Summary

Rational choice theory derives from neoclassical economics, utilitarianism, and game theory. It focuses on actors who are purposive with intentions and have preferences or values. Action is aimed at achieving objectives consistent with preference hierarchy. Major constraints include scarcity of resources and social institutions. Information availability affects choices. Aggregation mechanisms combine individual actions to produce social outcomes.

## Inferences

- Inference: Coleman's rational choice theory prioritizes the micro-to-macro linkage (how individual actions combine to produce system-level behavior) over macro-to-micro, macro-to-macro, and micro-to-micro linkages.
- Inference: Coleman's focus on corporate actors reflects his concern that in modern society, 'a large fraction of rights and resources, and therefore sovereignty, may reside in corporate actors.'
- Inference: Coleman's criticism of 'homo sociologicus' reflects his view that rational choice theory (homo economicus) better explains social phenomena.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text notes Coleman recognizes people do not always behave rationally but argues this makes little difference to theoretical predictions.
