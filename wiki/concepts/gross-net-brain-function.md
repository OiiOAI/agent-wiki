# Gross and Net Brain Function

## Summary

Deacon distinguishes between gross brain function (total computational capacity) and net brain function (computational capacity available for cognitive tasks after subtracting resources devoted to maintaining bodily functions). This distinction is crucial because larger brains must devote proportionally more resources to internal management and coordination, resulting in decreasing net efficiency with increasing size.

## Key facts

- Gross brain function is proportional to total brain tissue, but net brain function must subtract the fraction dedicated to body maintenance functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p163]
- Like muscles that must move themselves, brains face nonlinear constraints where larger brains must devote increasing proportions of their capacity to internal management. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p168]
- Larger brains gain greater gross computational capacity but with decreasing net computational efficiency per functional output. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p168]
- The proportion of neural computations devoted to input-output functions may decline with size, leaving larger brains progressively less efficient. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p168]
- Transfer of training to conditions where associative relationships are reversed correlates with gross brain size but not encephalization. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p172]
