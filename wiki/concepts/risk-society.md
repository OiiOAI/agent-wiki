# Risk Society

## Summary

The risk society is Ulrich Beck's concept describing a new phase of modernity characterized by the central importance of manufactured risks. While classical modernity focused on the distribution of wealth, advanced modernity centers on the distribution and management of risks produced by modernity itself.

## Key facts

- Beck argues that modernization dissolved the structure of feudal society in the nineteenth century and produced industrial society, and that modernization today is dissolving industrial society. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p460]
- In the risk society, the central issue is risk and how it can be prevented, minimized, or channeled, rather than wealth distribution. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p460]
- In classical modernity the ideal was equality, while in advanced modernity it is safety. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p460]
- The risks are, to a large degree, being produced by the sources of wealth in modern society, especially industry and its side effects. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p460]
- Beck notes that wealth accumulates at the top while risks accumulate at the bottom, meaning risks strengthen rather than abolish class society. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p460]
- Beck discusses the 'boomerang effect' whereby the side effects of risk strike back even at the centers of their production. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p461]

## Inferences

- Inference: The risk society thesis represents a transformation in the object of sociological analysis from distribution problems (classical modernity) to production problems (modernity's self-created risks).
- Inference: Advanced modernization has generated both unprecedented risks and unprecedented efforts to deal with those risks.
