# Neuropharmacology

## Summary

Neuropharmacology studies how drugs affect neural signaling. Drugs can increase or decrease neurotransmitter signaling through various mechanisms including influencing synthesis, receptor binding, reuptake, or degradation.

## Key facts

- Neuroactive and psychoactive drugs broadly fall into two categories: those that increase signaling across a synapse and those that decrease it. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p709]
- Strategies to increase signaling include: stimulating synthesis via precursor administration (L-DOPA for Parkinson's), administering synthetic neurotransmitter mimics (psilocybin), inhibiting degradative enzymes, and inhibiting reuptake pumps (Prozac as an SSRI). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p709]
- Curare blocks acetylcholine receptors, preventing diaphragm contraction and stopping breathing. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p710]
