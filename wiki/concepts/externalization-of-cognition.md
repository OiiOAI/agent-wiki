# Externalization of Cognition

## Summary

Externalization refers to the process by which humans convert internal thoughts into physical artifacts, language, writing, and technology. This process enables knowledge sharing across individuals and generations, fundamentally extending human cognitive capabilities beyond what individual brains can achieve alone.

## Key facts

- The ability to read the unique minds of other individuals through externalized objects, symbols, texts, and institutions gave rise to the cognitive revolution phase of human evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p227]
- Externalization of brain function enables both broadcasting and copying of complex ideas, intentions, emotions, and hopes from brain to brain without the need for immortality of the ideas' creator. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p228]
- Artifacts and words can readily communicate semantic information from one brain to many without laborious episodic explorations by each individual. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p225]
- After just 6 months of literacy training, several areas of the brain reconfigures, including the thalamus and even the brainstem, demonstrating that externalization affects brain structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p227]
- Conceiving a multiple-component tool, such as a rock tied to a rod to make a hammer, requires a hierarchical algorithm, a natural precursor for language syntax. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p225]
- The cognitive evolution of the human race started with tool-making and language and transitioned through an Urban Revolution to the present Information Age. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p240]

## Inferences

- Inference: Externalization is not merely a product of cognition but actively shapes and restructures the brain itself, suggesting a feedback loop between brain evolution and cultural development.
