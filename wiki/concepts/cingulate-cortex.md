# Cingulate Cortex

## Summary

The cingulate cortex is a large region of cerebral cortex located near the brain's midline, divided into anterior (areas 24, 25, 32, 33) and posterior (areas 23, 31, 29, 30) sectors. It is a massively somatosensory structure receiving internal milieu and visceral signals, yet also involved in motor control, attention, emotion, and consciousness. As a second-order mapping structure, the cingulate participates in generating the imaged account of the organism-object relationship that underlies core consciousness.

## Key facts

- The cingulate cortex receives inputs from all divisions of the somatosensory system, including internal milieu, visceral, and musculoskeletal signals, while also being involved in motor execution for vocalization, limb movements, and viscera. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p262]
- Bilateral anterior cingulate lesions cause akinetic mutism—suspended animation with impaired consciousness and extended consciousness while preserving wakefulness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p262]
- Both anterior and posterior cingulate independently assemble second-order maps, with the posterior sector possibly being most indispensable for consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p181]
- Bilateral lesions to medial parietal regions behind the posterior cingulate (areas 31, 7, 19) also cause profound consciousness disturbances comparable to bilateral cingulate damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p263]
- The cingulate cortex qualifies as a second-order structure because its subregions and massive somatosensory inputs can give rise to an integrated view of the entire body state. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p265]
- The cingulate cortex receives signals from main sensory channels via both thalamic projections and direct projections from higher-order cortices in inferotemporal, polar temporal, and lateral parietal regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p265]
- PET scans of newborn brains show remarkable activity in the cingulate cortices, matching the structures needed for proto-self and second-order maps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The functional overlap in the cingulate—sensory, motor, attention, emotion, consciousness—mirrors the overlap seen in the upper brain stem, suggesting a continuity of functions related to organism representation.
- Inference: The early functional maturity of the cingulate at birth suggests it plays a foundational role in establishing consciousness from the start of life.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The intrinsic anatomy and many connections of the cingulate remain largely uncharted territory.
- Uncertain: No case of bilateral lesion involving all cingulate cytoarchitectonic regions has been described.
