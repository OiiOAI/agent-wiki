# The Turkey Problem

## Summary

A metaphor illustrating how fragile systems become blind to accumulating risks through prolonged stability. A turkey fed for 1000 days 'confirms' through accumulated evidence that the butcher loves it—until Thanksgiving. The problem represents the fundamental error of mistaking absence of evidence of harm for evidence of absence of harm, leading to maximum confidence precisely when catastrophe becomes imminent.

## Key facts

- The turkey is fed for a thousand days, with every day confirming to its analysts that butchers love turkeys with increased statistical confidence—the butcher will keep feeding until a few days before Thanksgiving. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p128]
- The key error is mistaking absence of evidence (of harm) for evidence of absence—a mistake Taleb identifies as prevalent in intellectual circles and grounded in social sciences. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p129]
- The mission 'how not to be a turkey' requires distinguishing true stability from manufactured stability. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p129]
- The removal of the Baath Party and toppling of Saddam Hussein in 2003 exemplifies constrained volatility exploding: over 100,000 deaths, and a decade later the region remained unstable. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: The turkey problem explains why financial crises appear to come as 'surprises' despite being predictable in retrospect—the system was becoming increasingly fragile but the fragility was invisible during the stable period.
