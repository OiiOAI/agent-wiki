# Consensual Nonmonogamy

## Summary

Consensual nonmonogamy refers to negotiated agreements between partners that permit sexual or emotional connections with others outside the primary relationship. Perel examines various forms including open marriages, swinging, and polyamory, exploring why people choose these arrangements, how they navigate jealousy, and the challenges of maintaining transparency and trust.

## Key facts

- Consensual nonmonogamy means both partners have equal say in the decision to take unfulfilled hankerings elsewhere, unlike infidelity which is described as a unilateral decision. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p191]
- Polyamory emphasizes creating meaningful connections with multiple partners, in contrast with casual hookups; practitioners characterize it as involving mindfulness, maturity, and substantial communication. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p226]
- The term 'polyamory' entered The Oxford English Dictionary in 2006. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p226]
- Successful nonmonogamy requires equal footing and trust; both parties need to feel they are choosing from a position of parity. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p224]
- Perel argues that boundaries in nonmonogamous relationships differ from borders: 'Boundaries are what is right at the time, for particular people involved in a particular situation,' whereas borders 'claim the unquestionable and rigid authority of law.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p223]
