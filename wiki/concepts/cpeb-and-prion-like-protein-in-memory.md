# CPEB and Prion-Like Protein in Memory

## Summary

CPEB (cytoplasmic polyadenylation element binding protein) is a neuronal isoform that functions as a self-perpetuating switch regulating local protein synthesis at synapses during long-term memory maintenance. Unlike pathological prions, CPEB exists in two conformational states—a soluble inactive monomer and an aggregated active form—with the active state capable of recruiting monomers to join the aggregate and activating translation of dormant mRNAs. This prion-like property may explain how memory can persist for days to weeks despite the short half-life of most proteins.

## Key facts

- Aplysia CPEB exists in two conformational states: a soluble inactive form and an aggregated active form capable of self-perpetuation by recruiting soluble monomers into aggregates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1528]
- In response to serotonin, local synthesis of CPEB increases until a threshold concentration is reached that switches CPEB to its aggregated, active conformation, which then activates translation of dormant mRNAs at individual synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1528]
- Blocking CPEB locally at an activated synapse blocks the long-term maintenance of synaptic facilitation beyond 24 hours but not its initiation or early maintenance, indicating CPEB is specifically required for the late maintenance phase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1528]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery that Drosophila CPEB with a glutamine-rich prion-like domain is required for long-term courtship memory suggests that prion-like mechanisms for memory maintenance are conserved across species.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise molecular events that convert CPEB from its soluble to its aggregated state in response to serotonin remain to be fully specified.
