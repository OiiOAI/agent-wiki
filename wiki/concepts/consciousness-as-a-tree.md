# Consciousness as a Tree

## Summary

Jaak Panksepp's model of consciousness as a process that begins in the midbrain (the peri-aqueductal grey matter) and 'migrates' upwards through higher brain regions like the cingulate, temporal and frontal cortex, transforming as it travels, until it becomes high-level cognitive awareness in the frontal cortices. McGilchrist adopts this 'tree' metaphor to contrast with the prevailing view of consciousness as a detached 'bird' alighting on the brain.

## Key facts

- Panksepp sees consciousness as beginning very deep in the midbrain, specifically in the peri-aqueductal grey matter, and 'migrating' through higher brain regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p312]
- Panksepp describes consciousness as transforming as it travels upwards through the brain, through the 'cerebral canopy' until it becomes high-level cognitive awareness in the frontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p312]
- Panksepp rejects the view that consciousness arises from sensory-perceptual imagery, writing that consciousness 'is deeply enmeshed with the brain mechanisms that automatically promote action readiness'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p313]
- Consciousness is ultimately affective in nature and founded on 'motor processes that generate self-consciousness by being closely linked to body image representations'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p313]

## Inferences

- Inference: This model explains why split-brain patients do not experience fragmentation of the self: integration does not need to be achieved because consciousness is unified at low levels below the hemispheric divide.
- Inference: The 'fundamental sense of self' lies at a level below the hemispheric divide, with which each cognitively aware hemisphere at the highest level remains in touch.
