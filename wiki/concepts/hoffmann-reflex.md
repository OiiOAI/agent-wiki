# Hoffmann Reflex

## Summary

The Hoffmann reflex (H-reflex) is an electrically evoked analogue of the stretch reflex used to study monosynaptic reflex pathways in humans. It is elicited by electrically stimulating Ia sensory fibers in a peripheral nerve and recording the resulting EMG response in the homonymous muscle. The H-reflex has a longer latency than the direct M-wave because it involves central synaptic transmission.

## Key facts

- The H-reflex is evoked by electrically stimulating Ia sensory fibers from muscle spindles in a peripheral nerve and recording the reflex EMG response in the homonymous muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p768]
- At low stimulus strengths, a pure H-reflex is evoked because Ia sensory fiber threshold is lower than motor axon threshold. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p768]
- At intermediate stimulus strengths, both an M-wave (direct motor axon activation) and H-wave (reflex) appear, with the H-wave occurring later. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p768]
- The H-reflex is most easily measured in the soleus muscle by stimulating the tibial nerve. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p768]
