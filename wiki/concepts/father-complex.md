# Father Complex

## Summary

The father complex, central to the Rat Man's obsessional neurosis, refers to the constellation of ambivalent feelings—tender and hostile—directed toward the father. Freud demonstrated how the patient's symptoms expressed a conflict between obedience to his father's authority and his own amatory desires, with the father's influence persisting even after death.

## Key facts

- The patient declared he had been his father's best friend and his father had been his, except on one subject about which fathers and sons usually hold aloof. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3301]
- The patient's father, before marriage, had been a non-commissioned officer with a straightforward soldierly manner, hearty sense of humour, and kindly tolerance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3315]
- The patient had in childhood wished for his father's death so that a little girl might be kind to him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3299]
- The father's death occurred during the patient's absence, which became a source of intense self-reproach. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3296]
- The patient's flight into illness was made possible by his identifying himself with his father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3314]
