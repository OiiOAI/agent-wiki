# Real problem approach

## Summary

The 'real problem' approach to consciousness, as articulated by Anil Seth, accepts that consciousness exists and then asks how the various phenomenological properties of consciousness—how conscious experiences are structured and what form they take—relate to properties of brains that are embodied in bodies and embedded in worlds. Rather than attempting to solve the hard problem head-on or sidelining experiential qualities, this approach offers genuine hope of reconciling the physical with the phenomenal by dissolving, not solving, the hard problem.

## Key facts

- The real problem approach accepts that consciousness exists and asks how phenomenological properties relate to brain mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
- The approach aims to build explanatory bridges between mechanism and phenomenology that are not arbitrary but make sense. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
- The 'real problem' strategy echoes how scientific understanding of life transcended vitalism by individuating properties of living systems and accounting for each in terms of underlying mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
- The real problem approach dissolves rather than solves the hard problem of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
