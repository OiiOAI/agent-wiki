# Cephalization

## Summary

Cephalization is the evolutionary process of concentrating nervous tissue and sensory organs at the anterior (front) end of an animal. This occurs because the forward end encounters new environmental stimuli more frequently than other body parts. The leading end develops specialized sensory systems (olfaction, sight) and motor structures (jaws), ultimately giving rise to a head containing a brain protected by the skull. This process is recapitulated in both ontogeny (embryonic development) and phylogeny (evolutionary history).

## Key facts

- Cephalization is the evolutionary concentration of the nervous system at the front end of animals, where the leading end encounters whatever is new in the environment more often than other body parts. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p67]
- The process of embedding motricity involves pulling properties from outside and immediately inside through intrinsic oscillatory properties and electrical coupling, moving them up the neuraxis into brain encephalization. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p67]
- Cephalization is seen not only in phylogenetic development but also in ontogenetic development, as motor neurons become electrotonically decoupled as upper parts of the system make synaptic connections.
