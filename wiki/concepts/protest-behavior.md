# Protest Behavior

## Summary

Protest behavior consists of actions aimed at reestablishing contact with a partner and getting their attention when the attachment system is activated. It includes excessive attempts at contact, withdrawing, keeping score, acting hostile, threatening to leave, manipulations, and making a partner feel jealous.

## Key facts

- Protest behavior is any action that tries to reestablish contact with your partner and get their attention. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p75]
- Protest behavior includes excessive attempts to reestablish contact such as calling, texting, or e-mailing many times. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p75]
- Withdrawing behaviors include sitting silently, literally turning your back on your partner, or ignoring them. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p75]
- Keeping score means paying attention to how long it took for a return call and waiting equally long to return theirs. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p75]
- People with anxious attachment are particularly susceptible to protest behavior and chronically activated attachment systems. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p76]
