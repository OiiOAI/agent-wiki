# The Unconscious

## Summary

The unconscious refers to psychical processes that behave actively but do not reach consciousness. Freud emphasizes that the unconscious is not a philosophical dispute but a descriptive term for mental phenomena that are intense and productive yet remain apart from consciousness. Dreams and delusions arise from the same source—the unconscious. The unconscious knowledge possessed by the dreamer enables correct judgement that is misunderstood by consciousness.

## Key facts

- The unconscious is characterized by psychical processes which behave actively but do not reach the consciousness of the person concerned. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2938]
- Dreams and delusions arise from the same source—from what is repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2949]
- Dreams are described as the physiological delusions of normal people. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2949]
- The unconscious comprises acts which are merely latent (temporarily unconscious) and processes such as repressed ones which if they became conscious would stand in crude contrast to other conscious processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4546]
- In the unconscious there are only contents, cathected with greater or lesser strength; there is no negation, no doubt, no degrees of certainty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4558]
- Unconscious processes are timeless; they are not ordered temporally, are not altered by the passage of time, and have no reference to time at all. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4559]
- Unconscious processes are subject to the pleasure principle and their fate depends on how strong they are and on whether they fulfil the demands of the pleasure-unpleasure regulation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4559]
- The unconscious can only become cognizable through processes being lowered to an earlier stage by regression, as in dreaming and neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4560]
- The unconscious is not only the psychically repressed but also some impulses dominating the ego, forming the strongest functional antithesis to the repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4565]
- The most important distinction lies not between the conscious and the preconscious, but between the preconscious and the unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4565]
- A conscious presentation comprises the presentation of the thing plus the presentation of the word belonging to it, while an unconscious presentation is the presentation of the thing alone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4571]
- The system Ucs. contains the thing cathexes of the objects, the first and true object cathexes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4571]
- In schizophrenia, object cathexes are given up while the cathexis of word presentations is retained. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4571]
- The content of the Ucs. may be compared with an aboriginal population in the mind, with inherited mental formations constituting the nucleus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4566]
- Unconscious is no longer merely the name of what is latent at the moment but a particular realm of the mind with its own wishful impulses and mode of expression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5014]
- The latent dream thoughts discovered by interpreting dreams are not themselves part of this unconscious realm; they are thoughts that could have been conscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5014]
- A dream is constructed from day's residues combined with something from the unconscious realm—a repressed wishful impulse—which together determine dream formation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5014]
- The sense of symptoms is unknown to the patient and analysis regularly shows that symptoms are derivatives of unconscious processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p247]

## Inferences

- Inference: The censorship between Ucs. and Pcs. is the first line of defense, while the censorship between Pcs. and Cs. is the second, suggesting a layered structure of mental resistance.
- Inference: The Ucs. of one human being can react upon that of another without passing through consciousness, indicating direct unconscious communication between individuals.
- Inference: Freud anticipates the need to distinguish between two kinds of unconscious by different names, preparing the theoretical distinction that would later be elaborated in structural theory.
- Inference: The unconscious wish serves as the 'capitalist' providing psychical energy, while day's residues act as the 'entrepreneur' deciding how outlay is employed.
- Inference: Freud's discovery of the unconscious represents a 'third and most wounding blow' to human megalomania, following Copernicus (earth not center of universe) and Darwin (descent from animals).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanism by which the preconscious day residues are reinforced by unconscious drive impulses remains somewhat obscure.
- Uncertain: The precise nature of the inherited mental formations in the Ucs. is not fully elaborated.
- Uncertain: Freud indicates that full understanding of these distinctions requires investigation of neurotic symptoms, which will be undertaken later.
