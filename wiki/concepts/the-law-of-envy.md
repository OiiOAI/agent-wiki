# The Law of Envy

## Summary

The Law of Envy describes how humans are naturally compelled to compare themselves with one another, and how this can turn into deep envy—feelings of inferiority and frustration that lead to covert attacks and sabotage. Envy is particularly dangerous because it is rarely expressed directly and is quickly disguised to oneself as indignation or resentment. Envy occurs most commonly and painfully among friends, who can become 'fatal friends' capable of inflicting deep psychological damage through years of covert hostility disguised as friendship.

## Key facts

- Humans are naturally compelled to compare themselves with one another, measuring status, levels of respect, and attention received. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p322]
- Envy is disguised almost as soon as it is felt—it is reframed as unfairness, resentment, or anger rather than admission of inferiority. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p336]
- Envy occurs most commonly and painfully among friends, who have closer access and can discover weak points to wound most effectively. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p333]
- Gore Vidal wrote: 'Every time a friend succeeds, I die a little.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p335]
