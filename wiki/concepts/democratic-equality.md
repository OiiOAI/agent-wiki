# Democratic Equality

## Summary

Democratic equality is Rawls's preferred interpretation of the two principles of justice, combining the principle of fair equality of opportunity with the difference principle. It goes beyond both the system of natural liberty (which merely requires formal equality of opportunity) and the liberal interpretation (which adds fair equality of opportunity but still permits distribution to be determined by natural talents). Democratic equality recognizes that natural and social contingencies are equally arbitrary from a moral standpoint and seeks to mitigate the influence of both on distributive shares.

## Key facts

- Democratic equality combines the principle of fair equality of opportunity with the difference principle. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p77]
- Democratic equality corrects for the influence of natural talents on distributive shares, which the liberal interpretation still permits. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p76]
- Democratic equality is reached once we are troubled by the influence of both social contingencies and natural chance on the determination of distributive shares, since from a moral standpoint the two seem equally arbitrary. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p76]
- The liberal conception fails to correct for the natural distribution of abilities and talents, which is no less arbitrary from a moral perspective than social fortune. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p76]

## Inferences

- Inference: Democratic equality represents the most complete rejection of morally arbitrary factors influencing distributive shares, addressing both social and natural lotteries.
