# Neuron Doctrine

## Summary

The neuron doctrine is the principle that individual neurons are the elementary building blocks and signaling elements of the nervous system. It holds that neurons are discrete cells rather than part of a continuous syncytium, and that they communicate via specialized junctions called synapses.

## Key facts

- The neuron doctrine states that individual nerve cells (neurons) are the elementary building blocks and signaling elements of the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p9]
- Electron microscopy in the mid-1950s by Sanford Palay provided definitive evidence for synapses, demonstrating the neuron doctrine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p8]
- Ross Harrison showed that dendrites and axons grow from the cell body even when neurons are isolated in tissue culture, supporting the neuron doctrine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p8]
- The neuron doctrine holds that each neuron is a discrete cell with distinctive processes arising from its cell body, and that neurons are the signaling units of the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p105]
- Ramón y Cajal established the principle of dynamic polarization: electrical signals within a nerve cell flow from receptive sites (dendrites, cell body) to the trigger zone at the axon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p106]
- Ramón y Cajal established the principle of connectional specificity: nerve cells make specific connections with certain target cells and not with others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p106]
- The first principle states that the neuron is both the basic building block and the elementary signaling unit of the brain, with dendrites receiving signals and axons sending information. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p77]
- The second principle identifies synapses—sites where axon terminals of one neuron communicate with dendrites of another—characterized by the synaptic cleft, a small gap. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p78]
- The third principle is connection specificity: neurons do not form connections indiscriminately but communicate with specific target cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p78]
- The fourth principle is dynamic polarization: signals in neural circuits travel in only one direction, from dendrites to cell body to axon to synaptic terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p78]
- Golgi opposed the neuron doctrine throughout his life, believing nerve cells form a continuous nerve net rather than discrete units. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p80]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neuron doctrine laid the foundation for understanding neural circuitry and how information flows through the nervous system.
