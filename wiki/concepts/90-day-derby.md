# 90-Day Derby

## Summary

The 90-Day Derby refers to the cycle of consensus quarterly earnings estimates, corporate guidance about earnings, and intense attention paid to these numbers by Wall Street and corporate leadership. The IBES system began collecting estimates of future annual earnings in 1976, with estimates shortening from annual to quarterly. CEOs caught up in this cycle spend time preparing guidance and trying to make earnings predictable. The problem is that current earnings harvest past investments and actions, while current investments may be key to future harvests. The pressure induces wasteful decisions such as cutting R&D or making expensive acquisitions to avoid reporting lower earnings. The text recommends building a clientele of long-term investors and using longer vesting periods for executive stock compensation.

## Key facts

- The Institutional Brokers' Estimate System (IBES) began collecting estimates of future annual earnings of US corporations in 1976, with estimates shortening from annual to quarterly. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p235]
- Current earnings are the harvest of investments and actions taken in the past, not simply the result of current managers' hard work. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p237]
- Amazon has never paid a dividend since its IPO in 1997, yet its stock price has grown greatly, showing that value depends on the future, not current earnings. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p237]
- Buffett and Dimon argued that quarterly earnings guidance is a major driver of short-termism and companies frequently hold back on technology spending, hiring, and R&D to meet quarterly forecasts. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p238]
- Zotich cut R&D staff to boost short-term earnings, and over two years its stock price fell by 60 percent as competitors ramped up R&D. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p239]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 90-Day Derby distracts management from strategic concerns that actually affect long-term company value.
