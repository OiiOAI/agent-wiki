# Counterfactuals

## Summary

Counterfactuals are causal reasoning constructs that address 'what-if' questions about scenarios that did not or could not occur. They are essential for understanding individual-level causation, moral behavior, and scientific explanation, and form the building blocks of free will and social responsibility.

## Key facts

- Counterfactual reasoning deals with questions like 'Would Joe have lived had he not taken the drug?'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L91]
- Empirical observation can never confirm or refute counterfactual answers because they concern unobserved scenarios. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L94]
- Two people who share the same causal model will also share all counterfactual judgments. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L96]
- Counterfactuals are the building blocks of moral behavior as well as scientific thought. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L96]
- The 'algorithmization of counterfactuals' allows machines to participate in human-style retrospective thinking about alternative scenarios. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L97]
- Counterfactuals require comparing the observed world to a fictitious world where some observed fact is negated, enabling explanation-seeking inferences that distinguish human intelligence. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L380-L400]
- No experiment can deny treatment to an already treated person, so counterfactuals must be derived from causal models rather than observed data. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L380-L395]
- The laws of physics can be interpreted as counterfactual assertions, such as 'Had the weight on this spring doubled, its length would have doubled' (Hooke's law). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L400-L415]
- Counterfactuals enable learning from history and the experience of others—something no other species appears to do—providing flexibility and willingness to take responsibility. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L415-L430]
- The Lion Man sculpture (40,000 years old) represents the first known representation of an imaginary creature, emblematic of the capacity to reason about counterfactuals. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L415-L430]
- Counterfactuals lie on the third rung of the Ladder of Causation, above interventions (rung two) and observation (rung one). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2415-L2425]
- David Lewis proposed evaluating counterfactuals by comparing the actual world to the most similar possible world where the counterfactual is true. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2445-L2460]
- Thucydides' account of a tsunami demonstrates necessary causation: 'but for the earthquake, the tsunami could not have occurred.'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2428-L2440]
- Hume's counterfactual definition of causation: 'if the first object had not been, the second never had existed.'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2440-L2445]

## Inferences

- Inference: Counterfactual queries are the most challenging for AI systems, which is why Pearl emphasizes them as core to achieving human-level causal reasoning in machines.
- Inference: The ability to imagine counterfactuals was as profound for human evolution as anatomical changes, enabling survival, adaptation, and eventual domination of the planet within 10,000 years of the Lion Man's creation.
- Inference: Counterfactuals enable reasoning about individual-level causation and personal responsibility, which aggregate causal effects cannot address.
