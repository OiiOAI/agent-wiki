# Totemism

## Summary

Totemism is a social and religious system in which each clan is named after and identified with a particular totem (usually an animal, more rarely a plant or natural phenomenon). The totem serves as the clan's common ancestor, guardian spirit, and is protected by prohibitions against killing or consuming it.

## Key facts

- A totem is usually an animal (edible and harmless or dangerous and feared) and more rarely a plant or natural phenomenon, which stands in a peculiar relation to the whole clan as their common ancestor and guardian spirit. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4121]
- Clansmen are under a sacred obligation not to kill or destroy their totem and to avoid eating its flesh or deriving benefit from it in other ways, though the totemic character is inherent in all individuals of a given class, not just one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4121]
- In almost every place where totems are found, there is also a law against persons of the same totem having sexual relations and marrying—this is 'exogamy', an institution related to totemism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4122]
- The two basic laws of totemism (not killing the totem animal and avoiding sexual intercourse with totem clan members) are regarded by Freud as the oldest and most powerful human desires, connected to the nuclear complex of neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4147]
- A totem is a class of material objects which a savage regards with superstitious respect, believing that there exists between him and every member of the class an intimate and altogether special relation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4207]
- The essential characteristics of totemism are: totems were originally animals regarded as ancestors of clans; inheritance was through the female line; there was prohibition against killing the totem; and members of a totem clan were forbidden sexual intercourse with one another. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4210]
- Exogamy (prohibition against incest within the totem clan) is connected with the taboo nature of the totem itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4221]
- The horror of incest cannot be explained by an innate instinct against it, as psychoanalysis shows that earliest sexual excitations are of an incestuous character. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4224]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud connects totemism to the Oedipus complex by implying that the prohibitions against incest and patricide (killing the totem/father figure) have their origins in unconscious childhood desires.
- Inference: Freud argues that totemism corresponds to the narcissistic stage of libidinal development, both chronologically and in its content.
- Inference: The two principal ordinances of totemism—not killing the totem and not having sexual relations within the totem—coincide with the two crimes of Oedipus (killing the father, marrying the mother).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud notes that the facts of totemism are scarcely capable of being expressed in general terms, with scarcely a statement that does not call for exceptions or contradictions, given the various stages of decay and disintegration found among different races.
- Uncertain: The exact chronological relationship between totemism and exogamy remains disputed among authorities.
