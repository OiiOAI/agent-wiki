# Protostome-Deuterostome Split

## Summary

The fundamental division of bilaterally symmetric animals based on embryological development: protostomes (mouth-first) where the first blastopore becomes the mouth, versus deuterostomes (mouth-second) where it becomes the anus, with deuterostomes including the ancestors of vertebrates.

## Key facts

- In protostomes, the first opening of the blastula becomes the mouth end of the digestive tract (meaning 'mouth first'); the vast majority of invertebrates are protostomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p152]
- In deuterostomes, the first opening becomes the anus ('mouth second'), and among these are the invertebrate ancestors of vertebrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p152]
- Protostomes include insects, arachnids, crustaceans, mollusks, and various worms; deuterostomes include echinoderms (starfish), hemichordates (acorn worms), and chordates (including vertebrates). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p152]
- By 580 million years ago, both protostomes and deuterostomes had appeared, with their common ancestor (PDA) coming after the LCBA but before the split. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p153]
- Research on fruit flies, worms, sea slugs, and other protostomes can reveal important information relevant to humans because both groups inherited key survival genes from their common ancestor. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p153]
