# Pattern Separation and Pattern Completion

## Summary

Pattern separation and pattern completion are complementary processes in the hippocampus that allow distinct memories to be stored without interference (separation) and incomplete cues to retrieve complete memories (completion). The dentate gyrus supports pattern separation while CA3 recurrent collaterals support pattern completion.

## Key facts

- The dentate gyrus is important for pattern separation—the ability to store distinct representations of similar experiences without interference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1406]
- The CA3 region is important for pattern completion—the ability to retrieve a complete memory from partial cues, based on strong recurrent excitatory connections between CA3 pyramidal cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1407]
- Marr proposed that pattern completion in CA3 occurs because stored patterns in recurrent connections can be reactivated by a subset of the original activity pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1407]
- Lesions or genetic manipulations targeting the dentate gyrus impair the ability to discriminate between similar locations and contexts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1406]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mice with NMDA receptors deleted from CA3 neurons show normal spatial memory with full cues but impaired memory when given fewer spatial cues, supporting the role of CA3 recurrent collaterals in pattern completion.
