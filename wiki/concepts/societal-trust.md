# Societal Trust: The Principle of Contribution

## Summary

Societal Trust is established through contribution—the intent to create value, give back, and be a responsible global citizen. It is becoming both a social and economic necessity as organizations recognize that doing good is part of business itself, not separate from it.

## Key facts

- Societal trust is based on the principle of contribution: creating value instead of destroying it, giving back instead of taking. Trust is essential to a flat or open society according to Thomas Friedman. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p329]
- During the 1992 Los Angeles riots after the Rodney King verdict, all McDonald's restaurants in the devastated area remained untouched due to community perception that McDonald's cared about the community through literacy efforts and sports programs. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p327]
- The Giving Pledge, established by Bill Gates and Warren Buffett, encourages billionaires to give away most of their wealth, with Buffett contributing $30.7 billion to the Gates Foundation. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p330]
- Muhammad Yunus and Grameen Bank received the Nobel Peace Prize for microcredit work helping lift tens of millions out of poverty; 96% of borrowers are women. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p332]
- Call me, a Danish mobile carrier, built societal trust through a 'Speak Kindly' campaign and film studios project to encourage civility, resulting in 50% subscriber growth in a declining market and becoming Denmark's fastest growing mobile brand. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p333]
- Patricia Aburdene's Megatrends 2010 showed 'stakeholder superstars' (companies excelling with all stakeholders) outperformed the S&P 500 by 126% between 1984 and 1999. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p336]
- Global citizenship is described as an economic necessity; companies need meaning and purpose beyond profits, and corporate social responsibility will eventually become a price of entry. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p337]
