# The Chihuahua Fallacy

## Summary

The Chihuahua Fallacy is a thought experiment demonstrating that encephalization differences can result from body size changes without any corresponding brain evolution. Small dog breeds appear hyper-encephalized while large breeds appear hypo-encephalized, yet this variation does not reflect differences in cognitive ability. The fallacy illustrates how naive interpretations of brain/body ratios can lead to incorrect conclusions about brain evolution.

## Key facts

- Small dogs have comparatively high brain/body ratios and large dogs comparatively low ratios because brain size is far less variable than body size between dog breeds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p177]
- Chihuahuas appear hyper-encephalized compared to typical mammals despite having typical dog brains with typical dog abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p178]
- Domesticated species generally have smaller comparative brain sizes than their feral relatives, reflecting selection for body traits rather than cognitive traits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p179]
- Scientists studying brain evolution have 'consistently taken this same leap of faith' as would occur if only chihuahuas had survived, assuming brain evolution where there is none. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: The lesson of the Chihuahua Fallacy is that we cannot assume brain evolution has occurred without independent evidence that brain traits rather than body traits were the targets of selection.
