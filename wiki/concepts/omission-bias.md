# Omission Bias

## Summary

Omission bias is the tendency to prefer inaction over action even when both lead to equally bad outcomes. We rate passive harm (failing to call for help) as less horrible than active harm (pushing someone into a ravine), though both result in death.

## Key facts

- In omission bias, we rate passive harm as less horrible than active harm even though both lead to death. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p105]
- Active euthanasia, even if it is the explicit wish of the dying, is punishable by law, whereas deliberate refusal of life-saving measures is legal. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p105]
- The omission bias lies behind parents not vaccinating children: deliberate inaction somehow seems less grave than comparable action like intentionally infecting them. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p106]
