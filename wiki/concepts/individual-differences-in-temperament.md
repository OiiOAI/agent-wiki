# Individual Differences in Temperament

## Summary

Inherent variations among individuals in psychological characteristics such as vitality, proactivity, and temperament present from birth, which influence development and interact with environmental factors to shape autonomous functioning.

## Key facts

- Children are born with individual differences on psychological as well as physical variables. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188]
- Considerable research has documented that there are innate differences in temperament; some infants are personable and happy while others are withdrawn and irritable. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188]
- Some infants are vital and active while others are anergic and passive, and these qualities of vitality and proactivity are related to intrinsic motivation. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188]
- More vital and proactive children have a better start in developing an autonomous self-assured presence. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188]
- Children who are personable, vital, and proactive are likely to draw the best out of their caregivers and elicit greater involvement and autonomy support. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: Children's innate characteristics not only affect their behavior and development directly, but also affect the social environment that in turn affects the children's development.
