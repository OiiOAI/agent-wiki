# Language

## Summary

Language is uniquely human and arguably our greatest skill. It has multiple functional levels: phonemes (basic sound units), morphemes (smallest meaningful units), words, and sentences governed by syntax. Language acquisition follows universal patterns in all children, with a sensitive period in the first year of life. Language processing involves distributed neural networks including Broca's area, Wernicke's area, and additional regions in the left hemisphere.

## Key facts

- Language is defined by a finite set of sounds (phonemes) that can be combined with infinite possibilities to create semantic units called morphemes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1405]
- Morphemes are the smallest meaningful units of a language, including prefixes and suffixes that alter word meaning or grammar. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1405]
- Syntax specifies word and phrase order for a given language; English uses subject-verb-object order while Japanese typically uses subject-object-verb. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1405-1406]
- Language acquisition in children follows a universal pattern regardless of culture, with infants initially exhibiting universal patterns of speech perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1407]
- The left hemisphere is dominant for language in more than 95% of individuals, controlling grammar, lexicon, phonemic assembly, and phonetic production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1411]

## Inferences

- Inference: The universal pattern of language acquisition across cultures suggests strong biological constraints on how humans learn language.
- Inference: The left hemisphere dominance for language applies even to signed languages, indicating the specialization is for language processing rather than speech per se.
