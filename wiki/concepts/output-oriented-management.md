# Output-Oriented Management

## Summary

Output-oriented management is one of the three core ideas of High Output Management. It applies the principles and discipline of manufacturing—particularly production management—to all forms of business enterprise, including managerial work. All employees 'produce' in some sense, and understanding production principles provides a systematic way to manage any type of work.

## Key facts

- The first basic idea of the book is an output-oriented approach to management that applies production principles to all business activities. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L265-L267]
- All employees 'produce' in some sense—some make chips, others prepare bills, while still others create software designs or advertising copy. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L277-L279]
- The output of a manager is the output of the organizational units under his or her supervision or influence. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L257-L258]
- There are only two ways a manager can impact an employee's output: through motivation and through training. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L260-L261]

## Inferences

- Inference: Output-oriented thinking helps distinguish between being busy and creating value, shifting focus from activities to results.
- Inference: The concept applies the rigor of financial analysis to all types of work, making different activities comparable through their outputs.
