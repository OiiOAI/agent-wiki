# Threats vs. Intimidations

## Summary

A critical conceptual distinction in threat assessment: threats are unconditional statements of intent to harm without conditions or alternatives, while intimidations are conditional statements that offer a way to avert harm (containing words like 'if,' 'else,' 'unless,' or 'until').

## Key facts

- A threat is a statement of an intention to do some harm, period. It offers no conditions, no alternatives, no ways out. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p77]
- Intimidations are statements of conditions to be met in order to avert a harm, such as 'I will burn this building down if I don't get the promotion.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p77]
- Threats carry more likelihood of violence than intimidations because no conditions are offered, usually because the speaker sees few alternatives. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p77]
- Threats that are end-game moves introduced late in a controversy are more serious than those used early, as early threats likely represent emotional response rather than a decision to use violence. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p77]
- Threats are rarely spoken from a position of power; whatever power they have is derived from the fear they instill in the victim. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p78]
