# Life Narratives

## Summary

Life narratives are the stories people create to make sense of their lives, described by psychologist Dan McAdams as the third level of personality. These narratives are simplified and selective reconstructions of the past, often connected to an idealized vision of the future. Haidt uses this concept to explain how dispositional traits and characteristic adaptations bridge into adult political identity through the life stories people tell themselves.

## Key facts

- Life narratives are not necessarily true stories—they are simplified and selective reconstructions of the past, often connected to an idealized vision of the future. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p304]
- Even though life narratives are to some degree post hoc fabrications, they still influence people's behavior, relationships, and mental health. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p304]
- Life narratives are saturated with morality—when asked to account for the development of their religious faith and moral beliefs, conservatives underscored deep feelings about respect for authority, allegiance to one's group, and purity of the self, whereas liberals emphasized deep feelings regarding human suffering and social fairness. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p304]
