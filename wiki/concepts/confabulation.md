# Confabulation

## Summary

Confabulation is the tendency of the brain, when it cannot recall something, to make up something plausible that appears consistent to fill the gap rather than admit to a lack of understanding. The left hemisphere exhibits a strong tendency to confabulate, thinking it knows something it doesn't, and appears predisposed to repress negative emotions. The right hemisphere makes judgments about the truth or plausibility of narratives.

## Key facts

- In the presence of a right-sided lesion, the brain loses contextual information that would help it make sense of experience; the left hemisphere makes up a story, and, lacking insight, appears completely convinced by it. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p122]
- The left hemisphere exhibits a strong tendency to confabulate: it thinks it knows something, recognises something, which it doesn't, a tendency linked to its lack of ability to discriminate unique cases from the generalised categories into which it places them. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p122]
- Panksepp states that 'the linguistically proficient left hemisphere appears predisposed to repress negative emotions, and even chooses to confabulate.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p122]
- It is the right hemisphere that makes judgments about the truth or plausibility of narratives constructed by the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p122]
- In a split-brain experiment, the left hemisphere drew 'mistaken conclusions from the information available to it and lays down the law about what only the right hemisphere can know' yet 'did not offer its suggestion in a guessing vein but rather [as] a statement of fact'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p121]
