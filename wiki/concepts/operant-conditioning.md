# Operant Conditioning

## Summary

A learning mechanism where behavior is strengthened or weakened by consequences (rewards or punishments). Applied to business, it means maximizing positive outcomes from desired behaviors and preventing competing products from creating conflicting habits.

## Key facts

- For Coca-Cola, operant conditioning rewards include: food value in calories, flavor/texture/aroma, stimulus (sugar or caffeine), and cooling or warming effects depending on temperature. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p293]
- To prevent extinction of desired conditioned reflexes, make the product available everywhere—'a competing product, if it is never tried, can't act as a reward creating a conflicting habit.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p294]
- Pavlovian mere association: when people get bad news, they hate the messenger, making it hard for union representatives to tell workers that easy money is about to stop. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p239]
- The operant conditioning part of creating a successful beverage is solved by maximizing rewards of ingestion and minimizing possibilities that desired reflexes will be extinguished by competing products. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p307]
- For operant conditioning rewards, there are only a few practical categories: food value in calories, flavor/texture/aroma acting as stimuli, stimulants like sugar or caffeine, and cooling or warming effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p307]
- To prevent competing products from creating conflicting habits through operant conditioning, Coca-Cola made availability everywhere a permanent obsession—a competing product, if never tried, can't act as a reward creating a conflicting habit. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p307]
