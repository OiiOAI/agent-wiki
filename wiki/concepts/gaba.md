# GABA

## Summary

GABA (gamma-aminobutyric acid) is the brain's chief inhibitory neurotransmitter, used in 30-40% of all synapses. Associated with calming effects, it counterbalances glutamate (the excitatory neurotransmitter). Anti-anxiety drugs and alcohol enhance GABA's effect, while caffeine inhibits it. Dysfunction of the GABA system is associated with anxiety, panic attacks, palpitations, and insomnia.

## Key facts

- GABA is the chief inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain, used in 30 to 40 percent of synapses brain-wide. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p275]
- GABA is associated with a calming effect dubbed 'nature's Valium' and works to counter-balance glutamate. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p275]
- Anxiety, panic attacks, palpitations, and insomnia are all thought to manifest via dysfunction of the GABA system. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p276]
- Anti-anxiety drugs enhance the effect of GABA, as does alcohol, and both simultaneously inhibit glutamate. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p276]
- Meditation, yoga, and deep breathing exercises are excellent ways of achieving increased GABA. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p278]
- Hypothermic conditioning (cold showers or cryotherapy) normalizes the GABA/glutamatergic balance, with a significant drop in sympathetic activity and increase in GABA after acclimation. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p278]
