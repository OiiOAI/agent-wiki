# Ego Involvement

## Summary

Ego involvement refers to the process by which people's feelings of self-worth become dependent upon specified outcomes. Research by Ryan and colleagues consistently shows that ego involvement undermines intrinsic motivation, leads to reports of more pressure, tension, and anxiety about performance, impairs learning and creativity, and diminishes performance on tasks requiring flexible thinking and problem solving. Giving up ego involvement paradoxically allows people to perform better.

## Key facts

- Ego involvement undermines intrinsic motivation for tasks and leads subjects to report more pressure, tension, and anxiety about performance. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p125]
- Ego involvement impairs learning and creativity, and tends to diminish performance on tasks where flexible thinking and problem solving are required. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p126]
- To become more autonomous requires that people detach from their ego involvements and gradually give them up. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p126]
- Charlotte Selver's statement 'If you dare to be fat, then you can be thin' highlighted that giving up ego involvement and self-hatred paradoxically allows people to succeed. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p127]
- Being ego-involved means that people's feelings of self-worth are contingent upon some type of outcome such as being seen as intelligent, feminine, strong, artistic, or handsome. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- When people are ego-involved, they become highly rigid and controlling with themselves so they will appear intelligent, feminine, or whatever. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- Ego-involvements make people a pawn to their emotions; if they need to be seen as strong, being called a wimp will threaten their self-worth and could send them into a rage. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- By being ego-involved, people give others a weapon to use against them. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- By exploring their ego-involvements and how they affect interpretations given to stimuli, people can gain the capacity to regulate their own emotions without suppressing them and become more autonomous. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189-190]
