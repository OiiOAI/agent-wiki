# Skin in the Game

## Summary

An ethical and practical principle requiring that those who take risks or make decisions must bear some of the consequences. Part of Book VII on the ethics of fragility and antifragility, skin in the game addresses the agency problem where some become antifragile at the expense of others by getting gains from volatility while exposing others to downside risks. The principle mandates personal downside exposure for those advocating actions.

## Key facts

- Skin in the game is the principle that those who advocate risk to others must have personal downside exposure, making them accountable for their recommendations. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p22]
- The largest fragilizer of society and greatest generator of crises is the absence of skin in the game—some becoming antifragile at the expense of others by getting gains from volatility while exposing others to downside risks. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p22]
- Taleb practices what he preaches, stating he only writes about things he has done, and the risks he recommends are risks he has taken himself—'I will be the first to be hurt if I am wrong.'. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p32]
- Doxastic commitment refers to beliefs that go beyond talk, to which one is committed enough to take personal risks—a philosophical notion Taleb seeks to revive. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p32]
- The chief ethical rule states: 'Thou shalt not have antifragility at the expense of the fragility of others.'. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p22]
- Hammurabi's code, approximately 3,800 years old, required that if a builder's house collapsed and killed the owner, the builder would be put to death; if it killed the owner's son, the builder's son would be put to death. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p473]
- Taleb's fundamental rule: 'Never ask anyone for their opinion, forecast, or recommendation. Just ask them what they have—or don't have—in their portfolio.'. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p482]
- Thomas Friedman helped cause the Iraq war through his influential op-eds but paid no price for the mistake and continues writing for The New York Times. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p477]
- Joseph Stiglitz co-authored a report stating Fannie Mae's default risk was 'effectively zero,' then later claimed to have 'predicted' the 2008 financial crisis. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p480]
- The Stiglitz syndrome combines fragilista behavior (good intentions causing harm), ex post cherry-picking, and absence of skin in the game—allowing academics to contradict earlier statements without penalty. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p481]
- Robert Rubin earned $120 million in bonuses from Citibank over a decade; when Citibank collapsed, taxpayers compensated losses while he kept his money. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p492]
- The Romans forced engineers to spend time under bridges they built; the English extended this to require families of engineers to be present. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p474]
- Ralph Nader exhibits no divorce between what he preaches and his lifestyle; Taleb characterizes him as a 'secular saint.'. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p489]
- Academics proposing something in one paper can state the opposite in another without penalty, as consistency is only required within single papers, not across careers. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p481]
- The Captain and Ship Rule states that every captain goes down with the ship, removing the agency problem and lack of doxastic commitment. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p530]
- Doxastic commitment requires believing predictions and opinions only by those who have committed themselves to a belief and had something to lose by being wrong. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p529]
- Ethical problems as transfers of asymmetry involve someone stealing antifragility and optionality from others, getting upside while sticking others with downside. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p530]

## Inferences

- Inference: Skin in the game is presented as a necessary condition for ethical behavior and effective risk management, as it aligns incentives between decision-makers and those affected by decisions.
- Inference: The Stiglitz syndrome explains why economists continue to cause crises—those without skin in the game can be repeatedly wrong without consequences while cherry-picking their statements to appear prescient.
- Inference: Public corporations systematically transfer antifragility: managers benefit from volatility (cash in stock options) while shareholders and society absorb losses. Path 1 (market up 50% then back to start) is more profitable for managers than Path 2 (no movement).
- Inference: Adam Smith's actual views on capitalism aligned with skin in the game—he was 'extremely chary' of giving someone upside without downside and doubted limited liability of joint-stock companies.
- Inference: Skin in the game aligns incentives and prevents the agency problem where managers benefit personally from risks taken at others' expense.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How to operationalize skin in the game in complex modern institutions where risks are distributed and consequences are delayed.
