# Peace of Mind in Technical Work

## Summary

Peace of mind is presented as central to technical work and the perception of Quality. It is not a superficial concern but 'the whole thing'—a prerequisite for perceiving Quality which unites romantic and classic understanding. The narrator describes three levels: physical quietness, mental quietness (no wandering thoughts), and value quietness (no wandering desires). Peace of mind produces right values, right thoughts, right actions, and work that is a material reflection of serenity.

## Key facts

- Peace of mind is identified as the whole thing in technical work: the specs, measuring instruments, quality control, and final checkout are all means toward the end of satisfying the peace of mind of those responsible for the work. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p251]
- The narrator describes three levels of inner peace of mind: physical quietness, mental quietness (no wandering thoughts), and value quietness (no wandering desires). [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p252]
- Caring about one's work is defined as a feeling of identification with what one is doing, producing absence of subject-object duality at the cutting edge of consciousness. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p253]
- Peace of mind produces right values, right thoughts, right actions, and work which is a material reflection for others of the serenity at the center. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p253]

## Inferences

- Inference: Peace of mind in technical work parallels Zen Buddhist 'just sitting'—in both cases, the duality of self and object does not dominate consciousness.
