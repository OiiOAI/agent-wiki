# Cognitive Limitations in Strategy

## Summary

Rumelt argues that a fundamental challenge in strategy is working around one's own cognitive limitations and biases. Human minds are finite with limited cognitive resources, and attention illuminates one subject only to darken another. People often grab the first solution that pops into their heads and spend most of their effort justifying it rather than questioning it. This quick closure is especially problematic in strategy, where situations are ill-structured with too many variables and unknowns. Cultivating the ability to think about one's own thinking—to make judgments about one's own judgments—is more important than any analytical framework.

## Key facts

- The human mind is finite with limited cognitive resources; attention illuminates one subject only to darken another, and people can forget their larger purposes when distracted by immediate events. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p93]
- Most people, most of the time, solve problems by grabbing the first solution that pops into their heads—the first insight—and then justify it rather than question it. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p94]
- People tend to place more weight on vivid examples than on broad statistical evidence, and tend to see patterns where there is only randomness. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p94]
- The ability to think about one's own thinking—to make judgments about one's own judgments—is more important than any strategy concept, tool, matrix, or analytical framework. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p94]
- Frederick Taylor's advice to Andrew Carnegie was to make a list of the ten most important things he could do, and then start doing number one—the benefit came from constructing the list and reflecting on fundamental purposes. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p93]
