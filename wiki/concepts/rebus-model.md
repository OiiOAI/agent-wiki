# REBUS model

## Summary

The REBUS (RElaxed Beliefs Under pSychedelics) model, proposed by Robin Carhart-Harris, suggests that psychedelic drugs relax the grip of higher-level expectations concerning self and world, allowing different flows of information to emerge and interconnect in new ways. The drugs reduce precision-weighting on highly abstract top-level self-predictions, releasing bottom-up sensory and bodily information.

## Key facts

- According to REBUS, psychedelics relax the grip of higher-level expectations concerning self and world, allowing different flows of information to emerge and interconnect in new ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p197]
- Psychedelic drugs exert their strongest effects at higher levels of cortical processing, impacting higher-level elements of the predictive model concerning who we are, what we want, and how we conceive of reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p196]
- Psychedelics dampen the activity that communicates predictions between different neuronal populations and dampen activity of key brain networks, notably the 'default mode' or 'resting state' networks active during self-centric thinking and rumination. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p197]
- Neuroimaging studies showed that changes in conscious experience promoted by psychedelics are associated with decreases, rather than increases, of brain activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p197]
- At high doses, psychedelics seem to reduce the precision-weighting on highly abstract top-level self-predictions, thus releasing bottom-up sensory and bodily information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p197]

## Inferences

- Inference: The process of psychedelic action is akin to heating up metal to induce a temporary state of greater plasticity during which new forms can be explored, later stabilizing into longer-term beneficial patterns.
- Inference: Carhart-Harris describes the benefits of psychedelic interventions as 'shaking the snow-globe'—jarring the mind out of entrenched negative patterns to make it more flexible and open.
