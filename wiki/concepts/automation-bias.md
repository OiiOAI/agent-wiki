# Automation Bias

## Summary

Automation bias occurs when decision makers assume that computer systems know best and stop trying to figure out what to do, following the system's recommendations even when their own judgment would be superior. This phenomenon is particularly documented in aviation, where pilots and dispatchers with access to intelligent systems generating recommendations tend to make worse decisions. The risk increases with the sophistication of the technology, creating a paradox where better systems may lead to worse human performance.

## Key facts

- Automation bias causes airline pilots and dispatchers to make worse decisions when they have access to intelligent systems that generate recommendations, even when their own judgment would be better. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p327]
- Research by Michelle Yeh and Chris Wickens found that threat recognition rates improved with automated cueing but false alarm rates increased from 8% to 45%. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p375]
- When automated cueing was available, subjects noticed only 46% of unexpected threats compared to 59% when scanning without automatic cueing. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p375]
