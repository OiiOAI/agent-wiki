# Forgetting of Proper Names

## Summary

Freud's analysis of the forgetting of proper names, exemplified by the famous Signorelli case, demonstrates that name-forgetting is often motivated by repression. Names touching on personal, family, or professional complexes are particularly prone to being forgotten.

## Key facts

- The Signorelli example shows that the name Signorelli was forgotten because it was associated with repressed thoughts about death and sexuality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2004]
- Names are forgotten when they touch on personal complexes (family, professional) that the individual does not wish to remember. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2020]
- Name-forgetting is highly contagious: if one person forgets a name, others in conversation may forget it as well. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2036]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mechanism involves displacement along superficial associations rather than logical connections.
