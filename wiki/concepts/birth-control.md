# Birth Control

## Summary

Birth control is presented as an example of how the neocortex can thwart the old brain. By allowing the old brain's desire for sex while preventing unwanted offspring, birth control lets the neocortex win the battle between intelligence and primitive impulses.

## Key facts

- Birth control shows how the neocortex can use its intelligence to get the upper hand over the old brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p180]
- Genes propagate best when we have as many offspring as possible; the desire for sex is evolution's mechanism to serve genes' interests. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p180]
- The neocortex allows the old brain to get what it wants (sex) but prevents the undesirable end result (more children). [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p180]
- Empowering women to control their own fertility is presented as a solution that reduces population while increasing happiness and well-being. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p180]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hawkins argues that resistance to empowering women can be blamed on the old brain and viral false beliefs.
