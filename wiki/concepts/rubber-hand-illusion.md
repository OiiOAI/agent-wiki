# Rubber hand illusion

## Summary

The rubber hand illusion is a classic experiment demonstrating the plasticity of body ownership. When a rubber hand is stroked synchronously with one's hidden real hand, people experience the rubber hand as part of their own body, revealing that body ownership is a perceptual inference.

## Key facts

- The rubber hand illusion was first described in 1998 by Botvinick and Cohen. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p164]
- In the illusion, the volunteer places their real hand out of sight behind a partition while a rubber hand is placed in front in the position the real hand would normally occupy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p152]
- When the rubber hand and real hand are stroked synchronously, the rubber hand may begin to feel like part of the person's body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p152]
- Asynchronous stroking prevents the illusion from developing because of prior expectations that simultaneously arriving sensory signals have a common source. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p152]
- Individual differences in the strength of the rubber hand illusion correlate with hypnotic suggestibility. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p157]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation with hypnotic suggestibility indicates that implicit expectations drive body ownership experiences, supporting the view of body ownership as a top-down perceptual construction.
