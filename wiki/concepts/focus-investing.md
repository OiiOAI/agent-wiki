# Focus Investing

## Summary

Focus investing is an investment approach championed by Charlie Munger and Warren Buffett that involves concentrating portfolio holdings in a small number of high-conviction investments rather than diversifying widely. The philosophy holds that most investment insights are rare, so investors should load up heavily when they find opportunities rather than spreading capital thin.

## Key facts

- Focus investing implies ten holdings, not one hundred or four hundred. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p100]
- Munger states that ninety-eight percent of the investment world doesn't think this way. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p100]
- Buffett used the metaphor of a ticket with only twenty slots representing all investments in a lifetime, forcing careful consideration of each decision. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p188]
- Munger observes that all the people he knows who got rich did not rotate sectors. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p191]
- Excessive diversification is described as madness; good investments will involve relatively low diversification. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p102]
- The key insight is to wait for a near-cinch and load up, rather than trying to know everything about everything. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p187]
- Munger advocates concentrated portfolios: 'A portfolio of three companies is plenty of diversification'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p67]
- Munger calls his approach 'sit-on-your-ass investing' and cites benefits: lower brokerage costs, less nonsense listened to, and tax advantages of 1-3 percentage points per annum. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p66]
- Buffett's 20-slot punch card analogy: if you could only make 20 investments in a lifetime, you would think carefully and load up on what you really thought about. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p69]
- Munger uses Ted Williams' baseball strike zone analogy: wait for 'fat pitches' in your 'best cells' before swinging. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p68]
- Munger states: 'If you buy a few great companies then you can sit on your ass—that's a good thing'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p86]
