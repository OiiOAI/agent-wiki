# Neural parcellation

## Summary

Neural parcellation is a developmental process in which brain structures compete for synaptic space in proportion to their information-processing needs. It is described as a 'Darwinian-like process' that determines the relative sizes of functional brain regions and their patterns of connection. Parcellation is a zero-sum game: when one structure becomes partially enlarged, another is reduced.

## Key facts

- Parcellation is a zero-sum game: when one structure becomes partially enlarged, another is reduced. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p239]
- A larger retina or more densely innervated tactile receptor surface will require a larger network in order to break even in information-processing terms, and their projecting axons will compete in proportion to their numbers with other projections for synaptic space. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p238]
- The brain's wiring is determined by virtue of the interaction of information conveyed by its connections, so the way information is analyzed ultimately becomes reflected in the way brain regions are 'designed' by this activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p239]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since a Darwinian-like process in brain development determines relative sizes of functional brain regions, there is reason to suspect that a Darwinian-like functional consequence should result.
