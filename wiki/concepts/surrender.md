# Surrender

## Summary

Surrender is the practice of yielding to rather than opposing the flow of life, specifically by accepting the present moment unconditionally and without reservation. It involves relinquishing inner resistance to what is—which manifests as mental judgment and emotional negativity. True surrender is distinct from resignation or passivity; it does not prevent positive action but infuses action with Being-energy rather than ego-energy. The practice involves acknowledging resistance when it arises and focusing attention on the Now, dissolving unconscious resistance patterns.

## Key facts

- True surrender is the wisdom of yielding to the flow of life rather than opposing it, accepting the present moment unconditionally and relinquishing inner resistance to what is. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p183]
- Surrender is a purely inner phenomenon; it does not mean accepting an undesirable life situation but rather accepting the 'isness' of this moment while still taking appropriate action to change what can be changed. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p184]
- Acceptance of what is immediately frees one from mind identification and reconnects with Being, allowing a different quality of energy to flow into one's actions. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p184]
- Surrendering reconnects one with the Source-energy of Being; action infused with Being becomes 'surrendered action'—a joyful celebration of life energy rather than compulsive doing. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p185]
- Surrender is inner acceptance of what is without any reservations. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p192]
- Surrender does not transform what is, at least not directly. Surrender transforms you, and when you are transformed, your whole world is transformed. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p192]

## Inferences

- Inference: When one surrenders, the quality of consciousness at that moment becomes the primary determinant of future experiences, suggesting that surrendered states shape reality through the quality of presence they embody.
- Inference: Surrender is presented as the central practice for spiritual transformation in Tolle's teaching framework.
- Inference: The text distinguishes between accepting the present moment versus accepting life's circumstances or 'life situation'.
- Inference: Singer's business success and spiritual growth came not from planning or strategic decisions but from consistently surrendering to present moment requirements.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text does not specify how quickly or reliably surrender transforms an undesirable life situation.
