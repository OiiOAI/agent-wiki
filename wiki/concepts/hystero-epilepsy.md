# Hystero-Epilepsy

## Summary

Hystero-epilepsy (hystérie-épilepsie, grande hystérie) is a severe form of hysteria characterized by attacks of general convulsions as in epilepsy. Charcot originally used this term but later abandoned it, preferring 'grande hystérie,' because it led to endless misunderstandings. The condition should not be confused with the coexistence of hysteria and epilepsy as separate conditions. Precursors include feeling of suffocation, difficulty swallowing, headache, stomach-ache, vertigo, and dragging sensations in extremities.

## Key facts

- Charcot used 'hystero-epilepsy' for severe cases of hysteria which included in their attacks an epileptoid phase, but later abandoned this name because it leads to endless misunderstandings. - Precursors of hystero-epileptic attacks include: a feeling of suffocation, difficulty in swallowing, headache and stomach-ache, vertigo and certain peculiar dragging sensations in the extremities. - Unlike epileptics who fall suddenly and even into fire, hysterics try to fight against convulsions, save themselves from serious injuries and avoid dangerous situations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p102]
- While an epileptic is pale at the beginning of the attack and later cyanotic, a hysteric's face retains more or less its normal color. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p102]
- In hystero-epileptic attacks consciousness does not disappear completely except in the most severe cases. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p102]
- Injuries to the tongue from biting are rare in hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p102]
- Complete opisthotonus often occurs in hystero-epileptic attacks but not usually in epilepsy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p102]
