# Incentives and Motivation

## Summary

Charlie Munger emphasizes that incentives are powerful determinants of behavior, often overriding other considerations. He uses the Federal Express case to illustrate how getting incentives right solved a seemingly intractable operational problem, and criticizes how Wall Street's incentive structures harm clients.

## Key facts

- Munger states: 'As usual in human affairs, what determines the behavior are incentives for the decision maker.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p189]
- Federal Express solved its operational problem by changing from hourly pay to shift-based pay—paying people when the work was done rather than by the hour. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p190]
- The general systems of money management require people to pretend to do something they can't do and like something they don't. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p112]
- On a net basis, the whole investment management business gives no value added to all buyers combined. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p112]
- Wall Street's ethics average out to 'mediocre at best' and the general culture of investment banking has deteriorated over the years. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p113]
