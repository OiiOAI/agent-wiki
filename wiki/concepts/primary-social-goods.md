# Primary Social Goods

## Summary

Things it is supposed a rational man wants whatever else he wants — goods necessary means for executing any rational plan of life regardless of the plan's specific content. Rawls defines expectations as the index of primary social goods available to a representative individual. The primary social goods include rights and liberties, opportunities and powers, income and wealth, and a sense of one's own worth.

## Key facts

- Primary goods are things which it is supposed a rational man wants whatever else he wants; regardless of what an individual's rational plans are in detail, there are various things which he would prefer more of rather than less. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p93]
- The primary social goods in broad categories are: rights and liberties, opportunities and powers, income and wealth. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p94]
- A person's good is determined by what is for him the most rational long-term plan of life given reasonably favorable circumstances. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p94]
- The index problem largely reduces to that of weighting primary goods for the least advantaged, for those with the least authority and the lowest income. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p95]
- Expectations are defined as the index of primary goods that a representative man can reasonably look forward to; a person's prospects are improved when he can anticipate a preferred collection of these goods. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p96]
- The interpretation of expectations represents an agreement to compare men's situations solely by reference to things which it is assumed they all prefer more of — a publicly recognized objective measure. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p96]

## Inferences

- Inference: By founding expectations on primary goods rather than on subjective satisfaction, Rawls avoids the difficulties of interpersonal utility comparisons while still establishing a basis for rational choice in the original position.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The construction of the index itself — how the different primary social goods are to be weighed — involves relying upon intuitive capacities that cannot be avoided entirely.
