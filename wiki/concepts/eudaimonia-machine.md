# The Eudaimonia Machine

## Summary

The Eudaimonia Machine is an architectural concept designed by David Dewane for creating deep work environments. It is a one-story narrow rectangle with five rooms in a line: gallery, salon, library, office space, and deep work chambers. The lack of a shared hallway forces users to pass through each space to get deeper into the machine, with the deep work chambers being six-by-ten-foot rooms with eighteen inches of soundproof insulation.

## Key facts

- The Eudaimonia Machine is a one-story narrow rectangle made up of five rooms placed in a line with no shared hallway, forcing users to pass through one room to get to the next. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p57]
- The five rooms of the Eudaimonia Machine are: gallery, salon, library, office space, and deep work chambers. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p57-58]
- The deep work chambers are designed to be six by ten feet with eighteen inches of soundproof insulation, allowing for total focus and uninterrupted work flow. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p58]
- The machine is named after the Greek concept of eudaimonia—a state in which you achieve your full human potential. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p57]
