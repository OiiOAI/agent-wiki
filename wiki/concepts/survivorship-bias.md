# Survivorship Bias

## Summary

Survivorship bias is the error of focusing only on examples that passed some selection process while ignoring those that did not. Abraham Wald demonstrated this during World War II by recommending armor for the spots on returning planes that had no bullet holes—the missing planes were destroyed where the returning planes were undamaged.

## Key facts

- Abraham Wald recommended armor for spots where no planes had taken any hits, realizing the planes that didn't return were hit where they needed protection. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p117]
- Survivorship bias occurs when we only consider surviving examples and ignore failed ones that are not visible. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p117]
- Survivorship Bias causes people to systematically overestimate their chances of succeeding because failure is made less visible than success. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p13]
- Behind every successful author there are 100 writers whose books will never sell, another 100 who haven't found publishers, and 100 whose unfinished manuscripts gather dust. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p13]
- The Dow Jones Industrial Average Index consists of out-and-out survivors; failed and small businesses that never entered the stock market represent the majority of business ventures. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p14]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book's advice to visit 'graveyards of failed projects and careers' directly stems from the need to counteract survivorship bias by exposing oneself to examples of failure.
