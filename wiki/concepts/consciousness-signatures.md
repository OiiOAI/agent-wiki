# Consciousness signatures

## Summary

Consciousness signatures are reliable neural markers that distinguish conscious from unconscious processing. Research identified four major signatures: (1) global ignition of parietal and prefrontal circuits, (2) the P3 slow wave in EEG emerging ~300ms after stimulus onset, (3) sustained high-frequency gamma oscillations, and (4) long-distance synchronization forming a brain web. These signatures appear when information crosses the threshold for awareness and are absent during subliminal processing.

## Key facts

- Signature 1: Conscious perception triggers an intense neuronal activation leading to sudden ignition of parietal and prefrontal circuits, multiplying activation by 12 in key regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p151]
- Signature 2: The P3 wave (also called P300) is a slow positive voltage over the scalp that appears ~270-350ms after conscious perception, constituting a reliable marker. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p157]
- Signature 3: Conscious perception generates a late and sustained burst of high-frequency gamma-band activity (30+ Hz), absent on unconscious trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p171]
- Signature 4: Conscious perception involves massive long-distance synchronization across distant brain regions, forming a brain web with bidirectional communication. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p175]
- Conscious perception lags ~1/3 second behind the objective stimulus onset—the brain needs this time to accumulate evidence and trigger the ignition cascade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p159]
- Early brain responses (P1, N1) are identical for seen and unseen stimuli—the signatures diverge only at later stages when conscious access occurs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p156]

## Inferences

- Inference: The four signatures reflect a common mechanism—conscious perception requires recurrent processing that spreads from local sensory areas to global prefrontal-parietal networks, whereas unconscious processing remains confined to local circuits.
