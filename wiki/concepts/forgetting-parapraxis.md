# Forgetting as Parapraxis

## Summary

Forgetting, when it surprises us by breaking the expected rule that unimportant things are forgotten while important ones are preserved, reveals itself as motivated by unwillingness to remember distressing material. The forgetting of intentions specifically shows the operation of a counter-will that opposes the intended action. Analysis of such cases reveals conflicts between different psychical agencies, where suppressed impulses or objections find indirect expression through forgetting. The phenomenon demonstrates that forgetting is not merely a failure but an active psychological process with purpose and determination.

## Key facts

- Freud identifies that the motive for forgetting is invariably an unwillingness to remember something which can evoke distressing feelings. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2237]
- In forgetting of intentions, a conflict becomes tangible with a counter-will that opposes the intention without putting an end to it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2237]
