# Social preferences in primates

## Summary

Social preferences in primates refer to tendencies toward prosocial, altruistic, or fairness-related behavior. Research reveals that chimpanzees show indifference to the welfare of other group members in food-delivery tasks, while marmosets show prosocial preferences. The discrepancy may reflect differences in social organization (cooperative breeding versus individualistic societies), the presence of food rewards, or developmental factors.

## Key facts

- Silk et al. (2005) found that chimpanzees were indifferent to the welfare of other group members, choosing equally between 1/1 (equal reward to self and partner) and 1/0 (reward only to self) options regardless of partner presence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p276]
- Cooperatively breeding marmosets showed prosocial preferences, being significantly more likely to choose the 0/1 option (partner gets reward) when another marmoset was present than when alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p278]
- Warneken et al. (2007) found that chimpanzees spontaneously helped both humans and other chimpanzees to obtain out-of-reach objects without explicit reward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p280]
- Jensen et al. (2007) found no prosocial preferences in chimpanzees even when actors received no food themselves (0/1 versus 0/0 choice). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p279]
- Warneken and Tomasello (2006) showed that young chimpanzees took advantage of opportunities to provide instrumental assistance to familiar human experimenters in some situations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p280]

## Inferences

- Inference: The difference between marmosets and chimpanzees may reflect their social organization, with cooperative breeders being more other-regarding than individualistic species.
- Inference: Chimpanzee helping behavior in instrumental tasks versus lack of prosocial food-delivery may indicate context-specific rather than general prosocial tendencies.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the lack of prosocial behavior in some chimpanzee studies reflects cognitive limitations or motivational differences requires further investigation.
