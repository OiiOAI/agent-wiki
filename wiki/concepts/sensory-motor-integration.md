# Sensory-Motor Integration

## Summary

Sensory-motor integration in the spinal cord refers to the process by which sensory feedback and descending motor commands are combined at spinal neurons to produce coordinated movement. Spinal interneurons receive convergent input from multiple sensory modalities and from descending pathways, allowing reflexes to be modulated by behavioral context and enabling reflexes to contribute to voluntary movement.

## Key facts

- Spinal reflex pathways and descending motor pathways converge on common interneurons in the spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p762]
- Spatial summation experiments demonstrate that sensory and descending inputs converge on the same spinal interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p774]
- The Ib inhibitory interneuron receives convergent input from tendon organs, muscle spindles, cutaneous receptors, joint receptors, and descending pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p772]
- During locomotion, the reflex action of Ib sensory fibers on extensor motor neurons reverses from inhibition to excitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p772]
- Cutaneous reflexes produce coordinated withdrawal movements and postural adjustments that serve protective functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p771]
