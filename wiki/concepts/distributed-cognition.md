# Distributed Cognition

## Summary

Distributed Cognition is a psychological concept describing the process of externalizing thoughts into objective, reviewable formats to create an extended mind. By capturing ideas outside the head, the mind can generate additional ideas and continue thinking more effectively.

## Key facts

- Distributed cognition refers to getting things out of your head and into objective, reviewable formats, building an extended mind. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p114]
- The mind only provides as many ideas as one can effectively use; if ideas are not being collected in a trusted way, fewer ideas are generated. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p114]
- Few people can hold focus on a topic for more than a couple of minutes without some objective structure or tool to anchor their ideas. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p115]
- External brainstorming helps capture original ideas and generate new ones that might not have occurred without a mechanism to hold and reflect thoughts. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p114]
- The Combat Information Center distributed cognitive load among different roles: Radar Operator, Plot Recorder, Plotter, Radio Recorder, Evaluator, and CIC Talker. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p233]
- Greg Brougham recommends working with fine-grained objects and ensuring dis-intermediation as principles for leveraging distributed cognition. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p148]
- Plots in CICs became the 'brain' of effective Combat Information Centers, allowing rapid integration of visual information. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: Distributed cognition is essential for managing complexity where no single individual can process all relevant information.
