# Prisoner's Dilemma Game

## Summary

The Prisoner's Dilemma is a standard game theory scenario where two players can either cooperate or defect. The unique Nash Equilibrium is mutual defection, which yields worse outcomes for both players than mutual cooperation, yet mutual cooperation occurs about 50% of the time in experiments.

## Key facts

- In most iterations of the game, players exhibit much more cooperation than expected, with mutual cooperation occurring about 50% of the time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p65]
- Reciprocated cooperation with another human in a PDG leads to increased activation in both caudate and nucleus accumbens compared to identical amounts earned without social involvement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p72]

## Inferences

- Inference: The gap between Nash Equilibrium prediction (defect) and observed behavior (frequent cooperation) indicates that humans are not purely self-interested.
