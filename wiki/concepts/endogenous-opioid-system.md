# Endogenous Opioid System

## Summary

The endogenous opioid system comprises four major peptide families (endorphins, enkephalins, dynorphins, orphanin FQ) that act on opioid receptors (mu, delta, kappa) to modulate pain. These peptides are distributed in pain-modulatory circuits including the periaqueductal gray, nucleus raphe magnus, and dorsal horn.

## Key facts

- Morphine and other opioid alkaloids are potent agonists at mu receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p536]
- Opioids regulate nociceptive transmission by increasing K+ conductance and reducing Ca2+ entry into sensory nerve terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p539]
- The periaqueductal gray region is among the most sensitive sites for morphine-induced analgesia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p537]
