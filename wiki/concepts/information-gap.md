# Information Gap

## Summary

The information gap is the gulf between data that is available and the information that people really need to know. The book states that 'more data does not equal more information.' Across engineered systems, people face an ever-widening information gap as they try to find, sort, integrate, and process needed information from the vast amount of available data. The authors argue this gap is the critical problem that user-centered design must address.

## Key facts

- The information gap is defined as the gulf between the data that is available and the information that people really need to know. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p15]
- More data does not equal more information. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p24]
- The information gap exists because there is simply more information than any one person can handle in complex systems. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p23]
- Operators must find, sort, integrate, and process needed information from all that which is available, leading inevitably to an information gap. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p25]
