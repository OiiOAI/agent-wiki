# Information Gap

## Summary

The information gap is the gulf between data that is available and the information that people really need to know. The book states that 'more data does not equal more information.' Across engineered systems, people face an ever-widening information gap as they try to find, sort, integrate, and process needed information from the vast amount of available data. The authors argue this gap is the critical problem that user-centered design must address.

## Key facts

- The information gap is defined as the gulf between the data that is available and the information that people really need to know. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p15]
- More data does not equal more information. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p24]
- The information gap exists because there is simply more information than any one person can handle in complex systems. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p23]
- Operators must find, sort, integrate, and process needed information from all that which is available, leading inevitably to an information gap. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p25]
- The information gap is defined as the huge gap between the tons of data produced and disseminated and the operator's ability to find the necessary bits and process them to arrive at the actual information required for decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- The problem with today's systems is not a lack of information, but finding what is needed when it is needed. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- In the face of data torrents, many operators may be even less informed than ever before. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- It is becoming widely recognized that more data does not equal more information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- Issues of automation and 'intelligent systems' have frequently only exacerbated the information gap problem, rather than aided it. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- The criteria for system designs have correspondingly changed to include ensuring that information is provided in a way that is usable cognitively as well as physically. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
