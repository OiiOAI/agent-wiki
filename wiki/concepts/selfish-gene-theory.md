# Selfish Gene Theory

## Summary

The selfish gene theory is Dawkins' central thesis that the gene is the fundamental unit of natural selection, not the species, group, or individual. Genes are viewed as selfish agents that build survival machines (organisms) to ensure their own propagation. The theory explains how selfishness at the gene level can give rise to both selfish and altruistic behavior at the individual level.

## Key facts

- The selfish gene theory is Darwin's theory expressed in a way Darwin did not choose but whose aptness Dawkins believes Darwin would have recognized. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p16]
- Dawkins argues that the gene is the unit in the sense of replicator, while the organism is the unit in the sense of vehicle—both are important but represent distinct kinds of units. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p9]
- The book argues that altruism can be favored at other levels even though genes are selfish at their level. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p8]
- Kin altruism is presented as one way in which gene selfishness can translate into individual altruism, by helping kin who share copies of the same genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p8]
- Reciprocation is identified as Darwinian theory's other main generator of altruism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p8]
- Dawkins distinguishes between ultra-selfish genes (outlaw genes that work against the rest of the genome, like meiotic drive genes) and cooperating genes that form self-interested cartels. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p10]

## Inferences

- Inference: The selfish gene theory resolves the apparent dispute between genes and organisms as units of natural selection by recognizing both as valid but different kinds of units.
