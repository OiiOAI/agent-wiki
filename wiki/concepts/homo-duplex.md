# Homo Duplex

## Summary

Homo duplex is Haidt's term describing the dual nature of human beings as both selfish individuals (90 percent chimp) and groupish collaborators (10 percent bee). This concept, rooted in Durkheimian sociology, captures how humans evolved under multilevel selection to be contingent cooperators who can transcend self-interest and devote themselves to helping others or their groups. The term explains why human flourishing requires social order and embeddedness within moral communities.

## Key facts

- Humans are described as 'Homo duplex'; we are 90 percent chimp and 10 percent bee. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p291]
- Successful religions work on both levels of human nature to suppress selfishness, or at least to channel it in ways that often pay dividends for the group. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p291]
- A Durkheimian version of utilitarianism would recognize that human flourishing requires social order and embeddedness. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p293]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept synthesizes evolutionary psychology with Durkheimian sociology, suggesting that humans evolved psychological mechanisms for both individual survival and group cooperation.
