# Picture as Array

## Summary

Gibson's theory that a picture is a surface so treated that it makes available an optic array of arrested structures with underlying invariants of structure. He traces the evolution of his own definition through four stages: (1) an array of pencils of light, (2) an array of nested visual solid angles, (3) an array considered as a structure, and (4) an array of persisting invariants. The cross-sections of the visual angles are forms, but the invariants are formless. A picture requires two kinds of apprehension: direct perceiving of the picture surface along with indirect awareness of what it depicts.

## Key facts

- A picture is a surface so treated that it makes available a limited optic array of some sort at a point of observation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p289]
- A picture requires two kinds of apprehension that go on at the same time, one direct and the other indirect. There is a direct perceiving of the picture surface along with an indirect awareness of virtual surface—a perceiving, knowing, or imagining, as the case may be. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p302]
- The illusion of reality in pictures is a myth. The same automatic tests for reality that distinguish between a perception and a mental image also distinguish between a perception and a physical image. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p269]
- The 'fooling the eye' is possible only for a single eye at a fixed point of observation with a constricted field of view, for what Gibson calls aperture vision. Only the eye considered as a fixed camera can be deceived. The actual binocular visual system cannot. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p269]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since a picture always displays both invariants for the depicted layout and invariants for the surface as such, viewers can never be wholly deceived—they will always perceive the picture surface alongside the virtual scene.
