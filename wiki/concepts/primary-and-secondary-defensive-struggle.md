# Primary and Secondary Defensive Struggle

## Summary

Freud distinguishes between two phases in the patient's struggle against obsessional ideas. The primary defensive struggle involves the original repression and transformation of unconscious material into obsessional formations. The secondary defensive struggle begins when obsessional ideas force their way into consciousness and the patient carries on a conscious fight against them. During the secondary struggle, patients often do not know the actual wording of their own obsessional ideas—during psychoanalysis the disease 'grows bold enough to speak more plainly' and patients obtain clearer views of their pathological productions. The distortion in obsessional ideas exhibits traces of the primary defensive struggle, enabling persistence through conscious misapprehension.
