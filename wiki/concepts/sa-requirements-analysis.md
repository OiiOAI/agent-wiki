# SA Requirements Analysis

## Summary

SA Requirements Analysis is the process of systematically defining the situation awareness information needs that operators require to effectively make decisions in complex environments. Based on Goal-Directed Task Analysis, it identifies what information operators would ideally like to know to meet each goal, regardless of whether such information is currently available in existing systems. The analysis addresses all three levels of SA—perception (Level 1), comprehension (Level 2), and projection (Level 3)—and focuses on dynamic situational information rather than static knowledge.

## Key facts

- SA requirements analysis determines what aspects of the situation are important for a particular operator's SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p164]
- The analysis seeks to determine what operators would ideally like to know to meet each goal, recognizing they often must operate with incomplete information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p165]
- Static knowledge such as procedures or rules for performing tasks is outside the bounds of an SA requirements analysis; only dynamic situational information is addressed. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p165]
- The analysis is conducted using expert elicitation, observation of operator performance, verbal protocols, analysis of written materials and documentation, and formal questionnaires. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p164]

## Inferences

- Inference: By measuring against what operators ideally need rather than what current technology provides, the analysis avoids artificial ceiling effects in system evaluation.
