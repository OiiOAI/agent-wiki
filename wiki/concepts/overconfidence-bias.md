# Overconfidence Bias

## Summary

A systematic tendency for people to overestimate their own abilities, knowledge, and the accuracy of their predictions while being underconfident about the limits of their knowledge. Overconfidence manifests in calibration failures (actual hit rates far below stated confidence levels), better-than-average effects, and excessive optimism about one's own project's chances of success.

## Key facts

- Overconfidence is manifested in calibration failures: in one study, directors of finance stated 80% confidence intervals that contained the actual stock market outcome only 33% of the time. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p339]
- The better-than-average effect shows that 90% of drivers rate themselves as above average, which is mathematically impossible for the majority to be above the median. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p336]
- Entrepreneurs estimated their chances of success at 60% on average, compared to the actual 35% five-year survival rate for small businesses, with 33% claiming zero chance of failure. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p332]
- Kahneman and Lovallo coined the term 'bold forecasts and timid decisions' to describe how optimistic forecasters often take less risk than their confident predictions would suggest they should. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p341]

## Inferences

- Inference: Overconfidence is partly driven by WYSIATI: when estimating uncertainty, people rely only on information that comes to mind and construct coherent stories, failing to consider what they do not know.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text acknowledges that overconfidence is difficult to train away, with interventions showing limited success except in specific contexts like Shell geologists trained with known historical outcomes.
