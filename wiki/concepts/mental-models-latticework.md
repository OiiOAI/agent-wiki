# Mental Models (Latticework of)

## Summary

Charlie Munger's framework for acquiring worldly wisdom by building a latticework of mental models from multiple disciplines in one's mind, to which experience is hung and organized. The approach requires crossing jurisdictional boundaries between academic disciplines rather than staying siloed.

## Key facts

- Munger believes a certain system—almost any intelligent person can learn—works way better than systems most people use. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p214]
- You need a latticework of mental models in your head, and you hang your actual and vicarious experience on this latticework of powerful models. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p214]
- Munger urges ignoring jurisdictional boundaries to use models from all disciplines, despite academic and bureaucratic discouragement. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p216]
- The bridge analogy: just as good declarers need all six standard methods (long suit establishment, finesses, throw-in plays, crossruffs, squeezes, misleading defense), decision-makers need all major mental models and must know how they interact. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p217]
- Great thinkers think forward and backward—in bridge, thinking backward means considering what could go wrong; this same discipline works in life. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p217]
- Munger estimates one needs approximately 100 models and a few mental tricks, practiced throughout life. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p222]
- The approach requires learning not only what professors teach but also what they do not teach because they don't handle their subject correctly. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p228]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger advocates for self-education because academic structures discourage boundary-crossing, so individuals must supplement formal education with multidisciplinary learning.
