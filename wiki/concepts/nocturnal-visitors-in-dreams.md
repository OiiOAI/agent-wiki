# Nocturnal Visitors in Dreams

## Summary

Nocturnal visitors in dreams refer to figures such as robbers, burglars, and ghosts who pursue or frighten dreamers. Freud traces these figures to infantile reminiscences of nocturnal attendants who roused children to prevent bed-wetting or checked where children had put their hands during sleep. Analysis of anxiety dreams with these figures shows they represent the dreamer's father (robbers) and female figures in white nightgowns (ghosts).

## Key facts

- Robbers, burglars and ghosts originate from the same class of infantile reminiscence: nocturnal visitors who roused children and took them up to prevent bed-wetting. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p361]
- Analysis shows that in every case the robbers stood for the sleeper's father, whereas the ghosts corresponded to female figures in white nightgowns. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p361]

## Inferences

- Inference: The persistence of nocturnal visitor imagery into adulthood suggests these are condensed representations of childhood experiences with parental figures during vulnerable sleep states.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the identification of robbers with father and ghosts with female figures applies universally or represents specific patterns in Freud's clinical population.
