# Primal Scene

## Summary

A concept introduced by Freud referring to the child's observation of parental sexual intercourse, which becomes a foundational traumatic experience shaping subsequent psychosexual development. In the Wolf Man case, Freud reconstructed that the patient witnessed his parents having intercourse when he was approximately 1.5 to 2.5 years old, during an afternoon siesta. This observation was later revived in a dream at age 4, producing anxiety. Freud debated extensively whether such scenes were real memories or retrospective phantasies, ultimately concluding they were likely based on actual observations of animal intercourse that were then transferred to the parents.

## Key facts

- The Wolf Man allegedly witnessed a coitus a tergo (from behind) three times between his parents when he was approximately 1.5 years old. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5296]
- The child was in his parents' bedroom due to malaria, which made it possible for him to observe the scene. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5296]
- Freud acknowledged that primal scenes from infancy are typically not reproduced as recollections but must be constructed gradually from an aggregate of indications. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5309]
- Freud later proposed an alternative view: the child may have observed dogs copulating and then transferred this memory to his parents. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5314]
- Freud concluded with a non liquet (unclear verdict) regarding whether the primal scene was entirely real or partially reconstructed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5316]
- The child finally interrupted his parents' intercourse by passing a stool, which gave him an excuse for screaming—this was a sign of excitement of the anal zone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5335]
- The fact that the little boy passed a stool as a sign of his sexual excitement was to be regarded as a characteristic of his congenital sexual constitution—he at once assumed a passive attitude. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5336]
- Scenes of observing parental intercourse are unquestionably an inherited endowment, a phylogenetic heritage, but they may just as easily be acquired by personal experience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5350]
- A child catches hold of this phylogenetic experience where his own experience fails him; he fills in the gaps in individual truth with prehistoric truth and replaces occurrences in his own life with occurrences in the life of his ancestors. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5350]
- The primal scene had become transformed into the necessary condition for his recovery—his symptom of the veil being torn during evacuation expressed the wish to be copulated with by his father and bear him a child. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5353]

## Inferences

- Inference: The primal scene provided the child with visual evidence of female genitalia, confirming the reality of castration, which precipitated his anxiety.
- Inference: The wish to be born of his father, to be sexually satisfied by him, and to present him with a child—expressed in the language of anal erotism—completed the circle of the patient's fixation upon his father, representing the furthest expression of his homosexuality.
- Inference: The phantasy of rebirth was a softened (euphemistic) substitute for the phantasy of incestuous intercourse with the mother, while the womb phantasy derived from attachment to the father—together expressing the complete Oedipus complex.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the scene was a genuine observation of parental intercourse or a reconstruction based on observations of animal copulation remains uncertain.
- Uncertain: The exact age of the child during the alleged observation (1.5 vs 2.5 years) was not definitively established.
- Uncertain: Freud admits uncertainty about whether the primal scene was a phantasy or a real experience, though he considers the question not of very great importance.
- Uncertain: Whether the boy's micturition during the Grusha scene was a proof of sexual excitement or purely accidental cannot be definitively resolved.
