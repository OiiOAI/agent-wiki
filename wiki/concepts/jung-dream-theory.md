# Carl Jung's dream theory

## Summary

Carl Jung proposed that dreams play a compensatory role in personality development by presenting the dreamer with unconscious material requiring integration for a balanced sense of self. Jung believed dreams could draw from both the personal unconscious and the collective unconscious (genetically inherited ancestral patterns expressed as archetypes). He also attributed an anticipatory or prospective function to dreams. Unlike Freud, Jung viewed dreaming as a wholesome, natural process with creative and problem-solving dimensions.

## Key facts

- Jung believed dreams play a vital compensatory role in personality development by presenting unconscious material needing to be recognized and integrated. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p40]
- Jung proposed the concept of a collective unconscious, a deep stratum common to all humankind encompassing accumulated ancestral experience, expressed as archetypes. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p41]
- Jung believed dreams could have an anticipatory or prospective function, presenting visions of probable future situations and challenges. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p41]
- Jung described dreams as a wholesome, natural process highlighting creative, transcendent, and problem-solving dimensions, unlike Freud's view of dreams as pathological. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p41]
