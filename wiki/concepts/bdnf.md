# BDNF

## Summary

Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) is a protein that plays a crucial role in reinforcing plastic changes in the brain. Merzenich's research showed BDNF consolidates connections between neurons that fire together, promotes fatty coating around neurons for faster signals, activates the nucleus basalis for focused attention during the critical period, and eventually closes the critical period when sufficient.

## Key facts

- BDNF was discovered by Rita Levi-Montalcini and Stanley Cohen, who won the Nobel Prize in 1986. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p90]
- When neurons fire together, they release BDNF, which consolidates connections between them so they fire together reliably in the future. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p90]
- BDNF promotes growth of the fatty coat around neurons that speeds up electrical signal transmission. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p90]
- During the critical period, BDNF turns on the nucleus basalis, which allows focused attention and helps the brain remember experiences—Merzenich calls it 'the teacher in the brain.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p90]
- When sufficient BDNF is released, it turns off the nucleus basalis, ending the critical period and creating need for stability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p90]
- Merzenich theorizes that in autism, overexcitation causes massive premature BDNF release, reinforcing all connections indiscriminately and shutting down the critical period early, leaving undifferentiated brain maps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p91]
