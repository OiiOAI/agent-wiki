# Supplementary Motor Complex

## Summary

The supplementary motor complex (SMC), comprising the supplementary motor area (SMA) and presupplementary motor area (pre-SMA), plays a crucial role in selecting and executing appropriate voluntary actions, organizing movement sequences, acquiring motor skills, and applying contextual rules that govern behavior.

## Key facts

- The supplementary motor complex includes two areas with distinct cytoarchitectonic characteristics, connections, and functional properties: the caudal supplementary motor area (SMA) proper and the rostral presupplementary motor area (pre-SMA). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p934]
- Lesions of the SMC do not result in paralysis but produce problems initiating or suppressing movement, including utilization behavior where patients compulsively grab objects without considering need. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p934]
- A readiness potential arises in the SMC approximately 0.8 to 1.0 seconds before the onset of self-generated movement, providing evidence that this region is involved in forming the intention to move. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p934]
- Some SMC neurons discharge before a particular sequence of three movements but not before a different sequence of the same movements, even though the first movement is identical in both sequences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p935]
- Neurons in both prefrontal and premotor cortices respond more strongly when an animal chooses a movement using one rule but not another, showing that rule-dependent activity guides motor behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p936]
