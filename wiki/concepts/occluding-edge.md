# Occluding Edge

## Summary

The occluding edge is a radical discovery in ecological optics: the fact that there are places where opaque surfaces are seen one behind another, allowing perception of hidden surfaces. This discovery challenges the classical doctrine that to be perceived, a thing must be 'present to the senses.' Kaplan's 1969 experiment with progressive deletion/accretion of texture showed that hidden surfaces can indeed be perceived through motion information.

## Key facts

- Kaplan's 1969 experiment demonstrated that a hidden surface can be perceived: progressive deletion of texture on one side of an invisible contour, combined with preservation on the other side, generated the perception of a surface receding behind an occluding edge. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p212]
- If an occluded (hidden) surface can be perceived, the classical doctrine that perception requires stimulation of receptors is upset. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p181]
- The 'law of reversible occlusion' states that whatever goes out of sight comes into sight, and whatever comes into sight goes out of sight, as the observer moves or turns. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p150]
- The occluding edge is not a fact of physics or psychology as traditionally taught—it depends on the combined facts of a surface layout and a point of observation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p214]
- There are two sorts of occluding edges: apical and curved, which separate hidden and unhidden surfaces. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p215]
- To perceive an occluding edge of an object is to perceive both the separation and the junction of its far and near surfaces. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p215]
- The occluding edge specifies contact and the margin of safety—it is where collision with barriers and passage through openings are differentiated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p251]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery of occluding edges implies that perception is not limited to currently available sensory stimulation but can extend to what is specified by information about what is becoming visible or becoming hidden.
- Inference: The occluding edge concept challenges traditional depth perception theories by showing that depth and surface persistence are directly perceived, not constructed through cognitive processing.
