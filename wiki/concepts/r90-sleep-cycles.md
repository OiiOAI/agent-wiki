# R90 Sleep Cycles

## Summary

The R90 approach means Recovery in 90 Minutes—the length of time it takes to complete a full sleep cycle. Sleep cycles consist of 4-5 stages: Stage 1 (dozing), Stage 2 (light sleep), Stage 3-4 (deep sleep producing growth hormone), and REM (dreaming). The approach replaces hour-counting with cycle-counting, recommending 5 cycles (7.5 hours) as ideal for the average person, aiming for 35 cycles per week. A constant wake time is the anchor, with sleep time flexible in 90-minute increments.

## Key facts

- R90 means Recovery in 90 Minutes, which is the length of time it takes a person to go through the stages of sleep that constitute a complete cycle. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p37]
- Sleep cycles are composed of four (or sometimes five) distinct stages: NREM Stage 1 (dozing), NREM Stage 2 (light sleep), NREM Stage 3/4 (deep sleep), and REM. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p36]
- Deep sleep is where major physical restorative benefits occur, including increased release of growth hormone. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p37]
- REM sleep is where most dreaming occurs and is believed to have beneficial effects on creativity. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p38]
- Deep sleep accounts for a higher portion of earlier cycles while REM accounts for more in later cycles. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p38]
- Setting a constant wake time is one of the most powerful tools for improving sleep quality. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p39]
- The ideal is 35 cycles per week (5 cycles per night); 28-30 cycles is acceptable; anything less should be examined. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p45]
- Once sleep has been lost, it cannot be 'caught up,' but the body is remarkably good at compensating. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p38]
- The eight-hours-of-sleep-per-night idea is a relatively modern concept that became a supposed recommendation despite being an average amount. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p35]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sleeping in cycles allows for flexibility—athletes and busy individuals can adjust sleep times in 90-minute increments while maintaining the constant wake time.
- Inference: The approach empowers individuals to manage recovery rather than being controlled by sleep anxiety.
