# Receptor Tyrosine Kinase

## Summary

Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) are a distinct family of metabotropic receptors composed of single subunits with an extracellular ligand-binding domain, a transmembrane segment, and an intracellular region containing a tyrosine kinase domain. They are activated by peptide hormones including growth factors and initiate signaling cascades that regulate gene transcription and other cellular functions.

## Key facts

- Receptor tyrosine kinases are integral membrane proteins composed of a single subunit with an extracellular ligand-binding domain connected by a single transmembrane segment to a cytoplasmic region containing a protein kinase domain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p355]
- Receptor tyrosine kinases are activated when bound by peptide hormones including EGF, FGF, NGF, BDNF, and insulin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p355]
- Ligand binding causes two receptor monomers to form a dimer, enabling each monomer to phosphorylate its counterpart at tyrosine residues, activating the kinase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p355]
- Receptor tyrosine kinases initiate cascades involving adaptor proteins and protein kinases that often lead to changes in gene transcription through MAP kinase pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p355]
