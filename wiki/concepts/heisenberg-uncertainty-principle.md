# Heisenberg uncertainty principle

## Summary

The Heisenberg uncertainty principle states that position and momentum of a subatomic particle cannot both be measured with perfect precision simultaneously - the more precisely one is measured, the less precise the other becomes. Heisenberg's original name was the 'indeterminacy principle,' reflecting that this limit reflects actual indeterminacy in physical systems, not merely limits of measurement. The wavelike nature of quantum particles means position and momentum are both probabilistic and mathematically linked. The principle applies even to classical objects; the fuzziness is simply negligible relative to object size.
