# Multi-planet Species

## Summary

Becoming a multi-planet species is proposed as a way to reduce extinction risk. Mars is considered the best option for human colonization, but living there is extremely difficult due to lack of atmosphere, radiation exposure, and poisonous soil. Hawkins argues that intelligent autonomous robots with neocortex-like capabilities are necessary to prepare Mars for human habitation.

## Key facts

- Mars is considered the best option for human colonization despite being 'a terrible place to live' with no atmosphere, high radiation, poisonous soil, and no surface water. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p210]
- Preparing Mars for humans requires large airtight buildings, water and mineral extraction, and ultimately terraforming—huge infrastructure projects taking decades or centuries. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p210]
- Intelligent robotic engineers and construction workers could work using solar energy without needing food, water, or oxygen, and could work tirelessly. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p211]
- Hawkins argues that becoming a multi-planet species does not solve the fundamental problems arising from human evolutionary heritage, including the old brain and false beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p212]
- Human interstellar travel is predicted not to happen in less than fifty years and may never happen. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p213]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hawkins believes creating intelligent robots is achievable within several decades and will happen regardless through market forces.
