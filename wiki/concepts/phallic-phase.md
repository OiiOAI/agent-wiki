# Phallic Phase

## Summary

A developmental stage in Freud's theory where the genital organ has taken over the leading role, but the female genital remains undiscovered—the phallus is recognized as male only. This phase coexists with the Oedipus complex and ends with the threat of castration.

## Key facts

- In the phallic phase, the genital is the male one only, or more correctly the penis; the female genital has remained undiscovered. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6060]
- The destruction of the child's phallic genital organization is brought about by the threat of castration. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6061]
- The sight of female genitals provides the observation that finally breaks down the boy's disbelief in the threat of castration. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6061]
- The dissolution of the Oedipus complex involves the object cathexes being given up and replaced by identifications forming the nucleus of the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6062]
- The process of superego formation preserves the genital organ but removes its function, ushering in the latency period. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6062]
