# Global Neuronal Workspace

## Summary

The global neuronal workspace is Dehaene's theoretical model of consciousness. It proposes that consciousness is global information broadcasting within the cortex, arising from a neuronal network whose function is to share pertinent information throughout the brain. The theory suggests that giant cells with long axons interconnect the cortex, and when enough brain regions agree about sensory information, they synchronize into a large-scale state of global communication—a 'burst of high-level activation' that constitutes conscious experience.

## Key facts

- The global neuronal workspace theory proposes that consciousness is global information broadcasting within the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p25]
- The theory posits a special set of neurons with long axons that crisscross the cortex, interconnecting it into an integrated whole. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p26]
- When enough brain regions agree about the importance of incoming sensory information, they synchronize into a 'large-scale state of global communication'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p26]
- Daniel Dennett calls this idea 'fame in the brain'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p26]
- Consciousness is brain-wide information sharing: when information becomes conscious, it enters a storage area that makes it available to the rest of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p206]
- The workspace selects relevant information and disseminates it back to virtually any of the other processors, allowing conscious information to be named, evaluated, memorized, or used to plan the future. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p208]
- The global workspace comprises long-distance networks particularly concentrated in the prefrontal cortex, which evolved to select and broadcast information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p204]
- Without consciousness, information remains confined to local processors and cannot be routed through successive processing stages. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p139]
- The workspace model resembles a production system or Turing machine, allowing serial chaining of unconscious processors through conscious intermediates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p136]

## Inferences

- Inference: The global neuronal workspace provides computational autonomy to the brain, allowing thought experiments and mental operations detached from the external world. [Supporting facts: p26]
- Inference: The global neuronal workspace evolved because consciousness provides specific cognitive capacities that unconscious processing cannot—particularly the ability to route information through multiple successive operations and to share mental contents socially via language.
