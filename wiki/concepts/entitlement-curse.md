# Entitlement Curse

## Summary

The Entitlement Curse describes a modern affliction where individuals feel entitled to respect, attention, and success regardless of actual accomplishment. This sense of entitlement makes people ignore reality—specifically that others have no inherent reason to trust or respect them—and makes them lazy, insensitive, and self-absorbed. For leaders, the curse worsens: they sit back expecting loyalty, grow defensive when challenged, demand privileges while avoiding sacrifices, and blame others for mistakes. The curse generates hidden resentment and eventually triggers the ambivalence and turn against leaders that the Law of Ficklessness describes. The cure is adopting the opposite attitude—expecting nothing, earning everything, working harder than others, and prioritizing group welfare over personal ego.

## Key facts

- The Entitlement Curse makes people feel entitled to respect for their work regardless of actual accomplishment, expecting others to take their ideas seriously regardless of the thought invested. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p553]
- Modern entitlement stems from being spoiled by parents who made children feel that anything they did was golden, and from technology that gives immense powers without requiring effort. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p553]
- The curse makes people insensitive and self-absorbed, negating others' willpower and judgment, which is infuriating even if not consciously perceived. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p553]
- Leaders afflicted with entitlement unconsciously expect people to come to them with loyalty, grow defensive when ideas are challenged, and feel they should be exempted from sacrifices. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p554]
- They inspire resentment by appearing to take rather than give, creating basic insecurity about their legitimacy as leaders. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p571]
- The cure is adopting the opposite attitude: expecting nothing from others, working harder, sacrificing personal interests if necessary, being accountable for mistakes, and proving through action that the group's welfare matters more than the leader's ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p554]
- With this attitude, people open themselves to influence, want to win approval and respect, forgive mistakes more easily, and channel energy toward goals rather than infighting. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p555]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Entitlement Curse directly explains why leaders who appear to be 'friends' or 'equals' to their subordinates undermine their own authority—they signal weakness rather than strength, stirring the ambivalence toward resentment.
