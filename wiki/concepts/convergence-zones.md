# Convergence zones

## Summary

Convergence zones are small ensembles of neurons where dispositional representations exist as potential firing patterns. They are located throughout higher-order association cortices (occipital, temporal, parietal, frontal regions) and in basal ganglia and limbic structures. Convergence zones receive signals from multiple sources and maintain reciprocal feedforward and feedback connections, serving as 'third-party brokers' that link entity representations with body-state representations.

## Key facts

- Dispositional representations exist in potential state in small ensembles of neurons called convergence zones, located throughout higher-order association cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p122]
- Convergence zones operate as 'third-party' brokers through reciprocal feedforward and feedback connections, preserving the order of onset of brain activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p182]
- The several dispositional representations needed to reconstruct a complex concept like a face are located in several visual and higher-order association cortices and fire back synchronously to early visual cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p123]
