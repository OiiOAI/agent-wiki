# Cognitive Control in Value Updating

## Summary

Cognitive control in value updating refers to the use of abstract rule knowledge to rapidly adjust reinforcement expectations without relearning individual stimulus-outcome associations. This mechanism allows subjects to infer that if one CS-US contingency changes, another CS's contingency has also changed based on understanding the task structure.

## Key facts

- Knowledge of the rule that both CS and US contingencies switch simultaneously allows subjects to infer new contingencies for one CS by experiencing the other switched CS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
- Saez et al. found that monkeys acquired and retained a 'mental map'—an abstract cognitive representation of task rules—allowing immediate generalization to new CS-US combinations without relearning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
- The amygdala holds abstract contextual information relevant to forming predictions about anticipated reinforcement, applying it to compute outcome expectations that drive behavioral output. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
- Cognitive control includes planning over long timeframes, coordinating among multiple competing goals, inhibiting actions, maintaining information in working memory, assessing certainty, directing learning, and switching among goals when conditions change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p259]
- System 2 thinking (slow, effortful, largely conscious) is crucial when situations are novel or ambiguous, involving multilevel optimization, weighing different goals and strategies, and actively learning from experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p261]
- Conscious cognitive control does not always have to be engaged in the moment of action—many actions are guided by habits, and much decision-making is consciously done ahead of time through commitments and plans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prefrontal cortex's proposed role as a cognitive map of task space is not limited to prefrontal cortex; the amygdala also contributes to abstract contextual representations.
- Inference: Amygdala function extends beyond receiving gated input from prefrontal cortex to actively maintaining abstract contextual information for outcome predictions.
- Inference: The capacity for cognitive control is not an abstract metaphysical property but an evolved biological function dependent on proper functioning of distributed neural resources.
- Inference: dividual differences in cognitive control explain variation in traits like patience, prudence, and emotional stability, which are perceived as morally 'better' because self-control is essential for prosocial behavior.
