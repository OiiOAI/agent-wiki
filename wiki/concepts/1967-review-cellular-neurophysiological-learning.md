# 1967 Review on Cellular Neurophysiological Approaches to Learning

## Summary

The 1967 review 'Cellular Neurophysiological Approaches in the Study of Learning' by Kandel and Spencer announced the direction of merging cellular neurobiology with the study of simple behavior. This highly influential paper inspired investigators to take up reductionist approaches to learning and memory, leading to the development of simple experimental systems worldwide.

## Key facts

- The review emphasized the importance of discovering what happens at the synapse when behavior is modified by learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p196]
- They outlined a systematic cellular approach and discussed strengths and weaknesses of simple systems: snails, worms, insects, fish, and simple vertebrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p196]
- The review inspired simple experimental systems devoted to learning in leech, snail Limax, Tritonia, Hermissenda, honeybee, cockroach, crayfish, and lobster. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p197]
- Per Andersen wrote that the ideas influenced scientists working in synaptic plasticity before 1973. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p197]
- The review convinced David Cohen to develop brain-controlled changes in heart rate in pigeons through sensitization and classical conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p197]
- Joseph LeDoux modified Cohen's protocol for classical conditioning in rats, developing the best experimental system for studying cellular mechanisms of learned fear in mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p198]
