# Survival Circuits

## Summary

Survival circuits are brain mechanisms that detect threats and orchestrate defensive responses to help keep organisms alive. These are not 'fear circuits' or 'emotion circuits'—they are survival circuits that manage interactions with the environment as part of the daily quest to survive. They exist in some form across the animal kingdom and even in single-cell organisms.

## Key facts

- Survival circuits are brain mechanisms that detect threats and orchestrate defensive responses to help keep organisms alive and well. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p74]
- Survival circuits are not emotion circuits or feeling circuits—they are survival circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p74]
- Survival circuits include circuits for defense, acquiring nutrients, balancing fluids, thermoregulation, and reproduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p74]
- The capacity to detect and respond to danger is present in every animal, from worms to humans, suggesting these circuits evolved early. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p72]
- Survival circuits predate neurons, synapses, and circuits evolutionarily; similar functions exist in single-cell organisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p74]
- Survival circuits are hardwired circuits controlling innate behaviors that promote survival: nutrient/energy management, fluid/ion balance, defense, reproduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p337]
- LeDoux proposes using 'survival circuit' terminology to isolate objective function (response control) from presumed mental states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p337]
- When activated, defensive survival circuits trigger behaviors and physiological changes, inducing a global defensive survival state. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p337]
- Survival circuits control behavior nonconsciously; in conscious organisms, the global state can influence conscious emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p338]
- The amygdala is part of a defensive survival circuit, not a 'fear center' — it controls defensive responses but does not generate the conscious feeling of fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p333]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because survival circuits exist in invertebrates without complex nervous systems, conscious feelings like fear are not necessary for defensive behavior.
- Inference: Survival circuits connect humans to the deep evolutionary history of life, but conscious emotions are products of newer cognitive circuits that interpret survival circuit activity.
