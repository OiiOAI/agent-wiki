# Prefrontal Cortex and Symbol Learning

## Summary

The human prefrontal cortex is disproportionately enlarged compared to other species and provides the cognitive bias necessary for symbol learning. This overdevelopment acts as a 'fail-safe' mechanism ensuring essentially all humans acquire symbolic abilities despite cognitive costs. The prefrontal bias enables the counterintuitive learning strategies required to subordinate immediate indexical associations to abstract symbolic relationships.

## Key facts

- The human brain has been significantly overbuilt for learning symbolic associations, acting as a catalyst making the immensely improbable nearly inevitable. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p445]
- Human brain structure is an exaggerated caricature of the special demands imposed by symbol learning, particularly learning higher-order conditional associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p446]
- The prefrontal bias enables overcoming the emotional immediacy of powerful reinforcers and the salience of competing alternatives, which is essential for symbol learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p448]
- Language acquisition had to become fail-safe; an individual born into a symbolic culture with an ape's bias against acquiring symbolic associations would be deprived of access to most realms of know-how and have little chance to reproduce successfully. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p445]

## Inferences

- Inference: Once present, prefrontal learning biases became powerful sources of social evolutionary selection, independently recruited for functions in novel domains unrelated to language.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether prefrontal changes were primarily selected for symbol learning or emerged as a byproduct of other selection pressures.
