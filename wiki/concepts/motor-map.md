# Motor Map

## Summary

The motor map is a topographic representation of the body within the primary motor cortex, first demonstrated by electrical stimulation studies. The map shows an orderly plan with foot and leg representations medially and face and mouth areas laterally, with disproportionately large areas devoted to finely controlled body parts like fingers and face.

## Key facts

- Gustav Fritsch and Eduard Hitzig showed that electrical stimulation of a limited area along the rostral bank of the central sulcus evoked movements of the contralateral body, with lowest currents needed in a narrow strip. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p887]
- The motor map is not a point-to-point representation; the most finely controlled body parts (fingers, face, mouth) are represented by disproportionately large areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p888]
- The arm motor map has a concentric, horseshoe-shaped organization where neurons controlling distal arm (digits, wrist) are concentrated in a central core surrounded by neurons controlling proximal arm (elbow, shoulder). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p889]
- Motor maps can reorganize dynamically based on motor experience and after focal lesions, contributing to recovery of function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p856]
