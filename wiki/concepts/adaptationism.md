# Adaptationism

## Summary

Adaptationism is the view that if a trait is prevalent in a population, it must have evolved because it was adaptive. It was criticized by Stephen Jay Gould and Richard Lewontin as producing 'just-so stories' that explain too much and predict too little. They introduced the concept of 'spandrels'—traits that exist not because they are adaptive but as by-products of other selected traits. The debate was both scientific and political, with critics fearing adaptationism justified the status quo through naturalistic fallacy.

- Adaptationism assumes that if a trait is prevalent in a population, it evolved because it was adaptive. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p396]
- Gould and Lewontin criticized adaptationism as producing 'just-so stories' that explain too much and predict too little. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p396]
- They introduced 'spandrels'—traits that exist as by-products of other selected traits, not because they are adaptive themselves. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p397]
- Sociobiologists responded that adaptationist approaches predict many broad features of behavior and social systems across species. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p398]
