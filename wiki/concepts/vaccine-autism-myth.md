# Vaccine-Autism Myth

## Summary

The belief that vaccinations cause autism is presented as an example of a viral false belief. It cannot be directly observed by individuals, requires ignoring the opinion of hundreds of scientists and medical personnel, and spreads because believers feel a moral obligation to save children from a debilitating condition.

## Key facts

- No individual can directly sense whether vaccines cause autism; this requires a controlled study with many participants. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p182]
- Believers must ignore the opinion of hundreds of scientists and medical personnel, rationalizing that they are hiding facts for personal gain or are ignorant. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p182]
- The viral spread mechanism is that believers are told they have a moral obligation to convince others about the danger of vaccines to save children. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p182]
- Hawkins notes that believing vaccinations cause autism, even if it leads to the death of children, is not by itself an existential threat to humanity. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p182]
