# Epigenetic Regulation of Maternal Behavior

## Summary

Studies in rats show that maternal care (licking and grooming) during infancy produces lifelong changes in offspring behavior through epigenetic modifications of the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) gene promoter. High-LG mothers raise offspring with decreased DNA methylation of the GR promoter, higher hippocampal GR expression, and reduced stress responses, and these females become high-LG mothers themselves.

## Key facts

- Female offspring of high-licking-and-grooming (high-LG) mothers display high-LG behavior when they become mothers, while offspring of low-LG mothers display low-LG behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1274]
- Pups reared by high-LG mothers undergo selective demethylation of the glucocorticoid receptor gene promoter, resulting in higher GR expression and blunted stress responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1275]
- When female rat pups are cross-fostered at birth, their maternal behavior as adults resembles their foster mother rather than their biological mother, demonstrating environmental rather than genetic determination. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1275]
