# Master Builder Model

## Summary

The Master Builder model refers to the historical approach where a single expert designed, engineered, and oversaw construction from start to finish. This model became untenable as building complexity increased, forcing the construction industry to develop checklist-based coordination systems. Gawande contrasts this with medicine's continued reliance on the Master Physician model.

## Key facts

- For most of modern history, Master Builders designed, engineered, and oversaw construction from start to finish, building Notre Dame, St. Peter's Basilica, and the U.S. Capitol. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p48]
- By the mid-20th century, the variety and sophistication of construction advancements overwhelmed any individual's ability to master them all. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p48]
- Architecture split from engineering, and construction fragmented into 16 different trades, similar to how medicine fragmented into specialties. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p48]
- In construction, 'building failure' (partial or full collapse) occurs at a rate of less than 0.00002 percent annually despite millions of buildings. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p56]
- Joe Salvia noted that buildings now take a third less time to build than when he started his career despite being more complex. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p56]

## Inferences

- Inference: The construction industry's abandonment of the Master Builder model parallels what Gawande argues medicine must do—move from individual expertise to team-based systems with checklists.
