# Ion Channel Structure and Selectivity

## Summary

Ion channels are integral membrane proteins with a core transmembrane domain containing a central aqueous pore spanning the membrane width. The pore consists of a medium-wide tunnel leading into a wider inner chamber lined by hydrophobic amino acids, followed by the selectivity filter that is rate-limiting for ion passage. The filter contains rings of oxygen atoms that form binding sites for permeant ions, with eight oxygen atoms (four from each of two subunits) stabilizing each bound K+ ion. The selectivity filter is sized to optimally interact with K+ ions while excluding the smaller Na+ ions due to their larger hydration shells.

## Key facts

- X-ray crystallographic analysis of the bacterial KcsA K+ channel revealed that the channel pore has a medium-wide tunnel 18 Angstroms in length leading to a wider inner chamber 10 Angstroms in diameter, followed by the narrow selectivity filter only 12 Angstroms long. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p180]
- The selectivity filter contains 20 electronegative oxygen atoms from the protein backbone and side chains that form four binding sites for K+ ions, with each bound ion stabilized by eight oxygen atoms in two planes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p181]
- Na+ ions have a larger effective diameter than K+ ions in solution because their stronger electrostatic attraction to water causes them to have a larger cloud of water molecules (waters of hydration). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p168]
- K+ channels select K+ over Na+ because the filter width provides optimal electrostatic interactions with K+ ions as they pass but is too wide for the smaller Na+ ions to interact effectively with all eight oxygen atoms at any point along the filter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p181]
- Only two K+ ions can occupy the selectivity filter at any instant due to electrostatic repulsion, with a water molecule interposed between ions in adjacent binding sites, and the mutual repulsion ensures ions linger only briefly, resulting in high conduction rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p182]
