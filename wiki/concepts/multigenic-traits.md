# Multigenic Traits

## Summary

Multigenic traits are characteristics influenced by multiple genes rather than a single gene, and they do not follow simple Mendelian inheritance patterns. Two models explain multigenic disorders: the rare monogenic model (many genes in the population can contribute, each individually rare but with strong effect) and the polygenic/common variant model (a small number of relatively common mutations, each with small effect, interact to cause a disorder). Schizophrenia, autism, and depression are examples of multigenic traits, along with diabetes, coronary artery disease, and asthma.

## Key facts

- Multigenic traits do not have a simple recognizable pattern of inheritance like autosomal dominant, recessive, or X-linked. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p111]
- In the rare monogenic model, many genes in the population can contribute to a disorder, but each gene mutation is individually rare and has a strong effect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p111]
- In the polygenic/common variant model, a small number of relatively common mutations, each with small effect, interact together to cause a disorder.
