# Association Bias

## Summary

Association bias is the tendency to see connections where none exist. Our brain is a connection machine: if we eat an unknown fruit and feel sick afterward, we avoid it. This creates false knowledge as demonstrated by Pavlov's dogs who associated bell-ringing with food.

## Key facts

- Pavlov discovered that ringing a bell alone was enough to make dogs salivate; the animals' brains linked two functionally unrelated things. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p113]
- We often condemn bearers of bad news because we automatically associate them with the message's content (shoot-the-messenger syndrome). [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p114]
- Mark Twain wrote: 'We should be careful to get out of an experience only the wisdom that is in it – and stop there; lest we be like the cat that sits down on a hot stove-lid.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p114]
