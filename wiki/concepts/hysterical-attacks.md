# Hysterical Attacks

## Summary

Freud's account of hysterical attacks as involuntary motor expressions of unconscious phantasies, requiring the same interpretative technique as the analysis of dreams. The paper describes how attacks undergo condensation and distortion, represent multiple phantasies simultaneously, and can involve simultaneous contradictory actions expressing both masculine and feminine aspects of an underlying sexual phantasy.

## Key facts

- Hysterical attacks are phantasies translated into the motor sphere, projected on to motility and portrayed in pantomime, analogous to dreams expressed in hallucinatory form. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3107]
- A hysterical attack needs to be subjected to the same interpretative revision as night dreams, employing the same techniques of condensation and distortion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3107]
- Hysterical attacks become unintelligible through condensation, representing several phantasies in the same material simultaneously, with elements common to two or more phantasies constituting the nucleus of the representation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3107]
- Hysterical attacks may involve simultaneous contradictory actions, such as a patient pressing her dress against her body with one hand while trying to tear it off with the other, simultaneously playing both parts in the underlying sexual phantasy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3047]
- Hysterical attacks involve four main distortion mechanisms: (1) condensation of different phantasies, (2) multiple identification where the patient acts out both figures in a phantasy, (3) antagonistic inversion of innervations, and (4) reversal of chronological order. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3108]
- The onset of hysterical attacks follows laws: (1) associatively when the complex content is touched on, (2) organically when libidinal cathexis rises, (3) as a 'flight into illness' serving primary purpose of consolation, (4) as secondary gain from illness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3109]
- The hysterical attack takes the place of auto-erotic satisfaction previously practiced in childhood (such as masturbation by contact or thigh pressure). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3109]
- The motor discharge in hysterical attacks follows the reflex mechanism of coition, described in ancient times as 'minor epilepsy'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3110]
- Hysterical attacks and hysteria in general revive sexual activity from childhood that had a masculine character, particularly in girls who showed boyish nature before puberty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3110]
