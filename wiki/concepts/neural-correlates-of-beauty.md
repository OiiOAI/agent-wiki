# Neural Correlates of Beauty

## Summary

Research on how the brain processes beauty has revealed that the orbitofrontal cortex is more active when viewing beautiful paintings, while the motor cortex is more active when viewing ugly paintings, suggesting different brain pathways for positive and negative aesthetic judgments.

## Key facts

- Kawabata and Zeki found that the orbitofrontal cortex, engaged during perception of rewarding stimuli, was more active when viewing beautiful paintings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p240]
- The motor cortex was more active when viewing ugly paintings, similar to its activity with other unpleasant stimuli like social norm transgressions and fearful stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p240]
- Cela-Conde's group found the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex active during actual aesthetic judgments, supporting the hypothesis that changes in this brain region enabled artistic profusion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p241]
- Left hemisphere dominance was observed when something was judged beautiful, suggesting cerebral lateralization may play a role in aesthetic judgment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p241]

## Inferences

- Inference: The involvement of evolutionarily advanced prefrontal cortex regions in aesthetic judgment suggests artistic ability may be uniquely human.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact causal relationship between prefrontal cortex changes and artistic development 35,000 years ago remains speculative.
