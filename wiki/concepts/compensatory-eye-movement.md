# Compensatory Eye Movement

## Summary

Compensatory eye movement is the rotation of the eyes in precisely the opposite direction from and to exactly the same degree as head turning, effectively anchoring the eyes to the environment. Unlike pursuit or saccadic movements, it is not a response to visual stimuli but a coordination with head movement, maintaining stable perception of the world during looking around.

## Key facts

- Compensatory eye movement is a non-motion of the eyes relative to the environment, a posture like fixation, not a response to stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p232]
- When the head starts, the eye starts; when the head stops, the eye stops—the compensation is concurrent with head turning, not a reflex with latency. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p232]
- The neck muscles and eye muscles are innervated at the same time, reciprocally, during active head turning—similar to how flexor and extensor muscles coordinate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p232]
- Vertigo occurs when compensatory coordination breaks down, as after prolonged passive rotation in a swivel chair—the normal complementarity of exteroception and proprioception has failed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p233]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between active compensatory eye movement and the nystagmus reflex evoked by passive rotation shows that normal looking around involves coordination, not reflex response.
