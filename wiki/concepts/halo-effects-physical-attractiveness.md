# Halo Effects of Physical Attractiveness

## Summary

Halo effects occur when one positive characteristic of a person dominates how that person is viewed. Research demonstrates that physical attractiveness triggers automatic assumptions of favorable traits and produces significant social advantages across multiple domains including elections, hiring, legal outcomes, and helping behavior.

## Key facts

- We automatically assign favorable traits such as talent, kindness, honesty, and intelligence to attractive individuals without awareness of attractiveness playing a role. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p156]
- In Canadian federal elections, attractive candidates received more than two and a half times as many votes as unattractive candidates. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p156]
- 73 percent of Canadian voters denied that physical appearance influenced their votes, with only 14 percent allowing for the possibility. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p156]
- In simulated employment interviews, good grooming accounted for more favorable hiring decisions than job qualifications, despite interviewers claiming appearance played a small role. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p156]
- A Pennsylvania study found attractive defendants were twice as likely to avoid jail as unattractive ones. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p157]
- In a negligence trial, the better-looking defendant was assessed an average of $5,623 compared to $10,051 when the victim was more attractive. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p157]
- Both male and female jurors exhibited attractiveness-based favoritism. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p157]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistent finding that people deny the influence of attractiveness while exhibiting clear favoritism suggests the halo effect operates unconsciously, making it particularly difficult to counteract.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific mechanisms by which attractiveness influences perceptions of unrelated traits remain partially unclear.
