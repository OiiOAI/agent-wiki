# Hard-Science Organizing Ethos

## Summary

The organizing principles of hard science (math, physics, chemistry, engineering) that Munger advocates as a model for soft science disciplines, emphasizing discipline ranking by fundamentalness, mastery to tested fluency, attribution for cross-disciplinary work, and preference for fundamental explanations.

## Key facts

- Hard science has by wide margin the best record for avoiding unidisciplinary folly and making a big patch of multidisciplinary domain user-friendly. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p339]
- The fundamental organizing ethos includes: (1) rank and use disciplines in order of fundamentalness, (2) master to tested fluency the essential parts of math, physics, chemistry, and engineering, (3) never practice cross-disciplinary absorption without attribution, and (4) hypothesize and test new principles using methods similar to those that created successful old principles. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p340]
- The ethos of hard science does not say 'take what you wish' but 'learn it all to fluency, like it or not,' forcing rational organization of multidisciplinary knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p341]
- Munger assimilated the fundamental organizing ethos of hard science and pushed it further into softer and softer fare as his organizing guide and filing system. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p342]
