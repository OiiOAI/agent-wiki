# Intermale aggression

## Summary

Intermale aggression is a third form of aggressive behavior that can be provoked by electrical brain stimulation, in addition to predatory and affective attack. Research in rats has provided evidence for this third form. It may have some common influence on the brain system that elaborates intermale fighting, and may share circuits with territorial and sex-related aggression. There is preliminary evidence that one can activate aggressive intermale fighting independently of both predatory and affective attack systems.

## Key facts

- Subsequent studies in rats established a similar taxonomy of aggression types, with evidence for a third form: intermale aggression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Intermale, territorial, and sex-related aggression may have some common influence on the system that elaborates intermale fighting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- There is preliminary evidence that one can activate aggressive intermale fighting independently of predatory and affective attack systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- Intermale aggression may also have strong components of the other two forms of aggression (predatory and affective). [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p106]
- In nearly all mammalian species, males fight more than females because males possess more active aggression circuits that evolved to assure reproductive success. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p108]
- Testosterone sustains the genetic expression of vasopressin (AVP) in brain circuits, and males have more extensive vasopressinergic circuits than females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p109]
- When male rats are castrated, AVP is markedly reduced in approximately half of their vasopressinergic circuits, paralleled by declines in both sexuality and aggressiveness; replacement of testosterone into hypothalamic tissues restores these behavioral tendencies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p109]
- Centrally administered AVP increases intermale aggression in rats, while AVP receptor antagonists markedly reduce both aggression and territorial marking behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p109]
- The intermale aggression circuit runs from the medial amygdala through the preoptic and anterior hypothalamic areas down to the PAG, distinct from the trajectory of the RAGE system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p108]
- Brain manipulations that intensify anger and predatory aggression (such as ventromedial hypothalamic damage) do not necessarily intensify social aggression, suggesting these are independently controlled systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p108]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that intermale aggression may share common influences with territorial and sex-related aggression suggests these forms may serve reproductive and social status functions.
- Inference: Because victory in various forms increases testosterone secretion in males and humans, the neurophysiological solidification of assertiveness may be an end result of such hormonal feedback.
- Inference: AVP may facilitate intermale aggression partly by increasing a generalized male tendency toward behavioral persistence—the relentless pursuit of goals.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural circuits specific to intermale aggression are not well established, and it is unclear whether this represents a truly distinct system or a combination of existing systems.
