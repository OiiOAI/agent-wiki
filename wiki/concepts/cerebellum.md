# Cerebellum

## Summary

A small, densely packed brain structure containing half of the total nerve cells in the brain, involved in sensory integration, automatic motor function, and recently recognized roles in emotions, memory, language, and social interactions.

## Key facts

- The cerebellum is a small, densely packed part of the brain that contains half of the total nerve cells in the entire brain. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- It is involved in integrating sensory and automatic motor function and is constantly busy updating and computing incoming and outgoing information. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- Within the past two decades, scientists have recognized that the cerebellum is involved in maintaining rhythm and continuity for many brain functions such as emotions, memory, language, and social interactions. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- The cerebellum allows us to walk in a straight line. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- The cerebellum contains three pairs of deep nuclei (fastigial, interposed, and dentate) and is divided into vestibulocerebellum, spinocerebellum, and cerebrocerebellum based on input-output connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1014]
- The cerebellar cortex has three layers: molecular layer (Purkinje and basket/stellate cells), Purkinje cell layer, and granular layer (granule cells and Golgi cells). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1015]
- Two afferent systems carry information to the cerebellum: mossy fibers from spinal cord and brain stem, and climbing fibers from the inferior olive. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1015]
- Cerebellar disorders produce four characteristic symptoms: hypotonia, astasia-abasia (inability to stand), ataxia (coordination deficits), and intention tremor at movement endpoint. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1013]
- Patients with spinocerebellar disorders have difficulty adapting postural responses based on experience but can use immediate sensory feedback, while patients with Parkinson disease cannot change postural set when conditions change suddenly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1006]
- The cerebellum contains three layers: an inner granular layer, a middle Purkinje cell layer, and an outer molecular layer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- The two principal afferent inputs to the cerebellum are mossy fibers and climbing fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- The Purkinje cell layer is the output layer of the cerebellar cortex, consisting of a single sheet of Purkinje cell bodies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Purkinje cell axons conduct the entire output of the cerebellar cortex, projecting to deep nuclei or vestibular nuclei where GABA has an inhibitory action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- The cerebellum uses internal models to anticipate forces resulting from the mechanical properties of a moving limb. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1025]
- Damage to the vermis of the cerebellum abolishes the conditioned eyeblink response in rabbits without affecting the unconditioned response (eyeblink to puff of air), indicating the cerebellum stores the conditioned response trace. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1508]
- A lesion in the interpositus nucleus, a deep cerebellar nucleus, also abolishes conditioned eyeblink, and neurons in the same area show learning-dependent increases in activity that parallel the development of conditioned behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1508]
- Patients with cerebellar lesions have no difficulty with perceptual learning (e.g., mirror reading) even though their learning of motor skills is impaired, demonstrating that the cerebellum is specifically involved in motor but not perceptual implicit memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1505]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cerebellum's involvement in multiple higher-order functions beyond motor control suggests it plays a broader role in cognition than previously understood.
- Inference: The regularity of the cerebellar microcircuit across different functional regions suggests that similar computational operations are performed on different inputs and outputs, implying the cerebellum implements a common algorithm for motor learning and coordination.
- Inference: The three-layer organization reflects a hierarchical processing architecture where sensory information (mossy fibers) is integrated with error signals (climbing fibers) to generate motor commands.
- Inference: Motor learning in the cerebellum likely involves synaptic plasticity mechanisms such as long-term depression at parallel fiber-Purkinje cell synapses.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms by which the cerebellum contributes to emotions, memory, language, and social interactions are not fully detailed in this definition.
- Uncertain: The exact neural pathways for guidance of smooth pursuit by the vermis are not known.
- Uncertain: Whether complex spikes generated by climbing fiber input are transmitted down the Purkinje cell axon is not clear.
