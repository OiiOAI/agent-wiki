# Cerebellum

## Summary

A small, densely packed brain structure containing half of the total nerve cells in the brain, involved in sensory integration, automatic motor function, and recently recognized roles in emotions, memory, language, and social interactions.

## Key facts

- The cerebellum is a small, densely packed part of the brain that contains half of the total nerve cells in the entire brain. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- It is involved in integrating sensory and automatic motor function and is constantly busy updating and computing incoming and outgoing information. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- Within the past two decades, scientists have recognized that the cerebellum is involved in maintaining rhythm and continuity for many brain functions such as emotions, memory, language, and social interactions. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]
- The cerebellum allows us to walk in a straight line. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p222]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cerebellum's involvement in multiple higher-order functions beyond motor control suggests it plays a broader role in cognition than previously understood.
