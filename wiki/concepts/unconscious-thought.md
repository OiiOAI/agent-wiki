# Unconscious Thought

## Summary

Unconscious or spontaneous thought processes that occur during periods of distraction, which can benefit complex decision making. Research shows that for complex decisions involving many attributes, unconscious thought produces better decisions than conscious deliberation, likely due to broader information integration.

## Key facts

- For complex decisions with many attributes (12 attributes), the group that performed a distracting task made better decisions compared with the deliberate thinking group. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p370]
- For simple decisions (4 attributes), the group that engaged in deliberate thought made better choices than the unconscious thought group. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p370]
- Mental representations of alternatives change during unconscious thought, becoming clearer and better organized, leading to better decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p370]
- Conscious thought has relatively low capacity, leading choosers to take into account only a subset of relevant information, while unconscious thought has a broader focus allowing larger amounts of information to be integrated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p370]

## Inferences

- Inference: The unconscious thought effect may be particularly relevant for complex decisions that require integrating large amounts of information.
- Inference: Unconscious thought may facilitate reorganization of representations, leading to clustering and polarization of information.
