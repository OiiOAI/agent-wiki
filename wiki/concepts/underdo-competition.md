# Underdo Your Competition

## Summary

The concept of 'underdoing' competition means deliberately offering less than competitors rather than trying to outdo them with more features, lower prices, or larger teams. The authors argue that competing on having more leads to costly arms races and forces companies to be constantly defensive. Instead, they advocate solving only the simple problems and leaving difficult, complex problems to competitors. Examples include fixed-gear bicycles that have no gears or brakes, and the Flip camcorder which deliberately lacks common features like optical zoom or photo-taking ability.

## Key facts

- The authors argue against the Cold War mentality of one-upping competitors, stating it leads to never-ending battles costing massive amounts of money, time, and drive. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p107]
- Fixed-gear bicycles have boomed in popularity despite being low-tech, offering just one gear and sometimes no brakes, because they are simpler, lighter, cheaper, and require less maintenance. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p107]
- The Flip camcorder succeeded by deliberately lacking features like big screens, photo-taking ability, tapes or discs, menus, settings, video light, viewfinder, special effects, headphone jack, lens cap, memory card, and optical zoom. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p107]
- The authors advise highlighting the fact that your product does less and selling it as aggressively as competitors sell their extensive feature lists. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p108]

## Inferences

- Inference: The underdo strategy works because it forces focus on core functionality and user experience rather than feature proliferation.
