# Sensemaking

## Summary

Sensemaking is the process of sizing up situations and making meaningful interpretations of events. Introduced by Karl Weick, sensemaking describes what happens when we detect anomalies and must reinterpret our understanding of a situation. The process involves seeing cues, recognizing patterns, and generating stories and explanations to account for discrepancies. Gary Klein expanded the Recognition-Primed Decision model to include storybuilding for the approximately 10% of cases where pattern recognition is insufficient and conscious story construction is needed. Sensemaking can fail due to the 'mindset problem'—fixation on an initial interpretation that causes people to explain away contradictory evidence.

## Key facts

- Sensemaking is needed when we might identify several different patterns, or when we are having trouble finding any patterns. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p179]
- Storybuilding is required in about 10% of real-world crises where pattern recognition is insufficient. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p179]
- The mindset problem causes fixation—constructing a story, deciding on an interpretation, and locking into it while resisting signals that the story is inaccurate. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p180]
- Strategies to break a mindset include testing for fixation, assessing map-bending, setting tripwires, writing multiple scenarios, replacing interpretations, comparing options, and learning from breakdowns. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p181-185]
- Noise—irrelevant data that overlaps relevant data—is a pervasive barrier to accurate sensemaking. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p175]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of 'bending the map' (from hiking terminology) describes how initial navigational errors compound as people explain away subsequent anomalies to maintain consistency with their original erroneous belief
