# Sexual Conflict

## Summary

Sexual conflict describes the evolutionary conflict of interest between males and females over reproduction and parental investment. Because males can potentially father many more offspring than females (due to the asymmetry between small, numerous sperm and large, costly eggs), each sex has incentive to exploit the other—males to desert and seek additional mates, females to secure male commitment. The book analyzes this using ESS (Evolutionarily Stable Strategy) game theory with female strategies (coy vs. fast) and male strategies (faithful vs. philanderer), deriving stable population ratios.

## Key facts

- Sexual conflict arises because fathers and mothers are both interested in different halves of the same children, and each partner tries to exploit the other to invest more—ideally each would copulate with many mates and leave the partner to rear the children. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p176]
- The fundamental nature of maleness and femaleness stems from gamete asymmetry: males produce many small sperm while females produce few large eggs with extensive food reserves, leading to asymmetric parental investment from conception. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p177]
- Parker and others showed how male-female asymmetry could have evolved from an originally isogamous state: larger gametes had advantages, but this opened the door to exploitation by individuals producing smaller, more mobile gametes that sought out and fused with large ones, leading to the divergence into eggs and sperm. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p178]
- Fisher's principle states that the evolutionarily stable sex ratio is 50:50 under normal circumstances, because once one sex becomes rarer it becomes more valuable to produce offspring of that sex, driving ratios back toward equilibrium. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p180]
- Using game theory with coy/fast female strategies and faithful/philanderer male strategies, and arbitrary numbers for costs and benefits, the stable ESS ratio is approximately 5/6 of females coy and 5/6 of males faithful, showing the domestic-bliss strategy can be evolutionarily stable. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p189]
- In species where females favor the he-man strategy, they exercise maximum discrimination in mate choice, allowing only the best males to reproduce—this explains elephant seal harems and bird of paradise displays as cases where females, unable to get male help, instead seek good genes for their offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p193]
- Zahavi's handicap principle suggests that handicaps like peacock tails and deer antlers evolve because only genuinely fit males can survive despite the handicap, and females who choose handicapped males get good genes for their sons who will also be attractive to females of the next generation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p196]
- The Bruce effect (female mice aborting pregnancies when detecting a new male's scent) is explained as male mice destroying potential step-children to make females receptive to their advances, rather than as a group-level population control mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p183]

## Inferences

- Inference: Paternal care is common in fish but rare in land animals because in external fertilization neither parent is left 'holding the baby,' but in internal fertilization the female is left in possession of the embryo—giving males the prior decision to desert and placing females in Trivers's 'cruel bind'.
- Inference: The observation that human females rather than males typically engage in sexual advertisement (makeup, flamboyant dress) suggests, on evolutionary grounds, that in modern Western society females may compete for males rather than vice versa, contrary to typical animal patterns where males advertise.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Zahavi's handicap principle can be successfully formalized in mathematical models—Dawkins notes mathematical geneticists have so far failed to make it work, and expresses skepticism though not complete confidence.
