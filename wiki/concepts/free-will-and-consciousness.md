# Free Will and Consciousness

## Summary

Dehaene argues that free will requires distinguishing two intuitions: fundamental indeterminacy (which he considers dubious) and autonomy (which he considers the respectable notion). A decision is genuinely free when based on conscious deliberation that proceeds autonomously, carefully weighing pros and cons before committing to action—requiring no appeal to quantum physics.

## Key facts

- Dehaene distinguishes two intuitions about decisions: their fundamental indeterminacy (a dubious idea) and their autonomy (a respectable notion). [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p324]
- Brain states are not uncaused and do not escape the laws of physics, but decisions are genuinely free whenever they are based on conscious deliberation proceeding autonomously without impediment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p325]
- The global neuronal workspace allows collecting information from senses and memories, synthesizing it, evaluating consequences, pondering them, and using this reflection to guide actions—this is a willed decision. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p325]
- Daniel Dennett argues that pure randomness in the brain does not provide any 'kind of freedom worth having.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p324]

## Inferences
