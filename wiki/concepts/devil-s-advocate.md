# Devil's Advocate

## Summary

A devil's advocate is someone assigned to present arguments against a proposed position, strategy, or idea. The practice originated with the Catholic Church during canonization proceedings. Modern applications include CIA red teams, State Department Dissent Channels, and business practices where someone is tasked with arguing why a strategy might be ill-advised. Within truth-seeking groups, operationalizing skepticism through designated devil's advocates naturally raises alternate hypotheses and encourages open-minded consideration of alternatives.

## Key facts

- The term 'devil's advocate' developed from the Catholic Church's practice during canonization of hiring someone to present arguments against sainthood. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- The CIA uses red teams and the State Department has its Dissent Channel as forms of organized skepticism. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- Companies can implement anonymous dissent channels for employees to express dissenting opinions without fear of repercussions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- When seeking advice, asking specific questions that encourage others to find reasons why you might be wrong helps them feel comfortable disagreeing. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
