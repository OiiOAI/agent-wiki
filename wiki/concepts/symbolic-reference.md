# Symbolic Reference

## Summary

Symbolic reference is the unique mode of representation used in human language, where words and signs refer to objects, events, and relationships through arbitrary conventional relationships rather than through physical similarity or direct causal connection. Unlike iconic reference (based on similarity) or indexical reference (based on correlation), symbolic reference allows reference to abstract concepts and things not physically present, and maintains its meaning even without constant correlation between sign and referent.

## Key facts

- Symbolic reference differs from indexical reference in that it doesn't depend on physical co-occurrence or correlation with the referent; the word 'skunk' can be used frequently without encountering actual skunks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p70]
- The word 'skunk' refers to skunks even though speakers rarely encounter them, unlike alarm calls which depend on the presence of predators to maintain their reference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p70]
- Symbolic reference depends on the ability to interpret something in terms of conventional relationships, independent of direct experience with the referent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p74]
- In symbolic reference, token-token relationships form a closed logical group of combinatorial possibilities that determines reference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p92]
- Symbolic reference survives even when token-object correlations are rare or absent, as with unicorns or angels. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p87]
- The shift from indexical to symbolic reference is initially a change in mnemonic strategy—a recoding that recognizes higher-order regularity in associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p95]
- Compete lexigram-lexigram relationships form a complete system where each allowable or forbidden co-occurrence is defined. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p92]

## Inferences

- Inference: Symbolic reference is the core barrier preventing nonhuman species from developing language, not the complexity of language structure itself. [Based on: pp.42-44, where Deacon argues that stripping away complexity leaves only the problem of symbolic reference]
- Inference: Symbolic reference enables categorical generalization—the ability to correctly interpret novel tokens without trial-and-error, because the system of relationships constrains possibilities.
