# Freudian Ego

## Summary

A concept representing the organized, rational part of the psyche that mediates between the demands of the id, the superego, and reality. Freud described the ego as the external, peripheral layer of the id, modified by the influence of the external world. The ego is characterized by a trend toward unification and synthesis, and it operates according to the reality principle, mediating between the id's demands for immediate satisfaction and the constraints of external reality.

## Key facts

- The ego is an organization characterized by a very remarkable trend towards unification, towards synthesis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6407]
- The ego observes the external world with the help of its sense organ, the system of consciousness, so as to catch the favourable moment for harmless satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6412]
- The ego tames the id's impulses by replacing the pleasure principle with the reality principle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6412]
- The ego can intervene in the external world by changing it and establishing conditions for satisfaction—this becomes the ego's highest function. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6412]
- Large portions of the ego can remain permanently unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6409]
