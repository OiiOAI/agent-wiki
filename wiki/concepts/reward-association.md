# Reward Association

## Summary

Reward association is the process of attaching rewards to behaviors to encourage repetition. The brain's reward centers can be activated by unrelated rewards, creating new habit associations. Rewards also appear to restore willpower, not just encourage behavior. The strategy is to initially attach rewards to help form habits, then gradually let the behavior's natural satisfaction sustain it.

## Key facts

- Secondary rewards like sculpted abs or feeling great take longer to 'take hold' in the brain compared to primary sensory rewards like sugar that immediately activate the brain's reward centers. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p98]
- If exercise's endorphins and promise of eventual results don't sufficiently reward the brain initially, unrelated rewards can be attached until the brain associates the behavior with the reward. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p98]
- Laughter releases feel-good chemicals in the brain and can serve as a creative, unrelated reward for behaviors like writing. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p99]
- Celebrating wins is shown to be the most effective strategy for making people feel good about their lives, supporting Mini Habits' focus on celebrating small successes. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p99]
- Rewards of all kinds may restore willpower; a study found that swishing sugar solution (but not artificial sweetener) restored willpower despite not restoring glucose levels. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p101]
- Non-food rewards may be effective in restoring willpower, which is good news for people who want to lose weight. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p101]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mini habits benefit from reward association in two ways: encouraging repeated behavior and restoring the willpower needed to perform future mini habits.
