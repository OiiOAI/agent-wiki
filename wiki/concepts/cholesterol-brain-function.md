# Cholesterol in Brain Function

## Summary

Cholesterol plays essential roles in central nervous system function, with complex relationships between serum cholesterol levels and cognitive performance. Higher cholesterol levels in older age have been associated with better memory function, challenging the notion that lower cholesterol is universally beneficial for brain health.

## Key facts

- Cholesterol has specific regulation and role in central nervous system disorders. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Serum cholesterol levels are associated with cognitive performance in the Framingham Heart Study. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Higher total and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol levels are associated with better memory function in very elderly subjects. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- High total cholesterol levels in late life are associated with a reduced risk of dementia. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Cholesterol affects learning and memory through various neurological mechanisms. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]

## Inferences

- Inference: The relationship between cholesterol and brain function appears to be age-dependent, with higher late-life cholesterol potentially having protective effects against cognitive decline.
