# Emotions as Fixed Action Patterns

## Summary

Emotions are proposed as FAPs where the actions are not motor but premotor. They provide the premotor platform as drives or deterrents for most actions. Emotional states trigger motor FAPs and are accompanied by motor expressions (facial expressions, postures). The hypothalamus orchestrates the vegetative and endocrine accompaniments, while the amygdala generates the emotional feeling itself.

## Key facts

- Emotions are elements in the class of FAPs where the actions are not motor but premotor, serving as the premotor platform for drives or deterrents. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p160]
- Damage to the cingulate cortex may interfere with the feeling of pain and other emotional components, demonstrating the distributed nature of emotional processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p165]
- Stimulation of the amygdala by electrical activation or excitatory amino acids elicits physiological and behavioral signs consistent with fear and distress. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p169]
- Lesions of the amygdala produce Klüver-Bucy syndrome, where animals are unusually placid and emotionally unengaged, unable to generate emotional states. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p169]
- Sensations are intrinsic events of the nervous system, constructable in the absence of sensory pathway activation, as demonstrated during dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p165]
- Consciousness represents the solution for an overcomplete system, similar to FAPs—focusing to reduce choices of where attention is best directed. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: Emotions and their motor expressions both qualify as FAPs, indicating that the brain organizes affective and motor function through similar modular mechanisms.
