# Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve

## Summary

The Ebbinghaus forgetting curve describes the pattern of memory loss over time, showing rapid initial decline in the first hour after learning followed by a more gradual decline continuing for about a month. Hermann Ebbinghaus developed rigorous experimental methods for measuring memory using nonsense words that remain in use today.

## Key facts

- Ebbinghaus tested himself at various intervals after learning and determined the time to relearn each list, finding 'savings' in relearning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p221]
- Forgetting had at least two phases: a rapid initial decline sharpest in the first hour, then a much more gradual decline continuing for about a month. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p222]
- Ebbinghaus found a linear relationship between training repetitions on the first day and material retained the following day—practice makes perfect. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p221]
- He found that a list of six or seven items could be learned and retained in one presentation, while longer lists required repeated presentations. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p222]
- Ebbinghaus tested himself with thousands of nonsense syllables at six different times after studying, ranging from one hour to one month. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p29]
- The forgetting curve shows rapid drop-off in retention during the first few tests, with the rate slowing considerably afterward. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p30]
- Modern research has extended the forgetting curve to everyday events, demonstrating similar rapid initial forgetting followed by slower decline. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p30]
- Studies of Thanksgiving dinner memories showed vividness declined rapidly over three months, followed by gradual decline, consistent with the Ebbinghaus curve. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p31]
