# The Buddy System (Chapter 4)

## Summary

Chapter 4 of 'Thinking in Bets' titled 'The Buddy System' explores how truthseeking pods—groups committed to accuracy, accountability, and diversity—help individuals overcome cognitive biases. It uses the David Letterman-Lauren Conrad exchange as an example of inappropriate truthseeking, and provides evidence from AA, federal judges, and social psychologists showing how groups can both help and hinder objective thinking.

## Key facts

- Not all situations are appropriate for truthseeking, nor are all people interested in the pursuit. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1100-L1105]
- The chapter establishes that even with help, no one will ever perfectly overcome natural biases, but finding people to form a truthseeking pod will move the needle, with improvements that accumulate and compound over time. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1195-L1200]
- A productive decision-making pod requires: focus on accuracy (over confirmation), advance accountability, and openness to a diversity of ideas. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1245-L1255]
- The chapter provides examples from AA, Sunstein's study of federal judges, and Heterodox Academy to illustrate group dynamics and confirmatory drift. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1210-L1410]
