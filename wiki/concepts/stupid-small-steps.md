# Stupid Small Steps

## Summary

A strategy in mini habits where goals are scaled down to ridiculously small levels—such as opening a clothes drawer instead of completing a full workout, or doing one push-up—to bypass resistance and get started. The 'stupid' label indicates that the steps feel too trivial for the person's pride, but this triviality is precisely what allows action despite resistance.

## Key facts

- Stupid small steps work by sliding under the brain's resistance mechanisms, similar to how a jewel thief avoids security cameras. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p121]
- Examples include driving to the gym instead of working out, opening a clothes drawer, or doing one push-up. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p121]
- When a step 'sounds stupid,' that indicates the brain is giving permission to proceed, because the resistance threshold hasn't been triggered. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p121]
- The strategy applies even during burnout: one can say 'could we just do one push-up right now?'. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p121]
- The author's One Push-up Challenge started with feeling 'above' doing one push-up but eventually led to 30 minutes of exercise. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p122]
- The author writes that 'every giant accomplishment is made of very small steps,' making the approach precise rather than weak. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p122]

## Inferences

- Inference: The technique leverages the insight that resistance is often disproportionate to actual difficulty—making something trivially easy eliminates the source of resistance entirely.
