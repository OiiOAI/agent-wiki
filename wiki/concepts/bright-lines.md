# Bright Lines

## Summary

Bright lines are clear, simple, unambiguous rules that serve as powerful self-control tools. Coined by psychiatrist and behavioral economist George Ainslie, the concept addresses hyperbolic discounting—our tendency to value immediate rewards disproportionately over distant goals. When temptations are far away, we can resist them, but as they approach, they loom larger and our judgment warps. Bright lines provide an absolute standard ('total abstinence') rather than fuzzy boundaries ('moderate drinking') that are easier to rationalize around. Rules become especially powerful when framed as sacred or tied to a higher power.

## Key facts

- Bright lines are clear, simple, unambiguous rules that cannot be violated without obvious notice. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p171]
- Zero tolerance (total abstinence with no exceptions) is a bright line that works well in many self-control situations. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p171]
- When a rule is considered sacred—a commandment from God—the bright line becomes especially powerful through a self-fulfilling mandate. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p171]
- Eric Clapton's compulsion to drink vanished the instant he knelt at Hazelden and asked for God's help, after which he never doubted he would have the will to remain sober. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p172]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bright lines may work by reducing decision fatigue in tempting situations—when the rule is absolute, no mental energy is spent debating exceptions.
