# Modern Marriage

## Summary

Modern marriage differs fundamentally from historical forms. Where marriage was once an economic arrangement between families, contemporary marriage is a companionate relationship based on love, emotional intimacy, and sexual satisfaction. Perel argues that this transformation has raised expectations exponentially—modern spouses must be best friends, confidants, and passionate lovers simultaneously. This heightened demand may paradoxically make marriages more fragile and infidelity more tempting.

## Key facts

- For millennia, matrimony was less a union of two individuals than a strategic partnership between two families for economic survival and social cohesion. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p41]
- Traditional marital fidelity had nothing to do with love—it was a mainstay of patriarchy to ensure patrimony and lineage. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p42]
- The double standard for infidelity is as old as adultery itself, with men having tacit permission to stray while women faced severe consequences. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p42]
- Modern marriage now requires partners to be soul mates, lovers, teachers, and therapists simultaneously. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p46]
- Monogamy has shifted from meaning 'one person for life' to meaning 'one person at a time.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p44]
- Romantic love has become the dominant religion in Western culture, supplanting institutional religion as the arena for seeking meaning and transcendence. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p46]

## Inferences

- Inference: The rise of individualistic values and consumer culture has fundamentally transformed both why people marry and why they might betray that marriage.
- Inference: Modern couples face a paradox: they depend emotionally on their partners more than ever while having more freedom to leave.
