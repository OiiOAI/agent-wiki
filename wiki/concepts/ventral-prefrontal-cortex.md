# Ventral Prefrontal Cortex

## Summary

The ventral prefrontal cortex is a brain region consistently activated during word analysis tasks and language processing involving semantic operations. It is the principal recipient and source of connections linking auditory areas with other prefrontal and frontal areas, and plays critical roles in naming, grammatical decisions, verbal short-term recall, and verb-for-noun production.

## Key facts

- Electrical stimulation studies show the ventral prefrontal region is the principal recipient and source of connections linking auditory areas with other prefrontal and frontal areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p321]
- Simple word perception tasks activate the ventral prefrontal cortex, and this region is partially damaged in Broca's aphasics with large lesions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p319]
- The ventral prefrontal region is associated with naming and short-term verbal memory as shown by both stimulation studies and word list generation tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p319]
- PET studies show the left ventral prefrontal region is activated during word association tasks and grammatical/lexical analysis of words. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p328]
- The ventral prefrontal region does not appear to be involved in naming familiar objects, which instead recruits different sectors of the left middle and ventral temporal lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p329]
