# Evolutionary Epistemology

## Summary

Karl Popper's evolutionary epistemology posits that science makes progress through falsification. Theories are speculative conjectures tested by observation and experiment – those that fail must be eliminated and replaced. Popper named this process 'evolutionary epistemology' because knowledge grows through conjecture and refutation resembling natural selection.

## Key facts

- Popper advanced the idea that science made progress through falsification – a theory's claim to truth is only possible if it can be shown to be false. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p94]
- Popper asserted that theories are construed as speculative and tentative conjectures or guesses freely created by the human intellect in attempts to overcome problems encountered by previous theories. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p94]
- In 1961 Popper claimed that growth of knowledge 'is the result of a process closely resembling what Darwin called natural selection; that is, the natural selection of hypotheses'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p94]
- Boyd's OODA loop can be construed as a process of hypothesis testing – in The Conceptual Spiral he defined science as 'a self-correcting process of observation, hypothesis, and test'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p94]
- Richard Dawkins' concept of 'memes' as cultural replicators paralleled Popper's evolutionary epistemology – ideas replicate like genes and survive, mutate, or become extinct depending on their contribution to survival. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p95]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd incorporated Popper's evolutionary epistemology into his OODA loop concept as a model for how strategic decision-making should function as continuous hypothesis testing.
