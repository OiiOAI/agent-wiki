# Autonomic Nervous System

## Summary

The autonomic nervous system regulates involuntary physiological functions by controlling smooth muscle, cardiac muscle, and glands. It consists of three divisions: sympathetic, parasympathetic, and enteric. Autonomic motor neurons lie in ganglia outside the CNS and are controlled by preganglionic neurons in the brain stem and spinal cord.

## Key facts

- The autonomic system is divided into three divisions: sympathetic, parasympathetic, and enteric. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1063]
- All preganglionic autonomic neurons synthesize and release acetylcholine (ACh), which acts on nicotinic receptors on postganglionic neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1066]
- Sympathetic postganglionic neurons release norepinephrine; parasympathetic postganglionic neurons release acetylcholine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1066]
- The enteric nervous system contains as many as 100 million neurons and can maintain basic gastrointestinal functions even after sympathetic and parasympathetic pathways are cut. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1066]
- The baroreceptor reflex prevents orthostatic hypotension and fainting by compensating for rapid hydrostatic effects produced by changes in posture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1070]
