# Mental Attainment

## Summary

Mental attainment is the cognitive mechanism Oettingen proposes to explain why positive fantasies reduce motivation. When people fantasize about achieving a goal, their minds are fooled into believing the goal has already been achieved, creating a temporary sense of success and relaxation. This mental substitution for actual achievement reduces the perceived need for action, lowering physiological arousal (blood pressure) and ultimately diminishing actual performance. The phenomenon connects to David Hume's eighteenth-century observation that imagined sensations can rile us up as much as actual sensations.

## Key facts

- Mental attainment refers to the phenomenon where fantasizing about a goal fools the mind into actually believing that goal has been achieved. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p49]
- Research showed that the more positively students fantasized about taking revenge, the slower they were at identifying words relating to violence and aggression, suggesting mental goal attainment. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p51]
- Mental attainment causes people to believe they don't need to exercise or take other action to achieve goals because they have already mentally experienced success. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p51]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mental attainment appears to be the mechanism underlying the relaxing effect of positive fantasies and explains why fantasizing about goals produces the opposite of the energizing effect people expect.
