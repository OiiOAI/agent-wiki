# PN, PS, and PNS

## Summary

PN (Probability of Necessity), PS (Probability of Sufficiency), and PNS (Probability of Necessity and Sufficiency) are formal measures of causal attribution. These concepts enable the assessment of 'but-for' causation and were systematized by Tian and Pearl (2000) and further developed in Pearl (2009, Chapter 9).

## Key facts

- PN, PS, and PNS measures are based on Tian and Pearl (2000) and Pearl (2009, Chapter 9). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3812-L3813]
- Greiner (2008) provides a substantive introduction to 'but-for' causation in the law. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3851-L3852]

## Inferences

- Inference: PN measures the probability that the outcome would not have occurred absent the cause; PS measures the probability that the outcome would have occurred with the cause even without it.
