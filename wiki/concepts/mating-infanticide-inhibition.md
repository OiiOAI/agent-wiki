# Mating and Infanticide Inhibition

## Summary

Male rats exhibit a naturally occurring inhibition of infanticide following sexual experience. Males initially show approximately 80% infanticide rates at the beginning of testing, which gradually diminish to about 20% at three weeks following a single mating experience, then gradually return to original levels. This appears to represent a form of sociosexual memory whereby males recognize, on the basis of their own prior sexual activity, which pups are likely to be their own offspring.

## Key facts

- Male rats exhibit approximately 80% infanticide rates at the beginning of testing, which gradually diminish to approximately 20% at three weeks following a single mating experience, then gradually return to original levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p110]
- The timing of infanticide reduction corresponds to the approximately three-week gestational period in female rats, at about the time the male's own offspring might be born. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p110]
- Oxytocin, which is more prevalent in females than males, may promote the gradual induction of sociosexual memory that inhibits infanticide following mating. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p110]
- Infanticide in males is an evolutionarily adaptive strategy that eliminates lactation-induced infertility and increases the probability that new males can rapidly fertilize available females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p110]

## Inferences

- Inference: Males should have natural evolutionarily derived inhibitions against harming their own offspring, evolved to prevent destruction of their genetic investment.
