# Prefrontal leucotomy

## Summary

Prefrontal leucotomy was a surgical procedure developed by Egas Moniz and Almeida Lima for the treatment of mental illness and, in modified forms, for intractable pain. The procedure involved cutting connections in the prefrontal cortex. Observations of patients who underwent this operation for pain management revealed the crucial distinction between the sensory perception of pain and suffering: while patients still reported that 'the pains are the same' after surgery, they no longer experienced the emotional distress of suffering. Damasio notes that while the procedure relieved suffering, it had the negative consequence of blunting emotion and feeling.

## Key facts

- Prefrontal leucotomy was developed by Egas Moniz and Almeida Lima as a treatment for psychiatric conditions and later modified for management of intractable pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p285]
- The leucotomy operation appeared to abolish the emotional reaction that is part of what we call pain, ending the patient's suffering while leaving the 'pain image' (somatosensory mapping of pain landscape) intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p286]
- The leucotomy had a negative result: the blunting of emotion and feeling, whose long-term consequences are only now being appreciated fully. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p310]

## Inferences

- Inference: The case of prefrontal leucotomy provides evidence for the separation between the neural substrates of sensory pain perception and emotional suffering. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p286]
