# Separation Distress and Social Attachment

## Summary

Separation distress is a fundamental emotional response to social isolation that is mediated by specific brain systems. Social attachment and bonding serve as regulators that suppress separation distress. The brain systems for social separation-distress and social-reward are proposed to have evolutionary antecedents and neuropeptide intermediaries.

## Key facts

- Brain systems exist for the mediation of social separation-distress and social-reward with evolutionary antecedents and neuropeptide intermediaries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p207]
- Social attachment relationships serve as regulators of separation distress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p208]
- The smell of home is a potent alleviator of separation distress in rodents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p207]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mechanisms of social attachment that regulate separation distress likely provide a neural substrate for the concept of 'home' as a psychological construct, connecting social bonding to place attachment.
