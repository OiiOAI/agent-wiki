# The Aten Religion

## Summary

The Aten Religion was the monotheistic religious reform introduced by Pharaoh Akhenaten (c. 1375–1358 BCE), worshipping the Aten (sun disk) as the sole god. It rejected magic, sorcery, Osiris worship, and prohibited divine images, representing the first known attempt at strict monotheism in history. According to Freud, this religion was transmitted to the Jewish people through Moses.

## Key facts

- The Aten Religion was a strict monotheism that excluded all other deities and required belief in a single god. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7196]
- The religion excluded all elements of myth, magic, and sorcery, which proliferated in popular Egyptian religion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]
- The Aten was represented only as a round disk with rays ending in human hands—no personal image of the god was permitted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]
- The religion maintained complete silence about Osiris and the afterlife, in stark contrast to mainstream Egyptian religion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]
- According to Freud, Moses (who was Egyptian) transmitted this religion to the Jewish people. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7198]
- The religion declined and was abolished after Akhenaten's death, with his capital destroyed and his memory proscribed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Aten Religion shares key characteristics with later Jewish monotheism: rejection of magic, no image of God, silence about the afterlife—which Freud attributes to direct historical transmission via Moses.
