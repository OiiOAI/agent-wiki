# Blocking (Memory)

## Summary

Blocking is the sin of memory where information that has been encoded and stored cannot be retrieved, even though retrieval cues may be available. Unlike transience, the information has not faded; unlike absent-mindedness, attention was present during encoding. Blocking occurs most frequently with people's names due to the tenuous link between person identity nodes and lexical representations in the Burke and MacKay model.

## Key facts

- Blocking involves a kind of forgetting where the recalcitrant name or word has been encoded and stored but cannot be retrieved when needed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p93]
- Blocking on the names of familiar people is the single biggest memory complaint of adults past age fifty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p94]
- Proper names are more susceptible to blocking than common names because they are not connotative and lack associations with attributes of their bearers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p94]
- The Baker/baker paradox shows that recall of occupations is higher than recall of proper names even when the same words are used. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p95]
- In the Burke and MacKay model, proper names have a single fragile link between person identity nodes and lexical representations, unlike common names which have multiple converging links. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p97]
- People frequently recall a person's occupation without remembering their name, but no instances have been documented where a name is recalled without any conceptual knowledge about the person. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p96]
