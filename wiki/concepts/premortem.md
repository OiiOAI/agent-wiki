# PreMortem

## Summary

A PreMortem is a technique where team members anticipate potential failures before implementing a plan. Participants write down ways the plan could fail, helping identify blind spots and strengthening the statement of intent. It shifts team members from passive listeners to active anticipators.

## Key facts

- In workshops, participants issue actual intent statements they will use with their work teams, and other participants perform a PreMortem to identify potential confusions. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p256]
- The PreMortem exercise helps participants recognize how vague their intent statements actually are, which they often don't see until receiving feedback. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p256]
- For important tasks, it is worth the extra time to do a PreMortem on the task and intent, giving everyone a few minutes to write down ways they can become confused or mishandle the request. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p256]
- A premortem starts with the assumption that a plan or goal has failed, then identifies all possible reasons for that failure before implementation. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1924-L1927]
- Gabriele Oettingen's research found that subjects who imagined obstacles to their goals (mental contrasting) were more likely to achieve them than those who engaged in pure positive visualization; dreamers in weight loss studies lost 24 pounds less than negative imagers. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1937-L1943]
- Premortems implement the Mertonian norm of organized skepticism by changing group dynamics to give permission for dissent, with 'winning' being the most productive heckler. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1950-L1953]
- Backcasting and premortems complement each other: backcasting reveals the positive space of possible futures while premortems reveal the negative space. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1957-L1959]
- The premortem is conducted after a decision has been made in principle but before formal commitment, when team members imagine the plan failed and write a brief history of the disaster. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p317]
- Gary Klein, who usually defends intuitive decision-making, developed the premortem as a method to reduce overconfidence and improve organizational decision quality. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p317]
- The premortem has two advantages: it overcomes groupthink by legitimizing doubt, and it directs imagination toward potential threats that were previously ignored. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p317]
- Kahneman describes the premortem as 'not a complete cure' for overconfidence but as a technique that reduces damage from WYSIATI-based planning and uncritical optimism. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p317]

## Inferences

- Inference: The PreMortem technique is particularly valuable when combined with the STICC framework, as it addresses the calibration step by proactively identifying misunderstanding risks.
- Inference: A successful premortem creates organizational health by ensuring dissenting voices are represented in planning, reducing resentment if goals are not met and preventing suppression of diverse opinions.
- Inference: The premortem works because after a decision has been made, doubt is normally suppressed as evidence of disloyalty; the premortem creates a safe space for critical evaluation at a point when critical voices are typically silenced.
