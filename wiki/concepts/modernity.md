# Modernity (Taleb's Definition)

## Summary

Taleb defines modernity as humans' large-scale domination of the environment, the systematic smoothing of the world's jaggedness, and the stifling of volatility and stressors. Modernity represents the transfer of agency from natural systems and gods to rational human design, characterized by naive rationalism, statistics, optimization, and the denial of antifragility.

## Key facts

- Modernity is defined as humans' large-scale domination of the environment, systematic smoothing of the world's jaggedness, and stifling of volatility and stressors. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p146]
- Modernity corresponds to the extraction of humans from their randomness-laden ecology—physical, social, and epistemological—with the spirit of rationalization that society is understandable and must be designed. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p146]
- The emergence of the nation-state falls squarely into modernity—the concentration and magnification of human errors, moving from state monopoly on violence to monopoly on fiscal irresponsibility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p147]
- Modernity widened the difference between the sensational and the relevant; in a natural environment the sensational is sensational for a reason, but today we depend on press for essentially human things like gossip and remote anecdotes. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p147]

## Inferences

- Inference: Taleb's critique of modernity suggests that the drive to eliminate uncertainty, variation, and disorder is itself the primary source of fragility in contemporary systems.
