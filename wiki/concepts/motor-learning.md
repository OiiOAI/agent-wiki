# Motor Learning

## Summary

Motor learning refers to the processes by which the nervous system acquires new motor skills and adapts to changes in the sensorimotor system. It encompasses error-based learning (adapting internal models based on sensory prediction errors), reinforcement learning (adjusting actions to maximize reward), and skill learning (improving speed-accuracy trade-offs). Learning can be implicit (procedural) or explicit (declarative).

## Key facts

- Motor learning can be divided into error-based learning, which adapts internal models to perturbations, and skill learning, which improves performance in the absence of perturbations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p730]
- In error-based learning, the difference between predicted and actual outcomes (sensory prediction error) is used to update internal models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p730]
- Reinforcement learning adjusts motor commands to maximize reward and is particularly important for tasks requiring complex action sequences where error signals are delayed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p734]
- Motor adaptation involves multiple processes with different temporal properties: a fast process that adapts quickly but forgets rapidly, and a slow process with better retention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p779]
- The cerebellum is strongly implicated in fast trial-by-trial error-based learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p779]
- Once learned, deadaptation typically occurs faster than original learning, but associated sensory inputs can evoke retained adaptive behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p779]
- Motor experience can modify the motor map; in monkeys trained to use precise thumb, index finger, and wrist movements to extract treats, the area from which trained movements could be evoked by ICMS expanded substantially. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p854]
- The areas of the motor output map parallel the level of performance during acquisition of motor skill and decrease as skill is extinguished through lack of practice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p854]
- Motor adaptation to visual or mechanical disturbances leads to changes in the activity of M1 neurons, with changes specific to neurons that would make the greatest contribution to compensate for the external perturbation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p855]
- Error signals in M1 drive adaptation in reaching; M1 activity during postmovement visual feedback reflects the direction of reach endpoint errors, and ICMS simulating these error responses causes adaptive changes without actual reaching errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p856]
- Spike-timing dependent plasticity within local horizontal connections linking different parts of the arm motor map contributes to cortical reorganization underlying improved motor performance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p855]
- Monkeys could learn 'unnatural' changes in M1 neural activity decoder mapping if allowed enough practice over several days, demonstrating that neural circuits can learn new neural co-modulation structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p856]
- Most forms of motor learning involve procedural or implicit learning, so-called because subjects are generally unable to express what they have learned, and it can be retained for extended periods without practice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p813]
- Motor learning can adapt to novel dynamic conditions by learning an internal model that compensates for unexpected forces, demonstrated by after-effects when the force is removed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p814]
- Kinematic and dynamic motor learning rely on different sensory modalities: proprioception is more important for dynamic tasks, whereas kinematic adaptation can occur with visual guidance alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p814]
- Patients who have lost proprioception have particular difficulty controlling the dynamic properties of their limbs or learning new dynamic tasks without vision. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p814]

## Inferences

- Inference: Motor learning timescales vary from immediate adaptation to years of practice, reflecting the complexity of tasks and the need to unlearn evolutionary constraints.
