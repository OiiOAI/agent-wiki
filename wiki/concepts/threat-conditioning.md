# Threat Conditioning

## Summary

Threat conditioning (formerly called fear conditioning) is a Pavlovian conditioning procedure in which an innocuous conditioned stimulus (CS) is paired with an aversive unconditioned stimulus (US), typically footshock. After conditioning, the CS elicits innate defensive responses. LeDoux proposes using 'threat conditioning' instead of 'fear conditioning' to distinguish between nonconscious threat processing and conscious fear feelings.

## Key facts

- In threat conditioning, a neutral conditioned stimulus (CS) is paired with an aversive unconditioned stimulus (US), after which the CS alone elicits defensive responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p55]
- LeDoux proposes using 'threat conditioning' instead of 'fear conditioning' to avoid conflating nonconscious threat detection with conscious fear feelings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p71]
- The amygdala circuitry involved in threat conditioning detects threats and orchestrates defensive responses, but does not itself generate fearful feelings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p74]
- Threat conditioning can be used in both animals and humans to study the neural basis of defensive responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p106]
