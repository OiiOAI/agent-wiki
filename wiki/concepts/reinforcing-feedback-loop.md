# Reinforcing Feedback Loop

## Summary

A reinforcing feedback loop is an amplifying, self-multiplying structure that enhances whatever direction of change is imposed on it. It generates more input to a stock the more that is already there. Reinforcing loops are found wherever a system element has the ability to reproduce itself or grow as a constant fraction of itself. They are responsible for exponential growth or runaway collapses.

## Key facts

- Reinforcing feedback loops are self-enhancing, leading to exponential growth or to runaway collapses over time. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p50]
- The time it takes for an exponentially growing stock to double in size equals approximately 70 divided by the growth rate expressed as a percentage. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p51]
- Reinforcing loops are found wherever a stock has the capacity to reinforce or reproduce itself, including populations and economies. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p50]
- Systems with similar feedback structures produce similar dynamic behaviors even if the outward appearance of these systems is completely dissimilar. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p71]

## Inferences

- Inference: The doubling time shortcut (70/growth rate) provides a practical tool for understanding exponential growth in populations, economies, or any reinforcing system.
