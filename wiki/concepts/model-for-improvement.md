# Model for Improvement

## Summary

The Model for Improvement, developed by Langley, Nolan, and Nolan, supplements the PDSA cycle with three basic questions: What are we trying to accomplish? How will we know if a change is an improvement? What changes can we make that will result in improvement? It was expanded in 'The Improvement Guide' as a framework for the science of improvement.

## Key facts

- Langley, Nolan, and Nolan added three basic questions to supplement the PDSA cycle in 1994. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p8]
- In 1991, Moen, Nolan and Provost added to the planning step of the improvement cycle required prediction and associated theory, with the study step comparing observed data to prediction as a basis for learning. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p8]
- The Improvement Guide expands on The Model for Improvement as a basic framework for the science of improvement, supporting efforts from the informal to the most complex. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p8]
- The 7 basic tools (check sheet, histograms, Pareto chart, fishbone diagram, graphs, scatter diagrams, and stratification) together with the PDCA cycle and the QC story format became the foundation for kaizen in Japan. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p5]
