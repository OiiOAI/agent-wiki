# FEAR System (Brain Circuit)

## Summary

The FEAR system is a neural circuit that courses from the lateral and central nuclei of the amygdala, through the anterior and medial hypothalamus, down to the periaqueductal gray (PAG) of the midbrain. This system generates the emotional experience of fear along with characteristic autonomic responses (increased heart rate, blood pressure, startle reflex, elimination) and behavioral responses including freezing at low intensities and flight at higher intensities. Minor tranquilizers exert their antianxiety effects by reducing activity in this circuit.

## Key facts

- The FEAR circuit extends from the central and lateral amygdala through the anterior and medial hypothalamus to the periaqueductal gray (PAG) of the midbrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p112]
- Electrical stimulation of the FEAR system produces freezing behavior at low current levels and flight at higher intensities, accompanied by characteristic autonomic responses including increased heart rate, blood pressure, and startle reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p115]
- Humans stimulated in FEAR circuit sites typically report subjective feelings of fear described in metaphoric terms such as 'being chased' or 'entering a dark tunnel.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p115]
- Benzodiazepines reduce fear by facilitating GABA-mediated inhibition within the FEAR circuit, with BZ receptors concentrated along the circuit's trajectory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p117]
- Glutamate is a key transmitter that generates unconditioned fear responses and mediates fear conditioning in the amygdala via NMDA receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p117]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that decorticate animals can still exhibit fear behaviors when the FEAR system is stimulated suggests affective experience is an intrinsic subcortical function.
- Inference: Because electrical stimulation of classic spinothalamic pain systems does not produce fear, pain and fear systems are anatomically distinct despite interactions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the subjective experience of fear is mediated directly by this circuit or in conjunction with other brain areas such as the cortex.
