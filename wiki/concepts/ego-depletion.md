# Ego depletion

## Summary

Ego depletion is the temporary reduction in the self's capacity for volitional action (self-control, decision-making, initiating action) caused by prior exercise of willpower. Research cited in the book shows that willpower is a limited resource comparable to a muscle: it depletes quickly and requires time to recover. A broad assortment of self-regulatory acts deplete the same resource, including even being a victim of prejudice. The implication for knowledge work is that decisions about organization should be made once and standardized, reducing the daily drain on willpower so that mental resources can be directed toward actual thinking.

## Key facts

- Baumeister et al. (1998) defined ego depletion as 'a temporary reduction in the self's capacity or willingness to engage in volitional action.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Willpower depletes across many unrelated domains because all self-control acts draw on the same limited resource. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Even being a victim of prejudices can significantly deplete willpower through the effort of controlling stereotype influence. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- A standardized working environment reduces the number of decisions required daily, conserving willpower for content-related decisions. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Barack Obama and Bill Gates limit their wardrobe to reduce daily decisions, illustrating the principle at a personal level. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Research on ego depletion is noted to be under intense scrutiny, with some findings requiring 'a grain of salt.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Baumeister's 1996 study found that people who ate radishes instead of chocolate cookies spent less than half the time attempting a puzzle compared to chocolate eaters, demonstrating ego depletion. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p48]
- A 2010 meta-analysis of 83 studies on ego depletion identified five biggest factors causing depletion: effort, perceived difficulty, negative affect, subjective fatigue, and blood glucose levels. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p49]
- The meta-analysis found that motivational incentives, training on self-control tasks, and glucose supplementation promoted better self-control in ego-depleted people. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p50]
- A study found that people who made a difficult decision earlier in the day were more likely to cave in to temptation later, showing decisions also drain willpower. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p49]
- Rewards appear to restore willpower; a study showed that swishing sugar solution (but not artificial sweetener) restored willpower without restoring glucose levels. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p101]
- The term 'ego depletion' was coined by Baumeister as homage to Freud's energy model of the self. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p33]
- Ego depletion affects the anterior cingulate cortex, slowing its activity and reducing error-detection ability. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p35]
- Depleted persons react more strongly to all kinds of stimuli—sad movies make them extra sad, joyous pictures make them happier. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p35]
- People can unconsciously conserve willpower when they know additional demands are coming. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p39]
- A single finite amount of willpower is used for all manner of tasks including controlling thoughts, emotions, impulses, and performance. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p40]

## Inferences

- Inference: The slip-box method is designed to minimize ego depletion by standardizing note formats, storage locations, and workflows, so that each work session requires no organizational decisions, only content thinking.
- Inference: Mini habits minimize all five causes of ego depletion by being small (low effort), easy (low perceived difficulty), positive (minimal negative affect), energizing (low subjective fatigue), and efficient (preserving glucose).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The current state of replication debates around ego depletion research (post-2014) is not addressed in this book.
