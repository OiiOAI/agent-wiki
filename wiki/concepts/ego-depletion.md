# Ego depletion

## Summary

Ego depletion is the temporary reduction in the self's capacity for volitional action (self-control, decision-making, initiating action) caused by prior exercise of willpower. Research cited in the book shows that willpower is a limited resource comparable to a muscle: it depletes quickly and requires time to recover. A broad assortment of self-regulatory acts deplete the same resource, including even being a victim of prejudice. The implication for knowledge work is that decisions about organization should be made once and standardized, reducing the daily drain on willpower so that mental resources can be directed toward actual thinking.

## Key facts

- Baumeister et al. (1998) defined ego depletion as 'a temporary reduction in the self's capacity or willingness to engage in volitional action.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Willpower depletes across many unrelated domains because all self-control acts draw on the same limited resource. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Even being a victim of prejudices can significantly deplete willpower through the effort of controlling stereotype influence. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- A standardized working environment reduces the number of decisions required daily, conserving willpower for content-related decisions. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Barack Obama and Bill Gates limit their wardrobe to reduce daily decisions, illustrating the principle at a personal level. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
- Research on ego depletion is noted to be under intense scrutiny, with some findings requiring 'a grain of salt.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p70]
