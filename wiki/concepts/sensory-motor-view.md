# Sensory-Motor View

## Summary

The sensory-motor view holds that the original and fundamental function of nervous systems is to link perception with action—brains are for the guidance of action, and the only way to guide action usefully is to link what is done to what is seen, touched, and tasted. The senses track what's happening in the environment and nervous systems use this information to work out what to do.

## Key facts

- According to the sensory-motor view, the original function of nervous systems is to link perception with action, guiding behavior based on sensory input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p23]
- The sensory-motor view was simply assumed through much of the twentieth century, and it took time for an alternative action-shaping view to become clear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p24]

## Inferences

- Inference: The sensory-motor view treats organisms as having discrete 'actions' that need to be coordinated with senses, ignoring the prior problem of how organisms generate coherent actions in the first place.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The historical significance of the sensory-motor versus action-shaping distinction and how it maps onto evolutionary time remains speculative.
