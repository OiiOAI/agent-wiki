# Three Types of Leverage

## Summary

Leverage in negotiation is the ability to inflict loss and withhold gain, but Voss emphasizes that perceived leverage matters more than actual leverage. The three types are: Positive leverage (ability to provide or withhold what counterpart wants), Negative leverage (ability to make counterpart suffer through threats), and Normative leverage (using counterpart's own norms and standards to advance position). Leverage is fluid and shifts between parties depending on perceived fear of loss.

## Key facts

- Positive leverage is the ability to provide or withhold things your counterpart wants; whenever the other side says 'I want,' you have positive leverage. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p194]
- Negative leverage is a negotiator's ability to make the counterpart suffer, based on threats, and exploits loss aversion—the psychological finding that potential losses loom larger than similar gains. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p195]
- Normative leverage uses the other party's own norms and standards to advance your position; showing inconsistencies between their beliefs and actions creates normative leverage because no one likes to look like a hypocrite. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p196]
- Black Swans are leverage multipliers that give you the upper hand; effective negotiators look for pieces of information that reveal what is important to their counterpart. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p193]
