# Saccades

## Summary

Saccades are rapid eye movements that direct the fovea to different regions of the visual field, lasting less than 40 ms and occurring several times per second. They create challenges for visual processing because each saccade produces a discrete image centered at the new fixation point. The brain uses corollary discharge and visual masking to maintain a stable percept despite continuous retinal image shifts.

## Key facts

- Saccades usually last less than 40 ms and redirect the center of sight in the visual field, occurring several times per second with intervening fixation periods of several hundred milliseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p690]
- The pattern of saccades made while viewing a picture reflects the cognitive purpose of vision, with areas of apparent interest fixated most frequently. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p691]
- Change-blindness experiments demonstrate that we do not have a complete internal representation of the visual field between saccades. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p691]
- During saccades, stimuli presented just before the saccade appear closer to the pre-saccade fixation point, while stimuli presented after appear closer to the saccade target, demonstrating spatial compression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p693]
- Neurons in the parietal cortex shift their receptive fields before saccades, beginning to respond to stimuli that will be within the receptive field after the saccade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p693]
- Vision lapses during saccades due to saccadic suppression, which reduces sensitivity to low-frequency gratings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p694]

## Inferences

- Inference: The shift in receptive fields before saccades must be computed using an efferent copy of the saccade motor command (corollary discharge) rather than proprioceptive feedback, because the shift begins before the eye actually moves.
