# The Id (Das Es)

## Summary

The id (das Es) is one of the three structural components of the mind introduced in The Ego and the Id. It is the oldest and largest portion of the psychic apparatus, containing all the instinctual drives and the primary processes. The term was adopted by Freud from Georg Groddeck, following Nietzsche's usage, to designate the part of the psyche that is impersonal, subject to natural law, and unknown and uncontrollable.

## Key facts

- The id is described as a 'great reservoir of libido' from which object cathexes are sent out and into which they are withdrawn. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5926]
- At the very beginning, all the libido is accumulated in the id while the ego is still in process of formation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5931]
- The id is totally non-moral and contains the primal drives of Eros and Thanatos. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5938]
- The ego is developed from the id's nucleus, the Pcpt. system, and represents what may be called reason and common sense in contrast to the passions in the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5911]
- The id cannot say what it wants and has achieved no unified will; Eros and the death drive struggle within it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5943]
- Groddeck's term 'das Es' derives from his teacher Ernst Schweninger, though the usage goes back to Nietzsche. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5897]
