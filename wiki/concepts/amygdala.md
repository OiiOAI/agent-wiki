# Amygdala

## Summary

The amygdala is traditionally described as the seat of fear in the brain and a key structure in the brain's 'fear circuit.' However, LeDoux argues in Anxious that this view is incorrect—the amygdala is involved in threat detection and defensive responses but not in the conscious feeling of fear, which arises from different brain circuits.

## Key facts

- The amygdala is generally described as the seat of fear in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p11]
- The amygdala is crucially involved in the brain circuits that control freezing and other defensive responses to threat. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p11]
- These amygdala circuits are conserved throughout mammals, including humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p11]
- LeDoux argues that the amygdala is NOT the seat of fear, contradicting the conventional view. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p11]
- The amygdala contains the lateral (LA), central (CeA), and basal (BA) nuclei, each with distinct roles in threat processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p140]
- The lateral amygdala is the key site of plasticity where associative memories about threats are formed during Pavlovian conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p144]
- The central amygdala controls freezing behavior via connections to the periaqueductal gray area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p144]
- Damage to the human amygdala prevents Pavlovian threat conditioning from occurring. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p148]
- The amygdala can be thought of as the 'accelerator' of defensive reactions, while the ventromedial prefrontal cortex acts as the 'brake'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p147]
- Hebbian learning mechanisms underlie threat conditioning when weak and strong stimuli converge onto the same LA neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p143]
- The amygdala is central to both the unconscious and conscious components of emotion, coordinating the conscious experience of feeling and the bodily expression of emotion, particularly fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p351]
- Damage to the amygdala disrupts the ability of an emotionally charged stimulus to elicit an emotional response; damage to the hippocampus interferes with the ability to remember the context in which the stimulus occurred. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p351]
- The lateral nucleus of the amygdala mobilizes adaptive responses through connections with the hypothalamus (for fight-or-flight response) and the cingulate cortex (for conscious fear evaluation). [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p354]
- The neural systems that store unconscious emotionally charged memories are different from those that generate conscious feeling states. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p351]
- Single-unit recordings in both rodents and non-human primates have implicated neurons in the amygdala in encoding stimulus-reward associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p378]
- Lesions of the amygdala and orbitofrontal cortex or crossed unilateral lesions of both structures result in impairments in modulation of conditioned Pavlovian responses following outcome devaluation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p378]
- Amygdala receives dopaminergic input and is one of the major targets of midbrain dopamine neurons, alongside the ventral striatum and orbitofrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p379]
- The amygdala activates during aggression in humans (shown via neuroimaging) and stimulation produces rage. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p38]
- Humans with amygdala damage (from Urbach-Wiethe disease or surgery) are impaired at detecting angry facial expressions. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p38]
- Charles Whitman, the 1966 Texas Tower sniper, had a glioblastoma tumor pressing on his amygdala. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p40]
- The amygdala is most involved in feeling afraid and anxious, not just aggression—fear and aggression are not inevitably intertwined. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p40]
- In PTSD, the amygdala is overreactive to mildly fearful stimuli and slow to calm down after activation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p41]
- The central amygdala plays a key role in innate fears while the basolateral amygdala (BLA) learns fear associations. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p42]
- The amygdala injects implicit distrust and vigilance into social decision making—damage produces 'pathological altruism.'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p44]
- The amygdala is about uncertain, unsettled yearning for potential pleasure, anxiety about whether reward will materialize—not just pleasure itself. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p45]
- Violent psychopaths have smaller, less responsive amygdalae and show emotionless, instrumental violence rather than reactive aggression. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p49]
- The amygdala is a bilateral structure, meaning there are two of them, one in each hemisphere, mirroring each other. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p874]
- To be confident that the amygdala is selectively about aggression, one must show it activates more than other brain regions and does not activate during unrelated behaviors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p875]
- A case report describes a woman with epileptic seizures initiating in the amygdala who experienced delusions of being male before seizure onset. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p886]
- The amygdala deactivates in both men and women during orgasm. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p887]
- Stress increases overall excitability in the amygdala by inhibiting particular inhibitory GABA interneurons, causing excitatory glutamate-releasing neurons to increase activity. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p959]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because the amygdala is involved in nonconscious threat detection and responses, it can be activated by threat-related stimuli that a person is not consciously aware of.
- Inference: LeDoux proposes that the amygdala generates nonconscious motivational states that contribute to, but are not identical with, conscious feelings of fear or anxiety.
- Inference: The correspondence of findings in rats and humans regarding amygdala function suggests rodent studies can illuminate mechanisms relevant to human anxiety disorders.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific brain circuits responsible for conscious feelings of fear are not detailed in this chunk.
- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms by which LA plasticity stores long-term threat memories remains an active research area.
- Uncertain: Whether the amygdala's role in threat processing is conserved across all vertebrate species requires further comparative study.
