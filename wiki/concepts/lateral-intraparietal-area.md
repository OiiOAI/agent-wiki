# Lateral Intraparietal Area

## Summary

The Lateral Intraparietal Area (LIP) is a brain region important for decision-making located in the posterior parietal cortex. It lies at the nexus between sensory and motor processing and encodes the subjective desirability of potential choice targets during decision tasks.

## Key facts

- Area LIP encodes the desirability of choice stimuli rather than the probability of movement; firing rates varied along with changing desirability under forced-choice conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p69]
- Trial-by-trial fluctuations in LIP activity co-varied with a behavioral estimate of subjective desirability derived from reinforcement learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p69]
- Activity in LIP resembles the accumulation of sensory signals predicted by random-walk models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p415]
- LIP neurons encode a form of the accumulator variable used in making perceptual choices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p415]
- LIP neurons encode both fluid value and visual outcome value independently, suggesting they signal the abstract value of orienting to a visual stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p451]

## Inferences

- Inference: LIP activity represents value signals that guide attention and saccadic eye movement selection based on expected reward.
