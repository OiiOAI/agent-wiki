# Multilevel selection

## Summary

Multilevel selection (MLS) theory posits that natural selection operates simultaneously at multiple levels: individual, kin group, and group. Haidt distinguishes MLS-1 (selection among shifting groups) from MLS-2 (selection among stable groups) to explain how group-level adaptations could evolve despite individual-level selection favoring self-interest.

## Key facts

- MLS-1 (selection among shifting groups) requires only that social environments contain multiple kinds of competing groups; individuals with group-related adaptations succeed by finding themselves on winning teams more often. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p394]
- MLS-2 (selection among stable groups) focuses on groups as entities competing with each other over many generations, requiring strong group boundaries and high genetic relatedness inside groups. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p394]
- Okasha argues that MLS-1 leads to adaptations increasing group cohesiveness and suppressing free-riding, which then pave the way for MLS-2 in later stages of major transitions. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p394]
- Group selection or colony-level selection as Haidt describes it is compatible with Hamilton's inclusive fitness theory and Dawkins's 'selfish gene' perspective. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p392]

## Inferences

- Inference: Humans may be 'stalled' midway through a major transition, which Haidt describes as being '90 percent chimp and 10 percent bee.'
