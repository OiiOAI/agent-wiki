# Compulsion and Doubt

## Summary

Freud traces the origins of compulsion and doubt in obsessional neurosis to the ambivalent conflict between love and hatred. Doubt corresponds to the patient's internal perception of their indecision, which takes possession of them in the face of every intended action. Doubt is fundamentally a doubt of their own love—which ought to be most certain—diffusing over everything else and especially displacing onto what is most insignificant. Compulsion represents an attempt at compensation for doubt and correction of the intolerable inhibition. When the patient succeeds in bringing an inhibited intention to a decision through displacement, the substitutive act must be carried out with compulsive force. Commands and prohibitions alternate according to whether affectionate or hostile impulse controls the pathway to discharge.
