# Principle of Perfection

## Summary

The principle of perfection directs society to arrange institutions to maximize the achievement of human excellence in art, science, and culture. Rawls argues this principle would not be adopted in the original position because parties cannot risk their freedom by authorizing a standard of value to define what is to be maximized. While perfectionism is rejected as a political principle, excellence can be pursued within the limits of free association.

## Key facts

- The principle of perfection directs society to maximize the achievement of human excellence in art, science, and culture. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p296]
- The principle of perfection would not be adopted in the original position because parties cannot risk their freedom by authorizing a standard of value. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p298]
- Perfectionism is denied as a political principle; the human perfections are to be pursued within the limits of the principle of free association. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p299]
- The equal liberty of citizens does not presuppose that the ends of different persons have the same intrinsic value. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p299]
- The principle of perfection provides an insecure foundation for the equal liberties. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p300]
