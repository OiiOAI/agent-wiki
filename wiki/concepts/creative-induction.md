# Creative Induction

## Summary

Creative induction (also called constructive induction) is the mental operation of forming a new domain or concept by finding common qualities, attributes, or operations among unstructured particulars that were previously shattered by destructive deduction. It is related to the specific-to-general chain of induction, synthesis, and integration.

## Key facts

- A new domain or concept can be formed by finding common qualities, attributes, or operations among particulars swimming in a sea of anarchy. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- Through such connecting threads that produce meaning, one synthesizes constituents from across the shattered domains. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- Creativity is related to induction, synthesis, and integration since one proceeds from unstructured bits and pieces to a new general pattern or concept. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- Creative induction must avoid using only bits and pieces in the same arrangement associated with previously purged domains—otherwise nothing new is generated. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- Creative induction is related to the specific-to-general idea chain, induction, synthesis, and integration (as exemplified by integral calculus). [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p445]
- The integral calculus proceeds from a derivative to a general function, illustrating induction and synthesis. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p445]
