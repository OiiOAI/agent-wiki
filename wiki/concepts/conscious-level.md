# Conscious Level

## Summary

Conscious level concerns 'how conscious' an organism is, on a scale from complete absence (coma, brain death) to vivid waking awareness. Seth distinguishes conscious level from wakefulness (physiological arousal)—they often correlate but can dissociate, as in dreaming (asleep but conscious) or vegetative states (awake but apparently unconscious). Consciousness level depends on interactions within the thalamocortical system rather than overall neuronal activity or sheer number of neurons.

## Key facts

- Conscious level concerns how conscious someone is, on a scale from complete absence to vivid waking awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p36]
- The cerebellum has about four times as many neurons as the rest of the brain but seems barely involved in consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p44]
- Consciousness depends on how different parts of the brain speak to each other, particularly within the thalamocortical system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p45]
- Conscious level is not the same as wakefulness, as shown by dreaming (conscious but asleep) and vegetative state (awake but unconscious). [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p44]
- Cerebellar agenesis allows people to lead largely normal lives despite lacking a normal cerebellum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p44]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between conscious level and wakefulness suggests they depend on different underlying biological mechanisms.
