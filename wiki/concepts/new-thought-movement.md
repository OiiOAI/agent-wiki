# New Thought Movement

## Summary

The New Thought movement originated in 19th-century New England as a mutation of Protestant Christian individualism, holding that thinking about success leads to success. Elements of New Thought have seeped into strategic thinking through literature on leadership and vision, creating a belief that all you need to succeed is a positive mental attitude—which Rumelt identifies as a pathway to bad strategy.

## Key facts

- The New Thought movement combined religious sentiment with recommendations for worldly success, with the theory that thinking about success leads to success. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p35]
- Wallace Wattles' The Science of Getting Rich (1910) stripped out religious references and created quasi-religious incantations about man having godlike powers. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p35]
- Rhonda Byrne's The Secret (2007) is essentially identical to Mulford's original principle that you get what you think about, presented as if it were new. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p36]
- New Thought ideas entered strategy through Peter Senge's concept of shared vision in The Fifth Discipline (1990). [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p36]

## Inferences

- Inference: The doctrine that one can impose visions and desires on the world by the force of thought alone displaces critical thinking and good strategy.
