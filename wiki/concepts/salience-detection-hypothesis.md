# Salience Detection Hypothesis

## Summary

The salience detection hypothesis proposes that aesthetic chills occur when the brain identifies something as critical new information that resolves important uncertainties. This makes the chill a physiological echo of the aha moment when patterns are first spotted, enabling new and powerful predictions. Music is one of the most reliable inducers of aesthetic chills because it generates expectancies at multiple levels that are then resolved at key musical moments.

## Key facts

- Aesthetic chills occur when we encounter something our brain identifies as critical new information that resolves important uncertainties. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p110]
- The process of salient error reduction can occur even when we already know exactly how it is all going to end. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p110]
- Great works of music and literature are probability designs—artifacts engineered to interact reliably with our predictive brains by reliably building and then resolving expectations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: Aesthetic chills are a physiological marker of sudden increases in estimated importance or precision, reflecting how predictive processing turns resolution of uncertainty into felt experience [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p111]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Individual differences in susceptibility to aesthetic chills and their relationship to personality traits require further investigation
