# The naive

## Summary

A form of the comic characterized by remarks or actions that disregard an inhibition because the person does not possess it. The naive is distinguished from jokes by the complete absence of the psychical process in the first person—the producer has no arrière pensée and derives no yield of pleasure from producing it. Freud analyzes examples including a child's 'Bubizin' remark and children's theatrical productions, arguing that the naive represents a marginal case where the censorship is reduced to zero.

## Key facts

- The naive occurs if someone completely disregards an inhibition because it is not present in him—he appears to overcome it without any effort. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2813]
- The effect of the naive is irresistible because an inhibitory expenditure suddenly becomes unutilizable and is discharged by laughter without the need for distracting attention. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2813]
- The naive (in speech) agrees with jokes as regards wording and content but the psychical process in the first person who produces it is completely absent—he has no arrière pensée. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2815]
- The naive is most common in children and uneducated adults whose intellectual development is still childish, producing remarks describable as 'naive jokes.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2813]
- The naive presents itself as a species of the comic insofar as its pleasure springs from the difference in expenditure which arises in trying to understand someone else. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2818]

## Inferences

- Inference: The naive occupies a position between jokes and the comic—using joke techniques (condensation, indirect representation) but without the unconscious psychical process that characterizes jokes.
- Inference: The condition for recognizing something as naive (knowing the producer lacks the inhibition) involves 'empathy'—putting oneself in the producer's psychical state.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud notes that identifying the naive with 'naive-comic' is 'certainly not in every case admissible,' though he focuses on 'naive jokes' and 'naive smut' as sufficient for analysis.
