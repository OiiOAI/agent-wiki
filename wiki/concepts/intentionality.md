# Intentionality

## Summary

Intentionality is the phenomenological doctrine that consciousness is directed toward or 'intends' something beyond itself. Phenomenologists distinguish between object-directed intentionality (consciousness of something specific) and broader openness to the world or 'other.' Object-directed experiences have a clear subject-object structure, while other experiences (bodily feelings, moods, absorbed skillful activity) are intentional in the broader sense of being open to what is other without clear subject-object structure. Intentional experiences are conceived as mental acts animated by the lived body, with a correlational structure of noema (object as intended) and noesis (mental act).

## Key facts

- Consciousness is intentional in the sense that it 'aims toward' or 'intends' something beyond itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p33]
- Phenomenologists distinguish object-directed intentionality from broader openness to the world or 'other' (alterity). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p33]
- Object-directed experiences are those in which we are conscious of something in a more-or-less determinate sense. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p33]
- Bodily feelings, moods, and absorbed skillful activities are intentional in the broader sense of being open to the world without clear subject-object structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p33]
- Intentional experiences have a correlational structure of noema (the object as intended) and noesis (the mental act that intends it). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p35]
- Phenomenologists draw a distinction between presentational acts (perception, where object is given as present) and representational acts (memory, imagination, where object is given as absent).
