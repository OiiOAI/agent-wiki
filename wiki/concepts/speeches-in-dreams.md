# Speeches in Dreams

## Summary

Speeches in dreams refer to verbal expressions, dialogue, and language that appear within dream content. Freud argues that the dream-work cannot actually create speeches; rather, all speeches in dreams derive from fragments of real speeches made or heard by the dreamer. These fragments are extracted from context, cut into pieces, with some portions incorporated and others rejected, and often assembled in new orders that give them fresh meaning.

## Key facts

- The dream-work cannot actually create speeches; all speeches in dreams derive from fragments of speeches which have really been made or heard. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p374]
- The dream-work deals with speech fragments in the most arbitrary fashion, dragging them out of context and cutting them in pieces. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p374]
- Speeches in dreams often have a structure similar to breccia, where largish blocks of various kinds of stone are cemented together by a binding medium. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p375]
- Only speeches in dreams possessing sensory quality and described by the dreamer as speeches can be traced to real speeches; other verbal thoughts may be unmodified waking thoughts carried over into dreams.
