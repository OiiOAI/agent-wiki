# Individualism vs. Autonomy

## Summary

Individualism and autonomy are often confused but are profoundly different concepts. Individualism refers to being free to pursue one's own ends, emphasizing self-interest and self-assertion. Autonomy, in contrast, is about acting volitionally with a sense of choice, flexibility, and personal freedom while remaining open to relatedness and responsibility. A person can be individualistic without being autonomous if their pursuits are pressured or coerced. True autonomy includes self-knowledge and personality integration, which distinguishes it from mere individualism.

## Key facts

- Individualism refers to being free to pursue your own ends; it encompasses self-interest and emotional independence, standing in contrast to acting for the common good. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p144]
- Autonomy is about acting volitionally with a sense of choice, flexibility, and personal freedom, and feeling a true willingness to behave responsibly in accord with one's interests and values. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p145]
- The converse of being autonomous is being controlled, whether by others or by one's own introjects, and pressuring oneself undermines autonomy. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p145]
- Autonomy requires self-knowledge, which implies personality integration, and distinguishes autonomy from individualism. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p146]
- Carol Gilligan and others have confused individualism and autonomy, critiquing autonomy when what they were actually criticizing was masculine independence and Western individualism.
