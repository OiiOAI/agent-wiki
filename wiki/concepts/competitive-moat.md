# Competitive Moat

## Summary

A competitive moat is a durable competitive advantage that protects a business from competitors, analogous to the defensive trench around a castle. Charlie Munger discusses how moats are formed through scale advantages, brand identity, network effects, and other factors that make businesses difficult to displace.

## Key facts

- Munger states: 'Old moats are getting filled in and new moats are harder to predict, so it's getting harder.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p104]
- Scale advantages include cost reductions along the experience curve—more volume enables more efficient processing. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p165]
- Scale advantages come from geometry (tank construction), TV advertising reach, and informational advantages like brand recognition. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p166]
- Social proof from psychology gives huge advantage of scale for wide distribution, like Coca-Cola's global availability. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p167]
- Newspapers demonstrate winner-take-all scale dynamics: most US cities beyond a few large ones have only one daily newspaper. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p168]
- A great lesson in microeconomics is to discriminate between when technology will help you versus when it will kill you. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p178]
