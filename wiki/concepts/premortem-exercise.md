# PreMortem Exercise

## Summary

The PreMortem is a structured method for helping decision makers anticipate problems before they occur. Unlike a postmortem (which examines why something failed), the PreMortem imagines that a project has already failed and works backward to identify potential causes. The exercise involves six steps: preparation, imagining a fiasco, generating reasons for failure, consolidating lists, revisiting the plan, and periodically reviewing the list. Gary Klein's company uses the PreMortem as a crucial part of every project kickoff meeting, believing it helps build intuitions and sensitivities to future problems.

## Key facts

- The PreMortem begins with the assumption that the plan has failed, replacing complacency with active search for potential problems. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p129]
- The exercise takes 40 minutes to an hour and is typically conducted with project teams of 8-12 participants. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p127]
- The six steps are: (1) Preparation, (2) Imagine a fiasco, (3) Generate reasons for failure, (4) Consolidate the lists, (5) Revisit the plan, and (6) Periodically review the list. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p128]
- The PreMortem produces a higher level of candor than passive attempts at self-critiquing. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p130]
- As a by-product, team members become better at mentally simulating how a plan or project is likely to play out. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p127]

## Inferences

- Inference: The PreMortem differs from traditional risk analysis because it assumes failure rather than trying to quantify the likelihood of each risk
