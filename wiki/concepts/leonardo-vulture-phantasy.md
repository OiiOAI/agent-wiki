# Leonardo's Vulture Phantasy

## Summary

Leonardo's vulture phantasy is a childhood memory (or phantasy) recorded in his Codex Atlanticus notebook, in which Leonardo recalled being visited in his cradle by a vulture that 'opened my mouth with its tail, and struck me many times with its tail against my lips.' Freud interprets this phantasy as concealing an erotic memory of being suckled by his mother, transformed into a passive homosexual scenario through the mechanism of infantile sexual theory.

## Key facts

- Leonardo recorded in his Codex Atlanticus: 'It seems that I was always destined to be so deeply concerned with vultures; for I recall as one of my very earliest memories that while I was in my cradle a vulture came down to me, and opened my mouth with its tail, and struck me many times with its tail against my lips.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3520]
- Freud argues this is not a literal memory but a phantasy formed at a later date and transposed to childhood, based on the principle that childhood memories are 'elicited only at a later age when childhood is already past; in the process they are altered and falsified, and are put into the service of later trends.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3521]
- In the phantasy, the vulture's 'coda' (tail) is interpreted by Freud as a symbol for the male genital, making the phantasy's content an erotic fellatio scenario. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3523]
- Freud connects the vulture symbol to Egyptian hieroglyphics where the mother is represented by a picture of a vulture, specifically the goddess Mut. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3525]
- The ancient Egyptians believed vultures were all female and reproduced by being impregnated by the wind, a fable used by Church Fathers to support the virgin birth. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3526]
- Freud translates the phantasy as meaning: 'That was a time when my fond curiosity was directed to my mother, and when I still believed she had a genital organ like my own.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3533]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud infers that the phantasy indicates Leonardo spent the first years of his life not by his father's side but with his mother, 'so that he had time to feel the absence of his father.'
- Inference: Freud infers that the vulture phantasy confirms 'that Leonardo spent the first years of his life alone with his mother' which 'will have been of decisive influence in the formation of his inner life.'
- Inference: The phantasy 'was transformed into a passive homosexual phantasy,' indicating a connection between Leonardo's relation with his mother in childhood and his later manifest homosexuality.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges the philological issue that 'nibio' (the Italian word Leonardo used) means 'kite,' not 'vulture,' and admits he relied on German translations that used 'Geier' (vulture). The editors note this was pointed out by Irma Richter.
- Uncertain: Whether Leonardo actually knew the Egyptian connection or the Church Fathers' fable about vultures is uncertain, though Freud argues it is 'quite possible' given his extensive reading.
