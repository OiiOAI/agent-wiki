# Brain Exercises

## Summary

Brain exercises are targeted mental activities designed to strengthen weak brain functions based on neuroplasticity principles. Barbara Arrowsmith Young developed specific exercises for various cognitive deficits including symbol relationship problems, spatial reasoning, kinesthetic perception, visual span, auditory memory, and frontal lobe functions.

## Key facts

- Barbara designed brain exercises that exercised her most weakened function — relating a number of symbols to each other — instead of practicing compensation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- One exercise involved reading hundreds of cards picturing clock faces, attempting to tell the time, checking the answer, then moving to the next card as fast as possible. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- When she couldn't get the time right, she spent hours with a real clock, turning the hands slowly, trying to understand the relationships. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- She developed exercises for spatial difficulties, trouble with knowing where limbs were, and visual disabilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- Brain exercises work best and most quickly for people with only a few areas of difficulty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p53]
- The Arrowsmith School uses complex multi-handed clocks for symbol relationship exercises. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- Visual memory is strengthened by studying unfamiliar letter shapes like Urdu and Persian. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- Tracing intricate lines, squiggles, and Chinese letters while wearing an eye patch strengthens motor coordination areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- Auditory memory is improved through listening to CDs and memorizing poems. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p48]
- Brain exercises can help people with frontal lobe deficits involving impulse control, planning, strategy, relevance sorting, goal formation, and learning from mistakes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p49]
- Expending extra mental effort to support a weak area may divert resources from strong areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p50]
- Brain exercises have helped talented artists with weak shape recognition and lawyers with Broca's area pronunciation deficits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p49-p50]
