# Normalization of Deviance

## Summary

Normalization of deviance is a concept described by sociologist Diane Vaughan to describe the process where a clearly unsafe practice comes to be considered normal if it does not immediately cause a catastrophe. It represents a 'long incubation period before a final disaster with early warning signs that were either misinterpreted, ignored or missed completely.'

## Key facts

- Diane Vaughan coined the term 'normalization of deviance' to describe when clearly unsafe practices come to be considered normal. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p434]
- Vaughan's original example cited was the events leading to the Space Shuttle Challenger disaster. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p435]
- Normalization of deviance represents a 'long incubation period before a final disaster with early warning signs that were either misinterpreted, ignored or missed completely'. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p434]
- Todd Conklin stated that 'Workers don't cause failures. They trigger latent conditions that lie dormant in organizations waiting for a specific moment in time'. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p435]
