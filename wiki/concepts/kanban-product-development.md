# Kanban (for Product Development)

## Summary

In the Lean Startup context, kanban is a capacity constraint system where user stories progress through four states: backlog, actively being built, done (feature complete), and being validated. Only a limited number of stories can be in each state at once. Stories cannot be removed from the board until they have been validated, defined as knowing whether the story was a good idea to have been done in the first place.

## Key facts

- Under the kanban system, user stories are not considered complete until they lead to validated learning. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p130]
- Stories are categorized into four states: in the product backlog, actively being built, done, or in the process of being validated. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p130]
- Only so many stories can be in each of the four states at once; once a bucket becomes full, it cannot accept more stories until validation is complete. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p130]
- Only when a story has been validated can it be removed from the kanban board; if validation fails, the relevant feature is removed from the product. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p130]
- Grockit implemented this system and teams began measuring productivity according to validated learning, not the production of new features. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p131]
