# Pavlovian Values

## Summary

Pavlovian values are expectations about reward associated with stimuli or states that predict outcomes independent of any specific action. These values are encoded by a Critic module in reinforcement learning and can influence instrumental choice through Pavlovian-instrumental transfer. Neural evidence implicates the amygdala, orbitofrontal cortex, and ventral striatum in encoding Pavlovian values.

## Key facts

- Pavlovian values are learned associations between conditioned stimuli and outcomes that can influence instrumental choice even when the instrumental contingency is intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p369]
- In Pavlovian-instrumental transfer, presentation of stimuli trained as predictors of specific outcomes selectively biases choice toward actions associated with those same outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p371]
- The effect of Pavlovian cues on instrumental choices depends on the cue's predictive validity, suggesting animals interpret cues as providing information about the availability of specific goals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p371]

## Inferences

- Inference: Delamater (1995) found that degrading a stimulus's contingent predictive relationship eliminated Pavlovian-instrumental transfer, confirming that the effect depends on the cue's status as a net predictor of reward
