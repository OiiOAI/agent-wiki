# Reality Monitoring

## Summary

Reality monitoring is the process by which we distinguish between internally generated information (thoughts, feelings, imagination) and information from the outside world. Marcia Johnson pioneered this concept. Two factors help reality monitoring: (1) imagined events tend to be less detailed and vivid than real events, with fewer sensory details; and (2) people can consciously evaluate the quality of their memories by asking whether details come from actual experience or could have been constructed. Reality monitoring depends on the prefrontal cortex—damage here causes confabulation. Factors impairing reality monitoring include fatigue, stress, multitasking, aging, and vivid imagination.

## Key facts

- Marcia Johnson coined the term reality monitoring for the process of distinguishing internally generated information from outside world information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p83]
- Imagined events are less detailed and vivid than real events on average, making them easier to identify as imagined. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p83]
- Confabulation occurs when people with extensive prefrontal damage confidently recall things that never happened. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p85]
- Prefrontal damage impairs reality monitoring because this brain area helps evaluate the sources and accuracy of memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p84]
- People with more gray matter in the prefrontal cortex are better at reality monitoring. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p85]
