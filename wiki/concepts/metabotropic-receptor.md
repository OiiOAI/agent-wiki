# Metabotropic Receptor

## Summary

Metabotropic receptors are membrane proteins distinct from the ion channels they regulate, acting through G proteins and second-messenger cascades to produce slower, modulatory synaptic actions.

## Key facts

- Metabotropic receptors typically have seven membrane-spanning α-helices and activate G proteins that stimulate intracellular second-messenger production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Metabotropic receptor activation often produces slower synaptic actions lasting hundreds of milliseconds to minutes, modulating neuronal excitability and synaptic strength. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Second messengers activated by metabotropic receptors include cAMP and diacylglycerol, which activate protein kinases that phosphorylate various target proteins including ion channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Metabotropic receptors are composed of one or two subunits distinct from the ion channels they regulate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p237]
- Metabotropic receptors commonly have seven membrane-spanning α-helices and act through G proteins to alter intracellular metabolic reactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p237]
- Activation of metabotropic receptors often stimulates production of second messengers such as cAMP or diacylglycerol. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p237]
- Metabotropic receptors produce slower synaptic actions lasting seconds to minutes, modulating behavior rather than directly triggering rapid responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p238]
- Examples include GABAB receptors (which activate K+ channels) and metabotropic glutamate receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p237]

## Inferences

- Inference: Metabotropic receptors enable neuromodulation and plasticity, allowing synapses to adjust their strength over longer timescales.
- Inference: The slower signaling through metabotropic receptors is essential for behavioral state modulation and learning.
