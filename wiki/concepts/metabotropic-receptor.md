# Metabotropic Receptor

## Summary

Metabotropic receptors are membrane proteins distinct from the ion channels they regulate, acting through G proteins and second-messenger cascades to produce slower, modulatory synaptic actions.

## Key facts

- Metabotropic receptors typically have seven membrane-spanning α-helices and activate G proteins that stimulate intracellular second-messenger production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Metabotropic receptor activation often produces slower synaptic actions lasting hundreds of milliseconds to minutes, modulating neuronal excitability and synaptic strength. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Second messengers activated by metabotropic receptors include cAMP and diacylglycerol, which activate protein kinases that phosphorylate various target proteins including ion channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
