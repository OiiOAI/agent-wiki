# Short-Term Memory

## Summary

Short-term memory is a form of memory lasting minutes that involves functional changes in synaptic strength without requiring new protein synthesis. In Aplysia, short-term memory is mediated by modifications of preexisting proteins at synapses, such as enhanced neurotransmitter release through the cyclic AMP-protein kinase A pathway.

## Key facts

- Short-term memory results from a functional change in synapses, strengthening or weakening preexisting connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p226]
- Short-term memory does not require the synthesis of new protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p237]
- The duration of short-term memory storage depends on the length of time a synapse is weakened or strengthened. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p216]
- In short-term habituation, the sensory neuron releases less neurotransmitter; in short-term sensitization, it releases more. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p234]
- The increase in cyclic AMP and enhancement of transmitter release produced by a single learning trial lasts only minutes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p248]
