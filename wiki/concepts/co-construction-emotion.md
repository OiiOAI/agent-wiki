# Co-Construction of Emotion

## Summary

Emotional communication happens when two people predict and categorize in synchrony. Your emotions are guided by your predictions, and as observers perceive you, the emotions they perceive are guided by their predictions. We synchronize our non-verbal behaviors, biological states (like heart rates), and concepts for emotion. When communicating emotion, words and actions launch predictions in the listener's brain, helping to coordinate a shared concept. People communicate by synchronizing breathing, non-verbal behaviors, and even heart rates (in securely bonded pairs). Parents co-construct emotion with children through their choice of words, influencing the child's predictions and categorizations.

## Key facts

- Emotional communication happens when you and I predict and categorize in synchrony. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p216]
- We synchronize our non-verbal behaviors, biological states, and concepts for emotion when communicating. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p216]
- Parents who ask a child 'Are you upset?' instead of 'How are you feeling?' are co-constructing emotion and honing the child's concepts toward being upset. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p218]

- Inference: the classical view of emotion, responsibility for communication is all on the perceiver's end because emotions are supposedly displayed universally. In a construction mindset, you also bear responsibility to be a good sender.
