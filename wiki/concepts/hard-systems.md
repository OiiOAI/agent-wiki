# Hard Systems

## Summary

Hard Systems are defined as inanimate, non-living things in a business. Examples include computers, office equipment, color schemes, and physical infrastructure. Gerber emphasizes that Hard Systems can produce human and integrated results when properly designed, citing the Prevent-a-Smudge System as an example of using physical solutions to solve people-intensive problems.

## Key facts

- Hard Systems are inanimate, non-living things such as computers, equipment, or the colors in a reception area. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p241]
- The E-Myth Worldwide Prevent-a-Smudge System used a four-inch Lucite collar around white boards to prevent blue ink from smudging white walls. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p244]
- Gerber presents Hard Systems as solutions to typically people-intensive problems, stating that 'A Hard System for producing a human and totally integrated result' can work 'Without anyone having to pay attention to it.'. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p244]
