# Perception as Inference

## Summary

Perception as inference is the Helmholtzian (and Kantian) view that perception is not a passive bottom-up transduction of sensory signals into internal representations, but rather a constructive inferential process that combines top-down prior beliefs with bottom-up sensory evidence following Bayes' rule. Under this view, the brain is a predictive machine that constantly generates predictions (priors) about upcoming sensations and uses incoming sensory data to confirm or disconfirm these predictions. The prior encodes knowledge about the most likely hidden causes of sensations prior to observation; the likelihood maps hidden states to predicted observations. Bayes' rule updates the prior into a posterior probability of hidden states after receiving observations. This forms the foundation of the Bayesian brain hypothesis and the starting point of the 'low road' to Active Inference.

## Key facts

- Perception as unconscious inference dates to Helmholtz (1866) and reformulates the brain as a predictive machine that combines top-down priors with bottom-up sensory evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p23]
- Bayes' rule updates a prior probability P(x) into a posterior probability of hidden states P(x|y) after receiving observations, combining prior knowledge with sensory evidence through the likelihood P(y|x). [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p24]
- Perception is a constructive inside-out process in which sensations are used to confirm or disconfirm hypotheses about how they were generated, not a passive outside-in process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p24]
- Surprise (negative log evidence) measures how poorly a model fits the data it tries to explain; it is used in Bayesian model comparison to score models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p19]
- Bayesian surprise (KL-divergence between prior and posterior) measures how much beliefs must be updated following an observation, distinct from surprise which measures how unlikely the observation was. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p20]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perception as inference implies that perception is inherently probabilistic and uncertainty-aware, contrasting with maximum likelihood estimation which ignores prior plausibility and estimation uncertainty.
