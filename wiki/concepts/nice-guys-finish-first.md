# Nice Guys Finish First

## Summary

The phrase 'nice guys finish last' originated in baseball but was used by Garrett Hardin to summarize sociobiology. However, Axelrod's computer tournaments showed that 'nice' strategies—those that never defect first—actually finish first in the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma. This optimistic conclusion challenged the view that selfishness always wins.

## Key facts

- In Axelrod's first tournament, the 8 top-scoring strategies were all 'nice' strategies; the 7 'nasty' strategies trailed well behind. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p251]
- In the evolutionary simulation (Round 3), after about 1,000 generations, only nice but retaliatory strategies like Tit for Tat survived. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p254]
- Tit for Tat achieved 84% of the benchmark score while the most successful nasty strategy achieved only 66.8%. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p251]
- The only nasty strategy to survive beyond generation 200 was called Harrington, which temporarily prospered by exploiting 'softies' but went extinct when they were eliminated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p254]

## Inferences

- Inference: The success of nice strategies suggests that cooperation can evolve even in a fundamentally selfish world without requiring group selection
- Inference: Tit for Tat has 'higher-order stability' because it can benefit from local clustering due to genetic relatedness, allowing it to cross the critical frequency knife-edge
