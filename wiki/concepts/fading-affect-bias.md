# Fading Affect Bias

## Summary

The fading affect bias (FAB) is a form of transience whereby negative emotions associated with autobiographical memories fade faster over time than positive emotions. Research links FAB to psychological well-being, with weaker FAB associated with dysphoria, anxiety, eating disorders, and reduced grit.

## Key facts

- Diary studies show that negative emotions fade faster over time than positive emotions, even though the events themselves are still remembered. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p271]
- People with dysphoria, high anxiety, alexithymia, and eating disorders exhibit a much weaker FAB than psychologically healthier individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p272]
- Individuals high in Grit—perseverance toward long-term goals despite adversity—show a more robust FAB, suggesting forgetting negative experiences adaptively may support persistence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p272]
