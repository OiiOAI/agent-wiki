# Lead Poisoning and Attention

## Summary

Lead poisoning is a documented cause of attention problems and ADHD. Historical research by Bruce Lanphear and others demonstrated that widespread lead exposure from leaded petrol and lead paint was causing cognitive damage to millions of children. The resolution required citizens banding together to demand regulatory action rather than individual behavioral changes.

## Key facts

- Children exposed to lead were two and a half times more likely to meet criteria for ADHD. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p223]
- If a mother was exposed to lead during pregnancy and also smoked cigarettes, her child was eight times more likely to be diagnosed with ADHD. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p223]
- In 1975, the average American had a blood lead level of 15 micrograms per deciliter. Today it is 0.85 micrograms per deciliter. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p225]
- The IQ of the average American preschooler is estimated to have risen by five points as a result of the lead ban. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: The lead poisoning case demonstrates that environmental toxins can cause widespread attention problems, and that collective action through regulation is more effective than individual behavioral changes in addressing such public health crises.
