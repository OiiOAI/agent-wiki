# Goal-directed Choice

## Summary

Goal-directed choice is a decision-making process that involves computing action-outcome contingencies and evaluating the rewards associated with different outcomes. Unlike habitual systems, goal-directed systems can compute values in novel situations and adapt to rapidly changing environments by assigning values to actions that are commensurate with the expected reward they generate.

## Key facts

- Goal-directed systems assign values to actions by computing action-outcome contingencies and then evaluating the rewards associated with the different outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p427]
- Under ideal conditions, the value assigned to an action equals the average expected reward obtained from that action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p427]
- A key disadvantage of goal-directed systems is that they are computationally intensive and require a large amount of accurate information to forecast values correctly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p427]
- Goal-directed systems are capable of computing values in novel situations and in environments with rapidly changing action-outcome contingencies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p427]
- In goal-directed choice, the value of an action equals the reward obtained by eating the food to which it leads. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p416]
- In goal-directed choice, values do not depend on experimenter-controlled states that need to be estimated using sensory information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p416]
- The brain needs to identify the outcome associated with each action and estimate its reward value in goal-directed choice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p416]

## Inferences

- Inference: The computational intensity of goal-directed systems explains why organisms often rely on faster but less flexible habitual systems in stable environments.
- Inference: The vulnerability of goal-directed systems to uncertainty about action-outcome contingencies may explain why organisms sometimes make suboptimal choices.
