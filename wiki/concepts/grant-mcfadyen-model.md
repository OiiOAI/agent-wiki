# Grant McFadyen Model

## Summary

Munger's father's lesson about two clients: the wonderful Mr. McFadyen who treated everyone right had simple problems requiring little legal work, while the difficult Mr. X created constant legal minefields. The lesson: run your own life like McFadyen but serve difficult clients when necessary.

## Key facts

- Grant McFadyen was a self-made Irishman who ran away from an abusive farm, became Pioneer Ford dealer in Omaha, and was a brilliant man of enormous charm and integrity. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p261]
- Mr. X was a blowhard, overreaching, unfair, pompous, difficult man who created a 'walking minefield of wonderful legal business.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p261]
- Munger's father's lesson: 'Sell your services once in a while to an unreasonable blowhard if that's what you must do to feed your family. But run your own life like Grant McFayden.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p262]
- The teaching method used indirect storytelling requiring a 'slight mental reach' so Munger had to reach for the insight himself, making the lesson stick for decades. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p262]
