# Children's Understanding of Dreams

## Summary

Children gradually develop an adult understanding of what dreams are through a series of cognitive milestones. Research by Jean Piaget and others showed that preschoolers initially believe dreams are real and observable by others. Full understanding of dreams as internal, private, imaginary mental experiences typically develops around ages 8-11.

## Key facts

- Jean Piaget found that most preschoolers believed dreams were real, originated from outside the dreamer, and could be seen by others. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p21]
- Laurendeau and Pinard found that between ages 6-8, most children grasped that dreams were imaginary but not observable by others. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p21]
- By around age 11, children came to fully understand the nonphysical, private, and internal nature of dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p21]
- More recent work suggests some children understand dreams are not part of the external world as early as ages 3-5. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p22]
