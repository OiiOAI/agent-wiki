# Sarcomere

## Summary

The sarcomere is the basic organizational unit of skeletal muscle contractile proteins, bounded by Z disks. It consists of thick filaments (myosin) in the center and thin filaments (actin, tropomyosin, troponin) anchored at Z disks. Force generation occurs through the cross-bridge cycle, where myosin heads bind actin and undergo a power stroke. Sarcomere length determines the overlap between thick and thin filaments, influencing contractile force.

## Key facts

- The sarcomere is the repeating structural unit of muscle, bounded by Z disks, containing thick and thin filaments arranged in register. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p746]
- Thick filaments consist of many myosin molecules arranged with heads protruding around the filament, while thin filaments contain two helical strands of F-actin plus tropomyosin and troponin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p746]
- The sliding filament hypothesis proposes that thick and thin filaments slide past each other during contraction without the filaments themselves shortening. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p747]
- Each cross-bridge cycle involves ATP binding, detachment, cleavage, weak actin binding, power stroke, and force generation, with each stroke producing approximately 2 pN of force. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p750]
- At optimal sarcomere length (Lo), thick and thin filament overlap is maximal and relative force is greatest; force decreases at both longer and shorter lengths. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p751]
- Titin maintains the position of thick filaments in the sarcomere center and contributes significantly to force during lengthening contractions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p747]
