# Match Problems

## Summary

Match Problems are a classic creativity task involving an arrangement of matches that must be reorganized to make other patterns by removing matches. The task requires lateral transformations or set shifts to overcome perceptual or conceptual fixation. These problems have been used in neuroimaging and neuropsychological studies to identify brain regions involved in hypothesis generation, set shifting, and creative problem solving, particularly implicating the right ventral lateral prefrontal cortex.

## Key facts

- Match Problems involve an arrangement of matches that must be reorganized to make other patterns by removing matches, with the requirement that the final pattern contains no incomplete squares. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p417]
- Successful solutions to Match Problems require 'set shifts' or lateral transformations to overcome perceptual or conceptual fixation (Guilford, 1967). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p418]
- Mental representations that are imprecise, ambiguous, fluid, indeterminate, or vague facilitate lateral transformations, while precise and concrete representations can hinder them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p418]
- Goel and Vartanian (2005) found that contrasting set-shift trials with baseline trials revealed activation in left DLPFC and right ventral lateral PFC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p418]
- When Goel and Vartanian contrasted successfully solved set-shift trials with unsolved ones, there was exclusive activation in the right ventral lateral PFC, suggesting this region mediates set shifting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p419]
- Miller and Tippett (1996) found that patients with focal right frontal lobe lesions, especially right ventral PFC, were selectively impaired on problems requiring set shifts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p419]
- Miller and Tippett concluded that 'the right frontal region was found to play a predominant role in permitting a subject to shift approach, with the orbital and ventro-lateral region particularly necessary for permitting major strategy changes'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p419]

## Inferences

- Inference: The convergence of neuropsychological and neuroimaging evidence strongly supports the conclusion that the right ventral PFC is necessary and sufficient for set shifting in Match Problems.
