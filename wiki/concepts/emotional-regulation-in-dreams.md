# Emotional Regulation in Dreams

## Summary

Emotional regulation in dreams refers to the proposed function of dreams in processing and modulating emotions during sleep. Multiple models suggest dreaming plays roles in containing emotional surges, uncoupling emotions from physiology, reducing fear-based memories, and weaving emotional concerns into memory systems.

## Key facts

- One model views dreaming as a thermostat tasked with containing emotional surges and regulating the dreamer's mood across successive periods of REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Models propose that by combining negatively toned dream imagery with muscle paralysis, REM sleep dreaming carries out an adaptive desomatization function uncoupling emotions from their underlying physiology. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Neurocognitive models propose that one function of dreaming is to reduce or extinguish fear-based memories by allowing fear stimuli to be experienced in novel and emotionally varied circumstances. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Rosalind Cartwright offered similar ideas on the emotion-regulating function of dreaming in her studies of recently divorced men and women. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p115]
