# Christmas Truce of 1914

## Summary

The spontaneous cessation of hostilities along the Western Front during World War I, beginning around Christmas 1914. Officers on both sides agreed to halt fighting for retrieval of the dead, which expanded into joint burial services, exchanges of food and gifts, caroling, and even soccer matches in no-man's-land.

## Key facts

- The truce began as an agreement to halt hostilities during Christmas dinner and for retrieval of the dead, then spread to include joint burial services, exchanges of food, drink, and tobacco, with soldiers praying and caroling together and exchanging gifts. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p679]
- The truce worked partly because of the unique static nature of trench warfare (soldiers faced each other day after day), shared Judeo-Christian tradition and Western European culture, and top-down approval from officers and the Pope. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p679]
- It took officers' threats of court-martial to get everyone back to fighting, and with only sporadic exceptions, the Christmas Truce never happened again. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p679]
