# Groupthink

## Summary

Groupthink occurs when a group of smart people makes reckless decisions because everyone aligns their opinions with the supposed consensus. Individual members suppress dissenting views to maintain group harmony, leading to flawed decisions that each person would have rejected individually.

## Key facts

- Groupthink is where a group of smart people makes reckless decisions because everyone aligns their opinions with the supposed consensus, passing motions each individual would have rejected without peer pressure. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p65]
- The Bay of Pigs invasion was a classic groupthink failure where Kennedy's team completely underestimated Cuba's air force and expected a refuge 100 miles away despite an insurmountable swamp. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p65]
- To counter groupthink, one should appoint someone as devil's advocate who will question tacit assumptions even if risking expulsion from the group. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p66]
