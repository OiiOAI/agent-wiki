# Mystic Writing-Pad

## Summary

Freud's 1925 conceptual model of the psychic apparatus inspired by a commercial device. The model demonstrates how perception can be unlimited in receptive capacity while still preserving permanent memory traces, through the division of functions between a perceptual surface and underlying storage.

## Key facts

- The perceptual apparatus consists of two systems: Pcpt.-Cs., which receives perceptions but retains no permanent trace, and mnemic systems behind it which preserve permanent traces. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6120]
- The celluloid layer of the Mystic Pad corresponds to the protective shield against stimuli, while the waxed paper corresponds to the Pcpt.-Cs. system. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6121]
- The wax slab preserves permanent traces of all writings, analogous to the unconscious mnemic systems. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6122]
- Cathectic innervations are sent out and withdrawn in rapid periodic impulses from within into the Pcpt.-Cs. system. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6123]
- The discontinuity in the functioning of the perceptual system lies at the bottom of the origin of the concept of time. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6123]
