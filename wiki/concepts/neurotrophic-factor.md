# Neurotrophic Factor

## Summary

Neurotrophic factors are secreted proteins that promote neuronal survival by suppressing a latent apoptotic program. The neurotrophin family includes NGF, BDNF, and NT-3. They signal through Trk tyrosine kinase receptors (TrkA binds NGF, TrkB binds BDNF, TrkC binds NT-3) and p75 receptor. Target cells secrete limited amounts of neurotrophic factors; neurons that successfully innervate targets obtain sufficient factor while others undergo programmed cell death. Neurotrophins activate PI3K-Akt signaling which inhibits pro-apoptotic Bcl-2 family proteins.

## Key facts

- NGF was the first neurotrophic factor identified by Levi-Montalcini and Cohen, purified from snake venom and mouse salivary glands. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1253]
- Different neuronal populations depend on different neurotrophic factors: sympathetic neurons need NGF, proprioceptive sensory neurons need NT-3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1254]
- Neurotrophins bind Trk receptors (tyrosine kinases) for survival signaling and p75 for cell death; balance between these determines outcome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1254]
- Neurotrophin signaling is retrogradely transported from axon terminals to cell bodies in signaling endosomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1255]
