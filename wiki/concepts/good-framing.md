# Good Framing

## Summary

Some frames are better than others for reaching rational decisions. A good frame is wider and more inclusive, incorporating all relevant information and treating sunk costs as irrelevant. The 'lost ticket' problem shows that framing the loss as a reduction in overall wealth leads to the rational decision, while framing it as part of the entertainment account leads to inconsistent behavior.

## Key facts

- A good frame includes all outcomes that are relevant to the decision: in the theater ticket problem, framing the lost ticket as a reduction in overall wealth leads to the rational answer ('Would you buy the tickets if you lost the money instead?'). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p479]
- Sunk costs should be ignored in rational decision-making because they are already incurred and cannot be recovered; a good frame treats history as irrelevant to current choice. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p479]
- The MPG frame is a bad frame because it inverts the proper metric: gallons-per-mile (or liters per 100 km) gives accurate guidance about which vehicle saves more fuel, while MPG misleads by making proportional improvements look equivalent when they are not. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p480]
- Kahneman states: 'Frames should not be viewed as interventions that veil or distort preferences.' At least for the Asian disease problem and the lung cancer treatment framing, there is no underlying preference being veiled—there are only preferences among framed problems, and moral intuitions are attached to descriptions rather than reality. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p478]

## Inferences

- Inference: there are no 'true' underlying preferences independent of framing, then the choice of frame is itself a moral and policy decision, not a neutral presentation of pre-existing preferences.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all cases of framing produce arbitrary preferences or whether some domains have genuine preference-independent normative standards that a 'good' frame reveals.
