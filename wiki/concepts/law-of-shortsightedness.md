# Law of Shortsightedness

## Summary

The Law of Shortsightedness describes the human tendency to be most impressed by immediate, dramatic events while ignoring larger trends and long-term consequences. This tendency leads to poor decision-making, overreaction, and susceptibility to schemes promising quick gains.

## Key facts

- The human brain evolved to home in on the most dramatic features of the environment rather than examining the full context of events. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p202]
- We give more attention to immediate threats like terrorism than to abstract future dangers like global warming, despite the latter being greater. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p202]
- Short-term thinking is hardwired—we seek instant gratification and respond to whatever is most stimulating in the moment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p201]
- The antidote is developing a 'farsighted perspective' by detaching from immediate emotions, widening context, considering consequences, and maintaining focus on long-term goals. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p200]
