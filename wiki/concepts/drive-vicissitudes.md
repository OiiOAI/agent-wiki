# Drive Vicissitudes

## Summary

The term 'vicissitudes' (Schicksale) refers to the transformations that drive impulses (Triebregungen) can undergo in the mind. In 'Drives and Their Vicissitudes' (1915), Freud systematizes these possible fates: reversal into the opposite, turning upon the subject, repression, and sublimation. A drive at its simplest consists of a pressure (Drang), a goal (Ziel), an object (Objekt), and a source (Quelle). The concept is foundational to psychoanalytic drive theory and metapsychology.

## Key facts

- A drive is conceptualized as a measure of the demand made upon the mind for work in connection with the body. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4493]
- The concept of a drive lies on the frontier between the mental and the somatic; it is the psychical representative of the stimuli originating from within the organism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4492]
- Freud distinguishes a drive from a stimulus: a stimulus operates as a single impact, while a drive operates as a constant force. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4493]
- A primitive organism cannot take evasive action against drive needs as it can against external stimuli; drives are among the factors that compel the development of the mental apparatus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4493]
- The concept of a drive includes the concepts of source, aim, object, and stimulus (pressure). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4493]
- The four vicissitudes that a drive impulse may undergo are: reversal into its opposite, turning round upon the subject's own self, repression, and sublimation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4505]
- Reversal into the opposite resolves into two different processes: change from activity to passivity (sadism-masochism, scopophilia-exhibitionism), and reversal of content (transformation of love into hate). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4505-4506]
- The turning round upon the subject's own self involves a change of object while the aim remains unchanged, exemplified by masochism as sadism turned upon the ego and exhibitionism as looking at one's own body. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4506]
- The essential feature in the vicissitudes of drives lies in their subjection to the influences of the three polarities: activity-passivity (biological), ego-external world (real), and pleasure-unpleasure (economic). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4517]
