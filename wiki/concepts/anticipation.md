# Anticipation in Genetic Disorders

## Summary

Anticipation is a genetic phenomenon in which successive generations develop disease at earlier ages and with increasing severity. In trinucleotide repeat disorders such as Huntington disease and the spinocerebellar ataxias, the CAG repeat tract can expand during DNA replication, particularly during paternal transmission. Longer CAG tracts are more likely to further expand, explaining the striking pattern of anticipation in these families.

## Key facts

- Anticipation refers to the phenomenon where younger generations within a family have longer repeats and develop more severe symptoms at an earlier age than their parents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- The longer the CAG tract, the more likely it is to further expand during DNA replication, accounting for anticipation in trinucleotide repeat diseases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- CAG repeats are particularly unstable when transmitted through paternal germ cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- Disease severity, age of onset, and speed of progression correlate with repeat length in Huntington disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
