# Reappraisal

## Summary

The reflective cognitive process of reconsidering and changing the meaning given to emotion-eliciting stimuli, which gives people power over their emotions and enables better emotional regulation.

## Key facts

- The process of giving meaning to emotion-eliciting stimuli has two components: an immediate intuitive responding and a more reflective process that appraises what is happening. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188-189]
- The ability to think deliberately can adjust the immediate interpretation given to a stimulus, and the emotion experienced will change as the meaning changes. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- Fear and anger dissipate as one reappraises what actually happened. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- It is the reappraisal process that gives people power over their emotions. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p189]
- By learning to interpret stimuli differently, people can become more effective in managing their own emotions. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: The reappraisal process is a learnable skill that can be developed through practice, allowing people to reinterpret potentially threatening stimuli in less threatening ways.
