# Cynefin Framework

## Summary

The Cynefin Framework is a sense-making and decision-support tool developed by Dave Snowden. It categorifies contexts into five domains (Clear, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic, and Confused) and guides appropriate responses based on the nature of the domain. The framework distinguishes between ordered systems (where cause-effect relationships are knowable) and unordered systems (where they are not).

## Key facts

- The Cynefin Framework has five domains: Clear, Complicated, Complex, Chaotic, and Confused. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p270]
- In the Clear domain, best practice applies; in the Complicated domain, good practice applies; in the Complex domain, novel practice applies; and in the Chaotic domain, novel practice applies. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p271]
- The name 'Cynefin' comes from Welsh, meaning 'the place of your birth and upbringing, the environment in which you live and to which you are naturally acclimatised,' as defined by Nicolas Sinclair in his book on Kyffin Williams. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p250]
- The Cynefin Framework enables people with differing backgrounds and perspectives to come together, discuss difficult subjects, and agree on a path forward. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p251]
- The framework was developed by Dave Snowden at IBM around 2002 and later formalized through Cognitive Edge. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p255]
- The Cynefin Framework is described as a 'navigation tool' for working with uncertainty, change, and surprise. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p293]
