# Expansive Attitude

## Summary

An expansive (positive) attitude opens the lens through which we see the world, allowing for greater creativity, resilience, and social connection. Rather than constricting experience to manage fear, the expansive attitude embraces uncertainty, views adversity as learning, and maintains self-belief without false humility. This attitude creates positive self-fulfilling dynamics.

## Key facts

- The expansive attitude is defined by openness to new experiences, ideas, and emotions—the opposite of the constricted attitude's fear-based narrowing. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p268]
- To develop an expansive attitude, view yourself as an explorer: leave certainty behind, be curious about new ideas, and see no limits to where your mind can roam. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p279]
- Embrace obstacles as learning experiences and means to get stronger, rather than trying to repress or avoid negative experiences. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p280]
- Feeling destined for something great gives resilience when opposed, prevents internalizing doubts, and creates an enterprising spirit willing to take risks. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p282]
- Abraham Maslow's 'peak experiences'—moments of being lifted from the daily grind—can alter attitude permanently by expanding perceived possibilities. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p282]
- The expansive person accepts people as 'facts of nature'—diverse like flowers and rocks—reducing the need to take offense or feel challenged by differences. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p283]

## Inferences

- Inference: The expansive attitude can be cultivated through deliberate practice, much like physical conditioning, rather than being purely innate.
