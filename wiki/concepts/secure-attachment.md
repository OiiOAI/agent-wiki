# Secure Attachment

## Summary

Secure attachment is a psychological concept describing a child's fundamental need to trust caregivers and feel seen, accepted, and responded to. It is described as the stabilizer of a child's emotional life and the foundation for their ability to explore the world bravely. When children feel fully seen and acknowledged, they develop the security needed to take risks with both their actions and their emotions, knowing they will not be invalidated, rejected, punished, or shamed for their feelings.

## Key facts

- Secure attachment is at the heart of any child's ability to go forth bravely into the great wide world. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p227]
- Secure attachment is the stabilizer of a child's emotional life right on into middle school and adolescence, and on to the formation of adult relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p227]
- When a child feels fully seen and acknowledged by those around him, it's hard for him not to feel loved and secure. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p226]
- A child's sense of secure attachment allows him not only to take risks in the world but also to take risks with his own emotions. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p227]

## Inferences

- Inference: Secure attachment, as described in this source, forms the foundation upon which emotional agility can be built in children, as it enables them to experience and process emotions without fear of rejection.
