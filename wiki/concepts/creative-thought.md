# Creative Thought

## Summary

A cognitive process that produces solutions considered both novel and useful within a given context. Creative thinking involves a generation-and-verification cycle, where ideas are generated and then evaluated for viability. It involves contributions from cognitive control (lateral PFC), defocused attention (default network), and memory (temporal lobes).

## Key facts

- A solution is creative if it is considered both novel and useful within a given context. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p411]
- Creative thought involves a generation-and-verification cycle, where hypothetical plans or ideas are generated and then subjected to a selection process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p412]
- Divergent thinking tasks produce decreased beta range synchrony and increased alpha range synchrony over the frontal cortex, indicating loosened cognitive control during creative thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p357]
- REM sleep is marked by decreased noradrenaline and corresponding decrease in cognitive control, and subjects awoken from REM sleep show improved access to distant semantic relations and better anagram solving. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p357]
- Insight during remote associate problems is linked to activation in temporal lobe regions and default network regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p358]

## Inferences

- Inference: Lower cognitive control and a wider, defocused attentional state facilitate creative thinking by allowing access to remote semantic associations.
- Inference: Creative thought involves both generative stages (linked to default network and memory) and evaluative aspects (linked to lateral PFC).
