# Mindfulness of Current Thoughts

## Summary

A DBT distress tolerance practice for observing, describing, and letting go of thoughts without judgment. The technique involves saying 'The thought [describe thought] went through my mind' and then applying one or more strategies to observe and release thoughts.

## Key facts

- Mindfulness of current thoughts involves two main steps: first, saying 'The thought [describe thought] went through my mind' and second, applying strategies to observe and let go of thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p424]
- Thirteen distinct strategies are provided for practicing mindfulness of thoughts, including repeating thoughts aloud in different voices, relaxing the face and body, imagining not believing thoughts, refocusing on physical sensations, observing breath, labeling thoughts as thoughts, stepping back mentally, and using imagery. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p424]
- Imagery strategies for observing thoughts include imagining thoughts on a conveyor belt, boats on a river, train cars on tracks, leaves flowing on a river, clouds in the sky, or thoughts with wings flying away. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p424]

## Inferences

- Inference: The practice is designed to create distance between the individual and their automatic thoughts, reducing reactivity to distressing, anxiety-provoking, or anger-filled thoughts.
