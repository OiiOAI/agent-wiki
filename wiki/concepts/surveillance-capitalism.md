# Surveillance Capitalism

## Summary

A term coined by Shoshana Zuboff to describe an economic system where technology companies track, scan, and store users' online activities to build detailed psychological profiles (or 'voodoo dolls') that are sold to advertisers. These profiles allow unprecedented prediction and manipulation of user behavior. The model creates incentives for tech companies to maximize screen time regardless of user wellbeing.

## Key facts

- In surveillance capitalism, every message, status update, and search query is scanned and stored to generate advertising profiles for each user. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p136]
- Aza Raskin compared this to having 'a voodoo doll' inside Facebook's servers — a model of each user assembled from their metadata that becomes increasingly accurate over time. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p137]
- The business model requires maximizing screen time because that maximizes advertising exposure and data collection, creating incentives directly opposed to user wellbeing. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p138]

## Inferences

- Inference: Surveillance capitalism represents a 'quantum leap forward' beyond traditional advertising because it enables personalized manipulation at scale based on individual psychological profiles.
