# Reinforcement Learning

## Summary

Reinforcement learning (RL) encompasses computational frameworks and neural mechanisms by which organisms learn to select actions based on reward and punishment feedback, implemented through prediction error signals primarily mediated by dopaminergic systems.

## Key facts

- Phasic dopamine signals encode reward prediction errors, with increases for unexpected rewards and decreases (pauses) for unexpected punishments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p293]
- Reinforcement learning models typically decompose behavior into parameters including learning rate, outcome magnitude, and choice temperature (exploration/exploitation tradeoff). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p288]
- PD patients with ICDs rely more heavily on a ventral striatal critic model of stimulus value with impaired learning from negative prediction errors, while PD controls rely on a dorsal striatal actor model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p296]
- D1 and D2 receptors in the dorsal striatum are segregated into direct and indirect pathways facilitating and inhibiting action selection, respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p293]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between ventral striatal (critic) and dorsal striatal (actor) learning mechanisms suggests parallel reinforcement systems with different computational roles.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the distinction between model-free and model-based learning systems better explains the neural basis of reward versus punishment learning remains debated.
