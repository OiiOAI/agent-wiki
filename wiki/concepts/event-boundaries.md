# Event Boundaries

## Summary

Event boundaries are points where our sense of context dramatically changes, such as when we step through a doorway or experience a shift in topic. The hippocampus waits to store a memory until after an event boundary, allowing it to encode the memory with full context. However, context changes at boundaries also make it harder to recall things from moments earlier. This explains the common experience of walking into a room and forgetting why you went there. The hippocampus activates strongly at event boundaries to store memories. During the COVID-19 pandemic, lack of event boundaries (staying in the same room, doing similar activities) caused people to feel unanchored in time.

## Key facts

- Event boundaries occur when our sense of context dramatically changes, such as when stepping through a doorway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p50]
- The hippocampus waits to store a memory until right after an event boundary to encode the full event with context. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p51]
- Walking into a room and forgetting why you went there is a normal consequence of event boundaries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p51]
- During COVID-19 lockdowns, 95% of surveyed students felt days were going by slowly while 80% felt weeks were passing too quickly due to lack of event boundaries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p52]
