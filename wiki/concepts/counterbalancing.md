# Counterbalancing

## Summary

Counterbalancing replaces the myth of 'balanced life' with the practical approach of managing two distinct buckets: personal life and professional life. In personal life, one should go 'short'—never staying out of balance too long. In professional life, one should go 'long'—accepting extended periods of focus on the ONE Thing while compensating briefly when needed.

## Key facts

- A balanced life is described as a myth—extraordinary results require focused attention and time, making perfect balance impossible. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p58]
- An 11-year study of 7,100 British civil servants found those working more than 11 hours daily were 67% more likely to suffer heart disease. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p65]
- The book uses James Patterson's metaphor of life as juggling five balls: work (rubber), family/health/friends/integrity (glass). [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p68]
- In personal life, go short—avoid long periods out of balance. In professional life, go long—make peace with extended focus on ONE Thing. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p67]

## Inferences

- Inference: The two-bucket approach suggests that work and personal life have fundamentally different requirements for balance, requiring different strategies.
