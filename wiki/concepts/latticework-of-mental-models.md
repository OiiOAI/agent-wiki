# Latticework of Mental Models

## Summary

The latticework of mental models is a concept popularized by Charlie Munger representing an interconnected network of theoretical frameworks, concepts, and principles from various disciplines. Building such a latticework is superior to memorizing isolated facts because it enables better understanding, prediction, and application of knowledge across different situations.

## Key facts

- The more we know, the more information hooks we have to connect new information to, making it easier to form long-term memories. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p111]
- Building a latticework of theories and mental models to which information can stick creates a virtuous circle where learning facilitates learning. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p112]
- The slip-box builds the same connections in our heads while developing them in the slip-box, making facts easier to remember because they have a latticework to attach to. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p111]
- You can't really know anything if you just remember isolated facts and try to bang them back. If the facts don't hang together on a latticework of theory, you don't have them in a usable form. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p163]
- You have to have multiple models because if you have just one or two, the nature of human psychology is such that you'll torture reality so that it fits your model. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p164]
- The models must come from multiple disciplines because all the wisdom of the world is not to be found in one little academic department. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p164]
- Munger estimates that 80 to 90 important models carry about 90% of the freight in making someone worldly wise, and only a handful carry very heavy freight. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p164]
- The most reliable models come from hard science and engineering, followed by biology/physiology, then psychology. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p169]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger's latticework concept directly addresses why specialized experts often fail in complex real-world decisions—they lack the interdisciplinary framework needed to see problems from multiple angles.
- Inference: The concept implies that education departments create dysfunction by isolating disciplines, preventing students from developing the cross-disciplinary thinking Munger considers essential.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Munger lists approximately 100 important models but does not enumerate them all in this chunk; the specific count and identities of the handful carrying heavy freight are not fully specified.
