# Global Workspace Theory

## Summary

Global workspace theory, proposed by Bernard Baars and championed by Stan Dehaene, Lionel Naccache, and Jean-Pierre Changeux, holds that consciousness arises from the broadcasting of information from a cognitive workspace (essentially working memory) and the return of that broadcast information to the workspace, with repeated rebroadcasting. Various systems compete for access to the workspace, and attention selects what reaches it. Information in the workspace can be used for thinking, planning, and behavioral control, but conscious experience specifically requires widespread broadcasting and rebroadcasting that amplifies processing.

## Key facts

- Global workspace theory holds that consciousness arises from the broadcasting of information from the workspace and its return with repeated rebroadcasting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p233]
- Various brain systems compete for access to a cognitive workspace, and attention selects what reaches it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p233]
- Unlike higher-order theory, global workspace theory does not explicitly require that experience be the subject of a thought to be consciously experienced. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p234]
- GWT proposes that mental content becomes conscious when it gains access to a 'workspace' distributed across frontal and parietal cortical regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p22]
- Global workspace theory is a theory of 'access consciousness' rather than 'phenomenal consciousness'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p38]
- Conscious content broadcast through the global workspace can guide behaviour more flexibly than unconscious perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p22]

## Inferences

- Inference: Global workspace theory shares with higher-order theory the requirement that single-level processing is insufficient for conscious experience.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether broadcasting and rebroadcasting are necessary and sufficient for phenomenal experience is debated.
