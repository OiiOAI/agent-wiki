# Apollo and Dionysus

## Summary

McGilchrist employs Nietzsche's concepts of Apollo and Dionysus as metaphors for hemisphere functions. Apollo represents the left hemisphere's drive toward clarity, form, individuation, and rational order. Dionysus represents the right hemisphere's embrace of flux, unity, dissolution of boundaries, and connection to the primal. The highest achievements of Greek culture—particularly tragedy—occurred when Apollo and Dionysus were reconciled, neither dominating the other. McGilchrist traces a historical arc where this balance was first achieved in sixth-century Athens, then progressively surrendered to Apollonian dominance.

## Key facts

- Apollo represents the left hemisphere's drive toward clarity, form, and rational order; Dionysus represents the right hemisphere's embrace of flux, unity, and primal connection. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p379]
- Tragedy was born when Apollo and Dionysus were reconciled; Nietzsche saw the highpoint as the age of Aeschylus. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p393]
- The influence of Apollo is ambiguous: Apollo is 'the luminous one,' associated not only with rationality but with 'fantasy, of that which only seems to be the case.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p396]
- Eventually 'the ambiguous god of wine and death yielded the stage to Apollo and the triumph of rationality,' symptoms of the ageing of Greek civilization. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p393]
