# Brain Rhythms as Framework for Neural Syntax

## Summary

Brain rhythms form a hierarchical system that provides syntactical structure for spike traffic within and across neuronal circuits at multiple time scales. The hierarchy is characterized by discrete frequency bands spanning more than four orders of temporal magnitude (0.02 to 600 Hz), with neighboring bands having approximately a constant ratio of e (2.718). Cross-frequency phase coupling allows slower rhythms to modulate faster ones, creating nested structures analogous to language syntax where letters (neurons) combine into words (cell assemblies) and words into sentences (network patterns).

## Key facts

- Brain rhythms span approximately 0.02 to 600 Hz, covering more than four orders of temporal magnitude, with ten or more distinct frequency bands. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p145]
- Neighboring brain rhythm bands have approximately a constant ratio of e = 2.718, preventing perfect entrainment and creating perpetual metastability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p145]
- Every known pattern of local field potentials and oscillatory activity found in one mammalian species is also found in virtually all other mammals investigated to date. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p153]
- Inhibition is the foundation of brain rhythms, and every known neuronal oscillator has an inhibitory component. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p150]
- Inhibition of excitatory neurons can be conceived as the punctuation marks of a neural syntax that can parse and segregate neuronal messages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p150]
- Cross-frequency phase coupling allows slow oscillations to coordinate faster ones, with the fastest oscillations phase-locked to spikes of local neurons and to all slower rhythms of the hierarchy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p148]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hierarchical nature of cross-frequency-coupled rhythms may serve as a scaffold for combining neuronal letters into words and words into sentences.
- Inference: Human speech and musical syntax may have evolved using preexisting neural syntax supported by brain rhythms.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: It remains to be demonstrated whether and how brain networks actively use packaging mechanisms for their own communication purposes.
