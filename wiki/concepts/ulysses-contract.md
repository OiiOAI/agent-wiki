# Ulysses Contract

## Summary

A Ulysses contract is a precommitment strategy in which a past or present version of oneself prevents a future version from making an irrational decision. Named after Homer's Odysseus, who had his crew tie him to the mast and fill their ears with wax to resist the Sirens' song, these contracts create decision-interrupts that trigger deliberative thought before acting on impulses.

## Key facts

- Ulysses contracts involve past-us preventing present-us or future-us from acting irrationally, exemplified by Odysseus ordering his crew to bind him to the mast before encountering the Sirens. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1690-L1694]
- Ulysses contracts can be barrier-inducing (raising obstacles against bad decisions) or barrier-reducing (removing obstacles to good decisions). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1706-L1707]
- These contracts range from physically preventing action to simple verbal commitments, but all create decision-interrupts that prompt reflection. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1717-L1720]
- Setting up automatic retirement contributions is a Ulysses contract because changing the allocation requires specific steps, creating friction that interrupts impulsive decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1741-L1743]
