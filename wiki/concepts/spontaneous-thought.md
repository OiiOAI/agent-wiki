# Spontaneous Thought

## Summary

A form of cognition that occurs without conscious control over the direction of the thought process. Spontaneous thought shares features with goal-directed thought but differs in its reliance on the default network and temporal lobe memory regions rather than lateral prefrontal cognitive control. It may serve important functions in memory consolidation and creative problem solving.

## Key facts

- Spontaneous thought competes with goal-directed thought processes, just as goal-directed thought processes compete with each other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p351]
- Capacity for spontaneous thought increases during highly practiced tasks and decreases with age, paralleling changes in goal-directed thought capacity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p351]
- Brain regions recruited in spontaneous mentation during rest overlap with those recruited during goal-directed thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p351]
- Spontaneous thought may facilitate memory consolidation and the integration of isolated episodic experiences into a coherent autobiographical structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p372]

## Inferences

- Inference: Spontaneous thought may be essential for successful semantic memory formation, similar to sleep's role in consolidation.
- Inference: The temporal alternation between spontaneous and goal-directed thought may lead to beneficial outcomes like insights and creative ideas.
