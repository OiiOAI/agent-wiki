# The Dress (Viral Illusion)

## Summary

The Dress was a viral internet phenomenon from February 2015 where viewers disagreed sharply on whether a wedding dress appeared gold and white or blue and black. Research by Pascal Wallisch and colleagues found that different assumptions about the lighting in the room led different brains to make different inferences about the dress color. Larks (morning people) tended to see it as white and gold while owls (night people) saw it as blue and black, suggesting our daily habits influence basic color perception through predictive processing.

## Key facts

- In February 2015, the image of a wedding dress sparked 10 million retweets as viewers disagreed sharply on whether it appeared gold and white or blue and black. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p32]
- Different brains made different assumptions about the lighting in the room (natural versus artificial light, positioning of light source, shadow), leading some to infer gold and others blue. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p33]
- Pascal Wallisch's survey of 13,000 subjects found a strong correlation between assumed lighting conditions and perceived color—those assuming natural light saw white and gold while those assuming artificial light saw blue and black. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p33]
- Self-identified circadian profiles (larks versus owls) correlated strongly with dress perception—larks tended to see white and gold while owls saw blue and black, suggesting daily habits influence color perception through predictions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p34]
