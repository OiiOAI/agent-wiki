# Masochism and Brain Plasticity

## Summary

Sexual masochism represents a dramatic example of neuroplasticity because it rewires the brain so that physical pain becomes sexual pleasure. Research by Robert Stoller found that masochistic individuals had often suffered serious physical illnesses and painful medical treatments as children. They consciously reworked their pain and rage in fantasies, replaying trauma with happy endings and erotizing their agony. This demonstrates that the brain can attach any sensation to either the pleasure or pain system through novel plastic connections.

## Key facts

- Sexual masochism demonstrates plasticity because it fuses the sexual and aggressive systems, and goes further by taking something inherently unpleasant, pain, and turning it into pleasure. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p132]
- Robert Stoller discovered that masochistic participants had all had serious physical illnesses as children and underwent regular, terrifying, painful medical treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p133]
- As children, masochists consciously took their pain and inexpressible rage and reworked it in fantasies so they could replay the trauma with a happy ending, erotizing their agony. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p133]
- The human brain seems able to attach many feelings and sensations either to the pleasure system or to the pain system, each link requiring a novel plastic connection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p134]
- The fact that all serious masochists suffered in early childhood strongly suggests that this rewiring occurred during the critical periods of sexual plasticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p134]

## Inferences

- Inference: Masochism as a 'solution' to childhood trauma represents the brain's attempt to gain mastery over painful experiences by converting them into sources of pleasure.
- Inference: The extreme nature of masochism illustrates the full scope of neuroplasticity's capacity to rewire fundamental human experiences like pain and pleasure.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Not all individuals who experience childhood illness or pain develop masochistic tendencies, suggesting other factors are involved.
