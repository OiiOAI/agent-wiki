# Anton's Syndrome

## Summary

Anton's syndrome, also called 'blindness denial,' is a surprising condition where the patient is clearly unable to see but is unaware of that fact. Asked to describe things, the patient confabulates responses. Crick suggests such patients may produce visual images because brain damage prevents these images from competing with normal visual input, and damage elsewhere may have lost the critical faculty that would normally alert them that something was wrong.

## Key facts

- Anton's syndrome ('blindness denial') is when the patient is clearly unable to see but is unaware of that fact. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p167]
- Asked to describe the doctor's tie, the patient may say it is a blue tie with red spots when in fact the doctor is not wearing a tie at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p167]
- When pressed, the patient may volunteer that the light in the room seems a little dim. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p167]
- Crick proposes that such patients produce visual images because the brain damage is such that these images do not have to compete with normal visual input from the eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p168]
- Due to damage elsewhere, these patients may have lost the critical faculty that would normally alert them that something was wrong. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p168]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether this explanation of Anton's syndrome is correct remains to be seen, but it makes the condition appear not totally incomprehensible.
