# Quantum Indeterminacy

## Summary

Quantum indeterminacy refers to the fundamental randomness discovered in quantum physics where subatomic particles do not have definite properties until they interact. This challenges the idea that the future is completely predetermined by physical laws. Key contributors include Heisenberg's matrix mechanics, Schrödinger's wave function, Born's probability interpretation, Bell's theorem ruling out hidden variables, and the many worlds hypothesis.

## Key facts

- Quantum mechanics reveals that elementary particles like electrons can take only discrete states rather than continuous values. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p155]
- Heisenberg developed matrix mechanics treating particles as if in every state at once through calculations over possible values. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p155]
- Schrödinger developed an equivalent wave function treating particles as waves when not being observed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p155]
- Max Born interpreted the wave function as representing probabilities of finding particles with certain properties in experiments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p156]
- John Bell's theoretical work and subsequent experiments ruled out the existence of hidden variables determining quantum outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p157]
- The many worlds hypothesis proposes that all probable outcomes happen in parallel universes, but this does not eliminate randomness for any individual observer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p157]
