# Sexual Reproduction

## Summary

Sexual reproduction is a key eukaryotic innovation that involves the fusion of two gametes (sperm and egg), each contributing genetic material to create offspring with unique gene combinations. This process, which appears to have been present in the first eukaryotic common ancestor (LECA), enables genetic diversity that helps populations adapt to changing environments.

## Key facts

- Sexual reproduction requires two different mating types or sexes, one of which fertilizes the other. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p78]
- During fertilization, sperm and egg fuse and genes are mixed through recombination, resulting in offspring with unique gene combinations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p92]
- Sexual reproduction first emerged in unicellular eukaryotes (protists); complex organisms like animals evolved later. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p91]
- Genetic markers indicate the capacity for sex is universal in eukaryotes and was present since LECA. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p91]
- Sexual reproduction enables populations to better adapt to changing conditions by increasing genetic variability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p95]
