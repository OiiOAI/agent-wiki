# Self-Respect

## Summary

Self-respect (or self-esteem) is defined as having two aspects: a person's sense of his own value and conviction that his conception of his good is worth carrying out, and confidence in his ability to fulfill his intentions. Rawls claims it is perhaps the most important primary good, without which nothing seems worth doing.

## Key facts

- Self-respect has two aspects: (1) a person's sense of his own value, his conviction that his plan of life is worth carrying out; and (2) confidence in one's ability to fulfill one's intentions. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p391]
- Self-respect is perhaps the most important primary good. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p391]
- Without self-respect nothing may seem worth doing, desire and activity become empty and vain, and one sinks into apathy and cynicism. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p391]
- The conditions supporting the sense of one's own worth include having a rational plan satisfying the Aristotelian Principle, and finding one's person and deeds appreciated by others who are likewise esteemed. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p392]
- The Aristotelian Principle influences self-respect because others tend to value activities that display intricate talents and manifest discrimination and refinement. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p392]
