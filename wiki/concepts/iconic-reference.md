# Iconic Reference

## Summary

Iconic reference is the most basic form of referential relationship, mediated by perceptual similarity between a sign and its referent. According to Peirce's framework adopted by Deacon, icons arise from the failure to distinguish between two things—when a bird interprets moth wings as merely more tree bark due to their similarity, this establishes an iconic relationship. Iconic reference is the default and foundational level on which more complex forms of reference (indexical and symbolic) are built.

## Key facts

- Iconic reference is established when something is interpreted without being distinguished from something else, as when a bird interprets moth wings as just more tree bark due to their similarity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p78]
- Iconic relationships require no additional interpretive process beyond recognition—perceiving resemblance is sufficient. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p80]
- Iconic reference forms the base of the hierarchical reference structure, with indexical and symbolic reference built upon it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p78]
