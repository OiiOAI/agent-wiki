# Infantile Material in Dreams

## Summary

Freud demonstrates that impressions from earliest childhood, including experiences from the first three years of life, strive to achieve reproduction in dreams regardless of their actual content. His analysis of his own dreams, particularly those involving Rome and Hannibal, reveals how childhood memories and wishes determine the form of dream content.

## Key facts

- Freud traces his longing for Rome to impressions from his youth, including following Hannibal's footsteps as a schoolboy hero. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1459]
- The wish to go to Rome became in Freud's dream life a cloak and symbol for a number of other passionate wishes to be pursued with all the perseverance of the Carthaginian. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1460]
- A scene where his father told him about being knocked down and having his cap taken from him struck Freud as unheroic, and he contrasted it with Hannibal's father Hamilcar Barca making his boy swear to avenge the Romans. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1460]
- Freud could trace his enthusiasm for Hannibal to an earlier childhood connection through Masséna (Manasseh), one of Napoleon's marshals whose name appeared on wooden soldiers Freud collected. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1461]
- The deeper one carries the analysis of a dream, the more often one comes upon the track of experiences in childhood which have played a part among the sources of that dream's latent content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1461]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's childhood identification with Hannibal, who symbolized Jewish resistance against the Catholic Church, developed through the influence of anti-Semitic feelings among schoolmates.
- Inference: The progression from Napoleon through Masséna to Hannibal represents a chain of transference of emotional relations onto new objects, all rooted in early childhood.
