# Moral sense and the right hemisphere

## Summary

Moral values are irreducible aspects of the phenomenal world, like colour, linked to the capacity for empathy rather than reasoning. Moral judgments are unconscious and intuitive, deeply bound up with emotional sensitivity to others. Empathy is intrinsic to morality. The right ventromedial frontal cortex, with rich interconnections with limbic structures, is critical to every aspect of moral and social behaviour.

## Key facts

- Moral values are not something that we work out rationally on the principle of utility, but are irreducible aspects of the phenomenal world, like colour. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p129]
- Moral judgments are not deliberative, but unconscious and intuitive, deeply bound up with our emotional sensitivity to others; empathy is intrinsic to morality. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p129]
- Patients with lesions in the ventromedial frontal lobes are impulsive, fail to foresee consequences and are emotionally disengaged from others; specifically the right ventromedial frontal cortex, which has rich interconnections with limbic structures, is critical to every aspect of moral and social behaviour. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p129]
- Damage in the right prefrontal cortex may lead to frank psychopathic behaviour; psychopaths, who have no sense of guilt, shame or responsibility, have deficits in the right frontal lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p129]
- Our sense of justice is underwritten by the right hemisphere, particularly by the right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex; with inactivation of this area, we act more selfishly. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p130]
- The right hemisphere's capacity to inhibit our natural impulse to selfishness is also the area on which we most rely for self-control and the power to resist temptation. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p130]
