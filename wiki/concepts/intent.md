# Intent (Trust)

## Summary

Intent is the second Core of Credibility, defined as one's plan or purpose. It encompasses three components: Motive (the reason for doing something), Agenda (what you intend to promote because of your motive), and Behavior (the manifestation of motive and agenda). The motive that inspires greatest trust is genuine caring, and the agenda that inspires greatest trust is seeking mutual benefit. The Trustee Standard requires acting in the best interest of others.

## Key facts

- Motive is the 'why' that motivates the 'what'. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p107]
- The three accelerators for improving intent are: examine and refine your motives, declare your intent, and choose abundance. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p114]
- Abundance means there is enough for everybody and is a choice anyone can make regardless of economic status. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p118]
- Declaring intent signals behavior and lets people know what to look for. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p117]

## Inferences

- Inference: tent is foundational because while we judge ourselves by intent, we judge others by behavior, creating trust dynamics where transparency about intent becomes crucial for building credibility.
