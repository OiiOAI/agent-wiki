# Dissipative Structures

## Summary

Dissipative structures, discovered by Ilya Prigogine (Nobel Prize 1977), are ordered structures that emerge in open systems operating far from thermodynamic equilibrium. Unlike equilibrium structures (like crystals) that are static and can be isolated, dissipative structures exist only through continuous flow of energy and matter. Prigogine showed that dissipation becomes a source of order—instabilities and jumps to new forms emerge through fluctuations amplified by positive feedback. This theory implies a radical reconceptualization: shift from stability to instability, order to disorder, equilibrium to non-equilibrium, being to becoming.

## Key facts

- Prigogine discovered that classical thermodynamics only explains 'equilibrium structures' such as crystals, while biological cells and cities are fundamentally different because they exist only because they are open. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p135]
- In dissipative structures, dissipation of energy becomes a source of order rather than waste, emphasizing the close association between structure, order, and energy dissipation. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p134]
- Prigogine stated: 'nonequilibrium is the source of order, nonequilibrium brings order out of chaos,' as systems driven far-from-equilibrium can reach critical instability points where new ordered patterns emerge. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p135]
- In open systems, positive feedback loops—which had been regarded as destructive in cybernetics—appear as sources of new order and complexity when systems are far from equilibrium. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p135]
- Dissipative structures undergo 'bifurcation points' where systems may abandon their original path and choose between two possible new trajectories based on very small differences in controlling parameters. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p138]
- Prigogine's theory implies indeterminacy at the macroscopic level, adding another 'producer' of fundamental uncertainty to those identified by Gödel and Heisenberg. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p138]

## Inferences

- Inference: Prigogine's bifurcation concept provides a scientific basis for understanding how systems can undergo sudden regime changes—highly relevant to understanding strategic collapse or victory.
