# Social Norm Compliance

## Summary

Social norm compliance refers to the regulation of behavior based on internalized social rules. Brain stimulation studies have established that the right lateral prefrontal cortex (LPFC) has a causal role in social norm enforcement, particularly for fairness norms. Both voluntary compliance and sanction-induced compliance can be modulated by tDCS, with opposite effects on each type.

## Key facts

- Disruption of the right LPFC with low-frequency TMS impaired participants' ability to build a positive reputation in an iterated trust game, reducing returns of investments received. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p239]
- Disruption of the right DLPFC reduced subjects' willingness to reject unfair offers in the ultimatum game, showing a causal role in resisting selfish economic temptation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p239]
- tDCS over right LPFC affected sanction-induced norm compliance (increased by anodal, decreased by cathodal) and voluntary compliance in opposite ways (decreased by anodal, increased by cathodal). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p239]
- Disruption of DLPFC activity led only to a reduction of punishment for wrongful acts without affecting corresponding blameworthiness ratings, indicating effects on behavior regulation rather than belief representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p239]

## Inferences

- Inference: The opposite effects of tDCS on voluntary versus sanction-induced compliance suggest these two forms of norm enforcement rely on different neural mechanisms that can be dissociated.
