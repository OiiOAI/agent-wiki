# Comparative Neuroanatomy

## Summary

The study comparing brain structures across different species to understand similarities and differences. This field has revealed that while mammalian brains share basic organization, significant differences exist in connectivity, cortical architecture, and genetic regulation that may explain cognitive differences between species.

## Key facts

- Comparative neuroanatomy was central to debates following Darwin's Origin of Species about whether humans descended from apes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p18]
- Ralph Holloway proposed in the 1960s that evolutionary changes in cognitive capacity result from brain reorganization rather than changes in size alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p19]
- Todd Preuss argues that findings using rat and monkey brain models cannot necessarily be extrapolated to humans because mammalian brains differ widely at the microscopic level. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p19]
- The blue whale has a brain 5 times larger than a human brain but is not proportionally smarter due to controlling a larger body with simpler brain structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p19]
