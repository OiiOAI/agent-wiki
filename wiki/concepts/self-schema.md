# Self-Schema

## Summary

Self-schema are nonconscious representations underlying self-concept — encompassing skills, abilities, personality traits, social roles, self-worth, and expectations. The 'working self' is the momentary active variant. Self-schema are crucial for autonoetic consciousness and emotional experiences, which must involve the self to be truly emotional.

## Key facts

- Self-schema includes skills/abilities, foibles, social roles, psychological attributes, self-worth, body responses, future expectations, and feelings about possessions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p294]
- Conway's 'working self' reflects which self-schema components are active at any particular moment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p295]
- Self-schema are nonconscious representations made active by pattern completion, becoming the momentary working self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p295]
- Being autonoetic experiences, emotional feelings are personal and crucially involve the self — all emotional experiences involve the self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p344]
- The frontal pole has been implicated in conceptual self-awareness and the ability to use autobiographical memory in thinking about oneself. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p305]
