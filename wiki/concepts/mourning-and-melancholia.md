# Mourning and Melancholia

## Summary

Freud's 1915 paper compares mourning and melancholia, arguing that while both involve painful dejection and loss of interest, melancholia uniquely involves a profound diminution of self-regard and an impoverishment of the ego. In melancholia, the ego identifies with the lost object, transforming object loss into ego loss. This identification with the abandoned object, along with ambivalence toward the lost object, produces the characteristic self-reproaches directed at the self rather than the absent other.

## Key facts

- Mourning is regularly the reaction to the loss of a loved person, while melancholia may involve loss of a more ideal kind where the object has not necessarily died but has been lost as an object of love. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4611]
- In melancholia, the relation to the object is complicated by the conflict due to ambivalence, which may be constitutional or proceed from experiences involving the threat of losing the object. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4623]
- In melancholia, the object cathexis is withdrawn into the ego and establishes an identification of the ego with the abandoned object; thus the shadow of the object falls upon the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4616]
- The self-tormenting in melancholia signifies satisfaction of sadistic trends which have been turned upon the subject's own self. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4618]
- The ego can kill itself only if, owing to the return of the object cathexis, it can treat itself as an object and direct against itself the hostility which relates to an object. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4619]

## Inferences

- Inference: Melancholia borrows features from mourning (reaction to loss) combined with regression from narcissistic object choice to narcissism itself.
- Inference: The conflict due to ambivalence transforms the lost object relationship into a conflict within the ego between the critical agency and the altered ego.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The economic mechanisms by which mourning overcomes the loss and why mania follows melancholia are not fully explained.
- Uncertain: The precise role of constitutional versus experiential ambivalence in melancholia remains unclear.
