# Hormonal Contingency

## Summary

Hormones rarely act in isolation or determine behaviors outright. Instead, their effects are predominantly contingent and facilitative—hormones make organisms more sensitive to social triggers of existing behavioral tendencies and exaggerate preexisting tendencies rather than creating new ones. The same hormone can produce opposite effects depending on context, individual history, and social environment.

## Key facts

- Testosterone increases aggression only in individuals already prone to aggression, not creating aggression de novo. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p107]
- Oxytocin enhances charitability only in people already charitable. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p118]
- Vasopressin increases aggression most in male rodents who are already aggressive. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p119]
- The same testosterone level can promote aggression, mating, or play depending entirely on context. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p107]
- Testosterone's effects are hugely context dependent, amplifying rather than determining outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p107]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hormonal contingency explains why the same hormone can be implicated in both prosocial and antisocial behaviors—it's not the hormone that determines the outcome but the social context and individual history that the hormone amplifies
- Inference: This principle has implications for understanding individual differences: rather than asking what hormone levels predict behavior, the question becomes what combination of hormones and social context produces which behavioral tendencies
