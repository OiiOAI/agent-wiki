# Outcome Bias

## Summary

Outcome Bias is the tendency to evaluate decisions based on their result rather than the quality of the decision process at the time it was made. This fallacy leads to unfair assessments of good decisions that had bad outcomes due to randomness.

## Key facts

- The outcome bias is the tendency to evaluate decisions based on the result rather than on the decision process. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p55]
- With an average surgeon performing a procedure with 20% mortality rate, there is a 33% chance no one dies, demonstrating that outcomes can mislead about surgeon quality. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p56]
- A bad result does not automatically indicate a bad decision and vice versa; one should assess whether the reasons for choosing were rational and understandable. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p56]
