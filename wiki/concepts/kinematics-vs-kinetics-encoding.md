# Kinematics vs. Kinetics Encoding

## Summary

Motor cortex neurons encode both the spatial parameters of movement (kinematics: direction, speed, path) and the causal forces required to produce movement (kinetics: muscle forces, joint torques). This dual encoding allows the motor system to specify what movement to make and how to generate it.

## Key facts

- Kinematics refers to parameters describing the spatiotemporal form of movement such as direction, amplitude, speed, and path, while kinetics concerns the causal forces and muscle activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p903]
- Evarts showed that the activity of many motor cortex neurons changes with the direction of external load even when movement kinematics remain constant, demonstrating encoding of kinetics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p902]
- Population-vector analyses show that neural activity in the primary motor cortex contains information about the trajectory of hand motions (kinematics) during reaching movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p902]
- The true role of the motor cortex may be to perform the transformation between kinematic and kinetic representations of voluntary movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p906]
