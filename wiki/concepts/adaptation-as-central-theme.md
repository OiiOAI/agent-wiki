# Adaptation as Central Theme

## Summary

Adaptation is identified as the dominant overarching theme connecting all parts of Boyd's work. Rather than the narrow interpretation of 'rapid OODA looping,' Boyd's theory centers on the ability to adapt to unfolding, multidimensional events occurring at different time scales. The strategic aim is to diminish the adversary's capacity to adapt while improving one's own capacity to adapt as an organic whole, so that the adversary cannot cope while one can cope with events as they unfold.

## Key facts

- Boyd defined adaptability as the power to adjust or change in order to cope with new and unforeseen circumstances, noting it seemed the right counterweight to uncertainty. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p284]
- Boyd's strategic aim was stated as: to diminish adversary's capacity while improving our capacity to adapt as an organic whole, so that our adversary cannot cope while we can cope with events/efforts as they unfold. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p284]
- Boyd argued that maintaining ability to adapt while negating that ability to the opponent is the single all-embracing theme connecting the various parts of A Discourse. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p284]
- Boyd viewed contestants—armies, headquarters, societies—as living systems, as organisms, that aim to survive and prosper by observing, learning, and adapting. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p284]
- Boyd recognized adaptation occurs across various timescales and he developed a view on what adaptability means and requires at each level: tactical, grand tactical, strategic, and grand-strategic. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p284]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's adoption of a Neo-Darwinist/Complex Adaptive Systems perspective explains why adaptation is central—organisms that fail to adapt to their dynamic environment face elimination, whether in nature or in conflict.
