# Grip Strength

## Summary

Grip strength—how hard one can squeeze—is a powerful predictor of longevity and overall muscle strength. It serves as the primary interface between the body and the world, and is closely linked to rotator cuff strength and the ability to prevent falls.

## Key facts

- Grip strength is as robust a predictor of mortality as VO2 max and muscle mass. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p267]
- Low grip strength in the elderly is considered a symptom of sarcopenia. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p267]
- In 1985, men ages 20-24 had average right-handed grip strength of 121 pounds; by 2015, men of the same age averaged just 101 pounds. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p267]
- The standard for male patients is to carry half their body weight in each hand for at least one minute; for female patients, 75% of that weight. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p268]
- Dead-hanging from a pull-up bar is a test of grip strength: men should hang for at least two minutes and women for ninety seconds at age 40. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p268]
