# Cerebellar Damage

## Summary

Damage to the cerebellum produces characteristic motor symptoms including ataxia (lack of coordination), dysmetria (overshooting or undershooting targets), intention tremor, dysdiadochokinesia (impaired alternating movements), gait ataxia, and hypotonia. Cerebellar damage also affects proprioceptive abilities during active movement and can produce cognitive deficits including language and visuospatial dysfunction.

## Key facts

- Ataxia is the hallmark of cerebellar disorders, characterized by jerky, irregular, and highly variable movements with curved hand paths and dysmetric reaching. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p910]
- Intention tremor occurs at the end of reaching movements as a series of overshooting corrections, driven by time-delayed visual feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p910]
- Patients with cerebellar damage have difficulty holding the shoulder steady while moving the elbow due to poor predictions of how elbow movement mechanically affects the shoulder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p910]
- Cerebellar damage impairs proprioceptive acuity during active movement but not during passive movement judgments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p910]
- Damage to the right cerebellar hemisphere produces language dysfunction due to its interconnection with the left cerebral hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p911]
- Lesions of the vestibulocerebellum disrupt smooth-pursuit eye movements toward the side of the lesion while leaving vestibular reflexes intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p916]
