# The Three Yeses

## Summary

Chris Voss identifies three distinct types of 'Yes' that a negotiator must learn to distinguish: Counterfeit Yes (a deceptive response to escape), Confirmation Yes (a reflexive black-or-white response), and Commitment Yes (the real agreement that leads to action and signatures). Only the Commitment Yes has value.

## Key facts

- A counterfeit 'yes' is when the counterpart plans to say 'no' but gives 'yes' as an easier escape route or to obtain more information. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p73]
- A confirmation 'yes' is generally a reflexive response to a black-or-white question with no promise of action. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p73]
- A commitment 'yes' is the real deal—a true agreement that leads to action and ends with a signature on a contract. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p73]
- Human beings have become masters at giving counterfeit 'yes' because they are used to being pursued for the commitment 'yes' as a condition to find out more. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p73]
- Good negotiators know their job isn't to perform but to gently guide their counterpart to discover their goal as his own. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p73]
