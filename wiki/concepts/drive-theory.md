# Drive Theory (Dual-Drive Theory)

## Summary

The dual-drive theory posits two fundamental classes of drives operating in the psyche: Eros (the life drives, including sexual drives) and Thanatos (the death drive). These drives are conservative, seeking to restore an earlier state of things disturbed by the emergence of life. The theory was first proposed in Beyond the Pleasure Principle (1920) and forms a central framework in The Ego and the Id for understanding psychic functioning, pathology, and the relationships between the id, ego, and superego.

## Key facts

- Eros comprises the sexual drives proper and the drive impulses of an aim-inhibited or sublimated nature, as well as the self-preservative drive assigned to the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5925]
- The death drive (Thanatos) aims to lead organic life back into the inanimate state. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5925]
- The death drive expresses itself as a drive to destruction directed against the external world through the instrumentality of the muscular apparatus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5926]
- The two classes of drives are fused, blended and alloyed with each other regularly and very extensively. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5926]
- Defusion of drives occurs when the erotic component no longer has the power to bind the whole of the destructiveness that was combined with it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5939]
- The death drives are by their nature mute and the clamour of life proceeds for the most part from Eros. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5931]
- The superego arises from identifications which are in the nature of desexualization or sublimation, and when such transformation takes place, drive defusion occurs at the same time. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5939]

## Inferences

- Inference: The theory explains that the death drive was diverted from the self onto the external world through the instrumentality of Eros. Through the agency of Eros, the destructive drives directed towards the external world have been diverted from the self.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanism by which drive fusion occurs remains unexplained in the text, though it is stated to be indispensable to psychoanalytic conception.
