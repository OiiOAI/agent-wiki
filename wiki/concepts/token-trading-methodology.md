# Token Trading Methodology

## Summary

A novel experimental method developed by Chen, Lakshminarayanan, and Santos that allows capuchin monkeys to reveal their economic preferences in a situation analogous to human markets, using tokens that can be traded with different experimenters for different goods.

## Key facts

- The methodology capitalizes on capuchins' ability to be quickly trained to trade tokens for food rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p85]
- In the experimental set-up, capuchins were presented with two experimenters (distinguished by differently colored clothing) who each offered different kinds or amounts of goods in exchange for tokens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p85]
- This set-up permitted introducing price and wealth changes to examine how such changes affected capuchins' purchasing behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p85]
- The method also allowed observation of whether capuchins preferred options that stochastically dominated others and whether their preferences were affected by reference points and framing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p85]

## Inferences

- Inference: The token trading methodology provides a behavioral assay needed for neurophysiological insights into economic behavior in primates, as it creates situations analogous to human economic markets.
