# Expectations (vs. Fantasies)

## Summary

Expectations are beliefs or judgments about the future based on past experiences, distinct from fantasies which are free-flowing wishes unconnected to reality. Research by Albert Bandura and Martin Seligman established that positive expectations increase effort and achievement. Oettingen's work shows that expectations and fantasies operate differently: expectations energize action while fantasies relax and reduce it. Only when fantasies are combined with mental contrasting (considering obstacles) do they enhance the motivational effect of expectations.

## Key facts

- Martin Seligman conceived of optimism as beliefs or expectations about the future based on past experiences of success. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p16]
- Albert Bandura and Martin Seligman established that expectations based on past experience increase effort and actual achievement. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p17]
- In studies, women who assessed they were likely to lose weight based on past experience shed an average of 26 pounds more than those who didn't believe they would lose much weight. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p18]
- The key difference is that expectations are rational, experience-based judgments, while fantasies are free-flowing thoughts rooted in wishes. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p16]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between expectations and fantasies may explain why popular positive thinking advice fails: it conflates two psychologically distinct phenomena that operate through different mechanisms.
