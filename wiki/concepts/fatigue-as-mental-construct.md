# Fatigue as Mental Construct

## Summary

Fatigue is proposed to be a mental construct determined by the mind rather than biological physical limits. Research demonstrates that fatigue onset and peak are proportional to expected task duration rather than actual physical exertion, suggesting we impose structure on tasks and experience tiredness at predictable points regardless of objective capacity.

## Key facts

- In a jumping jack study, people asked to do 200 jumping jacks reported fatigue around 130-140 (two-thirds), while those asked to do 100 reported fatigue around 65-70—same proportional point. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p109]
- Typing errors appeared at the two-thirds mark regardless of whether participants typed one or two pages. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p109]
- In a driving study, fatigue was reported at approximately 50% of travel time and peaked around 75%, regardless of actual distance traveled. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p109]
- EEG data showed observable peaks of compensation signals in alpha band wave amplitude during fatigue periods, following the same pattern across different task durations. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p110]
- Professional ballerinas showed signs of fatigue about one-third through and peaked at three-quarters through the ballet position hold, regardless of actual hold duration. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p110]
- People told they had a 'tiring' gene had less endurance and poorer lung capacity regardless of their actual genetic profile, with changes in metabolic exchange rate. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since fatigue follows expectations rather than physical limits, knowing this allows people to willfully change when fatigue occurs, potentially enabling greater endurance.
