# Compersion

## Summary

This chunk introduces compersion as a term used in polyamorous and ethically nonmonogamous communities to describe a feeling of happiness at seeing one's partner enjoy sexual or romantic connection with someone else. Perel presents it as an example of people working to 'evolve beyond' jealousy, though she notes that even among nonmonogamous couples, intense jealousy still frequently occurs.

## Key facts

- Compersion is described as 'a feeling of happiness at seeing one's partner enjoy sexual contact with someone else,' used by those identifying as polyamorous. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p95]
- Perel notes that polyamorous people 'actively work to overcome jealousy, seeing it as part and parcel of the possessive relationship paradigm they are trying to best.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p95]
- Despite efforts to develop compersion, 'plenty of nonmonogamous couples' still 'struggle with intense bouts of jealousy,' according to Perel's observations. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p96]
- Anna, a polyamorous individual Perel quotes, states: 'I know she's careful not to intentionally trigger those responses in me, and I do the same for her, but we're not responsible for each other's feelings.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p96]
