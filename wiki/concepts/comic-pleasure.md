# Comic pleasure

## Summary

Pleasure derived from the comic, arising from a comparison between two cathectic expenditures concerned with the same function. Freud argues that comic pleasure results from differences in psychical expenditure being discharged when recognized as superfluous, analogous to how joke-work prevents the use elsewhere of liberated cathectic energy. The conditions for generating comic pleasure include cheerful mood, expectation of the comic, and automatic (unattended) comparison processes.

## Key facts

- Comic pleasure arises from a comparison between two cathectic expenditures that occur in rapid succession and are concerned with the same function, and these expenditures are discharged when declared superfluous. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2825]
- A person appears comic if, in comparison with oneself, he makes too great an expenditure on his bodily functions and too little on his mental ones—and laughter expresses pleasurable superiority. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2824]
- The most favorable condition for comic pleasure is a generally cheerful mood; in euphoria almost everything seems comic, probably by comparison with expenditure in a normal state. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2844]
- Comic pleasure is greatly interfered with if strong affect is released simultaneously—the difference in expenditure must be free for discharge rather than consumed by emotional response. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2845]
- The comic process must be able to take its course unobserved; it cannot bear being hypercathected by attention and must remain 'automatic.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2845]

## Inferences

- Inference: Comic pleasure and joke pleasure share the mechanism of discharge of economized expenditure, but differ in localization—joke pleasure is specifically linked to unconscious processes while comic pleasure involves preconscious comparison.
- Inference: The relativity of the comic (emerging and vanishing according to the observer's standpoint) results from its dependence on conditions like mood, attention, and emotional involvement, unlike jokes which are always made intentionally.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges he cannot give an exhaustive account of the nature of the comic due to limitations of his education and daily occupation, though he identifies its essential mechanism.
