# Surrender

## Summary

The central spiritual practice of Michael Singer's life journey, involving letting go of personal resistance to life's events and allowing the Universal Flow to guide one's path. Singer describes surrender not as passivity but as active acceptance that leads to purification and spiritual growth, using life's challenges as opportunities to release attachment and fear.

## Key facts

- Singer describes surrendering to life as 'Karma yoga at its highest' where one gives their life to the Universal Flow and it devours them in the process. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p175]
- After 30 years of experiencing perfection unfold, Singer felt nothing in him wanted to interfere with the flow ever again. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p174]
- Singer realized that life situations and challenges were rooting out what willful meditation had not eliminated. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p187]
- The FBI raid was described by Singer as an opportunity to use the situation to finally free himself from whatever was left of the scared person inside who had always held him back. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p193]

## Inferences

- Inference: Singer's practice of surrender appears to have enabled him to maintain inner peace during the FBI raid and subsequent legal proceedings without experiencing lasting psychological trauma
- Inference: The concept of surrender in this context is not about achieving outcomes but about maintaining inner freedom regardless of external circumstances
