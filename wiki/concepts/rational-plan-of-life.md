# Rational Plan of Life

## Summary

A rational plan of life is one consistent with principles of rational choice and chosen with full deliberative rationality. It is not a detailed blueprint but a hierarchy of subplans becoming less specific for distant periods. The rational plan determines a person's good and involves arranging activities harmoniously over time.

## Key facts

- A person's plan of life is rational if it is consistent with principles of rational choice and is that plan he would choose with full deliberative rationality. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p364]
- A rational plan is presumably one of many possible plans consistent with rational choice; principles of rational choice do not single out one plan as the best. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p364]
- A rational plan is not a detailed blueprint but a hierarchy of plans, becoming less specific for later periods. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p365]
- The principle of postponement holds that if we may later want to do one of several things but are unsure which, we should plan to keep alternatives open. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p365]
- A person is happy when successfully executing a rational plan under favorable conditions with reasonable confidence it can be carried through. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p365]
