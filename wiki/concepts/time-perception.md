# Time perception

## Summary

Time perception emerges from perceptual best guessing about the rate of change of sensory signals, not from any internal neuronal clock. Research by Warrick Roseboom showed that subjective duration judgments match computational estimates based on visual processing dynamics.

## Key facts

- We experience seconds, hours, months, and years, but have no 'time sensors' inside our brains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p132]
- There is no evidence for any 'neuronal clock' inside the head which measures out experiences in time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p132]
- Experiences of time are controlled hallucinations, emerging from a 'best guess' about the rate of change of sensory signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p132]
- Warrick Roseboom proposed that time is inferred based on the rate of change of perceptual contents in other modalities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p132]
- Human duration estimates matched neural network estimates based on accumulated rate of change of activity, showing no 'inner clock' is needed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p132]
- People systematically underestimate long durations and overestimate short durations (Vierordt's law), a signature of Bayesian inference where the mean acts as a prior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p139]

## Inferences

- Inference: Time perception being a controlled hallucination means that subjective time can be manipulated by altering perceptual content dynamics, not by manipulating any dedicated temporal mechanism.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural implementation of this rate-of-change estimation remains under investigation.
