# Commitment and Consistency

## Summary

The desire for consistency constitutes a powerful weapon of influence, creating internal and interpersonal pressures to behave consistently with prior commitments. Once a stand is taken or a choice made, people experience psychological forces to bring beliefs and behaviors into alignment with that commitment, even when the original commitment was trivial or coerced.

## Key facts

- Immediately after placing a bet, racetrack bettors are significantly more confident of their horse's chances than they were before the bet, despite the horse's actual odds unchanged. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p57]
- Sara's case demonstrates how choosing one option over another (Tim vs. wedding) increased devotion to that choice even when conditions promised were never fulfilled. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p58]
- Thomas Moriarty's beach theft experiment showed that simply agreeing to 'watch someone's things' increased intervention against a thief from 4 out of 20 to 19 out of 20. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p59]
- Automatic consistency offers a cognitive shortcut, allowing people to avoid the mental effort of re-evaluating issues once a decision has been made. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p60]
- People sometimes hide inside consistency walls to avoid troubling realizations—TM lecture attendees paid immediately after a logical demolition to avoid thinking about the critique. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p62]
- The Chinese POW camp program used commitment tactics to transform American prisoners, getting 'nearly all American prisoners in the Chinese camps' to collaborate to some degree. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p69]
- Writing is especially powerful as a commitment device because it provides physical evidence and creates a lasting personal reminder that is difficult to deny. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p74]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-image becomes squeezed from both inside (internal consistency pressure) and outside (others' perceptions based on our actions) when commitment is made.
- Inference: The power of consistency explains why people often feel worse about difficult decisions after backing down—retreat threatens the self-image built around the original commitment.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific neurological basis for the discomfort of inconsistency is not addressed.
