# Inhibition-Stabilized Network

## Summary

An inhibition-stabilized network is a neural circuit model in which excitatory neurons recurrently excite one another while inhibitory neurons recurrently inhibit one another, with excitatory cells exciting inhibitory neurons which provide feedback inhibition. This architecture is proposed as the operating mode of V1 visual cortex, where stronger recurrent excitation to inhibitory neurons than surround-induced excitation results in decreased inhibitory firing rates when external inputs are removed.

## Key facts

- In an inhibition-stabilized network, excitatory neurons recurrently excite one another while inhibitory neurons recurrently inhibit one another. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1665]
- Excitatory cells excite the inhibitory neurons, which in turn provide feedback inhibition to the excitatory cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1665]
- When a network is inhibition-stabilized, the withdrawal of recurrent excitation to inhibitory neurons is larger than the surround-induced increase of external excitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1665]
- As a result of the inhibition-stabilized state, inhibitory neurons receive less excitation than initially and their firing rate decreases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1665]
- Evidence suggests that V1 (primary visual cortex) operates as an inhibition-stabilized network. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1669]
