# Transference Neuroses

## Summary

The transference neuroses comprise anxiety hysteria, conversion hysteria, and obsessional neurosis. Freud's Overview compares these three conditions across six factors: repression, anticathexis, substitutive and symptom formation, relation to the sexual function, regression, and disposition. Each neurosis shows different patterns of regression and success in repression, with conversion hysteria achieving complete success at the cost of massive substitutive formation, while anxiety hysteria shows the least success and obsessional neurosis displays initial complete but non-lasting success.

## Key facts

- In anxiety hysteria, repression is least successful and is confined to preventing a preconscious and conscious representation; instead, a substitutive idea becomes preconscious and conscious, and phobia achieves its aim through inhibition of the affect of unpleasure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4634]
- In obsessional neurosis, success of repression is at first complete but not lasting, proceeding through phases of secondary repression with formation of obsessional ideas and tertiary phase producing renunciations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4634]
- In conversion hysteria, success from the outset is complete, but at the cost of massive substitutive formation, with the process of individual events of repression being more complete. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4634]
- The repressed drive impulse is always a libidinal one belonging to sexual life, while repression proceeds from the ego out of motives that can be summed up as not being able to or not wanting to. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4636]
- In conversion hysteria, there is a strong ego regression to a phase without separation of Pcs. and Ucs., without language and censorship, where the drive impulse returns to an earlier stage for discharge. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4637]
- Hysteria, anxiety hysteria and obsessional neurosis are the transference neuroses, which can be analyzed successfully. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5197]
- In transference neuroses, the analyst traces symptoms back to the basic situation in which sexual drives have come into conflict with self-preservative drives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5170]
- The transfer neuroses offered the most favourable material for tracing the course of libidinal cathexes and their transformations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]

## Inferences

- Inference: The common distinguishing feature of transference neuroses is clinging on to the object, in contrast to the narcissistic neuroses.
- Inference: The later the neurosis appears in life, the earlier the phase of libido to which it regresses.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that the discussion of phylogenetic factors extends beyond what can be verified.
- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which anticathexis achieves its effects in different neuroses are not fully elaborated.
