# The Cobra Effect

## Summary

The Cobra Effect is a phenomenon where incentive-based interventions produce unintended consequences that worsen the problem they were designed to solve. The term originates from a story in colonial India where a bounty on cobra heads led to the breeding of cobras for profit. The concept illustrates the dangers of measuring and manipulating outcomes without understanding complex system dynamics.

## Key facts

- The Cobra Effect originated from authorities in India who offered a bounty for dead cobra heads, initially reducing cobra numbers but subsequently leading to cobra breeding farms. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p281]
- When the authorities stopped the reward scheme, cobra breeders released the snakes, making the problem worse than initially. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p281]
- The Cobra Effect illustrates that 'what gets measured gets manipulated' and is relevant to the limitations of linear surveys in capturing well-being. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p281]
