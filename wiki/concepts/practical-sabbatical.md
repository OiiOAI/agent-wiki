# Practical Sabbatical

## Summary

Taking regular breaks from work—daily, weekly, or monthly—to recharge and avoid burnout. Designer Stefan Sagmeister swears by taking a full year off every seven years, but Kleon adapts this into practical sabbaticals: using commute time, exercise, or nature as ways to disconnect and let the mind relax and generate new ideas.

## Key facts

- Designer Stefan Sagmeister shuts down his studio every seven years and takes a year off. He says everything designed in seven years following a sabbatical had roots in thinking done during that sabbatical. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p182]
- Practical sabbaticals are daily, weekly, or monthly breaks where you walk away from work completely. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p184]
- Three prime spots to turn off your brain: Commute (write, doodle, read, or stare out the window; audiobooks for car commutes), Exercise (using your body relaxes your mind), Nature (park, hike, garden, fresh air). [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p184]
- Kleon's wife is quoted: 'If you never go to work, you never get to leave work.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p184]
- Robert Mitchum is quoted: 'Every two or three years, I knock off for a while. That way, I'm constantly the new girl in the whorehouse.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p184]
