# Hypervigilance

## Summary

Hypervigilance is a state in which a person is constantly scanning their environment for potential threats or danger, diverting cognitive resources away from focused attention. It develops when people experience repeated stressful or traumatic situations, including living in dangerous neighborhoods, experiencing abuse, or facing chronic stressors like financial insecurity.

## Key facts

- Hypervigilance is described as being 'essentially when you're looking out for the bear around every corner,' with attention focused on cues for potential danger rather than on the lesson or work at hand. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p189]
- Dr. Jon Jureidini explained that selective attention is 'a really good strategy in a safe environment' but 'a really dumb strategy' in a dangerous one, where you need to spread vigilance around looking for cues for danger. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p191]
- Nadine Burke Harris found that children who had been sexually abused had double the usual rate of diagnosable ADHD, suggesting trauma-induced hypervigilance mimicked attention disorders. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p191]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hypervigilance provides a mechanism explaining why stress and trauma lead to attention problems without requiring the assumption of an inherent neurological disorder.
