# Antidepressant Pharmacological Mechanisms

## Summary

Modern antidepressant medications primarily work by manipulating biogenic amine neurotransmitter systems in the brain. Tricyclic antidepressants inhibit reuptake of norepinephrine, serotonin, and dopamine at synapses. Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) more specifically block serotonin reuptake. MAO inhibitors block the enzyme monoamine oxidase that degrades biogenic amines. All of these drugs substantially reduce REM sleep, which may contribute to their therapeutic effects. The therapeutic lag of 2-4 weeks suggests that biogenic amine increases are not the primary mechanism—adaptive changes in receptor sensitivity and neuroplasticity likely underlie clinical improvement.

## Key facts

- Tricyclic antidepressants facilitate norepinephrine, serotonin, or dopamine reuptake at synapses, prolonging their synaptic availability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p67]
- Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) such as fluoxetine specifically block serotonin reuptake, with fewer side effects than tricyclics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p67]
- MAO inhibitors block monoamine oxidase, the enzyme that normally degrades biogenic amines following synaptic release, though they have more side effects including dietary restrictions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p67]
- All effective antidepressant drugs substantially reduce REM sleep, which may contribute to their therapeutic effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p78]
- REM sleep deprivation can counteract depression as effectively as antidepressant drugs, suggesting that suppression of REM-related processes may be a common therapeutic mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p78]
- Most modern antianxiety agents interact with benzodiazepine receptors facilitating GABA activity, while newer agents like buspirone interact with serotonin receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p67]
- Many investigators believe that functional psychiatric disorders result from neurochemical imbalances among multiple transmitter systems, rather than pathology in a single system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p67]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery that REM deprivation can alleviate depression suggests that REM-related neurochemical changes (such as cortisol surges) may maintain or worsen depressive states.
- Inference: The antidepressant effects of biogenic amine manipulation likely involve downstream adaptive changes in receptor systems and neural plasticity rather than direct neurotransmitter enhancement.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise molecular and cellular mechanisms by which enhanced biogenic amine availability eventually reduces depressive symptoms remain incompletely characterized.
