# Precautionary Principle in Food Safety

## Summary

The precautionary principle would require proving a chemical is safe before adding it to the food supply. However, in the US and many Western governments, companies are free to add many chemicals that have not been proven safe. Research on chemical combinations (cocktail effects) and low-level exposure impacts on psychiatric disorders is lacking due to funding difficulties.

## Key facts

- In the US, companies are free to add many chemicals to food that have not been proven safe. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p239]
- PFAS chemicals are likely detectable in all major US water supplies that use surface water. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p239]
- The cocktail effect—combining different chemicals—has not been adequately studied. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p240]
- Studies on low-level pesticide exposure and psychiatric disorders suggest some people may be more vulnerable than others. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p240]

## Inferences

- Inference: The burden of proof for chemical safety in food is placed on consumers rather than manufacturers.
