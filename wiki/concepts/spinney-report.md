# Spinney Report

## Summary

The Spinney Report, officially titled 'Defense Facts of Life,' was a devastating critique of Pentagon weapons procurement created by Chuck Spinney under Boyd's mentorship. The report documented how unnecessary complexity in weapons systems was wrecking the military budget and causing readiness problems, ultimately proving that virtually everything the Air Force promised about the F-15 and F-111D was false. It launched Spinney onto the national stage and became one of the most impactful documents ever produced in the Pentagon.

## Key facts

- Spinney's report showed that supporting the F-15 was more expensive than supporting the ancient B-52, and that in a full-scale war, supplies of the Air Force's favorite munitions would last only a few days. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p340]
- The report's most significant finding was that readiness problems were not caused by lack of funds but by Air Force leaders who deliberately bought expensive, overly complex weapons. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p340]
- Spinney made no recommendations in his briefing, choosing to be the 'wrecking crew' who tore the domain apart through destructive deduction. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p341]
- The Air Force could find no factual errors in the report and responded with derision, sarcasm, hyperbole, and personal attacks, including calling Spinney a 'captain who has never been shot at.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p342]
- Spinney appeared on the cover of Time magazine on March 7, 1983, identified as a 'Pentagon Maverick' with the cover question 'Are Billions Being Wasted?'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p360]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd convinced Spinney to appear on the Time cover as protection, understanding that becoming too prominent to attack was the best defense against Pentagon vengeance.
