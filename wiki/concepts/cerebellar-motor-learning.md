# Cerebellar Motor Learning

## Summary

The cerebellum is critical for motor learning in multiple movement systems including limb movements, eye movements, and walking. Learning occurs through long-term depression at parallel fiber-Purkinje cell synapses when climbing fibers signal errors, and also in deep cerebellar nuclei. The cerebellum participates in adaptation of the vestibulo-ocular reflex, classical conditioning of eye-blink responses, prism adaptation, and learning new walking patterns on split-belt treadmills.

## Key facts

- Climbing-fiber activity can induce long-term depression selectively at parallel fiber synapses that are activated concurrently with climbing fiber input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p924]
- The vestibulo-ocular reflex adapts to changes in visual scene size caused by magnifying or miniaturizing spectacles over several days. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p927]
- Adaptation of the vestibulo-ocular reflex is blocked by lesions of the floccular complex of the vestibulocerebellum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p927]
- Saccadic adaptation involves the oculomotor vermis in lobules V, VI, and VII, where Purkinje cells signal saccadic errors during learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p927]
- Patients with cerebellar damage cannot learn over hundreds of steps to make their walking pattern symmetric on a split-belt treadmill, though they can use feedback to immediately change walking patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p928]
- Learning occurs at multiple sites in the cerebellar circuit, including both cerebellar cortex and deep cerebellar nuclei, with potential transfer of learning from cortex to nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p928]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cerebellum may use synaptic changes to create and maintain accurate internal models that allow prediction of movement outcomes.
- Inference: Different forms of motor learning (VOR adaptation, saccadic adaptation, prism adaptation) may share common cellular mechanisms involving climbing fiber signaling of prediction errors.
