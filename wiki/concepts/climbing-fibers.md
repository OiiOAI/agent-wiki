# Climbing fibers

## Summary

Climbing fibers are one of the two principal afferent inputs to the cerebellum, originating in the inferior olivary nucleus. Each climbing fiber enwraps the cell body and proximal dendrites of a Purkinje neuron like a vine on a tree, making numerous synaptic contacts. Climbing fibers generate complex spikes—prolonged depolarization producing an initial large-amplitude action potential followed by a high-frequency burst of smaller-amplitude spikes. The climbing fiber system appears specialized for event detection rather than information encoding through firing rate.

## Key facts

- Climbing fibers originate in the inferior olivary nucleus and convey sensory information to the cerebellum from both the periphery and the cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Each climbing fiber contacts 1 to 10 Purkinje neurons, but each Purkinje neuron receives synaptic input from only a single climbing fiber. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Each action potential in a climbing fiber generates a protracted, voltage-gated Ca2+ conductance in the soma and dendrites of the postsynaptic Purkinje cell, producing a complex spike. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1019]
- In awake animals, climbing fibers spontaneously generate complex spikes at low rates, rarely more than one to three per second. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1019]
- The climbing fiber system appears specialized for event detection; the firing rate carries little or no information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1019]

## Inferences

- Inference: The highly specific connectivity of the climbing fiber system contrasts markedly with the massive convergence and divergence of mossy fibers, suggesting specialized function for precise control of Purkinje cells with related functions.
