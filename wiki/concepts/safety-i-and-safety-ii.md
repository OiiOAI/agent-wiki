# Safety-I and Safety-II

## Summary

Safety-I and Safety-II represent two contrasting paradigms in safety management. Safety-I focuses on preventing accidents through training, compliance rules, and error elimination, while Safety-II emphasizes that safety emerges from the ability to successfully adapt to varying conditions. Both approaches are seen as complementary rather than mutually exclusive.

## Key facts

- Safety-I, described by Erik Hollnagel, has for the past century emphasized training, eliminating errors, imposing compliance rules, reprimanding undesirable behaviors, assessing known hazards/risks, setting accident targets, and assigning blame. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p431]
- Erik Hollnagel states that Safety-I's weakness is that 'We can guard against everything that we can imagine. But we cannot imagine everything. Therefore, we cannot guard against everything.'. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p433]
- Safety-II relies on human judgment and experience to adjust behavior in the absence of explicit risk procedures, or when rules get in the way, or when unexpected changes signal danger emerging. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p433]
- Safety-II approaches include Safety Differently, New View, and Human Organization Principles (HOP). [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p431]
- Research concluded that combining Safety-I and Safety-II perspectives broadens understanding of safety management. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p439]
- Todd Conklin's 'Safety Spear' highlights the disconnect between workers at the sharp end and regulators at the blunt end who have the most authority. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p432]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Cynefin Framework maps Safety-I primarily to the Clear and Complicated domains, while Safety culture and complex safety issues reside in the Complex domain
- Inference: Organizations should manage a mixed portfolio combining Safety-I (robustness) and Safety-II (resilience) approaches
