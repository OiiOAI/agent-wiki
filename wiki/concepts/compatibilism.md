# Compatibilism

## Summary

Compatibilism is the philosophical position that free will is compatible with determinism. Mitchell critiques compatibilism, arguing that it cannot explain how free will or real agency could exist in a deterministic universe. Compatibilists typically treat people as if they have choice based on the complexity and unpredictability of brain processes, even while acknowledging that real choice does not exist.

## Key facts

- Daniel Dennett defends that organisms do things for reasons instantiated in neural or biochemical circuitry, but maintains determinism holds and no real choice exists. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p10]
- Compatibilists argue humans can be treated as if they have choice because brain processes are so complicated as to be completely unpredictable in practice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p10]
- Mitchell argues compatibilism cannot explain how free will or real agency can possibly exist in a deterministic universe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p10]
- Compatibilism typically holds that we have something resembling a spirit or soul that embodies free will, which coexists with biology that can sometimes constrain it—the idea that well-intentioned spirit can be thwarted by sufficiently weak flesh. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p603]
- Sapolsky describes the compatibilist view as imagining a homunculus sitting in a concrete bunker in the brain, controlling behavior, separate from 'squishy biological brain yuck' and operating independently of material science. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p604]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sapolsky argues that compatibilism, even in its softened forms, tacitly invokes a homunculus operating outside the rules of science whenever it claims any behavior results from 'free choice' rather than biological determination.
