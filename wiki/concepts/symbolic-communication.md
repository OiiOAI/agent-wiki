# Symbolic Communication

## Summary

Symbolic communication is a mode of representation in which the relationship between a sign and its referent is arbitrary and independent of any concrete or indexical connection, allowing reference to abstract, intangible, or absent objects. This form of communication is uniquely developed in humans and is considered the foundation of language, requiring a cognitive shift that makes it extremely difficult for other species despite their sophisticated nonsymbolic communicative abilities.

## Key facts

- Symbolic communication requires learning associations that are not useful until the complete system of interdependent associations is assembled. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p408]
- The first symbolic systems were likely fragile modes of communication that were difficult to learn, inefficient, slow, inflexible, and probably applied to a very limited communicative domain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p409]
- Symbolic abilities do not necessarily represent more efficient communication, but instead represent a radical shift in communicative strategy from indexical to symbolic reference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p409]
- Most of the symbol use in a society, even excluding language, is not embodied in material artifacts but only in ceremonies, habits, and rules that govern everyday life. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p396]

## Inferences

- Inference: The evolution of symbolic communication created a mode of extrabiological inheritance with autonomous life of its own, reshaping human brains and minds through co-evolutionary processes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the first symbolic systems were vocal, gestural, or a combination of different modes of expression in early hominids.
