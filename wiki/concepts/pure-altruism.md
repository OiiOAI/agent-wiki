# Pure Altruism

## Summary

Pure Altruism is an economic model assuming people derive utility only from the level of a public good itself, not from the act of giving. The model predicts free-riding in public goods provision. Warm-glow and impure altruism models augment this by including utility from the act of giving, explaining why charitable giving is more common than pure altruism predicts.

## Key facts

- In the Pure Altruism model, individuals maximize utility u = u(xi, G) where they care about the level of public good G but not about how that level is achieved. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p304]
- The Pure Altruism model predicts free-riding—by all but a few with highest benefits—in providing public goods, yet charitable giving is remarkably widespread in reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p305-306]
- ImPure Altruism models include both pure altruism and warm-glow motives, assuming preferences u = u(xi, gi, G) where people care about both the level of the public good and their own contribution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p308]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that 89% of US households give to charity, with even nearly half of households below $10,000 income giving, cannot be explained by Pure Altruism alone and requires additional motives like warm-glow.
- Inference: Government expenditures crowd out some private giving but substantially less than dollar-for-dollar, inconsistent with Pure Altruism predictions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact weighting of pure versus warm-glow elements in explaining charitable giving varies across studies.
