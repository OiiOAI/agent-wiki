# Misattribution (Memory)

## Summary

Misattribution is the memory sin where people remember events that never happened, misattribute memories to the wrong source, or credit spontaneous thoughts to their own imagination. It includes déjà vu, source memory failures, memory conjunction errors, and cryptomnesia. The chapter discusses how binding failures and faulty retrieval monitoring contribute to misattributions, with serious implications for eyewitness testimony and legal proceedings.

## Key facts

- Misattribution involves three types: remembering events that never happened, correctly remembering what happened but misattributing it to the wrong time or place, and crediting a spontaneous image to imagination when it was actually recalled from something previously experienced. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p127]
- Mistaken eyewitness identifications have been involved in 69 percent of approximately 375 wrongful convictions overturned by DNA evidence according to the Innocence Project in 2021. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p128]
- Source misattributions occur when people correctly recall a fact or recognize a person but misattribute the source of their knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p129]
- Memory binding failures contribute to source confusions when individual parts of an experience are retained but not properly linked together. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p130]
- Memory conjunction errors occur when people remember correctly pieces of information but mistakenly combine them into new configurations, such as remembering 'Spanish' after seeing 'spaniel' and 'varnish'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p132]
- The hippocampus plays an important role in binding processes; patients with hippocampal damage are especially likely to make memory conjunction errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p132]
