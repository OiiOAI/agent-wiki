# Green-Beard Effect

## Summary

The green-beard effect is a hypothetical and then discovered mechanism where organisms with a conspicuous trait (like a green beard) cooperate preferentially with others sharing that trait. Proposed by Hamilton, it requires a gene that codes for growing the trait, recognizing it in others, and cooperating with those who have it. Found in yeast and humans, it represents a simplified form of kin selection based on a single locus rather than genome-wide relatedness.

## Key facts

- Hamilton speculated that cooperation could occur based on sharing a conspicuous trait rather than genome-wide relatedness. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p355]
- Green-beard genes exist in yeast, where cells form cooperative aggregates with cells expressing the same cell-surface adhesion protein. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p355]
- In humans, minimal group paradigms show that arbitrary traits can function as green-beard effects, producing cooperation among those sharing meaningless markers.
