# Cortical Minicolumns

## Summary

Vertically organized columns of neurons that cross the six layers of the cerebral cortex and function as fundamental processing units. In humans, particularly in the planum temporale, these minicolumns show unique features including larger size and wider spacing between columns in the left hemisphere compared to other primates.

## Key facts

- Neuronal columns are widely accepted as the fundamental processing unit within the cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p33]
- The average relative thickness of the supragranular layer is 19% in rodents but 46% in primates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p34]
- The increased space between minicolumns in the left human planum temporale results in fewer minicolumns being interconnected, suggesting a more elaborate and less redundant local processing architecture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p36]
