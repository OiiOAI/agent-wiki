# Reason vs. Rationality (Nous vs. Logos)

## Summary

McGilchrist distinguishes between reason (Greek nous, Latin intellectus, German Vernunft) and rationality (Greek logos/dianoia, Latin ratio, German Verstand). Reason is flexible, shaped by experience, involves the whole being, and is congenial to the right hemisphere. Rationality is more rigid, rarified, mechanical, governed by explicit laws—congenial to the left hemisphere. Reason was traditionally considered the higher faculty because rationality cannot ground itself; its value must be intuited, leading to an infinite regress unless founded on reason.

## Key facts

- Reason (nous) is characterized by intuition and grasps first principles through induction, while rationality (logos) is based on argumentation and proof. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p456]
- Rationality cannot constitute—cannot ground—itself according to its own principles; all rationality can do is provide internal consistency once the system is up and running. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p456]
- Kant's formulation A (reason) B (rationality) A (reason) mirrors the cooperative process between hemispheres: right hemisphere delivers to left, left unfolds and returns to right in enhanced form. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p457]
- Lakoff and Johnson argue that reason is evolutionary, not purely conscious, largely metaphorical and imaginative, and emotionally engaged—shaped by the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p460]

## Inferences

- Inference: The traditional primacy of reason (right hemisphere) over rationality (left hemisphere) suggests the right hemisphere is evolutionarily and functionally prior.
