# Managerial Leverage

## Summary

Managerial leverage is a core concept in High Output Management that measures the impact of what managers do to increase the output of their teams. High managerial productivity depends on choosing to perform tasks that possess high leverage. The concept is expressed as: Managerial Output = Sum of (Leverage × Activity) for all activities performed.

## Key facts

- A manager's output equals the output of his organization plus the output of neighboring organizations under his influence. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L424-L425]
- Managerial productivity can be increased in three ways: by speeding up activities, by increasing the leverage of activities, and by shifting the mix from lower to higher leverage activities. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L498-L500]
- High-leverage activities occur when many people are affected by one manager, when brief actions affect behavior over a long period, or when a unique piece of knowledge affects a large group. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L504-L507]
- Managerial leverage can be negative, such as when a manager arrives unprepared to a meeting or spreads depression throughout an organization. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L525-L530]
- Meddling is an example of negative leverage where a supervisor assumes control rather than letting subordinates work through problems, reducing initiative over time. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L547-L552]

## Inferences

- Inference: The leverage concept transforms how managers should evaluate their daily activities, prioritizing those with multiplicative rather than additive effects.
- Inference: Understanding leverage helps managers distinguish between being busy and being productive.
