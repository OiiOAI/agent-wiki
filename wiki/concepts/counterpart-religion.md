# Counterpart Religion

## Summary

Understanding a counterpart's 'religion' means gaining a holistic understanding of their worldview—what matters to them spiritually, culturally, or professionally. This concept involves moving beyond the negotiating table into the life and emotional reality of the counterpart. Once understood, you can position demands within their worldview, showing respect that gets attention and results. The Dwight Watson tractor protest case (2003) demonstrates how identifying Watson's Christian worldview (the 'Dawn of the Third Day') led to resolution.

## Key facts

- Digging into your counterpart's 'religion' implies moving beyond the negotiating table into the life, emotional and otherwise, of your counterpart where Black Swans often dwell. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p197]
- In March 2003, Voss used knowledge of Dwight Watson's Christian beliefs about 'the Dawn of the Third Day' to resolve a 48-hour standoff where Watson had threatened to blow up the Washington National Mall. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p199]
- The similarity principle states that people trust those viewed as similar or familiar; triggering the instinct of shared worldview gives immediate influence. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p200]
- Research shows people respond favorably to requests made with a reason, regardless of whether the reason makes logical sense. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p202]
