# Phrenology

## Summary

A discipline concerned with determining personality and character based on the detailed shape of the skull, developed by Franz Joseph Gall. While Gall's localization of mental functions to specific brain regions was prescient, his methodology—correlating skull bumps with mental faculties rather than examining brain lesions—was scientifically naive. Phrenology was rejected by Pierre Flourens but eventually influenced modern neuropsychology.

## Key facts

- Phrenology was concerned with determining personality and character based on the detailed shape of the skull. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- Gall assumed that the size of an area of brain was related to the mental faculty represented in that area, and that exercise of a mental faculty would cause corresponding brain region growth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- Gall first had the idea as a young boy when he noticed classmates who excelled at memorizing had prominent eyes, which he attributed to overdevelopment of brain regions for verbal memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- Broca wrote in 1861 that if there were ever a phrenological science, it would be the phrenology of convolutions (in the cortex), not the phrenology of bumps (on the head). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- Franz Joseph Gall suggested the brain can be divided into separate 'organs' or regions, with 19 regions responsible for faculties shared with other animals and 8 specific to humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p3]
- Phrenology is ridiculed as pseudoscience because bumps on the skull have very little to do with the shape and regionalization of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p4]
- The author compares modern cognitive neuroscience's search for brain regions corresponding to psychological terms to phrenology, calling this practice 'neo-phrenology'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p7]
- Gall represented to neuroscience what Jean-Baptiste Lamarck represented to evolution - a reminder that being wrong does not mean being useless in science. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p4]
