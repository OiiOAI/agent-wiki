# Distributed Processing

## Summary

Distributed processing is the principle that complex cognitive functions result from interactions among multiple specialized brain regions. Rather than single points, behaviors emerge from interconnected nodes with distinctive functions that work together.

## Key facts

- Wernicke proposed that complex cognitive functions result from interconnections between several functional sites, integrating localized function within a connectionist framework. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p17]
- All cognitive abilities result from interaction of many processing mechanisms distributed in several brain regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p21]
- Damage to a single pathway within a network may affect information carried by that pathway without disrupting the entire system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p21]
