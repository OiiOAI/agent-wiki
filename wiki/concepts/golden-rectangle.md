# Golden Rectangle

## Summary

The golden rectangle is a mathematical concept in which the ratio of the longer side to the shorter side equals approximately 1.62 (the golden ratio, φ). Artists and architects have used this proportion for millennia, believing it produces aesthetically pleasing compositions. The golden rectangle is formed by adding a rectangle to a square such that the outer rectangle maintains the same proportions as the inner rectangle.

## Key facts

- The golden rectangle has a width to height ratio of approximately 1.62. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p301]
- The golden rectangle is formed when a square is added to a rectangle such that the ratio of the outer rectangle equals the ratio of the inner rectangle. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p301]
- The golden rectangle's aspect ratio is close to that of a 5:3 rectangle, differing by only 3%. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p303]
