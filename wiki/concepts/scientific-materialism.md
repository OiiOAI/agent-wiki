# Scientific Materialism

## Summary

Scientific materialism, or positivism, is the philosophical movement that emerged in the mid-19th century holding that science is the only foundation for knowing and understanding the world. McGilchrist terms this the 'Second Reformation'—a shift paralleling the 16th-century Reformation in its movement away from the capacity to understand metaphor and incarnation toward literalistic thinking. It emerged from the French Enlightenment through Auguste Comte and was advanced by Feuerbach, Vogt, and Büchner.

## Key facts

- Scientific materialism holds that reality was what science could deal with, and only that was real. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p526]
- Comte asserted that science was not only our sole source of genuine knowledge about the world, but that it was the only way to understand humanity's place in the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p525]
- Karl Vogt proclaimed that thought, the secretion of the brain, could be changed like other bodily secretions by diet. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p526]
- Lyon Playfair declared that 'science is a religion and its philosophers are the priests of nature'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p527]
