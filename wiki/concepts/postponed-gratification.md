# Postponed Gratification

## Summary

Research by Nicole Mead and Vanessa Patrick found that telling yourself 'not now, but later' is more effective for self-control than flat denial or immediate indulgence. People who postponed dessert cravings were less troubled by intrusive thoughts about food and ate significantly less candy than those who told themselves they could never have it. The postponed pleasure partially satisfies the craving, and the anticipation of future enjoyment allows time to savor it, reducing the need to binge. This 'postponed pleasure' strategy outperforms both absolute denial and immediate consumption.

## Key facts

- People who told themselves 'not now, but later' were less troubled by visions of chocolate cake than those who either ate it or flatly denied themselves. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p215]
- People in the postponement condition ate significantly less M&M's left in front of them than those in the self-denial condition who had refused candy. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p215]
- Postponing pleasure allows time to enjoy anticipation, resulting in less need to binge and more inclination to eat moderately when indulgence finally occurs. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p216]
