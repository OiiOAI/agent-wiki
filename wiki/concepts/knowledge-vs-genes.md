# Knowledge vs. Genes

## Summary

Hawkins presents a fundamental choice: should humanity's future be driven by natural selection and selfish gene propagation, or by intelligence and the desire to understand the world? He argues that knowledge has direction and end goals—unlike genes which simply replicate without direction—making knowledge preservation more worthy as an objective.

## Key facts

- Genes are just molecules that replicate without heading in any particular direction, and no gene is intrinsically better than another. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p221]
- Knowledge has direction: Newton created a theory of gravity, Einstein created a better theory, and we will never return to having no theory of gravity. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p221]
- Knowledge also has an end goal: questions like 'how big is the Earth?' had answers that were eventually achieved, just as current questions about the universe may eventually be answered. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p222]
- A future driven by genes has little direction and only short-term goals of staying healthy, having children, and enjoying life. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p222]
- Humans don't have to choose one future over the other; we can live on Earth while simultaneously dedicating resources to ensuring knowledge preservation. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p222]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hawkins envisions intelligent machines as 'progeny based on intelligence' that could spread knowledge throughout the galaxy while genes remain tied to biological reproduction.
