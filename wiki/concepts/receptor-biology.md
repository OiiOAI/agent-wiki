# Receptor (Biology)

## Summary

Receptors are proteins with binding sites complementary in shape to their signaling molecules (neurotransmitters or hormones). The binding of a ligand triggers conformational changes that open channels or activate cellular responses, described as 'key-in-a-lock' matching.

## Key facts

- Receptors have distinctive binding pockets complementary to the shape of their specific neurotransmitter or hormone. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p705]
- Neurotransmitters fit into receptors 'like a key into a lock' with high specificity—no other molecule fits snugly. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p705]
- Receptor avidity can change, altering how snugly the hormone fits and thus the duration of effects; receptor numbers in target cells can also change, altering sensitivity. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p726]
