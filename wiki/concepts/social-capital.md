# Social Capital

## Summary

Social capital refers to the networks, norms, and trust that enable collective action within societies. Fukuyama discusses its role in economic development and civic life, drawing on Putnam's research documenting its decline in America.

## Key facts

- Putnam's research documented the decline of civic engagement and social capital in American society. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p448]
- The erosion of social capital has been accompanied by changes in American rights discourse, according to Glendon (1991). [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p449]
- Fukuyama argues that market freedom can erode certain social goods that depend on community and trust. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p449]

## Inferences

- Inference: Social capital is presented by Fukuyama as a crucial factor in economic prosperity and democratic stability, contrasting with purely materialist explanations of development

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The causal relationship between social capital decline and other social problems remains contested
