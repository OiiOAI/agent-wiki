# Masturbation in Psychoanalysis

## Summary

Based on discussions at the Vienna Psychoanalytic Society (1912), this section summarizes Freud's views on masturbation across the lifespan: (1) infantile masturbation including all auto-erotic activities, (2) childhood masturbation fixed to erotogenic zones, and (3) masturbation at puberty. Masturbation corresponds to infantile sexual activity retained at mature age and can be pathogenic through organic injury, laying down psychical patterns avoiding alteration of external world, or fixing infantile sexual aims and psychical infantilism. However, it also renders severe perverse inclinations harmless and averts worst consequences of abstinence.

## Key facts

- Masturbation is divided according to age into: (1) infantile including all auto-erotic activities, (2) childhood fixed to erotogenic zones, (3) at puberty either continuous with childhood or separated by latency. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3954]
- The sense of guilt attached to masturbation is a point of agreement among the discussants, though its origin remains debated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3954]
- Masturbation can cause damage in three ways: through organic injury by unknown mechanism; through laying down a psychical pattern avoiding alteration of external world; through fixation of infantile sexual aims and persistence of psychical infantilism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3959]
- Masturbation makes possible the creation of sexual developments and sublimations in phantasy which are not advances but injurious compromises, though it renders severe perverse inclinations harmless. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3960]
- Masturbation represents a therapeutic return: during treatment, a patient venturing to take up masturbation again may represent a great advance, even if not intended as permanent stop. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3961]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud defends his position against Stekel that to deny pathogenic effects of masturbation contradicts fundamental views on neurosis aetiology.
- Inference: Female masturbation deserves special study with emphasis on modifications arising in relation to age, particularly its originally clitoridal nature.
