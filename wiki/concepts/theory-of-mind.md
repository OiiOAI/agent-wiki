# Theory of Mind

## Summary

Theory of mind refers to the ability to understand that other people have mental states that differ from one's own, including beliefs, desires, and intentions. This 'mentalizing' ability is thought to depend on specific brain mechanisms and circuits, and its impairment, termed 'mind blindness,' is hypothesized to underlie social deficits in autism spectrum disorder. The mentalizing system involves medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal junction, amygdala, and inferior temporal cortex.

## Key facts

- Theory of mind is the ability to understand the mental states of others, including their beliefs, intentions, and knowledge, based on observation of their behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- The inability to mentalize, termed 'mind blindness,' was first tested in children with autism using the Sally-Anne test. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- The mentalizing system involves four brain regions: medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal region, amygdala, and inferior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1527]
- Children with ASD often fail the Sally-Anne test, unable to predict where a puppet will look for an object that was moved while the puppet was out of the room. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- Theory of Mind was coined by David Premack and Guy Woodruff in 1978 to describe the ability to infer mental states from behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p57]
- Theory of Mind is typically fully developed in children by age 4-5 and appears to be independent from IQ. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p57]
- Individuals with autism have deficits in theory of mind and are impaired in reasoning about mental states of others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p57]
- Humans can track multiple orders of intensionality (knowing that someone knows that someone else knows something). [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Sally and Ann false-belief task, which children pass around age 4-5 but chimpanzees fail, suggests inhibition may be the limiting factor rather than conceptual understanding
- Inference: Povinelli argues that chimpanzee mental states are primarily about ecological concerns (food locations, predators, social rank) rather than abstract reasoning about others' beliefs

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether chimpanzees possess a theory of mind remains contested; Tomasello's group believes they understand some psychological processes while Heyes and Povinelli argue the evidence is inconclusive
