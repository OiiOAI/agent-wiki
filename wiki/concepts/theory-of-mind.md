# Theory of Mind

## Summary

Theory of mind refers to the ability to understand that other people have mental states that differ from one's own, including beliefs, desires, and intentions. This 'mentalizing' ability is thought to depend on specific brain mechanisms and circuits, and its impairment, termed 'mind blindness,' is hypothesized to underlie social deficits in autism spectrum disorder. The mentalizing system involves medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal junction, amygdala, and inferior temporal cortex.

## Key facts

- Theory of mind is the ability to understand the mental states of others, including their beliefs, intentions, and knowledge, based on observation of their behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- The inability to mentalize, termed 'mind blindness,' was first tested in children with autism using the Sally-Anne test. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- The mentalizing system involves four brain regions: medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal region, amygdala, and inferior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1527]
- Children with ASD often fail the Sally-Anne test, unable to predict where a puppet will look for an object that was moved while the puppet was out of the room. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- Theory of Mind was coined by David Premack and Guy Woodruff in 1978 to describe the ability to infer mental states from behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p57]
- Theory of Mind is typically fully developed in children by age 4-5 and appears to be independent from IQ. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p57]
- Individuals with autism have deficits in theory of mind and are impaired in reasoning about mental states of others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p57]
- Humans can track multiple orders of intensionality (knowing that someone knows that someone else knows something). [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p58]
- Theory of mind is the ability to understand other people's beliefs, intentions, desires, and mental states without necessarily sharing their emotional states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p254]
- ToM develops during childhood: children age 4 and older start correctly attributing false beliefs to others, and by age 5 over 90% of children understand false belief tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p255]
- The neural network for theory of mind includes posterior superior temporal sulcus extending into temporoparietal junction, medial prefrontal cortex, and sometimes temporal poles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p255]
- Autistic spectrum disorders show severely delayed mentalizing abilities, which may explain deficits in communication and social interaction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p255]
- People with ASD and Asperger syndrome have often been found to have difficulties in attributing mental states to others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]
- The proposed double dissociation suggests patients with autism may lack mentalizing abilities but may not be deficient in sharing others' feelings, while patients with psychopathy lack empathy but are unimpaired in understanding others' thoughts and beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]
- Empathic motivation may be a better predictor of engagement in other-regarding behavior than cognitive perspective-taking, because empathy engages motivational and emotional brain circuitries while theory of mind relies on networks typically less relevant for motivation and emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p254]
- Psychopaths may lack empathy but not cognitive perspective-taking, explaining why they can engage in antisocial behavior while being very good at manipulating others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p254]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Sally and Ann false-belief task, which children pass around age 4-5 but chimpanzees fail, suggests inhibition may be the limiting factor rather than conceptual understanding
- Inference: Povinelli argues that chimpanzee mental states are primarily about ecological concerns (food locations, predators, social rank) rather than abstract reasoning about others' beliefs
- Inference: Theory of mind and empathy appear to rely on distinct neural circuitries, as evidenced by their different impairment patterns in clinical populations (autism affects ToM but not necessarily empathy; psychopathy affects empathy but not ToM)
- Inference: The medial prefrontal cortex may represent mental states decoupled from reality, while the superior temporal sulcus processes causal relationships between visual motion/action and intended goals
- Inference: The distinction between 'cold' cognitive mentalizing and 'hot' empathic understanding may represent two distinct roots to understanding other people's minds.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether chimpanzees possess a theory of mind remains contested; Tomasello's group believes they understand some psychological processes while Heyes and Povinelli argue the evidence is inconclusive
- Uncertain: No fMRI study has yet demonstrated a clear dissociation between mentalizing and empathizing deficits in psychopathy and autism as of this research.
