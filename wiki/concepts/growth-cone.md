# Growth Cone

## Summary

The growth cone is a specialized sensory-motor structure at the tip of extending axons and dendrites. Discovered by Santiago Ramon y Cajal, it contains filopodia (actin-rich sensory tentacles), lamellipodia (motile veils), and a central core rich in microtubules and organelles. Growth cones sense guidance cues through receptors on filopodia and transduce them into cytoskeletal rearrangements that drive extension, turning, or retraction. Calcium gradients and cyclic nucleotides (cAMP, cGMP) are key second messengers linking receptor activation to cytoskeletal responses.

## Key facts

- The growth cone has three compartments: central core (microtubules, organelles), lamellipodia (motile veils), and filopodia (actin-rich sensory tentacles). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1265]
- Filopodia sense environmental cues through surface receptors, enabling growth cone navigation around obstacles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1265]
- Growth cone motility is regulated by calcium concentration within a narrow optimal range (set point), with gradients directing turning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1268]
- The same guidance cue can be attractive or repulsive depending on intracellular cAMP levels, demonstrating second messenger modulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1268]
