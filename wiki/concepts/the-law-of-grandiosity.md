# The Law of Grandiosity

## Summary

The Law of Grandiosity describes how humans have a deep need to think highly of themselves, and when that self-opinion diverges enough from reality, they become grandiose. Success tends to elevate natural grandiosity to dangerous levels, causing people to forget the role of luck and the contributions of others, imagine they have the golden touch, and make irrational decisions. The antidote involves maintaining realistic self-assessment, analyzing success to acknowledge luck and others' contributions, and tying feelings of greatness to actual work and achievements rather than ego gratification.

## Key facts

- We humans have a deep need to think highly of ourselves, and if that opinion diverges enough from reality, we become grandiose. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p358]
- Often a small measure of success will elevate our natural grandiosity to even more dangerous levels, confirming our high self-opinion and causing us to forget the role luck may have played. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p358]
- Success has an irresistible pull that tends to cloud our minds, making us more attuned to ego and fantasies than to the people we work for and our audience. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p371]
- Dostoyevsky wrote in Crime and Punishment: 'Existence alone had never been enough for him; he had always wanted more. Perhaps it was only from the force of his desires that he had regarded himself as a man to whom more was permitted than to others.'.
