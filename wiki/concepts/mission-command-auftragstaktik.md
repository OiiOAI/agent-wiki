# Mission Command (Auftragstaktik)

## Summary

Boyd developed a command philosophy derived from Blitzkrieg and Auftragstaktik that emphasizes trust, implicit communication, and lateral relations over top-down hierarchy. This approach ensures that all levels within a decentralized organization can adapt to their environments while heading in a common direction.

## Key facts

- Auftragstaktik builds on and requires trust among and across all levels, along with initiative, flexibility and freedom of action for lower levels. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p288]
- Boyd advocates lateral relations and avoiding a top-down hierarchical system; command must be top-down, lateral as well as bottom-up with continuous open two-way communication. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p288]
- Trust is described as the glue of Boyd's command system and the basis for social cohesion. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p288]
- Higher commands must shape the 'decision space' of subordinate commanders, connected by a common frame of reference and direction. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]
- Boyd re-invents and reformulates Auftragstaktik as the organic way of ensuring decentralized organizations can adapt while remaining unified. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p289]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's command philosophy is essential for his operational art to succeed because it enables units at all levels to maintain sufficient interaction with their environment while pursuing common goals.
