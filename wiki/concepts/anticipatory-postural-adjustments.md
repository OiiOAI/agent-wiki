# Anticipatory Postural Adjustments

## Summary

Anticipatory postural adjustments are proactive muscular activations that precede voluntary movements to maintain stability. The nervous system uses advance knowledge of the effects of voluntary movements on postural alignment to activate postural muscles before the primary movement. These adjustments are specific to biomechanical conditions and task requirements, and are absent when external support eliminates the need for postural stabilization.

## Key facts

- Anticipatory postural adjustments precede voluntary movements and are activated by the nervous system's advance knowledge of how movement will affect postural stability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p892]
- When a freely standing subject rapidly pulls on a handle, leg muscles (gastrocnemius and hamstrings) are activated before arm muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p892]
- Anticipatory postural adjustments are absent when external support (such as a rigid bar at the shoulders) prevents the body from rotating forward during arm pull. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p893]
- The amplitude and lead time of anticipatory postural adjustments scale with the force of the planned voluntary movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p892]
- During walking, the center of mass is shifted forward and laterally by unweighting one leg before the stepping program initiates, enabling forward progression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p892]

## Inferences

- Inference: Anticipatory postural adjustments require internal models that predict the mechanical consequences of planned voluntary movements on body stability.
