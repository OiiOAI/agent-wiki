# Out-of-the-Loop Syndrome

## Summary

Out-of-the-loop syndrome occurs when automation undermines situation awareness by taking people out of the loop. In this state, people develop poor SA on both how the automation is performing and the state of the elements the automation is supposed to be controlling. When automation fails or reaches conditions it is not equipped to handle, the person is unable to detect the problem, properly interpret the information presented, and intervene in a timely manner.

## Key facts

- Out-of-the-loop syndrome occurs when automation takes people out of the loop, leading to poor SA on both automation performance and the state of controlled elements. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p60]
- When automation is performing well, being out-of-the-loop may not be a problem, but when automation fails the person is often unable to detect and respond in time. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p60]
- The 1987 Detroit aircraft crash on takeoff occurred partly because pilots were unaware the automated takeoff configuration warning system had failed. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p60]
- A key premise of user-centered design is that while a person does not need to perform every task, they do need to be in control of managing what systems are doing. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p31]
- The out-of-the-loop syndrome results from three primary mechanisms: reduced vigilance and complacency, passive rather than active information processing, and poor system feedback quality. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p193]
- In one study, 81% of reported problems in cruise automation were associated with not monitoring the system. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p193]
- Moray argues that people employ attention close to optimally based on utility likelihood, missing automation problems not due to over-trust but due to effective sampling strategies for usually reliable sources. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p195]
- Endsley and Kiris found much lower understanding (Level 2 SA) when people performed tasks with automated aids, even though their Level 1 SA was not affected. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p196]
- Out-of-the-loop syndrome occurs when the operator suffers from reduced awareness of the state of automation and the system parameters it is controlling. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p314]
- Contributing factors to out-of-the-loop syndrome include vigilance, complacency, monitoring, passive processing, and inadequate system feedback. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p314]
- Out-of-the-loop syndrome can be completely avoided if automation is not used (Principle 34). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p314]
- SA-oriented design seeks to maintain better synergy between automation and human operators than traditional approaches have provided. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p314]
- Automation should be employed to support the operator, rather than automating to the fullest extent possible and assigning leftover tasks to humans. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p314]
- Automation should assist operators in carrying out routine actions rather than trying to perform higher-level cognitive tasks (Principle 35). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p315]
- Automation should support SA rather than provide decisions (Principles 36 and 43). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p315]
- Automation should allow the operator to be in control and in the loop (Principle 37). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p315]
- Helping operators understand what the automation is doing, how it is doing it, and what it will do next reduces out-of-the-loop performance problems (Principle 44). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p315]
- SA-oriented design recommends systems be designed to bring operators up to speed quickly in case of SA loss. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p315]
