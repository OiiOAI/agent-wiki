# Drift to Low Performance

## Summary

Drift to low performance (or eroding goals) occurs when performance standards are allowed to be influenced by past performance, particularly when there is a negative bias in perceiving past performance. This creates a reinforcing feedback loop: lower perceived performance leads to lower goals, which reduces corrective action, which lowers actual performance further. The antidote is to keep standards absolute and to set goals based on the best performances rather than the worst.

## Key facts

- The actor tends to believe bad news more than good news—best results are dismissed as aberrations while worst results stay in memory. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p151]
- The desired state is influenced by the perceived state: 'Well, that's about all you can expect'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p152]
- Also called the 'boiled frog syndrome'—a frog in gradually heated water won't jump out, but a frog in hot water immediately will. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p152]
- The same structure can create 'drift toward high performance' if standards are enhanced by best performances instead of discouraged by worst. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p153]
- Allowing performance standards to be influenced by past performance, especially if there is negative bias in perceiving past performance, sets up a reinforcing feedback loop of eroding goals. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p223]
- The remedy is to keep performance standards absolute, or better yet, let standards be enhanced by the best actual performances instead of being discouraged by the worst. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p223]
- The goal of morality has been eroded in modern industrial culture through the mechanism of this archetype, where examples of bad behavior are magnified while good behavior goes unnoticed. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p216]
