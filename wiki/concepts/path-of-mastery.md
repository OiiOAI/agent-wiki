# The Path of Mastery

## Summary

The Path of Mastery is one of the Three Commitments to the ONE Thing, representing a commitment to becoming one's best through deliberate practice. Based on Ericsson's research, mastery requires approximately 10,000 hours of deliberate practice, equivalent to about four hours daily over ten years. The book emphasizes that mastery is a journey rather than a destination, and that one becomes a master of what is known and an apprentice for what lies ahead. The path creates a platform for learning other skills and makes future dominoes fall more easily.

## Key facts

- Mastery means giving the best you have to become the best you can be at your most important work. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p142]
- Ericsson's 1993 research identified that elite violinists accumulated more than 10,000 hours of practice by age 20. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p143]
- Elite performers complete their mastery journey in about ten years, averaging three hours of deliberate practice daily. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p143]
- The 10,000 hours translates to four hours daily over 250 workdays per year. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p144]
- Michelangelo said that if people knew how hard he worked to gain his mastery, it wouldn't seem wonderful. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p144]
- Mastery is described as a path one goes down, not a destination one arrives at. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p142]
- Jigoro Kano, founder of judo, reportedly asked to be buried in his white belt, symbolizing lifelong apprenticeship. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p178]
