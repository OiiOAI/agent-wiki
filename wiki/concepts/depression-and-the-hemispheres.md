# Depression and the hemispheres

## Summary

Depression is associated with reduced right posterior activity in addition to increased right frontal activity in most cases. Greater right than left activation is related to depressed mood. Increased electrical activity in the right-prefrontal region is a marker of depression. Depression resulting from damage to the right hemisphere has more of indifference or apathy, in contrast to the anxious, disturbed depression with biological features resulting from lesions to the left hemisphere.

## Key facts

- Depression per se is probably associated with reduced right posterior activity in addition to increased right frontal activity in most cases. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p98]
- Greater right than left activation is related to depressed mood; specifically increased electrical activity in the right-prefrontal region is a marker of depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p98]
- Lesion studies confirm the relationship between activity of the right frontal lobe and depression, and neuroimaging studies suggest a correlation between left frontal hypofunction and depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p98]
- Depression resulting from damage to the right hemisphere has more of indifference or apathy—a global, vague lifelessness—in contrast to the anxious, disturbed depression, accompanied by biological features, resulting from lesions to the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p99]
- Those with right-hemisphere lesions tend to deny or minimise their condition (anosognosia), while those with left-hemisphere lesions are more likely to become depressed about their condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p98]
- The right hemisphere's tendency toward melancholy can be seen as related to being more in touch with what's going on and more in touch with, and concerned for, others; sadness and empathy are highly correlated. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p128]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation between depression and right hemisphere activity suggests that realism about one's condition—including its limitations and losses—may come at an emotional cost.
