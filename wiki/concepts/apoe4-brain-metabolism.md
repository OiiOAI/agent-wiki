# ApoE4 and Brain Glucose Metabolism

## Summary

The ApoE4 allele, the most well-defined Alzheimer's risk gene carried by over a quarter of the population, is associated with reduced glucose metabolism in the brain starting as early as the twenties and thirties. Carriers have a two- to twelvefold increased risk of developing Alzheimer's depending on whether they have one or two copies. Interestingly, noncarriers who develop Alzheimer's also display the same reduced glucose metabolism, suggesting impaired brain-glucose metabolism as a possible causative factor.

## Key facts

- Carriers of the ApoE4 allele have been demonstrated to exhibit low glucose metabolism in the brain, seemingly across the age spectrum, starting as early as the twenties and thirties. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p207]
- Carriers of the ApoE4 allele have either a two- or twelvefold increased risk of developing Alzheimer's depending on whether they've inherited one or two copies. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p207]
- Noncarriers who develop Alzheimer's disease ultimately display the same reduced glucose metabolism in their brains as ApoE4 carriers, implicating impaired brain-glucose metabolism as a possible causative factor. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p207]
- By the time a person is diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease, brain glucose metabolism is already reduced by 45 percent compared to healthy people. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p209]
- Insulin resistance in the body predicted diminished glucose metabolism in the brains of cognitively normal adults. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p209]
