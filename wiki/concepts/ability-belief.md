# ability belief

## Summary

Ability belief is a person's conviction that they can successfully accomplish a public-figure attack. De Becker identifies it as the preeminent pre-incident indicator: without this belief, an attack cannot occur.

## Key facts

- De Becker states: 'If we had to choose just one PIN we'd want to be aware of above all others, it would be the one we call ability belief. This is a person's belief that he can accomplish a public-figure attack. Without it, he cannot.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p164]
- The author argues that media attention on attackers 'says, You see; it can be done,' thereby bolstering ability belief in potential future attackers. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p165]
- De Becker notes that attacks cluster temporally: President Ford was attacked twice within two weeks; President Clinton was attacked twice within six weeks. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p165]
- The author proposes that law enforcement describe arrested attackers in 'less glamorous light' to avoid promoting ability belief: 'We found him hiding in the bathroom—in the clothes hamper. He's never succeeded at anything else.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p166]
