# Relative subjective value (RSV)

## Summary

Relative subjective value (RSV) is SV normalized to the limited dynamic range of neurons in the lateral intraparietal area (LIP). This normalization is accomplished through divisive normalization mechanisms similar to those observed in other cortical areas. RSV represents the instantaneous value of each movement in the saccadic repertoire, linearly proportional to RSV for positively-valued movements.

## Key facts

- Activity in LIP encodes relative subjective value (RSV) under many conditions, linearly related to reward probability and magnitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- RSV is derived from SV through divisive normalization in posterior parietal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- The shift to RSV guarantees distribution of SVs of the current choice-set over the limited dynamic range of LIP neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]

## Inferences

- Inference: The need for RSV (normalization) arises because LIP neurons are limited to approximately 100 Hz dynamic range with Poisson-like error distributions, which would otherwise make it difficult to represent SVs of varying magnitudes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How the normalization process specifically operates at the neural circuit level remains to be fully characterized.
