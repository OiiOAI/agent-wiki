# Signs of Envy

## Summary

Greene identifies four key signs that envy is present: (1) Microexpressions—a momentary look of disdain and hostility in the eyes, often followed by a strained fake smile; (2) Poisonous praise—comments that praise in a way that implies negative motivations or mentions something lost; (3) Backbiting—gossip about others with evident enjoyment, implying nobody is really that great; (4) The push and pull—a pattern of intense friendliness followed by pointed criticism, creating confusion and dependency. Single signs may indicate passive envy, but patterns and repetitions indicate more dangerous active envy.

## Key facts

- Microexpressions of envy affect the entire face—the eyes bore momentarily with disdain and hostility, the corners of the mouth turn down, the nose sneers, the chin juts out. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p338]
- Schopenhauer suggested testing for envy by telling suspected enviers good news about yourself—you will notice a quick expression of disappointment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p339]
- Poisonous praise is paradoxical praise that implies negative motivations—for instance, praising money made rather than the work itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p339]
- The push and pull pattern involves unusual eagerness to befriend, followed by pointed criticism, then a return to warmth, trapping the target between affection and pain. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p340]
