# The Royal Road to the Unconscious

## Summary

Freud famously declared 'The interpretation of dreams is the royal road to a knowledge of the unconscious activities of the mind.' Dreams demonstrate that what is suppressed continues to exist in normal people as well as in those with pathology, and remains capable of psychical functioning. By analysing dreams, we can take a step forward in understanding the composition of the mental apparatus, and this beginning enables further analysis based on pathological structures.

## Key facts

- Dreams have proved that what is suppressed continues to exist in normal people as well as abnormal, and remains capable of psychical functioning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1841]
- Dreams show one of the paths leading to an understanding of the structure of the mental apparatus, with its two psychical systems, the censorship, and the relations of both to consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1841]
- The psychical mechanism employed by neuroses is not created by pathological disturbance but is present already in the normal structure of the mental apparatus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1841]
- Illnesses termed 'functional' do not presuppose disintegration of the apparatus or production of fresh splits; they are explained on a dynamic basis by strengthening and weakening of various components in the interplay of forces. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1841]
