# Three Negotiation Styles

## Summary

Voss identifies three broad categories of negotiators based on research consolidated with field experience: Analysts (methodical, data-driven, prefer working alone), Accommodators (relationship-focused, prefer continuous communication), and Assertives (time-is-money mindset, goal-oriented, focus on getting heard). Each style has distinct characteristics, communication preferences, and effective tactical approaches. Most people have a dominant style but can throttle up nondominant styles when needed.

## Key facts

- People fall into three broad negotiation categories: Accommodators, Assertives, and data-loving Analysts. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p169]
- Analysts are methodical and diligent, believe time is of little consequence if working toward the best result, and are hypersensitive to reciprocity. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p170]
- Accommodators prioritize relationship-building and free-flowing information exchange over task completion, often building rapport without accomplishing anything. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p171]
- Assertives believe time is money, focus on accomplishing goals over perfection, and want to be heard before they can listen to others. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p172]
- A study found 65 percent of lawyer-negotiators used cooperative styles while only 24 percent were assertive, and more than 75 percent of the effective group came from the cooperative type. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p169]
- The three types interpret silence differently: Accommodators view silence as anger, Analysts use it as an opportunity to think, and Assertives interpret silence as having nothing to say. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since there are three negotiation types and most people default to projecting their own style, there's a 66 percent chance your counterpart has a different style than yours (the 'I am normal' paradox).
- Inference: The Ackerman model works across all three styles because it incorporates reciprocity, extreme anchors, and loss aversion—tactics that resonate differently with each type.
