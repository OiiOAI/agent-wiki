# Femininity

## Summary

In Lecture XXXIII of the New Introductory Lectures, Freud presents his views on the psychical peculiarities of mature femininity, arguing that female development is complicated by a pre-Oedipus attachment to the mother, bisexuality, and the traumatic discovery of penile absence. He describes repeated alternation between masculine and feminine phases in women's lives and attributes part of 'the enigma of women' to this bisexuality.

## Key facts

- Freud credits Dr Ruth Mack Brunswick with the first description of a neurosis fixed in the pre-Oedipus stage that never reached the Oedipus situation, taking the form of jealous paranoia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6960]
- Freud describes shame in women as serving primarily to conceal genital deficiency, though he acknowledges it later takes on other functions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6961]
- Freud attributes larger narcissism to femininity, making the need to be loved stronger than the capacity to love. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6961]
- Freud connects penis envy to women's physical vanity as late compensation for original sexual inferiority. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6961]
- Freud observes that women's object choice often follows the paternal type when the girl has remained in attachment to her father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6962]
- Freud states that the pre-Oedipus affectionate attachment to the mother is the decisive phase for a woman's future, during which preparations are made for acquiring characteristics needed for the sexual function and social tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6963]
- Freud suggests that women's lesser sense of justice is related to the predominance of envy, since demand for justice is a modification of envy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6963]
- Freud notes that women often display psychical rigidity and unchangeability, with their libido having taken up final positions incapable of exchange. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6964]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's observation that psychoanalytic treatment of adult women faces particular difficulties due to psychical rigidity may reflect therapeutic limitations he later elaborated in 'Analysis Terminable and Interminable' (1937).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges uncertainty about what should be attributed to sexual function versus social breeding in explaining feminine characteristics.
