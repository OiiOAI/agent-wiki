# Representational Error

## Summary

A representational error occurs when relevant information in a situation is correctly perceived but misinterpreted to fit an incorrect internal situation representation. This phenomenon causes operators to explain away alarms or cues that should indicate their understanding is wrong, instead forcing new information to conform to their existing mental model. Nearly two-thirds of all cues did not result in revision of representational errors in air traffic control studies.

## Key facts

- Representational errors have been identified in medical diagnosis, system diagnosis, and aviation operations. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p180]
- Jones and Endsley found that despite obvious cues that initial representations were wrong, controllers found ways to fit new information to that representation even when far-fetched rationale were required. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p180]
- In their study, nearly two-thirds of all cues did not result in revision of the representational error. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p180]
- Pilots in numerous aviation accidents explained away GPWS warnings to fit the situation they believed themselves to be in, falsely attributing warnings to sink rate or configuration conditions rather than actual problems. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p180]

## Inferences

- Inference: Representational errors can be very difficult to correct because if cues can be misinterpreted to fit the error, odds are high the error will persist unless the cues provided are unambiguous as to their meaning.
