# Highly Superior Autobiographical Memory (HSAM)

## Summary

HSAM is a condition first described in 2006 in which individuals possess prodigious memory for their personal past experiences. Unlike professional mnemonists who use techniques, HSAM individuals show exceptional autobiographical memory without such methods, though they perform normally on standard laboratory memory tests.

## Key facts

- HSAM was first described in 2006 in Jill Price (AJ), who could recall what she was doing on any date from 1974 onward with extraordinary accuracy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p58]
- HSAM individuals perform normally or below average on standard laboratory tests of memory but show exceptional autobiographical memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p58]
- Structural MRIs revealed differences in volume of nine brain regions between HSAM and control groups, including the parahippocampal gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p59]
- HSAM individuals show increased activity in twice as many brain regions as controls during autobiographical recall, with stronger hippocampus-prefrontal coupling. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p59]
- HSAM individuals are characterized by high levels of obsessive behaviors resembling OCD, including compulsive need to organize environments and rumination about past experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p59]
- Unlike non-HSAM controls, HSAM individuals recalled significantly more details about events from one month, one year, and ten years earlier. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p59]
