# Cytoplasmic Polyadenylation Element Binding Protein (CPEB)

## Summary

CPEB is a regulator of local protein synthesis at synapses that exists in two conformational states: a soluble inactive form and an aggregated active form that is self-perpetuating, resembling prion proteins. This prion-like property allows CPEB to maintain long-term changes at synapses necessary for persistent memory storage.

## Key facts

- Aplysia CPEB has two conformational states: a soluble inactive form and an aggregated active form that recruits monomers to join the aggregate, making it self-perpetuating. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1376]
- Serotonin increases local synthesis of CPEB until a threshold concentration is reached that switches CPEB to the aggregated, active state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1376]
- Active CPEB aggregates bind the cytoplasmic polyadenylation element (CPE) site of mRNAs, recruiting poly(A) polymerase machinery and allowing poly(A) tails to be added to dormant mRNAs for translation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1376]
- Blocking CPEB locally at an activated synapse blocks the long-term maintenance of synaptic facilitation after 24 hours but not its initiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1375]
- The N-terminal domain of CPEB rich in glutamine residues corresponds to the prion-like domain; deletion of this domain in Drosophila eliminates long-term courtship memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1378]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prion-like mechanism of CPEB provides a molecular explanation for how memory can persist for long periods—the active form is self-sustaining and does not require continuous signaling.
