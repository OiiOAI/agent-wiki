# Dream Consciousness

## Summary

Dream consciousness refers to the subjective conscious experiences we have during sleep. Revonsuo reconceptualizes dreaming as the phenomenal level of organization in the brain, forming a real-time model of the world, one's internal state, and external position in the environment. The paper argues that dream consciousness is an organized and selective simulation of the perceptual world, not random noise, and that it is specialized in simulating threatening events from our ancestral past.

## Key facts

- Dreaming is defined as a subjective experience during sleep, consisting of complex and organized images that show temporal progression. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p2]
- Dream consciousness is reconceptualized as the phenomenal level of organization in the brain, which forms the brain's real-time model of the surrounding world, internal state, and external position in the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p6]
- Dream consciousness is characterized as an organized and selective simulation of the perceptual world, constructing a complex offline model where there is typically an active dream self with a body-image similar to waking experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p6-p7]
- During dreaming, we are in an uncritical, delusional state that efficiently prevents us from recognizing the simulation as unreal, which is necessary for the simulations to be taken seriously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The reinterpretation of dreams - An evolutionary hypothesis of the function of dreaming.pdf#p13]

## Inferences

- Inference: The theory distinguishes dream consciousness (subjective experience) from REM sleep (physiological state), arguing that dreaming needs explanation independent of neurophysiological REM sleep mechanisms.
