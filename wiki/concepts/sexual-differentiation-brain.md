# Sexual Differentiation of the Brain

## Summary

Sexual differentiation of the brain refers to the developmental processes that create anatomical and functional differences between male and female neural circuits. The initial genetic trigger is the SRY gene on the Y chromosome, which directs the embryonic gonads to develop into testes. Testes then produce hormones (primarily testosterone and anti-Mullerian hormone) that organize sexual differentiation of both genitalia and the brain. Later, hormones act to activate sex-specific behaviors. The distinction between sex (biological) and gender (social/psychological) is important for understanding human sexual dimorphism.

## Key facts

- The SRY gene on the Y chromosome directs differentiation of the embryonic gonad into testes; inactivation or deletion of SRY leads to complete sex reversal despite XY genotype. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1308]
- Females have two X chromosomes but early in embryogenesis one X chromosome in each cell is randomly inactivated, so both sexes have a single active X chromosome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1308]
- Sex hormones produced by the testes, including testosterone and anti-Mullerian hormone, organize sexual differentiation of both genitalia and the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1309]
- Gender (social/behavioral roles) is distinct from sex (biological characteristics), and includes gender role and gender identity as separate dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1308]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hormonal regulation of sexual differentiation creates feedback loops where the nervous system, which is profoundly influenced by sex steroids, also controls their synthesis through the hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal axis.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The relative contributions of genes and experience to gender identity and gender role behaviors in humans remain highly debated and incompletely understood.
