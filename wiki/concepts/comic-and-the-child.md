# The Comic and the Child

## Summary

Freud's analysis of the relationship between the comic and infantile mental life, proposing that comic pleasure derives from comparing the adult ego with the child's ego, and that children themselves lack a feeling for the comic despite fulfilling conditions that would produce comic effects in adults.

## Key facts

- Children themselves do not strike us as in any way comic, though their nature fulfils all the conditions which, if we compare it with our own nature, yield a comic difference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2848]
- Children produce a comic effect on us when they conduct themselves not as a child but as a serious adult, and they produce it then in the same way as other people who disguise themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2848]
- Children are without a feeling for the comic because they lack the standard contained in the comparison 'This is how I should do it, how I did it'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2849]
- Children laugh from pure pleasure in a variety of circumstances that we feel as 'comic' and cannot find the motive for, whereas a child's motives are clear. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2849]
- The comic feeling may be translated as: 'Those things are comic which are not proper for an adult.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2851]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's complete comparison leading to the comic runs: 'That is how he does it – I do it in another way – he does it as I used to do it as a child.' This suggests comic laughter always applies to comparison between the adult's ego and the child's ego.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud states he is 'unable to decide whether degradation to being a child is only a special case of comic degradation, or whether everything comic is based fundamentally on degradation to being a child.'
