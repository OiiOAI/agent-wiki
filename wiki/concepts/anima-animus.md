# Anima and Animus

## Summary

According to Carl Jung, the anima (for males) and animus (for females) are unconscious contrasexual parts of personality that emerge when we become fascinated with someone of the opposite sex. These entities originate from: (1) hormones and genes of the opposite sex that exist in everyone, and (2) the profound influence of the opposite-sex parent during formative years. When we fall in love, we often project these internal figures onto actual partners—seeking qualities we never developed in ourselves or attempting to recreate or fix early relationships with our parents. This explains why we often act irrationally in love, seeing people not as they are but as our projections. Understanding our anima/animus helps us recognize patterns in relationships and integrate repressed qualities.

## Key facts

- Carl Jung gave the name anima (for males) and animus (for females) to entities that exist in the unconscious but emerge when fascinated by the opposite sex. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p411]
- Anima and animus originate from hormones and genes of the opposite sex plus influence of the opposite-sex parent. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p411]
- The association with the parent of the opposite sex is charged with physical, sensual connections, excitement, fascination, or disappointment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p412]
- If the relationship to the opposite-sex parent was mostly positive, people project desirable qualities that parent had onto partners. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p413]
- If feelings toward the parent were ambivalent, people fall in love with someone resembling that parent in hopes of fixing the original relationship. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p414]
- People project their own anima or animus onto desired women or men, falling in love with projections rather than real people. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p413]
- Hypermasculine men have a very feminine anima lurking below the surface, and hyperfeminine women have a very masculine animus. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p415]
- Richard Nixon, despite projecting a macho image, constantly commented on and fussing over colors of suits and office drapes. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p415]
