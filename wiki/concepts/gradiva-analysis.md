# Delusions and Dreams in Jensen's Gradiva

## Summary

Freud's psychoanalytic interpretation of Wilhelm Jensen's novella 'Gradiva' (1903), in which he analyzes the delusional formation of archaeologist Norbert Hanold, who falls in love with a marble relief and later discovers the real woman (Zoe Bertgang) behind his fantasy. Freud demonstrates how the delusion represents repressed childhood memories and how it is resolved through the woman herself undertaking a form of psychical treatment.

## Key facts

- Norbert Hanold, a young archaeologist, discovers a relief of a girl stepping along with a peculiar gait and becomes obsessed with it, naming her 'Gradiva'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2907]
- Hanold dreams of Pompeii's destruction and sees Gradiva lying down to sleep on the temple steps, then later encounters her as a living woman in Pompeii. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2907]
- Zoe Bertgang reveals herself as Hanold's childhood friend whom he had forgotten due to repression, having turned away from living women toward archaeology. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2925]
- The name 'Gradiva' proves to be a derivative and translation of the repressed surname 'Bertgang' (meaning 'one who steps along brilliantly'). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2929]
- Freud draws an analogy between the burial and excavation of Pompeii and the mental processes of repression and analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2931]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud notes that Jensen himself may not have consciously intended the psychoanalytic meaning Freud derives from the text.
