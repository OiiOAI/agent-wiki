# Nonperceptual Awareness

## Summary

Gibson proposes a taxonomy of nonperceptual awareness including: (1) remembering—an awareness of surfaces that have ceased to exist or events that will not recur; (2) expecting, anticipating, planning, or creatively imagining—awareness of surfaces that do not exist or events that do not occur but could arise within the limits of possibility; (3) day-dreaming, dreaming, or wishfully/fearfully imagining—awareness of surfaces or events that do not exist and are outside the limits of possibility. He suggests these are explained by a perceptual system that has become sensitized to certain invariants and can operate without the constraints of the stimulus flux.

## Key facts

- Reminiscence, expectation, imagination, fantasy, and dreaming actually occur, but they do not have an essential role to play in perceiving. They are kinds of visual awareness other than perceptual. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p275]
- To perceive is to be aware of the surfaces of the environment and of oneself in it. The full awareness of surfaces includes their layout, their substances, their events, and their affordances. This definition includes within perception a part of memory, expectation, knowledge, and meaning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p275]
- A perceptual system that has become sensitized to certain invariants and can extract them from the stimulus flux can also operate without the constraints of the stimulus flux. The adjustment loops for looking around, looking at, scanning, and focusing are then inoperative. The visual system visualizes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p275]
