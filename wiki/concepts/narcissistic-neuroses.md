# Narcissistic Neuroses

## Summary

Narcissistic neuroses are disorders (including dementia praecox, paranoia, and melancholia) characterized by the patient's inability to form transference. Sufferers reject the analyst with indifference rather than hostility, making them inaccessible to psychoanalytic treatment. Freud theorized that in these disorders, object cathexes have been given up and object libido transformed into ego libido. This distinguishes them fundamentally from transference neuroses.

## Key facts

- Patients with narcissistic neuroses have no capacity for transference or only insufficient residues of it—they reject the doctor with indifference, not hostility. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5199]
- In dementia praecox, the libidinal cathexis of objects is lacking; the libido turned away from objects returns to the ego and becomes the source of megalomania. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
- The process detaching libido from objects in dementia praecox is closely related to repression and is its counterpart. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5176]
- In all the narcissistic neuroses, fixation points for the libido go back to far earlier phases of development than in hysteria or obsessional neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5176]
- Paranoia is the form of disease in which a person defends himself against a homosexual impulse which has become too powerful. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5179]
- In melancholia, the self-reproaches torment the patient but in fact apply to another person, the sexual object which has been lost or become valueless. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5181]
