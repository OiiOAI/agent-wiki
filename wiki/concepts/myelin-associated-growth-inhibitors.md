# Myelin-Associated Growth Inhibitors

## Summary

Myelin-associated growth inhibitors are proteins in central nervous system myelin that actively prevent axon regeneration after injury. Key inhibitors include Nogo, myelin-associated glycoprotein (MAG), and oligodendrocyte-myelin glycoprotein (OMgp). These proteins bind to common receptors (NogoR, PirB) and activate intracellular signaling cascades involving RhoA and ROCK, leading to growth cone collapse and inhibition of axon outgrowth. Their presence explains why CNS regeneration fails while PNS regeneration succeeds.

## Key facts

- CNS myelin contains growth-inhibitory proteins absent from peripheral myelin, including Nogo, MAG, and OMgp, which potently inhibit neurite outgrowth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1291]
- Blocking myelin formation in the spinal cord enhances sprouting of sensory fibers following injury, demonstrating that myelin actively inhibits regeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1291]
- Nogo, MAG, and OMgp bind to common receptors (NogoR, PirB) that activate RhoA-ROCK signaling, leading to growth cone collapse and inhibition of actin polymerization.
