# Mental Health Screening via Social Media

## Summary

Andrew Reece's research demonstrated that by analyzing social media content using computational methods, depression could be detected as well as or better than general practitioners, and could identify depression several months before clinical diagnosis. This suggests significant potential for early intervention in mental health.

## Key facts

- Reece's study analyzed 279,951 Twitter posts and 43,950 Instagram posts to identify features that might help predict depression using color analysis, face detection, semantic analysis, and natural language processing. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p183]
- The model was able to differentiate between healthy and depressed individuals as well as or better than general practitioners' category-based diagnoses. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p184]
- Depression could be diagnosed several months prior to a clinician's diagnosis when analyzing Twitter data posted before the first clinical diagnosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p184]

## Inferences

- Inference: Early screening through social media analysis could potentially make hospital care for mental illness unnecessary if intervention occurs early enough.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether reliance on these computer programs might lead to mindless application if they become outdated or if the information they were built on becomes irrelevant.
