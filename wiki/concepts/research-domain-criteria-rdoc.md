# Research Domain Criteria

## Summary

Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) is an NIMH framework launched in 2010 that proposes studying mental disorders based on underlying brain mechanisms rather than traditional DSM diagnostic categories. RDoC rests on three concepts: mental and behavioral problems are brain problems, neuroscience tools can identify underlying dysfunctions, and biological markers can guide diagnosis and treatment. The framework organizes psychological constructs into five functional domains: negative valence systems, positive valence systems, cognitive systems, arousal/regulatory systems, and social processing systems.

## Key facts

- RDoC was developed under Tom Insel, director of NIMH, as an alternative to symptom-based DSM categories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p349]
- The five RDoC functional domains are negative valence, positive valence, cognitive, arousal/regulatory, and social processing systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p350]
- RDoC is well-suited for basic mechanism research that can be studied similarly in animals and humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p351]

## Inferences

- Inference: RDoC's emphasis on dimensional rather than categorical approaches may better capture the heterogeneity within current diagnostic categories and identify transdiagnostic mechanisms.
