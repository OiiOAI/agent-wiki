# Hormesis

## Summary

Hormesis is the biological principle whereby small doses of stress from exercise, fasting, extreme temperatures, or certain foods cause cells to adapt and grow stronger rather than weaker. This mechanism explains how acute stressors like workouts, sauna use, and intermittent fasting can promote more efficient cells and greater long-term health through adaptive responses.

## Key facts

- Hormesis is the mechanism by which small doses of stress from exercise, sauna, or temporary calorie restriction can promote more efficient cells and greater long-term health. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p343]
- While large doses of a particular stressor might harm you, small doses cause your cells to adapt and grow stronger. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p343]
- Heat shock proteins (HSPs) are activated by heat stress and act to guard other proteins by protecting them from misfolding, which is directly implicated in Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Lewy body dementia. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p362]
- Regular sauna use four to seven times per week led to a 65 percent reduced risk of developing Alzheimer's disease or other dementia in a 20-year study of over 2,000 people. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p362]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hormetic principle suggests that strategic exposure to various stressors—exercise, heat, cold, fasting—may be beneficial rather than harmful when applied appropriately.
