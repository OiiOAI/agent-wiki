# Hormesis

## Summary

Hormesis is the biological principle whereby small doses of stress from exercise, fasting, extreme temperatures, or certain foods cause cells to adapt and grow stronger rather than weaker. This mechanism explains how acute stressors like workouts, sauna use, and intermittent fasting can promote more efficient cells and greater long-term health through adaptive responses.

## Key facts

- Hormesis is the mechanism by which small doses of stress from exercise, sauna, or temporary calorie restriction can promote more efficient cells and greater long-term health. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p343]
- While large doses of a particular stressor might harm you, small doses cause your cells to adapt and grow stronger. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p343]
- Heat shock proteins (HSPs) are activated by heat stress and act to guard other proteins by protecting them from misfolding, which is directly implicated in Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and Lewy body dementia. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p362]
- Regular sauna use four to seven times per week led to a 65 percent reduced risk of developing Alzheimer's disease or other dementia in a 20-year study of over 2,000 people. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p362]
- Hormesis occurs when a small dose of a harmful substance is actually beneficial for the organism, acting as medicine; a little bit of an otherwise offending substance benefits the organism. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p59]
- Hormesis was first scientifically described in 1888 by German toxicologist Hugo Schulz, who observed that small doses of poison stimulate yeast growth while larger doses cause harm. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p59]
- Taleb describes hormesis as a 'proto-antifragile' concept—still at the more modest level of robustness, but a step toward antifragility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p59]
- Hormesis was mistakenly associated with homeopathy after the 1930s and lost scientific respect, though the mechanisms are extremely different. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p60]
- Depriving systems of stressors is not necessarily good and can be downright harmful—this applies to both biological organisms and complex systems. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p60]
- Hormesis is a bit of a harmful substance, or stressor, in the right dose or with the right intensity that stimulates the organism and makes it better, stronger, healthier. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p528]
- The hormetic response graph shows initial convex benefits as dose increases, then concavity as harm begins at higher doses, flattening at maximum harm. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p560]
- Danchin's work shows hormesis operating at population levels of molecules, cells, or organisms, with some entities faring extremely well to compensate for collapse of others. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p582]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hormetic principle suggests that strategic exposure to various stressors—exercise, heat, cold, fasting—may be beneficial rather than harmful when applied appropriately.
- Inference: Hormesis provides a biological foundation for understanding why antifragile systems require some measure of stress or harm to thrive, and why deprivation of stressors leads to fragility.
- Inference: Hormesis explains why exercise, fasting, and mild stress strengthen biological systems rather than breaking them.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which hormesis operates across different biological systems remain to be fully characterized.
