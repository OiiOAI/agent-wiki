# Cognitive Slowing in Aging

## Summary

Cognitive slowing is one of the most robust findings in aging research, where older adults consistently perform more slowly than younger adults across many cognitive and psychomotor tasks. This slowing is typically proportional across task conditions and increases with task complexity, potentially affecting SA acquisition particularly in dynamic, externally-paced situations.

## Key facts

- Older adults consistently perform more slowly than younger adults on many cognitive and psychomotor tasks, with performance often represented as a linear function with slope greater than 1. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p292]
- Salthouse argued that age-related changes in speed may account for much of the age-related variability in cognitive performance across a variety of tasks. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p292]
- The impact of slowing increases with task complexity, making it particularly disadvantageous in complex situations where cues change at a relatively rapid, externally paced rate. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p297]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cognitive slowing may strain older adults' capabilities to keep pace with information flow in complex SA environments.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: There is disagreement whether slowing represents the primary factor behind age-related decline or is a manifestation of a more general unidentified aging process.
