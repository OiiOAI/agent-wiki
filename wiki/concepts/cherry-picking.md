# Cherry-picking

## Summary

Cherry-picking is the selective presentation of favorable data while hiding unfavorable information. It occurs in hotel brochures, company reports, and even academic research. The antidote is asking about "leftover cherries"—failed projects and missed goals—which reveal far more than successes.

## Key facts

- Hotels, car brochures, and real estate listings carefully select only flattering images, hiding unflattering aspects. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p209]
- Annual reports cherry-pick by talking up achieved goals and not mentioning unmet ones. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p209]
- Anecdotes are particularly tricky cherry-picks because they are mini-stories that exploit our vulnerability to narrative. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p210]
- Taleb noted that academia "shows how indispensable her methods are," discussing what was accomplished but not what wasn't—pure cherry-picking. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p210]
