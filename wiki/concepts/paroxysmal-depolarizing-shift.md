# Paroxysmal Depolarizing Shift

## Summary

The paroxysmal depolarizing shift (PDS) is a stereotypic electrical response of neurons in seizure foci, consisting of a sudden depolarization (20-40 mV, 50-200 ms) that triggers a train of action potentials, followed by an afterhyperpolarization. It is shaped by intrinsic membrane properties and synaptic inputs from excitatory (glutamatergic) and inhibitory (GABAergic) neurons.

## Key facts

- The paroxysmal depolarizing shift is a depolarization that is sudden, large (20-40 mV), and long-lasting (50-200 ms), triggering action potentials at its peak. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The depolarizing phase results primarily from activation of AMPA- and NMDA-type glutamate receptor-channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The afterhyperpolarization is generated by GABAA and GABAB receptor-mediated Cl- and K+ conductances, and by voltage- and Ca2+-dependent K+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The disappearance of the afterhyperpolarization is critical for the onset of focal onset seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The paroxysmal depolarizing shift is a stereotypic synchronized electrical response of neurons within a seizure focus, characterized by a sudden large depolarization (20-40 mV) lasting 50-200 ms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1172]
- The depolarizing phase results primarily from activation of AMPA- and NMDA-type glutamate receptor-channels as well as voltage-gated Na+ and Ca2+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1172]
- The afterhyperpolarization following the PDS is generated by K+ channels and GABA receptor-mediated Cl- conductance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1172]
- The gradual disappearance of afterhyperpolarization is an important factor in the onset of a focal seizure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1172]

## Inferences

- Inference: The balance between excitatory glutamatergic inputs and inhibitory GABAergic outputs in generating the PDS suggests that any factor shifting this balance toward excitation could precipitate seizure activity.
