# Unhooking Techniques

## Summary

Unhooking techniques are strategies from Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) designed to help individuals see thoughts for what they are—mere words and pictures—rather than treating them as absolute truths. These playful, often humorous methods help create distance between the person and their thoughts, reducing the thoughts' ability to control behavior and emotional responses. The techniques include thanking the mind, playing with text formatting, using silly voices, visualizing thoughts on a television screen, adding subtitles or soundtracks, and shifting the location of thoughts in imagination. The goal is not to eliminate thoughts but to change one's relationship with them.

## Key facts

- Unhooking is not about getting rid of unpleasant thoughts but seeing them as simply words and dropping the struggle with them. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p110]
- The primary aim of unhooking is to free you from the tyranny of your mind so you can invest time, energy, and attention in more important things. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p110]
- When you unhook from a troublesome thought, feeling better is a beneficial by-product, not the main goal. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p110]
- Unhooking techniques can be combined with naming the story (e.g., 'Here's the failure story, thanks Mind') or acknowledging the mind's good intentions. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p105]
- Playing with text involves spacing words apart, running them together, changing colors and fonts, and animating them to reduce their impact. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p106]
- Silly voices technique involves replaying self-critical thoughts in cartoon character voices like Mickey Mouse, Bugs Bunny, or SpongeBob to reduce their emotional charge. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p107]
- Jana, who suffered from chronic depression, found relief by hearing her mother's abusive criticisms as a screeching old woman from Monty Python's Life of Brian. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p108]
