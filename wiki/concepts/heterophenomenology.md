# Heterophenomenology

## Summary

Heterophenomenology, as proposed by Daniel Dennett, is a resolutely third-person approach to studying consciousness that defines mental images as intentional objects of subjects' beliefs and assembles from the third-person perspective a comprehensive recording of the beliefs subjects express about their images. It treats the 'heterophenomenological world' as a purely notional or fictional realm to be compared against neural reality.

## Key facts

- In heterophenomenology, mental images are defined as intentional objects of the subjects' beliefs; the aim is to assemble from the third-person point of view a comprehensive recording of the beliefs subjects express about their images. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p297]
- The heterophenomenologist regards intentional objects as occupants of the 'heterophenomenological world,' to be treated as a purely notional realm or fictional world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p297]
- Thompson argues that heterophenomenology is not a purely third-person approach because it employs the 'intentional stance,' interprets speech acts as expressions of belief, and involves talking things over with subjects—all fundamentally second-person activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p302]
- Thompson contends that descriptive first-person reports carry no particular commitments from the subject about what is going on in their brain and should not be evaluated by comparing what they assert to what is going on neurally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p298]

## Inferences

- Inference: Heterophenomenology builds in a biased conception that first-person reports should be evaluated for their truth in relation to neural activity, assuming analytical isomorphism between experiential content and neural representational format.
