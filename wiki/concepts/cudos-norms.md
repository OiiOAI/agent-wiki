# CUDOS Norms

## Summary

CUDOS is an acronym for the scientific norms articulated by Robert K. Merton, representing the institutional ethos of science: Communism (shared ownership of findings), Universalism (truth claims evaluated by universal criteria regardless of source), Disinterestedness (acting for the benefit of science rather than personal gain), and Organized Skepticism (systematic scrutiny of all ideas before acceptance). Duke applies these norms to decision-making groups seeking truth, arguing they provide a framework for constructive group deliberation. The norms help counteract biases like groupthink, self-serving assessment, and shooting the messenger.

## Key facts

- CUDOS stands for Communism (shared ownership), Universalism (universal truth criteria), Disinterestedness (science-first motivation), and Organized Skepticism (systematic scrutiny). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2100-L2120]
- Merton articulated these norms in his 1942 essay 'The Normative Structure of Science'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2100-L2120]
- Disinterestedness helps counteract conflicts of interest and self-serving bias in truth-seeking groups. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2100-L2120]
- Organized skepticism provides a framework for constructive dissent and devil's advocacy in groups. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2100-L2120]
- Universalism supports diversity of viewpoints in decision groups by evaluating ideas on their merits regardless of who proposes them. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2100-L2120]

## Inferences

- Inference: CUDOS norms can be adapted as guidelines for any group seeking to make better decisions through rigorous deliberation.
