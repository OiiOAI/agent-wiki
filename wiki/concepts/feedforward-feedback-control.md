# Feedforward and Feedback Control

## Summary

Feedforward and feedback control are two fundamental strategies for motor control. Feedforward control generates motor commands without monitoring sensory consequences, enabling rapid movements like saccades and the vestibulo-ocular reflex. Feedback control uses sensory signals to correct errors during movement but is limited by sensory delays that can cause instability. Most skilled movements combine both strategies, using forward models to predict consequences and compensate for delays.

## Key facts

- Feedforward control generates motor commands without sensory feedback monitoring, enabling movements faster than sensorimotor loop delays. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p716]
- Feedback control uses sensory signals to correct movement errors but is sensitive to delays that can cause oscillations and instability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p719]
- The vestibulo-ocular reflex uses open-loop feedforward control because the vestibular system directly senses head rotation without requiring visual feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p717]
- High feedback gain combined with physiological delays (80-100 ms) can lead to overcorrection and oscillatory instability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p720]

## Inferences

- Inference: The optimal balance between feedforward and feedback control depends on movement speed requirements and environmental predictability.
