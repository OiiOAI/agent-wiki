# Agency

## Summary

In this book, agency refers to the capacity of living organisms to be causal forces in the world—to make choices and act autonomously rather than merely being acted upon. Mitchell argues that agency is the defining characteristic that distinguishes living things from non-living matter, and that it evolved from the earliest origins of life through increasingly sophisticated mechanisms of perception, decision-making, and action.

## Key facts

- Living organisms are autonomous entities, imbued with purpose and able to act on their own terms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p19]
- Agency requires that behavior is not completely determined by any given physical stimulus—organisms exhibit variability in behavioral outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p60]
- Organisms infer what is out in the world, process information in the context of their internal state, and actively make holistic decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p62]
- Agency represents a wholly different type of causation from anything seen before in the universe—the causation is informational rather than purely physical. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p62]

## Inferences

- Inference: The author distinguishes between being 'caused' (mere physical interactions) and acting 'for reasons' (agency), arguing the latter is what characterizes living organisms
