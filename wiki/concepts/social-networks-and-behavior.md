# Social Networks and Behavior

## Summary

Behavior spreads through social networks, including smoking cessation, obesity, and eating habits. Christakis and Fowler's 2008 study in the New England Journal of Medicine found that quitting smoking was 'contagious'—if a spouse, sibling, or friend quit, the odds of others quitting increased dramatically. Even coworkers had a substantial effect in small firms. Obese people tend to cluster together socially, as do thin people. Social distance matters more than physical distance—your risk of obesity increases more from a friend's weight gain than from a neighbor's. This suggests that spending time with people who share healthy habits reinforces one's own self-control.

## Key facts

- Quitting smoking spread through social networks: if a spouse, sibling, or friend quit, the odds of others quitting increased dramatically. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p164]
- Smokers who live mainly among nonsmokers tend to have high rates of quitting, indicating the power of social influence for quitting. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p164]
- Social distance matters more than physical distance—your risk of obesity increases more because your best friend gains weight than because your next-door neighbor gains weight. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p210]
