# Neurophenomenology

## Summary

Neurophenomenology is an approach to studying consciousness that integrates phenomenological descriptions of experience with neuroscientific investigation of brain processes, mediated by dynamic systems theory. Formulated by Francisco Varela, its working hypothesis holds that phenomenological accounts of the structure of experience and their counterparts in cognitive science relate to each other through reciprocal constraints. The approach comprises three main elements: (1) phenomenological accounts of experiential structure, (2) formal dynamical models of these invariants, and (3) realizations of these models in biological systems. A key proposal is that large-scale neural integration via phase synchrony corresponds to the present cognitive moment, with the integration-relaxation dynamics at the 1 scale (250-500 milliseconds) being strict correlates of present-time consciousness. The approach aims to naturalize phenomenological accounts of time-consciousness by showing that the threefold structure of primal impression-retention-protention has analogues in brain dynamics.

## Key facts

- Varela's working hypothesis states that phenomenological accounts and cognitive science accounts relate through reciprocal constraints. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p321]
- Neurophenomenology comprises three elements: phenomenological accounts, formal dynamical models, and biological realizations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p321]
- Varela proposes three hypotheses: (1) neural assemblies underlie cognitive acts, (2) assemblies are selected through phase-locking, (3) integration-relaxation processes are correlates of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p322]
- The 1 scale of large-scale integration refers to the duration of fractions of a second (250-500 milliseconds or more). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p323]
- Phase synchrony in beta and gamma frequencies (above 15 hertz) plays important roles in perceptual and attententional processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p325]
- The neurodynamical "now" has an incompressible width—a duration-block with forward and rearward ends, not a knife-edge present. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p326]
- Experimental neurophenomenology collects first-person data through phenomenological methods to constrain interpretation of neurophysiological processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p330]
- Varela describes neurophenomenology as "triple-braided" because three types of analysis—phenomenological, biological, and dynamical—are equally needed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p347]

## Inferences

- Inference: Neurophenomenology differs from standard neural correlates of consciousness (NCC) approaches by focusing on formal structure rather than representational content matching.
- Inference: The approach is non-reductive because no attempt is made to reduce one domain to another.
