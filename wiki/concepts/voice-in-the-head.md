# The Voice in the Head

## Summary

A concept central to Singer's spiritual journey—the constant stream of thoughts and commentary that the mind generates. In winter 1970, he first noticed this voice while sitting on a couch, creating a profound separation between himself as the observer and the mental activity he was watching.

## Key facts

- Singer first became aware of the voice in winter 1970 while sitting on a couch with his friend Ronnie, noticing he was uncomfortable with silence and thinking of what to say. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p16]
- He described the experience as suddenly being able to remain above his mind and quietly watch thoughts being created. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p16]
- He compared the voice to sitting next to someone in a movie theater who never stops talking. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p17]
- The voice constantly makes comments about what it likes and dislikes, what one is supposed to be doing, and what went wrong in the past. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p19]
- Singer found the voice drove him crazy and he wanted it to stop talking. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p18]

## Inferences

- Inference: Singer's discovery that he could observe the voice rather than be identified with it became the foundation of his spiritual practice—recognizing the difference between the observer and the mental activity.
