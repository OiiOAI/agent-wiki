# Consciousness

## Summary

Consciousness is a multifaceted phenomenon that includes the capacity for self-awareness, being awake and aware, and awareness of specific mental states. For the question of cognitive control, the most relevant aspect is consciousness as access to one's own thoughts and reasoning processes. Conscious perception requires the activity of prefrontal regions, which become active slightly later than early visual areas when stimuli are consciously perceived. The selective filtering of what reaches consciousness is itself actively controllable, allowing us to choose what we attend to and hold in working memory.

## Key facts

- Consciousness requires the activity of prefrontal regions, which become active slightly later than early visual areas when stimuli are consciously perceived versus subliminally processed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p262]
- The key to control is precisely the selectivity of conscious awareness—we are configured so that most cognitive processes operate subconsciously, with only certain types of information bubbling up to consciousness on a need-to-know basis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p262]
- Conscious exercising of cognitive control involves selectively bringing together information about disparate aspects of the world, the state of our bodies, and our current cognitive processes with metacognitive signals of certainty into a common global neuronal workspace. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p264]

## Inferences

- Inference: Conscious experience enables imagination—we are aware not of all neural firing but of the meanings carried by select patterns, allowing us to manipulate ideas in truly novel, creative ways.
- Inference: The evolution of the ability to model our own thoughts likely went hand in hand with the ability to model other people's thoughts, as both involve recursive self-modeling capabilities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How conscious experience arises from neural processes remains unexplained—this is the 'hard problem' of consciousness.
