# False Purposes

## Summary

False purposes are external sources that supply the illusion of purpose and transcendence without providing genuine fulfillment. They contrast with real purposes that come from within as a personal calling one feels intimately connected to. False purposes lead downward to the animal side of human nature—to addictions, loss of mental powers, mindless conformity, and cynicism. Five common forms include: the pursuit of pleasure, causes and cults, money and success, attention/fame, and cynicism.

## Key facts

- False purposes come from external sources—belief systems swallowed whole, conformity to what others are doing—while real purposes come from within. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p472]
- False purposes lead downward to the animal side: addictions, loss of mental powers, mindless conformity, and cynicism. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p472]
- The pursuit of pleasure follows a dynamic of diminishing returns, leading to either escalating consumption or addiction with diminished health and mental powers. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p473]
- Causes and cults are characterized by vagueness about goals, substituting extreme conviction for clear vision, with enemies quickly found as the source of all problems. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p473]
- Crowds in causes and cults make people highly suggestible, relieved of personal responsibility, and can lead to violence while feeling transported beyond themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p473]
- The pursuit of money and status often leads to careers disconnected from actual inclinations, becoming crowded and competitive, with eventual restlessness and boredom. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p474]
- Cynicism represents having 'the void as purpose,' stemming from laziness and offering consolation for lack of accomplishments while disguising fear of trying and failing. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p476]

## Inferences

- Inference: Eliminating impulses toward false purposes naturally gravitates people toward higher purposes in their unavoidable search for meaning.
