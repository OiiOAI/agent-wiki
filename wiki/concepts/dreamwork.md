# Dreamwork

## Summary

Dreamwork refers to the process of exploring and understanding dreams for psychological insight, either in therapeutic settings or through personal practice. The authors critique traditional dream interpretation approaches (such as dream dictionaries and seeking expert interpretations) as excluding the dreamer from the interpretative process, and instead advocate for approaches that actively involve the dreamer in discovering personal meanings.

## Key facts

- Dreamwork or dream interpretation is used only occasionally in most forms of psychotherapy, despite being a potentially helpful tool. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p196]
- Many clinicians do not feel competent to work with clients' dreams, as few clinical programs include courses on sleep or dreamwork. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p196]
- Contemporary models of dreamwork integrate ideas and methods from multiple schools of thought, including Gestalt, Jungian, phenomenological, and psychoanalytic approaches. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p197]
- To be effective, dream exploration requires the active involvement of the person who experienced the dream. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p195]

## Inferences

- Inference: The authors advocate for collaborative dreamwork approaches that empower the dreamer rather than relying on external experts or universal dream dictionaries.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: While studies support the effectiveness of dreamwork, quantifying its precise benefits within psychotherapy remains difficult.
