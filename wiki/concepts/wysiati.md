# WYSIATI

## Summary

WYSIATI (What You See Is All There Is) is a key principle explaining how System 1 constructs coherent narratives from limited information while being insensitive to what it does not know. The bias occurs because System 1 excels at creating best-fitting stories from available data without recognizing missing information. This principle explains overconfidence, framing effects, and neglect of base rates in intuitive judgment.

## Key facts

- WYSIATI means System 1 jumps to conclusions based on whatever information is available, ignoring what it does not know. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p89]
- The Steve librarian example illustrates WYSIATI: personality description is vivid and available, but base rate statistics (20 farmers per librarian) are ignored. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p14]
- WYSIATI leads to overconfidence because people form coherent stories from limited evidence without recognizing gaps. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p90]
- WYSIATI explains framing effects: people only see one version of information and take that as all there is. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p91]
- The Moses Illusion demonstrates WYSIATI: people fail to notice that Noah, not Moses, brought animals on the ark. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p74]
