# Combined Parapraxes

## Summary

Combined parapraxes occur when two or more parapraxic mechanisms operate together in a single event—for example, when forgetting combines with error, or when symptomatic acts combine with mislaying. These combinations demonstrate that the unconscious motive persists in finding expression even when one pathway is blocked, adapting its method while maintaining the same outcome. The phenomenon of repeated parapraxes using different mechanisms illustrates the purposeful nature of unconscious motivation, contradicting the notion that parapraxes are matters of chance.

## Key facts

- Combined parapraxes occur when a parapraxis combines elements of different types—such as forgetting and error occurring together. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2200]
- A case is described where a man regularly forgot committee meetings of a literary society after his play was promised production elsewhere; when he finally remembered, he arrived on the wrong day, having combined forgetting with temporal confusion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2200]
- A lady who unconsciously wanted to keep an antique gold medal brought by her brother-in-law first 'absent-mindedly' took it, then cleverly mislaid it when she tried to return it—combining symptomatic act with mislaying. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2201]
- Ernest Jones left a letter lying for several days, then forgot to address it, then forgot to put a stamp on it—demonstrating how the same parapraxis adapts its method while pursuing the same unconscious goal. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2201]
- Freud states that combined parapraxes give a vivid impression of a will striving for a definite aim and contradict the notion that parapraxes are matters of chance.
