# Self-Opinion

## Summary

Self-opinion is the perception people hold about their own character and worthiness. It can be accurate or inaccurate, but it determines much of how they think and behave. Greene identifies three universal self-opinions that nearly all people hold: autonomy, intelligence, and goodness. These universals, along with more personalized self-opinions, form the foundation of human psychology that influencers must understand and address.

## Key facts

- The three universal self-opinions are: 'I am autonomous, acting of my own free will'; 'I am intelligent in my own way'; and 'I am basically good and decent.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p230]
- People will tell themselves they chose to act even when they were manipulated or succumbed to peer pressure, maintaining the illusion of free will. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p231]
- A plumber may consider his knowledge of house mechanics and manual skills as forms of intelligence equal to academic smarts. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p231]
- Self-opinion is primary—it determines what ideas people will entertain; they gravitate toward philosophies that validate their self-image. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p232]
- Challenging a person's self-opinion causes them to become defensive and resistant, with walls that never come down. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: Persuasion succeeds when you confirm someone's self-opinion rather than challenge it, fulfilling their greatest emotional need for validation.
