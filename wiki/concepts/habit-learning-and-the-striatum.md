# Habit Learning and the Striatum

## Summary

Habit learning is a form of implicit memory in which behaviors become associated with specific stimuli through repeated practice, becoming automatic. Habit learning depends on the striatum (caudate nucleus and putamen), distinct from hippocampus-dependent spatial/explicit memory. In an eight-arm maze, the caudate nucleus is required for 'win-stay' tasks (lever pressing when a light signals reward) but not for 'win-shift' tasks requiring the hippocampus.

## Key facts

- Patients with basal ganglia disorders are severely impaired in probabilistic classification tasks (habit learning) but perform normally on declarative memory tests, whereas amnesic patients with medial temporal lobe lesions show the opposite pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1506]
- Damage to the caudate nucleus disrupts 'win-stay' tasks (learning regularities constant across days) but not 'win-shift' tasks (remembering specific locations visited on a given day), while hippocampal lesions have the opposite effect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1534]
- LTP at corticostriatal synapses (excitatory projection from cortex to striatum) requires NMDA receptors and CREB, and selective impairment of CREB function in the striatum impairs striatum-dependent learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1534]

## Inferences

- Inference: The parallel impairment of both synaptic plasticity and learning when CREB function is inhibited in the striatum supports the broader hypothesis that transcription-dependent alterations in synaptic strength provide a general mechanism for implicit memory.
