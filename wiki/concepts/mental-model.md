# Mental model

## Summary

Mental models are general representations of how things work, providing a framework for understanding many scenarios, each with its own specific parameters. Unlike situation models which represent specific situations, mental models are general frameworks. Four changes in an operator's mental model occur as skill develops: (1) growth of a database of possible events and corrective actions reaching automatic control; (2) growth of event rates and transition rates enabling probabilistic predictions; (3) reduction in novel events but increased complexity of remaining ones; (4) enrichment of understanding of functional properties enabling better diagnostic activities. Mental models cannot be directly observed; researchers infer them by measuring observable behaviors.

## Key facts

- A mental model is a general framework for understanding many scenarios, each with its own specific parameters, whereas a situation model represents one's SA and understanding of a specific situation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p322]
- Theorists remind scientists that they cannot directly observe another's mental model; researchers infer another person's mental model by measuring observable behaviors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p322]
- As skill develops, operators grow a richer database of possible events and possible corrective actions that can reach automatic cognitive control. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p325]
- Mental models grow a rich database of overall event rates as well as event transition rates, enabling operators to estimate probabilities and predict likely following events. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p325]
- As skill develops, the set of truly novel events becomes fewer but the remaining ones become more complex due to emergent interactions between multiple events. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p326]
- Experts' mental models promote better diagnostic activities by providing richer understanding of functional properties of the system, task goals, operator's capabilities, and environmental factors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p327]

## Inferences

- Inference: A better mental model of the human-machine system and environment fosters better situation models, but mental models are rarely perfect because they cannot anticipate all possible events.
