# Dream Incubation

## Summary

Dream incubation refers to various techniques employed during wakefulness to help a person dream about a specific topic or obtain solutions to specific problems. The practice can be traced back four thousand years to the Mesopotamians and was widespread in ancient Greece, where people seeking healing traveled to shrines dedicated to Asclepius. Modern applications include Thomas Edison's technique of napping with a key to solve stubborn problems, Salvador Dalí's 'slumber with a key' method, and Adam Horowitz's Dormio technology. The authors recommend dream incubation as an absolutely free, time-effective, and harmless way of harnessing dream creativity.

- Salvador Dalí described his 'slumber with a key' technique in his 1948 book, 50 Secrets of Magic Craftsmanship. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p184]
- Dormio is a modern electronic version of these techniques, developed by Adam Horowitz at MIT, which prompts users and detects sleep onset to collect hypnagogic dream reports. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p185]
- Up to a third of participants can solve problems with personal meaning over the course of a week using dream incubation techniques. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p187]
- The dream incubation method involves choosing an appropriate night, thinking about the problem, summarizing it in a short phrase, repeating the phrase while falling asleep, and recording dreams upon awakening. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p193]
- The authors propose that the brain uses the sleep-onset period to tag current concerns or incomplete processes for later processing during sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p194]
