# Fight-or-Flight Response

## Summary

The body's built-in emergency physiological reaction to threat, mobilizing body and brain for immediate action. Involves the amygdala triggering norepinephrine and epinephrine release, followed by cortisol through the HPA axis. While evolutionarily adaptive for survival, modern humans often cannot physically respond to stressors, leading to chronic stress with damaging effects on brain structure and health.

## Key facts

- The amygdala serves as the brain's 'panic button,' setting off the chain reaction of the stress response within ten milliseconds of sensing a threat. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p55]
- Norepinephrine triggers electrical impulses through the sympathetic nervous system to activate the adrenal gland to dump epinephrine (adrenaline) into the bloodstream. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p55]
- The HPA (hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal) axis involves messengers traveling through the bloodstream to activate cortisol release from the adrenal gland. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p55]
- Rockefeller scientist Bruce McEwen states: 'The mind is so powerful that we can set off the stress response just by imagining ourselves in a threatening situation.'. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p56]
- The brain uses 20 percent of available glucose despite accounting for only about 3 percent of body weight, making cortisol's role in providing steady glucose flow critical. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p58]
- Chronic stress can cause the hippocampus to physically shrivel like a raisin due to dendrite atrophy and neuron death from excess cortisol. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p65]
- The fight-or-flight response is a primitive survival reflex originating in the midbrain found in all mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, and most fish. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p152]
- When the brain perceives a significant threat, adrenaline floods the body, muscles tense, and heart and lungs speed up to pump oxygenated blood to muscles. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p153]
- In modern life, the fight-or-flight response often fires up in situations where it's more of a hindrance than a help, as the mind sees potential danger almost everywhere. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p153]
- Fear, anxiety, and panic are related to the 'flight' aspect of the response, while irritation, anger, and rage are related to 'fight.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p153]
