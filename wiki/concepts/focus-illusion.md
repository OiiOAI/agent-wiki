# Focus Illusion

## Summary

The focus illusion (focalism) is a cognitive bias where whatever we are currently thinking about looms disproportionately large in our global evaluations of life. Because attention is selective and WYSIATI applies, people overestimate how much a salient feature (like California's climate) contributes to well-being while neglecting other factors that actually determine experienced happiness.

## Key facts

- The essence of the focus illusion: 'Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p521]
- Kahneman and Schkade found no difference in life satisfaction between students in California and Ohio/Michigan, contradicting the common belief that people are happier in California—because climate, though salient, is not an important determinant of well-being. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p522]
- When asked how much enjoyment people get from their car, they answer based on how much enjoyment they get when thinking about their car, ignoring that they rarely think about their car while driving—demonstrating substitutability and duration neglect. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p523]
- People with recent spinal cord injuries return to near-normal levels of experienced well-being within about a month of their accident, because attention shifts away from the disability during normal activities—yet life satisfaction evaluations remain depressed because the question reminds them of their condition. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p524]
- People who know disabled persons estimate 41% of time in bad mood for those disabled one year; people who do not know anyone disabled estimate 68%—showing that those who observe adaptation expect it while others do not. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p525]

## Inferences

- Inference: The focus illusion explains 'miswanting'—people buy big-ticket items expecting lasting happiness increases that fail to materialize because attention shifts away from the new acquisition and adapts to it, while the initial excitement was an artifact of focusing on it.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which the focus illusion explains chronic dissatisfaction with life improvements in general, versus adaptation effects.
