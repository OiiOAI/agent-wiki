# Muscle Spindle

## Summary

The muscle spindle is a sensory receptor located within muscle that detects changes in muscle length and velocity. It contains intrafusal muscle fibers innervated by gamma motor neurons and sensory endings (type Ia and II fibers) that signal length changes to the spinal cord and brain.

## Key facts

- Muscle spindles are small encapsulated sensory receptors located within the fleshy part of a muscle that signal changes in the length of the muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p845]
- There are two types of intrafusal muscle fibers: nuclear bag fibers (divided into dynamic and static types) and nuclear chain fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p845]
- Type Ia sensory axons spiral around the central region of all intrafusal muscle fibers and serve as primary sensory endings, highly sensitive to both muscle length and the velocity of stretch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p845]
- Gamma motor neurons innervate the contractile polar regions of intrafusal fibers and adjust the sensitivity of muscle spindles by contracting the intrafusal fibers when extrafusal fibers are stimulated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p845]
