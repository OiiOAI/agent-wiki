# Shaping

## Summary

Shaping is a behavioral technique used in constraint-induced (CI) therapy that molds behavior in very small incremental steps. Developed from behaviorist principles, it rewards not only successful completion of a task but also the first modest gestures toward it. In CI therapy, shaping allows patients to make gradual progress by breaking down complex motor tasks into achievable sub-goals, providing positive reinforcement at each stage. This technique is essential to the effectiveness of CI therapy.

## Key facts

- Taub learned that simply offering rewards for successful reaching didn't work; he turned to shaping to mold behavior in very small steps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p150]
- A deafferented animal would get a reward not only for successfully reaching for food but for making the first, most modest gesture toward it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p150]
- The Taub clinic always uses the behavioral technique of shaping, taking an incremental approach to all tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- The essence of the CI therapy cure is incremental training or shaping, increasing in difficulty over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]
