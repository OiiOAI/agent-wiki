# Teleoperation

## Summary

Teleoperation refers to the remote control of vehicles where the operator is not onboard. It is classified into two main types: exocentric teleoperation (direct line of sight to the vehicle) and egocentric teleoperation (camera views showing 'out-the-window' perspective). Semi-automated and automated control modes add complexity. Teleoperation challenges include time lags in data transmission, intermittent and noisy data, loss of direct sensory cues, and operator disorientation. Performance degrades significantly when camera views are misaligned with controller movement axes.

## Key facts

- Exocentric teleoperation provides direct line of sight to the vehicle with direct path/speed control through a hand-held controller. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p239]
- Egocentric teleoperation uses on-board cameras to show the 'out-the-window' view as though the operator is viewing from onboard the vehicle. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p239]
- Performance times for tasks with visually reversed or inverted images are two to three times longer than for normal camera views with aligned viewpoints. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p250]
- Automated compensation to correct visual-control incompatibilities significantly aids performance when the camera view is misaligned to the operator. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p250]
