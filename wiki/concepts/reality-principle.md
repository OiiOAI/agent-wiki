# Reality Principle

## Summary

The reality principle is the governing principle of the ego that replaces the pleasure principle once the ego has developed sufficiently to take bearings in the present and assess earlier experiences. It operates by interpolating thought activity between the demand made by a drive and the action that satisfies it, calculating consequences of proposed courses of action through experimental actions (Probehandlungen) before deciding whether to pursue, postpone, or suppress the drive demand altogether as being dangerous.

## Key facts

- The ego uses sensations of anxiety as a signal to give warning of dangers that threaten its integrity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7356]
- The ego guards against confusion between memory traces and perceptions through reality-testing, which is allowed to fall into abeyance in dreams on account of conditions in sleep. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7356]
- Just as the id is directed exclusively to obtaining pleasure, the ego is governed by considerations of safety. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7356]
