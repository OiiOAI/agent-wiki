# The Indirect Approach

## Summary

The Indirect Approach is a military strategy concept developed by Basil Liddell Hart emphasizing avoidance of direct confrontations in favor of psychological and physical dislocation of the enemy. It seeks to reduce fighting to the slenderest possible proportions by penetrating weak points rather than strong positions.

## Key facts

- The Indirect Approach holds that the achievements of great captains had rarely been brought about by direct clash of forces but usually involved prior psychological and physical dislocation of the enemy. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p63]
- Liddell Hart argued that 'the most effective indirect approach is one that lures or startles the opponent into a false move – so that, as in ju-jitsu, his own effort is turned into the lever of his overthrow'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p63]
- Strategy has for its purpose the reduction of fighting to the slenderest possible proportions, and the perfection of strategy would be to produce a decision without any serious fighting. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p64]
- Liddell Hart asserted that dislocation is the aim of strategy; its sequel may be either the enemy's dissolution or his easier disruption in battle. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p64]
- Psychological dislocation fundamentally springs from the sense of being trapped – a move around the enemy's front against his rear has the aim of avoiding resistance both on its way and in its issue. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p64]
- Liddell Hart argued 'It is through the distraction of the commander's mind that the distraction of his forces follows. The loss of his freedom of action is the sequel to the loss of his freedom of conception'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p65]
