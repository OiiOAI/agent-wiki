# Security and Adventure

## Summary

The fundamental tension in modern relationships arises from two opposing human needs: the need for security (permanence, stability, reliability) and the need for adventure (novelty, change, excitement). Modern romance promises both can be met by one person, but this often proves contradictory.

## Key facts

- Humans need security: permanence, reliability, stability, and continuity, which ground us in experience, alongside the need for novelty and change, which give life fullness and vibrancy. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p18]
- Modern life turns to one person to provide what an entire village once did: grounding, meaning, continuity, and romantic/sexual fulfillment—a burden that strains relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p9]
- The anchor and wave dynamic describes how people alternate between seeking grounding and safety, and seeking boldness and risk—the same pattern seen in childhood exploration. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p18]
- Passion is commensurate with the amount of uncertainty one can tolerate; creating 'gentle imbalance' helps maintain desire in long-term relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p23]

## Inferences

- Inference: The expectation that one partner provide both security and adventure represents a historically unique demand on modern marriage, contrasting with earlier eras when passion was sought outside marriage.
