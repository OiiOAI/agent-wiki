# Grid Cells

## Summary

Grid cells are neurons in the medial entorhinal cortex that fire whenever an animal is at any of several regularly spaced positions forming a hexagonal grid-like array. Discovered in 2005, they provide a metric for space that is independent of visual landmarks.

## Key facts

- Grid cells fire at multiple regularly spaced positions forming a hexagonal grid-like array when the animal moves about the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1408]
- Grid cell firing is expressed in all environments including complete darkness, indicating independence from visual input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1408]
- Grid spacing increases from approximately 30-40 cm at the dorsal pole to several meters at the ventral pole of the medial entorhinal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1410]
- Within a grid cell module, pairs of grid cells maintain the same firing relationships across different environments—their rigidity contrasts with the plasticity of hippocampal place cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1412]
- Grid cells in the medial entorhinal cortex fire at regularly spaced positions forming a triangular grid-like array. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
- Grid cells were discovered in 2005 by Edvard and May-Britt Moser and their colleagues in Norway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
- Grid cells provide a Cartesian-like external coordinate system that is independent of context, landmarks, or specific markings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]
- The gridded spatial information conveyed by entorhinal inputs is transformed within the hippocampus into unique spatial locations represented by place cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1561]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hexagonal grid pattern suggests a geometric basis for spatial representation that may be conserved across species.
- Inference: The rigid firing patterns of grid cells contrast with the plastic place cell maps, suggesting different functional roles: grid cells provide a universal metric while place cells encode unique spatial contexts.
