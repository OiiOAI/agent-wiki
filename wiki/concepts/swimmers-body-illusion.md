# Swimmer's Body Illusion

## Summary

The Swimmer's Body Illusion describes the error of confusing selection factors with results. Just as professional swimmers have good bodies because they were selected for their physique rather than developing it through swimming, we often mistake selection effects for causal outcomes in business and personal contexts.

## Key facts

- Professional swimmers don't have perfect bodies because they train extensively; they are good swimmers because of their physiques which were the selection factor, not the result. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p15]
- Harvard's reputation as a top university may stem from recruiting the brightest students rather than from the quality of its teaching. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p15]
- Cheerfulness is largely a personality trait that remains constant throughout life, making self-help advice from naturally happy people potentially treacherous for those who are not. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p16]
