# Black Box Model

## Summary

The black box model represents any production or work process as having inputs (raw materials and labor), outputs (finished products), and internal workings that are not immediately visible. Managers can 'cut windows' into the black box using indicators to see what is happening inside and anticipate future output.

## Key facts

- The black box model represents production processes with inputs (raw materials and labor) flowing in, and outputs flowing out. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L464-L467]
- Windows cut into the black box using indicators allow managers to better understand internal workings and assess likely future output. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L469-L472]
- Leading indicators show in advance what the future might look like, allowing time for corrective action before problems occur. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L474-L476]
- Linearity indicators plot progress against time, showing early warning when actual progress deviates from the ideal straight line toward a target. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L488-L495]

## Inferences

- Inference: The black box model can represent not just manufacturing but any organizational process including recruiting, training, and administrative work.
- Inference: Effective management requires not just monitoring outputs but understanding and monitoring the internal process that creates them.
