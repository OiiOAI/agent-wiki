# Civil Society

## Summary

Civil society, as discussed by Fukuyama, refers to a complex welter of intermediate institutions including businesses, voluntary associations, educational institutions, clubs, unions, media, charities, and churches. Fukuyama argues that liberal political and economic institutions depend on a healthy and dynamic civil society for their vitality. Civil society builds on the family, which is the primary instrument by which people are socialized into their culture and through which values and knowledge are transmitted across generations.

## Key facts

- Civil society consists of intermediate institutions including businesses, voluntary associations, educational institutions, clubs, unions, media, charities, and churches. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p14]
- Liberal political and economic institutions depend on a healthy and dynamic civil society for their vitality. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p14]
- Civil society builds on the family as the primary instrument of socialization and cultural transmission. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p14]
- A thriving civil society depends on habits, customs, and ethics that cannot be legislated into existence but must be nourished through awareness and respect for culture. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p14]
