# CLC Chloride Channels

## Summary

CLC proteins comprise a family of chloride channels and Cl−-H+ cotransporters. Vertebrate CLC channels are double-barreled, with each subunit forming a separate ion pathway. CLC Cl− channels maintain the resting potential in skeletal muscle (ClC-1), while CLC Cl−-H+ exchangers transfer two Cl− ions for one proton using the H+ electrochemical gradient.

## Key facts

- CLC channels are homodimers with two identical pores that function independently, showing three current levels: both closed (0), one open (1), both open (2). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p233]
- Each CLC pore has an hourglass profile with a 12 Å selectivity filter just wide enough for three fully dehydrated Cl− ions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p233]
- The human ClC-1 channel is important for maintaining the resting potential in skeletal muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p233]
