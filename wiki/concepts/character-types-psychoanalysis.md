# Character Types in Psychoanalysis

## Summary

Freud's analysis of specific character types encountered in psychoanalytic work, focusing on 'the exceptions' (those who claim exemption from life's demands due to perceived congenital disadvantages) and 'those wrecked by success' (individuals who fall ill precisely when deeply rooted wishes are fulfilled). Literary examples include Shakespeare's Richard III and Lady Macbeth, and Ibsen's Rebecca from Rosmersholm.

## Key facts

- The 'exceptions' claim they have renounced enough and suffered enough, and have a claim to be spared further demands; they will submit no longer to any disagreeable necessity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4712]
- The attitude of 'exceptions' connects to congenital disadvantage; they believe nature has done them a grievous wrong and life owes them reparation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4714]
- Richard III represents an 'enormous magnification' of the claim to be an exception due to congenital deformity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4714]
- People occasionally fall ill precisely when a deeply rooted and long-cherished wish has come to fulfilment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4716]
- The ego tolerates a wish as harmless so long as it exists in phantasy alone and seems remote from fulfilment, but defends itself against such a wish when it approaches fulfilment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4717]
- Forces of conscience forbid the subject to gain the long-hoped-for advantage from the fortunate change in reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4718]
- Lady Macbeth's collapse after success may relate to childlessness, which represents the punishment for crimes against the sanctity of generation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4724]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'exception' character type develops when early childhood experiences create a sense of having been unjustly damaged, leading to claims for compensation.
- Inference: Success causes illness when internal frustration (conscience) suddenly operates after external frustration is replaced by wish fulfillment.
- Inference: Lady Macbeth and Macbeth together represent two parts of a single psychical individuality, with her carrying the fear and remorse that he initially expressed.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud admits uncertainty about Lady Macbeth's collapse: 'What motives can have been which in so short a space of time could turn the hesitating, ambitious man into an unbridled tyrant... it is, in my view, impossible to guess' (p.4722).
