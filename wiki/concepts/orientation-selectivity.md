# Orientation Selectivity

## Summary

Orientation selectivity is the property of primary visual cortex neurons to respond preferentially to line segments or edges of a particular orientation. This emergent property was discovered by David Hubel and Torsten Wiesel in 1958. Unlike retinal ganglion cells with circular receptive fields, cortical neurons analyze contours rather than just contrast.

## Key facts

- Hubel and Wiesel discovered that cortical neurons respond selectively to lines of particular orientations, responding optimally to a narrow range of approximately 40°. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p605]
- Orientation selectivity emerges from the arrangement of inputs from lateral geniculate nucleus cells whose circular center-surround receptive fields are aligned to represent a particular axis of orientation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p605]
- Simple cells have receptive fields divided into discrete ON and OFF subregions and are highly selective for the position of a line or edge in space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p605]
- Complex cells lack discrete ON and OFF subregions and respond similarly to light and dark at all locations across their receptive fields, firing continuously as a line or edge traverses the field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p606]
- End-inhibited neurons have excitatory centers flanked by inhibitory regions sharing the same orientation preference, making them selective for line curvature and corners. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p607]

## Inferences

- Inference: Orientation selectivity represents the first emergent computational property in the visual hierarchy, transforming retinotopic input into orientation-tuned representations.
