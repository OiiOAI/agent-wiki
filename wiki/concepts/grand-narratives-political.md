# Grand Narratives (Political)

## Summary

Grand narratives are the moral stories that organize political communities, identified by sociologist Christian Smith. Each narrative identifies a beginning ('once upon a time'), a middle (in which a threat or challenge arises), and an end (in which a resolution is achieved). Haidt discusses the 'liberal progress narrative' and the 'Reagan narrative' as examples, showing how each political ideology constructs heroic stories that derive moral force from different combinations of the six moral foundations.

## Key facts

- Grand narratives identify a beginning ('once upon a time'), a middle (in which a threat or challenge arises), and an end (in which a resolution is achieved). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p306]
- Each narrative is designed to orient listeners morally—to draw their attention to a set of virtues and vices, or good and evil forces—and to impart lessons about what must be done now. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p306]
- The liberal progress narrative derives its moral force primarily from the Care/harm foundation and the Liberty/oppression foundation. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p307]
- The Reagan narrative relies on at least five of the six moral foundations: Liberty (as freedom from government constraint), Fairness (as proportionality), Loyalty (soldiers and the flag), Authority, and Sanctity. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p308]
