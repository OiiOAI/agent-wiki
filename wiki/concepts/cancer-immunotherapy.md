# Cancer Immunotherapy

## Summary

Cancer immunotherapy harnesses the immune system to fight cancer. Key approaches include CAR-T cell therapy (genetically modified T cells), checkpoint inhibitors (ipilimumab targeting CTLA-4, pembrolizumab targeting PD-1), and adoptive cell therapy. When immunotherapy works, 80-90 percent of complete responders remain disease-free fifteen years out. About one-third of cancers can be treated with immunotherapy, benefiting about 8 percent of potential cancer deaths.

## Key facts

- CAR-T therapy involves taking T cells from a patient's blood, adding antigen receptors targeted to the patient's tumors via genetic engineering, multiplying them, and infusing them back. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p166]
- The FDA approved the first two CAR-T treatments in 2017, for adult lymphoma and acute lymphoblastic leukemia. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p166]
- Checkpoint inhibitors include ipilimumab (targeting CTLA-4) and pembrolizumab (targeting PD-1), which help make cancer visible to the immune system. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p167]
- James Allison and Tasuku Honjo won the 2018 Nobel Prize for their work on checkpoint inhibitors. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p167]
- When immunotherapy works and patients achieve complete remission, 80-90 percent remain disease-free fifteen years out. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p171]
- Approximately 8 percent of potential cancer deaths could be prevented by current immunotherapy approaches. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p169]
