# Multiple Emphasis on Components training protocol

## Summary

The Multiple Emphasis on Components (MEC) protocol, developed by Gopher, Weil, and Siegel (1989), is a training approach where participants learn a complex task composed of several components by practicing individual components within the context of the whole task. This protocol has been shown effective in training attention management skills and transfers to actual flight performance in Israeli Air Force flight school cadets. The main benefit appears to be training effective attention management skills, as trainees using MEC were less disrupted by secondary tasks than those not using the procedure.

## Key facts

- The MEC protocol requires trainees to practice the whole task while emphasizing individual components, instructed to perform other components without sacrificing performance on the emphasized one. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p329]
- Trainees using the MEC protocol were less disrupted by the addition of secondary tasks than trainees not using the MEC procedure. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p330]
- Training using emphasis instructions transferred to actual flight performance of Israeli Air Force flight school cadets. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p330]
- Gopher et al. argued that the emphasis-change protocol trained skills needed for coping with attention management demands of flight training. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p330]
