# Mental images

## Summary

Mental images are neural representations that become images in our minds. They can be perceptual (formed when perceiving external stimuli) or recalled (formed when conjuring up memories or plans). Images are based on topographically organized representations occurring in early sensory cortices, formed either under control of sensory receptors or under control of dispositional representations.

## Key facts

- Having a mind means an organism forms neural representations which can become images, be manipulated in thought, and eventually influence behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p109]
- Images are based directly on neural representations organized topographically in early sensory cortices, not in other brain regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p118]
- Images are not stored as facsimile pictures; the brain does not file Polaroid pictures. Mental images are momentary constructions attempting to replicate patterns once experienced. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p120]
- Thought is made largely of images, including both nonverbal images and words which exist as auditory or visual images in inner speech before being spoken or written. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p126]
