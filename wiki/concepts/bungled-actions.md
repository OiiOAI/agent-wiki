# Bungled Actions

## Summary

Bungled actions (German: Fehlgreifen) are motor disturbances where wrong results deviate from what was intended. Freud argues these are not mere accidents but express unconscious intentions, often through symbolic representation. Examples include misusing keys, going one floor too high, picking up wrong objects, and breaking things.

## Key facts

- Freud uses the term 'bungled actions' to describe cases in which a wrong result—deviation from what was intended—seems to be the essential element. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2142]
- Freud reports repeatedly pulling his latch-key instead of ringing the bell at patients' houses, interpreting this as a tribute to houses where he felt at home, symbolically acting as if he lived there. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2143]
- Ernest Jones reported that when disturbed from engaging work to go to the hospital, he often tried to open his laboratory door with his desk key at home, unconsciously demonstrating where he would rather be. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2144]
- Freud broke his inkpot cover after his sister commented that his inkstand did not match his attractive desk, interpreting this as forcing her to fulfill her hinted intention to buy him a nicer one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2147]
- A doctor knocked over an earthenware vase given by a former patient, an act Jekels interpreted symbolically as representing a woman and connected to the doctor's unconscious conflicts about his dead wife. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2150]
- Bungled actions are particularly suitable for use as self-reproach, where the present mistake seeks to represent a mistake committed elsewhere. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2146]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bungled actions serve as compromise formations between unconscious intentions and conscious resistance, achieving aims that conscious processes would reject.
- Inference: The 'violence' combined with 'unerring aim' in some bungled actions parallels motor manifestations of hysteria and somnambulism, suggesting similar unconscious mechanisms.
