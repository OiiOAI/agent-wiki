# Autism Spectrum Condition

## Summary

Autism spectrum condition (ASC) is being reconsidered through the lens of predictive processing. Rather than reflecting weakened prior expectations (as earlier theories suggested), evidence favors the theory that ASC involves actively overweighting incoming sensory evidence—enhanced sensory precision. This means individuals with ASC encounter an excess of highly detailed sensory information from both inside their body and the outside world, impeding the identification of broader context.

## Key facts

- Autism spectrum condition was initially thought to reflect weakened prior expectations, but Mooney image studies showed no difference in the ability to use acquired prior knowledge between those with ASC and neurotypical participants. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p63]
- Converging evidence favors the idea that a tendency to overweight sensory evidence (enhanced sensory precision) is the core difference separating autism spectrum condition from the more neurotypical profile. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p64]
- Individuals with ASC report experiencing excessive amounts of detail—for example, feeling the specific components of hunger (irritation, sadness) separately rather than piecing them together into the unified experience of hunger. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p64]
- The McGurk effect—a merging of auditory and visual speech information—is diminished or absent in those with ASC, suggesting they take incoming sounds at closer to face value. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p67]
- Repetitive and stereotyped behaviors in ASC such as rocking or hand-flapping might emerge as ways to self-generate predictable streams of sensory input. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p65]

## Inferences

- Inference: Autism spectrum condition results in what may intuitively be a more accurate perception in some contexts, insofar as perception is less influenced by visual-auditory illusions, though the price may be a tendency to make costly mistakes in others.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural mechanisms underlying enhanced sensory precision in ASC are still being researched.
