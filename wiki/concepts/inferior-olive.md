# Inferior Olive

## Summary

The inferior olive (IO) is a group of central neurons that plays a fundamental role in movement coordination. IO axons form climbing fibers that travel to the cerebellum and terminate on Purkinje cells. IO neurons display intrinsic oscillatory properties, firing action potentials at 1-2 Hz while their membrane potential oscillates spontaneously at 8-12 Hz. The IO is proposed as a timing mechanism for the rhythmic orchestration of premotor signals required for coordinated movement.

## Key facts

- The inferior olivary nucleus (IO) plays a fundamental role in movement coordination; its axons form climbing fibers that terminate on Purkinje cells in the cerebellar cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p50]
- IO neurons fire action potentials at 1-2 Hz while their transmembrane voltage oscillates spontaneously at 8-12 Hz, occurring at the peak of each wave. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p51]
- Damage to the IO or to climbing fibers causes immediate, severe, and irreversible abolition of many aspects of motor coordination including timing and spatial negotiation of movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p51]
- Harmaline application to the IO enhances membrane potential oscillations, locking cells into synchronous 10-Hz discharge and inducing 10-Hz behavioral tremor, which is abolished upon experimental destruction of the IO. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p54]
