# Formal Constraints of the Concept of Right

## Summary

Five formal conditions that reasonable conceptions of justice must satisfy: (1) generality — principles must be formulable without proper names or rigged descriptions; (2) universality — principles must hold for everyone in virtue of being moral persons; (3) publicity — principles are to be publicly recognized as a final court of appeal; (4) ordering — principles must impose an ordering on conflicting claims; (5) finality — principles are the final court of appeal in practical reasoning, overriding considerations of prudence and self-interest.

## Key facts

- Principles should be general — formulable without the use of proper names or rigged definite descriptions, expressing general properties and relations. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p128]
- Principles are to be universal in application — holding for everyone in virtue of their being moral persons, and it would be self-defeating for everyone to act upon them otherwise. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p129]
- The condition of publicity arises from the contractarian standpoint: parties assume they are choosing principles for a public conception of justice that everyone will know about. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p130]
- A conception of right must impose an ordering on conflicting claims; it should be complete and transitive. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p130]
- The condition of finality holds that principles are the final court of appeal in practical reasoning; conclusions from these principles override the demands of law, custom, prudence, and self-interest. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p131]
- These formal constraints rule out the variants of egoism: the generality condition eliminates first-person dictatorship and free-rider forms, while the ordering condition eliminates general egoism. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: By themselves the five conditions exclude none of the traditional conceptions of justice but they do eliminate egoism, which shows they are not without moral force despite being purely formal.
