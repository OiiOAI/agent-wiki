# Hypnoid State

## Summary

A hypnoid state is a dissociated psychical condition, resembling hypnosis, which facilitates the development of hysterical symptoms. Breuer distinguished hypnoid hysteria from defence hysteria, arguing that in hypnoid hysteria an idea becomes pathogenic because it was received during this special psychical state and has from the first remained outside the ego—no psychical force was required to keep it apart. Anna O.'s case was considered the paradigm example. The tendency to autohypnosis facilitates both conversion and suggestion, erecting 'on the top of the minor hysterias the higher storey of major hysteria'. Freud later expressed scepticism about finding pure hypnoid hysteria and suspected that defence might underlie even cases initially classified as hypnoid.

## Inferences

- Inference: The hypnoid state provides a theoretical explanation for how ideas can become isolated from the ego without requiring active defensive repulsion.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledged uncertainty about whether hypnoid and defence hysteria have distinct origins, suspecting they might converge with defence as the primary factor.
