# Infantile Sexuality

## Summary

Infantile sexuality refers to Freud's theory that sexual drives are present from birth and undergo development during childhood, rather than emerging only at puberty as popularly believed. Freud argued that the popular view of sexuality as absent in childhood is 'not merely a simple error but one that has had grave consequences' for understanding human development. The theory encompasses auto-erotic activities, erotogenic zones, and the eventual development of normal sexual life or perversions/neuroses.

## Key facts

- Popular opinion holds that the sexual drive is absent in childhood and sets in at puberty, but Freud maintained this view gives 'a very false picture of the true situation'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2412]
- Sexual life usually emerges in a form accessible to observation around the third or fourth year of life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2448]
- Germs of sexual impulses are present in the newborn child and continue to develop for a time before being overtaken by a progressive process of suppression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2448]
- During the period of sexual latency, mental forces are built up that later impede the sexual drive—disgust, feelings of shame, and claims of aesthetic and moral ideals. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2449]
- Sublimation involves the diversion of sexual drive forces from sexual aims to other cultural achievements, occurring at the cost of infantile sexual impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2449]
- Freud stated that a child has sexual drives and activities from the first, coming into the world with them, and that there is no difficulty in observing these manifestations in children. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3410]
- Freud cited Sanford Bell's 1902 study documenting love between the sexes in children as young as three, four, and five years old. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3410]
- The first phase of infantile sexual life was termed 'auto-erotism', where satisfaction is obtained from the subject's own body without reference to extraneous objects. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3411]
- Erotogenic zones include the genitals, oral, anal, and urethral orifices, and certain sensory surfaces like skin. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3411]
- Infants perform actions having no purpose other than obtaining pleasure, such as sensual sucking at the mother's breast. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p313]
- Infants soon discover the excitable regions of their genitals and find their way from sucking to masturbation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p314]
- If a child has a sexual life at all, it is bound to be of a perverse kind, since children lack what makes sexuality into the reproductive function. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p316]
- Perverse sexuality is nothing else than magnified infantile sexuality split up into its separate impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p311]
- The sexual function exists from the beginning of life, initially attached to other vital functions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6261]
- Sexual drives begin by manifesting through component drives dependent upon erotogenic zones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6261]
- Sexual development passes through oral, anal-sadistic, and genital stages before establishing the primacy of the genitals. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6262]
- Sexual life in humans shows diphasic onset, with a first climax in the fourth or fifth year followed by latency. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6263]
- The latency period builds reaction-formations of morality, shame, and disgust. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6263]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of infantile sexuality fundamentally challenged Victorian-era assumptions about childhood innocence.
- Inference: The theory connects early childhood experiences to later psychopathology through the mechanism of repression and its effects on sexual development.
- Inference: Freud argued that 'most people have forgotten their own infantile sexual activity under the pressure of their education to a civilized life, and do not wish to be reminded of what has been repressed.'
