# Materialism (philosophy of mind)

## Summary

The philosophical position that the mind is the activity of neurons, with no distinction between mind and brain. Materialism holds that thoughts, memory, and self-awareness arise from physical brain activity. This view implies that consciousness is not a separate entity but emerges from neural processes, leading to implications about machine consciousness and the possibility of artificial awareness.

## Key facts

- For some philosophers, just as electricity is the motion of electrons and temperature is the kinetic energy of molecules, the mind is the activity of neurons—materialism recognizes no distinction between mind and brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p139]
- Materialism does not say the mind is the product of the brain's activity, but rather that it IS that activity—to say the mind is the product is a form of dualism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p139]
- Francis Crick's 'Astonishing Hypothesis' states that personal identity, free will, and consciousness are no more than the behavior of a vast assembly of nerve cells and their associated molecules. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p139]

## Inferences

- Inference: materialism is correct, then consciousness could in principle exist in any substrate that implements the right functional organization, including silicon-based computers.
