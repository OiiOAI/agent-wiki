# Ecological Events

## Summary

Ecological events are changes in the environment at the level of substances and surfaces, as distinguished from microphysical and astronomical events. They are classified into three main varieties: changes in layout of surfaces (caused by forces), changes in color and texture (caused by chemical composition changes), and changes in surface existence (caused by state changes). Unlike abstract time, the flow of ecological events is heterogeneous and differentiated. Some events are reversible (displacements, turns) while others are not (disruption, decay).

## Key facts

- Ecological events are classified into: changes of layout (caused by forces), changes of color and texture (caused by chemical composition changes), and changes in existence of surfaces (caused by state changes). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p118]
- Events are nested within longer events—episodes within episodes—suggesting that some event sequences have outcomes implicit at the outset. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p125]
- Some ecological events are reversible (displacements, turns) while others are not (disruption, decay), contrasting with physical mechanics where time 'has no arrow' for most equations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p125]
- Time consists of the sequential order of events, and space consists of the adjacent order of surfaces—sequential order cannot be permuted while adjacent order can. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p124]

## Inferences

- Inference: The irreversibility of some ecological events (breaking, decay) suggests that 'time's arrow' derives from the nature of events themselves, not from abstract thermodynamic principles like entropy increase.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: A complete mathematical framework for describing the various types of ecological events and their corresponding optical disturbances has not been developed.
