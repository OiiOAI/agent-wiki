# Coherence (in Strategy)

## Summary

Coherence in strategy refers to the coordination of actions—resource deployments, policies, and maneuvers—that are consistent and mutually supporting. The coordination of action provides the most basic source of leverage or advantage available in strategy. Strategic actions that are not coherent are either in conflict with one another or taken in pursuit of unrelated challenges.

## Key facts

- The actions within the kernel of strategy should be coherent—resource deployments, policies, and maneuvers that are consistent and coordinated. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p41]
- The coordination of action provides the most basic source of leverage or advantage available in strategy. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p41]
- Strategic actions that are not coherent are either in conflict with one another or taken in pursuit of unrelated challenges. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p41]
- Strategic coordination or coherence is not ad hoc mutual adjustment; it is coherence imposed on a system by policy and design. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p41]

## Inferences

- Inference: Coherence is distinct from coordination through decentralized mutual adjustment—it requires centralized design and policy intervention to overcome natural system tendencies.
