# Neurotransmitter Phenotype Specification

## Summary

The choice of neurotransmitter is a core component of neuronal identity, determined by transcriptional programs. Different brain regions use different strategies: in the cerebral cortex, glutamatergic pyramidal neurons derive from the cortical ventricular zone (controlled by neurogenin-1/2), while GABAergic interneurons migrate from ganglionic eminences (controlled by Mash1). Extrinsic factors including neural activity and hormonal milieu can induce transmitter switching.

## Key facts

- In the cerebral cortex, glutamatergic pyramidal neurons depend on bHLH factors neurogenin-1 and neurogenin-2, while GABAergic interneurons depend on Mash1. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1143]
- In the cerebellum, GABAergic neurons (Purkinje, Golgi, basket, stellate) derive from the ventricular zone and depend on Ptf1a, while glutamatergic granule cells migrate from the rhombic lip and depend on Math-1. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1145]
- Sympathetic neurons that innervate sweat glands switch from noradrenergic to cholinergic transmitter phenotype upon target contact, induced by interleukin-6-like cytokines. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1146]
- Changes in electrical activity can cause some motor neurons to synthesize GABA instead of acetylcholine, and some inhibitory neurons to use glutamate along with GABA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1147]
