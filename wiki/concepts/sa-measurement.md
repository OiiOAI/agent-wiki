# SA Measurement

## Summary

SA measurement approaches are classified into four categories: process measures, direct measures, behavioral measures, and performance measures. Process measures (verbal protocols, communication analysis, psychophysiological metrics) infer SA from cognitive processes. Behavioral and performance measures infer SA from observable actions. Direct measures include subjective measures (SART, SA-SWORD, SARS) and objective measures (SAGAT, real-time probes). SAGAT is considered the gold standard, using freeze-and-query methodology during simulation to directly assess operator SA against ground truth.

## Key facts

- SA measurement approaches are classified into four categories: process measures, direct measures, behavioral measures, and performance measures. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p259]
- SAGAT (Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique) freezes simulation activity at random intervals, blanks displays, and queries operators on their SA, then compares responses to ground truth. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p290]
- SAGAT has demonstrated predictive validity—pilots with good SA as measured by SAGAT are far more likely to have good outcomes than those who did not. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p291]
- Subjective measures like SART correlate poorly with objective measures of SA (zero correlation found in research), and may reflect operator confidence rather than actual SA. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p286]
- Performance measures have limited diagnosticity for SA because good SA is necessary but not sufficient for good performance—operators may perform well by luck or poor SA may coexist with good performance. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p265]
- Team SA measurement involves assessing SA for each team member and examining shared SA by comparing responses to common queries, categorizing results into accurate shared SA, non-shared SA, or inaccurate shared SA. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p277]
