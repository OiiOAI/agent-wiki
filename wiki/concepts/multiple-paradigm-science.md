# Sociology: A Multiple-Paradigm Science

## Summary

Ritzer's framework identifies three dominant paradigms in sociology: the social-facts paradigm (focusing on macro-level social structures), the social-definition paradigm (focusing on how actors define situations), and the social-behavior paradigm (focusing on observable behavior). Each paradigm is analyzed in terms of four components: exemplar, image of subject matter, methods, and theories.

## Key facts

- The social-facts paradigm uses Durkheim's work (particularly The Rules of Sociological Method and Suicide) as its exemplar, focuses on social facts or large-scale social structures, employs interview-questionnaire and historical-comparative methods, and encompasses structural-functionalist and conflict theories. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p565]
- The social-definition paradigm uses Max Weber's work on social action as its exemplar, focuses on how actors define situations and the effect on action and interaction, employs observation as its distinctive method, and encompasses action theory, symbolic interactionism, phenomenology, ethnomethodology, and existentialism. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p565-566]
- The social-behavior paradigm uses B.F. Skinner's work as its exemplar, focuses on unthinking behavior of individuals and rewards/punishments, employs the experiment as its distinctive method, and encompasses behavioral sociology and exchange theory. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p566]
- The interview-questionnaire method is dominant in all three paradigms. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p566]
