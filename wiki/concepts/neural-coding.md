# Neural Coding

## Summary

Neural coding describes how sensory information is represented by the pattern of action potentials (spike trains) in neurons. It involves understanding how stimulus features such as intensity, timing, location, and quality are encoded in neural activity, and how these codes are transformed through successive stages of sensory processing.

## Key facts

- Sensory receptors respond to specific classes of stimulus energy (mechanical, light, chemical, thermal) and transduce this energy into electrical signals encoded as trains of action potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p392]
- Vernon Mountcastle pioneered the study of neural coding by showing that single-cell recordings of spike trains provide a statistical description of neural activity evoked by physical stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p388]
- Sensory information is represented by the firing patterns of neurons, including firing rate (which often correlates with stimulus intensity), timing of spikes relative to stimulus onset, and which neurons in a population are active. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p388]
