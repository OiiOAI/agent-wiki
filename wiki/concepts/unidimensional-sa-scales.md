# Unidimensional SA Scales

## Summary

Unidimensional scales are the simplest form of subjective SA assessment, typically requiring operators to rate their SA on a linear scale (e.g., 1-10 or a continuous line). Research has consistently found these scales lacking in diagnostic power, construct validity, and reliability. Studies demonstrate that unidimensional scales fail to reflect differences in SA that are evident in performance measures, and they do not provide sufficient sensitivity to capture the complexity of the SA construct.

## Key facts

- Unidimensional scales are attractive because they are easily implemented and interpreted, with results directly readable from the scale. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p132]
- A study examining unidimensional scales in skill-based behavior tasks found a lack of diagnostic power and supported the assertion that a multidimensional scale is needed. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p132]
- In Vidulich et al. (1993), unidimensional Overall-SA ratings showed no statistically significant effects despite significant performance differences across display conditions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p133]
- The test-retest correlation for unidimensional scales was not statistically different from zero, indicating an absence of scale reliability. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p133]
- The general consensus is that a single scale is not sufficient to capture the richness and complexity of the SA construct. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p132]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistent failure of unidimensional scales to detect known differences in SA suggests that the SA construct is inherently multidimensional.
