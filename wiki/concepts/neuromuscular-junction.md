# Neuromuscular Junction

## Summary

The neuromuscular junction is the synapse between a motor neuron and a skeletal muscle fiber, representing a well-studied model of directly gated (ionotropic) chemical synaptic transmission. Acetylcholine released from motor nerve terminals activates nicotinic ACh receptors in the muscle end-plate, producing the end-plate potential.

## Key facts

- The motor axon splits into fine branches forming synaptic boutons positioned over junctional folds in the muscle membrane (end-plate), separated by a 100-nm synaptic cleft. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p241]
- The motor neuron releases acetylcholine (ACh), which binds to nicotinic ACh receptors concentrated at the crest of junctional folds at approximately 10,000 receptors per µm². [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p242]
- The end-plate potential is very large (approximately 70 mV), usually sufficient to trigger voltage-gated Na+ channels and generate an action potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p242]
- The nicotinic ACh receptor is an ionotropic receptor—a single macromolecule that both binds ACh and forms an ion channel. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p242]
- The end-plate current reverses at 0 mV because the ACh receptor-channels are nearly equally permeable to both Na+ and K+. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p245]
- The nicotinic ACh receptor is composed of five subunits: two α-subunits and one β-, γ-, and δ-subunit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p251]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neuromuscular junction serves as a model system because it involves a single transmitter acting on a single receptor type.
