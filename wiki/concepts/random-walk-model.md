# Random Walk Model of Decision Making

## Summary

The random walk model describes decision-making as a stochastic process where evidence accumulates over time until reaching a decision threshold. Originally developed for memory retrieval tasks by Ratcliff (1978), the model has been successfully applied to perceptual decisions and economic choices. When accumulated evidence for two options is similar, response times lengthen and choice becomes more stochastic.

## Key facts

- In the random walk model, the decision is taken when the difference between cumulative evidence in favor of one alternative and the other is larger than a critical threshold. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p39]
- In Newsome's experiments with monkeys judging coherent dot motion, single neuron firing rates in MT area could predict stochastic choice patterns in response to noisy sensory signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p5]
- The psychometric-neurometric match refers to the correlation between behavioral measurements (psychometric) and matching neural measurements (neurometric). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p6]
- Response time increases approximately exponentially with decreasing distance between quantities being compared, consistent with the random walk model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p39]
- The random-walk model has three key components: processes capturing incoming sensory evidence, an accumulator measuring net evidence, and thresholds implementing the decision criterion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p414]
- Direction-sensitive neurons in area MT encode information about the direction of motion in the stimulus display. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p414]
- LIP neurons exhibit threshold-like behavior resembling the accumulation of sensory signals predicted by the model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p415]

## Inferences

- Inference: The random walk model provides a bridge between neural measurements and behavioral outcomes by specifying how neural activity translates into decisions through an accumulation process.
- Inference: Response time and error rate in the random walk model are jointly determined by the drift rate and decision threshold, allowing predictions that can be tested against neural data.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the decision thresholds in the random walk model are fixed or vary with task difficulty remains an active area of research.
