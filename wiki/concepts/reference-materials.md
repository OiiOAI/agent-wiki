# Reference Materials

## Summary

Reference materials are nonactionable information one wants to keep for various reasons. Once distinguished from actionable items, they become just a library with no psychological pull. Reference systems include general-reference filing, large-category filing, contact managers, and libraries/archives.

## Key facts

- Reference material requires no action; it is information one wants to keep. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p224]
- Once the distinction between actionable and nonactionable is made, what remains as reference should have no pull or incompletion associated with it—it becomes just one's library. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p224]
- Reference systems include: general-reference filing (paper, e-mail, digital), large-category filing, contact managers, and libraries/archives. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p224]
- Most people wind up needing from one to four physical file drawers, many dozens of e-mail reference folders, and digital storage locations ranging from a few to hundreds. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: The filing system should be a simple library of data, easily retrievable—not a reminder for actions, projects, priorities, or prospects.
