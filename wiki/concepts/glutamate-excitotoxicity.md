# Glutamate Excitotoxicity

## Summary

Glutamate excitotoxicity is a pathological process where excess glutamate causes toxic Ca2+ overload through NMDA and AMPA receptors, activating proteases and producing free radicals that damage or kill neurons.

## Key facts

- Even brief exposure to high glutamate concentrations can kill many neurons in tissue culture through glutamate excitotoxicity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p285]
- High intracellular Ca2+ during excitotoxicity activates calcium-dependent proteases and phospholipases and produces toxic free radicals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p285]
- Glutamate excitotoxicity may contribute to neuronal damage in stroke, status epilepticus, and neurodegenerative diseases like Huntington disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p285]
