# Marketing Strategy

## Summary

A Marketing Strategy starts, ends, lives, and dies with the customer. It requires understanding that customers make irrational decisions based on unconscious perceptions and expectations, not rational analysis. The two essential pillars supporting a successful marketing program are Demographics (who buys) and Psychographics (why they buy for specific reasons).

## Key facts

- Your Marketing Strategy starts, ends, lives, and dies with your customer—when it comes to marketing, what you want is unimportant; it's what your customer wants that matters. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p218]
- Despite what we would like to believe, the decision was made unconsciously and instantaneously, often long before meeting the seller. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p221]
- If a customer says 'I want to think about it,' they are not going to think about it—the sale was either already made or lost at the psychographic moment of truth. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p221]
- Demographics is the science of marketplace reality that tells you who buys; Psychographics is the science of perceived marketplace reality that tells you why certain demographic types buy for one reason while others buy for another. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p222]
- The famous dictum 'Find a need and fill it' should say 'Find a perceived need and fill it'—if the customer doesn't perceive a need, he doesn't have one, even if he actually does. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: Marketing strategy must be grounded in scientific understanding of customer psychology rather than intuition or common sense.
- Inference: The 'unforgiving age' described in the book demands that small businesses take marketing as seriously as large corporations do.
