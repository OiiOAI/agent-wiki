# The Strategic Game of ? and ?

## Summary

The Strategic Game of ? and ? is Boyd's third Green Book presentation exploring what lies at the heart of strategic encounters. Boyd reveals that the central theme is interaction and isolation—the game of diminishing an adversary's ability to communicate or interact with his environment while sustaining or improving one's own. Through a broad survey spanning mathematical logic, physics, thermodynamics, biology, psychology, anthropology, and conflict, Boyd demonstrates that interaction permits vitality and growth while isolation leads to decay and disintegration.

## Key facts

- Boyd revealed that strategy is a game in which one must be able to diminish an adversary's ability to communicate or interact with his environment while sustaining or improving one's own. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p261]
- Boyd identified three dimensions for applying this principle: physical (matter-energy-information), mental (emotional/intellectual activity to cope with the physical world), and moral (cultural codes that constrain and focus responses). [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p261]
- Boyd's final Art of Success formulation states: shape the moral-mental-physical atmosphere to magnify inner spirit and influence adversaries and uncommitted, yet be able to morally-mentally-physically isolate adversaries from allies and one another to magnify their friction and bring about collapse. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p266]
- Boyd defined strategy as a mental tapestry of changing intentions for harmonizing and focusing efforts as a basis for realizing some aim in an unfolding world of many bewildering events and contending interests. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p268]
- The purpose of strategy is to improve ability to shape and adapt to unfolding circumstances so that one can survive on one's own terms. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p268]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Strategic Game represents Boyd's most abstract conceptualization of strategy itself, transcending military specifics to identify universal dynamics of competition and survival applicable to any organizational entity.
