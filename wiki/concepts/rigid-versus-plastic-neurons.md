# Rigid versus Plastic Neurons

## Summary

Hippocampal neurons exist on a continuum from rigid (high-firing, preconfigured) to plastic (low-firing, modifiable) cells. Rigid neurons belong to the high-firing end of the log-normal distribution and provide a backbone into which slower-firing plastic cells can be incorporated after experience. This organization prevents catastrophic interference while allowing learning.

## Key facts

- Rigid neurons belong to the high-firing end of the population, are more strongly connected to each other, and their afferent connections may be saturated and cannot get much stronger. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p347]
- Plastic neurons show lower mean firing rates, more specific firing fields, and larger changes in firing patterns during the first minutes of exploration than rigid cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p347]
- Learning-induced plasticity is not equally distributed among hippocampal pyramidal neurons; sequences in novel environments are formed from neurons that already had place cell features before experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p347]
- Fast-firing rigid cells provide a backbone into which slow-firing plastic cells can be rapidly incorporated during exploration. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p348]
- During non-REM sleep, high-firing neurons tend to fire earlier at up-state onset than low-firing neurons, redistributing synaptic weights and narrowing the firing rate distribution. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p342]

## Inferences

- Inference: This continuum organization explains why memory replay events send a synthesis of preexisting and recently learned information to downstream neurons.
- Inference: The rigid-plastic continuum represents a solution to the catastrophic interference problem that plagues connectionist models.
