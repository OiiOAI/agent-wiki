# The Call to Adventure

## Summary

The first stage of the hero's journey, when destiny summons the hero and transfers his spiritual center from society to an unknown zone. The call may begin with a blunder revealing an unsuspected world, or manifest as signs of increasing force. The herald who announces the adventure is often dark, loathly, or terrifying—representative of the rejected, unconscious powers within.

## Key facts

- A blunder—apparently the mere chance—reveals an unsuspected world, and the individual is drawn into a relationship with forces that are not rightly understood. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p46]
- The call rings up the curtain, always, on a mystery of transfiguration—a rite, or moment, of spiritual passage, which, when complete, amounts to a dying and a birth. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p46]
- Typical of the circumstances of the call are the dark forest, the great tree, the babbling spring, and the loathly, underestimated appearance of the carrier of the power of destiny. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p46]
- The frog of fairy tales is the herald or announcer of the adventure, often dark, loathly, or terrifying, judged evil by the world, yet opening the way through the walls of day into the dark where the jewels glow. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p47]
- The Buddha legend of the Four Signs shows the call appearing as old age, disease, death, and finally a monk—each sign witnessed by the prince. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p49-51]
