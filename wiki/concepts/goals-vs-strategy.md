# Goals vs. Strategy

## Summary

Good strategic goals are the outcome of strategy, not its input. Strategy work builds a bridge between general desires and ambitions and the specifics of action. Goals are decisions masquerading as aspirations—when specific goals are set, they determine where executives will spend time and where resources will be allocated. An unsupported goal set without analysis of underlying problems is bad strategy. IBM CEO John Akers set a goal to increase revenues from $46 to $180 billion without recognizing the sunset on mainframes. Sendia Products tried to double revenues from $50 to $100 billion despite already failing at entering adjacent markets. The pressure of arbitrary growth goals corrupted top management into making up numbers and promises that couldn't be kept. Good objectives resolve ambiguity, are achievable by the organization, represent clear choices, and narrow focus.

## Key facts

- Good strategic goals are the outcome of strategy, not its input; they cannot be deduced from goals alone. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p218]
- IBM CEO John Akers set an unsupported goal in 1985 to increase revenues from $46 to $180 billion, leading to overinvestment in staff as the mainframe era sun set. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p221]
- Sendia Products tried to double revenues from $50 to $100 billion in five years with no clear source of advantage; both new initiatives failed within three years and the CEO was terminated. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p222]
- Curtiss-Wright's Tim Berner sought conglomerate growth through acquisitions, causing total value to sink from $10 billion to $2.9 billion over three years. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p220]
