# Recognition-Primed Decision Model

## Summary

The Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) model describes how experienced decision makers rapidly assess situations and select courses of action. Rather than generating multiple options and comparing them, skilled practitioners recognize patterns, expect certain events to occur, and evaluate a single course of action mentally before committing to it. The model posits that expertise lies in perception rather than in having more rules or facts.

## Key facts

- The RPD model claims that skilled decision makers generate feasible courses of action as the first ones they consider, so there is no need to generate many options. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p205]
- One pathway in the RPD decision cycle is to seek additional information, which requires expertise—experienced decision makers spot opportunities where helpful information can be readily obtained. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p194]
- Experts can run mental simulations to evaluate whether a course of action would work and to anticipate what might go wrong. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p196]

## Inferences

- Inference: The RPD model challenges the assumption that good decision making requires exhaustive option generation and comparison, showing instead that pattern recognition enables rapid, effective decisions in time-pressured situations.
