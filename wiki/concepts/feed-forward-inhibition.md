# Feed-Forward Inhibition

## Summary

Feed-forward inhibition is a neural circuit mechanism where a neuron both excites its target and simultaneously activates an inhibitory interneuron that suppresses the target after a delay, sharpening signals temporally.

## Key facts

- In feed-forward inhibition, neuron A stimulates neuron B while also stimulating an interneuron that inhibits B, arriving after the initial excitation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p712]
- This circuit produces temporal sharpening of signals—neuron B fires briefly then is silenced. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p712]
- The A→C→B loop (two synaptic steps) arrives later than direct A→B excitation, creating a time window for signal sharpening. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p713]
