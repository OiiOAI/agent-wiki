# Reification

## Summary

Reification (Verdinglichung) is a concept developed by Georg Lukacs in 'History and Class Consciousness' (1922) based on Marx's concept of commodity fetishism. It refers to the process by which people come to believe that social structures have a life of their own and exist independently of human activity, even becoming objective and coercive.

## Key facts

- Lukacs characterized the commodity as 'the central, structural problem of capitalist society.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p169]
- In reification, people tend to lose sight of the fact that they produce commodities and give them value; value comes to be seen as being produced by a market independent of the actors. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p169]
- The crucial difference between fetishism of commodities and reification lies in extensiveness: fetishism is restricted to the economic institution, while reification is applied to all of society including the state, law, and economy. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p170]
- Lukacs integrated insights from Weber and Simmel into his analysis of reification, though he embedded it in Marxian theory and saw it as a problem limited to capitalism. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p170]
- Lukacs described reification as a process where 'Man in capitalist society confronts a reality made by himself (as a class) which appears to him to be a natural phenomenon alien to himself.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p170]
