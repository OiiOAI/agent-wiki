# Pain Pathway

## Summary

Pain pathways are neural circuits that transmit nociceptive information from peripheral receptors to the brain. Five major ascending pathways convey nociceptive information: the spinothalamic, spinoreticular, spinomesencephalic, cervicothalamic, and spinohypothalamic tracts. The spinothalamic tract is the most prominent pathway, transmitting pain signals to both lateral thalamus (sensory-discriminative aspects) and medial thalamus (affective-emotional aspects). Cortical processing involves the somatosensory cortex, cingulate gyrus, and insular cortex.

## Key facts

- The spinothalamic tract includes axons of nociception-specific, thermosensitive, and wide-dynamic-range neurons in laminae I and V through VII of the dorsal horn. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p594]
- Electrical stimulation of the spinothalamic tract is sufficient to elicit the sensation of pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p594]
- Lesioning the spinothalamic tract (anterolateral cordotomy) can result in marked reduction in pain sensation on the contralateral side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p594]
- The lateral nuclear group of the thalamus is thought to be concerned with processing information about the precise location of an injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p596]
- The insular cortex receives direct projections from the thalamus and processes information about the internal state of the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p597]
- Patients with lesions of the insular cortex present the striking syndrome of asymbolia for pain: they perceive noxious stimuli as painful but fail to display appropriate emotional responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p597]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dual representation of pain in both lateral (sensory-discriminative) and medial (affective-emotional) thalamic pathways, with cortical integration in somatosensory, cingulate, and insular areas, explains why pain has both sensory and emotional dimensions.
