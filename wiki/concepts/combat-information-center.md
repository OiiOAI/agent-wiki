# Combat Information Center

## Summary

The Combat Information Center (CIC) was a WWII naval innovation that distributed cognitive load across multiple specialized roles to improve situational awareness. Admiral Nimitz fostered its development through enabling constraints and parallel safe-to-fail experimentation.

## Key facts

- Nimitz issued instructions on November 26, 1942 ordering each ship to establish a CIC as a center for collecting, evaluating, and distributing combat information. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p230]
- The CIC used plots as the 'brain' of the system, providing a virtual top-down view of all contacts. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p232]
- Different ship types developed different CIC structures: destroyers typically had the executive officer run the CIC, while battleships used gunnery department officers. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p231]
- The CIC evolved from an information system to a system of distributed cognition integrated with command functions. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p233]
