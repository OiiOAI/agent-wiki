# Surfing Model

## Summary

A business metaphor for capturing and maintaining leadership in a technology wave or market opportunity. A surfer who catches a wave and stays there can go a long time; getting off the wave means becoming mired in shallows. This requires being on the edge of developments and maintaining position through continuous improvement.

## Key facts

- The surfing model: when a surfer gets up and catches the wave and just stays there, he can go a long, long time. But if he gets off the wave, he becomes mired in shallows. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p190]
- IBM surfed successfully for sixty years before being bounced off the wave by technological and market changes. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p203]
- Gillette keeps surfing along new technology, which is fairly simple compared to microchips but hard for competitors to replicate, maintaining near the edge of improvements in shaving. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p216]

## Inferences

- Inference: The model implies that competitive advantage comes not from being first but from staying on the wave through continuous innovation and attention to what customers value.
