# Multidisciplinary Education Reform

## Summary

Munger's critique that academia is organized into siloed departments with territorial boundaries that prevent the integration of knowledge necessary for solving real-world problems, and his proposed remedy through multidisciplinary skill development.

## Key facts

- Academia discourages jumping jurisdictional boundaries—big bureaucratic businesses do too, and all create dysfunctions by Balkanizing reality into little individual departments with territoriality and turf protection. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p216]
- Munger proposes a course called 'Remedial Worldly Wisdom' lasting three weeks or a month that would include properly taught psychology and other essential multidisciplinary content. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p275]
- Pilot training's six-element system provides the best educational model: wide formal education, practice-based fluency, forward and reverse thinking, allocation by importance, mandatory checklists, and regular simulator maintenance. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p313]
- Munger advocates using the pilot training model scaled up for broadscale professional education, with multidisciplinary coverage of immense amplitude and ever-maintained practice-based fluency. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p314]
- University of Chicago abolished its entire psychology department when it could not fix its deficiencies—an extreme but instructive response to academic failure. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p280]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger sees the failure to integrate psychology with other disciplines as the most significant educational gap, with consequences for both academic understanding and business practice (e.g., New Coke fiasco).
