# Gene/Environment Interaction

## Summary

A central concept in behavioral genetics where the effect of a gene on behavior depends on the environment, and vice versa. Gene/environment interactions are ubiquitous and can be dramatic, meaning it is not meaningful to ask what a gene 'does' in isolation, only what it does in a particular environment. Examples include MAO-A variants interacting with childhood abuse to predict antisocial behavior.

## Key facts

- Having the low-activity version of MAO-A tripled the likelihood of antisocial behavior, but only in people with a history of severe childhood abuse. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p262]
- The 5HTT serotonin transporter gene variant associated with more serotonin in the synapse is associated with adult aggressiveness, but only when coupled with childhood adversity. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p262]
- Kids with the 7R dopamine receptor variant are less generous than average, but only if they show insecure attachment to their parents; secure-attachment 7Rs show more generosity. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p264]
- Heritability of IQ is around 70 percent in high-SES families but only 10 percent in low-SES kids, showing how SES environments interact with genetic potentials. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p257]
- In a 1999 Science study, genetically identical mice showed massive gene/environment interactions even when tested in obsessively similar lab conditions. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: The MAO-A study shows that genetic variants create vulnerabilities that are activated by environmental adversity rather than determining behavior directly.
