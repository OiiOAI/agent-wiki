# Impulsivity (neuroscience)

## Summary

Impulsivity is a multidimensional construct referring to the tendency to respond prematurely or in an unduly risky fashion. In neuroscience research, three main forms of impulsivity are distinguished: waiting impulsivity (based on premature responding in the five-choice serial reaction time task and delayed temporal discounting of reward), risky choice impulsivity (based on probabilistic discounting of reward), and stopping impulsivity (based on stop-signal inhibition). These forms have distinct neural substrates and are differentially sensitive to neurotransmitter manipulations, suggesting that impulsivity is not a unitary construct.

## Key facts

- Three main forms of impulsivity are distinguished in animal and human studies: waiting impulsivity, risky choice impulsivity, and stopping impulsivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p85]
- Waiting impulsivity is assessed by premature responding in the five-choice serial reaction time task (5CSRTT) and by temporal discounting procedures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p85]
- Risky choice impulsivity refers to the tendency to prefer less certain, large-magnitude rewards over smaller but more certain rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p85]
- Stopping impulsivity is widely assessed by stop-signal reaction time procedures in rodents and humans, requiring inhibition after rather than before response initiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p85]
- Individual differences in premature responding on the 5CSRTT exhibit trait-like characteristics and are heritable. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p88]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation of neural substrates for different impulsivity types suggests that clinical disorders involving impulsivity may require differentiated treatment approaches targeting specific neural circuits.
- Inference: High impulsivity on the 5CSRTT predicts vulnerability to cocaine, nicotine, and sucrose reinforcement, suggesting a common behavioral endophenotype for substance abuse.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise computational algorithms used by different brain regions to compute different forms of impulsivity remain under investigation.
