# Inconsistency-Avoidance Tendency

## Summary

The brain conserves programming space by being reluctant to change, maintaining habits (both constructive and destructive), previous conclusions, loyalties, and commitments. This tendency works with Doubt-Avoidance Tendency to create first-conclusion bias. Munger cites Franklin's 'An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure' and Darwin's practice of seeking disconfirming evidence as antidotes.

## Key facts

- Munger quotes Lord Keynes that universities didn't reject new ideas due to intrinsic difficulty but because they were inconsistent with old ideas in place. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p506]
- Munger compares the human mind to the human egg—when one sperm enters, an automatic shut-off device bars any other sperm, illustrating how the mind resists new ideas once committed. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p506]
- Max Planck noted that radically new ideas in physics were seldom accepted by the old guard; progress was made by a new generation less brain-blocked by previous conclusions. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p507]
- Benjamin Franklin used Inconsistency-Avoidance Tendency to gain approval by maneuvering important men to do him small favors like lending him books. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p510]
- Chinese brainwashing during the Korean War used compliance techniques based on triggering Inconsistency-Avoidance Tendency through small, progressive commitments. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p510]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger views Inconsistency-Avoidance as explaining both the stability that enables civilization (through maintained commitments) and much of individual folly (through unexamined beliefs).
