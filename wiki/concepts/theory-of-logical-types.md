# Theory of Logical Types

## Summary

Derived from Whitehead and Russell's Principia Mathematica, this theory provides the framework for understanding second-order change. Its central axiom states that 'whatever involves all of a collection must not be one of the collection'—mankind is the class of all individuals but is not itself an individual. The theory establishes that logical levels must be kept strictly apart to prevent paradox and confusion, and that going from one level to the next higher (member to class) entails a discontinuity or transformation that provides a way out of a system. This explains why attempting first-order change in situations requiring second-order change leads to paradox and failure.
