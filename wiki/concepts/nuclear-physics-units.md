# Radioactivity and radiation units

## Summary

Units used to measure radioactive decay, radiation dose, and related nuclear phenomena. Key units include becquerel (radioactivity), gray (absorbed dose), sievert (effective dose), and curie (historical radioactivity unit).

## Key facts

- One becquerel equals one radioactive disintegration per second. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L2620-L2620]
- One gray is defined as the dose of 1 joule of energy absorbed by 1 kg of matter. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L2620-L2620]
- The sievert equals the actual dose of radiation multiplied by a factor varying according to radiation danger. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L2620-L2620]
- The Hiroshima bomb produced about 15 kilotons of energy; the largest nuclear weapon ever detonated produced 57 megatons. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L2620-L2620]
