# Fear Structures

## Summary

Fear structures (schemas) are mental representations of fear in the brain proposed by Peter Lang, analogous to Aaron Beck's notion of schemas. Lang viewed fear as a program for escaping or avoiding danger containing propositions about threats, physiological changes, actions, and stimulus/response meanings. According to emotional processing theory (Foa and Kozak), effective therapy requires complete activation of the fear structure followed by insertion of new information incompatible with pathological content. LeDoux critiques this view, arguing it implies a unitary fear system rather than separate implicit (defensive circuits) and explicit (conscious feeling) processes.

## Key facts

- According to emotional processing theory, fear structures include propositions about threats, physiological changes, actions, and meanings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p378]
- LeDoux argues against a unitary fear system, proposing that defensive systems work implicitly while fear is constructed as conscious feeling via cognitive systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p380]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether fear structures as proposed by Lang and emotional processing theory accurately reflect brain organization remains debated.
