# Owner's Compensation

## Summary

Owner's Compensation is the amount reserved for equity owners who work in the business, representing pay for the work they do. It should be on par with the going rate for the work performed, equivalent to what would be paid to hire a replacement. The allocation percentage is based on the work done, not necessarily equity split.

## Key facts

- Owner's Comp is the money reserved for equity owners to get paid for the work they do for the company. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p97]
- The salary should be on par with the going rate for the work done—the salary you would have to pay your replacement. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p97]
- Owner's Comp TAP must at minimum cover Owner Comp draw; set the percentage one and a quarter times the salary amount to adjust for revenue fluctuations. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p98]
- In early company days when revenue is below $250,000, the owner is likely the only employee and must be doing 90 percent of the work. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p100]
- Don't cut your salary to make the numbers work; making yourself the sacrificial lamb does not promote efficiency, it hinders it. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p101]

## Inferences

- Inference: The owner is positioned as the 'most important employee' who must be taken care of financially.
