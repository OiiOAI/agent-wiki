# Good Regulator Theorem

## Summary

The Good Regulator Theorem is a 1970 theorem by William Ross Ashby and Roger Conant stating that 'every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system.' This principle illustrates how effective control systems must possess internal representations of what they regulate, with System B—a thermostat that predicts temperature responses—used as an explanatory example versus simpler System A feedback control.

## Key facts

- The Good Regulator Theorem states that 'every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p175]
- The theorem comes from a 1970 paper by William Ross Ashby and Roger Conant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p175]
- System A represents simple feedback control (if temperature too low, switch on), while System B can predict how temperature responds to heating being on or off. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p175]
- System B's predictions are based on properties of the house (room size, radiator location, wall materials) and external weather conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p175]
- The distinction between 'being a model' and 'having a model' matters: systems with explicit generative models capable of counterfactual predictions 'have' models, while simple feedback thermostats merely 'are' a model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p187]
