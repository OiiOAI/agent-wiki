# Sine-Wave Speech

## Summary

Sine-wave speech is a technique invented at Haskins Labs in the early 1970s that artificially degrades normal speech by replacing key parts of the sound stream with pure tone whistles. Initially it sounds like unintelligible ascending and descending beeps and whistles, but after hearing the original sentence, the sine-wave version is clearly heard as structured speech. This demonstrates how predictions help sort out signal from noise and how perception is improved by the presence of a good predictive model.

## Key facts

- Sine-wave speech was invented at Haskins Labs in the early 1970s as a means of studying speech perception by replacing key parts of the sound stream with pure tone whistles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p29]
- Sine-wave speech initially sounds like unintelligible ascending and descending beeps and whistles, but after hearing the original sentence, the degraded version is heard clearly as structured speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p30]
- This phenomenon demonstrates that perception is improved by the presence of a good predictive model—predictions help sort out the signal from the noise, transforming meaningless beeps into a structured sentence.
