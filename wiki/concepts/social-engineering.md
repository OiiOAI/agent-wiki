# Social Engineering

## Summary

Fukuyama argues that in postindustrial societies, further improvements cannot be achieved through ambitious social engineering—the top-down design and implementation of social arrangements. He contends that the Clinton administration's difficulties with health care reform in 1994 demonstrated American skepticism toward large-scale government management. In Europe, the reform agenda consists of cutting back the welfare state rather than expanding it. Fukuyama asserts that stable family structures and durable social institutions cannot be legislated into existence the way a government can create a central bank or an army.
