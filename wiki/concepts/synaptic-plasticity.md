# Synaptic Plasticity

## Summary

Synaptic plasticity refers to activity-dependent changes in the strength of synaptic connections, including homosynaptic changes (input activity-dependent) and heterosynaptic changes (modulatory transmitter-dependent), underlying learning and memory.

## Key facts

- Synaptic plasticity enables modification of neural circuit strength based on behavioral context or experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p240]
- Homosynaptic plasticity results from the activity of the synapse itself, while heterosynaptic plasticity depends on extrinsic factors often due to modulatory transmitter release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p240]
- Synaptic plasticity modifications range in duration from seconds to a lifetime and provide the cellular substrate for different forms of memory storage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p240]
- Synaptic strength can be strongly influenced by the pattern of activity of the presynaptic neuron, manifesting as synaptic depression or facilitation during trains of action potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p397]
- Synapses with an initial high probability of release normally undergo depression because the high rate of release transiently depletes docked vesicles; synapses with low initial release probability undergo facilitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p397]
- During tetanic stimulation, residual calcium accumulates in presynaptic terminals, enhancing synaptic transmission for minutes to hours by activating calcium-dependent enzymes that increase vesicle priming. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p398]
- Presynaptic terminals can be modulated by axo-axonic synapses that regulate transmitter release through changes in calcium influx or direct actions on the release machinery. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p398]
- Post-tetanic potentiation can persist for several minutes to an hour at some synapses, resulting from residual Ca2+ that activates Ca2+-sensitive enzymes increasing vesicle priming. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p335]
- Synaptic depression during prolonged stimulation results from depletion of releasable synaptic vesicles at active zones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p335]
- During tetanic stimulation the RIM and Munc13 complex helps maintain transmitter release by acting through diacylglycerol to promote vesicle priming. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p335]
- Long-term potentiation is a cellular mechanism of fear learning and memory in the amygdala. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]
- Hippocampal long-term synaptic plasticity involves signal amplification of NMDA receptors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]
- 17beta-estradiol enhances NMDA receptor-mediated EPSPs and long-term potentiation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]
- Oxytocin promotes long-term potentiation by enhancing local translation of Protein Kinase Mzeta. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]
- Testosterone depletion in adult male rats increases mossy fiber transmission, LTP, and sprouting in area CA3 of hippocampus. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]
- Experience-dependent structural synaptic plasticity occurs in the mammalian brain across the lifespan. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]
- Psychomotor stimulants and neuronal plasticity are linked, with LTP potentially triggering addiction. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p762]

## Inferences

- Inference: Dysfunction in synaptic plasticity mechanisms contributes to various neurological and psychiatric disorders, including Alzheimer's disease and schizophrenia.
