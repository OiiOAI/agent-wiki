# Psychoanalytic Feminism

## Summary

Psychoanalytic feminism, reformulating the theories of Freud, attempts to explain patriarchy by mapping the emotional dynamics of personality. It holds that patriarchy is a universally pervasive system sustained by all men in their individual daily actions, and women resist only occasionally but more often acquiesce or work for their own subordination. The theory locates the source of men's energy to sustain patriarchy in ambivalent unconscious emotions toward the mother/woman who reared them—need, love, hate, possessiveness, and fear over her ability to thwart one's will. It discounts the argument that cognitive calculus of practical benefits is sufficient to explain male support for patriarchy.

## Key facts

- Psychoanalytic feminism reformulates the theories of Freud and his intellectual heirs (Benjamin, Chodorow) to explain patriarchy through the emotional dynamics of personality. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p370]
- Psychoanalytic feminists see patriarchy as a universally pervasive system that all men work to create and sustain in their individual daily actions, with women often acquiescing or working for their own subordination. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p370]
- The puzzle psychoanalytical feminists set out to solve is why men everywhere bring such unremitting energy to the task of sustaining patriarchy and why there is an absence of countervailing energy on the part of women. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p370]
- Psychoanalytic feminists locate the energic underpinnings of patriarchy in the socioemotional environment of early childhood, where all societies' infants experience their earliest development in a close, intimate relationship with a woman, their mother. - Chodorow argued that the male child attempts to achieve a rapid separation of identity from the woman/mother, and in adulthood the emotional carryover toward women energizes a quest for a woman who meets his emotional needs yet is dependent on and controlled by him. ## Inferences

- Inference: Psychoanalytic feminism requires a great deal more elaboration of both sociological factors and change strategies, as critics note it draws a straight line from human emotions to female oppression without adequately exploring intermediate social arrangements.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Several theorists have discussed unacknowledged ethnic, class, and nationality assumptions in psychoanalytic feminist theories and their generalization from white, upper-middle-class, North Atlantic family experience.
