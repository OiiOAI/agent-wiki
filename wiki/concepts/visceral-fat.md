# Visceral Fat

## Summary

Visceral fat accumulates between organs in the abdomen, unlike subcutaneous fat which sits beneath the skin. While subcutaneous fat is relatively harmless and acts as a metabolic buffer, visceral fat secretes inflammatory cytokines (TNF-alpha and IL-6) in close proximity to vital organs. This may explain why visceral fat is linked to increased risk of both cancer and cardiovascular disease. Fat storage capacity varies widely among individuals.

## Key facts

- Visceral fat accumulates in between organs in the abdomen, as opposed to subcutaneous fat beneath the skin. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p104]
- Visceral fat cells secrete inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-alpha and IL-6 in close proximity to vital organs. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p104]
- This may explain why visceral fat is linked to increased risk of both cancer and cardiovascular disease. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p105]
- Subcutaneous fat acts like a metabolic buffer zone, absorbing excess energy and storing it safely until needed. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p103]
- Fat storage capacity varies widely: some people have the equivalent of a five-gallon bucket, others a full-sized hot tub. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p105]
- Even just 4.5 pounds of visceral fat in a 200-pound, 40-year-old man places them at the top 5 percent of cardiovascular and diabetes risk. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p105]
