# Defence and Repression

## Summary

Freud distinguished 'defence' (Abwehr) as the general term for all ego techniques against dangerous drive demands, while 'repression' (Verdrängung) was one specific mechanism. Different neuroses employ different defensive techniques: hysteria primarily uses repression with amnesia; obsessional neurosis employs regression, reaction-formation, isolation, and undoing techniques. The choice of defensive method is related to the stage of libidinal organization and the structure of the ego at the time of conflict.

## Key facts

- Defence is the general designation for all techniques the ego employs in conflicts leading to neurosis, while repression is one specific method among them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6377]
- In hysteria, the ego turns away from the disagreeable drive impulse and leaves it to pursue its course in the unconscious with minimal further involvement. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6335]
- In obsessional neurosis, the ego enforces regression of the libido from the phallic to the sadistic-anal stage, along with severe reaction-formations and superego severity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6335]
- Repression requires permanent anticathectic expenditure; if this expenditure ceases, the repressed drive would flow into consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6372]
