# Nightmares

## Summary

Nightmares are highly disturbing dreams that seem to awaken the sleeper, distinguished from bad dreams by this awakening criterion. Nightmares affect 10 to 30 percent of adults at least once a month, with approximately 85 percent reporting at least one nightmare per year. The most common nightmare themes include physical aggression (49 percent of nightmares), interpersonal conflicts (21 percent), failure or helplessness (16 percent), and health-related concerns (9 percent). Women experience more interpersonal conflict nightmares while men experience more disaster and insect-related nightmares.

## Key facts

- Nightmares affect 10 to 30 percent of adults at least once a month, with approximately 85 percent of adults reporting at least one nightmare per year. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p168]
- Lifetime prevalence of nightmares is close to 100 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p168]
- Nightmares occur largely during REM sleep in the second half of the night and are followed by quick orientation and detailed recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p168]
- Physical aggression themes occur in 49 percent of nightmares, followed by interpersonal conflicts at 21 percent and failure or helplessness at 16 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p171]
- Men's nightmares are more likely than women's to contain insects or disasters like floods, earthquakes, and wars, while interpersonal conflicts are twice as common in women's nightmares. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p172]
- The most frequently reported causes for awakening from a nightmare were immediate threat (42 percent), emotional intensity (25 percent), and intentionally waking to escape (14 percent). [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p172]

## Inferences

- Inference: Nightmares with themes of falling or paralysis may be attributable to parasomnias rather than actual dream content.
