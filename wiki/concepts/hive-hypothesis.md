# Hive Hypothesis

## Summary

Haidt's hypothesis that humans are 'conditional hive creatures' with the ability (under special conditions) to transcend self-interest and lose themselves temporarily in something larger than themselves. The 'hive switch' is proposed as a group-related adaptation made possible by oxytocin and mirror neurons. It explains phenomena such as rally-round-the-flag responses, collective effervescence at sports events and religious gatherings, and the powerful bonding effects of synchronous movement. The hypothesis draws on Durkheim's concept of Homo duplex—humans existing simultaneously as individuals and as parts of larger societies.

## Key facts

- Humans are '90 percent chimp and 10 percent bee,' having dual nature shaped by both individual-level and group-level selection. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p242]
- Oxytocin appears to promote parochial altruism—bonding people to their groups without increasing love for out-groups. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p256]
- Mirror neurons help people empathize with others, but particularly those who share their moral matrix. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p258]
- Collective effervescence—passion and ecstasy generated by group rituals—pulls humans temporarily into the sacred realm where self disappears. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p248]
- Awe in nature can flip the hive switch even when alone, triggering feelings of being 'part or particle of God.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p249]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hive switch may explain why people join cults, serve in militaries, or make extreme sacrifices for nations—all require temporary suppression of self-interest for group benefit.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms underlying the hive switch remain incompletely understood, and whether it represents a specific adaptation or a byproduct of other systems is debated.
