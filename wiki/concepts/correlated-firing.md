# Correlated Firing

## Summary

Correlated firing is a proposed solution to the binding problem, based on the idea that what matters is not just the average firing rate of a neuron but the exact moments at which each neuron fires. Neurons associated with one object's properties would fire at the same moments (correlated), while neurons associated with a different object would fire at different moments. Spikes arriving at a neuron at the same moment produce a larger effect than the same number arriving at different times.

## Key facts

- Correlated firing is based on the idea that what matters is not just the average rate of firing of a neuron but the exact moments at which each neuron fires. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p211]
- If the neurons of one set fire together at certain moments and neurons of another set fire together at different moments, the brain will not confuse neurons from different sets. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p211]
- Spikes arriving at a neuron at the same moment will produce a larger effect than the same number of spikes arriving at different times. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p211]
- Correlated firing was proposed by Christoph von der Malsburg in 1981. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p211]
- For correlated firing to work, the firing of neurons in each set should be strongly correlated with each other, while firing of neurons in different sets should be weakly correlated or not at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p211]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The times of firing within a set may only need to be simultaneous within a few milliseconds due to dendritic delays.
