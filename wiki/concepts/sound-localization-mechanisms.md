# Sound Localization Mechanisms

## Summary

Sound localization uses three major cues: interaural time differences for low frequencies, interaural intensity differences for high frequencies, and spectral filtering by the head and ears for vertical localization. These cues are computed by specialized circuits in the brainstem and integrated in the superior colliculus.

## Key facts

- In humans, the maximal interaural time difference is approximately 600 microseconds, allowing localization to within about 1 degree directly ahead. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p699]
- Interaural intensity differences are the primary localization cue for sounds with frequencies greater than about 2 kHz in humans, where head shadowing produces intensity differences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p709]
- Spectral filtering by the head, shoulders, and external ears produces frequency-dependent peaks and notches that vary with vertical sound location. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p700]
- The precedence effect allows animals to ignore reflections of sounds by suppressing simulated echoes through persistent inhibition in the inferior colliculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p711]
- The superior colliculus creates an unambiguous spatial map of sound by merging binaural timing and intensity cues with monaural spectral cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p712]

## Inferences

- Inference: Animals with small heads like birds have smaller maximal interaural time differences (35 microseconds in small birds) yet can localize sounds precisely, suggesting their neural circuits have higher temporal precision.
- Inference: The alignment of auditory, visual, and somatosensory maps in the superior colliculus facilitates sensorimotor integration for orienting movements.
