# Mimicry in Language Evolution

## Summary

Mimicry is proposed as the evolutionary mechanism by which meaning between organisms developed. Animals learn to associate the motor behavior they observe in others with their own internal states, creating commonality of understanding. This occurred primarily through auditory mimicry (hearing sounds, reproducing them until they match) and visual mimicry (extrapolating patterns, as in octopus camouflage).

## Key facts

- The 'infectious' nature of brain activity—laughter being contagious—suggests abstractions can spread between nervous systems through mimicry. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p236]
- Mimicry flourishes through auditory systems because animals can hear the sounds they make and match them to sounds they hear. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p240]
- Vocalization evolved from a pain/attack response (crying out) to generalized arousal expression for internal stimuli (realizing you locked your keys in the car). [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p241]
- The flounder exemplifies visual mimicry through camouflage—it creates visual patterns on its skin that match the sea floor beneath it, even though it cannot see its own underside. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p242]
- Understanding between organisms evolved through trial and error: animals that didn't recognize threatening FAPs (like bared teeth) were selected out. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p237]

## Inferences

- Inference: The emergence of mimicry as the bridge between internal states and external communication suggests that consciousness of others ('theory of mind' capabilities) may have evolved before full self-awareness.
