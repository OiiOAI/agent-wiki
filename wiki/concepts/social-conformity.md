# Social Conformity

## Summary

Social conformity is the tendency to align one's attitudes, beliefs, and behavior to those of others. Neural studies show that posterior medial frontal cortex (pMFC) is activated when there is conflict between one's own opinion and group opinions, while the ventral striatum is activated when one's opinion matches the group. This shares neural mechanisms with reinforcement learning, suggesting that social conformity may operate via reward-based learning processes.

## Key facts

- People tend to align their attitudes, beliefs, and behavior to other people, a phenomenon known as 'social conformity'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p203]
- The pMFC and insula were significantly activated when participants' ratings conflicted with group ratings, whereas the ventral striatum was activated when ratings matched with group ratings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p204]
- The pMFC is activated by a variety of aversive outcomes, while the ventral striatum is activated by positive outcomes, suggesting shared mechanisms between reinforcement learning and social conformity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p204]
- Increased dopamine via methylphenidate enhances social conformity, suggesting dopaminergic mechanisms are involved. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p205]
- Conformity in primate hierarchies has been demonstrated using transitive logic; animals use observed dominance interactions to update their own social relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p1161]
- The same conforming behavior does not occur in orangutans, who are solitary primates. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p1162]
- Contagious yawning in chimps is most readily evoked by familiar chimps, then familiar humans, then unfamiliar humans, but not by unfamiliar chimps or other primates. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p1163]
