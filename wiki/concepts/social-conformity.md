# Social Conformity

## Summary

Social conformity is the tendency to align one's attitudes, beliefs, and behavior to those of others. Neural studies show that posterior medial frontal cortex (pMFC) is activated when there is conflict between one's own opinion and group opinions, while the ventral striatum is activated when one's opinion matches the group. This shares neural mechanisms with reinforcement learning, suggesting that social conformity may operate via reward-based learning processes.

## Key facts

- People tend to align their attitudes, beliefs, and behavior to other people, a phenomenon known as 'social conformity'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p203]
- The pMFC and insula were significantly activated when participants' ratings conflicted with group ratings, whereas the ventral striatum was activated when ratings matched with group ratings. - The pMFC is activated by a variety of aversive outcomes, while the ventral striatum is activated by positive outcomes, suggesting shared mechanisms between reinforcement learning and social conformity.
