# Lay Analysis

## Summary

The practice of psychoanalytic treatment by individuals who are not medical doctors. Freud argued vigorously for the right of lay analysts to practice, contending that proper training in depth psychology—not medical credentials—qualifies someone to practice analysis. He maintained that medical education does not prepare doctors for psychoanalytic work and that non-medical practitioners with proper training could be equally or more suitable analysts.

## Key facts

- Freud defined a 'quack' not as someone without a medical diploma, but as anyone who undertakes a treatment without possessing the knowledge and capacities necessary for it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6438]
- Freud asserted that doctors form a preponderating contingent of 'quacks' in analysis, frequently practising analytic treatment without having learnt it and without understanding it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6438]
- Freud argued that medical education is more or less the opposite of what is needed as a preparation for psychoanalysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6439]
- Medical education does nothing towards the understanding and treatment of neurotics, and gives doctors a false and detrimental attitude toward psychical factors. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6439]
- Freud maintained that the important question is not whether an analyst possesses a medical diploma but whether he has had the special training necessary for the practice of analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6458]
- Freud stated that psychoanalysis is a part of psychology, not of medical psychology, but simply of psychology—comparable to how electricity and X-rays have medical applications but belong to physics. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6458]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's position on lay analysis reflected his broader view that psychoanalysis had applications beyond medicine, including to the study of civilization, religion, art, and child-rearing.
