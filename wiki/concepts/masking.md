# Masking (Subliminal Perception)

## Summary

Masking is a technique where a visual stimulus (the target) is sandwiched between other images (masks), rendering it invisible even with considerable effort. This creates a complete form of invisibility where subjects report seeing nothing and cannot name or remember the masked stimulus. The boundary between seeing and not seeing is relatively sharp, typically around 40-60 milliseconds. Masking provides ideal conditions for studying unconscious processing because the invisible stimulus can still be processed by the brain.

## Key facts

- Masking makes images 'utterly invisible' even with considerable effort, unlike inattentional blindness which is labile. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p55]
- The threshold for conscious perception with masking is approximately 50 milliseconds, with images being invisible at 30ms and visible at 60ms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p58]
- When subjects report seeing masked words, they can name them accurately, proving subjective reports correspond to actual conscious access. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p59]
- Masked words leave no trace in memory: after 20 presentations of 37 invisible words, subjects could not distinguish them from new words. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p60]
- Masking prevents conscious perception of briefly presented stimuli by presenting a subsequent masking image. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p38-42]
- Masked words can be processed unconsciously, influencing subsequent behavior and decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p56-66]
- The technique is used to identify signatures of consciousness by comparing brain responses to masked versus unmasked stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p117-119]
- Subliminal priming through masking can influence emotional responses and value-based decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p77-79]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that masking creates complete invisibility while still allowing brain processing proves that consciousness is not required for all visual processing, only for certain downstream effects. [Supporting facts: p55-60]
- Inference: Masking demonstrates that extensive neural processing can occur without conscious awareness, challenging views of consciousness as necessary for complex information processing
