# Cortical Processing Streams in Auditory Cortex

## Summary

Auditory cortical circuits are segregated into separate processing streams analogous to the visual system. The ventral 'what' stream processes sound identity and object features, projecting from rostral/ventral auditory areas to anterior prefrontal cortex. The dorsal 'where' stream processes sound location and motion, projecting from caudal/dorsal areas to posterior parietal cortex. This segregation allows parallel processing of 'what' and 'where' information critical for speech and spatial hearing.

## Key facts

- The ventral 'what' stream and dorsal 'where' stream originate in different parts of primary and belt auditory cortex and project to distinct regions of prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p718]
- Caudal and parietal auditory areas are more active when a stimulus must be localized or moves, while ventral areas are more active during identification of the same stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p718]
- The rostral and ventral areas of monkey auditory belt connect primarily to rostral and ventral areas of the temporal lobe, while caudal areas project to dorsal and caudal temporal lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p718]
- Although the idea of segregated object identification and location streams is attractive, it is likely an oversimplification as medial-belt areas project to both dorsal and ventral frontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p718]

## Inferences

- Inference: The segregation of 'what' and 'where' streams in auditory cortex parallels the visual system's dorsal/ventral stream organization, suggesting a common principle of cortical sensory processing.
