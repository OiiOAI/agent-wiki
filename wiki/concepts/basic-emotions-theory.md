# Basic Emotions Theory

## Summary

Basic emotions theory, originating with Silvan Tomkins and developed by Paul Ekman and Carroll Izard, proposes that several primary emotions are genetically built into the human brain by natural selection and expressed identically across individuals regardless of cultural background. Each innate emotion is wired into an 'affect program'—a hypothetical subcortical neural structure involving the limbic and arousal systems. When a trigger stimulus is present, the affect program activates and bodily responses characteristic of that emotion are expressed. Critics, particularly Lisa Barrett and James Russell, argue that emotions like fear are not biologically prepackaged but are psychologically constructed concepts labeled with culturally learned words.

## Key facts

- Basic emotions theory proposes that several primary emotions are genetically built into the human brain by natural selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p180]
- Affect programs are hypothetical subcortical neural structures that are activated by emotional trigger stimuli and produce characteristic bodily responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p180]
- Ekman identified six basic emotions: anger, fear, disgust, surprise, happiness, and sadness, each with characteristic universal facial expressions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p181]
- Lisa Barrett and James Russell argue that basic emotions are psychologically constructed concepts labeled with culturally learned words, not biologically prepackaged innate states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p183]
- Silvan Tomkins proposed basic emotions including fear, anguish, shame, disgust, rage, joy, interest, and surprise as biologically-based states expressed in facial expressions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p199]
- Robert Plutchik, Carroll Izard, and Paul Ekman used universality of facial expressions as evidence for basic emotions, with Ekman's ideas becoming especially influential. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p199]
- Jaak Panksepp identified distinct emotion circuits by stimulating limbic areas in rats and called them emotion operating systems, assuming they generate both behavior and core feeling. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p200]
- When threats are presented subliminally to humans, heart beats faster, palms sweat, and muscles tense, but subjects are not aware and don't report fear, suggesting amygdala processes danger nonconsciously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p201]
- LeDoux argues that basic emotions theory is limited because behaviors triggered by subcortical circuits do not necessarily indicate conscious feelings—only behavioral and physiological responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p201]

## Inferences

- Inference: LeDoux agrees with Barrett and Russell that conscious feelings labeled with basic emotion terms are not prepackaged innate states unleashed by stimuli but are cognitively assembled in consciousness.
- Inference: The debate over basic emotions centers on whether observable behavioral responses can reveal conscious feelings, with LeDoux maintaining they are dissociable.
- Inference: Panksepp's operating systems only control behavior nonconsciously, then the assumption that animal behavior reveals animal feelings is unwarranted.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The neural implementation of affect programs remains hypothetical.
- Uncertain: Whether animals have emotional experiences comparable to human feelings cannot be directly determined from behavior alone.
