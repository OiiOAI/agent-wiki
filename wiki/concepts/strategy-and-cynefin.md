# Strategy and Cynefin

## Summary

The relationship between the Cynefin Framework and strategy involves understanding that strategy is not a noun (a fixed plan) but a verb (a continually evolving process). Strategy requires different approaches depending on which Cynefin domain the situation occupies, and leaders must navigate movement through and between domains.

## Key facts

- Strategy should be considered a verb rather than a noun—a continually evolving process that guides attention, decision-making, and action. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p310]
- In the Complex domain, strategy shifts from planning and implementation toward moving in the right direction at the right tempo. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p316]
- Henry Mintzberg describes strategy as the realized actions resulting from the combination of intended and emergent strategies. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p313]
- Strategy in the ordered domains (Clear and Complicated) relies on systems dynamics using a mechanical metaphor, assuming the future is known or knowable through analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p314]
- The Complicated-Complex transition serves as immunity against strategic drift, where a company loses its competitive edge. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p318]
