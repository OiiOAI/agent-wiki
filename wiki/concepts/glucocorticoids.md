# Glucocorticoids

## Summary

Glucocorticoids (including cortisol) are steroid hormones released during stress that affect metabolism, immune function, and brain function. They follow an inverted U-shaped dose-response curve in the brain—moderate increases enhance memory, while larger prolonged increases impair it. Chronic stress and glucocorticoid overexposure can damage hippocampal neurons.

## Key facts

- Glucocorticoids follow an inverted U curve in the brain where moderate rises enhance memory while larger prolonged increases impair it. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p958]
- The brain uses two receptor systems for glucocorticoids: MR receptors responsive to small increases mediate stimulatory effects, while GR receptors responding only to big prolonged increases mediate adverse effects. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p958]
- Chronic stress and glucocorticoid overexposure can kill hippocampal neurons, though relevance to more moderate sustained stress is unclear. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p975]
- The suppression of immunity and inflammation during chronic stress is caused by glucocorticoids, which is why synthetic glucocorticoids like cortisone are used to treat autoimmune diseases. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p957]
