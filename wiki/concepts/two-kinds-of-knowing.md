# Two kinds of knowing

## Summary

McGilchrist distinguishes between two fundamentally different ways of knowing: 'kennen' (knowing through encounter, experience of the particular individual, personal, intuitive, embodied) and 'wissen' (knowing facts, categorical, impersonal, fixed, certain). These correspond roughly to right and left hemisphere processing respectively.

## Key facts

- In one sense, knowledge is an encounter with something or someone 'other' (a truth embodied in the phrase 'carnal knowledge'); we know someone in the sense that we have a 'feel' for who they are, as an individual distinct from others. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p142]
- This kind of knowledge permits a sense of the uniqueness of the other; it is also uniquely 'my' knowledge, not something that can be passed on unchanged to another person. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p142]
- The knowledge of facts is not usually well applied to knowing people; describing someone by facts alone is like describing an inanimate object or a corpse. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p143]
- In German, kennen can be applied to a lot more than our acquaintances; it may help us understand, rather than simply amass information about, a host of things in the world, animate and inanimate. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p143]
- Knowing music, like knowing works of art, is a matter of kennenlernen; coming through the right hemisphere, such living creations are seen as being essentially human in nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p144]
- The first apprehension of anything is by the right hemisphere while it remains new, and while we are getting to know it (in the sense of kennenlernen); but it is soon taken over by the left hemisphere, where it becomes familiar, in the sense that it is now known (gewußt) and therefore certain (gewiß). [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p144]
