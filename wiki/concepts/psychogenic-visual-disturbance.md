# Psychogenic Visual Disturbance

## Summary

Hysterical blindness understood through psychoanalysis as resulting from powerful unconscious ideas that achieve somatic expression. Freud argued that autosuggestion requires the concept of the unconscious to be properly understood, and that the 'ego drives' play a crucial role in repression.

## Key facts

- Hysterical blindness was explained by Charcot, Janet, and Binet through hypnosis and suggestion, producing blindness experimentally by introducing the idea of non-seeing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3646]
- Freud argued that autosuggestion cannot be understood without the concept of the unconscious, which philosophers often reject. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3646]
- This 1910 paper introduced the term 'ego drives' explicitly, identifying them with self-preservative drives and their role in repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3645]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's use of 'ego drives' in this paper marked an important step in his drive theory, distinguishing between sexual and egoistic (self-preservative) drives.
