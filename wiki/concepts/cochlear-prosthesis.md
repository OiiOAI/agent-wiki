# Cochlear Prosthesis

## Summary

Cochlear prostheses bypass damaged hair cells by electrically stimulating spiral ganglion neurons through electrodes implanted in the cochlea. The devices use the tonotopic organization of the cochlea to provide frequency information, with approximately 350,000 implants worldwide.

## Key facts

- Cochlear prostheses bypass damaged organ of Corti by stimulating spiral ganglion neurons through electrodes placed in the scala tympani. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p673]
- An array of approximately 20 electrodes can mimic complex sounds by stimulating clusters of neurons representing similar frequencies along the tonotopic axis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p673]
- The number of implanted cochlear prostheses worldwide is now approaching 350,000. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p673]
- Best outcomes allow individuals to understand speech nearly as well as normally hearing people and conduct telephone conversations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p673]
- Outcomes vary widely; patients with extensive nerve fiber degeneration near the electrode array derive little benefit from prostheses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p673]
