# Contrast-Misreaction Tendency

## Summary

The nervous system does not naturally measure in absolute scientific units but relies on contrast. Small-scale damage includes buying overpriced add-ons; large-scale damage includes marrying someone tolerable only compared to terrible parents. The tendency also explains why frogs in slowly heated water don't jump out.

## Key facts

- A buyer from out of town might be shown three awful houses at ridiculously high prices, then a merely bad house at a moderately too high price, making the broker an easy sale. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p573]
- Vendors create artificially high prices to make their standard prices seem low, a practice that works even when people know it is being attempted. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p573]
- Ben Franklin's aphorism 'A small leak will sink a great ship' is useful precisely because the brain misses the functional equivalent of small leaks. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p575]
- When a man's steps toward disaster are small and consecutive, Contrast-Misreaction Tendency lets him go too far because each step presents too small a contrast from his present position. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p574]
- Man may buy an overpriced $1,000 leather dashboard merely because the price is so low compared to his concurrent purchase of a $65,000 car. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p579]
- When a man's steps toward disaster are very small, Contrast-Misreaction Tendency will often let him go too far to avoid it because each step presents so small a contrast from his present position. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p581]
- Franklin's aphorism 'A small leak will sink a great ship' is so useful precisely because the brain so often misses the functional equivalent of a small leak. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p581]

## Inferences

- Inference: Contrast-Misreaction explains why bureaucratic and organizational failures often occur through gradual drift rather than sudden collapse—each individual decision seems reasonable in isolation.
