# Transaction Costs in Government Purchasing

## Summary

Government purchasing agents face different transaction cost dynamics than commercial agents. While a commercial purchasing agent typically solicits bids from the top three or four suppliers based on reputation for quality, reliability, or price, government agents are required to open bids to all potential suppliers, and losing bidders have unlimited right of recusal—a regulatory framework designed to prevent favoritism but increasing transaction costs.

## Key facts

- A commercial purchasing agent typically selects from the top three or four suppliers based on reputation for quality, reliability, or price rather than soliciting bids from all theoretically capable suppliers. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p408]
- Government purchasing agents are required to open bids to all potential suppliers, and parties making losing bids have unlimited right of recusal, designed to prevent favoritism. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p408]

## Inferences

- Inference: Government procurement regulations aimed at fairness create friction costs (transaction costs) that reduce efficiency compared to commercial purchasing relationships built on trust and reputation.
