# Derealization

## Summary

Derealization is a phenomenon where the subject feels that a piece of reality is strange or not real, serving as a means of defense against threatening external or internal stimuli.

## Key facts

- Derealization is a phenomenon where the subject feels that a piece of reality or a piece of his own self is strange to him; in the latter case it is called depersonalization. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7060]
- Derealizations serve the purpose of defense, aiming at keeping something away from the ego, at disavowing it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7060]
- Derealizations are failures in functioning, abnormal structures similar to dreams, which serve as models of psychological disorder. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7060]

## Inferences

- Inference: Derealizations arise from the ego's attempt to ward off distressing feelings, similar to the mechanism seen in the dream censorship, where unacceptable content is modified to become acceptable to consciousness.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact psychological processes underlying derealization are described as still little understood and obscure.
