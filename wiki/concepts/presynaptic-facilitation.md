# Presynaptic facilitation

## Summary

Presynaptic facilitation is a form of modulation in which axo-axonic synapses increase the amount of transmitter released by the postsynaptic cell. It can be caused by enhanced Ca2+ influx through various mechanisms, including closure of K+ channels (prolonging action potential duration) or direct enhancement of Ca2+ entry through Ca2+-permeable receptor-channels.

## Key facts

- Presynaptic facilitation can be caused by enhanced influx of Ca2+ into presynaptic terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p337]
- In Aplysia, serotonin acts through cAMP-dependent protein phosphorylation to close K+ channels, prolonging the presynaptic action potential and increasing Ca2+ influx. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p337]
- Presynaptic facilitation can be caused directly by Ca2+-permeable receptor-channels enhancing Ca2+ influx, or indirectly through depolarization of the presynaptic terminal activating voltage-gated Ca2+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p337]
