# Emotion

## Summary

Emotion is characterized as a "prototype whole-organism event" mobilizing virtually every aspect of the organism—brain, body, and behavior. Etymologically, emotion (from emovere) means outward movement, resembling intentionality's sense of directedness. From an enactive perspective, emotion is not a function in the input-output sense but a feature of the action-perception cycle: the endogenous initiation and direction of behavior into the world. Emotion is integral to protention—affect and action tendencies shape the forward-looking aspect of time-consciousness. Affective valence operates as a "primordial dynamism" manifesting in movement/posture valences (toward/away), affective valences (attraction/repulsion), and social valences (dominance/submission). Marc Lewis's model describes emotional self-organization at three timescales: microdevelopment of emotion episodes (seconds/minutes), mesodevelopment of moods (hours/days), and macrodevelopment of personality (months/years). Each emotional interpretation involves a trigger phase, self-amplification phase, self-stabilization phase, and learning phase, modeled as a bifurcation in emotion-cognition state space. Emotions act as global order parameters shaping neurodynamical events and the flow of consciousness.

- Emotion is not a function in the input-output sense but a feature of the action-perception cycle—the endogenous initiation and direction of behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p354]
- Affective valence manifests in movement valences (approach/withdrawal), feeling valences (pleasant/unpleasant), social valences (dominance/submission), and normative valences (good/bad). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p366]
- Emotional interpretations emerge through trigger, self-amplification, self-stabilization, and learning phases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p362]
- Moods represent enduring intentional orientations that modify the emotion-cognition state space and persist when no action can resolve them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p367]
- Emotions help achieve an effective mental life narrative by flashing warnings when the past is fracturing or the future is narrowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p57]
- Fear is the brain's signal that 'You have no plan'—the brain has evolved a bias to action, and when it doesn't have a plan, fear makes it receptive to outside influence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p58]
- Anger reveals that the future has narrowed to a single branch—one plan can work if executed assertively, but the cost is steep: burnout, shortened fuse, elevated stress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p61]
- Grief signals that you have experienced something disturbing that you cannot reconcile with your worldview; shame signals that you have acted inconsistently with your personal path. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p65]
- Dumb pride is a positive feeling toward a past action that everyone else thinks you should regret—it is the rebel self-respect that Antigone felt when buried alive for burying her brother. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p66]
- Maverick gratitude comes from noticing when you get thanked for a thankless task that put you at odds with the world—it validates your core sense of purpose. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p68]
- Emotion Reset works by recalling a time when you made a new plan under pressure, triggering the thought 'I have done it before; I can do it again' to calm aggression and release intuition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p62]
- Emotions are complicated collections of chemical and neural responses forming a pattern, with all emotions having some kind of regulatory role to play for the organism's survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p51]
- Emotions are biologically determined processes depending on innately set brain devices laid down by long evolutionary history. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p51]
- The devices which produce emotions occupy a fairly restricted ensemble of subcortical regions beginning at the brain stem and moving up to the higher brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p51]
- All emotions can be engaged automatically, without conscious deliberation, despite individual variation and cultural shaping. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p51]
- Emotions use the body as their theater (internal milieu, visceral, vestibular, musculoskeletal systems) but also affect the mode of operation of numerous brain circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p51]
- Emotions are part of bioregulatory devices with which organisms come equipped to survive. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p53]

## Inferences

- Inference: Emotional fluctuations act as control parameters that induce bifurcations from one present moment of consciousness to another, playing a major role in generating the flow of consciousness.
- Inference: Emotion and consciousness are evolutionarily intertwined, with emotion likely set before the dawn of consciousness, suggesting affect is more primitive than cognitive awareness.
