# Emotion

## Summary

Emotion is characterized as a "prototype whole-organism event" mobilizing virtually every aspect of the organism—brain, body, and behavior. Etymologically, emotion (from emovere) means outward movement, resembling intentionality's sense of directedness. From an enactive perspective, emotion is not a function in the input-output sense but a feature of the action-perception cycle: the endogenous initiation and direction of behavior into the world. Emotion is integral to protention—affect and action tendencies shape the forward-looking aspect of time-consciousness. Affective valence operates as a "primordial dynamism" manifesting in movement/posture valences (toward/away), affective valences (attraction/repulsion), and social valences (dominance/submission). Marc Lewis's model describes emotional self-organization at three timescales: microdevelopment of emotion episodes (seconds/minutes), mesodevelopment of moods (hours/days), and macrodevelopment of personality (months/years). Each emotional interpretation involves a trigger phase, self-amplification phase, self-stabilization phase, and learning phase, modeled as a bifurcation in emotion-cognition state space. Emotions act as global order parameters shaping neurodynamical events and the flow of consciousness.
