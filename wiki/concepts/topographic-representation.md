# Topographic Representation

## Summary

Topographic representation is the orderly mapping of sensory receptor surfaces (such as the body surface, retina, or cochlea) onto successive stages of the nervous system. This organization preserves spatial relationships, creating somatotopic (body), retinotopic (retina), and tonotopic (sound frequency) maps.

## Key facts

- The receptor surface is represented topographically in early stages of each sensory system, with adjacent receptors in the periphery represented by adjacent neurons in the CNS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p393]
- Topographic maps are refined by sensory experience and can be modified by learning or following injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p393]
