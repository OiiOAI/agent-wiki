# Conscious Feeling of Fear

## Summary

LeDoux argues that conscious feelings of fear are not produced directly by survival circuits but rather emerge when nonconscious threat-processing ingredients are cognitively interpreted in consciousness. Fear is a conscious state that emerges when defensive survival circuit activation is cognitively assembled with relevant memories and awareness that the threat affects oneself.

## Key facts

- LeDoux argues that conscious feelings of fear are not produced directly by survival circuits but emerge through cognitive interpretation of their nonconscious consequences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p77]
- To experience fear is to know that YOU are in danger—this involvement of the self is a defining feature of fear and anxiety. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p32]
- Feelings like fear require that we have the concept of fear, based on words and their extended meanings, acquired through language and cultural experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p77]
- Animals cannot label and interpret survival circuit activity in the ways made possible by human language, so their experiences may differ from human fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p83]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because conscious fear requires cognitive interpretation, studying nonconscious threat processing in animals does not directly reveal the nature of conscious fear feelings.
