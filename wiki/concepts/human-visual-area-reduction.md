# Human Visual Area Reduction

## Summary

Human visual areas are disproportionately smaller than predictions extrapolated from the trend observed in other primates. This finding was first shown by Holloway (1979), who noted that human visual areas deviated from the primate allometric trend. The posterior shift of the lunate sulcus (marking the anterior boundary of primary visual cortex) in australopithecine endocasts was interpreted by Holloway as indicating proportional reduction of visual areas similar to modern humans. However, Deacon argues this shift may result from upright posture changes rather than neural reorganization.
