# Four-Day Work Week

## Summary

The four-day work week is a workplace reform that reduces working hours from five days to four while maintaining the same pay. Research, particularly from Perpetual Guardian in New Zealand, demonstrates that this change can maintain or improve productivity while significantly reducing stress and improving employees' ability to focus.

## Key facts

- Perpetual Guardian found that after implementing a four-day week, social media use at work fell by 35 percent, while engagement and teamwork went up by 30 to 40 percent, and stress levels dropped by 15 percent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p204]
- In 1920s Britain, W.G. Kellogg cut staff from an eight-hour day to a six-hour day, and workplace accidents fell by 41 percent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p205]
- In 2019, Microsoft Japan reported a 40 percent improvement in productivity with a four-day week. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p205]
- Toyota in Gothenburg cut two hours per day off the workweek, and mechanics produced 114 percent of what they had before, with profits going up by 25 percent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistent findings across different countries and time periods suggest that reduced working hours improve focus through multiple mechanisms: better rest, reduced stress, and more time for recovery.
