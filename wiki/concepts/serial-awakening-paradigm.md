# Serial awakening paradigm

## Summary

A methodology where participants are awakened throughout the night and asked to report whether they had been experiencing anything prior to the awakening. The study used high-density EEG recordings and standardized questionnaires to assess dream content, distinguishing between dreaming with recall (DE), dreaming without recall (DEWR), and no experience (NE). This approach enables within-state comparison of neural activity associated with conscious versus unconscious experiences.

## Key facts

- Experiment 1 studied 32 naive subjects with 233 total awakenings, while Experiment 2 studied 7 trained subjects with 815 awakenings across multiple overnight sessions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Upon awakening, subjects were asked about their mental activity just before the alarm sound, describing the most recent content and rating it on thought-like vs. perceptual dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Subjects estimated dream duration and reported specific content categories including faces, spatial settings, movement, and speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Experiment 3 used real-time bispectral thresholding (low-frequency 0.5-4.5 Hz and high-frequency 18-25 Hz) to predict dream experience before awakening. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p6]
