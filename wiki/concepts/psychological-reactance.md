# Psychological Reactance

## Summary

Psychological reactance theory explains how threats to or elimination of personal freedoms increase the desirability of the restricted item. This motivation to reclaim lost freedoms drives many behaviors from childhood 'terrible twos' defiance through teenage rebellion to adult responses to censorship and scarcity.

## Key facts

- Psychological reactance emerges around age two when children first recognize themselves as separate individuals with choices. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p221]
- Toddlers reached obstructed toys three times faster than unobstructed toys when barriers were big enough to be true obstacles. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p221]
- The "Romeo and Juliet effect" was confirmed: couples with parental interference reported greater love and desire for marriage than those without, and romantic feelings cooled when interference weakened. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p223]
- When phosphate detergents were banned in Dade County, Miami residents rated them as gentler, more effective, and better whiteners than Tampa residents, despite no actual product changes. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p225]
- University of North Carolina students became more opposed to coed dorms after learning an opposing speech would be banned, without ever hearing it. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p226]

## Inferences

- Inference: Scarcity's power comes from two sources: our learned shortcut that rare things are more valuable, and psychological reactance when our freedom to obtain something is limited.
