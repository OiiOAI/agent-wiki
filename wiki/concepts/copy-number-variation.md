# Copy Number Variation

## Summary

Copy number variation describes genomic deletions and duplications involving up to 100 consecutive genes on a chromosome. Once underappreciated, copy number variants are now recognized as a significant source of genetic variation in humans. Approximately 10% of autistic patients carry de novo gene copy number variants that neither parent carries, caused by sporadic mutations of genomic structure in the germline.

## Key facts

- Copy number variation describes genomic deletions and duplications of up to 100 consecutive genes on a chromosome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1478]
- Copy number variation has emerged as an important genetic mechanism in autism, with 10% of autistic patients carrying de novo gene copy numbers that neither parent carries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1478]
- De novo copy number variants are caused by sporadic mutations of genomic structure in the germline cells that give rise to sperm and ova. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1478]
- Although copy number variants are almost always inherited, they may explain difficulties in identifying autism-susceptibility genes in genetic studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1478]
