# Irrationality

## Summary

The Law of Irrationality describes how humans are largely unaware of how deeply emotions dominate them, causing them to veer toward ideas that soothe their ego, seek confirmation of existing beliefs, and see what they want to see depending on mood. The book identifies two grades of irrationality: low-grade (continual emotional effects on thinking through biases) and high-grade (explosive emotional reactions triggered by external factors).

## Key facts

- Humans are largely unaware of how deeply emotions dominate them, creating a disconnect from reality that is the source of bad decisions. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p25]
- The book identifies six mental biases that distort thinking: confirmation, conviction, appearance, group, blame, and superiority bias. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p44]
- High-grade irrationality occurs when emotions become inflamed, causing the mind to tunnel into the emotion until it culminates in rash action. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p47]
- High-grade irrationality can be triggered by childhood wounds, sudden gains or losses, rising pressure, inflaming individuals, and group effects. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p48]
- The crash of 2008 is presented as an example of collective irrationality driven by emotional infection rather than external factors. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p36]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book suggests that irrationality is partly a function of brain structure—the split between the limbic brain (emotion) and neocortex (cognition) that evolved over millions of years.
