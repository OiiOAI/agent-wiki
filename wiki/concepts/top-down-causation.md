# Top-down causation

## Summary

Top-down causation is the principle that the way a system behaves depends on how it is configured, which can constrain lower-level components and functionally select among patterns of those components. This principle allows for causation beyond the bottom-up determination of classical physics. In living organisms, the configuration itself is shaped by selection over millennia and by individual experience over a lifetime. Top-down causation is instantiated through 'criterial causation' where the criteria for how neurons respond to inputs are set over multiple timescales by evolution, experience, goals, and moment-to-moment processes.

## Key facts

- Top-down causation means the way a system is configured can constrain lower-level components and select among patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p181]
- In living organisms, this configuration is shaped by selection over millennia and by individual experience over a lifetime. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p181]
- Causation in living systems is not wholly instantaneous - organisms act on information from their history embodied in their structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p185]
- Abstract entities like thoughts and beliefs can have causal influence through 'criterial causation' - the criteria for response set over evolutionary and experiential timescales. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p212]

## Inferences

- Inference: Top-down causation does not violate physical law - it is demonstrated in everyday computer programs where abstract programs impose constraints on physical electron flows.
