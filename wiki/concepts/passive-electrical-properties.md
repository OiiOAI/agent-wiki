# Passive Electrical Properties

## Summary

The passive electrical properties of neurons include membrane capacitance and membrane/axoplasmic resistance, which affect the efficiency and velocity of signal conduction. These properties determine how electrical signals spread passively through the neuron and influence action potential propagation.

## Key facts

- Membrane capacitance slows the time course of electrical signals because charge must flow onto and off the membrane capacitance before the voltage can change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
- Membrane and axoplasmic resistance affect the efficiency of signal conduction through their influence on how far current can flow passively down an axon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
- Large axons are more easily excited than small axons because they have lower internal (axoplasmic) resistance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
- Passive membrane properties and axon diameter affect the velocity of action potential propagation, with myelination greatly increasing conduction velocity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
