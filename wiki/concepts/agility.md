# Agility (Boyd's Definition)

## Summary

Boyd defined agility specifically as the ability to rapidly change one's orientation—essentially one's worldview—in response to what is happening in the external world. This differs from conventional meanings of agility as mere physical speed or flexibility. In Boyd's framework, agility is the output of maneuver conflict philosophy, while the organizational climate attributes form the input. The key is not physical velocity but the quickness of the entire OODA cycle, particularly the time it takes to transition from one orientation state to another. Agility enables organizations to keep their orientation well-matched to reality during times of ambiguity, confusion, and rapid change when the natural tendency is to become disoriented.

## Inferences

- Inference: Since agility specifically concerns changing orientation rather than just moving faster, organizations should focus on decision-making processes and mental adaptability rather than purely physical or technological speed.
- Inference: The concept implies that disorientation under stress is the natural state that must be actively counteracted, making orientation management the core strategic challenge.
- Inference: The chess analogy (playing with doubled moves) illustrates how a smaller, more agile competitor can overcome larger disadvantages through speed.
