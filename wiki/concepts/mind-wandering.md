# Mind Wandering

## Summary

A spontaneous form of cognition characterized by thoughts unrelated to the current task. Approximately 30% of thoughts in daily life are classified as mind wandering. It is associated with default network activity, defocused attention, and has been shown to provide cognitive benefits for creativity, emotional regulation, and complex decision making.

## Key facts

- Ninety-six percent of American adults report some kind of daydreaming each day, and approximately 30% of thoughts people experience in daily life can be classified as mind wandering. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p351]
- Mind wandering is associated with lowering of cognitive control and a defocusing of attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p356]
- When creative tasks are preceded by a period of distraction, the generated ideas are less obvious and more creative compared with those generated after deliberate thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p368]
- Mind wandering can provide effective emotional regulation and the opportunity to formulate an alternate perspective in assessing emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p368]
- Jonathan Smallwood found that the more you let your mind wander, the better you are at having organized personal goals, being creative, and making patient, long-term decisions. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p104]
- Nathan Spreng explained that mind-wandering allows 'more extended trains of thought to unfold, which allows for more associations to be made,' leading to creative insights. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p105]
- Harvard scientists Dan Gilbert and Matthew Killingsworth found that when people are mind-wandering, they rank themselves as less happy than during almost any other activity — 'A wandering mind is an unhappy mind.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p108]
- However, mind-wandering only becomes torment in situations of high stress or danger — in conditions of safety and low stress, it can be a 'gift, a pleasure, a creative force.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p109]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mind wandering may represent the subjective experience of ongoing off-line processing, including reactivation and recombination of recent experiences during memory consolidation.
- Inference: The cognitive benefits of spontaneous thought for complex decisions may be due to its broader focus, allowing larger amounts of information to be integrated.
- Inference: The paradox of mind-wandering — beneficial for cognition but often unpleasant — suggests that our distracted culture may be preventing the very mental processes that would help us thrive.
