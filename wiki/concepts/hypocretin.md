# Hypocretin

## Summary

Hypocretin (also known as orexin) is a neuropeptide produced by a small cluster of neurons in the hypothalamus. Hypocretin-1 and hypocretin-2 (orexin A and B) are transmitted to brain regions responsible for REM sleep and wakefulness, including the locus coeruleus. Loss of hypocretin-producing neurons causes narcolepsy.

## Key facts

- Hypocretin-1 and -2 (also called orexin A and B) are neuropeptides produced in a small cluster of neurons within the hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1207]
- Hypocretin neurons project widely throughout the brain, including to brain stem nuclei responsible for REM sleep such as the locus coeruleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1207]
- Activation of hypocretin receptors in the locus coeruleus and other neurons has a slow excitatory effect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1207]
- Narcoleptic dogs have a defect in the gene coding for the hypocretin-2 receptor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1207]
- CSF hypocretin levels are remarkably reduced in narcoleptic humans even though the hypocretin genes are not mutated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1207]
