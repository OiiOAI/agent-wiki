# The Hard Problem

## Summary

The Hard Problem, coined by David Chalmers in the early 1990s, asks why physical processes give rise to rich inner life at all—why there is something it is like to have conscious experiences. It contrasts with 'easy problems' that explain functional and behavioural properties. The hard problem questions why experience accompanies physical processing, even after all functional explanations are complete.

## Key facts

- The term 'hard problem' was coined by Australian philosopher David Chalmers in the early 1990s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p23]
- The hard problem asks why physical processing gives rise to a rich inner life at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p23]
- Chalmers contrasts the hard problem with 'easy problems' concerning functional and behavioural properties like sensory processing, action selection, and attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p24]
- Even after explaining all functional properties, a further unanswered question remains: why is the performance of these functions accompanied by experience?. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p24]
