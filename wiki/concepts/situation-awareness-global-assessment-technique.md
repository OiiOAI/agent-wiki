# Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique

## Summary

SAGAT (Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique) is a measurement methodology developed by Endsley (1988) that involves freezing a simulation and asking systematic, preplanned questions about the state of the task or environment. The technique uses computer administration to assure control of question delivery, with questions drawn at random from a pre-developed set. SAGAT provides rich data about aggregate levels of specific SA classes and is useful for assessing individual differences in SA and comparing displays designed to support SA at an aggregate level. However, the randomness of questions does not allow evaluation of acquisition of specific SA features that might require using a particular display.

## Key facts

- SAGAT was developed by Endsley in 1988 and formalizes the freeze technique for assessing SA during simulation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p58]
- The technique uses computer administration of SA questions during simulation freeze to assure control of question administration. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p58]
- Questions are specific and drawn at random from a predeveloped set at each sampling point. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p58]
- SAGAT cannot answer specific questions about SA at particular critical points in a scenario due to randomized questioning. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p58]
- The technique is useful for assessing aggregate individual differences in SA and may be diagnostic of selection or training needs. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p58]
- Because probes are randomized, it is difficult to assign SA failure to a specific display or system without corroborating information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p58]
- Endsley (1995b) found no significant differences in performance between subjects who received probes and those who did not, using between-subjects design. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p59]

## Inferences

- Inference: The primary value of SAGAT is in aggregate comparisons across treatment conditions rather than identifying specific SA failures at critical moments
