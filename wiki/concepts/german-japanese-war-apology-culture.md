# German and Japanese War Apology Culture

## Summary

A comparison of how Germany and Japan have addressed their wartime atrocities, examining formal apologies, public gestures of contrition, and the treatment of revisionist figures who deny historical atrocities.

## Key facts

- Until 1993, no Japanese prime minister had formally apologized for Japan's role in World War II. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p436]
- No Japanese politician had made a gesture comparable to Willy Brandt falling to his hands and knees in contrition for the Holocaust. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p436]
- In Germany, Holocaust denial revisionists are regarded as part of a crackpot fringe. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p436]
- In Japan, respectable politicians like Shintaro Ishihara and academics like Soiichi Watanabe have denied that the Nanking Massacre was an atrocity. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p436]
- Germany has had liberal asylum laws but it remains extremely difficult to become a German citizen, with long-term Turkish residents not being considered 'real Germans.'. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p436]
- Ian Buruma wrote 'The Wages of Guilt: Memories of War in Germany and Japan' (1994), comparing German and Japanese memories of war. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p436]

## Inferences

- Inference: The contrast suggests different cultural approaches to collective guilt and national identity, with Germany more willing to accept international scrutiny of its wartime behavior.
