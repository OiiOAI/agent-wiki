# Loss Aversion

## Summary

Loss aversion is a behavioral economics concept describing humans' tendency to avoid losing something than to acquire something of equal value. Michalowicz uses this concept to explain why entrepreneurs cling to unsustainable expenses (like luxury cars) rather than making the painful but necessary cuts to achieve financial freedom.

## Key facts

- Michalowicz states: 'It is common for us emotional humans to give up the stuff we can no longer afford by small degrees. We cling because we hate loss. More specifically, we have a far greater desire to avoid losing something than we have to acquire something.'. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p188]
- The Endowment Effect (placing higher value on what we possess) combined with loss aversion creates 'a stubbornness resembling that of a three-year-old in a tug-of-war for a beloved blankie.'. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p188]
- Loss aversion gives rise to risk aversion for mixed (gain-loss) prospects, typically requiring at least twice as much upside as downside to accept such gambles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p143]
- Willingness to accept (WTA) to part with an object generally exceeds willingness to pay (WTP) to obtain the same object, a pattern robust in laboratory studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p145]
- Rabin (2000) showed that a concave utility function over states of wealth cannot explain the normal range of risk aversion for mixed gambles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p143]
- Loss aversion contributes to the inertial tendency to stick with status quo options and the reluctance to trade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p145]
- The coefficient of loss aversion λ was estimated at 2.25 by Tversky and Kahneman (1992). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p149]

## Inferences

- Inference: Loss aversion should be distinguished from convexity of the value function for losses: loss aversion drives risk aversion for mixed prospects, while convexity drives risk seeking for pure loss prospects.
