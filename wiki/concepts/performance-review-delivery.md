# Performance Review Delivery

## Summary

The optimal timing for delivering written performance reviews relative to face-to-face discussions. Grove recommends giving written reviews before discussions so subordinates can read, digest, and prepare emotionally and rationally.

## Key facts

- Giving the written review before the face-to-face discussion is recommended because the subordinate can read the whole thing privately, digest it, react or overreact, and look at the messages again before meeting. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1796-L1800]
- If given the review first, a subordinate may find a phrase he didn't 'hear' earlier and blow up over it. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1782-L1784]
- Delivering the written review during the discussion leaves no time for the subordinate to think about what it says, causing him to walk away muttering about what he should have said. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1789-L1792]
- Grove reads approximately a hundred evaluations annually, commenting and sending them back for rewrites or complimentary notes. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1803-L1805]

## Inferences

- Inference: The face-to-face discussion is meant to be a 'meeting of minds' where both parties can engage meaningfully.
- Inference: Written reviews serve as preparation material for the more important verbal exchange.
