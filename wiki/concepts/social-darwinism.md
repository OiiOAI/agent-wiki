# Social Darwinism

## Summary

Social Darwinism is an application of Darwinian ideas about natural selection to society. Herbert Spencer coined the phrase 'survival of the fittest' and argued that society should be left to evolve without interference. William Graham Sumner was the major exponent of Social Darwinism in the United States.

## Key facts

- Spencer was a Social Darwinist who held the evolutionary view that the world was growing progressively better and should be left alone. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p72]
- Spencer adopted the Darwinian view that a process of natural selection, 'survival of the fittest,' occurred in the social world. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p73]
- William Graham Sumner was the major exponent of Social Darwinism in the United States. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p83]
- Social Darwinism came to be regarded as a crude legitimation of competitive capitalism and the status quo. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p84]
