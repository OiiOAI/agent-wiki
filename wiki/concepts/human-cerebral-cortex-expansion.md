# Human Cerebral Cortex Expansion

## Summary

The human brain contains approximately 1000-fold more neurons than mouse brains and 100-fold more than rhesus macaques. This expansion is due in part to a large pool of neuronal progenitors including outer radial glia, which lack contact with the ventricular surface but can generate neurons. Cerebral organoids generated from iPSCs reveal species-specific features of human cortical development.

## Key facts

- The primate cortex has larger size, more discrete areas, more layers, and higher neuronal packing density compared to rodents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1141]
- A main contributor to cortical expansion in primates is outer radial glia, a second type of radial glial cell that lacks contact with the ventricular surface but can generate neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1142]
- Human cerebral organoids contain a bilayered, large subventricular zone with numerous outer radial glia, whereas mouse organoids have a smaller subventricular zone with predominantly inner radial glia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1143]
- Organoids derived from patients with cortical malformations such as lissencephaly may be used to elucidate disease mechanisms and test therapies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1143]
