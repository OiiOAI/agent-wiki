# Commoditization and Industry Structure

## Summary

Munger contrasts industries where brands and consolidation lead to profitability (like cereals) versus commodity businesses where competition destroys returns (like airlines). He examines why some competitive markets yield good returns while others become destructively competitive.

## Key facts

- The net amount of money made by airline shareholders since Kitty Hawk is now a negative figure. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p176]
- Cereal companies make fifteen to fifty percent on capital despite appearing to compete crazily with promotions and coupons. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p176]
- Brand identity in cereals must account for much of the difference from airlines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p176]
