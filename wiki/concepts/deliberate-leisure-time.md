# Deliberate Use of Leisure Time

## Summary

The practice of scheduling leisure activities in advance rather than defaulting to entertainment websites, as advocated by Arnold Bennett in 1910.

## Key facts

- Bennett proposed that the sixteen hours outside work should be seen as a 'day within a day' used for self-improvement, similar to how an aristocrat would. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p123]
- Addictive entertainment sites thrive in a vacuum: if you haven't planned something for a given moment, they beckon as an appealing option. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p125]
- Structured hobbies provide good fodder for leisure time as they generate specific actions with specific goals. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p125]
- Bennett argued that mental faculties are capable of continuous hard activity and want change, not rest. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p125]

## Inferences

- Inference: Filling leisure time with quality alternatives to addictive websites preserves the ability to resist distraction and concentrate while potentially leading to greater life fulfillment.
