# Anosognosia

## Summary

A neuropsychological condition in which patients with left-side paralysis from right hemisphere damage are unaware of their deficit. Beyond denial of paralysis, these patients show impaired emotion, feeling, and decision-making, sharing the core features of the Gage-Elliot syndrome. The condition results from damage to somatosensory cortices in the right hemisphere that map the body's current state.

## Key facts

- Anosognosia denotes the inability to acknowledge disease in oneself, as when a patient with left-side paralysis reports feeling fine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p82]
- Anosognosia for left-side paralysis occurs specifically with right hemisphere damage affecting somatosensory cortices including the insula, areas 3, 1, 2, and S2, and their connections to thalamus, basal ganglia, and prefrontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p85]
- Patients with anosognosia receive catastrophic news (stroke, cancer) with equanimity rather than anguish, lacking emotional response to their dire situation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p84]
- Anosognosic patients resemble prefrontal-damaged patients in being unable to make appropriate personal and social decisions and showing unusual tolerance for pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p87]
- Insight into illness generally is dependent on the right hemisphere, and those with damage to the right hemisphere tend to deny their illness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p126]
- A patient with a completely paralysed limb may refuse to accept that there is anything wrong with it, and will come up with the most preposterous explanations for why he is not able to move it on request. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p126]
- Denial of illness can be temporarily reversed by activating the affected right hemisphere, and denial of illness can be induced by anaesthetising the right hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p127]
- This happens to some degree in the majority of cases after a stroke affecting the left side of the body (involving right-hemisphere damage), but practically never after a right-sided stroke. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p126]
- Anosognosia denotes the inability to recognize a state of disease in one's own organism; patients with left-side paralysis may sincerely report they are fine despite being entirely paralyzed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p210]
- Anosognosia occurs with right-hemisphere damage, affecting the insula, cytoarchitectonic areas 3, 1, 2 in the parietal region, and area S2, disrupting connections with thalamus, basal ganglia, and motor and prefrontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p211]
- Anosognosia results from inability to represent current body states through somatosensory systems, compromising the highest level of integrated organism representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p212]
- Core consciousness is preserved in anosognosia because second-order accounts can still be created from lower-level proto-self representations in the brain stem, but autobiographical memory cannot be fully updated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p212]
- Anosognosia is a hybrid disorder affecting autobiographical self and extended consciousness while partially impairing the proto-self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p213]

## Inferences

- Inference: The right hemisphere dominance for integrated body sense explains why right-side paralysis (left hemisphere damage) does not cause anosognosia—language dominance in the left hemisphere requires different bilateral processing
- Inference: Anosognosia is not merely a failure of perception but a wilful denial, suggesting the left hemisphere's tendency to confabulate explanations when it cannot acknowledge a problem.
- Inference: The denial in anosognosia is not psychodynamically motivated but neurobiologically determined, as patients with left-hemisphere damage to the same regions do not develop anosognosia.
