# Anosognosia

## Summary

A neuropsychological condition in which patients with left-side paralysis from right hemisphere damage are unaware of their deficit. Beyond denial of paralysis, these patients show impaired emotion, feeling, and decision-making, sharing the core features of the Gage-Elliot syndrome. The condition results from damage to somatosensory cortices in the right hemisphere that map the body's current state.

## Key facts

- Anosognosia denotes the inability to acknowledge disease in oneself, as when a patient with left-side paralysis reports feeling fine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p82]
- Anosognosia for left-side paralysis occurs specifically with right hemisphere damage affecting somatosensory cortices including the insula, areas 3, 1, 2, and S2, and their connections to thalamus, basal ganglia, and prefrontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p85]
- Patients with anosognosia receive catastrophic news (stroke, cancer) with equanimity rather than anguish, lacking emotional response to their dire situation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p84]
- Anosognosic patients resemble prefrontal-damaged patients in being unable to make appropriate personal and social decisions and showing unusual tolerance for pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p87]

## Inferences

- Inference: The right hemisphere dominance for integrated body sense explains why right-side paralysis (left hemisphere damage) does not cause anosognosia—language dominance in the left hemisphere requires different bilateral processing
