# Child Analysis

## Summary

Freud describes the application of psychoanalysis to children, noting that child analysis has become the domain of women analysts. He discusses the differences from adult analysis—the child's lack of superego, limited free association, and different transference dynamics. He proposes prophylactic analysis of teachers as more efficacious than analysis of children and essential for the difficult task of education.

## Key facts

- Freud states that a child is a very favourable subject for analytic therapy with thorough and lasting results, though technique must be largely altered from adults. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6974]
- Freud notes that children possess no superego initially, and free association does not carry far with them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6974]
- Freud observes that child analysis has automatically become the domain of women analysts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6975]
- Freud proposes that the only appropriate preparation for the profession of educator is thorough psychoanalytic training, ideally including personal analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6976]
- Freud suggests the analysis of teachers and educators is a more efficacious prophylactic measure than the analysis of children themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6976]

## Inferences
