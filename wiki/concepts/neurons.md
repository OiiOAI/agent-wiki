# Neurons

## Summary

Neurons are highly specialized cells for the generation and conduction of electrical signals, connected by synapses that transmit chemical signals. They possess a polarized structure with dendrites for receiving signals, an axon for sending signals, and a cell body containing the nucleus. This architecture enables complex information processing, integration of multiple inputs, and coordinated output across neural networks.

## Key facts

- Neurons are highly specialized for the generation and conduction of electrical signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p99]
- They communicate with each other through the release and detection of neurotransmitters at specialized structures called synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p100]
- Neurons send long, thin cellular projections that branch to form treelike structures enabling connections with many other neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p100]
- Dendrites are specialized for receiving signals and axons for sending signals, creating a polarized input/output structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p100]
- Branching dendrites collect signals from many cells, allowing neurons to perform integrative operations on incoming information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p100]
