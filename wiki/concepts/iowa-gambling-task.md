# Iowa Gambling Task

## Summary

The Iowa Gambling Task (IGT) is a widely used behavioral task sensitive to ventromedial frontal lobe damage. Participants choose from four decks of cards with different reward and punishment profiles, requiring them to learn to avoid high-risk, long-term loss-making decks. Performance on the IGT is impaired in schizophrenia and VMF patients, reflecting deficits in reward learning under uncertainty.

## Key facts

- Reward-based decision-making on the IGT is impaired in psychosis and this effect is sensitive to medication status. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p253]
- When the requirement to overcome an initial bias to high-reward, high-risk options is removed from the IGT, VMF patients perform as well as healthy controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p281]
- The IGT shares with reversal learning tasks the requirement to flexibly update behavior in response to shifting reward contingencies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p281]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The IGT requires several cognitive processes in addition to reward sensitivity, including working memory and executive function, making it difficult to isolate specific reinforcement learning deficits.
