# Iowa Gambling Task

## Summary

The Iowa Gambling Task (IGT) is a widely used behavioral task sensitive to ventromedial frontal lobe damage. Participants choose from four decks of cards with different reward and punishment profiles, requiring them to learn to avoid high-risk, long-term loss-making decks. Performance on the IGT is impaired in schizophrenia and VMF patients, reflecting deficits in reward learning under uncertainty.

## Key facts

- Reward-based decision-making on the IGT is impaired in psychosis and this effect is sensitive to medication status. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p253]
- When the requirement to overcome an initial bias to high-reward, high-risk options is removed from the IGT, VMF patients perform as well as healthy controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p281]
- The IGT shares with reversal learning tasks the requirement to flexibly update behavior in response to shifting reward contingencies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p281]
- The Iowa gambling task was developed by Bechara, Damasio, Damasio, and Anderson in 1994 to detect decision-making impairment in VM patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p108]
- The task is carried out in real-time and resembles real-world contingencies using reward and punishment (winning and losing money). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p108]
- The task creates a conflict between an immediate, luring reward and a delayed, probabilistic punishment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p108]
- The task successfully detected the decision-making impairment in VM patients that was not revealed by standard neuropsychological tests. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p108]
- The way to do well on the task is to follow hunches and gut feelings, reflecting the role of emotion in advantageous decision making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p108]
- In the IGT, decks A and B yield $100 per pick but encounter higher punishments ($150-$350 per 10 picks), while decks C and D yield $50 but lower punishments, making A/B disadvantageous and C/D advantageous. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p113]
- Normal subjects avoided bad decks A and B and preferred good decks C and D, while VM patients did not avoid bad decks and indeed preferred them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p115]
- Normal subjects generated anticipatory SCRs before selecting from risky decks A and B, while VM patients failed to generate these anticipatory responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p116]
- Only 70% of normal controls reached the conceptual period (understanding task contingencies), yet 30% who did not still performed advantageously, suggesting implicit learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p120]
- 50% of VM patients reached the conceptual period and stated explicitly which decks were good/bad, yet still performed disadvantageously, showing knowledge-action dissociation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p120]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The IGT requires several cognitive processes in addition to reward sensitivity, including working memory and executive function, making it difficult to isolate specific reinforcement learning deficits.
