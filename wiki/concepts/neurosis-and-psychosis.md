# Neurosis and Psychosis: Genetic Formulation

## Summary

In 'Neurosis and Psychosis' (1924), Freud applied the structural model of ego, id, and superego to formulate the fundamental genetic distinction between neuroses and psychoses: neurosis results from conflict between the ego and the id (repression of instinctual demands), while psychosis results from conflict between the ego and the external world (dissociation from reality). Freud further proposed that a third group—narcissistic neuroses such as melancholia—arise from conflict between the ego and the superego. All three psychoneuroses share a common etiology: frustration of childhood wishes, always rooted in external reality but mediated through the superego. The outcome depends on economic factors (relative magnitudes of conflicting forces) and the ego's capacity to negotiate between its various dependencies.

## Key facts

- Freud proposed that neurosis is the result of conflict between the ego and its id, while psychosis is the outcome of disturbance in the relations between the ego and the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6034]
- Freud proposed a third category—narcissistic neuroses (e.g., melancholia)—arising from conflict between the ego and the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6036]
- The common etiology of all psychoneuroses is frustration of deeply rooted childhood wishes, always externally caused but mediated through the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6035]
- In Meynert's amentia (acute hallucinatory confusion), the ego neither accepts current perceptions nor retains the significance of its internal world, creating autocratically a new external and internal world governed by the id's wishful impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6035]
- Freud noted that the ego can avoid rupture by 'deforming itself'—a concept anticipating the later theory of ego cleavage, visible in perversions and certain pathological character traits. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6036]
