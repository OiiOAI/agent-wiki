# How to Write a Review

## Summary

Reviews of papers or books should contain a summary with details, evaluation, and opinion about who should read the work. Winston recommends using the VSN-C framework to shape the summary and emphasizes including specific details because details sell. The reviewer should be specific about exactly who should read the work so readers feel precisely described and are more likely to be persuaded.

## Key facts

- A review of a paper or book should include a summary with details, plus your evaluation and your opinion about who should read the entire work. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p339]
- You should use VSN-C (Vision, Steps, News, Contributions) to shape your summary with artistic license about which elements to include. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p339]
- When a work has merit, be specific about exactly who should read it—the more precisely described a reader feels, the more likely they are to be persuaded. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p340]
- Details sell; effective reviews mention specific years, places, and concrete details rather than vague descriptions. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p340]
