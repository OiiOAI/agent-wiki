# The Shadow

## Summary

The Shadow refers to the parts of one's personality that are denied, repressed, or concealed from conscious awareness. According to Greene, emphatic traits often rest on top of their opposite traits, with the underlying weakness or antisocial trait constituting a key component of the Shadow. The deeper the repression, the greater the volatility of the Shadow, which eventually surfaces through moods, obsessions, secret vices, and self-destructive behavior under stress or with age.

## Key facts

- The emphatic trait generally rests on top of the opposite trait, distracting and concealing it from public view. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p307]
- Some people unconsciously develop the opposite trait as a resilience or toughness that lies on the outside like a protective shell, to disguise underlying softness, vulnerability, or insecurity. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p307]
- The deeper the repression, the greater the volatility of the Shadow. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p307]
- Carl Jung wrote: 'Everyone carries a shadow, and the less it is embodied in the individual's conscious life, the blacker and denser it is.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p321]
