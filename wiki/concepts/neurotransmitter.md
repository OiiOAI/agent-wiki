# Neurotransmitter

## Summary

Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers that allow neurons to communicate across synapses. The brain's primary neurotransmitters include glutamate (excitatory), GABA (inhibitory), acetylcholine (learning and memory), serotonin (mood), dopamine (reward and motivation), and norepinephrine (focus and attention). Dysfunction in neurotransmitter systems is associated with various neurological and psychiatric conditions, and optimizing neurotransmitter function through lifestyle factors is central to cognitive health.

## Key facts

- Glutamate is the brain's chief excitatory neurotransmitter, used by more than half of all neurons, and is involved in learning, memory, and synaptogenesis. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p275]
- GABA is the chief inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain, used in 30 to 40 percent of synapses brain-wide, and is associated with a calming effect dubbed 'nature's Valium'. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p275]
- Acetylcholine is a neurotransmitter involved in REM sleep, learning, and memory, with low levels associated with Alzheimer's disease. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p279]
- The brain's primary neurotransmitters are synthesized from amino acids: glutamate from glutamine, GABA from glutamate, acetylcholine from choline, serotonin from tryptophan, dopamine and norepinephrine from tyrosine. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p279]
- The COMT gene produces an enzyme that breaks down dopamine in the prefrontal cortex, with variations affecting whether someone is more of a 'worrier' (A allele) or 'warrior' (G allele). [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p294]
