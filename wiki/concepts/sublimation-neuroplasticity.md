# Sublimation

## Summary

Sublimation is the process by which brute animal instincts are civilized through brain plasticity. When instinctual brain areas are linked to cognitive-cerebral areas through plastic brain changes, they form new wholes that temper each other. This explains how activities like competitive sports, chess, and art allow predatory instincts to be expressed in modified, socially acceptable forms. The plastic brain enables neurons from instinctual parts to be literally wired together with intellectual areas and pleasure centers.

## Key facts

- Areas that evolved to perform hunter-gatherer tasks such as stalking prey can be sublimated into competitive games because our brains evolved to link different neuronal groups and modules in novel ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p301]
- There is no reason why neurons from instinctual parts of our brains cannot be linked to our more cognitive-cerebral ones and to our pleasure centers, so that they literally get wired together to form new wholes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p301]
- When an instinct is linked to a civilized activity, the two activities temper each other—playing chess is no longer about bloodthirsty stalking, though it retains some of the exciting emotions of the hunt. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p301]
- Civilization is a series of techniques in which the hunter-gatherer brain teaches itself to rewire itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p301]
- Freud believed civilization rests on the partial inhibition of sexual and aggressive instincts, and that we could go too far in repressing our instincts, leading to neuroses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p300]
