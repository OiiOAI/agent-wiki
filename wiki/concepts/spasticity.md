# Spasticity

## Summary

Spasticity is a neurological condition characterized by hyperactive tendon reflexes and increased resistance to rapid muscle stretching. It results from damage to descending motor pathways caused by stroke, spinal cord injury, traumatic brain injury, or diseases like multiple sclerosis. Spasticity develops over days to weeks following lesions and involves plastic changes at multiple sites in stretch reflex circuitry, including increased neurotransmitter release from sensory Ia axons and changes in motor neuron properties.

## Key facts

- Spasticity is characterized by hyperactive tendon jerks and increased resistance to rapid stretching, with active reflex contraction occurring only during rapid stretch. - Spasticity is seen following lesions of descending motor pathways from stroke, brain or spinal cord injuries, and degenerative diseases including multiple sclerosis. - Spasticity develops over days to weeks following lesions, paralleling plastic changes including increased transmitter release from Ia axons and motor neuron hyperexcitability.
