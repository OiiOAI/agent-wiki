# Spasticity

## Summary

Spasticity is a neurological condition characterized by hyperactive tendon reflexes and increased resistance to rapid muscle stretching. It results from damage to descending motor pathways caused by stroke, spinal cord injury, traumatic brain injury, or diseases like multiple sclerosis. Spasticity develops over days to weeks following lesions and involves plastic changes at multiple sites in stretch reflex circuitry, including increased neurotransmitter release from sensory Ia axons and changes in motor neuron properties.

## Key facts

- Spasticity is characterized by hyperactive tendon jerks and increased resistance to rapid stretching, with active reflex contraction occurring only during rapid stretch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Spasticity is seen following lesions of descending motor pathways from stroke, brain or spinal cord injuries, and degenerative diseases including multiple sclerosis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Spasticity develops over days to weeks following lesions, paralleling plastic changes including increased transmitter release from Ia axons and motor neuron hyperexcitability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Spasticity results in strong facilitation of transmission in the monosynaptic reflex pathway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Spasticity is primarily a phasic phenomenon where active reflex contraction occurs only during rapid stretch; when the muscle is held lengthened the reflex contraction subsides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p861]
- The pathophysiology of spasticity involves strong facilitation of synaptic transmission in Ia sensory fibers in the monosynaptic reflex pathway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p861]
- A common therapeutic procedure today is to mimic presynaptic inhibition in Ia fiber terminals by intrathecally administering baclofen, a GABAB receptor agonist. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p861]
- Changes in the background activity of alpha motor neurons and interneurons, particularly modifications enabling sustained firing in response to brief excitatory input, are probably more important causes of spasticity than gamma motor neuron overactivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p861]
