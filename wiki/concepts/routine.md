# Routine

## Summary

Routine is the practice of designing consistent patterns of behavior that make executing essential activities the default position, reducing the need for willpower and conscious decision-making. Research shows that nearly 40 percent of daily choices are unconscious habits, and with repetition, neural pathways strengthen until activities become second nature.

## Key facts

- Michael Phelps followed the exact same pre-race routine for every competition over many years, including specific warm-up exercises, sitting positions, and visualization. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p186]
- Phelps practiced 'watching the videotape'—visualizing his perfect race in detail every night before sleep and every morning upon waking. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p187]
- Charles Duhigg explained that once mental work shifts to the basal ganglia, the brain can work almost completely automatically, freeing mental space for other tasks. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p190]
- Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi found that highly creative people use strict routines to free their minds from demands on attention. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p191]

## Inferences

- Inference: The power of routine lies in its ability to conserve willpower—once an essential activity is automated through repetition, executing it requires no conscious effort or self-control.
