# Strategic Objective

## Summary

A Strategic Objective is a very clear statement of what a business must ultimately do to enable the owner to achieve their Primary Aim. It includes financial standards (gross revenues, profits, exit strategy), market positioning, demographic and psychographic profiles of target customers, and standards for how the business will operate. Unlike a business plan, it is a product of the life plan.

## Key facts

- Your Strategic Objective is a very clear statement of what your business has to ultimately do for you to achieve your Primary Aim. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p150]
- There is ultimately only one reason to create a business of your own: to sell it, to do it, finish it, and get paid for it. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p152]
- An Opportunity Worth Pursuing is a business that can fulfill the financial standards created for the Primary Aim and Strategic Objective. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p153]
- The product is what the customer feels as he walks out of the business, not what he feels about the commodity. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p154]
- Charles Revson's quote: 'In the factory Revlon manufactures cosmetics, but in the store Revlon sells hope.'. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p154]
- People buy feelings, not commodities. As the world becomes more complex, the feelings customers want become more urgent, less rational, and more unconscious. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p155]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Strategic Objective bridges personal life goals and concrete business metrics, making abstract aspirations measurable.
- Inference: Understanding the difference between commodity (what customers walk out with) and product (how they feel) is fundamental to strategic positioning.
