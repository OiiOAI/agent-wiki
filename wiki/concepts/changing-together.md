# Changing Together

## Summary

Changing Together is Fogg's framework for group behavior change. He identifies two approaches: 'Ringleader' (leading and sharing methods openly) and 'Ninja' (sneaking Behavior Design in subtly). The framework emphasizes the household as the unit of change, the power of positive feedback in the 'Power Zone,' and that behavior change is not solitary—each change ripples out to shape families, communities, and societies.

## Key facts

- Fogg advocates viewing the household, not the individual, as the unit of change. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p261]
- Two ethical maxims guide helping others: (1) Help people do what they already want to do, (2) Help people feel successful. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p263]
- As the Ringleader, you share what you've learned and act together; as the Ninja, you sneak Behavior Design in subtly without others knowing. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p264]
- The Behavior Model segments people into four types: Dolphins (motivated and able), Turtles (motivated but unable), Crabs (able but unmotivated), and Clams (neither motivated nor able). [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p281]
- Focus first on Dolphins and Turtles; Crabs and Clams should be set aside or given different activities. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p281]
- Behavior Design is not a solitary pursuit; each change shapes families, communities, and societies. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p249]
- Change leads to change—start people on the path from the place they want to begin. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p262]
