# Forgetting of Dreams

## Summary

Dreams are typically forgotten upon waking due to multiple factors: their lack of logical coherence, isolation from waking memory networks, sensory dominance upon awakening overwhelming weak dream images, and the apparent absurdity or indifference of dream content.

## Key facts

- Strümpell argued that dreams are forgotten because they lack the logical connections and meaning that aid waking memory retention; dream compositions are 'barren of the qualities which would make it possible to remember them.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1324]
- Strümpell described dream structures as floating 'in psychical space like clouds in the sky, scattered by the first breath of wind' upon waking. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1324]
- Jessen warned that when recalling dreams, 'waking consciousness unwittingly makes interpolations in the memory of a dream,' filling gaps with plausible material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1325]
- Forgetting of dreams is not arbitrary but largely determined by the dream censorship and resistance against the penetration of dream thoughts into consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1762]
- Doubt whether a dream or certain details have been correctly reported is a derivative of the dream censorship, never attacking the more intense elements of a dream but only the weak and indistinct ones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1761]
- In analysing a dream, Freud insists that the whole scale of estimates of certainty shall be abandoned and the faintest possibility that something occurred in the dream shall be treated as complete certainty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1761]
- If a first account of a dream is hard to follow, asking for a repetition reveals weak spots in the dream's disguise because the patient replaces expressions that threaten to betray meaning with less revealing ones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1760]
- Forgetting of dreams depends far more upon resistance than upon the alienation between waking and sleeping states. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1763]
- Parts of dreams rescued from oblivion are invariably the most important parts, lying on the shortest road to the dream's solution. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1763]

## Inferences

- Inference: The state of sleep makes dream formation possible because it reduces the power of the endopsychic censorship. During sleep, the resistance loses some of its power, permitting dream formation; upon waking, the resistance regains full strength and proceeds to get rid of what it was obliged to permit.
- Inference: Dreams are no more forgotten than other mental acts and can be interpreted years later with the same success as recent dreams, provided the internal resistances have been overcome.
