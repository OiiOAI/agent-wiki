# The Monkey Analogy — Worthless Stones

## Summary

The monkey analogy illustrates how people trap themselves by holding on to things of no value, giving up their freedom for worthless stones. A stone is placed in a vase fixed in the ground; the monkey grabs the stone, which cannot be pulled out through the narrow neck, and the monkey remains trapped rather than releasing the worthless stone. The analogy teaches that if you allow yourself to hold on to worthless stones in life—whether routines, jobs, relationships, or familiar patterns—you may end up giving your freedom and happiness away.

## Key facts

- In the monkey analogy, to catch a monkey, a stone is pushed into a vase fixed in the ground so it only just fits through the neck; the monkey grabs the stone but cannot remove it and remains trapped. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p151]
- Even though the stone is of no value to the monkey, it will not let go and remains stubborn, allowing itself to be caught because it cannot release the worthless stone. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p151]
- If you allow yourself to hold on to worthless stones you may end up giving your freedom away—stress dominating your life because you cling to things not good for you means you must accept losing happiness. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p152]
- Worthless stones in life may be things like routines, familiar patterns of working, jobs, hobbies, places, or relationships—anything preventing you from being happy. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p152]
- The advice is to have the courage to let go of worthless stones and not cling on because of fear, familiarity, or stubbornness—your freedom and happiness are worth more than any stone. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p152]
