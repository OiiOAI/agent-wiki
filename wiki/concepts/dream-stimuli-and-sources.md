# Dream Stimuli and Sources

## Summary

Freud reviewed four main sources of dreams: external sensory stimuli, internal subjective sensory excitations, internal organic somatic stimuli, and purely psychical sources. He argued that while somatic stimuli could trigger dreams, they did not fully explain dream content, pointing to psychical determinants.

## Key facts

- External sensory stimuli during sleep (noises, temperature changes, physical sensations) can be woven into dreams, as illustrated by Maury's experimental studies where he induced dreams by applying sensory stimuli. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1306]
- Maury's guillotine dream was triggered by the headboard of his bed falling and striking his cervical vertebrae, demonstrating how somatic stimuli are interpreted symbolically in dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1308]
- Freud noted that subjective visual sensations (idioretinal light) and auditory phenomena serve as sources of dream images, as demonstrated by Ladd's experiments waking subjects during hypnagogic states. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1313]
- Freud wrote that 'les rêves d'origine absolument psychique n'existent pas' (dreams of purely psychical origin do not exist), reflecting the prevailing medical view that somatic factors must initiate dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1321]
