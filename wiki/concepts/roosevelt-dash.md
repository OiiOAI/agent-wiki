# Roosevelt Dash

## Summary

A deep work training technique where you identify a high-priority deep task and give yourself a hard deadline that drastically reduces normal estimated time, forcing intense concentration.

## Key facts

- Theodore Roosevelt at Harvard studied only about a quarter of the typical day but earned honor grades by working with blistering intensity during dedicated study periods. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p99]
- Roosevelt's 'concentration was so intense, and his reading so rapid, that he could afford more time off than most.'. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p99]
- Roosevelt dashes should be attempted no more than once a week initially to allow time for rest between sessions. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p100]
- These dashes provide interval training for attention centers of the brain and are incompatible with distraction due to tight deadlines. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p100]

## Inferences

- Inference: Roosevelt dashes leverage artificial deadlines to systematically increase the concentration level achievable, providing both training benefit and forcing resistance to distraction.
