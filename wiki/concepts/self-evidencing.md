# Self-Evidencing

## Summary

Self-evidencing is the process by which an adaptive system acts to garner sensory data consistent with its internal generative model, thereby maximizing model evidence. Coined by Hohwy (2016), it is the behavioral expression of the free energy principle in Active Inference: an agent self-evidences by engaging in action-perception loops that keep its free energy (and thus surprise) low. Self-evidencing provides a link between the free energy principle and the Bayesian brain, showing that existence itself can be understood as a process of evidence accumulation for one's own generative model. The concept implies that agents are optimistic—they assume their preferred sensory states are likely—which is necessary to prescribe active states that maintain their existence.

## Key facts

- Self-evidencing means acting to garner sensory data consistent with (i.e., affording evidence to) an internal model, hence maximizing model evidence and minimizing surprise. - The term self-evidencing was coined by Hohwy (2016) to describe the process of maximizing model evidence through action and perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p46]
- Self-evidencing implies that agents have an implicit optimism bias—they assume preferred sensory states are likely—which is necessary to prescribe active states that maintain their existence. - Any adaptive system that actively samples sensations to minimize variational free energy is equivalently a self-evidencing agent. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-evidencing provides a unified explanation for both perception and action: perception updates beliefs to better explain existing sensory data (increasing evidence for the model), while action changes which sensory data are gathered (selecting data that is more consistent with the model).
