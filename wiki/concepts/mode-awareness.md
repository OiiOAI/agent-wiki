# Mode Awareness

## Summary

Mode awareness refers to the operator's understanding of what mode an automated system is currently operating in and how that mode affects system behavior. Poor mode awareness leads to the 'What is it doing now?' and 'I wonder why it is doing that?' confusion reported by operators. Mode awareness problems result from subtle mode transitions, proliferating modes in complex systems, and poor feedback on system state.

## Key facts

- Wiener and Curry first documented mode awareness problems in pilots working with automated systems, noting comments like 'What is it doing now?' and 'I wonder why it is doing that?'. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p198]
- Mode proliferation occurs as new modes are added to deal with different requirements and provide product differentiation, increasing system complexity. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p199]
- Sarter concluded that pilots frequently fail to verify mode selections, notice automatic mode changes, or process mode annunciations in sufficient depth to understand implications for aircraft behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p199]
- Efforts to improve salience of mode transitions through other modalities (e.g., tactile cues) have been successful in reducing SA problems associated with poor awareness of mode information. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p198]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'entropic growth of complexity' in automation systems results from lack of a consistent global model to drive design, as different designers add functions that may not be consistent with other functions or modes.
