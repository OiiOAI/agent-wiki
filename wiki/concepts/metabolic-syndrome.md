# Metabolic Syndrome

## Summary

Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of related disorders first labeled by Gerald Reaven, characterized by five criteria: high blood pressure, high triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, central adiposity, and elevated fasting glucose. Approximately 120 million Americans meet these criteria. The X factor is insulin resistance. About 90 percent of the US population has at least one of these criteria.

## Key facts

- Metabolic syndrome is defined by five criteria: high blood pressure (>130/85), high triglycerides (>150 mg/dL), low HDL cholesterol (<40 mg/dL in men or <50 mg/dL in women), central adiposity (waist circumference >40 inches in men or >35 in women), and elevated fasting glucose (>110 mg/dL). [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p99]
- Meeting three or more of these criteria means you have metabolic syndrome, affecting approximately 120 million Americans according to a 2020 JAMA article. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p99]
- About 90 percent of the US population has at least one criterion for metabolic syndrome. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p99]
- Gerald Reaven labeled this collection of disorders 'Syndrome X' in the 1980s, determining that insulin resistance was the X factor. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p99]
- Approximately one-third of obese people by BMI are metabolically healthy, while 20-40 percent of nonobese adults may be metabolically unhealthy. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p99]
- People who are normal weight but metabolically unhealthy have more than triple the risk of all-cause mortality and/or cardiovascular events compared to metabolically healthy normal-weight individuals. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p101]
