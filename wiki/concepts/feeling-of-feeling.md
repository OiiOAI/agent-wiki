# Feeling of Feeling

## Summary

The 'feeling of feeling' refers to the conscious awareness of having an emotional feeling, which requires core consciousness. While emotions and their accompanying feelings can occur nonconsciously, knowing that one has a feeling requires the second-order representation of the emotion-induction sites and the changes they produce in the proto-self. This metacognitive process transforms raw feelings into consciously accessible experiences.

## Key facts

- Feeling an emotion consists of having mental images arising from neural patterns representing changes in body and brain that make up an emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p280]
- Knowing that we have a feeling occurs only after second-order representations are built, depicting the relationship between the organism and the emotion object. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p280]
- Emotion precedes feeling, and feeling precedes knowing that one is feeling—expression precedes feeling which precedes consciousness of feeling. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p284]

## Inferences

- Inference: The sequential emergence of emotion, feeling, and knowing suggests that even our most intimate subjective experiences are built from layered neural processes, not from a single unified state.
