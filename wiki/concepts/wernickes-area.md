# Wernicke's Area

## Summary

Wernicke's area is a region in the left posterior superior temporal lobe involved in auditory comprehension and word meaning. Damage produces Wernicke aphasia, characterized by fluent but incoherent speech with frequent word selection errors (paraphasias) and poor comprehension. The modern view is that Wernicke's area is part of a system associating speech sounds with concepts rather than the sole center of auditory comprehension.

## Key facts

- Wernicke's area is located in the left posterior section of the auditory association cortex (Brodmann's area 22) and is involved in word meaning and comprehension. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1418]
- Wernicke aphasia is characterized by fluent but incoherent speech, frequent phonemic and verbal paraphasias, and poor comprehension. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1418]
- The modern view is that Wernicke's area is part of a system associating speech sounds with concepts, including many brain regions that subserve grammar, attention, and knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1418]
- In 1874, Carl Wernicke demonstrated that damage to the posterior part of the temporal cortex on the left side produced a profound disturbance of the ability to understand speech, along with a tendency to speak fluently but with anomalous words and word combinations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p304]
- Wernicke's area can roughly be identified with the superior and middle temporal gyrus and is adjacent to auditory cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p305]
- Wernicke provided the first systemic analysis of relationships between brain damage and aphasia by organizing his theory around a diagrammatic analysis of cortical areas and putative connections with respect to input and output functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p305]
