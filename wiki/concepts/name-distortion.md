# Name Distortion

## Summary

Name distortion is a form of parapraxis where names are unintentionally altered, often reflecting unconscious attitudes toward the named person. Freud argues that hostile attitudes lead to distortions that serve as unconscious insults.

## Key facts

- Distorting names is very often a form of insulting their owners. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2102]
- A person who distorted Freud's name likely despised psychoanalysis while appearing courteous. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2072]
- Aristocracy is particularly prone to distort the names of doctors they consult, revealing inner despise despite external courtesy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2073]
- Replacing one name with another signifies appreciative feeling which has to remain in the background. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2073]

## Inferences

- Inference: Name distortion and name substitution represent opposite poles—hostility versus identification or admiration.
