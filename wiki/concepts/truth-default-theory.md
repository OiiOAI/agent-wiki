# Truth-Default Theory

## Summary

Truth-Default Theory (TDT) is a psychological theory developed by Tim Levine explaining why humans are so poor at detecting lies. The theory posits that humans operate with a default assumption that others are telling the truth, and only abandon this assumption when doubts become overwhelming and cannot be explained away.

## Key facts

- Tim Levine conducted hundreds of experiments showing that people correctly identify liars only about 54% of the time. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p45]
- Research by Hee Sun Park revealed that humans are much better than chance at identifying truth-tellers but much worse than chance at identifying liars. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p46]
- Truth-default theory holds that people default to believing others are honest because lying is rare and social cooperation depends on assuming others are truthful. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p60]
- Exiting the truth-default state requires a 'trigger'—evidence so definitive that doubts can no longer be explained away. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p46]

## Inferences

- Inference: Truth-Default Theory suggests that the poor lie-detection performance of professionals like police officers, judges, therapists, and CIA officers reflects universal human psychology rather than incompetence.
