# Spiral causation

## Summary

Spiral causation is the book's proposed solution to the apparent problem of circular causation in living systems. Critics argue that top-down causation creates circular reasoning—where the whole is supposed both to cause the organization of the parts and to be caused by that organization at the same time. The book argues this problem is an illusion of perspective: causation in living systems cannot be understood with an instantaneous perspective. What appears circular is actually a spiral, extended through time. Living beings do not cause themselves in an instant but cause themselves through time, with interactions up and down the hierarchy spread over time rather than occurring instantaneously.

## Inferences

- Inference: This temporal understanding of causation resolves the apparent paradox of self-causation in living organisms, which maintain themselves by continuously regenerating the constraints that keep them organized.
