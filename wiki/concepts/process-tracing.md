# Process Tracing

## Summary

Process tracing is a methodology for studying situation awareness and decision making, typically used with simulated incidents but also applicable to actual performance. Unlike retrospective methods like CDM, process tracing involves observing participants as they perform tasks, using think-aloud protocols and other techniques to gain insight into thought processes linked to task performance. The method allows for greater experimental control and comparison between experts and novices.

## Key facts

- Process tracing methods are used with simulated incidents (though they can be used with actual performance) and offer greater control of research variables than retrospective methods. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p80]
- Process tracing allows researchers to develop incidents that tap into variables of greatest interest, study both experts and novices in the same tasks, incorporate unobtrusive measures, and halt simulations for detailed questioning. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p80]
- Process tracing has disadvantages: effort may be required to construct simulations, the method is limited in discovering new causal factors, and simulations can be unrealistic regarding stressors such as fatigue, threat, and high workload. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p81]
- A powerful application of process tracing is contrasting the SA of experts to novices when faced with the same circumstances, as demonstrated by DeGroot (1946/1965) with chess positions and Charness (1989) with bridge hands. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p81]
- Shanteau (1995) found that experts don't have more data than novices; rather, experts have the ability to identify which cues to use, whereas novices tend to place too much emphasis on less diagnostic cues. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p86]

## Inferences

- Inference: Process tracing complements CDM by allowing controlled observation of decision processes rather than relying on retrospective reconstruction.
