# Narrow Framing

## Summary

Narrow framing is the tendency to evaluate each decision in isolation rather than considering it as part of a broader portfolio of similar decisions. Combined with loss aversion, narrow framing leads to suboptimal choices: a person who avoids a fair gamble in isolation would accept a bundle of many such gambles because the law of large numbers eliminates the risk. The key to rational decision-making is to broaden the frame and aggregate decisions.

## Key facts

- When given problem (i) 'gain $240 for certain vs. 25% chance of $1,000' and problem (ii) 'lose $750 for certain vs. 75% chance of losing $1,000', 73% of respondents choose the combination of the certain gain and the gamble in problem (ii), which is the strictly dominated combination AD (25% chance of $240 and 75% chance of losing $760) over BC (25% chance of $250 and 75% chance of losing $750). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p432]
- Paul Samuelson posed to a friend the question of accepting a 50-50 bet to lose $100 or win $200; the friend refused one bet but agreed to 100 repetitions, recognizing that aggregation reduces risk. Samuerson proved mathematically that a utility maximizer who refuses one such bet should refuse the series, illustrating the conflict between normative theory and reasonable intuition. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p435]
- Rabin and Thaler calculated that aggregating 100 repetitions of the $100/$200 gamble yields an expected value of $5,000 with only a 1/2,300 chance of any loss and a 1/62,000 chance of losing more than $1,000—demonstrating that rejection of the single gamble reflects an absurdly high loss-aversion coefficient. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p435]
- Participants told to 'think like a trader' or to view each decision as part of a portfolio showed reduced loss aversion and increased risk-taking, because broad framing attenuates the emotional response to individual losses. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p438]
- Individual investors can benefit from broad framing by checking their portfolios only quarterly rather than daily, because daily monitoring amplifies the pain of small frequent losses over the pleasure of small frequent gains, leading to destructive portfolio churning. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p438]

## Inferences

- Inference: Risk policy—having a consistent rule for recurring risky decisions (e.g., 'always buy insurance' or 'never buy extended warranties')—is a form of broad framing that avoids the cumulative cost of narrow framing combined with loss aversion.
