# Shunting Inhibition

## Summary

Shunting inhibition is a form of synaptic inhibition where opening of chloride channels near the resting potential increases membrane conductance, reducing the effectiveness of excitatory inputs by short-circuiting the depolarizing current. This effect is 'subtractive' on excitatory inputs because it reduces depolarization without changing the reversal potential.

## Key facts

- Opening of Cl− channels helps hold the membrane near its resting potential during an EPSP by increasing the weighting factor for the Cl− battery. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p336]
- The amplitude of depolarization during an EPSP is inversely proportional to total membrane conductance; opening Cl− channels makes the neuron more leaky, reducing EPSP amplitude (shunting effect). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p336]
- Inhibitory inputs that hyperpolarize the cell perform a subtraction on excitatory inputs, whereas the shunting effect performs a division on EPSP amplitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p336]
