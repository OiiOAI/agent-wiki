# Phenomenological Sociology

## Summary

Phenomenological sociology traces its development to Alfred Schutz's 1932 publication "The Phenomenology of the Social World" in Germany. Schutz was concerned with how people grasp the consciousness of others while living within their own stream of consciousness, and with intersubjectivity as a concern for the social world, especially the social nature of knowledge. Much of his work focuses on the "life-world" or world of everyday life—an intersubjective world where people both create social reality and are constrained by preexisting social and cultural structures. Schutz differentiated between intimate face-to-face relationships ("we-relations") and distant, impersonal relationships ("they-relations"). The theory addresses the dialectical relationship between how people construct social reality and the obdurate social and cultural reality they inherit.
