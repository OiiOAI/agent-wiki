# Life as a Series of Moments

## Summary

The philosopher challenges the conception of life as a linear journey toward a destination (like climbing a mountain). Instead, he proposes that life is a series of dots—moments called 'now.' Adults who don't understand this impose 'linear' lives onto young people. True living is like dancing, where the dancing itself is the goal, not arriving somewhere. This 'energeial' life contrasts with 'kinetic' life, where movement has a starting point and end point and efficiency matters. One should live as if dancing, finding fulfillment in each present moment.

## Key facts

- Life should not be treated as a line but as a series of dots. Seemingly linear existence is actually a series of moments called 'now.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p268]
- We can live only in the here and now; our lives exist only in moments. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p268]
- Life is like dancing: the dancing itself is the goal, and no one is concerned with arriving somewhere by doing it. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p271]
- Energeial movement treats the process itself as the outcome, like dance or a journey where the traveling itself is meaningful rather than reaching a destination. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p272]

## Inferences

- Inference: The philosopher distinguishes 'energeial' (Aristotle's term) from 'kinetic' life, where kinetic refers to ordinary efficient motion toward a destination.
