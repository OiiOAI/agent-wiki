# Prereflective Bodily Self-Consciousness

## Summary

Prereflective bodily self-consciousness is the non-object-directed, implicit awareness of one's body as a perceiving and acting subject, distinct from both the body image (body as object of consciousness) and the body schema (unconscious sensorimotor processes). It corresponds to the relation of the lived body to itself, experiencing one's body as perceiving rather than as perceived.

## Key facts

- Prereflective bodily self-consciousness is logically prior to reflection (which presupposes something to reflect upon) and temporally prior (what one reflects upon is a hitherto unreflected experience). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p248]
- The body image is the body as an intentional object of consciousness; the body schema is an integrated set of dynamic sensorimotor processes that organize perception and action in a subpersonal and nonconscious manner. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p247]
- Prereflective bodily self-consciousness is evident in touch: not only do we feel the things we touch, but we feel ourselves touching them and touched by them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p248]
- Sartre describes this sort of self-consciousness as 'non-positional' or 'non-thetic' because it does not posit one's body as an object. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p248]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dynamic process of 'self-othering' in bodily self-experience—where one's body becomes other to itself—anticipates the way another bodily subject would experience one, grounding empathy and intersubjective recognition.
