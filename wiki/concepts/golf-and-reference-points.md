# Golf and Reference Points

## Summary

The game of golf provides a natural field laboratory for studying reference points and loss aversion, because par defines the zero point: scoring below par (birdie) is coded as a gain and scoring above par (bogey) is coded as a loss. Pope and Schweitzer analyzed 2.5 million golf putts and found that players were 3.6% more successful when putting for par (avoiding a loss) than when putting for a birdie (securing a gain)—a direct demonstration that loss aversion affects professional performance.

## Key facts

- Pope and Schweitzer analyzed over 2.5 million golf putts and found that professional golfers were 3.6% more successful when putting to avoid a bogey (loss) than when putting to secure a birdie (gain), a statistically significant difference that they attributed to loss aversion. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p392]
- The 3.6% performance gap was observed across all distances from the hole, suggesting loss aversion operates as a constant motivational bias rather than only at specific difficulty levels. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p392]
- Tiger Woods, who was included in the study, could have earned nearly $1 million more per season if he had putted for birdies as well as he putted for par, demonstrating the economic significance of the loss-aversion effect at the highest professional levels. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p392]
- The golf putting study illustrates the power of a theoretical concept to generate precise, non-obvious hypotheses that yield surprising empirical findings—even experienced professionals were unaware that their putting performance depended on whether they were avoiding a loss or securing an additional gain. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p392]

## Inferences

- Inference: The golf finding suggests that performance in any competitive domain where a clear reference point exists (sales quotas, project deadlines, academic benchmarks) is likely affected by loss aversion, with performance on the loss side of the reference point being systematically superior.
