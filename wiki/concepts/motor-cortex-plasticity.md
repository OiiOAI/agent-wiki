# Motor Cortex Plasticity and Skill Learning

## Summary

The motor cortex exhibits remarkable plasticity in response to skill learning and sensory deprivation. Training on motor skills expands the cortical area from which skilled movements can be elicited, and removal of sensory input leads to rapid reorganization of motor maps through changes in horizontal connections and local inhibitory circuits.

## Key facts

- After monkeys became adept at retrieving treats from a small well, the area of motor cortex in which intracortical microstimulation could evoke the skilled movements was larger than before training. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p910]
- If a monkey did not practice the motor task for a lengthy period, its skill level decreased along with the cortical area from which relevant movements could be elicited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p910]
- Within minutes after transection of the facial nerve innervating lip muscles, stimulation of the lip-muscle site in motor cortex began to evoke contractions of forearm muscles, demonstrating rapid reorganization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p910]
- Injection of bicuculline into a forearm-muscle site blocked GABA-mediated inhibition, allowing stimulation of a whisker site to excite output neurons in forelimb sites through horizontal axonal collaterals normally restricted by inhibitory interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p911]
- Adaptive changes in motor cortex activity lag improvement in motor performance by several trials during adaptation, suggesting that learning-related adjustments to motor commands are initially made elsewhere, with the cerebellum as one strong candidate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p913]
- The motor map of the primary motor cortex is not static but rather creates a dynamic, adaptive map that generates motor commands required to accomplish desired actions under different conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p913]
