# Cell Assembly

## Summary

A cell assembly is a group of excitatory neurons mutually coupled by strong synapses, forming a functional unit of the nervous system as proposed by Donald Hebb. Cell assemblies store memories through Hebbian plasticity and can maintain persistent activity patterns for short-term memory.

## Key facts

- A cell assembly is a group of excitatory neurons mutually coupled by strong synapses, created through the strengthening of synapses caused by the simultaneous activation of the neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1645]
- Cell assemblies can complete activity patterns—when only part of a stored pattern is activated, the entire pattern can be reactivated through spread of activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1646]
- The CA3 region of the hippocampus contains pyramidal neurons that make synapses onto each other recurrently, and Hebbian plasticity has been observed at these synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1646]
- Interference between memories limits storage capacity—if too many overlapping patterns are stored, interference eventually becomes catastrophic and the stored patterns disappear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1647]

## Inferences

- Inference: Pattern completion in cell assemblies may underlie memory retrieval, where partial sensory information triggers recall of complete memories.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The biological mechanisms of persistent activity are still controversial—it could arise from synaptic loops or from intrinsic properties of single neurons.
