# Cell Assembly

## Summary

A cell assembly is a group of excitatory neurons mutually coupled by strong synapses, forming a functional unit of the nervous system as proposed by Donald Hebb. Cell assemblies store memories through Hebbian plasticity and can maintain persistent activity patterns for short-term memory.

## Key facts

- A cell assembly is a group of excitatory neurons mutually coupled by strong synapses, created through the strengthening of synapses caused by the simultaneous activation of the neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1645]
- Cell assemblies can complete activity patterns—when only part of a stored pattern is activated, the entire pattern can be reactivated through spread of activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1646]
- The CA3 region of the hippocampus contains pyramidal neurons that make synapses onto each other recurrently, and Hebbian plasticity has been observed at these synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1646]
- Interference between memories limits storage capacity—if too many overlapping patterns are stored, interference eventually becomes catastrophic and the stored patterns disappear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1647]
- Donald Hebb coined the term cell assembly and proposed that a discrete, physically interconnected group of spiking neurons is the unit that can represent a distinct percept, cognitive entity, or concept. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p85]
- Hebb's rule states 'neurons that fire together wire together'—when one cell repeatedly assists in firing another, the axon of the first cell develops synaptic knobs in contact with the soma of the second cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p86]
- The best prediction of spike timing of single hippocampal neurons from peer activity occurs within a 10-30 millisecond window, corresponding to the membrane time constant of cortical pyramidal cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p93]
- Approximately 1% of hippocampal pyramidal cells fire in a gamma cycle, and an estimated 150-300 pyramidal cells firing within a gamma cycle may comprise an effective assembly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p97]
- Buzsáki compares cell assemblies to 'letters' in the brain's vocabulary, with gamma wave frames (10-30 ms epochs) defining their temporal boundaries, and concatenated assemblies forming 'neuronal words.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p96]
- Cooperative assembly partnership tolerates spike rate variation in individual cells because the total excitatory effect of the assembly is what matters to the reader, allowing neurons to compute probabilities rather than convey deterministic information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: Pattern completion in cell assemblies may underlie memory retrieval, where partial sensory information triggers recall of complete memories.
- Inference: Cell assemblies can be defined from the perspective of neuronal reader mechanisms because the biological relevance of a particular constellation of active neurons can only be judged from its consequences.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The biological mechanisms of persistent activity are still controversial—it could arise from synaptic loops or from intrinsic properties of single neurons.
- Uncertain: Without an explicit goal, it is not possible to quantitatively define which neurons belong to the primary assembly and which represent feedback activation or newly recruited assemblies.
