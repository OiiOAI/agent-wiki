# Out-of-body experience

## Summary

Out-of-body experiences (OBEs) are sensations of seeing oneself from an external perspective. Research shows OBEs result from disruption in brain regions processing vestibular input and multisensory integration, supporting the view that the first-person perspective is a perceptual construction.

## Key facts

- Out-of-body experiences can be induced by electrical stimulation of the angular gyrus at the junction of the temporal and parietal lobes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p154]
- Patient G.A., stimulated in her right superior temporal gyrus by Wilder Penfield, reported 'a queer sensation that I am not here ... As though I were half here and half not here'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p154]
- OBEs result from unusual activity in brain regions dealing with vestibular input and multisensory integration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p154]
- When normal activity in vestibular and multisensory systems is disrupted, the brain reaches an unusual 'best guess' about the location of its first-person perspective. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p154]
- People who report OBEs probably do have the experiences they describe, but this does not mean immaterial selves leave physical bodies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p154]

## Inferences

- Inference: OBEs demonstrate that the first-person perspective is assembled from multiple sensory streams and can be disrupted, suggesting it is a construction rather than a fixed property of consciousness.
