# Automatic Responding in Modern Life

## Summary

The epilogue of 'Influence' discusses how modern life demands increasingly automatic, single-feature decision-making similar to lower animals' automatic behavior patterns. Humans have created such a complex, fast-paced, information-laden environment that we must increasingly rely on shortcuts and heuristics. While this shortcut approach is normally efficient and reliable, it becomes problematic when exploited by compliance professionals who falsify or misrepresent the cues that trigger these automatic responses.

## Key facts

- Modern life demands that we frequently use single-feature shortcut decision-making, similar to lower animals whose elaborate behavior patterns are triggered by lone stimulus features. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p244]
- The six most reliable single pieces of information for compliance decisions are reciprocation, consistency, social proof, liking, authority, and scarcity. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p245]
- We are likely to use these single cues when we lack inclination, time, energy, or cognitive resources for complete analysis—under conditions of being rushed, stressed, uncertain, indifferent, distracted, or fatigued. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p245]
- Human knowledge is said to double every eight years in fields such as physics, and 90 percent of all scientists who have ever lived are working today. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p246]
- Cialdini advocates counterassault against compliance professionals who falsify, counterfeit, or misrepresent evidence that naturally cues shortcut responses. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p248]
