# Planning to Adapt

## Summary

Planning to Adapt is an approach that accepts uncertainty and builds flexibility into plans rather than trying to anticipate every contingency. Rather than designing rigid detailed plans, this philosophy encourages developing plans that serve as platforms for improvisation and adaptation when circumstances change unexpectedly.

## Key facts

- Precise and detailed plans make the most sense in stable settings where key variables are well understood; in uncertain environments, plans should leave room for improvisation. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p219]
- Adaptation is harder than planning because it means modifying a plan in progress while trying to assess a moving target. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p219]
- The more details in a plan, the harder it is to adapt, and the more brittle the plans may be. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p211]
- Plans make people insensitive to anomalies that tell them it's time to adapt because plans describe what is relevant and anything irrelevant doesn't get much attention. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p211]
- Highly integrated plans are more efficient but more brittle; modular plans are less efficient but allow changes to one module without affecting others. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p215]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Planning to Adapt approach is particularly relevant for volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous (VUCA) environments where traditional strategic planning tends to become obsolete quickly.
