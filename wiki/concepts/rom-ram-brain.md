# ROM and RAM Functions of the Brain

## Summary

Panksepp uses a computer analogy to distinguish between innate, genetically dictated brain functions (ROM-like) and flexible, experience-dependent learning (RAM-like). ROM functions represent ancient emotional operating systems concentrated in subcortical regions, while RAM functions are recent capacities concentrated in neocortex that allow learning and cognitive flexibility.

## Key facts

- ROM functions are more ancient and reside in lower, subcortical brain regions, while RAM functions are more recent and concentrated in neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p19]
- The basic emotional value-systems are heavily concentrated in ventral parts of the 'old-mammalian' brain including hypothalamic-limbic circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p19]
- The flexibility of learning is mediated more by RAM-type processes in dorsal 'neomammalian' brain regions elaborated by thalamic-neocortical systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p19]
- Humans have far more RAM-like neuro-computational space than other mammals due to greater neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p21]

## Inferences

- Inference: All mammals share similar ROM emotional functions, allowing animal models to inform human emotional neuroscience

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise boundaries between ROM and RAM processes in the brain are artificial distinctions for explanatory purposes
