# Readiness Potential

## Summary

The readiness potential is a small slope-like EEG signal originating from the motor cortex that reliably precedes voluntary actions. Benjamin Libet's famous 1980s experiments showed that this brain signal begins hundreds of milliseconds before conscious intention to move. Aaron Schurger's 2012 reinterpretation proposed that readiness potentials are not signatures of the brain initiating action but rather artifacts of measurement—they reflect fluctuating brain activity that occasionally crosses a threshold, triggering action.

## Key facts

- The readiness potential was first recorded in the 1960s by Hans Kornhuber and Lüder Deecke, who called it the Bereitschaftspotential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p212]
- Libet found that after averaging across trials, the readiness potential was identifiable hundreds of milliseconds before the conscious intention to move. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p203]
- Libet proposed 'free won't'—the idea that conscious inhibition could intervene between the urge and action—but this does not work because conscious inhibition is no more miraculous than the original conscious intention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p204]
- Schurger realized that researchers systematically ignore all the other times when voluntary actions don't happen, potentially missing similar activity patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p205]
- Schurger found that when people responded quickly to a beep prompt, their EEG showed what looked like a readiness potential extending back before the beep, even though no voluntary action was being prepared. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p205]
- Schurger proposed that the readiness potential is a fluctuating pattern of brain activity that occasionally passes a threshold, triggering voluntary action—not a signature of brain initiation of action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: Through Schurger's experiment, readiness potentials appear to be activity associated with the brain accumulating sensory data to make Bayesian best guesses—in other words, neural fingerprints of a special kind of controlled hallucination.
