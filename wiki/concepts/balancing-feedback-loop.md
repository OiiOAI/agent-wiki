# Balancing Feedback Loop

## Summary

A balancing feedback loop is a goal-seeking or stability-seeking feedback structure that opposes whatever direction of change is imposed on the system. If a stock is pushed too far up, the balancing loop tries to pull it back down; if pushed too far down, it tries to bring it back up. Balancing loops are sources of both stability and resistance to change.

## Key facts

- Balancing feedback loops are equilibrating or goal-seeking structures that are both sources of stability and sources of resistance to change. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p47]
- Each balancing loop tries to keep a stock at a given value or within a range of values. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p46]
- A stock-maintaining balancing feedback loop must have its goal set appropriately to compensate for draining or inflowing processes that affect that stock. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p58]
- The information delivered by a feedback loop can only affect future behavior; it can't deliver a signal fast enough to correct behavior that drove the current feedback. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: The thermostat example demonstrates that balancing loops require appropriate goal settings to account for competing flows (like heat leaking out of a room).
