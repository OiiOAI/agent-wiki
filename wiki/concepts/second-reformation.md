# The Second Reformation

## Summary

The Second Reformation is McGilchrist's term for the mid-19th century intellectual movement of scientific materialism/positivism that he sees as paralleling the 16th-century Reformation. Just as the Reformation shifted away from metaphor and incarnation toward literalism, the Second Reformation had difficulty with the interpenetrating realms of matter and spirit, embracing instead a thoroughgoing materialism that McGilchrist associates with left-hemisphere dominance.

## Key facts

- The Reformation of the sixteenth century involved a shift away from the capacity to understand metaphor and incarnation towards a literalistic way of thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p524]
- In the mid-19th century in Germany, scientific materialism arose with roots in the Reformation and difficulty with the interpenetration of matter and spirit. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p525]
- Feuerbach and fellow materialists saw only the left hemisphere alternatives: matter or ideal—in rejecting the ideal they were compelled to accept only matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p526]
- The denial of the divine was as important to materialists as the elevation of matter—there is more than a little of the Promethean about the materialists. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p527]

## Inferences
