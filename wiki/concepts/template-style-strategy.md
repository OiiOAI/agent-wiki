# Template-Style Strategy

## Summary

Template-style strategy is a fill-in-the-blanks approach to strategic planning that substitutes vision, mission, values, and strategies for the hard work of analysis and coordinated action. This approach offers a one-size-fits-all substitute for genuine strategic thinking and is one of the three main pathways to bad strategy.

## Key facts

- Template-style strategy typically requires filling in: a Vision (what the organization will be), a Mission (the purpose), Values (principles), and Strategies (aspirations labeled as strategies). [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p34]
- This template approach was enthusiastically adopted by corporations, school boards, university presidents, and government agencies despite producing pious statements of the obvious. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p35]
- Template-style strategy frees consultants from the onerous work of analyzing true challenges and opportunities faced by the client. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p35]
- Most corporate strategic plans are simply three-year or five-year rolling budgets combined with market share projections, not actual strategy. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p29]
