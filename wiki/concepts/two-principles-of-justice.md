# Two Principles of Justice

## Summary

The two principles of justice are Rawls's proposed principles that would be chosen in the original position. The first principle requires equal right to the most extensive basic liberty compatible with similar liberty for others. The second principle holds that social and economic inequalities are to be arranged so they are both (a) reasonably expected to be to everyone's advantage, and (b) attached to positions and offices open to all. These principles apply to the basic structure of society and are arranged in serial order with the first principle prior to the second, meaning basic liberties cannot be sacrificed for economic gains.

## Key facts

- The first principle states: each person is to have an equal right to the most extensive basic liberty compatible with a similar liberty for others. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p64]
- The second principle states: social and economic inequalities are to be arranged so they are both (a) reasonably expected to be to everyone's advantage, and (b) attached to positions and offices open to all. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p64]
- The two principles are to be arranged in serial order with the first principle prior to the second, meaning a departure from equal liberty cannot be justified by greater social and economic advantages. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p65]
- The basic liberties of citizens include political liberty (the right to vote and be eligible for public office), freedom of speech and assembly, liberty of conscience and freedom of thought, freedom of the person and right to hold property, and freedom from arbitrary arrest and seizure. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p64]
- The two principles apply primarily to the basic structure of society, distinguishing between liberties of equal citizenship and social and economic inequalities. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p64]

## Inferences

- Inference: The serial ordering of principles expresses an underlying preference among primary social goods that justifies the priority of liberty.
