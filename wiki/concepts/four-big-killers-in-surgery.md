# Four Big Killers in Surgery

## Summary

The four categories of surgical mortality and morbidity that checklists aim to address: infection, bleeding, unsafe anesthesia, and the unexpected.

## Key facts

- Surgery has four big killers wherever it is performed: infection, bleeding, unsafe anesthesia, and what can only be called the unexpected. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p77]
- For the first three killers (infection, bleeding, unsafe anesthesia), science and experience have provided preventive measures that practitioners believe they consistently follow but actually do not. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p77]
- The fourth killer—the unexpected—is a fundamentally different kind of failure stemming from the complex risks of opening a person's body and attempting intervention. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p77]
- The four big killers require different approaches: the first three are amenable to classic checklist items, while the unexpected requires team communication to identify each patient's unique dangers. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p77]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between the first three killers and the fourth suggests that simple procedural checklists are necessary but insufficient for surgical safety—they must be combined with team communication elements.
