# Goblins

## Summary

Goblins are deeply embedded, destructive beliefs or automatic programmes that are firmly fixed in the Computer and extremely difficult to remove. They are typically established during early childhood (before age eight) when the brain hard-wires information, and must be managed and contained rather than eliminated.

## Key facts

- Goblins are unhelpful beliefs or behaviours that are firmly fixed and extremely difficult to remove from the Computer. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p58]
- Goblins typically occur before age eight and become hard-wired into the Computer during early childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p58]
- The Fridge Door Syndrome is described as the most common Goblin, where a child's worth becomes dependent on achievements displayed publicly. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p58]
- Goblins require containment strategies rather than removal attempts, since they cannot be eliminated like Gremlins. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between Goblins and Gremlins is practical: Goblins must be worked around, while Gremlins can be actively removed through the replacement process.
