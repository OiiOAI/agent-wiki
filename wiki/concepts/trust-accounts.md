# Trust Accounts

## Summary

Trust Accounts are a metaphor for the trust balance in any relationship, similar to a bank account. Behaving in trust-building ways makes deposits while trust-destroying behaviors make withdrawals. Key principles include: each Trust Account is unique, not all deposits and withdrawals are equal, what constitutes a deposit to one person may not to another, withdrawals are typically larger than deposits, and sometimes the fastest way to build trust is to stop making withdrawals.

## Key facts

- Trust Accounts are similar to Emotional Bank Accounts introduced in The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p166]
- Warren Buffett said it takes twenty years to build a reputation and five minutes to ruin it. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p167]
- Each relationship has two trust accounts—their perception of trust and your perception of trust may differ. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p168]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Trust Account metaphor provides a practical framework for understanding why trust restoration is difficult—once an account is depleted or closed, rebuilding requires significant deposits over time.
