# Crossing the First Threshold

## Summary

At the entrance to the zone of magnified power, the hero encounters threshold guardians that bound the world in the four directions. These custodians represent the limits of the hero's present sphere. Beyond them lies darkness, the unknown, and danger. The regions outside the normal traffic of the village are populated with dangerous presences reflecting unconscious content.

## Key facts

- At the entrance to the zone of magnified power, the hero comes to the threshold guardians at the bounds of the world in the four directions—standing for the limits of the hero's present sphere or life horizon. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p66]
- Beyond the threshold is darkness, the unknown, and danger; just as beyond the parental watch is danger to the infant and beyond the protection of society danger to the member of the tribe. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p66]
- The folk mythologies populate with deceitful and dangerous presences every desert place outside the normal traffic of the village—incestuous libido and patricidal destrudo reflected back against the individual. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p67]
- The Arcadian god Pan is the best known Classical example of this dangerous presence dwelling just beyond the protected zone of the village boundary. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p68]
- The pairs of opposites (being and not being, life and death, beauty and ugliness, good and evil) are the clashing rocks (Symplegades) that crush the traveler, but between which the heroes always pass. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p73]
