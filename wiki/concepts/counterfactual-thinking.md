# Counterfactual Thinking

## Summary

Counterfactual thinking is the ability to generate explanations and predictions that are inconsistent with current data—to imagine a world different from the one currently being observed. Experts can generate counterfactuals because they have learned not to place too heavy a reliance on data. Novices have difficulty imagining alternatives to their current situation, signaling a lack of expertise.

## Key facts

- A weather forecaster unable to imagine when ceiling might lift earlier than predicted was unable to generate counterfactuals, signaling he was not an expert. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p194]
- Novices have difficulty imagining a world different from the one they are seeing. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p194]
