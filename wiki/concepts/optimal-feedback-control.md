# Optimal Feedback Control

## Summary

Optimal feedback control is a theoretical framework for understanding sensorimotor control that proposes the nervous system minimizes a cost function combining task inaccuracy and effort. Rather than specifying desired trajectories, this model specifies feedback gains that determine how motor commands respond to state errors. The system corrects only task-relevant deviations while allowing task-irrelevant variability (minimal intervention principle).

## Key facts

- Optimal feedback control uses a cost function combining task inaccuracy and effort to generate appropriate motor commands for any body state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p728]
- In optimal feedback control, the controller specifies how feedback gain for positional errors changes over time rather than specifying a desired trajectory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p728]
- The minimal intervention principle states that corrections should only occur for deviations that affect task success, allowing variability in task-irrelevant dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p729]
- Optimal feedback control does not make a hard distinction between feedforward and feedback control; their balance varies continuously based on predictions versus sensory input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p728]
- This framework combines trajectory generation, noise, and motor cost and provides a clear comparison for experimental results in motor control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p729]
- Optimal feedback control divides voluntary movement into three key processes: state estimation, task selection, and control policy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p865]
- State estimation uses forward internal models with efference copies of motor commands and external sensory feedback to estimate present body and environment state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p865]
- The control policy involves context- and time-dependent adjustments to feedback circuit gains rather than purely feedforward calculations of movement trajectories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p865]
- Optimal feedback control explains how motor performance can be highly variable yet successful across changing conditions and behavioral goals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p865]
