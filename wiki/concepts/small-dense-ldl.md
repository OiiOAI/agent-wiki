# Small Dense LDL

## Summary

Small, dense LDL particles are the most risk-promoting and easily oxidized variant of LDL. They form when LDL particles shrink after dropping off their cholesterol passengers, and the recycling process is disrupted. This can occur through LDL damage (oxidation or glycation) or when the liver's recycling function is impaired due to oxidative stress from excessive carbohydrate or alcohol consumption.

## Key facts

- Small, dense LDL particles are the most risk-promoting and easily oxidized variant of LDL. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p166]
- LDL particles can be damaged through oxidation (from time spent in the bloodstream and exposure to oxidative by-products) or glycation (bonding of sugar molecules). [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p167]
- When the liver is digesting carbs, alcohol, or other toxins, it doesn't prioritize the recycling of lipoproteins. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p167]
- When subjects went on a high-carb, low-fat diet, their levels of oxidized cholesterol surged by 27 percent, even though total cholesterol did not change. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p167]
