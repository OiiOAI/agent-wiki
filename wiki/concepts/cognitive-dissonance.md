# Cognitive Dissonance

## Summary

Cognitive dissonance theory (Leon Festinger) proposes that mismatches between expectations and outcomes create inner discordance that is stressful and demands reduction. LeDoux applied this to split-brain research, proposing that when the left hemisphere becomes aware of behaviors it didn't initiate, dissonance results and confabulation of reasons reduces the dissonance.

## Key facts

- Festinger's cognitive dissonance theory proposes that mismatches between expectations and outcomes create inner discordance that is stressful and demands reduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p264]
- Split-brain patients confabulate explanations when their left hemisphere (which lacks information about right hemisphere responses) must account for unexpected behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p264]
- LeDoux proposes that confabulation serves to reduce cognitive dissonance and protect the unity of the self. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p273]
- Today 'postdecision rationalization' examines how people retroactively justify their decisions and actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p264]
- Leon Festinger and Merrill Carlsmith found that students who received only $1 for lying about enjoying boring tasks rated the work as significantly more enjoyable than those paid $20. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p117]
- Aesop's fox reinterpreted the situation: 'These aren't even ripe yet' rather than admitting he couldn't reach the grapes. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p117]

## Inferences

- Inference: The tendency to confabulate explains why humans often don't know the true reasons for their behaviors — conscious narrative is constructed post-hoc.
