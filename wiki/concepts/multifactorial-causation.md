# Multifactorial Causation

## Summary

Multifactorial causation refers to the principle that human social behaviors are influenced by numerous biological and environmental factors simultaneously, making prediction at the individual level difficult even when group-level explanations are robust. This explains why neuroscience can explain much about behavior yet fail to predict individual outcomes.

## Key facts

- While frontal cortex damage reliably produces odd behavior in conversation (about 75 percent predictability), predicting whether someone will do something horrifically violent is under 50 percent, and predicting that an abused child will become an abusive adult is also under 50 percent. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p617]
- Marvin Minsky defined free will as 'internal forces I do not understand'—people intuitively believe in free will partly because most people know next to nothing about those internal forces. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p618]
- Predicting depression might reach above 50 percent predictability only after adding knowledge of serotonin transporter gene status, childhood adversity, poverty, glucocorticoid levels, culture type, and menstrual status—many factors not yet discovered. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p618]
