# Non-REM sleep

## Summary

Non-REM sleep comprises three stages (N1, N2, and N3, with N3 being deep slow-wave sleep), occurring between periods of REM. Dream reports are obtained from all non-REM stages, with reports from N1 occurring approximately 75 percent of awakenings and N2 reports rising to 50–70 percent in modern studies. Dream reports from non-REM tend to be shorter and less vivid than those from REM.

## Key facts

- Dream reports are obtained from non-REM N1 approximately 75 percent of awakenings, indistinguishable from the 80 percent rate in REM. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
- For non-REM N2 awakenings, dream report rates have risen from 7 percent in 1957 to a best estimate of 50 to 60 percent in modern studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
- N3 (slow-wave) sleep shows large, synchronized neural firing that declines as the night progresses. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p53]
- Sleep-dependent memory improvements for motor skills depend on N2 sleep, while verbal memory depends on N3 sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p79]

## Inferences

- Inference: Non-REM sleep also supports dreaming and memory processing, contradicting the assumption that only REM sleep is associated with dream experiences.
