# Electroencephalography (EEG)

## Summary

EEG monitors the joint dendritic activities of large ensembles of neurons through electrodes on the skull surface. It provides real-time monitoring of brain electrical activity and is the best technique for following cognitive and emotional brain processes in humans.

## Key facts

- Hans Berger first noted EEG brain waves in 1929, discovering the alpha rhythm (approximately 10 cycles per second) during relaxed states with eyes closed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p51]
- Five general categories of brain waves are recognized: delta (0.5-3 Hz), theta (4-7 Hz), alpha (8-12 Hz), beta (13-30 Hz), and gamma (above 30 Hz). [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p51]
- Event-related desynchronization (ERD) represents repeated arousal shifts from resting alpha state with respect to specific environmental events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p53]
- Normal children exhibit brain arousal (ERD) to mother's picture, while autistic children exhibit the opposite pattern (ERS), which can be partially normalized by naltrexone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p53]

## Inferences

- Inference: EEG alpha blocking occurs when resting alpha waves disappear during attention and information processing, reflecting neurons firing more independently rather than in synchrony.
- Inference: Theta waves mark active information processing in the hippocampus during exploration and REM sleep.
