# Freudian Superego

## Summary

A concept representing a special agency within the ego that serves as the vehicle of conscience and moral restrictions. Freud described the superego as a precipitate of the first object cathexes of the id and the heir to the Oedipus complex after its demise. It can confront the ego and treat it like an object, often treating it very harshly. Mental health depends on the superego being normally developed and sufficiently impersonal.

## Key facts

- The superego is a precipitate of the first object cathexes of the id and is the heir to the Oedipus complex after its demise. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6431]
- The superego can confront the ego and treat it like an object, and it often treats it very harshly. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6431]
- The superego is the vehicle of the phenomenon called conscience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6432]
- Mental health depends on the superego being normally developed—sufficiently impersonal—which is precisely what it is not in neurotics. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6432]
- Neurotics' superego confronts their ego as a strict father confronts a child, and their morality operates in a primitive fashion where the ego gets itself punished by the superego.
