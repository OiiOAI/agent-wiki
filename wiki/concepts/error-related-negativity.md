# Error-Related Negativity

## Summary

The error-related negativity (ERN) is a negative event-related potential waveform peaking approximately 50-100ms after error commission over frontocentral scalp regions. Research suggests larger ERN amplitudes in depression, reflecting increased error monitoring and affective responses to mistakes.

## Key facts

- The ERN is a response-locked negative waveform that peaks over frontocentral scalp regions approximately 50-100ms following error commission. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p294]
- The feedback-related negativity (FRN) is a negative deflection with frontocentral scalp distribution peaking approximately 200-400ms following feedback indicating a mistake. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p294]
- Larger amplitude ERNs have been observed in both medicated and unmedicated major depressive disorder samples compared to healthy controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p294]
- Depressed individuals display significantly increased medial frontal negativity after receiving negative feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p294]
- Increased ERN in depression predicts poor response to antidepressant treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p295]

## Inferences

- Inference: Larger ERNs in depression may reflect increased affective responses to errors and difficulty adjusting behavior after perceived failures.
- Inference: Oversensitivity to errors may be a trait-like marker of depression that persists after clinical recovery.
