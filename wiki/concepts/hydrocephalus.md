# Hydrocephalus

## Summary

Hydrocephalus is an increase in the volume of the cerebral ventricles caused by oversecretion of CSF, impaired absorption, or obstruction of CSF pathways. It can be communicating or non-communicating and causes brain enlargement or compression depending on age.

## Key facts

- Hydrocephalus has three possible causes: oversecretion of cerebrospinal fluid, impaired absorption of CSF, or obstruction of CSF pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1629]
- Impaired CSF absorption at the arachnoid villi is a common cause of communicating hydrocephalus following subarachnoid hemorrhage, trauma, or bacterial meningitis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1629]
- Normal-pressure hydrocephalus is characterized by dementia, urinary incontinence, and a disorder of gait called apraxia, with normal routine lumbar puncture pressure but episodic elevations on continuous monitoring. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1629]
- The aqueduct of Sylvius is a particularly vulnerable site for obstruction from congenital malformations, gliosis, or tumor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1630]
