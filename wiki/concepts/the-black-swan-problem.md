# The Black Swan Problem

## Summary

The impossibility of calculating risks of consequential rare events and predicting their occurrence. Taleb argues that fragility can be measured while risk cannot, providing a solution to the Black Swan problem by focusing on sensitivity to harm rather than prediction of events. Black Swans are large-scale unpredictable events of massive consequence that dominate history while being systematically underestimated.

## Key facts

- It is far easier to figure out if something is fragile than to predict the occurrence of an event that may harm it; fragility can be measured while risk is not measurable. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p21]
- Taleb proposes to stand current approaches to prediction, prognostication, and risk management on their heads, replacing discussion of risk with the notion of fragility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p21]
- Black Swans are large-scale unpredictable and irregular events of massive consequence, unpredicted by a certain observer who is generally called the 'turkey' when surprised and harmed by these events. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p23]
- The odds of rare events are simply not computable; the rarer the event, the less tractable and the more confident these 'scientists' involved in predicting have become—a paradox of expertise. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p24]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Black Swan problem motivates the entire antifragility framework—instead of trying to predict rare events, one should focus on building systems that benefit from volatility and are robust to harm.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all Black Swans are truly unpredictable or if some categories of rare events may be partially foreseeable.
