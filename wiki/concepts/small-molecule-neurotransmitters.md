# Small-Molecule Neurotransmitters

## Summary

Small-molecule neurotransmitters are low-molecular-weight chemical messengers including acetylcholine, glutamate, GABA, glycine, biogenic amines (dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine, serotonin, histamine), and ATP/adenosine. They are synthesized locally in nerve terminals and concentrated in small synaptic vesicles (~40 nm diameter) for rapid calcium-triggered exocytosis.

## Key facts

- A classical neurotransmitter must be synthesized in the presynaptic neuron, accumulated in vesicles, released in amounts sufficient to exert action, and removed from the extracellular space by specific mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p406]
- Small-molecule neurotransmitters are packaged in small electron-lucent vesicles (~40 nm diameter) associated with specific Ca2+ channels at active zones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p406]
- The enzymes catalyzing synthesis of small-molecule neurotransmitters, except dopamine beta-hydroxylase, are cytosolic, allowing synthesis throughout the neuron including presynaptic terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p415]
- Glutamate is produced from alpha-ketoglutarate (a TCA cycle intermediate) and is the primary excitatory transmitter in the CNS; GABA is synthesized from glutamate by glutamic acid decarboxylase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p411]
