# Sensorimotor Transformation

## Summary

Sensorimotor transformations are the neural processes that convert sensory inputs into motor outputs, enabling the brain to generate appropriate movements. These transformations occur through a cascade of processing stages that convert information about target location, limb position, and desired movement into muscle contractions.

## Key facts

- Sensory inputs include both extrinsic information about the state of the world (e.g., spatial location of a target via auditory and visual inputs) and intrinsic information about our body (kinematic and kinetic information). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p794]
- The calculation of an endpoint from a set of joint angles is termed forward kinematic transformation, whereas calculation of a set of joint angles that can reach an endpoint is termed inverse kinematic transformation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p796]
- The transformation that calculates torques at each joint necessary to achieve a desired joint angle trajectory is known as the inverse dynamic transformation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p796]
- Different coordinate systems are used in sensorimotor transformations, including extrinsic systems (allocentric or egocentric using exteroceptive information) and intrinsic systems based on proprioceptive information such as muscle lengths or joint angles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p800]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hierarchical organization of sensorimotor transformations allows higher brain levels to plan general goals while lower levels handle implementation details.
- Inference: The brain's use of multiple coordinate systems at different stages provides flexibility and robustness in movement planning and execution.
