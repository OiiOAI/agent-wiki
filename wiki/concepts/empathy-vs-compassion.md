# Empathy vs Compassion Distinction

## Summary

Empathy enables sharing and understanding others' emotions without confusing them with one's own, engaging a network centered on the anterior insula and anterior midcingulate cortex associated with negative affect. Compassion involves concern for others' suffering combined with motivation to increase their welfare, associated with positive feelings of warmth and concern, engaging the medial orbitofrontal cortex and ventral striatum. Critically, empathy alone may not be sufficient to motivate prosocial behavior; only compassion consistently promotes helping others.

## Key facts

- Empathy is defined as the ability to share and understand the emotions of others while not confusing these emotions with one's own. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p248]
- Compassion has been defined as concern for the sufferings of others combined with the motivation to increase their welfare. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p250]
- Empathic responses to pain are not universal and can be modulated by perceived intensity of observed pain, who is suffering, why they are suffering, available attentional resources, and personality characteristics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p249]
- Empathy can lead to either empathic distress (wanting to relieve oneself of vicarious pain) or empathic concern/compassion (wanting to help others), with different motivational consequences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p250]
- Taking a first-person perspective while empathizing was associated with withdrawal and distress, whereas taking others' perspective was associated with increased concern and altruistic motivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p251]
- Compassion training increased positive affect and enhanced activity in the putamen, ventral tegmental area, pallidum, and medial orbitofrontal cortex, areas implicated in reward and affiliation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p252]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between empathy and compassion suggests they rely on different neural substrates and have different implications for prosocial behavior.
- Inference: Short-term compassion training can shift empathic responses from negative affect (distress) to positive affect (concern), suggesting these states are malleable.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The field is still in its infancy regarding the direct neural link between compassion-related neural activity and prosocial behavior.
