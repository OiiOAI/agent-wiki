# Hero as World Redeemer

## Summary

Heroes of the highest illumination are the world redeemers, the so-called incarnations. Their myths open out to cosmic proportions; their words carry authority beyond anything pronounced by heroes of the scepter and the book. The incarnation refutes by his presence the pretensions of the tyrant ogre, enacting the herolife with the freedom of a work done only to make evident what might have been accomplished with a mere thought.

## Key facts

- Two degrees of initiation are distinguished in the mansion of the father; from the first the son returns as emissary, but from the second, with the knowledge that 'I and the father are one.' Heroes of this second, highest illumination are the world redeemers. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p273]
- Killer-of-Enemies of the Jicarilla Apache declared: 'The world is just as big as my body. The world is as large as my word. And the world is as large as my prayers...I am all over. Don't think I stay only under the earth or up in the sky, or only in the seasons, or on the other side of the waters. These are all my body.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p273-p274]
- The legend describes the period of desolation as caused by a moral fault on the part of man (Adam in the garden, Jemshid on the throne), yet from the standpoint of the cosmogonic cycle a regular alternation of fair and foul is characteristic of the spectacle of time. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p275]
- The hero of yesterday becomes the tyrant of tomorrow unless he crucifies himself today. The savior figure who eliminates the tyrant father and then assumes the crown is stepping into his sire's stead; to soften the harsh patricide, the legend represents the father as some cruel uncle or usurping Nimrod. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p276]

## Inferences

- Inference: World redeemers embody the paradox of the hero's journey: the same cosmic energy that sustains creation must also ultimately destroy it, and the redeemer represents the conscious embrace of this transformative necessity.
