# Modes of Control

## Summary

Three invisible and pervasive means by which actions can be controlled in organizations: free-market forces (price-based exchange), contractual obligations (defined work standards), and cultural values (shared objectives and trust). The most appropriate mode depends on individual motivation and environmental complexity/uncertainty/ambiguity (CUA factor).

## Key facts

- The three modes of control are free-market forces (based on price and self-interest), contractual obligations (defined work standards), and cultural values (shared objectives and trust). [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1405-L1407]
- Free-market forces work when goods and services possess very clearly defined dollar value; contractual obligations are needed when value is hard to establish. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1415-L1417]
- Cultural values mode of control is needed when the environment changes more rapidly than rules can change, or when circumstances are too ambiguous for contracts. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1425-L1427]
- The CUA factor (complexity, uncertainty, ambiguity) of an environment, combined with individual motivation (self-interest to group-interest), determines the most appropriate mode of control. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1435-L1437]
- When self-interest is high and CUA is low, the market mode is most appropriate; when group-interest and CUA are both high, cultural values become the best choice. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1440-L1442]
- When CUA is high and individual motivation is based on self-interest, no mode of control will work well—producing chaos like 'every man for himself on a sinking ship.'. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1442-L1444]
