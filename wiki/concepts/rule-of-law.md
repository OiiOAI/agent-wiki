# Rule of Law

## Summary

The rule of law is the regular and impartial administration of law, which Rawls terms 'justice as regularity.' It is closely connected to liberty because vague or imprecise laws make the boundaries of liberty uncertain, thereby restricting freedom through reasonable fear. The rule of law encompasses several precepts: the principle that 'ought implies can' (laws must be achievable), that similar cases be treated similarly, that there be no offense without a law (nullum crimen sine lege), and principles of natural justice ensuring the integrity of the judicial process.

## Key facts

- The rule of law is formal justice applied to the legal system—regular and impartial administration of public rules. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p218]
- A legal system is a coercive order of public rules addressed to rational persons for regulating conduct and providing the framework for social cooperation. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p218]
- The precept 'ought implies can' requires that laws demand only what men can reasonably be expected to do, that authorities act in good faith, and that impossibility of performance be recognized as a defense. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p219]
- The rule of law includes the principle that similar cases be treated similarly, limiting the discretion of judges and requiring justification for distinctions between persons. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p220]
- The precept 'nullum crimen sine lege' demands that laws be known and expressly promulgated, that their meaning be clearly defined, that statutes be general, and that penal laws not be retroactive. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p220]
- Natural justice requires orderly trials, rational procedures of inquiry, independent and impartial judges, and fair and open hearings. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p221]
