# The Nine-Dot Problem

## Summary

A classic puzzle used in the book to illustrate first-order versus second-order change. The puzzle requires connecting nine dots arranged in a square formation using four straight lines without lifting the pen. The solution requires extending lines beyond the assumed square boundary—a second-order change that violates the self-imposed constraint. The example demonstrates that failure to solve a problem often lies not in the impossibility of the task but in the attempted solution's assumptions.
