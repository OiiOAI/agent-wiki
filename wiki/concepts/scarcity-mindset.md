# Scarcity Mindset

## Summary

A scarcity mindset is a way of viewing the world through the lens of insufficiency, characterized by the belief that information is scarce and must be hoarded. People operating under this mindset constantly crave more information, driven by fear of missing out. The book contrasts this with an abundance mindset and presents the shift from scarcity to abundance as a fundamental transformation that occurs when building a Second Brain.

## Key facts

- Through the lens of scarcity, people constantly crave more, more, more information, a response to the fear of not having enough. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p199]
- The paradox of hoarding is that no matter how much we collect and accumulate, it is never enough. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p199]
- The lens of scarcity tells us that the information we already have must not be very valuable, compelling us to keep searching externally for what is missing inside. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p199]
- Langer argues that health should not be seen as a static condition randomly distributed; good health may be equally available to virtually all of us. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p51-52]
- Research by Langer and Thompson found that most behaviors people tried unsuccessfully to change were the same behaviors they valued about themselves when described in positive terms (e.g., 'inconsistent' vs. 'flexible'). [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p57-58]
- Langer's vision study showed that 40 percent of MIT ROTC students displayed improved vision after pretending to be pilots in a flight simulator, demonstrating that mindset can eliminate physical limitations. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p61-62]
- Inverting the standard eye chart (making letters larger going down) led to improved performance because it changed people's expectations, illustrating that there is no fixed 'normal' level of ability. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p62]
- Langer argues that the assumption of scarcity benefits those at the top because 'to be on top, someone needs to be on the bottom' and high status is maintained by creating criteria that keep current leaders at the top. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p51-52]
