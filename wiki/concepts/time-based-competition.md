# Time-Based Competition

## Summary

Time-based competition is the strategic approach that uses decision cycle speed as the primary weapon to offset opponent advantages in size, technology, and position. By executing OODA loops faster than competitors, organizations can create and exploit opportunities before opponents can react, causing disorientation, confusion, and paralysis. This approach was demonstrated in the Blitzkrieg, the Honda-Yamaha War, and by Southwest Airlines. The concept challenges conventional thinking that emphasizes size, technology, or market share as primary competitive advantages.

## Key facts

- The winners were able to make things happen that their opponents may have anticipated, but not when their opponents might have expected. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p18]
- Our view of the world, our 'orientation,' depends heavily on things happening close in time to when we expect them to happen. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p18]
- Mismatches in time can be disorienting; under stress, disoriented people become demoralized, frustrated, and panicked. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p18]
- The critical element was that Honda was both learning what customers 'wanted' and was helping to shape them at the same time during the H-Y War. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p20]
- Time has also been a foundation of business strategy in the Far East, particularly among the Japanese automakers. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p19]

## Inferences

- Inference: Time-based competition reframes the competitive battlefield from physical attributes (size, technology, resources) to temporal ones (speed of observation, orientation, decision, and action).
- Inference: Organizations can achieve competitive advantage not by matching opponent capabilities but by changing the tempo and pattern of competition itself.
