# Reflective Equilibrium

## Summary

Reflective equilibrium is the method Rawls uses to justify principles of justice. It is a state in which our principles and judgments coincide, and it is reflective since we know to what principles our judgments conform and the premises of their derivation. The method involves working back and forth between principles and considered judgments until they match. We begin with weak conditions and see if they yield a significant set of principles; if discrepancies arise, we either modify the conditions of the contractual circumstances or revise our judgments. This process accommodates both reasonable philosophical conditions on principles and our considered judgments of justice.

## Key facts

- Reflective equilibrium is the state where our principles and judgments coincide, and it is reflective since we know to what principles our judgments conform and the premises of their derivation. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p30]
- In reaching reflective equilibrium we work from both ends: we describe the original position with generally shared weak conditions, then check if these yield principles matching our considered convictions. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p30]
- When discrepancies arise between principles and judgments, we can either modify the account of the initial situation or revise our existing judgments. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p30]
- The best account of a person's sense of justice is not the one which fits their judgments prior to examining any conception, but the one which matches their judgments in reflective equilibrium. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p53]
- In justice as fairness, the two principles would be chosen in the original position and would give a better match with our considered judgments on reflection than traditional alternatives. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p54]
