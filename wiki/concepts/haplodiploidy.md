# Haplodiploidy and Social Insects

## Summary

Haplodiploidy is a sex-determination system found in Hymenoptera (ants, bees, wasps) where males are haploid and females diploid. Hamilton's relatedness hypothesis suggested this makes sisters more closely related than mothers are to daughters, predisposing females to stay and help. This chunk discusses criticisms of overemphasis on haplodiploidy, alternatives like cyclic inbreeding in termites, and the 'hollow tree' or 'going concern' principle.

## Key facts

- Hamilton's haplodiploid relatedness hypothesis has paradoxically embarrassed his more general kin selection theory; people confuse the specific hypothesis with the whole theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p368]
- Linda Gamlin's article on naked mole rats treats them as embarrassing for Hamilton simply because they are not haplodiploid, despite haplodiploidy occupying only four of Hamilton's fifty pages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p369]
- Hamilton himself suggested in 1972 a cyclic inbreeding theory for termites: incestuous royal pairs make offspring highly homozygous, and when two inbred reproductives from different colonies mate, their offspring are uniformly heterozygous. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p369]
- During the incest phase of termite colonies, an individual is typically closer genetically to siblings than to potential offspring, providing a precondition for sterile worker castes analogous to haplodiploidy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p370]
- Even without special relatedness effects, monogamy alone makes siblings genetically as valuable as offspring, making the surprising thing that there aren't more species with sterile workers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p370]
- Grafen pointed out that workers may be indifferent between rearing siblings and offspring at any population sex ratio because rare males in a female-biased population have high reproductive value. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p372]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prevalence of 'helping at the nest' in birds and mammals represents a 'watered-down' version of the sterile worker phenomenon, consistent with the 'going concern' or 'hollow tree' principle.
- Inference: The termite cyclic inbreeding theory shows that genetic conditions for eusociality can arise through mechanisms other than haplodiploidy.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact conditions under which the Hamilton relatedness hypothesis versus the cyclic inbreeding theory best explain eusociality remain to be determined.
