# Eye-Head System

## Summary

The eye-head system refers to the integrated functioning of eyes and head for visual exploration. Gibson distinguishes five types of eye movements: fixation, saccadic movement, pursuit movement, convergence-divergence, and compensatory movement. The compensatory eye-head coordination is particularly emphasized as keeping eyes anchored to the environment during head turning, preventing the world from appearing to swing.

## Key facts

- The compensatory eye movement compensates for head turning by rotating eyes in precisely the opposite direction to the same degree, keeping them anchored to the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p232]
- When the head turns, the eyes turn in the opposite direction through the same angle, maintaining stable orientation to the environment except for brief jumps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p237]
- The purpose of compensatory eye-head coordination is to keep the eyes oriented to unchanging features of the environment while the observer looks around. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p237]
- Humans have frontal eyes requiring head turning to look around, whereas horses have lateral eyes allowing panoramic vision without head movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p225]
- Saccadic eye movements cannot be explained as responses to retinal stimuli bringing objects to the fovea—this is a fallacy from artificial laboratory conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: The eye-head system exemplifies nested perceptual organs—body contains head contains eyes—each with exploratory functions at different scales.
