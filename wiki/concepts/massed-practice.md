# Massed Practice

## Summary

Massed practice is a training principle developed by Edward Taub that involves concentrating an extraordinary amount of exercise into a short time period. In CI therapy, this means six hours of training per day for ten to fifteen consecutive days. Taub found this intensive, concentrated training far more effective than long-term but less frequent training. The principle is similar to language immersion, where intensive exposure and practice trigger faster and more complete learning.

## Key facts

- CI therapy involves six hours of training per day for ten to fifteen days straight. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p155]
- Taub calls this intensive, concentrated training 'massed practice' and found it far more effective than long-term but less frequent training. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]
- Massed practice helps rewire patients' brains by triggering plastic changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]
- Taub notes that three hours a day may produce good results, and increasing movements per hour is better than the exhausting six hours of treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]
