# Alternate Rebellion and Adaptive Denial

## Summary

Two DBT distress tolerance strategies for managing addiction urges. Alternate rebellion provides alternative ways to express rebellious impulses without engaging in addictive behaviors. Adaptive denial involves reframing urges and using time-based delay strategies to resist addiction.

## Key facts

- Alternate rebellion involves planning and executing alternative behaviors when the urge for addictive behaviors arises, providing ways to express rebellious impulses without engaging in actual addiction. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p410]
- Adaptive denial strategies include reframing an urge for a problem behavior as an urge for something else, putting off addictive behavior for specified time periods, and reminding oneself that only temporary abstinence (an hour or a day) is required. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p410]

## Inferences

- Inference: These strategies address the psychological function that addiction serves (rebellion, denial of vulnerability) by providing alternative means to achieve those psychological outcomes.
