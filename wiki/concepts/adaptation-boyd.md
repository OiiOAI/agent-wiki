# Adaptation (Boyd's central theme)

## Summary

Adaptation is the key theme in Boyd's work, encompassing the capability to evolve, learn, and adapt better and faster than opponents. Boyd argues that to survive and prosper, one must create and maintain fit with the environment while denying this capability to adversaries.

## Key facts

- To adapt means to create and maintain a fit with the environment, which one wants to deny opponents through conventional or guerrilla warfare. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p285]
- Boyd made the processes of thinking, learning and adaptation key military concepts, being the first strategist to do so. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p293]
- The focus on adaptability shows in Boyd's discussion of modes of conflict, where he acknowledges preferences but avoids dogmatism. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p293]
- Boyd describes A Discourse as applicable to all types of warfare and extends it to economies, societies, and science. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p294]
- The larger theme of Boyd's work is multidimensional organizational adaptation in a dynamic non-linear environment. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p295]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's focus on adaptation connects his military theory to evolution theory, complexity theory, and organizational learning, suggesting A Discourse is better described as a general theory of strategic behavior of complex adaptive systems in adversarial conditions.
