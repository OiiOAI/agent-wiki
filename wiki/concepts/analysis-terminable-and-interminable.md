# Analysis Terminable and Interminable

## Summary

This 1937 paper by Freud examines the limitations and difficulties of psychoanalytic therapy. Freud argues that successful analytic outcomes depend on three factors: traumatic etiology (favorable), constitutional strength of drives (unfavorable), and alterations of the ego acquired during defensive struggles (unfavorable). He emphasizes the paramount importance of the quantitative factor—the relation between drive strength and ego strength—and concludes that analysis can only succeed when the ego is not too severely altered by its defensive processes.

- The outcome of analytic treatment depends on three factors: the influence of traumas, the constitutional strength of the drives, and alterations of the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7377]
- Cases of predominantly traumatic etiology offer the most favorable field for analysis and can be definitively ended. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7374]
- A constitutional strength of drive and an unfavorable alteration of the ego are the factors which prejudice the effectiveness of analysis and may make its duration interminable. - The quantitative factor—the relation between the strength of the drive and the strength of the ego—determines whether repressions can hold or will fail under increased drive pressure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7379]
- In every phase of the patient's recovery, the analyst has to fight against his inertia, which is ready to be content with an incomplete solution. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7384]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud suggests that shortening the duration of analytic treatment cannot be achieved without compromising therapeutic efficacy; rather, analysis requires sufficient time to address the ego's alterations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges uncertainty about whether analysis can permanently dispose of drive conflicts, noting that his experience 'is not yet wide enough for us to come to a settled conclusion.'
