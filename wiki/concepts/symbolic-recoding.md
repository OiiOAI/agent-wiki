# Symbolic Recoding

## Summary

Symbolic recoding is the cognitive process by which independently learned indexical associations (token-object correlations) are reorganized with respect to token-token relationships, shifting mnemonic strategy from remembering individual correlations to tracking the system of logical relationships between tokens. This transition takes no additional learning time—it is an insight-like recognition.

## Key facts

- Symbolic recoding is initially a change in mnemonic strategy, offloading redundant details from working memory by recognizing higher-order regularity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p95]
- The recoding is not an added learning step but the noticing of system-level correspondences implicitly present between token-token and object-object relationships. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p99]
- Symbolic recoding produces implicit knowledge as a spontaneous byproduct—Sherman and Austin 'knew' something they had never explicitly learned. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p98]
- Once recoded, competing indexical associations become mutually supportive semantic categories providing mnemonic redundancy rather than interference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p103]

## Inferences

- Inference: The shift to symbolic reference enables logical or categorical generalization—correct responses to novel items based on abstract features (e.g., solidity) rather than prior exposure.
