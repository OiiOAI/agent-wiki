# Mitochondrial Dysfunction in Neurodegeneration

## Summary

Mitochondrial dysfunction is a key contributor to neurodegenerative diseases including Parkinson disease and the polyglutamine disorders. Studies have revealed that proteins implicated in Parkinson disease, including parkin, PINK1, and DJ-1, regulate mitochondrial turnover and quality control. Loss of PINK1 function leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and dopaminergic neuron impairment, and parkin and PINK1 work together in a process called mitophagy to remove damaged mitochondria.

## Key facts

- Mitochondrial dysfunction is evident in polyglutamine disorders and Parkinson disease, with both morphological and functional studies providing evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1556]
- Parkin and PINK1 regulate mitochondrial turnover in a process termed mitophagy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1556]
- Loss of PINK1 in Drosophila leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, dopaminergic neuron impairment, and motor abnormalities that can be rescued by parkin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1556]
- Lymphoblast mitochondria from Huntington disease patients and brain mitochondria from Huntington mouse models have lower membrane potential and depolarize at lower Ca2+ loads than controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1556]
- Dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta are particularly vulnerable due to their dependence on Ca2+ channels for rhythmic firing, causing baseline mitochondrial stress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1550]
