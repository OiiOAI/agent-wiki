# Axon Initial Segment

## Summary

The axon initial segment (AIS) is a specialized region of the axon where the density of voltage-gated sodium channels is highest, resulting in the lowest threshold for action potential generation in the neuron. Synaptic potentials from dendrites and cell body must be transmitted passively to the AIS for integration and action potential initiation.

## Key facts

- The axon initial segment has a lower threshold for action potential generation than the cell body or dendrites because it has a higher density of voltage-dependent Na+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- At the initial segment, the depolarization increment required to reach threshold for an action potential (−55 mV) is only 10 mV from the resting level of −65 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- The membrane of the cell body must be depolarized by 30 mV before reaching its threshold (−35 mV), in contrast to the 10 mV required at the initial segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p339]
- The decision as to whether a neuron fires an action potential is determined by the size of the resulting depolarization at the axon initial segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p341]
