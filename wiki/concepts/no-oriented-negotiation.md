# No-Oriented Negotiation

## Summary

No-Oriented Negotiation is the approach of seeking and embracing 'No' from counterparts rather than pursuing 'Yes.' This technique provides the speaker with feelings of safety and control, and 'No' often marks the beginning of real negotiation rather than its end.

## Key facts

- 'No' is not rejection but often means 'I am not yet ready to agree,' 'You are making me feel uncomfortable,' 'I do not understand,' or 'I want to talk it over with someone else'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p72]
- Saying 'No' gives the speaker feelings of safety, security, and control, and prompts them to articulate what they actually want. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p80]
- A test-market of 'No'-oriented fund-raising scripts by Ben Ottenhoff achieved a 23 percent better rate of return than traditional 'Yes pattern' scripts. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p82]
- Jim Camp's book Start with NO advises giving the counterpart permission to say 'No' from the outset, called 'the right to veto'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p71]
- The email technique to provoke 'No' uses the sentence 'Have you given up on this project?' which offers the other party feelings of safety while encouraging them to define their position. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p83]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'No'-oriented approach works because it satisfies the primal human needs for autonomy and control, reducing defensiveness and opening genuine engagement
