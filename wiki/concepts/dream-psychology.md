# Dream Psychology

## Summary

Dream psychology concerns the distinctive mental processes operating during sleep, including the transformation of thoughts into vivid images, the hallucinatory character of dream experience, and the dramatization of dream content as seemingly real events.

## Key facts

- Freud stated that dreams think essentially in images rather than concepts, and with sleep approaching, involuntary ideas arise all of which fall into the class of images. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1328]
- Fechner proposed that 'the scene of action of dreams is different from that of waking ideational life,' suggesting dreams involve a different mode of mental functioning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1327]
- Dreams construct situations and represent events that seem to be actually happening, with the dreamer attaching complete belief to hallucinations until waking. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1329]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's later Chapter VII develops Fechner's suggestion into a full account of the mental apparatus with systems for perception, memory, and unconscious thought operating differently during sleep.
- Inference: The dramatization of ideas in dreams reflects the primary process operating without the ego's reality-testing functions.
