# Mesencephalic Locomotor Region

## Summary

The mesencephalic locomotor region (MLR) is a midbrain structure that initiates and maintains locomotion and controls speed. Located in or around the cuneiform nucleus, the MLR is composed of the cuneiform nucleus (CNF) and pedunculopontine nucleus (PPN). Glutamatergic neurons in both nuclei control speed and gait selection, with alternating gaits at slower speeds and synchronous gaits like gallop and bound at higher speeds. The MLR projects to brain stem reticular formation neurons that descend to spinal locomotor circuits.

## Key facts

- The MLR was first identified in cats as a region in or around the cuneiform nucleus below the inferior colliculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p847]
- The MLR consists of the cuneiform nucleus (CNF) with glutamatergic neurons and the pedunculopontine nucleus (PPN) with both glutamatergic and cholinergic neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p847]
- Increasing stimulation intensity of the MLR progressively increases locomotion speed and switches from alternating gaits to synchronous gaits like gallop and bound. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p847]
- The MLR projects to neurons in the medial reticular formation whose axons descend in the ventrolateral funiculus to the spinal locomotor system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p848]
- Mark Shik, Fidor Severin, and Grigori Orlovsky found that tonic electrical stimulation of the mesencephalic locomotor region initiates stepping when decerebrate animals are placed on a treadmill. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p876]
- The rhythm of the locomotor pattern is related only to the intensity of electrical stimulation of the MLR, not to its pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p876]
- Weak stimulation of the MLR produces a slow walking gait, which accelerates as intensity increases, with stronger stimulation producing trotting and finally galloping. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p877]
- Signals from the MLR are transmitted to the spinal cord by glutamatergic neurons with axons traveling in ventral reticulospinal pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p877]
