# Goal Values

## Summary

Goal values represent the rewarding values of specific outcomes and control actions that are sensitive to both the contingency between actions and outcomes and to changes in the value of those outcomes. Psychologically, goal-directed actions satisfy two criteria: knowledge of the action-outcome contingency and valuation of the outcome as a desirable goal. Neural evidence implicates dorsomedial striatum and medial prefrontal cortex in encoding goal values.

## Key facts

- Goal-directed actions depend on two factors: knowledge of the contingency between the action and some outcome, and the valuation of that outcome as a desirable goal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p368]
- Goal values are computationally grounded in both transition and reward functions, making them sensitive to both contingency and outcome value manipulations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p359]
- Lesions of dorsomedial striatum in rats impair both learning and expression of goal-directed behavior if induced either before or after training. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p382]
