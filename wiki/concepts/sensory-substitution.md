# Sensory Substitution

## Summary

Sensory substitution is the process of feeding information from one sense to the brain via a different sensory channel. Paul Bach-y-Rita pioneered this field, demonstrating that if one sense is damaged, another can take over for it. His devices convert signals (visual, balance-related, etc.) into electrical patterns delivered through skin surfaces, particularly the tongue, which has no insensitive layer and serves as an ideal 'brain-machine interface.' The brain then learns to interpret these signals as the original sense.

## Key facts

- Bach-y-Rita defined sensory substitution as the process where if one sense is damaged, another can sometimes take over for it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p24]
- The tongue serves as the ideal 'brain-machine interface' because it has no insensitive layer of dead skin. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p29]
- The brain processes electrical patterns universally—all sense receptors translate different energies into electrical signals that are the 'universal language' inside the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p28]
- Brain scans confirmed that tactile images entering through tongues are processed in the brain's visual cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p34]
