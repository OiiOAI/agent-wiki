# Distress Tolerance

## Summary

Distress tolerance is one of the four pillars of DBT and refers to the ability to handle emotional stressors. Peter Attia visualizes distress tolerance as a vertical window that opens and closes—narrowing increases dysregulation. Many behaviors expand this window including exercise, sound sleep, good nutrition, family time, medications, deep social connections, time in nature, and recreational activities free of self-judgment.

## Key facts

- Distress tolerance can be visualized as a window that opens and closes vertically; the narrower this window becomes, the more likely one is to become dysregulated. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- Goals should be to keep the distress tolerance window as wide as possible and to be attentive to anything that might narrow it. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- Behaviors that expand the distress tolerance window include: exercise, sound sleep, good nutrition, time with family, medications, deep social connections, spending time in nature, and recreational activities without self-judgment. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p409]
