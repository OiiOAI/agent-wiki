# Affects in Dreams

## Summary

Affects in dreams are emotional experiences that accompany dream content. Freud argues that affects experienced in dreams are in no way inferior to those experienced in waking life. Analysis shows that while ideational content undergoes displacement and substitution through dream distortion, affects remain largely unaltered. This means that the affect accompanying dream material may not match the displaced ideational content, creating apparent emotional inconsistencies. Affects are the constituent least influenced by dream distortion and can serve as pointers to the original unconscious material.

- Analysis shows that ideational material undergoes displacements and substitutions whereas affects remain unaltered during dream distortion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p412]

- The release of affect and the ideational content do not constitute an indissoluble organic unity; they may be detached from each other by analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p412]
- Affect may appear completely detached from its original idea and be introduced at another point in the dream, following the principle of antithesis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p414]
- The detachment of affects from the ideational material which generated them is the most striking thing which occurs during the formation of dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1713]
- A dream is in general poorer in affect than the psychical material from the manipulation of which it has proceeded; the dream-work brings about a suppression of affects. - The suppression of affect is a consequence of the inhibition which the contraries in the dream thoughts exercise upon each other and which the censorship exercises upon the impulsions suppressed by it. - Every element in a dream can, for purposes of interpretation, stand for its opposite just as easily as for itself; this also applies to the affects attaching to dream thoughts. - Affects in dreams are fed from a confluence of several sources and are overdetermined in their reference to the material of the dream thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1729]

## Inferences

- Inference: The persistence of affects when ideational content is displaced suggests that affects in dreams originate from unconscious processes that are more directly connected to emotional centers than to secondary process thinking.
- Inference: Freud proposes that the release of affects is analogous to motor and secretory innervation—a centrifugal process directed towards the interior of the body. During sleep, the sending out of motor impulses towards the external world appears to be suspended, making the centrifugal calling up of affects more difficult.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that no complete theoretical explanation of the suppression of affect can be given without a most painstaking investigation of the theory of affects and the mechanism of repression.
