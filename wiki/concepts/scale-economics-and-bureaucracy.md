# Scale Economics and Bureaucracy

## Summary

Scale economics refer to advantages that larger organizations possess, including purchasing power, cost reductions, and specialization. However, Munger emphasizes that scale also creates disadvantages through bureaucracy, perverse incentives, and organizational inertia, as demonstrated by Sears' decline versus Walmart's rise.

## Key facts

- The great defect of scale is that as organizations get big, they get bureaucracy and with it territoriality grounded in human nature. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p170]
- Sam Walton 'invented practically nothing' but copied everything smart and did it with more fanaticism and better employee manipulation. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p174]
- Sears had 'layers and layers of people it didn't need' and was slow to think, while Walmart was lean and mean. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p175]
- Jack Welch's strategy at GE was: 'We're either going to be #1 or #2 in every field we're in or we're going to be out.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p169]
- Chain stores gain huge purchasing advantages, laboratory experimentation capabilities, and specialization benefits. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p173]
