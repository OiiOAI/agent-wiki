# Two Moments Of Resistance

## Summary

Most resistance to new behaviors occurs at two specific moments: before starting and after starting when considering doing more. The brain resists change but is more tolerant of small steps. Mini habits address the first resistance by requiring impossibly small actions. The second resistance can often be shattered by the first step, but if not, additional micro-goals can be set to continue.

## Key facts

- The cat-in-snow analogy demonstrates that springing massive changes on the brain causes it to retreat to comfortable routines, while introducing changes gently in small doses makes it curious to explore. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p59]
- The first wall of resistance is at the very start of any task; once you take action, there is a second wave of resistance related to continuing or doing more. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p60]
- The basal ganglia doesn't care to 'defend' against small steps, only drastic changes; by changing slowly and taking one step at a time, you play by your brain's rules. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p60]
- When the author thought about doing even a 10-minute chunk during his first workout, his brain said no, but when he asked to just get the exercise mat out, it said yes. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p60]

## Inferences

- Inference: The strategy of setting additional micro-goals ('one more,' 'two more') allows continuation without triggering the brain's resistance to larger commitments.
