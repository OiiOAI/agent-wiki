# Sensory-Motor Learning

## Summary

Sensory-motor learning describes how the brain learns models of the world by observing how sensory inputs change as we move. Unlike learning a melody (which has fixed order), most world knowledge requires active exploration because the order of sensations depends on our movements. The brain predicts the next sensory input based on movement, updating its model when predictions fail.

## Key facts

- The brain learns its model of the world by observing how sensory inputs change as we move; there is no other way for the brain to learn. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p44]
- To learn a model of a new house, you have to look in different directions and walk from room to room; without movement, no learning occurs. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p45]
- To learn what a computer mouse feels like, you must run your fingers over it; to learn what it looks like, you must view it from different angles. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p45]
- With sensory-motor learning, the order of sensations is not fixed; what you see when entering a room depends on which direction you turn your head. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p45]
- The question of how the neocortex works can be phrased as: how does it learn a predictive model through movement?. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p45]

## Inferences

- Inference: This theory explains why children learn so much about the physical world through play and exploration, and suggests that passive observation alone is insufficient for deep learning.
