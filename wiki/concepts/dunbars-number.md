# Dunbar's Number

## Summary

Dunbar's number, after anthropologist Robin Dunbar, identifies natural limits on group sizes and the depth of relationships humans can maintain. Research shows approximately five people for close personal relationships and working memory, fifteen for deep trust, fifty for mutual trust, and 150 for remembering capabilities. In software organizations, effective teams should be around five to nine people, with Amazon's 'two-pizza' rule being a well-known example. Larger groupings should also follow these limits, with tribes limited to about fifty people and divisions to around 150 or 500.

## Key facts

- Dunbar found fifteen to be the limit of people one person can deeply trust, with around five people known and trusted closely. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L862-L864]
- Anthropological research shows around five people is the limit for close personal relationships and working memory. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L872-L873]
- Around fifteen people is the limit for experiencing deep trust within a group. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L873-L874]
- Around fifty people is the limit for mutual trust within larger groupings. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L874-L875]
- Around 150 people is the limit for remembering capabilities of others in a group. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L875-L876]
- Amazon is known for limiting the size of its software teams to those that can be fed by two pizzas, roughly seven to nine people. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L860-L862]
- Effective software teams should have a maximum size of around seven to nine people. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L858-L859]
- Groupings of teams (tribes) should be limited to no more than fifty people. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L881-L882]
- High-trust organizations may sustain larger teams of up to around fifteen people, but such organizations are rare. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L867-L869]
- Dunbar calculated from human neocortex ratios that the social group size is approximately 150, confirmed by studies of hunter-gatherer clans and horticultural societies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p103]
- Dunbar's number matches traditional Christmas card list sizes, military unit organization sizes, and upper limits of informally-run businesses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p103]
- Conversation groups self-limit to about four individuals, which Dunbar correlates with chimp grooming where one grooms three others simultaneously, explaining the 3 × 55 = 165 calculation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p105]
