# Dissociation

## Summary

Dissociation is a mental state where the mind detaches from the world or from itself, occurring naturally when we experience negative surprise. It helps us grow by separating our brain from its old worldview, providing space to develop new rules. However, it can become unhealthy if the brain cannot develop those new rules, leading to mental numbness and absence of joy. Mindfulness and meditation can inadvertently reinforce dissociation by prompting it.

## Key facts

- Dissociation occurs when our mind detaches from the world or from itself, happening naturally when we experience a negative surprise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p122]
- Dissociation helps us grow by separating our brain from its old worldview, providing space to develop new rules of life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p123]
- Dissociation can turn unhealthy if the brain cannot imagine new rules that aren't too dark, too grim, or too nihilistic — or if it cannot imagine any new rules at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p123]
- Mindfulness and meditation work by prompting dissociation, potentially reinforcing rather than alleviating the patient's root condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p124]
