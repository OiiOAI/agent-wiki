# Contextual Modulation

## Summary

Contextual modulation refers to the interaction of two classes of inputs to a neural circuit: primary driving inputs and modulatory inputs that control the system response. The driving inputs are typically feed-forward connections from earlier processing areas, while modulatory inputs arise from recurrent interactions at the same level or feedback from higher areas. This modulation implements a tuning of the neural input-output function and is fundamental to both sensory processing and value coding in decision circuits.

## Key facts

- Contextual modulation is defined as the interaction of two classes of inputs to a given neuron or neural circuit: primary driving inputs and modulatory inputs that control the system response to driving inputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p126]
- The prototypical example of contextual modulation is surround suppression in visual cortical neurons, where responses to stimuli in the classical receptive field are decreased by stimuli in the nonclassical receptive field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p126]
- Contextual modulation implements nonlinear processing that can effectively decorrelate the outputs of a neural circuit, producing higher information densities under the efficient coding framework. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p126]
- Both spatial and temporal contextual modulation contribute to efficient coding by accounting for statistical regularities in the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p126]
- Contextual modulation of neural responses is a widespread phenomenon governing neural responses from low-level features like stimulus orientation to high-level features like invariant object identity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p127]
