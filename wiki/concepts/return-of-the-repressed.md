# The Return of the Repressed

## Summary

The return of the repressed is a psychoanalytic mechanism describing how repressed material from the unconscious resurfaces in distorted form. Freud illustrates this with character development examples (a girl who opposes her mother later becomes like her; Goethe's old age resembling his father) and applies it to collective phenomena, arguing that Moses's monotheism represented a tradition reviving a primal memory of the great father that had been repressed after the primalhorde was dissolved.

## Key facts

- Processes analogous to the return of the repressed occur in normal character development: identifications made in early childhood are later repudiated but ultimately re-establish themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7287]
- The strongest compulsive influence arises from impressions received when the child's psychical apparatus is not yet completely receptive; these impressions can be developed 'after any interval of time' like a photographic exposure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7287]
- Neurotic symptoms are described as 'the return of the repressed,' characterized by far-reaching distortion of the returning material compared to the original. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7289]
- Freud applies this mechanism to Jewish history: the religion of Moses was rejected and half-forgotten, then broke through as tradition, transforming Yahweh into the god of Moses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7286]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of return of the repressed applied to religious history suggests that collective psychological processes mirror individual neurotic mechanisms, with tradition functioning as inherited memory traces.
- Inference: The comparison between individual childhood trauma and collective primaeval events implies a phylogenetic dimension to the unconscious, where species-wide experiences leave permanent psychic residues.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the 'phylogenetic inheritance' of primal memories actually exists cannot be empirically verified within psychoanalytic methodology.
