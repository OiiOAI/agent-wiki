# Metabolic Flexibility

## Summary

Metabolic flexibility is the body's ability to adapt fuel utilization to changes in fuel availability, enabling someone to function well without food for extended periods and handle varied diets without adverse reactions. It is achieved by eating a diet rich in whole foods—particularly 800 grams of fruits and vegetables daily and adequate protein—while avoiding constant snacking and processed foods that cause blood sugar instability.

## Key facts

- Metabolic flexibility is defined as the body's ability to adapt fuel utilization to changes in fuel availability. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1561-L1562]
- A metabolically flexible person can wake up in the morning, eat nothing, and go do a workout or work without becoming exhausted. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1563-L1565]
- A metabolically flexible person can handle foods that are high in fat and calories without feeling sick or having diarrhea. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1565-L1567]
- Metabolic flexibility is a characteristic of diabetes, and metabolic inflexibility is associated with this condition. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1599-L1600]
- With metabolic flexibility, someone is less likely to need to snack constantly or refuel during simple activities like a two-hour hike because blood sugar stays stable. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1600-L1603]
- Metabolic flexibility is promoted by eating 800 grams of fruits and vegetables per day, monitoring protein intake, and eating regular meals rather than constant snacking. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1612-L1616]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book implies that metabolic flexibility was normal in prehistoric humans because food availability was erratic, and our bodies evolved to handle feast and famine cycles.
- Inference: The book suggests that modern eating patterns (constant snacking, processed foods) have compromised our natural metabolic flexibility.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The book does not cite specific studies proving that improving diet will restore metabolic flexibility in individuals who have lost it.
