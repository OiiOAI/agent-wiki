# Right to Disconnect

## Summary

The 'right to disconnect' is a legal principle that entitles workers to clearly defined work hours, during which they can be contacted by employers, with all other hours being out of bounds. France implemented this law in 2016 after research showed that the expectation of constant availability was causing widespread anxiety and burnout.

## Key facts

- In 2016, France passed a law establishing the right to disconnect, requiring companies with more than fifty people to formally negotiate hours when workers can be contacted. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p211]
- One study found that a third of French professionals felt they could never unplug for fear of missing an expected email. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p210]
- Another study found that just the expectation of being on call causes workers anxiety, even if they don't actually get contacted on any given night. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p210]
- The pest-control company Rentokil had to pay a branch manager €60,000 after he was penalized for not responding to off-hours emails. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p211]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The effectiveness of the law depends on enforcement; the book notes that change is happening too slowly because the law is not being enforced by a tough regulator.
