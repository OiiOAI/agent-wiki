# Defence (Abwehr)

## Summary

Defence (Abwehr) is the psychical mechanism by which an incompatible idea threatening the patient's happiness or self-esteem is deliberately repressed from consciousness. This refusal on the part of the patient's ego to accept the incompatible idea constitutes the motive for the splitting of consciousness in hysteria, while conversion constitutes its mechanism.

## Key facts

- The motive for splitting of consciousness is defence (Abwehr): the refusal on the part of the patient's whole ego to come to terms with an incompatible ideational group. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p166-167]
- Ideas from which consciousness is diverted, which are not thought about, are withdrawn from the wearing-away process and retain their quota of affect undiminished. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p214]
- The patient succeeded in sparing herself the painful conviction that she loved her sister's husband by inducing physical pains instead; it was in moments when this conviction sought to force itself upon her that her pains came on, thanks to successful conversion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p139-140]

## Inferences

- Inference: The resistance with which patients meet the reproduction of traumatic scenes corresponds to the energy with which the incompatible idea had been forced out of their associations.
