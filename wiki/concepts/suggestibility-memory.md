# Suggestibility (Memory)

## Summary

Memory suggestibility refers to an individual's tendency to incorporate misleading information from external sources into personal recollections. It is closely related to misattribution because the conversion of suggestions into inaccurate memories involves misattribution. Suggestibility can create false memories in eyewitnesses, therapy patients, and children, with serious legal and social consequences.

## Key facts

- Suggestibility in memory refers to an individual's tendency to incorporate misleading information from external sources into personal recollections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p156]
- In the El Al crash study, 55% of respondents claimed to have seen television film of the plane hitting an apartment building, even though no such film existed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p155]
- Suggestive questions can create false memories even when people recall that the misinformation was suggested, demonstrating the power of misleading suggestions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p157]
- Confirming feedback like saying 'okay' after an eyewitness identification can significantly inflate the witness's confidence in their memory, even when the identification is incorrect. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p159]
