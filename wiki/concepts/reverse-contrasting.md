# Reverse Contrasting

## Summary

Reverse contrasting (also called indulging) is a control condition and alternative strategy where individuals imagine a desired future and then focus on positive aspects of the present reality rather than obstacles. Unlike mental contrasting, reverse contrasting does not create cognitive associations between obstacles and instrumental behaviors and does not change how obstacles are perceived. Research consistently shows it does not produce the beneficial effects of mental contrasting.

## Key facts

- In reverse contrasting, participants imagine the positive future and then the positive aspects of the present reality, without contrasting future with obstacles. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p89]
- Students who reverse contrasted showed no relationship between cognitive association strength and expectations of future success. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p91]
- In reverse contrasting groups, the meaning of reality did not change significantly as students became more confident they could attain wishes. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p93]
- Over 40% of people spontaneously indulge in positive fantasies compared to only 16% who spontaneously mentally contrast. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p120]

## Inferences

- Inference: Reverse contrasting represents the more natural human tendency to focus on positive outcomes without confronting obstacles.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether reverse contrasting has any independent benefits beyond mental contrasting is not addressed.
