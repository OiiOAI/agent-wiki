# Inferiority Complex

## Summary

An inferiority complex differs from a healthy feeling of inferiority. While feelings of inferiority are universal and can serve as catalysts for growth, an inferiority complex is a condition where someone uses their feeling of inferiority as an excuse not to act, thinking 'I cannot succeed because of X.' This leads to fabricated causal relationships and avoiding challenges rather than overcoming them.

## Key facts

- Feeling of inferiority and inferiority complex are distinct: the former is healthy and motivates growth, while the latter is an excuse for inaction. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p85]
- The inferiority complex uses apparent cause-and-effect reasoning: 'I'm not well educated, so I can't succeed,' when in reality the person lacks the courage to change. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p85]
- People who develop inferiority complexes do not have the courage to compensate through striving and growth, leading them to claim 'If only I had X, I would be successful.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p87]

## Inferences

- Inference: The statement 'I can't because of X' actually translates to 'I don't want to try because it's scary.'
