# Sensory Clarity

## Summary

Sensory clarity is the capacity to perceive body sensations (and other sensory input) with high resolution, specificity, and detail. The book argues that modern humans have become trapped in the 'conceptual mind,' reducing rich sensory experiences to mental shorthand (e.g., 'tree') and missing the actual sensory richness of experience. Sensory clarity practice involves investigating sensations at increasingly fine levels of detail: exploring 3D morphology, temporal changes (pulsing, shifting), subtle textures, and micro-regions. The book claims this practice develops the insula (brain region associated with visceral sensation), creates a feedback loop of enhanced perception, and allows meditators to discover subtler dimensions of experience that are normally hidden. Viktor Frankl's quote about the 'space between stimulus and response' is cited as describing the behavioral flexibility that increased sensory clarity provides.

## Key facts

- Modern humans are described as 'stuck in the head'—over-conceptualizing experience and missing direct sensory contact. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p173]
- Sensory clarity practice involves investigating body sensations in fine detail: 3D shape, changes over time, subtle textures, and micro-regions. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p180]
- The book claims sensory clarity practice develops the insula, a brain region associated with feeling body sensations. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p178]
- Viktor Frankl is quoted: 'Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p135]
- Increasing sensory clarity is presented as the key to breaking through meditation plateaus after initial progress. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conceptual mind evolved for efficiency (processing 'tree' is faster than experiencing all bark textures), but this efficiency has a cost of reduced sensory richness and enjoyment.
