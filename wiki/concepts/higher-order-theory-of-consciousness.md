# Higher-Order Theory of Consciousness

## Summary

Higher-order theory (HOT) proposes that conscious awareness results when nonconscious first-order sensory information is cognitively re-represented by a higher-order mental state. David Rosenthal's version holds that only the lower-order state is consciously experienced; the higher-order state makes consciousness possible. LeDoux favors HOT over global workspace theory and extends it with a multistate hierarchical model involving prefrontal circuits.

## Key facts

- In HOT, conscious awareness occurs when nonconscious first-order sensory information is re-represented by a higher-order state. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p276]
- Hakwan Lau and Richard Brown proposed HOROR theory (higher-order representation of a representation), suggesting higher-order states alone may be sufficient for conscious experience even when first-order states are absent (as in Charles Bonnet syndrome hallucinations). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p277]
- Lau and Rosenthal identified the dorsal lateral prefrontal cortex, ventral lateral prefrontal cortex, and frontal pole as key nodes in the higher-order consciousness network. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p279]
- The frontal pole is uniquely developed in humans and has the greatest capacity of any brain area for conceptual processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p279]
- LeDoux extends HOT into a multistate hierarchical model where the higher-order prefrontal network draws on diverse lower-order inputs: sensory, memory, conceptual, and self-related representations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p302]

## Inferences

- Inference: LeDoux prefers HOT over GWT because HOT attempts to explain how conscious content is actually experienced, not just how information becomes globally available.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Critics argue prefrontal areas lack fine-grain representations for rich perceptual experiences; LeDoux addresses this by noting that higher-order representations can draw on diverse lower-order content.
