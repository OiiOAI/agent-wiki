# Calibrated Questions

## Summary

Calibrated Questions are open-ended queries that begin with 'How?' or 'What?' that the other side can respond to but that have no fixed answers. They buy time, give the counterpart the illusion of control, and force them to apply mental energy to solving your problems without giving away your position.

## Key facts

- Calibrated questions are described as 'queries that the other side can respond to but that have no fixed answers,' buying time and giving the counterpart the illusion of control. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p9]
- Voss used calibrated questions against Harvard professors Mnookin and Blum, asking 'How am I supposed to do that?' which shifted the conversation to how they would solve his problems. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p9]
- The key calibrated question 'How am I supposed to know he is alive?' was used to frustrate Mnookin and shift the negotiation frame. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p9]
- Voss describes the approach as 'passive-aggressive,' using the same three or four open-ended questions repeatedly until counterparts 'get worn out answering'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p12]
- Calibrated questions avoid verbs like 'can,' 'is,' 'are,' 'do,' or 'does' which create closed-ended yes/no responses. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p136]
- The question 'How am I supposed to do that?' serves as a gentle way to say 'no' without triggering defensive reactions. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p147]
- Starting questions with 'why' is accusatory in any language and should be avoided except when defensiveness supports your goal. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p136]
- Calibrated questions offer no target for attack and educate counterparts on problems rather than causing conflict by telling them what the problem is. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p135]
- The secret to gaining upper hand is giving the other side the illusion of control through calibrated questions. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p137]

## Inferences

- Inference: Calibrated questions work because they engage the counterpart's neocortex (rational brain) rather than triggering the reptilian fight-or-flight response, making them more receptive to problem-solving.
