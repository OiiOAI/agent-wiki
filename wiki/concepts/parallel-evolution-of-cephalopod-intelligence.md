# Parallel evolution of cephalopod intelligence

## Summary

The hypothesis that complex nervous systems evolved at least twice within cephalopods—once in the octopus lineage and once in the lineage leading to cuttlefish and squid—based on genetic evidence and cognitive studies showing episodic-like memory in cuttlefish.

## Key facts

- Genetic evidence shows that protocadherins diversified in octopus evolution and separately in squid evolution after their split, indicating at least three expansions of brain-building molecules. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p155]
- Christelle Jozet-Alves and colleagues demonstrated episodic-like memory in cuttlefish using a 'what-where-when' test, showing memory of where and when a certain food was available. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p155]
- The existence of episodic-like memory in mammals, birds, and cuttlefish is a striking example of what is almost certainly parallel evolution in different lines. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p156]
- The relation between octopuses and cuttlefish is analogous to the relation between mammals and birds, with the split around 270 million years ago leading to parallel evolution of large brains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p156]

## Inferences

- Inference: The parallel evolution of intelligence within cephalopods suggests it was not an accident but a predictable outcome of certain evolutionary pressures.
