# Lexical Difference Principle

## Summary

A more general formulation of the difference principle to cover cases where close-knitness fails: (1) first maximize the welfare of the worst-off representative man; (2) for equal welfare of the worst-off, maximize the welfare of the second worst-off, and so on; (3) for equal welfare of all preceding n—1 representatives, maximize the welfare of the best-off. Rawls states he will use the simpler difference principle form but the lexical version provides fuller rigor for edge cases.

## Key facts

- The lexical difference principle states: first maximize the welfare of the worst-off representative man; second, for equal welfare of the worst-off, maximize the welfare of the second worst-off, and so on until the last case where for equal welfare of all preceding n-1 representatives, maximize the welfare of the best-off. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p85]
- Close-knitness is assumed to simplify the statement of the difference principle but is not essential; the lexical version covers cases where the least advantaged are not affected by some changes in expectations of the best off. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p85]
- Rawls will always use the difference principle in the simpler form; the lexical version is provided for completeness. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p85]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The lexical ordering of the two principles (prioritizing liberty over economic benefits) is discussed in later sections (§§39, 82) rather than fully justified here.
