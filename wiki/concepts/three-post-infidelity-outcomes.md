# The Three Post-Infidelity Outcomes

## Summary

Esther Perel's categorization of how couples who choose to stay together after infidelity end up in one of three distinct patterns: (1) Sufferers, who remain stuck in bitterness and recrimination; (2) Builders, who revert to a peaceful status quo without significant transformation; and (3) Explorers, who use the crisis as a catalyst for deeper connection and personal growth. This framework is adapted from Helen Fisher's work on love and attachment.

## Key facts

- Sufferers are couples in which the affair becomes a 'black hole ensnaring both parties in an endless round of bitterness, revenge, and self-pity,' with the affair tagged onto every disagreement years later. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p292]
- The case study of Debbie and Marc illustrates the Sufferers: Marc's dalliances 'take up more space in their bed today than when they were happening,' and Debbie hasn't had sex with him since the affair three years prior. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p292]
- Builders remain together because they 'value commitment and the life they've created' but their marriages 'revert to a more or less peaceful version of the status quo antebellum—the way things were, without their relationship undergoing any significant change'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p293]
- Perel quotes Joanna: for builders, 'forgiveness did not come with the fanfare of epiphany but with pain gathering up its things, packing up, and slipping away unannounced in the middle of the night' (attributed to Khaled Hosseini). [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p294]
- Explorers are couples for whom the affair 'becomes a catalyst for transformation' and who 'come to see the infidelity as an event that, though insanely painful, contained the seeds of something positive'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p295]
- Explorers distinguish wrong from hurtful, 'paving a smoother road for clemency,' and can speak about the betrayal with 'a fundamental acceptance of their human flaws'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p296]
- Perel credits Helen Fisher for the typology underlying the three post-infidelity outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p239]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel suggests that the Sufferers pattern may indicate couples who would have ended up in similar conflicts even without infidelity, implying the affair may expose rather than create fundamental incompatibilities
- Inference: Perel notes that Explorers shift from speaking of 'When you did this to me' to 'When we had our crisis,' indicating shared narrative ownership of the event
