# Neuronal Stem Cells

## Summary

Immature cells in the brain that can divide and differentiate to become neurons or glial cells. Unlike specialized cells, they can continue dividing endlessly without aging, earning them the description 'eternally young, baby cells of the brain.' Found in the hippocampus, olfactory bulb, and in dormant states in other areas, they are the source of neurogenesis throughout life.

## Key facts

- Neuronal stem cells are cells that can divide and differentiate to become neurons or glial cells, which support neurons in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p253]
- Stem cells lack specialization and all look identical before differentiation, but they can continue dividing endlessly without signs of aging. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p253]
- For this reason, stem cells are often described as 'the eternally young, baby cells of the brain.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p253]
- They were long overlooked because they went against the theory that the brain was like a complex machine or computer, since machines don't grow new parts. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- They have been found active in the hippocampus and olfactory bulb, and dormant and inactive in the septum, striatum, and spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- Researchers are working on treatments that might activate dormant stem cells with drugs, transplant stem cells into injured areas, or induce them to move to damaged areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery that stem cells exist in the brain and can produce new neurons throughout life fundamentally challenges the earlier view that the brain is a closed system incapable of regeneration.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Treatments to activate dormant stem cells, transplant them, or induce migration to damaged areas remain under investigation and are not yet established therapies.
