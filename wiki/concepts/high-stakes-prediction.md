# High-Stakes Predictions

## Summary

Predictions about outcomes that carry significant consequences, such as life, death, or major life changes. The book outlines eleven elements that improve the accuracy of such predictions: measurability, vantage, imminence, context, pre-incident indicators, experience, comparable events, objectivity, investment, replicability, and knowledge.

## Key facts

- Prediction without preparation is just curiosity—prediction must be followed by preparation to avoid or exploit an outcome. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p63]
- The eleven elements of prediction are: Measurability, Vantage, Imminence, Context, Pre-incident Indicators (PINs), Experience, Comparable Events, Objectivity, Investment, Replicability, and Knowledge. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p69]
- Pre-incident indicators are the most valuable element for prediction if they are detectable and occur at a useful time distance from the outcome. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p71]
- Behavior is like a chain—violent outcomes are only one link in a process that started long before the violent act. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p72]
- The RICE evaluation (Reliability, Importance, Cost, Effectiveness) is used to determine how much preparation is appropriate for a given outcome. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p63]
