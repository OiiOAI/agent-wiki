# Neural Regeneration

## Summary

Neural regeneration refers to the regrowth and repair of neurons and their connections after injury. The PNS of mammals and the CNS of lower vertebrates show robust regeneration, while the mammalian CNS regenerates poorly due to inhibitory molecules in myelin, glial scarring, and the absence of intrinsic growth programs in mature neurons.

## Key facts

- Axon regeneration is robust in the peripheral nervous system of mammals and in the CNS of lower vertebrates, but very poor in the mammalian CNS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Components of myelin, including Nogo and myelin-associated glycoprotein, inhibit neurite outgrowth and contribute to poor CNS regeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Injury-induced glial scarring creates a physical barrier that hinders axonal regeneration in the CNS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- An intrinsic growth program that is active during development becomes downregulated in mature neurons, limiting their regenerative capacity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Formation of new connections by intact axons can lead to functional recovery after CNS injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]

## Inferences

- Inference: Understanding why CNS regeneration fails in mammals while PNS regeneration succeeds may lead to therapeutic interventions that promote repair after spinal cord injury and stroke.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The chapter mentions several therapeutic approaches being explored, but their clinical efficacy remains to be fully established.
