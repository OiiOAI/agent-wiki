# Philosophical Zombies

## Summary

Philosophical zombies are hypothetical creatures indistinguishable from conscious beings but lacking any inner experience. The zombie thought experiment argues that if such creatures are conceivable, consciousness cannot be a physical phenomenon. Seth counters that conceivability arguments are weak—the more we know about brains, the less conceivable zombies become, similar to imagining an A380 flying backwards.

## Key facts

- A philosophical zombie is a creature indistinguishable from a conscious creature but with no inner universe or felt experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p28]
- The zombie argument uses conceivability to suggest consciousness cannot be physical, but conceivability arguments are intrinsically weak. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p28]
- The more one knows about the brain and its relation to conscious experience, the less conceivable a zombie becomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p29]
- The zombie argument's flaw is interpreting failure of imagination as insight into necessity, similar to vitalism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p35]
