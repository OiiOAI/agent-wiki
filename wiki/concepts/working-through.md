# Working Through

## Summary

Working through (Durcharbeitung) is the psychoanalytic process by which the patient gradually overcomes resistances after they have been made conscious. Freud introduced this concept to address the observation that merely pointing out a resistance does not cause its immediate cessation. The patient must become conversant with the resistance and, despite it, continue the analytic work. This laborious process effects the greatest changes in the patient and distinguishes analytic treatment from treatment by suggestion. Freud correlated it with the abreaction of strangulated affects in hypnotic treatment.

## Key facts

- Naming a resistance does not cause its immediate cessation; the patient must work through it by continuing analytic work despite it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3872]
- Working through is correlated with abreaction—discharging affects that have been strangulated by repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3873]
- Working through may prove an arduous task requiring patience from both analyst and patient, but it is this process rather than mere interpretation that effects the greatest changes.
