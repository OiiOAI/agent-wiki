# Similarity and Compliance

## Summary

Similarity is a powerful factor in producing liking and compliance. People are more likely to help, comply with, and like those who are similar to them in opinions, personality traits, background, or lifestyle. Compliance professionals exploit this through dress similarity, claimed background similarities, and mirroring techniques.

## Key facts

- People are more likely to help those who dress similarly to them; in one study, requests were granted in over two-thirds of cases when dressed similarly versus less than half when dissimilar. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p158]
- Marchers in antiwar demonstrations were more likely to sign petitions from similarly dressed requesters without reading them first, demonstrating automatic response to similarity. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p158]
- Car salesmen are trained to identify customer interests from trade-ins and mention similar interests to build rapport. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p158]
- Insurance customers were more likely to buy from salespeople similar to them in age, religion, politics, and smoking habits. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p158]
- Modern sales training programs urge trainees to "mirror and match" customers' body posture, mood, and verbal style. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p158]

## Inferences

- Inference: The effectiveness of even manufactured similarity suggests that compliance professionals can easily exploit this principle, making it important for consumers to be cautious of salespeople who 'just seem to be just like you.'
