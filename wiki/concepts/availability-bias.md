# Availability Bias

## Summary

The Availability Bias is the tendency to create a picture of the world using examples that most easily come to mind, leading to systematic overestimation of dramatic or recent events and underestimation of mundane but statistically common risks.

## Key facts

- The availability bias says we create a picture of the world using the examples that most easily come to mind, even though things don't happen more frequently just because we can conceive of them more easily. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p34]
- Due to availability bias, we systematically overestimate the risk of plane crashes, car accidents or murder while underestimating diabetes or stomach cancer. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p34]
- The availability bias presented banks with billions in losses because they continued using the Black-Scholes formula for pricing derivatives even after knowing it didn't work. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p35]
