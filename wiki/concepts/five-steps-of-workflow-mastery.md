# Five Steps of Workflow Mastery

## Summary

The five steps of workflow mastery form the core operational process of GTD: (1) Capture items requiring attention into trusted external systems; (2) Clarify what each item means and determine if action is required; (3) Organize results into categorized lists and systems; (4) Reflect on contents regularly to maintain system integrity; (5) Engage by choosing and executing actions based on context, time, energy, and priority.

## Key facts

- The five steps are: Capture, Clarify, Organize, Reflect, and Engage. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p59]
- The workflow management process is horizontal—it maintains coherence across all activities in which one is involved. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p54]
- Most people have major weaknesses in their capture process, with commitments still held only in their heads. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p60]
- Capture tools include physical in-trays, paper-based note-taking devices, digital/audio devices, and e-mail systems. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p63]
- Three success factors for capturing are: getting everything out of your head, minimizing the number of capture locations, and emptying capture tools regularly. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p65]
- The quality of workflow management is only as good as the weakest link in this five-phase chain. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p59]
