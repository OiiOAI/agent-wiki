# Exhibition Dreams

## Summary

Exhibition dreams involve the dreamer feeling ashamed or embarrassed about being naked or insufficiently dressed in the presence of strangers, often accompanied by the inability to move or hide. Freud traces these dreams to childhood experiences of exposure and the wish to exhibit, connecting them to both infantile sexuality and the later concept of perversion.

## Key facts

- The core of a dream of exhibiting lies in the figure of the dreamer himself and his inadequate clothing, which emerges indistinctly. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p245]
- Children frequently manifest a desire to exhibit; they may lift their clothes in front of strangers with no shame. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p244]
- In hysteria and obsessional neurosis, the actual spectators of the infantile scene of exhibiting have appeared in dreams; in paranoia, these spectators reappear as invisible observers. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p246]
- The feeling of being inhibited in dreams serves to represent a conflict of will or a negative; the unconscious purpose requires the exhibiting to proceed while the censorship demands that it shall be stopped. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p246]
- Freud connects exhibition dreams to the fairy tale The Emperor's New Clothes, where all spectators pretend not to notice the emperor's nakedness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p243]
