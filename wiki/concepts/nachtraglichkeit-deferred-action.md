# Translation of Freud's Nachträglichkeit as 'Deferred Action'

## Summary

Nachträglichkeit, the concept whose significance in Freud's work was emphasized by Lacan (1953) and Laplanche & Pontalis (1968), presents substantial translation difficulties. Strachey rendered it as 'deferred action' but used many other translations inconsistently. The German term carries connotations of revenge and grudge (via nachtragend) that are absent from 'deferred action'. Critics argue the standard translation inadequately captures both the 'deferred' and 'retrospective' aspects—the concept involves not only delayed consequences but also how later events can revise memory traces of original events. Proposed alternatives include retroaction, afterwardsness, and retranscription, with 'afterwardsness' probably coming closest to the German original.

## Key facts

- Strachey usually rendered Nachträglichkeit as 'deferred action' but also used after-effect, subsequently, belatedly, in arrears, in retrospect, and later inconsistently. - Thomä & Cheshire argue that 'deferred action' is 'in many ways misleading' because the German word carries connotations of revenge and grudge absent from the English. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7568]
- Laplanche (1997) emphasizes that Nachträglichkeit refers to both 'a deferred action from the past in the direction of the present' and 'the reverse'—two arrows of time. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7568]
- Proposed alternatives in literature include retroaction, afterwardsness, retrospective attribution, hysterogenesis, and subsequentiality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7569]
- Mahony (1984a) suggests Nachträglichkeit ranks alongside Freud's primal words with antithetical meaning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7569]

## Inferences

- Inference: The translation difficulty reflects genuine theoretical complexity in Freud's concept, which involves both temporal deferral and retrospective revision of meaning.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Freud's original technical usage (limited to memory traces of childhood sexual scenes) should constrain broader contemporary interpretations remains contested.
