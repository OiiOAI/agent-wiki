# Kantian Interpretation of Justice as Fairness

## Summary

Rawls offers a Kantian interpretation of justice as fairness based on Kant's notion of autonomy. A person acts autonomously when principles of action are chosen as the most adequate expression of their nature as a free and equal rational being. The original position interprets the standpoint of noumenal selves—free and equal rational beings choosing principles while abstracted from social and natural contingencies. Acting from the principles of justice expresses one's nature as a free and equal rational being and constitutes categorical imperatives that apply regardless of particular aims.

## Key facts

- Kant held that a person acts autonomously when the principles of action are chosen as the most adequate possible expression of their nature as a free and equal rational being. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p233]
- The veil of ignorance deprives parties in the original position of knowledge that would enable them to choose heteronomous principles—principles based on social position or natural endowments. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p233]
- The principles of justice are categorical imperatives: they apply to a person in virtue of their nature as a free and equal rational being, not because one has a particular desire or aim. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p234]
- The motivational assumption of mutual disinterest in the original position allows for freedom in choosing a system of final ends—no prior constraints are imposed on what persons may desire. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p234]
- Rawls addresses Sidgwick's objection that the scoundrel expresses his true self just as much as the saint by arguing that the original position shows which principles free and equal rational persons would choose to express their nature as such. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p235]
- The desire to act justly derives in part from the desire to express most fully what we are or can be—free and equal rational beings with liberty to choose—and failure to act on the moral law gives rise to shame rather than mere guilt. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p236]

## Inferences

- Inference: Kant's main aim, according to Rawls, is to deepen Rousseau's idea that liberty is acting in accordance with a law we give ourselves, leading not to a morality of austere command but to an ethic of mutual respect and self-esteem.
