# Internalization of Experience

## Summary

Internalization is the brain's ability to disengage from immediate sensory inputs and motor outputs through self-organized activity. Buzsáki proposes that cognition depends on prior action-based experiences that allow internally generated sequences to test 'what if' scenarios and anticipate consequences of alternative actions without actually taking them. The core mechanism involves corollary discharge circuits supplemented with memory loops that substitute for body and environment feedback, creating a 'virtual world' through vicarious or imagined experience tested against stored knowledge.

## Key facts

- For a brain network, there is no difference between sensory inputs or activity conveyed by the same upstream neuronal group in the absence of external inputs—the brain cannot distinguish between real and simulated actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p104]
- Internalized actions (simulations of real actions) allow the brain to inspect its own computations, evaluate imagined scenarios, and estimate consequences of potential actions without overt inputs or outputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p104]
- Cognition is time-deferred action—the internal content remains a belief until validated in real-world interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p105]
- Sleep paralysis illustrates the importance of action grounding—when stimuli impinge on sensors but cannot be acted upon, they make no sense and induce no meaning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p80]
- The rubber hand illusion demonstrates that illusory touch arises from lack of grounding by action—if the participant moves her hand while the rubber hand is touched, no illusion arises. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: Processes like input-dependence, self-organized activity, and output disengagement are strongly intertwined, with emergence of cognition being gradual and quantitative rather than step-like and qualitative.
