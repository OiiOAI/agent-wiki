# NVC Mourning

## Summary

NVC mourning is the process of fully connecting with unmet needs and feelings generated when we have been less than perfect. It is an experience of regret that helps us learn from what we have done without blaming or hating ourselves, opening ourselves to feelings that arise out of awareness of how our behavior ran counter to our own needs and values.

## Key facts

- When our consciousness is focused on what we need, we are naturally stimulated toward creative possibilities for how to get that need met. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p180]
- Moralistic judgments obscure such possibilities and perpetuate a state of self-punishment. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p180]
- Feelings such as sadness, frustration, disappointment, fear, or grief mobilize us to pursue and fulfill what we need or value, unlike the disconnection brought on by guilt, shame, and depression. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mourning process is distinct from self-punishment because it focuses on unmet needs rather than moralistic judgment of the self as wrong or bad.
