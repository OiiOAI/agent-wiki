# Newly Experienced Scarcity

## Summary

Newly experienced scarcity refers to items or freedoms that have recently become less available, which are valued more highly than items that have been scarce all along. Research shows that a drop from abundance to scarcity produces a more positive reaction than constant scarcity. This principle applies to consumer goods, political freedoms, and interpersonal dynamics.

## Key facts

- Newly scarce cookies were rated significantly higher than cookies that had been scarce from the outset. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p231]
- James C. Davies proposed that revolutions are most likely where economic and social conditions improved and then experienced a sharp reversal. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p231]
- Revolutionaries are more likely to be those who have experienced at least some taste of a better life rather than the traditionally most downtrodden. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p231]
- When newly established freedoms become less available, people desire them more than ever and often resist violently. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p231]
- Parents who enforce discipline inconsistently produce generally rebellious children because they inadvertently establish freedoms for the child. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: The newly experienced scarcity principle explains why the August 1991 Soviet coup failed—people who had been granted glasnost freedoms under Gorbachev violently resisted when those freedoms were threatened.
- Inference: The 'Romeo and Juliet effect' reported by a reader from Blacksburg, Virginia demonstrates the principle in family dynamics: parental opposition to a relationship increased the daughter's affection, effectively creating a 'freedom' that became threatened.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Davies's analysis of 1960s black riots cites income returning to 74 percent from 80 percent of white family income, but the causal mechanism assumes this specific reversal figure is the key variable rather than other simultaneous factors.
