# Self-Forgiveness in NVC

## Summary

Self-forgiveness in NVC occurs when we connect empathically with the need we were trying to meet when we took an action we now regret. Human beings are always acting in service of needs and values, whether the action meets the need or not. The process holds both parts of ourselves—the self that regrets and the self that acted.

## Key facts

- Human beings are always acting in the service of needs and values, whether the action does or does not meet the need. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p181]
- Self-forgiveness occurs the moment empathic connection is made with the underlying need. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p181]
- We are compassionate with ourselves when we can embrace all parts of ourselves and recognize the needs and values expressed by each part. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p183]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction is that self-forgiveness follows mourning—we first connect with unmet needs and then recognize how the regretted action was an attempt to serve life, even if it fell short.
