# The Plateau

## Summary

The plateau is the extended period during skill acquisition where apparent progress stops, representing the majority of time spent on the master's journey. Leonard emphasizes that loving the plateau—not just tolerating it—is essential to mastery. The plateau triggers emotional responses and reveals hidden motivations, making it psychologically challenging despite requiring continued diligent practice.

## Key facts

- Leonard describes the plateau as a period of apparent no-progress that follows the initial spurt of learning. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L153-L156]
- For most people, the plateau triggers disowned emotions and flushes out hidden motivations. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L140-L141]
- Leonard recounts that after about a year and a half of aikido practice, he learned to welcome plateaus as opportunities for continued practice. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L236-L239]
- Practice exists only in the present moment, unlike goals which exist in the future, making plateau time valuable in itself. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L258-L260]
- Olympic gymnast Peter Vidmar emphasized underlying enjoyment of the work rather than the medal as motivation. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L246-L248]

## Inferences

- Inference: The plateau may serve a consolidation function, allowing the habitual behavior system to fully integrate new skills before the next spurt of learning occurs.
