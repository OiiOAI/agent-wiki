# Electroconvulsive Therapy

## Summary

Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT) is a highly effective treatment for serious major depressive disorder, particularly when medications and psychotherapy have proven ineffective. Administered with modern anesthesia, it involves electrical stimulation just above the threshold to produce a generalized seizure. The major side effect is variable anterograde and retrograde amnesia.

## Key facts

- ECT administered with modern anesthesia is medically safe and highly effective for acute treatment of serious major depressive disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1565]
- ECT is most often used when depressive symptoms are severe and medications and psychotherapies have proven ineffective. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1565]
- The major side effect of ECT is variable anterograde and retrograde amnesia, which can be minimized but not eliminated by unilateral electrode placement.
