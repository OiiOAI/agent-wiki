# Vocal Feedback Processing in Auditory Cortex

## Summary

During speaking and vocalizing, the auditory cortex processes self-generated vocal feedback to monitor and correct vocal production. Self-initiated vocalizations induce suppression of auditory cortex responses that begins hundreds of milliseconds before vocal onset. This suppression is reduced when vocal feedback is perturbed, indicating involvement in error detection. A subset of neurons show vocalization-related excitation that may maintain sensitivity to external sounds during speaking.

## Key facts

- Responses in human auditory cortex to one's own voice during speaking are smaller than responses to playback of the same sounds, a phenomenon called vocalization-induced suppression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- Vocalization-induced suppression in auditory cortex begins several hundred milliseconds prior to vocal onset, suggesting modulation from vocal motor areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- When vocal feedback is experimentally altered (e.g., pitch shifted), auditory cortex suppression is reduced, indicating neurons participate in feedback monitoring networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- Approximately 30% of marmoset A1 neurons show vocalization-related excitation that may help maintain sensitivity to external acoustic environment during vocalizing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]

## Inferences

- Inference: The pre-vocal suppression suggests that motor commands generate predictions of expected auditory feedback that suppress responses to self-generated sounds.
