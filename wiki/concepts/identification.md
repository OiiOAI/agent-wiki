# Identification

## Summary

Identification is the earliest expression of an emotional tie with another person, playing a central role in both individual development and group formation. Freud distinguishes between identification as a process where the ego molds itself after a model, and object choice where the object is what one would like to have. In the context of group psychology, identification forms the basis of mutual ties between group members.

## Key facts

- Identification is known to psychoanalysis as the earliest expression of an emotional tie with another person. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5679]
- Identification is ambivalent from the very first; it can turn into an expression of tenderness as easily as into a wish for someone's removal. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5679]
- In identification the ego has enriched itself with the properties of the object, having 'introjected' the object into itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5688]
- The mutual tie between members of a group is in the nature of an identification based upon an important emotional common quality, likely the nature of the tie with the leader. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5681]
- When a person has to give up a sexual object, there ensues an alteration of his ego which can be described as a setting up of the object inside the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5915]
- The character of the ego is a precipitate of abandoned object cathexes and contains the history of those object choices. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5915]
- The superego originates from identifications with the father which were the child's first and most important identifications made in earliest childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5916]
- Through identification with the father, the superego retains the character of the father and the parental influence perpetuates the factors to which it owes its origin. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5921]
- By transforming erotic object libido into narcissistic libido, the ego makes it easier for the id to give up objects. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5915]
- Identification seeks to satisfy the wish of the drive by transforming the ego itself into the desired object, so that it becomes both at the same time: the desiring subject and the desired object. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7030]
- Aggressive activity towards the father leads to father identification and the creation of the superego, while passivity towards the father becomes mother identification. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7032]
- Identification with Christ is a regular occurrence in the mental life of a Christian, reconciling both passive submission to the father and masculine power. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7033]
- Identification with the father is the normal resolution of the Oedipus complex when the boy becomes a man and has a son of his own. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7033]
