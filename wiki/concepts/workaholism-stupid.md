# Workaholism Is Stupid

## Summary

The concept argues against the glorification of overwork, stating that working more hours does not mean caring more or accomplishing more, and that workaholics create more problems than they solve. The authors contend that workaholics try to fix problems with brute force rather than intelligence, create crises to justify their overtime, damage morale of colleagues who work reasonable hours, and ultimately do not accomplish more than non-workaholics. The real hero is someone who figures out a faster way.

## Key facts

- The authors argue that workaholics wind up creating more problems than they solve and that working more doesn't mean caring more or getting more done. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p22]
- Workaholics are described as trying to fix problems by throwing sheer hours at them, making up for intellectual laziness with brute force, resulting in inelegant solutions. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p22]
- The authors argue that workaholics create crises (often unwittingly) so they can feel like heroes for working overtime. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p22]
- Workaholics make people who don't stay late feel inadequate for merely working reasonable hours, leading to guilt and poor morale. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p22]
- The authors conclude that workaholics don't actually accomplish more than non-workaholics and that 'workaholics aren't heroes, they don't save the day, they just use it up.'. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p22]

## Inferences

- Inference: The glorification of overwork may be a cultural artifact that persists despite being counterproductive to actual productivity.
