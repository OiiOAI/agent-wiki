# Cathartic Method

## Summary

The cathartic method is a psychotherapeutic procedure developed by Breuer and elaborated by Freud for treating hysterical symptoms. The method involves bringing to light the memory of the event that provoked a symptom, arousing its accompanying affect, and having the patient describe the event in detail and express the affect in words. This 'abreaction' ends the operative force of the non-abreacted idea by allowing its strangulated affect to find an outlet through speech. The method works by subjecting the pathogenic idea to associative correction, either by introducing it into normal consciousness or by removing it through suggestion. Freud later extended the method by developing the pressure technique to bypass hypnosis and overcome resistance.

## Key facts

- Breuer and Freud reported that each individual hysterical symptom immediately and permanently disappeared when the memory of the provoking event was brought to light and its accompanying affect was aroused and expressed in words. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p769]
- The cathartic method brings to an end the operative force of the idea which was not abreacted in the first instance by allowing its strangulated affect to find a way out through speech. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p769]
- Freud claimed the cathartic method is well able to get rid of any hysterical symptom but is completely powerless against neurasthenia and only rarely influences anxiety neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p774]
- Freud found the cathartic method not a causal but a symptomatic therapy, which gets rid of existing results of pathogenic factors while a causal therapy would prevent further effects. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p775]
- Breuer observed that symptoms disappeared when patients recollected pathogenic experiences under hypnosis with accompanying expression of affect, a process termed 'catharsis'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3400]
- Freud abandoned hypnosis because he could not reliably induce it in most patients, leading him to develop free association as an alternative technique. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3401]
- The case of Breuer's patient Anna O. demonstrated that hysterical symptoms were mnemic symbols of traumatic experiences that could be removed by recalling the determining cause with accompanying affect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3401]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cathartic method represents the first form of psychoanalytic therapy, establishing the principle that insight and emotional expression of repressed material are therapeutic.
- Inference: The cathartic method revealed that hysterical patients 'suffer from reminiscences' and that 'their symptoms are residues and mnemic symbols of particular (traumatic) experiences.'
