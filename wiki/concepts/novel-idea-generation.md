# Novel Idea Generation

## Summary

Novel idea generation is the process of combining previously unrelated concepts to create new ideas with emergent properties. This process is foundational to creativity, involving both the generation of constituent concepts and their combinatorial integration. The selection of concepts to combine is not random but partly intentional, guided by awareness of the problem and the person's experience and expertise. Creative individuals show faster judgment of relatedness between concepts, providing an advantage in identifying promising concept pairs for combination.

## Key facts

- Novel idea generation involves the novel and useful combination of concepts previously thought to be unrelated, as proposed by Poincaré (1913). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p414]
- When previously unrelated concepts are brought together and combined, the resultant concepts can contain novel emergent properties that none of the constituent concepts contained beforehand. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p414]
- Campbell (1960) argued that the activation of constituent ideas is not goal-oriented but rather 'blind', though many scholars have questioned this assumption. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p414]
- The problem itself likely acts as a prime for activating relevant content in consciousness through spreading activation, making the search non-random. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p414]
- Vartanian, Martindale, and Matthews (2009) found that people with higher divergent thinking ability are faster at judging whether two concepts are related or unrelated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p415]
- The ability to judge relatedness faster provides an advantage in the number of potentially useful relationships assessed per unit of time, resulting in higher frequency of creative ideas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p415]
- There was no link between IQ and speed of judging relatedness, demonstrating individual differences in creativity unrelated to general intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p415]

## Inferences

- Inference: The faster relatedness judgment in creative individuals suggests that early stages of problem solving involve strategic selection of concepts rather than purely associative processes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which creative individuals achieve faster relatedness judgment remain to be fully elucidated.
