# Diphasic Object Choice

## Summary

Diphasic object choice refers to the pattern that the process of finding a sexual object occurs in two waves. The first wave begins between the ages of two and five and is brought to a halt by the latency period, characterized by infantile sexual aims. The second wave sets in with puberty and determines the final outcome of sexual life. The resultants of infantile object choice are carried over into the later period, either persisting or being revived at puberty.

## Key facts

- Object choice occurs in two waves: the first between ages two and five (brought to a halt by latency), the second at puberty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2470]
- The resultants of infantile object choice are carried over into the later period and either persist or are revived at puberty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2470]
- As a consequence of repression developing between the two phases, infantile object choices prove unutilizable for their original sexual aims and represent what may be described as the 'affectionate current' of sexual life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2470]
- The diphasic nature of object choice is of the highest importance in regard to disturbances of the final outcome of sexual life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2470]
