# Speed-Accuracy Trade-off

## Summary

The speed-accuracy trade-off describes the inverse relationship between movement speed and accuracy. As movement speed increases, accuracy decreases because higher speeds require greater muscle force, which introduces more variability due to motor noise. This fundamental constraint shapes motor skill acquisition and movement planning.

## Key facts

- Movement variability increases with movement speed because faster movements require greater muscle force, which introduces more noise from motor neuron recruitment fluctuations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- Force production variability increases with force level over a large range, captured by a constant coefficient of variation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- Skill learning involves being able to increase speed without sacrificing accuracy, as in learning to play the piano. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- The decrease in movement accuracy with increasing speed is known as the speed-accuracy trade-off and is not fixed but improves with practice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- Fast movements are less accurate than slow ones; people slow their movements when accuracy is demanded, as shown by Robert Woodworth in the 1890s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p799]
- Neural noise in motor commands increases with larger motor commands, limiting the ability to move rapidly and accurately at the same time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p799]
- Fitts's law describes the relationship between movement amplitude, accuracy, and duration, relating movement duration to the index of difficulty based on target width and movement amplitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p803]
