# Belief Calibration

## Summary

Belief calibration is the skill of more accurately representing the world by using experience and information to objectively update beliefs. The author proposes expressing confidence on a 0-10 scale (translating to percentages) or as ranges of plausible alternatives, which brings the probabilistic nature of beliefs to plain sight. This shifts the narrative from being 'right' or 'wrong' to making small adjustments in degrees of certainty, making truthseeking easier.

## Key facts

- Instead of saying 'I'm confident,' we should express how confident we are on a scale of 0-10, where 10 means certain and 0 means certain the belief is false. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L780-L790]
- Expressing beliefs as uncertain invites others to be collaborators by signaling they can contribute without implying 'you're wrong.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L810-L820]
- Scientists express uncertainty through p-values and confidence intervals, allowing others to assess quality and inviting community testing and challenge. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L830-L840]
- Acknowledging uncertainty creates open-mindedness and makes us less likely to succumb to motivated reasoning since small adjustments feel better than being 'wrong.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L755-L765]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book argues that calibrating beliefs and expressing uncertainty makes us better truthseekers and more credible communicators, as others recognize we're trying to get at truth rather than simply affirm existing beliefs.
