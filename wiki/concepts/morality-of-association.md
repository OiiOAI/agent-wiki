# Morality of Association

## Summary

The second stage of moral development, covering a wide range of cases depending on the association in question. The content is given by the moral standards appropriate to the individual's role in the various associations to which he belongs, including the family, school, neighborhood, and later adult statuses and occupations.

## Key facts

- The morality of association includes a large number of ideals each defined in ways suitable for the respective status or role, such as the ideal of a good son or daughter, a good student, a good friend and citizen. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p414-p415]
- Acquiring a morality of association rests upon the development of intellectual skills required to regard things from a variety of points of view and to think of these together as aspects of one system of cooperation. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p416]
- The content of the morality of association is characterized by the cooperative virtues: those of justice and fairness, fidelity and trust, integrity and impartiality. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p419]
