# Speed Regulator

## Summary

Speed regulators are built-in mechanisms that help startup teams find their optimal pace of work, preventing destructive over-acceleration. The concept is epitomized by the Toyota proverb 'Stop production so that production never has to stop' and by the andon cord in lean manufacturing, which brings work to a halt as soon as an uncorrectable quality problem surfaces. The key insight is that shortcuts taken in product quality, design, or infrastructure today may wind up slowing a company down tomorrow.

## Key facts

- The andon cord brings work to a stop as soon as an uncorrectable quality problem surfaces, forcing investigation—this is one of the most important discoveries of the lean manufacturing movement: you cannot trade quality for time. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p212]
- Defects cause a lot of rework, low morale, and customer complaints, all of which slow progress and eat away at valuable resources. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p212]
- Shortcuts taken in product quality, design, or infrastructure today may wind up slowing a company down tomorrow. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p213]
- The paradox of speed regulation is demonstrated at IMVU: early versions shipped with bugs and bad design couldn't get customer feedback, but as products improved, low quality inhibited learning when defects prevented customers from experiencing the product's benefits. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p213]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Five Whys serves as a speed regulator because the more problems encountered, the more proportional investment is required in solutions, naturally slowing teams down until the preventive efforts pay off and speed increases again.
