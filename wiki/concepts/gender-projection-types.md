# Gender Projection Types

## Summary

Greene identifies six common types of gender projections that represent ways people unconsciously project their anima or animus onto romantic partners: The Devilish Romantic (women attracted to rake-like men who remind them of flirtatious fathers), The Elusive Woman of Perfection (men projecting ideal qualities onto hard-to-pin-down women), The Lovable Rebel (women attracted to nonconformist men as expression of their own repressed rebellion), The Fallen Woman (men attracted to women from different backgrounds who represent their repressed sensual side), The Superior Man (women attracted to brilliant, powerful men as sources of reflected strength), and The Woman to Worship Him (men seeking women to fulfill the mother role of adoration and comfort).

## Key facts

- The Devilish Romantic projection affects women who had intense, flirtatious relationships with the father and seek to recapture that dynamic. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p418-419]
- Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis exemplifies the Devilish Romantic projection, repeatedly choosing men resembling her adored but narcissistic father Jack Bouvier. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p419]
- The Elusive Woman of Perfection projection affects men who had mothers not totally there for them, creating a vague fantasy of ideal mates. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p420]
- The Lovable Rebel projection affects women with strict, distant fathers—their repressed anger goes into the animus seeking external rebellion. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p422]
- The Fallen Woman projection (seen in Proust's Swann) reflects the classic split of mother/whore in men who secretly crave what is opposite to themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p423]
- The Superior Man projection stems from feelings of inferiority and internalized critical voices about how women should behave. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p425]
- George Eliot's Dorothea Brooke exemplifies the Superior Man projection, idealizing Edward Causabon as a brilliant scholar who would educate and elevate her. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p424-425]
- The Woman to Worship Him projection stems from men whose mothers adored them, creating addiction to maternal attention and adoration. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p426]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene presents these projection types as templates for self-recognition, suggesting that understanding one's pattern is the first step toward breaking unproductive cycles
