# Hero as Warrior

## Summary

The hero as warrior is an archetypal role wherein the hero acts as agent of the cosmogonic cycle, clearing monsters from the land and suppressing usurping tyrants. The mythological hero is the champion not of things become but of things becoming—the dragon to be slain is the monster of the status quo: Holdfast, the keeper of the past.

## Key facts

- The mythological hero is the champion not of things become but of things becoming; the dragon to be slain is precisely the monster of the status quo: Holdfast, the keeper of the past. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p263]
- The world period of the hero in human form begins only when villages and cities have expanded over the land; many monsters remaining from primeval times still lurk in outlying regions and must be cleared away. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p264]
- Kut-o-yis (Blood Clot Boy) exterminated tribes of cruel bears and snakes, each time sparing one female about to become a mother 'If he had not done this, there would have been no bears [snakes] in the world.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p265]
- Saint Martha encountered a dragon half animal, half fish, larger than an ox, with teeth as sharp as horns and great wings—she sprinkled holy water on it, exhibited a crucifix, and the monster came like a lamb; the town was named Tarascon in remembrance. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The warrior hero's work of 'clearing the field' serves a cosmological function—removing the obstacles to human flourishing that represent the old order or the stagnation of established structures.
