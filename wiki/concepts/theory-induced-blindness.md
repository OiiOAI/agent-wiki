# Rationality Blindness

## Summary

Theory-induced blindness (buta akibat teori) is the tendency to fail to notice facts that contradict a dominant theory, because the theory itself defines what is considered relevant and shapes the questions that get asked. Kahneman and Tversky discovered they had been blind to the distinction between gains and losses for 250 years because Bernoulli's theory provided no natural place for this distinction, making it literally unimaginable within the theory's framework.

## Key facts

- Kahneman and Tversky realized they had made theoretical progress when they could no longer reconstruct why they had failed to see something obvious for so long—the gap between their prior blindness and their new clarity marked genuine discovery. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p361]
- The failure to distinguish between gains and losses was not a minor oversight: it went unnoticed for over 250 years despite the efforts of many brilliant economists, because the dominant theory of expected utility had no place for the distinction. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p362]
- The same blindness affected Prospect Theory itself: the assumption that the reference point has zero value leads to absurd consequences such as treating 'nothing' as equally neutral in a 1-in-a-million lottery and a 90% chance of winning a million—a failure to account for disappointment. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p371]
- Standard indifference curves in economics omit the reference point, leading students and scholars to believe the reference point does not matter—Bernoulli's error reappears in introductory textbooks through this omission. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p374]

## Inferences

- Inference: Theory-induced blindness explains why some critiques of Prospect Theory may themselves be blind to its limitations: any theoretical framework defines the questions asked and the evidence considered relevant, creating blind spots that are invisible from within.
