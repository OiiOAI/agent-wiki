# Sensitization

## Summary

Sensitization is an enhanced response to a wide variety of stimuli following presentation of an intense or noxious stimulus. In Aplysia, sensitization of the gill-withdrawal reflex involves presynaptic facilitation of synaptic transmission from sensory neurons to motor neurons, mediated by serotonin release from modulatory interneurons activated by tail shock.

## Key facts

- A single tail shock produces short-term sensitization lasting minutes; five or more shocks produce sensitization lasting days to weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1317]
- Sensitization involves serotonin release from modulatory interneurons acting on G-protein-coupled receptors in sensory neurons, increasing cAMP and PKA activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1317]
- PKA phosphorylation of K+ channels broadens action potentials, enhancing Ca2+ influx and transmitter release from sensory neuron terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1318]
- A single tail shock in Aplysia produces short-term sensitization lasting minutes; five or more shocks produce long-term sensitization lasting days to weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1516]
- Presynaptic facilitation in Aplysia sensory neurons involves two molecular pathways: serotonin binding activates Gs protein (increasing cAMP and PKA, which closes K+ channels, broadening action potentials and enhancing Ca2+ influx) and Gq/11 protein (activating phospholipase C, producing DAG and PKC, which mobilizes vesicles from reserve to releasable pools). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1517]
- Long-term sensitization involves growth of new synaptic connections: the number of presynaptic terminals in sensory neurons of the gill-withdrawal circuit doubles, and motor neuron dendrites grow to accommodate additional synaptic input. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1524]

## Inferences

- Inference: The bidirectional synaptic changes underlying habituation (weakening) and sensitization (strengthening) at the same sensory-motor synapses demonstrate that modifiable synapses can participate in more than one type of learning.
