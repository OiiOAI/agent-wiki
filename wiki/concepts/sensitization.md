# Sensitization

## Summary

Sensitization is an enhanced response to a wide variety of stimuli following presentation of an intense or noxious stimulus. In Aplysia, sensitization of the gill-withdrawal reflex involves presynaptic facilitation of synaptic transmission from sensory neurons to motor neurons, mediated by serotonin release from modulatory interneurons activated by tail shock.

## Key facts

- A single tail shock produces short-term sensitization lasting minutes; five or more shocks produce sensitization lasting days to weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1317]
- Sensitization involves serotonin release from modulatory interneurons acting on G-protein-coupled receptors in sensory neurons, increasing cAMP and PKA activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1317]
- PKA phosphorylation of K+ channels broadens action potentials, enhancing Ca2+ influx and transmitter release from sensory neuron terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1318]
