# Orbitofrontal Cortex in Value and Decision-Making

## Summary

The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) is critically involved in encoding the incentive value of outcomes and in value-guided decision-making. The medial and lateral OFC represent different aspects of value processing, with medial OFC encoding general value signals and lateral OFC being involved in cognitive control processes when reward-related responses require suppression. OFC damage affects both temporal and probabilistic discounting, though effects depend on lesion timing, task parameters, and exact lesion location within OFC subregions.

## Key facts

- The orbitofrontal cortex is associated with encoding the expected value of reward and updating incentive value in response to devaluation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p91]
- Large OFC lesions can produce paradoxical preference for delayed food rewards, contrasting with findings that OFC lesions made prior to acquisition of delay-discounting procedures increase impulsive choice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p91]
- Ventrolateral OFC lesions induce stable patterns of impulsive choice, whereas medial OFC lesions after retraining have opposite effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p92]
- Reversible inactivation of medial OFC increases risky choice on probabilistic discounting, irrespective of whether odds of obtaining larger reward decrease or increase over a session. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p93]
- Lateral OFC lesions slow reversal learning when reward contingencies are switched, suggesting a role in cognitive control when reward responses require suppression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p92]

## Inferences

- Inference: The differential effects of medial versus lateral OFC lesions suggest that these subregions play distinct roles in value-guided behavior, with medial OFC involved in outcome valuation and lateral OFC in cognitive control.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The discrepancies in OFC lesion effects across studies may reflect differences in training history, cue presence during delays, and precise lesion location.
