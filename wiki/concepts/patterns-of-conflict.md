# Patterns of Conflict

## Summary

"Patterns of Conflict" was John Boyd's final and most profound intellectual achievement. Synthesizing decades of study in philosophy, scientific theory, military history, psychology, and other disciplines with his combat experience, Boyd created a dazzling six-hour briefing that articulated patterns of conflict and competing. The briefing profoundly influenced both the U.S. Army and Marine Corps to change their basic doctrines on war fighting, with results evident in the Gulf War.

## Key facts

- Boyd synthesized his studies of philosophy, theory of science, military history, psychology, and other disciplines into the "Patterns of Conflict" briefing. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p12]
- The briefing was six hours long. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p13]
- "Patterns of Conflict" convinced both the U.S. Army and Marine Corps to change their basic doctrines on war fighting. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p13]
- The results of Boyd's teachings were manifested in the decisive victory of the Gulf War in 1991. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p13]
- Boyd first delivered 'Patterns of Conflict' in September 1976, one month after completing 'Destruction and Creation.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p322]
- Versions of the briefing were designated 'Warp I' through 'Warp XII' with 'wicker' modifications, each dated and signed with Boyd's signature. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p322]
- The briefing went through dizzying nomenclature changes almost weekly, with Boyd's final two-day delivery of his complete work taking approximately fourteen hours. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p323]
- Boyd required audiences to hear the full brief, refusing to give abbreviated versions; he once refused to brief the Chief of Naval Operations when asked for a shorter version. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p323]
- The final source list for 'Patterns' numbered 323 books covering military history from Sun Tzu through World War II. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p323]
- The briefing identified four qualities needed to shape the environment: variety, rapidity, harmony, and initiative. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p330]
- Boyd used the briefing to update and affirm Sun Tzu's philosophy while repudiating von Clausewitz's emphasis on decisive battles and minimizing friction rather than maximizing the enemy's friction. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p332]
- Boyd described 'Patterns of Conflict' as 'not a theory, it's a briefing' lasting five hours, which he refused to compress. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p372]
- The briefing covered Sun Tzu, the Battle of Leuctra (371 B.C.), Arbela (331 B.C.), Cannae (216 B.C.), Genghis Khan, Belisarius, Napoléon, Heinz Guderian, and great commanders. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p373]
- Boyd taught how to 'defog' von Clausewitz and explained concepts including OODA Loops, Schwerpunkt, Fingerspitzengefuhl, surfaces and gaps, mission orders, and 'water going downhill.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p373]
- The briefing ran eight hours at the Amphibious Warfare School in January 1980, with students staying past nightfall, such was its impact. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p373]
- Boyd assembled all his briefings along with 'Destruction and Creation' into a one-inch-thick document titled 'A Discourse on Winning and Losing' with a green cover, known as the 'Green Book.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p375]
- Wyly distributed four hundred copies of the Green Book to Marine officers at Quantico. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p375]
- Boyd taught that 'People should come first. Then ideas. And then hardware,' inverting conventional military wisdom that prioritized hardware. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p376]
- Boyd emphasized inductive thinking and taught that there were always multiple solutions to problems, never committing to a single solution. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p376]
- Spinney identified that the Gulf War's success was directly attributable to 'Patterns of Conflict,' particularly the concepts of multiple thrusts and deception that caused Iraqi forces to surrender by the thousands. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p419]
- Only three living people can deliver the 'Patterns of Conflict' briefing: Chuck Spinney, Chet Richards, and Pierre Sprey. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p434]

## Inferences

- Inference: The influence on the Gulf War suggests the briefing contained actionable strategic principles for modern warfare.
- Inference: The briefing's two core messages were: (1) warfare's essence is cheng and ch'i (orthodox and unorthodox approaches), and (2) success requires operating at a faster OODA Loop than the opponent.
- Inference: The Gulf War demonstrated that Boyd's briefing contained actionable tactical and strategic principles that could be applied to real warfare with devastating effect against a conventional enemy.
