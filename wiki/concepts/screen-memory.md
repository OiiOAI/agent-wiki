# Screen Memory

## Summary

A screen memory is a childhood memory that serves as a protective or substitutive veil over more significant but less accessible memories or experiences. Freud first introduces this concept in discussing the dream of the book-destruction scene in childhood, which he connects to his later bibliophile tendencies. Screen memories function by displacement, replacing significant content with seemingly trivial but emotionally linked material.

## Key facts

- Freud states he 'had early discovered, of course, that passions often lead to sorrow,' and connected this memory of destroying a book in childhood to later bibliophilic tendencies. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1438]
- The childhood scene of pulling apart a book 'leaf by leaf, like an artichoke' was recognized by Freud as a 'screen memory' for his later book-collecting passion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1438]
- Screen memories owe their existence to displacement: they are substitutes in mnemic reproduction for other impressions that are really significant. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2038]
- Screen memories can be retrogressive (where the content belongs to later years but screens earlier experiences) or pushed-ahead (where recent indifferent impressions screen earlier experiences). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2039]
- Childhood memories often function as screen memories, showing remarkable analogy with the legends and myths nations preserve. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2042]

## Inferences

- Inference: Screen memories represent a form of protective displacement where emotionally significant material is replaced by less threatening content that preserves some connection to the original.
- Inference: The formation of screen memories parallels the mechanism of dream-work condensation.
