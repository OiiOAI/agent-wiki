# Nonlinearity

## Summary

Nonlinearity occurs when the relationship between cause and effect is not proportional—a small change in one variable does not produce a proportional change in another. Nonlinear systems are harder to predict and control because they can flip a system from one mode of behavior to another. They are a chief cause of shifting dominance between feedback loops.

## Key facts

- A nonlinear relationship is one in which the cause does not produce a proportional effect; the relationship can only be drawn with curves, not a straight line. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p117]
- In a nonlinear system, twice the push could produce one-sixth the response, or the response squared, or no response at all. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p117]
- Nonlinearities are important because they change the relative strengths of feedback loops and can flip a system from one mode of behavior to another. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p117]
- Many relationships in systems are nonlinear; their relative strengths shift in disproportionate amounts as the stocks in the system shift. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p120]
