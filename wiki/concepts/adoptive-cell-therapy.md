# Adoptive Cell Therapy (ACT)

## Summary

Adoptive cell therapy (ACT) is a class of immunotherapy where T cells are transferred into a patient to bolster their ability to fight their own tumor. T cells are either harvested from the tumor (tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes or TILs) and multiplied, or harvested from blood and genetically modified to recognize the specific tumor. ACT effectively designs a customized anticancer drug for each individual patient.

## Key facts

- Adoptive cell therapy involves transferring supplemental T cells into a patient to bolster their ability to fight their own tumor. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p170]
- Tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are T cells that already recognize the tumor as a threat but may be too few to mount a complete response. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p170]
- By removing TILs and multiplying them by a factor of 1,000 or more, then reinfusing them, a much better response can be expected. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p170]
- ACT effectively means designing a new, customized anticancer drug for each individual patient. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p170]
