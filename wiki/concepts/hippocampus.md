# Hippocampus

## Summary

A brain structure that serves as the way station for learning and memory, gathering stimuli from throughout the brain, cross-referencing new with stored information, and bundling memories for the prefrontal cortex. It contains many cortisol receptors, regulates the fight-or-flight response, and is one of two brain structures that produces new nerve cells.

## Key facts

- The hippocampus is the way station for many aspects of learning and memory, gathering incoming stimuli from throughout the brain and cross-referencing new information with stored information. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p223]
- It bundles information together as a memory that is sent to the prefrontal cortex for processing. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p223]
- The hippocampus contains a vast number of cortisol receptors and is the first step in regulating the feedback loop of the fight-or-flight response. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p223]
- Its close relationship with cortisol leaves the hippocampus particularly vulnerable to the ravages of stress and aging. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p223]
- The hippocampus is one of only two structures in the brain that clearly produces its own new nerve cells. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p223]
- The hippocampus sits along the inner edge of the cortical sheet and is crucial in learning and memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p124]
- The hippocampus integrates information arriving simultaneously from different parts of the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p128]
- Neurons in the hippocampus become active in small, transient groups called assemblies or ensembles, even at rest. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p128]
- The hippocampus encodes a spatial map of the environment and where the animal is in it, including direction and facing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p130]
- Synaptic plasticity in the hippocampus is gated by neuromodulators like dopamine, serotonin, and acetylcholine, which signal attention, arousal, salience, reward, or surprise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p132]
- The hippocampus is critical for forming new long-term memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p46]
- Before the 1930s, anatomists believed the hippocampus's main function was to support the sense of smell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p31]
- Scoville removed the front half of Henry's hippocampus along with the amygdala and entorhinal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p30]
- The entorhinal cortex is the major route by which information reaches the hippocampus for memory consolidation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p31]
- Damage to both hippocampi causes profound anterograde amnesia while sparing other cognitive abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p31]
- James Papez's 1937 paper described the Papez circuit, including the hippocampus, as providing a mechanism for feeling and expressing emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p31]
- Brenda Milner's 1957 paper on Patient H.M. definitively linked the formation of new memories to the hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p41]
- H.M. (Henry Molaison) had about 5 centimeters of tissue removed from the left and right hippocampus along with surrounding neocortical tissue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p41]
- The hippocampus organizes memories according to context—when and where they happened—rather than according to what happened. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p44]
- The hippocampus is the first region affected by Alzheimer's disease, explaining why patients in early stages frequently get lost. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p47]
- Domoid acid poisoning in sea lions causes significant hippocampal damage, leaving them disoriented and unable to recall foraging sites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p46]
- Adults do not have reliable episodic memories from before the age of two due to ongoing hippocampal development in early childhood. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p50]
- The hippocampus forms memories that capture context, while the amygdala connects those memories with survival circuits that generate raw sensations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p97]
- The hippocampus and amygdala together enable recalling what happened and reexperiencing how we felt when it happened. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p97]
- Activity in the hippocampus spiked when people saw a word and formed a memory that helped them recall contextual details later. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p115]
- Emrah Düzel found that older adults with high brain levels of tau and beta-amyloid (proteins accumulating in Alzheimer's) showed a dampened hippocampal response to novel pictures, predicting poorer memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p129]
- Jennifer Ryan and Neal Cohen found that people with amnesia whose hippocampus was damaged did not fixate on changed parts of images—their 'spider-sense' was gone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery that hippocampus damage causes amnesia came from studying cases like Henry, F.C., and P.B., where bilateral damage resulted in memory loss.
- Inference: The hippocampus acts like an index or pointer system that connects different cell assemblies active during a past event, allowing us to reactivate all components of a memory when we revisit the original context.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Research on H.M. was complicated because his surgery also damaged surrounding tissue and white matter, so separating hippocampal-specific functions required studying patients with more localized damage.
