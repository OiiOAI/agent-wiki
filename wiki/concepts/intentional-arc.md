# Intentional Arc

## Summary

The intentional arc is Merleau-Ponty's term for the integrated structure that subtends the life of consciousness, binding together sensibility and motility, perception and action. Things in the world have motor senses or affordances that elicit appropriate actions, but these affordances exist only in relation to the motor skills of the subject. The intentional arc is an existential structure prior to the subject-object dichotomy.

## Key facts

- The intentional arc is what Merleau-Ponty calls the 'intentional arc' subtending the life of consciousness, which integrates sensibility and motility, perception and action (1962, p. 136). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p245]
- Things in the world bring forth suitable intentional actions and motor projects from the subject (the subject is a project of the world), but things in the world have specific motor senses or affordances only in relation to the motor skills of the subject (the world is projected by the subject). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p245]
- The intentional arc and being-in-the-world are neither purely first-personal (subjective) nor purely third-personal (objective), neither mental nor physical; they are existential structures prior to and more fundamental than these abstractions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p245]
