# Procrastination

## Summary

Procrastination is a nearly universal vice affecting 95% of people at least sometimes, with over 20% of surveyed people internationally considering it a defining personal characteristic. Research by Baumeister and Tice found that procrastinators got worse grades but enjoyed better health early in the semester—until the end, when all-nighters caused a sharp rise in illness and stress. Impulsiveness, not perfectionism, is the trait most consistently linked to procrastination. The 'deadline test' showed that procrastinators underestimated how long tasks would take and suffered severe consequences for missing final deadlines.

## Key facts

- Procrastinators received lower grades but reported better health early in the semester, only to suffer more stress and illness at the end when forced to complete work last-minute. - Impulsiveness, not perfectionism, is the trait most consistently linked to procrastination. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p219]
- In one study, college seniors predicted 34 days to complete their honors thesis but actually took 56 days, with only a handful finishing by their best-case prediction. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p227]
- Procrastination is idiotic because no project completes itself, yet people delay despite knowing tasks are beneficial. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p187]
- Baumeister's experiment showed that students forbidden from eating cookies gave up on maths problems twice as fast—willpower is depleted by self-control. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p187]
- Willpower needs time to refuel; eating and relaxing restore it. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p188]
- External deadlines (from authorities like teachers or the IRS) work better than self-imposed deadlines. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p188]
