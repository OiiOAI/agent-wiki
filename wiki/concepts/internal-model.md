# Internal Model

## Summary

Internal models are neural representations that relate motor commands to the sensory signals expected as a result of movement. Forward models estimate future sensory inputs based on motor outputs, while inverse models calculate motor outputs from sensory inputs to achieve desired sensory consequences.

## Key facts

- An internal model that represents the causal relationship between actions and their consequences is called a forward model because it estimates future sensory inputs based on motor outputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p797]
- A copy of a descending motor command acting on the sensorimotor system is passed into a forward model that acts as a neural simulator; this copy is known as an efference copy or corollary discharge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p797]
- An internal model that calculates motor outputs from sensory inputs is known as an inverse model, which determines what motor commands are needed to produce particular movements necessary to achieve a desired sensory consequence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p797]
- Internal models may not exactly match true relationships because of structural differences (models only approximate true relationships) or errors in the model's parameters such as incorrect estimates of segment lengths. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p797]
- The idea that the nervous system has internal models to guide behavior originated in Kenneth Craik's 1943 book 'The Nature of Explanation'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p798]

## Inferences

- Inference: Forward and inverse models in series can produce consistent behavior if their structures and parameters are correct, with the forward model's output matching the inverse model's input.
