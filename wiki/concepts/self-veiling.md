# Self-Veiling

## Summary

Self-veiling is the necessary act by which finite players suspend their freedom to act seriously within their chosen roles. Players must intentionally forget the inherently voluntary nature of their play, adopting masks that allow them to play with appropriate seriousness. This is a contradictory act—a free suspension of freedom—because no amount of veiling can conceal the veiling itself. The veiling must be practiced both by players and by the audience who witnesses the performance.

## Key facts

- Some self-veiling is present in all finite games; players must intentionally forget the inherently voluntary nature of their play, else all competitive effort will desert them. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p25]
- Self-veiling is a contradictory act—a free suspension of freedom—and no amount of veiling can conceal the veiling itself. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p27]
- The actress who makes Ophelia appear as this woman must keep her own person distinct from the role; what she feels as the person she is has nothing to do with Ophelia. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p25-26]
