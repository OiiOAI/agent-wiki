# Reference Dependence

## Summary

The psychological phenomenon where outcomes are evaluated not in absolute terms but as gains or losses relative to a neutral reference point, typically the status quo. This phenomenon is central to prospect theory and has been demonstrated in both humans and capuchin monkeys.

## Key facts

- Capuchins evaluate their payoffs relative to arbitrary reference points established by what is initially shown or promised, just as humans do. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p88]
- European starlings (Sturnus vulgaris) also show framing-based risk-preference reversal, suggesting reference dependence may extend broadly across the animal kingdom. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p89]

## Inferences

- Inference: Reference dependence appears to be a fundamental characteristic of valuation systems across species, possibly evolved long ago in our common ancestor with other primate species.
