# Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity

## Summary

Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) is a form of synaptic plasticity where the relative timing of presynaptic and postsynaptic activity determines whether LTP or LTD occurs. If the presynaptic cell fires just before the postsynaptic cell, LTP results; if the postsynaptic cell fires first, LTD results.

## Key facts

- In STDP, if the presynaptic cell fires just before the postsynaptic cell (within milliseconds), LTP occurs; if the postsynaptic cell fires just before the EPSP, LTD occurs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1396]
- The pairing rules of STDP follow Hebb's postulate and result largely from the cooperative properties of the NMDA receptor-channel. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1396]
- STDP can be induced by pairing a single presynaptic stimulus with a single postsynaptic action potential at low frequency (one pair per second), which is more natural than high-frequency tetanic stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1396]
