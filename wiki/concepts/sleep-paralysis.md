# Sleep paralysis

## Summary

Sleep paralysis occurs when a person wakes from REM sleep but the REM atonia (loss of muscle tone) fails to switch off, leaving them paralyzed with their eyes open. The brain often continues dreaming, integrating visual hallucinations with the bedroom environment, leading to perceptions of intruders, monsters, or other frightening figures. Approximately a quarter of adults have experienced this at some point, most commonly after sleep deprivation.

## Inferences

- Inference: Sleep paralysis provides evidence that REM sleep mechanisms (dreaming and atonia) can continue into the waking state when state transitions fail.
- Inference: Sleep paralysis demonstrates the intermixing of REM sleep processes with waking consciousness, representing a state where REM atonia and REM-like hallucinations persist into wakefulness.
- Inference: Sleep paralysis represents a state where REM sleep processes continue into wakefulness, similar to how cataplexy affects narcolepsy patients.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neurophysiological mechanisms that cause some individuals to experience recurrent sleep paralysis while others never experience it are not fully understood.
