# Extended Consciousness

## Summary

Extended consciousness is the complex form of consciousness that provides an elaborate sense of self—an identity and a person placed at a point in individual historical time, richly aware of the lived past and anticipated future, and cognizant of the surrounding world. It has several levels of organization, evolves across the lifetime, depends on conventional and working memory, and attains its highest reaches in humans through language.

## Key facts

- Extended consciousness provides the organism with an elaborate sense of self—an identity and a person—placed at a point in individual historical time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Extended consciousness makes the organism keenly cognizant of the world beside it, aware of the lived past and the anticipated future. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Extended consciousness depends on conventional memory and working memory, and when at its human peak, is enhanced by language. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Extended consciousness is built on the foundation of core consciousness; it is not an independent variety of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Impairments of extended consciousness allow core consciousness to remain unscathed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- The autobiographical self depends on systematized memories of situations in which core consciousness was involved in knowing invariant characteristics of an organism's life. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p18]
- Extended consciousness requires autobiographical memory, working memory, language, and the intelligent manipulation of facts. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p119]
- Patient David demonstrates that extended consciousness can be profoundly impaired while core consciousness remains perfectly intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p117]
- Extended consciousness declines progressively in Alzheimer's disease, first affecting autobiographical self before affecting core consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p103]
- Unlike core consciousness, extended consciousness has levels or grades and varies in richness based on the availability of autobiographical knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p121]
- Extended consciousness goes beyond the here and now, flanking the present with the past and the anticipated future. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p195]
- The autobiographical self hinges on consistent reactivation of selected autobiographical memories, which the brain treats as objects capable of generating pulses of core consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p196]
- Extended consciousness is based on two enabling contributions: the ability to learn and retain records of experiences, and the ability to reactivate those records so they can generate a sense of self knowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p197]
- Working memory holds active, over substantial periods, the multiple images defining the autobiographical self and the object, in timescales of seconds, minutes, hours, and years. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p199]
- Extended consciousness is necessary for internal deployment of recalled knowledge in different sensory systems and for subsequent manipulation in problem solving. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p202]
- Loss of core consciousness entails loss of extended consciousness, but impairments of extended consciousness are compatible with preserved core consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p201]
- Extended consciousness requires autobiographical memory, language, working memory, and reasoning ability operating over core consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p311]
- The autobiographical self emerges from the accumulation of repeated instances of core consciousness connected to a body of memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p304]
- Extended consciousness is disrupted by damage to dorsolateral prefrontal cortices and frontal pole, although core consciousness may remain intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p270]

## Inferences

- Inference: Extended consciousness represents the form of consciousness most people identify with being conscious, encompassing self-awareness, identity, and personhood.
- Inference: Apes and possibly some dogs have an autobiographical self and thus some form of extended consciousness, though not full personhood.
- Inference: Extended consciousness represents the integration of core consciousness with the vast memory systems of the brain, allowing for the rich self-awareness characteristic of human experience.
- Inference: The difference between human and nonhuman consciousness may be more a matter of extended than core consciousness.
