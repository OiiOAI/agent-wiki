# Meaning and Value in Biology

## Summary

Meaning and value in biological systems are not inherent in external things but arise from the interaction between organisms and their environment through natural selection. Good things are those that increase persistence; bad things decrease it. The value inheres in the whole system: organism interacting with environment and consequences over time. Mitchell argues that purpose and meaning are crucial for understanding what life is and how true agency can exist.

## Key facts

- Before living organisms existed, nothing in the universe was for anything. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p42]
- Good things are ones that increase persistence; bad things decrease persistence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p66]
- The value of something is not inherent solely in the thing itself or in the signal about it but in the whole system viewed through the feedback from natural selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p66]
- Natural selection packs causal potential into life—potential energy that can be used to direct behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p67]
