# Sprague Experiment

## Summary

A landmark neuroscience experiment conducted by J.M. Sprague and colleagues in which bilateral lesions were made in cats' upper brain stem, cutting ascending sensory tracts and interrupting auditory and vestibular inputs while leaving lower brainstem reticular nuclei intact. The animals remained awake but showed profound behavioral changes.

## Key facts

- After bilateral lesions cutting somatosensory inputs to upper midbrain, hypothalamus, thalamus, and cerebral cortex, cats displayed abolition of emotionality and neglect of olfactory stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p359]
- The lesioned cats exhibited aimless, stereotypical behaviors unrelated to surroundings and to the animals' needs, and were described as looking like automata. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p359]
- The animals were awake but deprived of emotion and unconnected with the situation, remaining so for two and a half years until sacrifice. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p360]
- Damasio interprets this study as suggesting that intact reticular nuclei can generate wakefulness and permit behavior, but do not guarantee adaptive behavior hallmarking consciousness and planning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p360]
- The study suggests that a continuous diet of current-body-state signals must be necessary to maintain emotion, and likely consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p360]
