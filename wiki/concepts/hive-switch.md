# Hive switch

## Summary

The 'hive switch' is Haidt's term for psychological mechanisms that enable humans to temporarily set aside self-interest and act as part of a larger collective. When activated through rituals, synchronized activities, or shared experiences, people can experience reduced self-awareness and increased feelings of connection to their group.

## Key facts

- The hive switch is activated by group activities including synchronized movement (marching, dancing), shared emotional experiences (rituals, concerts), and altered states of consciousness. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p404]
- Oxytocin plays a role in hive psychology by promoting group bonding and altruism, working through dopamine (reward) and serotonin (reduced anxiety). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p406]
- The 'rally-round-the-flag' effect demonstrates the hive switch: external threats trigger reflexive solidarity and group bonding. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p392]
- Haidt describes the hive switch as operating alongside older primate selfishness, making humans 'Homo duplex' (divided beings with both individual and group-level nature). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p393]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hive switch helps explain why humans can sustain large-scale cooperation even among non-kin, unlike most other primates.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The neurobiological mechanisms underlying the hive switch remain incompletely characterized; Haidt acknowledges he is not a neuroscience expert.
