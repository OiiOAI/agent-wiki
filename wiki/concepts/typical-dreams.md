# Typical Dreams

## Summary

Typical dreams are dream themes reported by a large proportion of the population across time, regions, and cultures. Studies show that no typical dream is truly universal, with only four of the top fifteen themes having a prevalence exceeding 70 percent. The most common typical dreams include being chased or pursued (85 percent), sexual experiences (78 percent), school/ teachers/studying (77 percent), and falling (76 percent). Typical dreams have been documented across millennia and across cultures, with remarkably consistent rank orderings.

## Key facts

- The most frequently reported typical dream is being chased or pursued, reported by 85 percent of people. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p164]
- Sexual experiences are reported in 78 percent of typical dreams, with men reporting such dreams more often than women. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p164]
- Only four of the top fifteen typical dream themes have a prevalence exceeding 70 percent, and none exceed 85 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p164]
- The four most frequently reported dream themes in a 1958 study were all among the top five reported by Canadian students forty years later. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p163]
- Women are more likely than men to report dreams about school, teachers, studying, or about a living person who is dead, but less likely to report sexual dreams or dreams of finding money. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p164]
- Animals appear in up to 40 percent of children's dreams but in only about 5 percent of adult dreams in urban populations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p161]
- The four most frequently reported typical dreams are: being chased or pursued (85%), sexual experiences (78%), school/teachers/studying (77%), and falling (76%). [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p175]
- No typical dream is truly universal; only four of the top fifteen themes have a prevalence exceeding 70 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p176]
- Typical dream themes show remarkable consistency across cultures, with American, Japanese, German, and Canadian students reporting similar rank orderings. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p174]
- Many themes commonly discussed in popular media are not actually common; only 35% report inappropriately dressed dreams and fewer than 30% report teeth falling out dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p176]
- Studies show consistent gender differences: women more likely to report school/teacher dreams, men more likely to report sexual experiences or finding money. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p176]
- Typical dreams are dreams which almost everyone has dreamt alike and which we are accustomed to assume must have the same meaning for everyone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p241]
- Dreams of being naked or insufficiently dressed in the presence of strangers sometimes occur with the additional feature of there being a complete absence of any such feeling as shame on the dreamer's part. - Children frequently manifest a desire to exhibit, and in dreams of being naked, the latent content is concerned with forbidden wishes that have fallen victim to repression. - In dreams of the death of loved relatives, the meaning is a wish that the person in question may die. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p249]
- Dreams of a parent's death apply with preponderant frequency to the parent who is of the same sex as the dreamer: men dream mostly of their father's death and women of their mother's. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p256]
- Dreams of the death of loved relatives involve a repressed wish finding expression while evading censorship, often combined with daytime worries about the person's safety. - The wish to have a parent dead is universal, and such dreams reveal 'the fate of all of us, perhaps, to direct our first sexual impulse towards our mother and our first hatred and our first murderous wish against our father.'. - Flying, falling, and giddiness dreams originate from childhood games involving movement, which are reproduced in dreams without the hands that held the child up, leaving the sensation of floating or falling unsupported. - Examination dreams (dreams of having failed examinations) occur only in people who successfully passed, and their anxiety relates to taking responsibility rather than to actual academic failure. - Flying and floating dreams in males usually have a grossly sensual meaning, while in women they may express a desire to be elevated above contamination or a wish to be like a bird. - Dreams of flying reproduce impressions of childhood involving games of movement shown by uncles and parents, such as rushing across rooms with outstretched arms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p393]

[raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p347]

## Inferences

- Inference: Typical dreams allow the dreaming brain to ask key what-if questions and explore possible answers by plunging dreamers into virtual worlds.
- Inference: Typical dreams may represent universal human concerns and conflicts that the brain processes through metaphor during sleep.
- Inference: The explanation for same-sex parent death dreams being more frequent is that a sexual preference makes itself felt at an early age, with boys regarding their fathers and girls their mothers as rivals in love.
- Inference: The 'paradise' of childhood nakedness represents a mass phantasy of the childhood of the individual, explaining why humans were naked in paradise before shame and anxiety awoke.
- Inference: Typical dreams provide evidence that certain unconscious wishes (such as hostility toward parents) are universal rather than individually pathological.
- Inference: Most dreams of adults deal with sexual material and give expression to erotic wishes, as the sexual drive has been subjected to more suppression than any other drive since childhood.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that dreamers often cannot produce associations to typical dreams, limiting interpretation techniques.
- Uncertain: Whether the category of 'dream phantasies' (dreams not subject to condensation and displacement) truly exists; Freud expressed uncertainty about this in a 1930 footnote.
