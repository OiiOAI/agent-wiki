# Group Dynamics

## Summary

Group dynamics refer to observable patterns that human groups fall into almost automatically throughout history. These include: group culture (the style, mood, and ideal the group imagines for itself), group rules and codes (implicit standards of conduct), the group court (hierarchy centered on a leader with courtiers), the group enemy (reflexive focus on outsiders to unite the tribe), and group factions (splitting into subgroups over time). Larger and more established groups exert greater control over individuals and become more conservative over time.

## Key facts

- Group culture centers on an ideal the group imagines for itself—liberal, modern, progressive, ruthlessly competitive, tasteful—and the culture often reflects the founders of the group. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p509]
- The larger the group and the more established the culture over time, the more likely it will control the individual rather than the reverse. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p509]
- No matter the type of culture, the longer a group exists and the larger it grows, the more conservative it will become as people try to hold onto what they have made. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p509]
- Group rules and codes are never written down but are implicit, with violations risking becoming a nonentity or being fired without necessarily knowing the cause. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p510]
- The court dynamic centers on a leader with courtiers whose power depends on closeness to the leader, with flattery being the most immediate way to gain attention. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p511]
- Standing out too much in a court as too brilliant or charming will stir up envy, so courtiers learn to downplay successes and strategically give others credit. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p512]
- Any group will reflexively focus on some hated enemy to help bring the tribe together, with differences greatly exaggerated. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p513]
- Over enough time, individuals in a group will begin to split off into factions because in a group over a certain size, differences become noticeable and power to influence as individuals is reduced. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p514]

## Inferences

- Inference: Understanding group dynamics allows individuals to navigate social spaces strategically while maintaining independence, and to resist the downward pull of primitive group behaviors.
