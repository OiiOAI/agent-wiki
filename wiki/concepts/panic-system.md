# PANIC System

## Summary

The PANIC system generates the emotional distress that occurs when social bonds are threatened or severed. It produces crying, vocalizations, and other responses associated with grief, loneliness, and social separation. This system underlies the formation and dissolution of social attachments.

## Key facts

- Young rats exhibit peak separation distress vocalizations when isolated, demonstrating innate social attachment systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p26]
- Separation distress vocalizations decrease as animals mature, following species-typical developmental trajectories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp,Jaak.pdf#p26]
- PANIC system arousal can generate long-lasting emotional states that contribute to mood disorders. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp.pdf#p27]
- The PANIC system mediates separation distress, which promotes active solicitation of help and social support when social bonds are threatened. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]
- People who suffer from repeated panic attacks typically have had childhood histories characterized by separation anxiety problems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]
- Both separation distress and panic attacks are commonly accompanied by autonomic symptoms including weakness, difficulty breathing, and a lump in the throat. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]
- Tricyclic antidepressants like imipramine reduce both separation-distress vocalizations in animals and panic attacks in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]
- SSRIs and MAO inhibitors that facilitate serotonin activity are effective in controlling panic attacks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Opiates reduce separation distress but not fearful behaviors, while benzodiazepines reduce fear but are less effective for separation calls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]
- The neuropeptide CRF participates in both FEAR and PANIC processes, with stronger effects on PANIC in young chicks and primates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]
- Presentation of fearful stimuli reduces separation calls, suggesting separation distress does not promote activity in fear circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p146]

## Inferences

- Inference: The PANIC system likely involves the periaqueductal gray and oxytocin/vasopressin systems based on neural circuitry involved in social attachment
- Inference: The PANIC system appears to have evolutionary adaptive value: the initial protest phase (crying) may help parents find lost offspring, while the subsequent despair phase conserves energy and minimizes predator detection
- Inference: The persistent stress of social isolation leads to overresponsiveness of the pituitary adrenal system, which may contribute to the despair and depression following social loss

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Species differences in separation distress timing reflect different developmental strategies rather than fundamental differences in emotional systems
- Uncertain: Klein's alternative view suggests panic attacks arise from a suffocation alarm mechanism that may be functionally coupled to the PANIC system, but the relationship remains to be demonstrated
