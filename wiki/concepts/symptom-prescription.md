# Symptom Prescription

## Summary

Symptom prescription is a paradoxical intervention technique where the therapist instructs the patient to deliberately perform or produce their symptom. By making the patient actively 'do' the symptom rather than fight it, the willpower-based attempted solution is disrupted, and the symptom loses its self-perpetuating character. This technique is particularly effective when the patient's efforts to control or eliminate the symptom are themselves maintaining the problem.

## Key facts

- Symptom prescription instructs patients to deliberately perform or produce their symptom, making them actively do it rather than fight it, thereby disrupting the willpower-based attempted solution. - For the insomniac who tries to force sleep, the prescription is to lie in bed and not close eyes until fast asleep—forcing him to stay awake prevents the paradox of willing sleep. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L83-85]
- For public speaking fear, the person is instructed to preface the speech by announcing extreme nervousness and probable overwhelm—this completely reverses the attempted concealment solution. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L124-127]
- Patients often accept paradoxical behavior prescriptions with a big smile, as if catching on to the essentially humorous yet deeply serious nature of paradox. ## Inferences

- Inference: Symptom prescription works because the patient's attempted solution IS the problem—once they stop fighting the symptom, it can resolve.
