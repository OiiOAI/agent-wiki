# Schema

## Summary

Schema are mental libraries of prototypical situations developed over time through direct experience or vicarious learning. They represent pattern consisting of the state of relevant elements in memory, allowing instant recognition of known situation classes through pattern matching. This mechanism provides a significant shortcut for SA and decision-making—rather than processing data to determine Level 2 or 3 SA, information is recalled as part of the schema. Klein's recognition-primed decision making leverages this capability. Critical cues trigger schema activation, and scripts (set action sequences) often accompany schemas to reduce working memory load.

## Key facts

- Schema represent prototypical states of the mental model, allowing pattern matching between current and learned situations for instant recognition. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p238]
- Recognition-primed decision making leverages schema to provide SA information through recall rather than working memory processing. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p238]
- Critical cues trigger schema activation, and new situations need not be exactly like previously encountered ones to achieve a match—only a few critical cues may be required. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p238]
- Scripts (set sequences of actions) associated with schemas diminish working memory load for generating and selecting behaviors. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p238]
