# Dreaming experience

## Summary

Dreaming experience (DE) refers to the subjective report of conscious experiences upon awakening from sleep. The study distinguished DE (with content recall), DEWR (dreaming experience without recall - having an experience but unable to remember content), and NE (no experience). DE was associated with decreased low-frequency and increased high-frequency EEG activity in the posterior cortical hot zone during both NREM and REM sleep.

## Key facts

- The study used three categories for subjective reports: Dreaming Experience with recall (DE), Dreaming Experience Without Recall (DEWR), and No Experience (NE). [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Subjects rated dreams on a scale from exclusively thought-like (no sensory content) to exclusively perceptual (vivid sensory content). [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]
- Thought-like dream experiences correlated with frontal high-frequency activity, while perceptual experiences correlated with parietal, occipital, and temporal high-frequency activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p5]
- The contrast between DE and DEWR in low-frequency range showed no significant differences, suggesting the posterior hot zone activation marks experiences themselves rather than recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/The neural correlates of consciousness and unconsciousness in sleep.pdf#p3]

## Inferences

- Inference: Reports of forgotten experiences (DEWR) and absent experiences (NE) carry distinct EEG signatures, supporting the trustworthiness of subjective dream reports when carefully collected
