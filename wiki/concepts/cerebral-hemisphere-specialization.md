# Cerebral Hemisphere Specialization

## Summary

Cerebral hemisphere specialization refers to the functional differences between the left and right hemispheres, with each hemisphere being primarily concerned with sensory and motor processes on the contralateral side of the body. The left hemisphere is typically dominant for language in most people.

## Key facts

- Each hemisphere is concerned primarily with sensory and motor processes on the contralateral (opposite) side of the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- In most people, the left hemisphere is dominant for language processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Studies by Sperry, Gazzaniga, and Bogen on split-brain patients revealed that each hemisphere has a consciousness functioning independently of the other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p21]
- The corpus callosum connects the two cerebral hemispheres and allows communication between them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p14]
