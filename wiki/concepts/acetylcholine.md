# Acetylcholine

## Summary

Acetylcholine (ACh) was the first neurotransmitter discovered. It mediates neuromuscular transmission and widespread brain functions including attention, arousal, and REM sleep regulation through distinct muscarinic and nicotinic receptor subtypes.

## Key facts

- ACh was the first neurotransmitter identified, discovered by Otto Loewi who demonstrated that vagus nerve stimulation released a chemical (vagusstoff) that slowed heart rate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p59]
- Six major cholinergic cell groups (Ch-1 to Ch-6) exist in the brain, with Ch-4 in the basal forebrain projecting throughout the cortex and degenerating in Alzheimer's disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p62]
- ACh is synthesized from choline and acetyl CoA via choline acetyltransferase (ChAT), and degraded by cholinesterase into reusable choline and acetate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p58]
- Cholinergic systems mediate attention and arousal in all sensory systems, with ascending projections from mesencephalic Ch-5 and Ch-6 facilitating thalamic information processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p62]
- ACh was identified as a chemical transmitter by Otto Loewi in the 1920s, demonstrating that the vagus nerve slows the heart by releasing a chemical substance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p242]
- Acetylcholine acts on excitatory nicotinic receptors at all neuromuscular junctions to trigger muscle contraction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p250]
- Acetylcholinesterase rapidly hydrolyzes ACh in the synaptic cleft, limiting transmitter action to one hit before receptor dissociation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p255]
