# Primary emotions

## Summary

Primary emotions are innate, preorganized emotional responses that occur through prewired neural circuits, particularly involving the amygdala and anterior cingulate. They are triggered automatically by specific stimulus features without requiring conscious evaluation, and include basic emotions such as fear, anger, happiness, sadness, and disgust.

## Key facts

- Primary emotions depend on limbic system circuitry, with the amygdala and anterior cingulate being the prime players. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p153]
- The amygdala possesses dispositional representations which trigger the enactment of a body state characteristic of specific emotions when key stimulus features are detected. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p151]
- Primary emotions operate through a preorganized mechanism: particular stimuli excite specific patterns of body reaction without requiring mental evaluation of the stimulus significance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p150]
- The neural machinery for emotion-related facial movements is different from the machinery for voluntary facial control; emotional smiles are controlled from limbic cortices and basal ganglia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p161]
- Primary emotions are activated by innate dispositional representations when early sensory cortices detect key features of stimuli (e.g., large size, certain motions, growling sounds). [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p151]
- The amygdala was implicated in emotion by studies including those of Pribram, Weiskrantz, Aggleton, Passingham, LeDoux, Rolls, Davis, Squire, and surgical stimulation studies by Penfield and Gloor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p153]
- Kluver-Bucy syndrome, resulting from resection of temporal lobe tissue containing the amygdala, produces affective indifference among other symptoms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p153]
- William James proposed that emotions result from perception of bodily changes: 'What kind of an emotion of fear would be left if the feeling of quickened heart-beats nor of shallow breathing... were present?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p129]

## Inferences

- Inference: Primary emotions represent an evolutionarily ancient mechanism for rapid response to survival-relevant stimuli.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The complete set of stimuli features that trigger specific primary emotions has not been fully characterized.
