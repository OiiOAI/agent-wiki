# Meta-Moment

## Summary

The Meta-Moment is a tool developed by Marc Brackett and Robin Stern at the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence to help people respond as their 'best selves' rather than reacting impulsively to emotional triggers. It involves pausing to create space before responding, then asking 'How would my best self respond?' The technique activates the parasympathetic nervous system, reducing cortisol release and lowering emotional temperature. The Meta-Moment has four steps: sense the shift, stop/pause and breathe, see your best self, and strategize and act.

## Key facts

- The Meta-Moment was developed by Robin Stern and Marc Brackett at the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence to help people find beneficial emotion regulation strategies. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p143]
- The Meta-Moment involves four steps: (1) sense the shift in thinking or physiology, (2) stop or pause and breathe deeply, (3) see your best self through adjectives or imagery, and (4) strategize and act using RULER tools. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p145]
- Pausing and taking a deep breath activates the parasympathetic nervous system, which reduces cortisol release and naturally lowers emotional temperature. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p143]
- Visualizing one's best self redirects attention away from the trigger toward one's values, helping choose a helpful regulation strategy. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p144]
- Brackett describes using the Meta-Moment when a student publicly challenged him, allowing him to respond calmly ('I'd love to connect with you later to hear your thoughts') rather than reacting dismissively.
