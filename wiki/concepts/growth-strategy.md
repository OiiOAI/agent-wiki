# Growth (Strategy)

## Summary

Healthy growth is not engineered by acquisition—it is the outcome of growing demand for special capabilities or of expanded capabilities, the reward for successful innovation, efficiency, and creativity. Growth by acquisition typically destroys value because buyers pay premiums plus fees. The proposition that growth itself creates value is an article of unquestioned faith that fails to recognize that most profits in commodity industries are illusory because they are plowed back into new capacity.

## Key facts

- Healthy growth is not engineered; it is the outcome of growing demand for special capabilities, the reward for successful innovation, cleverness, efficiency, and creativity. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p64]
- When you buy a company especially a public company you usually pay too much—a premium over ordinary market value usually about 25 percent plus fees. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p63]
- In commodity industries growth based on substitution has a clear ceiling—once the conversion to the substitute has taken place the growth grinds to a sudden halt. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p62]
- Crown Cork & Seal under Avery pursued rapid growth by acquisition leaving the company's traditional competitive advantage behind without replacing it. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p62]

## Inferences

- Inference: Crown's shift from focus on short runs (Connelly era, 18.5% annual return) to growth by acquisition (Avery era, 2.4% annual return) illustrates how abandoning a coherent strategy destroys shareholder value.
