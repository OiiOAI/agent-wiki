# Breathing and CO₂ Tolerance

## Summary

Built to Move presents breathing as a foundational Vital Sign, with three key components: spacious breathing (diaphragmatic belly-ribs-chest expansion), nose breathing, and CO₂ tolerance. The Breath-Hold Test (BOLT) measures CO₂ tolerance; higher scores indicate better tolerance. The authors argue that breathing well directly affects body mechanics, pain perception, and overall health, and that effective breathing supports all other Vital Signs.

## Key facts

- Effective breathing involves three fundamentals: spacious breathing (belly, ribs, chest expansion), nose breathing, and maximizing CO₂ tolerance. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L440-L443]
- Diaphragmatic breathing creates intra-abdominal pressure that provides spine stability, helping prevent back pain. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L448-L450]
- The Breath-Hold Test (BOLT) measures CO₂ tolerance by timing how long someone can hold an exhale after a normal breath in through the nose. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L460-L467]
- Below 10 seconds indicates CO₂ tolerance is way below normal; 30-40 seconds is considered normal. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L468-L472]
- Mouth breathing is associated with insomnia, sleep apnea, snoring, higher blood pressure, and musculoskeletal issues including forward head posture. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L525-L528]
- Nose breathing releases nitric oxide (NO) which widens blood vessels and enables 18% more oxygen to reach cells. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L530-L532]
- Slower breathing develops CO₂ tolerance, increases blood flow to the brain, and helps lungs absorb more oxygen. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L536-L538]
