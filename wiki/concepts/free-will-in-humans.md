# Free will in humans

## Summary

The book argues that free will in humans builds on the biological foundation of agency present in all living organisms. While questions of free will extend beyond basic agency to whether we can choose our reasons and be causes of our own character, the author argues that evolution has provided mechanisms - particularly reflective cognition, self-awareness, and self-control - that grant us genuine capacity for choice. We are not completely at the mercy of prior causes but actively shape our own character over lifetimes through introspection, metacognition, and intentional self-regulation.

## Key facts

- The remaining problem of biological determinism asks: are we able to choose our reasons? Are we causes of our own character and the criteria for our actions?. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p218]
- The author argues we have the capacity for reflective cognition, introspection, imagination, and metacognition that lets us identify and think about our own beliefs and motivations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p219]
- We have the capacity of self-control: intentional adjustment of behavior by selecting objects of attention and options for action to consider. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p219]
- The author claims agency - the capacity to act with causal power for one's own reasons - is the defining feature of life itself, and the bedrock for understanding free will in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p219]

## Inferences

- Inference: Free will does not require absolute freedom - that would not be a coherent notion - but rather the capacity to be a genuine cause of one's actions through reflective processes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific neural mechanisms enabling reflective self-awareness and self-control in humans remain to be fully elucidated.
