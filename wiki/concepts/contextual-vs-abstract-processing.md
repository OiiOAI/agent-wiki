# Contextual vs. Abstract Processing

## Summary

The right hemisphere understands things in their full context, while the left hemisphere abstracts information from context, enabling categorization and manipulation. McGilchrist argues this distinction is fundamental to understanding how each hemisphere creates a different 'version' of reality.

## Key facts

- The left hemisphere is the hemisphere of abstraction, which as the word itself tells us is the process of wresting things from their context. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p78]
- Anything that requires indirect interpretation, not explicit or literal, that requires contextual understanding, depends on the right hemisphere for its meaning. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p78]
- The right hemisphere understands from indirect contextual clues, whereas the left hemisphere identifies by labels rather than context. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p78]
- The left hemisphere operates an abstract visual-form system, storing information that remains relatively invariant across specific instances, producing abstracted types or classes; the right hemisphere is aware of and remembers what distinguishes specific instances. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p79]
- The left hemisphere's decontextualised thinking tends toward a slavish following of internal logic even if this contravenes experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p78]

## Inferences

- Inference: The left hemisphere's power to abstract and categorize enables scientific and technological progress, but when unconstrained by the right hemisphere's contextual understanding, it can produce distorted views of reality.
