# Sources of Power

## Summary

Gary Klein's 1998 book 'Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions' documents how implicit communications and what the Germans call Fingerspitzengefühl ('fingertip feel' or intuition) actually work. This research validates that experience-based decision-making under time pressure relies on mental simulations rather than rational analysis, which directly supports Boyd's concepts about rapid OODA loops and pattern recognition.

## Key facts

- Gary Klein's 'Sources of Power' documents that implicit communications and intuition actually work in decision-making. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p148]
- The follow-on book 'Intuition at Work' (2003) is also recommended as excellent. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p148]
- Klein's research demonstrates that decision-making under time pressure relies on mental simulations. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p148]
- The conventional sources of power include deductive logical thinking, analysis of probabilities, and statistical methods. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p21]
- The sources of power needed in natural settings include intuition, mental simulation, metaphor, and storytelling. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p21]
- The power of intuition enables decision makers to size up situations quickly through pattern recognition. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p21]
- The power of mental simulation lets decision makers imagine how a course of action might be carried out. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p21]
- The power of metaphor allows decision makers to draw on experience by finding parallels between current and previous situations. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p21]
- The power of storytelling helps consolidate experiences to make them available for future decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p21]
- The term sources of power was originated by Doug Lenat (1984) in artificial intelligence research to designate analytical abilities. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p25]
- The nine sources of power include intuition, mental simulation, leveraging points, seeing the invisible, storytelling, analogical reasoning, reading minds, rational analysis, and the team mind. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p345]
- The two primary sources of power are pattern recognition (intuition) and mental simulation, with storytelling relying on mental simulation processes and metaphors relying on pattern recognition. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p346]
- Expertise and improvisation derive from both pattern recognition and mental simulation. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p346]
- The ability to read minds depends on how well one can mentally simulate the thinking of another person. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p346]
- Sources of power operate generatively, channeling decision making rather than exhaustively filtering permutations. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p344]
- These sources trade accuracy for speed and allow errors, and they can be used in context with interactive causes. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p344]

## Inferences

- Inference: Klein's research on natural decision-making supports Boyd's emphasis on rapid pattern recognition and mental models rather than analytical deliberation.
- Inference: The book argues that training should focus on expanding experience bases and pattern recognition rather than formal analytical methods for time-pressured decision making.
- Inference: difficult tasks, people blend different sources of power and integrate them to fit situational needs.
