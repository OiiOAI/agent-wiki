# Structural Equation Models

## Summary

Structural equation models provide the mathematical foundation for formalizing causal relationships and counterfactual reasoning. The definition of counterfactuals as derivatives of structural equations was introduced by Balke and Pearl (1994a, 1994b), enabling the estimation of probabilities of causation.

## Key facts

- Haavelmo (1943) established the statistical implications of systems of simultaneous equations. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3837-L3839]
- The definition of counterfactuals as derivatives of structural equations was introduced by Balke and Pearl (1994a, 1994b). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3799]

## Inferences

- Inference: Structural equation models originated in econometrics (Haavelmo, 1943) and later became central to the causal inference framework developed by Pearl.
