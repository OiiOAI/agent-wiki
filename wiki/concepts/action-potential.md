# Action Potential

## Summary

The action potential is the fundamental, universal electrical language of the nervous system—a brief all-or-none electrical change that propagates along axons. Information is conveyed by frequency modulation (spike trains) rather than amplitude.

## Key facts

- Lord Adrian discovered in 1926 that neuronal firing consists of all-or-none electrical changes known as action potentials, where information is conveyed by frequency modulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
- Neuronal firing (depolarization) consists of an influx of positively charged sodium ions, followed by repolarization via potassium outflow. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p50]
- John Eccles, Alan Hodgkin, and Andrew Huxley received the 1963 Nobel Prize for discoveries concerning ionic mechanisms of excitation and inhibition in nerve cell membranes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p50]
- At synapses, frequency-coded action potential signals are converted to graded amplitude-modulated signals via neurotransmitter release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
