# Action Potential

## Summary

The action potential is the fundamental, universal electrical language of the nervous system—a brief all-or-none electrical change that propagates along axons. Information is conveyed by frequency modulation (spike trains) rather than amplitude.

## Key facts

- Lord Adrian discovered in 1926 that neuronal firing consists of all-or-none electrical changes known as action potentials, where information is conveyed by frequency modulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
- Neuronal firing (depolarization) consists of an influx of positively charged sodium ions, followed by repolarization via potassium outflow. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p50]
- John Eccles, Alan Hodgkin, and Andrew Huxley received the 1963 Nobel Prize for discoveries concerning ionic mechanisms of excitation and inhibition in nerve cell membranes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p50]
- At synapses, frequency-coded action potential signals are converted to graded amplitude-modulated signals via neurotransmitter release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
- The action potential has four key properties: initiation threshold, all-or-none amplitude, conduction without decrement, and refractory period. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p259]
- Depolarization opens voltage-gated Na+ channels, causing Na+ influx that drives Vm toward ENa (+55 mV); K+ channels open more slowly, causing repolarization toward EK (−75 mV). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p219]
- The absolute refractory period corresponds to the time when Na+ channels remain inactivated and cannot be opened by depolarization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]
- The relative refractory period follows, when partial recovery from Na+ channel inactivation and residual K+ conductance increase the threshold for firing another action potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]
- The action potential is all-or-none, generated when membrane depolarization reaches threshold at approximately -55 mV, resulting from a chain reaction of voltage-dependent channel opening. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p91]
- Action potentials are approximately 100 mV in amplitude and last about one-thousandth of a second, traveling down the axon in a self-regenerating fashion. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p91]
- When an action potential reaches axon terminals, it causes neurotransmitter release via voltage-dependent mechanisms, initiating synaptic transmission. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p92]

## Inferences

- Inference: The threshold for action potential generation occurs when the net ionic current changes from outward to inward, at approximately −50 mV in many neurons.
