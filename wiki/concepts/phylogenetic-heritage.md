# Phylogenetic Heritage

## Summary

Phylogenetic heritage refers to inherited dispositions from ancestral prehistory that shape the individual's psychosexual development. Freud discusses scenes of observing parental intercourse, seduction in childhood, and threats of castration as possibly being phylogenetic inheritances rather than personal experiences. He agrees with Jung that such heritage exists but considers it a methodological error to seize on phylogenetic explanation before ontogenetic possibilities have been exhausted. Phylogenetic motives themselves stand in need of elucidation, often afforded by factors in the individual's childhood.

## Key facts

- Scenes of observing parental intercourse, of being seduced in childhood, and of being threatened with castration are unquestionably an inherited endowment, a phylogenetic heritage, but they may just as easily be acquired by personal experience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5350]
- A child catches hold of this phylogenetic experience where his own experience fails him; he fills in the gaps in individual truth with prehistoric truth. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5350]
- It is a methodological error to seize on a phylogenetic explanation before the ontogenetic possibilities have been exhausted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5350]
- What was originally produced by certain circumstances in prehistoric times and was then transmitted in the shape of a predisposition to its reacquirement should, since the same circumstances persist, emerge once more as a concrete event in the experience of the individual. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5350]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud distinguishes his position from Jung's by maintaining that both phylogenetic and ontogenetic factors should be acknowledged, and that phylogenetic motives themselves often require ontogenetic factors for their elucidation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact relationship between phylogenetic inheritance and actual experience in shaping neuroses remains theoretically contested.
