# Gage Matrix

## Summary

The 'Gage matrix' refers to the consistent pattern of neuropsychological deficits produced by damage to the ventromedial prefrontal cortices: impaired reasoning and decision-making in personal and social contexts, combined with reduced emotional reactivity and feeling, occurring against a background of otherwise intact cognition including memory, language, attention, and working memory for non-emotional content.

## Key facts

- The Gage matrix includes: decision-making defect, flat emotion and feeling, normal social knowledge base, and preserved higher-order neuropsychological functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p71]
- Similar profiles occur in Patient A (Brickner, 1932), Hebb-Penfield patient (1940), Ackerly-Benton patient (1948), and in modern patients with bilateral ventromedial prefrontal damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p75]
- Patients with this profile can perform normally on tests of social knowledge but fail in real-life situations requiring choices, demonstrating that the defect is not in knowledge but in the translation of knowledge into appropriate action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p68]
- The shared essence of this condition across different etiologies (tumor, trauma, developmental damage) is damage to the ventromedial sector of the prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p76]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistency of the Gage matrix across different causes and historical periods suggests that the ventromedial prefrontal cortex serves a specific computational function in normal human cognition that cannot be compensated by other brain regions.
