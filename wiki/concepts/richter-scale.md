# Richter Scale

## Summary

A logarithmic measure of earthquake magnitude, named after American seismologist Charles Richter. Each whole number represents ten times the magnitude and about 32 times the energy of the previous number.

## Key facts

- The Richter scale is a logarithmic scale beginning at 0, with each whole number representing ten times the magnitude of the previous one. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L1-L460]
- Each whole number increase represents about 32 times the amount of energy released. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L1-L465]
- The Richter scale uses data recorded on seismographs, differing from the Mercalli scale which measures observed effects. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L1-L470]
- The 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake measured 9.3 on the Richter scale, one of the largest ever recorded. [raw/books/strategy/How_to_Measure_Anything.epub#L1-L475]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Richter scale has been largely superseded by the Moment Magnitude Scale due to saturation problems at high values.
