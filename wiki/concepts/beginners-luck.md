# Beginner's Luck

## Summary

Beginner's luck is a branch of association bias where people create false links with past success. People new to a game who lose early fold, but those who strike lucky tend to keep going and increase stakes until probabilities 'normalize'. The housing bubble allowed even incompetent amateur brokers to flourish.

## Key facts

- Casino players call it beginner's luck: people who are new to a game and lose in the first few rounds are usually clever enough to fold, but whoever strikes lucky tends to keep going and increase the stakes. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p115]
- During the U.S. housing boom, dentists, lawyers, teachers and taxi drivers gave up their jobs to flip houses, and their initial fat profits had nothing to do with any specific skills. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p116]
- To distinguish beginner's luck from real talent: if you are much better than others over a long period of time, talent likely plays a part. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p116]
