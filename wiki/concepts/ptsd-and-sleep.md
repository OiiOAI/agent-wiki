# Post-traumatic stress disorder and sleep

## Summary

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) is described as a disorder of sleep-dependent memory evolution. Traumatic memories are normally processed during sleep, losing intrusive detail, weakening emotional intensity, and gaining contextual understanding. When this processing fails, memories become locked in stasis as PTSD. A hallmark of PTSD is nightmares that are near-veridical replays of traumatic events—something that normally never occurs in healthy dreaming.

## Key facts

- PTSD can be understood as a disorder of sleep-dependent memory evolution, where traumatic memories fail to be processed normally during sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p81]
- Normal memory processing during sleep involves loss of detail, weakening of emotional response, and development of contextual understanding of traumatic events. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p81]
- A hallmark of PTSD is nightmares that are near-veridical replays of traumatic events, which normally never occur in healthy dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p81]

## Inferences

- Inference: The disruption of normal dreaming in PTSD demonstrates that healthy dreams process and transform memories rather than replaying them verbatim.
