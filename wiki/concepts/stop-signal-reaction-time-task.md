# Stop-Signal Reaction Time Task

## Summary

The stop-signal reaction time (SSRT) task is a paradigm for measuring response inhibition and volitional control. In this task, humans or animals are trained to respond quickly to imperative signals, but on a proportion of trials a stop signal instructs cancellation of the response. The task requires cancellation of an already-initiated response and is distinguished from measures of premotor impulsivity. The SSRT task reflects a race between go and stop processes, with effective translation achieved in nonhuman primates, rats, and mice.

## Key facts

- In the SSRT task, subjects must cancel a response when a stop signal is presented after the imperative signal, requiring inhibition after response initiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p87]
- The SSRT task is distinguished from waiting impulsivity as measured by the 5CSRTT, with distinct underlying neural networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p87]
- Neural networks mediating stop-signal inhibition include the right inferior frontal gyrus, the supplementary motor area, and the basal ganglia, especially the subthalamic nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p93]
- Lesions of the rat caudate-putamen significantly retard SSRT, while damage to the nucleus accumbens core has no effect on SSRT, demonstrating fractionation of impulsivity constructs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p93]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociable effects of caudate-putamen versus nucleus accumbens lesions on SSRT versus 5CSRTT demonstrate that different forms of impulsivity are mediated by distinct neural circuits.
