# Implementation Intentions

## Summary

Implementation intentions are explicit if-then plans that specify how to overcome anticipated obstacles when pursuing goals. Developed by Peter M. Gollwitzer, they involve forming statements like 'If situation x arises, then I will perform response y.' Research shows implementation intentions have medium-to-large effects on behavior, helping people get started on tasks, seize opportunities, overcome initial reluctance, and protect against distractions.

## Key facts

- In one study, 71% of participants who specified when, where, and how they would write a report sent it back, compared to only 32% who had not formed a plan. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p109]
- Among drug addicts, 8 of 10 who formed implementation intentions wrote their resumes by 5 p.m., while none of 10 who only expressed intention did so. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p110]
- Meta-analysis of nearly 100 studies found implementation intentions had medium-to-large effects on actual behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p109]
- Implementation intentions work by preactivating in the mind the situation of an obstacle or opportunity arising, enabling automatic rather than conscious responses. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p110]
- When combined with mental contrasting (forming MCII or WOOP), participants showed substantially more progress in breaking snacking habits than either technique alone. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p113]

## Inferences

- Inference: mplementation intentions operate nonconsciously by creating automatic links between situational cues and goal-directed behaviors.
- Inference: They are most effective when combined with mental contrasting, as mental contrasting identifies the relevant obstacles and implementation intentions specify how to respond.
