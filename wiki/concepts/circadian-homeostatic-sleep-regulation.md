# Circadian and Homeostatic Sleep Regulation

## Summary

Sleep is regulated by two complementary processes: the homeostatic drive for sleep, which increases with time awake, and circadian rhythms controlled by the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus. The homeostatic pressure for sleep depends on humoral factors including adenosine accumulation. Circadian rhythms are endogenous ~24-hour cycles generated by a molecular clock in SCN neurons. These two processes interact to produce consolidated wakefulness during the day and sleep at night.

## Key facts

- Sleep is regulated by two processes: homeostatic pressure that increases with wakefulness, and circadian rhythms that promote sleep during the biological night. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1103]
- Adenosine accumulates during wakefulness and contributes to homeostatic sleep pressure; caffeine promotes wakefulness by blocking adenosine receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1103]
- The suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) is the master circadian clock, containing neurons with ~24-hour molecular rhythms entrained by light via the retinohypothalamic tract. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1103]
- Circadian control of sleep depends on SCN projections to hypothalamic sleep-regulating regions including the VLPO. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1103]
