# Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)]

## Summary

Lp(a) is a dangerous lipoprotein formed when an LDL particle fuses with apolipoprotein(a). The apo(a) wraps around the LDL with kringle structures that scoop up oxidized lipids. Lp(a) is the most prevalent hereditary risk factor for heart disease and can cause premature heart attacks even when conventional cholesterol panels appear normal. An estimated 20-30 percent of the US population has elevated levels. It also damages the aortic valve.

## Key facts

- Lp(a) is formed when a garden-variety LDL particle is fused with another protein called apolipoprotein(a). [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p135]
- The apo(a) protein has multiple kringle structures that scoop up oxidized lipid molecules and carry them along. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p135]
- Lp(a) is the most prevalent hereditary risk factor for heart disease. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p136]
- An estimated 20-30 percent of the US population has Lp(a) levels high enough to increase cardiovascular risk. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p136]
- Lp(a) is particularly destructive to the aortic valve, promoting the formation of bony particles that lead to stenosis. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p136]
- Elevated Lp(a) often announces itself via a sudden, seemingly premature heart attack. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p135]
