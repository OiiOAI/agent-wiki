# Melancholia

## Summary

Melancholia is a nutritional neurosis characterized by mourning over loss of libido. Freud distinguished it from anxiety neurosis by theorizing that melancholia results from accumulation of psychical sexual tension (libido), while anxiety neurosis results from accumulation of physical sexual tension that cannot be psychically bound.

## Key facts

- Melancholia consists in mourning over loss of libido - a loss in the life of the drives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p228]
- Anorexia nervosa of young girls appears to be melancholia where sexuality is undeveloped. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p228]
- There are three forms of melancholia: genuine severe melancholia recurring periodically, neurasthenic melancholia from excessive masturbation, and anxious melancholia combining anxiety neurosis and melancholia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p229]
- Anaesthesia plays a role in melancholia because the psychical sexual group is strengthened by voluptuous feeling and weakened by its omission. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p231]
- Potent individuals easily acquire anxiety neuroses, while impotent ones incline to melancholia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p232]
- In melancholia, the self-reproaches with which patients torment themselves actually apply to another person, the sexual object which has been lost or become valueless through its own fault. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5181]
- In melancholia, by a process of narcissistic identification, the lost object is set up in the ego itself and is, as it were, projected onto the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5181]
- A melancholic's propensity to suicide is made more intelligible by the understanding that the patient's embitterment strikes with a single blow at his own ego and at the loved and hated object. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5181]
- In melancholia and mania, a special method of dealing with a conflict is involved whose underlying determinants agree precisely with those of the other neuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5182]

## Inferences

- Inference: melancholia, the psychical sphere draws in upon itself, producing suction on adjoining amounts of excitation, causing associations to lose their excitation which manifests as pain.
- Inference: While in neurasthenia excitation runs out through a 'hole' in the somatic sexual sphere, in melancholia the hole is in the psychical sphere.
