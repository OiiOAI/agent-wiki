# Eye Tracking

## Summary

Eye tracking is a methodology for recording eye movements to study visual perception. Modern eye trackers simply film the pupil with a digital camera. In Alfred Yarbus's time, experiments were more tedious, with eye movements recorded using the reflection of a beam of light on a small mirror mounted on a contact-lens-like device implanted in subjects' eyeballs. Eye tracking has been applied to study how people view art, revealing differences between viewing original works in galleries versus computer reproductions.

- Modern eye trackers simply film the pupil with a digital camera. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p171]
- In Yarbus's time, eye movements were recorded using the reflection of a beam of light on a small mirror mounted on something resembling a contact lens that was implanted in the subject's eyeballs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p171]
- Eye tracking studies at the Tate Gallery showed that fixation patterns were radically different when people looked at reproductions stored in a computer compared to original works of art. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p172]
- Studies of faces using eye trackers have shown that people tend to concentrate on the eyes when looking at a face. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p172]
