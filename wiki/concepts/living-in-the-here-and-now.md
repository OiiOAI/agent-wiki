# Living in the Here and Now

## Summary

The philosophical stance advocated by Adlerian psychology that one should shine a 'bright spotlight' on the present moment rather than dwelling on the past or future. Life is conceived as a series of discrete moments (dots) rather than a continuous story, and living earnestly in each present moment constitutes a complete, happy life.

- Life should be lived as a series of moments, and neither the past nor the future exists in the here and now. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p275]
- The philosopher uses a spotlight metaphor: a bright spotlight on here and now makes the past and future invisible, while dim light on one's entire life allows one to 'see' both. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p275]
- Living earnestly in the here and now means doing what one can do now, earnestly and conscientiously, without needing a grand objective. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p276]
- One should 'dance the dance'—do little by little every day—rather than postponing life for a distant future objective. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p277]
- When one adopts the energeial viewpoint, life is always complete—a life ending at twenty and one ending at ninety are both complete lives of happiness. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p278]
