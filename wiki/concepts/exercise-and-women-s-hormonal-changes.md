# Exercise and Women's Hormonal Changes

## Summary

Exercise is particularly important for women because it balances the effects of hormonal fluctuations throughout life. For PMS, exercise reduces 150 documented symptoms including depression, irritability, concentration problems, and physical pain. During pregnancy, ACOG recommends 30 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise daily. Exercise during pregnancy benefits babies neurologically and cognitively. For postpartum depression, studies show walking with strollers significantly reduces depression scores. For menopause, exercise helps with mood instability, guards against heart disease, breast cancer, and cognitive decline.

## Key facts

- Approximately 75% of women experience premenstrual distress, physical or emotional, and 14% miss school or work due to PMS. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p157]
- A 1992 Duke University study found aerobic runners improved significantly more than strength trainers on 18 of 23 mental measures of PMS, especially depression, irritability, and concentration. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p161]
- In 2002 the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists began recommending at least 30 minutes a day of moderate intensity aerobic exercise for pregnant women. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p161]
- Exercise reduces by half the risk of developing abnormal glucose levels that can lead to gestational diabetes; briskly walking five hours a week reduces this risk by 75%. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p163]
- A study of postpartum women who walked with strollers three times a week reduced their Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale scores from 17.4 to 4.6 over 12 weeks. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p169]
- Postmenopausal women over 65 with higher physical activity levels were 50% less likely to develop any form of dementia. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: Exercise may be even more effective for new mothers experiencing depression than for the general population because it normalizes neurotransmitter levels affected by hormonal withdrawal after childbirth.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Evidence for exercise effects on hot flashes and night sweats during menopause is inconclusive; some studies show correlation while others show no association.
