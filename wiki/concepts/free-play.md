# Free Play

## Summary

Free play is unstructured, child-directed play without adult supervision or intervention. Scientific research shows it is essential for developing creativity, social bonds, and a sense of aliveness. Dr. Isabel Behncke identified these as the three main areas where play impacts child development. The book argues that free play has been systematically eliminated from childhood through homework increases, organized activities, parental fear, and school pressures, causing widespread harm to attention development.

## Key facts

- Dr. Isabel Behncke identified three main areas where play has a major impact: creativity and imagination, social bonds, and aliveness—the core of becoming a functioning human being. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p264]
- During free play, children learn to create games, persuade others, read people, negotiate, cope with disappointment, and experience exhilaration through challenge. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p263-264]
- Lenore Skenazy argues that free play has been turned into supervised play, draining it of most of its value like processed food. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p264]
- Time children used to spend playing is now overwhelmingly spent on homework (which exploded by 145 percent between 1981 and 1997), screens, and shopping with parents. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p265]
- A 2004 study found that U.S. kids spent 7.5 hours more each week on academics than they had twenty years before. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p265]
- Professor Peter Gray's research found that rats allowed to play for one hour a day grew up braver, more exploratory, better able to cope with new situations, and significantly smarter than play-deprived rats. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p280]
- Jonathan Haidt argues that anxiety is skyrocketing partly because children are deprived of play that teaches them to cope with the unexpected. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p265]

## Inferences

- Inference: The systematic elimination of free play may be contributing to rising rates of anxiety and attention disorders in children by depriving them of opportunities to develop coping skills.
