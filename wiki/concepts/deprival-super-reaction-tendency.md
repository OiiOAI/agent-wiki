# Deprival-Super Reaction Tendency

## Summary

Man reacts much more strongly to loss than to equivalent gain. This includes both losing possessed rewards and losing almost-possessed rewards. The tendency causes misframing of problems, comparing what is near rather than what matters, and irrational gambling behavior including the passion to 'get even' after losses.

## Key facts

- Munger recounts his own costly mistake: declining to buy additional underpriced Belridge Oil shares because he would have needed to borrow money, costing him approximately $5.4 million when Shell bought the company. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p561]
- Many Berkshire shareholders never sell shares after immense gains because they can't stand having their holdings be smaller, causing them to miss rebalancing opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p562]
- Deprival-Super Reaction Tendency causes much labor resistance to wage reductions, even when survival of the business requires them. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p559]
- Slot machine creators exploit this weakness by producing meaningless near-miss results that greatly increase play by fools who think they have nearly won. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p560]
- Buffett's antidote to open-outcry auctions is simply not to attend them. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p560]

## Inferences

- Inference: Deprival-Super Reaction Tendency combined with Inconsistency-Avoidance explains why people often sink resources into failing ventures—each additional commitment seems small compared to the total already invested, triggering a 'can't stop now' mentality.
