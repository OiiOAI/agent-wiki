# Mind-Body Control

## Summary

Mind-body control refers to S.'s demonstrated ability to voluntarily regulate autonomic physiological processes including heart rate, body temperature, pain perception, and visual adaptation through the power of vivid imagination and mental visualization.

## Key facts

- S. demonstrated the ability to accelerate his pulse from 70-72 to 100 beats per minute by imagining himself running after a departing train. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p149]
- S. demonstrated the ability to slow his pulse to 64-66 beats per minute by visualizing himself lying in bed falling asleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p149]
- S. was able to raise the temperature of his right hand two degrees by visualizing himself putting his hand on a hot stove. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p151]
- S. was able to lower the temperature of his left hand one and a half degrees by visualizing himself holding a piece of ice. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p151]
- S. demonstrated that his electroencephalogram showed distinct depression of alpha waves when he imagined a blazing 500-watt light flashing in his eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p152]
- S. used visualization to control pain during dental procedures, imagining pain as an orange-red thread that he cut and reduced to a tiny point. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p151]

## Inferences

- Inference: S.'s ability to alter his EEG readings through imagination suggests that the boundary between imagined and real experiences was genuinely blurred at the neurological level for him.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: S.'s pain control methods were never verified under controlled laboratory conditions, though his physiological control abilities were observed by colleagues.
