# Tilt

## Summary

Tilt is a poker term describing a state of emotional unhingement in decision-making caused by recent bad outcomes. When the emotional center of the brain (the amygdala) activates, it shuts down the prefrontal cortex—the brain's cognitive control center—resulting in reactive, irrational decisions likely to produce more negative outcomes. The term originated from pinball machines that would disable themselves when violently shaken, flashing 'tilt' to indicate tampering.

## Key facts

- Tilt occurs when emotional reactions to bad outcomes compromise subsequent decision-making, creating a feedback loop of poor decisions and worse outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1643-L1646]
- The neurological mechanism of tilt involves the amygdala shutting down the prefrontal cortex, analogous to a shaken pinball machine disabling its flippers. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1651-L1654]
- Tilt manifests through verbal cues (incredulous exclamations, expressions of exasperation) and physiological signs (flushed cheeks, racing heart, rapid breathing). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1655-L1658]
- Tilt is not limited to poker; any outcome can trigger emotional reactions that compromise decisions in relationships, work, or other life contexts. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1662-L1664]

## Inferences

- Inference: The antidote to tilt involves recognizing warning signs early, precommitting to actions like walking away, and using time-travel strategies (10-10-10 questions, imagining past experiences) to regain perspective before making decisions.
