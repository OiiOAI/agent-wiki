# Synaptic Vesicle Cycle

## Summary

The synaptic vesicle cycle is the process by which synaptic vesicles store neurotransmitter, transport to the active zone, fuse with the plasma membrane to release contents (exocytosis), and are retrieved and recycled for reuse. The cycle includes vesicle docking, priming to a releasable state, calcium-triggered fusion, and clathrin-mediated endocytosis for recycling.

## Key facts

- Synaptic vesicles are typically clear, small, and ovoid with a diameter of approximately 40 nm, containing neurotransmitter concentrated within them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p379]
- Active zones contain clusters of synaptic vesicles above electron-dense material attached to the internal face of the presynaptic membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p379]
- Docked vesicles are those physically bound to the active zone and are thought to be immediately available for release (readily releasable pool). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p379]
- Active zones are generally found in precise apposition to postsynaptic membrane patches containing neurotransmitter receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p379]
- Synaptic vesicles are filled with neurotransmitters by active transport using the proton-motive force generated by the V-ATPase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]
- Vesicles dock at the active zone and undergo ATP-dependent priming that makes them competent for Ca2+-triggered fusion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]
- Following exocytosis, vesicle membrane is retrieved through endocytosis and recycled either directly or via endosomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]
- Three mechanisms for vesicle retrieval exist with distinct kinetics: reversible fusion pore (tens to hundreds of milliseconds), clathrin-mediated endocytosis (up to a minute), and bulk retrieval. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conservation of vesicle cycle machinery from worms to humans indicates this is fundamental to chemical synaptic transmission across evolution.
