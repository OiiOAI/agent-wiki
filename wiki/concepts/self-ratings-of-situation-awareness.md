# Self-Ratings of Situation Awareness

## Summary

Self-rating techniques involve operators subjectively assessing their own SA and reporting it on a rating scale. While self-ratings provide insight into cognitive processes and operator confidence, they have significant limitations: operators cannot be aware of their own lack of SA, ratings may be biased by performance outcomes regardless of actual SA quality, and human judgment errors (anchoring, availability, recency) affect accuracy. A key finding is that pilots who rated a display as providing good SA actually had the highest rate of inverted recoveries, demonstrating that subjective ratings do not always reflect reality.

## Key facts

- A main criticism of self-rating techniques is that operators cannot be aware of their own lack of SA, though multidimensional scales break SA into components that may be available for self-rating. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p129]
- Self-rating measures provide insight into the underlying cognitive process involved in the reported subjective SA experience. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p130]
- Fracker and Vidulich (1991) found that the display producing the best subjective SA ratings also resulted in the greatest percentage of inverted recoveries, demonstrating that operators believed they were upright when actually inverted. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p130]
- Venturino et al. (1989) found high correlation between post-trial subjective SA and performance, with operators rating SA as good if the trial had a positive outcome regardless of what influenced performance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p130]
- Self-assessments of SA assume individuals can judge and accurately report feelings, thoughts, and facts from experience, but errors in human judgment and recall hinder accuracy. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: The inverted recovery finding strongly suggests that self-ratings measure perceived rather than actual SA, supporting the need for objective validation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The author notes that finding out what is not known requires knowledge about the questions that need to be asked, acknowledging challenges in scale development.
