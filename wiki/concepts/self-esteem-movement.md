# The Self-Esteem Movement

## Summary

The self-esteem movement was a well-intentioned psychological approach that promoted praising children unconditionally to boost achievement. Led by psychotherapists like Nathaniel Branden, it claimed that low self-esteem caused virtually every social problem. However, a comprehensive review by the Association for Psychological Science found that high self-esteem does not lead to better performance at school or work, does not prevent substance abuse, and does not produce better citizens. The movement appears to have contributed to rising narcissism among young Americans while academic performance declined. The review found only two clearly demonstrated benefits: increased initiative and improved subjective well-being.

## Key facts

- The Association for Psychological Science review found that high self-esteem does not make people more effective, lead to better grades, or prevent drug use. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p176]
- The review found only two clearly demonstrated benefits of high self-esteem: increased initiative and a general sense of well-being. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p177]
- Narcissism has increased sharply in recent decades among young Americans, evident in rising expectations of grades without studying and rapid promotion. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p178]
- High self-esteem benefits accrue to the self while costs are borne by others through side effects like arrogance and conceit. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p177]
