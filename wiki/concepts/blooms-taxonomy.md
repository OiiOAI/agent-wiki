# Bloom's Taxonomy

## Summary

Bloom's Taxonomy is a framework developed by Benjamin Bloom for categorizing educational goals into six levels: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, and Create. A revised version updated the terminology and suggested that several competencies may be acquired simultaneously rather than sequentially.

## Key facts

- Benjamin Bloom chaired a committee that developed the taxonomy in 1956. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p173]
- The six levels are acquired from bottom to top: Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, and Create. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p173]
- Some argue that the top three layers describe competencies that should be acquired simultaneously, not sequentially. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p174]
- The taxonomy helps instructors allocate time and plan examinations. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p174]
