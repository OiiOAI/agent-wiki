# Parochial altruism

## Summary

Parochial altruism is the combination of in-group favoritism with out-group hostility that emerges under certain conditions. Research shows that oxytocin promotes both group bonding and parochialism—increased generosity toward in-group members and sometimes increased hostility toward out-groups.

## Key facts

- Oxytocin increases trust and cooperation within groups but can also increase ethnocentrism, as demonstrated by De Dreu et al. studies showing oxytocin promotes 'parochial altruism in intergroup conflict.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p406]
- Parochial altruism may have evolved because groups with members willing to sacrifice for each other outcompeted groups of pure self-interested individuals or those with members who defected. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p406]
- Research by De Dreu et al. (2011) found that oxytocin promotes human ethnocentrism, raising the question of whether the same hormone underlies both cooperation and conflict. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p406]

## Inferences

- Inference: Parochial altruism helps explain why humans can sustain cooperation in groups but also engage in intergroup conflict and warfare.
