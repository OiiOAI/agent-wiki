# Fragility Detection

## Summary

Fragility can be detected by examining convexity effects: if harm accelerates as a variable deviates further from baseline, the system is fragile (concave). Taleb's fragility detection heuristic examines whether additional deviations cause disproportionately larger harm — acceleration indicates concavity and fragility. This method can identify fragile institutions, projects, and policies without requiring accurate models.

## Key facts

- To detect fragility: if additional deviation in a variable causes accelerating harm (not just proportional harm), the system is fragile. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p369]
- Traffic is fragile: 90,000 cars for one hour then 110,000 for another produces much worse congestion than 100,000 cars for two hours. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p346]
- If government deficits worsen disproportionately with each additional deviation in unemployment (especially with debt), deficits are concave to economic conditions. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p369]
- A company with operational leverage: if a 10 percent sales increase produces less profit increase than the profit decrease from a 10 percent sales drop, the company is fragile. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p369]
- Small probabilities are fragile to errors — a small change in assumptions can make a probability rise from one per million to one per hundred. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p371]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Fannie Mae case study demonstrates this: Taleb saw that an upward move in an economic variable led to massive losses while a downward move led to small profits — losses accelerating with deviation indicated monstrous concavity and inevitable blowup.
