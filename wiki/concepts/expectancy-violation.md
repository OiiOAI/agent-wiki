# Expectancy Violation

## Summary

Expectancy violation occurs when events deviate from what an expert anticipates based on prior experience and mental models. This noticing of broken patterns or violated expectancies is a core aspect of expertise. In the Recognition-Primed Decision model, detecting an expectancy violation triggers diagnostic efforts, including efforts to take advantage of unexpected opportunities. The ability to form expectancies requires experience; without experience, one cannot recognize when expectancies are broken.

## Key facts

- Expectancy violation refers to when events deviate from what an expert anticipates based on prior experience. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experiencing expectancy violations is a critical cue that surprises experts but not novices, because novices lack the expectancies. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- In the RPD model, detecting an expectancy violation triggers diagnostic efforts, including exploiting unexpected opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experience is necessary for forming expectancies, and only through expectancies can someone notice when something did not happen. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]

## Inferences

- Inference: The RPD model treats expectancy violations not merely as problems but as potential opportunities, suggesting that experienced decision makers are alert to unexpected developments that could be advantageous.
