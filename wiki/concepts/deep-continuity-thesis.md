# Deep Continuity of Life and Mind

## Summary

The deep continuity thesis (also called the 'strong continuity thesis') holds that life and mind share a set of basic organizational properties, and that the organizational properties distinctive of mind are an enriched version of those fundamental to life. Mind is life-like and life is mind-like. According to this view, basic concepts needed to understand life—self-organization, collective dynamics, circular causal processes, autopoiesis—also turn out to be central to understanding mind. Jonas went further, arguing that the continuity includes phenomenological aspects: subjective and experiential aspects of mental life are continuous with certain basic features of life itself.

## Key facts

- The strong continuity thesis holds that basic concepts for understanding life (self-organization, autopoiesis, circular causal processes) are also central to understanding mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p130]
- Jonas argued that the continuity of life and mind is not simply organizational or functional but also phenomenological—human experience is continuous with basic features of life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p130]
- Any living system is both an autopoietic and a cognitive system, establishing a deep continuity between life and mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p157]
