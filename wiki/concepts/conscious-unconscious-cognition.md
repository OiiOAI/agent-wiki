# Conscious and Unconscious Cognitive Processes

## Summary

Conscious and unconscious cognitive processes have distinct neural correlates. Most cognitive processing occurs without awareness; only percepts (not cognitive processing itself) enter consciousness. Unconscious processes can be demonstrated in patients with brain damage, such as blindsight patients who can detect visual properties without conscious awareness, or neglect patients who respond to stimuli they do not consciously perceive. The control of action is largely unconscious, with awareness of intention coming after neural preparation for movement.

## Key facts

- Most cognitive processing occurs without entering consciousness; only percepts, not cognitive processing, are conscious phenomena. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1427]
- Blindsight patients with lesions in primary visual cortex claim to see nothing in affected visual fields but can detect movement or color better than chance when guessing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1427]
- Patients with unilateral neglect from right parietal damage ignore objects on the left side of space but can respond to this information unconsciously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1427]
- Libet's experiments showed that awareness of the urge to move occurs hundreds of milliseconds after the beginning of the readiness potential, demonstrating unconscious initiation of voluntary acts.
