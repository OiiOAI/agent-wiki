# Co-Regulation

## Summary

Co-regulation describes the mutual emotional influence people have on each other in relationships. Originally a term describing the caregiver-infant dyad, it has expanded to encompass all human interactions. A caregiver who reliably provides physical and verbal comfort teaches infants that emotional distress is manageable, making co-regulation the precursor to healthy self-regulation. In adult relationships, co-regulation can be intentional (speaking soothingly to someone upset) or automatic (moods affecting each other). The concept is central to understanding how parents shape children's emotional development.

## Key facts

- Co-regulation was originally a term describing the back-and-forth between caregiver and infant to support a baby's stress regulation, and is the precursor to healthy self-regulation. - A caregiver who reliably provides physical and verbal comfort teaches the infant that emotional distress is manageable; a caregiver who does not provide such support teaches that the infant may be at the mercy of their emotions.
