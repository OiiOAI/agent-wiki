# Glycine Receptor

## Summary

The glycine receptor is a pentameric ionotropic receptor-channel that mediates inhibitory synaptic transmission primarily in the spinal cord and brainstem. It is structurally similar to GABA_A receptors and requires binding of up to three glycine molecules to open, with positively charged residues in the pore conferring selectivity for chloride ions over cations.

## Key facts

- Glycine receptors are composed of three α- and two β-subunits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p335]
- Glycine receptors require the binding of up to three molecules of ligand to open. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p335]
- Glycine receptor-channels contain neutral or positively charged basic residues at positions homologous to those in GABA receptors, contributing to their selectivity for anions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p336]
- Missense mutations in the α-subunit of the glycine receptor underlie familial startle disease (hyperekplexia), characterized by abnormally high muscle tone and exaggerated responses to noise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p336]
- The poison strychnine, a plant alkaloid compound, causes convulsions by blocking the glycine receptor and decreasing inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p336]
