# Concept neurons

## Summary

Concept neurons are neurons in the hippocampus and surrounding areas that respond to abstract concepts rather than specific sensory stimuli. They fire in response to different photographs of the same person, to written names, and even to spoken names, demonstrating that they encode the meaning extracted from stimuli rather than visual features.

## Key facts

- Concept neurons respond to different types of sensory stimuli—seeing a photograph of a person, or reading or hearing that person's name all give rise to the same concept and elicit similar responses in single hippocampal neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p134]
- The encoding of concepts by neurons in the hippocampus is relatively fast—it may take a couple of days, maybe a few hours, or perhaps just seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p134]
- Concepts encoded by these neurons tend to be familiar ones, since these are the exact concepts most likely to be kept in memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p138]
- Concept neurons encode associations between concepts, which constitute the basis of memory itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p136]

## Inferences

- Inference: Place cells in rodents and concept neurons in humans may have the same type of memory-related function, with the difference being due to the types of things different species tend to remember.
