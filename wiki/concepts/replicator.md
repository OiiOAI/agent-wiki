# Replicator

## Summary

A replicator is any entity that makes copies of itself. In Dawkins' framework, the gene is a replicator—a unit of heredity capable of surviving through many generations. The book traces life back to the first replicator molecules that arose in the primordial soup, which had the extraordinary property of being able to create copies of themselves. Replicators are the fundamental entities that natural selection acts upon.

## Key facts

- The first replicator arose by accident in the primordial soup, with the extraordinary property of being able to create copies of itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p41]
- Replicators that were inherently more stable would have become relatively numerous in the soup due to their longevity and longer time available for making copies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p43]
- Speed of replication or fecundity was another property of replicators that would have been positively selected. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p43]
- Accuracy of replication was a third characteristic that would have been positively selected among replicators. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p43]
- As copying errors were made and propagated, the primeval soup became filled with several varieties of replicating molecules descended from the same ancestor. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p43]
- Eventually replicators constructed containers or survival machines for themselves, which may have been how the first living cells appeared. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p45]
- The fundamental units of natural selection, the basic things that survive or fail to survive, that form lineages of identical copies with occasional random mutations, are called replicators. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p295]
- Replicators don't behave, don't perceive the world, don't catch prey or run away from predators; they make vehicles that do all those things. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p295]
- The immortal replicator is the only kind of entity that has to exist in order for life to arise, anywhere in the universe. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p307]
- George C. Williams defined a gene as any portion of chromosomal material that potentially lasts for enough generations to serve as a unit of natural selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p308]

## Inferences

- Inference: The replicator concept forms the foundation for understanding genes as immortal entities that survive in the gene pool, while the bodies they build are temporary vehicles.
