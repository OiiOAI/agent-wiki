# Oxytocin and Trust

## Summary

Oxytocin (OT) is a neuropeptide that facilitates social affiliation and has been shown to increase trust in humans. Intranasal administration of oxytocin leads to increased trust in the Trust Game and decreases amygdala activation for fearful stimuli, suggesting a mechanism by which OT could increase trust by reducing fear of betrayal.

## Key facts

- In a Trust Game, intranasal administration of oxytocin led to an increase in trust placed by Investors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p322]
- The effect of oxytocin on trust was not general for all types of decisions and was not observed for risk or games with random outcomes, but was specific for consequential social interactions with other humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p322]
- Compared with placebo, oxytocin decreased amygdala activation for fearful/threatening faces and scenes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p322]
- Oxytocin reduces stress and anxiety, suggesting a potential mechanism by which it could increase trust; namely, by reducing the fear of betrayal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p322]

## Inferences

- Inference: Oxytocin may selectively modulate neural circuits involved in processing social threat, facilitating trust behavior in interactive contexts.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural pathways through which oxytocin modulates trust behavior are not fully specified.
