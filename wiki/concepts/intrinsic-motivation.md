# Intrinsic Motivation

## Summary

Intrinsic motivation refers to engaging in an activity because it is inherently interesting, enjoyable, or meaningful to the individual, rather than for external rewards. Research by Ed Deci and Richard Ryan found that intrinsic motivation makes it easier to focus and sustain attention compared to extrinsic motivation, where actions are driven by external pressures or anticipated rewards. The book argues that modern childhood deprives children of opportunities to develop intrinsic motives, contributing to attention problems.

## Key facts

- Intrinsic motivation occurs when you do something because you love it, not for a reward farther down the line. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p266]
- Ed Deci and Richard Ryan discovered that it's easier to focus on something and stick with it if your motives are intrinsic. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p266]
- Most people learn focus by doing something that is either very important or very interesting to them, learning the habit of focus by being interested enough to notice what's going on. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p267]
- Children today live almost entirely according to what adults tell them to do, depriving them of chances to find what emotionally turns them on. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p267]
- When L.B. was being forced to act on extrinsic motivations, he couldn't focus and was bored; when given freedom to develop intrinsic motivation through play, his focus flourished. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and and How to Think.pdf#p271]

## Inferences

- Inference: The decline of free play and increase in structured activities may be systematically undermining children's ability to develop intrinsic motivation, which is essential for sustained attention.
