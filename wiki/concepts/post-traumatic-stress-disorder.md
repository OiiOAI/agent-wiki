# Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

## Summary

Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) affects an estimated 30 percent of those exposed to highly traumatic events. Research applying predictive processing to PTSD has found that severity correlates with unusually large increases in precision-weighting on prediction error signals in response to unexpectedly negative outcomes. This may help explain why some people develop PTSD while others in the same situation do not—different brains respond differently to prediction error signals following sensory surprise.

## Key facts

- It is estimated that up to 30 percent of those exposed to highly traumatic events such as rape, combat, or domestic violence will go on to develop some form of PTSD. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p69]
- Research with combat veterans found that PTSD severity was extremely well correlated with unusually large increases in precision-weighting on prediction error signals in response to unexpectedly negative outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p70]
- In the most severely affected individuals, the response to failing to predict a shock was to radically overweight the missed cue and thereby become hypersensitive to its future occurrence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p70]

## Inferences

- Inference: Different human brains respond differently to prediction error signals following sensory surprise, explaining why some individuals are more susceptible to PTSD and other debilitating conditions.
