# Resistance

## Summary

Resistance is the psychical force that opposes the return of repressed pathogenic ideas to consciousness during therapy. Freud observed that insistence on the part of the physician was not powerful enough to deal with resistance in serious cases of hysteria. Resistance manifests in various forms: patients may claim nothing has occurred to them, make excuses about distraction, or disavow emerging memories as unimportant. A pathogenic recollection is recognizable by the fact that the patient describes it as unimportant and nevertheless only utters it under resistance. The therapeutic task consists in overcoming resistance by inducing the patient to reproduce and verbalize the pathogenic impressions that caused the symptom.

## Inferences

- Inference: Resistance is not merely an obstacle to therapy but provides evidence of the same psychological forces that generated the hysterical symptom.
- Inference: The magnitude of resistance varies and affects the length of the associative chain needed to reach unconscious material—greater resistance indicates greater distortion.
- Inference: The discovery of resistance explained why hypnosis had failed: it suppressed resistance from the doctor's perception rather than addressing it.
- Inference: The concept that resistance comes from the ego, not the unconscious, explains why intellectual insight can help overcome it—unlike repressed material which requires different mechanisms
