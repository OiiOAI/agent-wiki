# Advance in Intellectuality

## Summary

Freud's argument that the Mosaic prohibition against making images of God represented a triumph of intellectuality over sensuality, creating a 'renunciation of drive' with profound psychological consequences. This dematerialization of God elevated abstract thought over sensory perception, fundamentally shaping Jewish intellectual character.

## Key facts

- The Mosaic prohibition against making an image of God meant that sensory perception was given second place to an abstract idea—a triumph of intellectuality over sensuality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7275]
- Freud connects this advance to the earlier development of the 'omnipotence of thoughts'—an overestimation of intellectual acts' power to alter the external world, which represents an important stage in hominization. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7276]
- This characteristic development of Jewish nature—inclination to intellectual interests—was introduced by the Mosaic prohibition against worshipping God in a visible form. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7278]

## Inferences

- Inference: The pre-eminence given to intellectual labours over two thousand years helped check brutality and violence, directing Jewish development toward the 'worthier alternative' of mind over muscle.
