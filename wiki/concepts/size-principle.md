# Size Principle

## Summary

The size principle describes how motor neurons are recruited in order of increasing size during voluntary muscle contraction. Smaller motor neurons with higher input resistance reach threshold first for a given synaptic current, while larger motor neurons require greater excitatory input. This results in weak motor units being activated before strong ones, providing smooth gradation of muscle force.

## Key facts

- The size principle states that motor neurons are recruited in order of increasing size, from smallest to largest, as muscle force increases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p744]
- Smaller motor neurons have higher input resistance due to fewer ion channels, resulting in greater membrane potential change for a given synaptic current. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p744]
- The order of motor unit recruitment is highly correlated with motor neuron cell body size, axon diameter, and the force the muscle fibers can exert. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p743]
- Orderly recruitment is determined by intrinsic properties of spinal motor neurons rather than by selective synaptic inputs from supraspinal regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p744]
- Orderly recruitment allows fine force control at low forces because early-recruited motor units have small innervation numbers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p739]
