# Seduction

## Summary

Seduction is defined as 'the process by which a man induces a woman to become as invested in him as he is in her.' Manson identifies two ways seduction can happen: (1) a man creates the perception that he is far less invested than he actually is (neediness disguised as non-neediness through performance), or (2) a man actually is less invested (genuine non-neediness). The book advocates for the second method as the only fulfilling long-term solution, arguing that performance-based approaches only mask the underlying problem of neediness and lead to dysfunctional relationships.

- Women have evolved a sexuality that is more psychological than physical, rooted in the need for security and connection. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p28]
- Because men value sex more than women at the beginning of a relationship and sexual opportunities are scarcer for men, women tend to be less invested and more confident early in interactions. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p29]
- There are two ways for seduction to happen: (1) creating a perception of lower investment through performance, or (2) actually being less invested. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p29]
- Performance-based dating advice technically 'works' but is not fulfilling — you're not solving the problem (neediness), you're just covering it up. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p29]
- The story pattern of dating failure is consistent: Guy meets girl, shows less investment, sex occurs, guy becomes more invested, relationship falls apart. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p33]

- Sex is a side effect of this process. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p270]
- Women are generally always less invested at the beginning of an interaction because they almost always have more sexual and romantic options than men do. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p270]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since women can detect neediness intuitively, performance-based approaches will eventually fail because the true level of investment becomes apparent over time.
