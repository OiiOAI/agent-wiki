# Integrated Information Theory

## Summary

Integrated Information Theory (IIT), proposed by Giulio Tononi, claims that consciousness is identical to integrated information (Φ, phi). IIT proposes that any system with high Φ—generating more information as a whole than its parts—has consciousness. The theory is axiom-based, starting from principles that conscious experiences are both informative and integrated. IIT implies panpsychism and faces challenges in measuring Φ due to requiring intrinsic rather than observer-relative information.

## Key facts

- IIT proposes that consciousness is identical to integrated information, measured by Φ (phi). [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p66]
- Φ measures how much a system generates as a whole over and above what its parts generate independently. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p66]
- High Φ requires both high information (the system's state rules out many alternatives) and high integration (the whole is more than the sum of parts). [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p67]
- A single photodiode has zero or near-zero Φ because its state carries very little information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p67]
- A camera sensor array has high information but zero Φ because individual photodiodes are causally independent. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p67]
- IIT explains the cerebellum's minimal role in consciousness through its anatomy—many semi-independent circuits that are Φ-unfriendly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p68]
- IIT requires measuring intrinsic information—what a system could do, not just what it does—which is extremely difficult for real brains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p70]

## Inferences

- Inference: T's panpsychism is restrained—consciousness is not everywhere but wherever Φ is non-zero.
- Inference: T is correct, even atoms may be a little bit conscious due to their cause-effect structure.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Φ can actually be measured for complex biological systems remains uncertain; the theory's primary claim may be untestable in practice.
