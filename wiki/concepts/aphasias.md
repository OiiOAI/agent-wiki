# Aphasias

## Summary

Aphasias are language disorders resulting from brain damage that affect the ability to produce or comprehend language. The classical aphasias—Broca's, Wernicke's, conduction, and global—can be understood within the dual-stream model of language processing, with deficits mapped to either dorsal or ventral stream damage.

## Key facts

- Broca's aphasia results from damage to Broca's area (left inferior frontal gyrus), surrounding frontal fields, underlying white matter, insula, and a small portion of the anterior superior temporal gyrus, causing nonfluent, effortful speech with relatively preserved comprehension for simple sentences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1383]
- Broca's aphasics have difficulty understanding sentences whose meaning depends on grammatical structure, such as 'The girl that the boy is chasing is tall' versus 'The apple that the girl ate was green.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1383]
- Wernicke's aphasia results from damage to the posterior section of the left auditory association cortex and causes fluent but often nonsensical speech with poor comprehension. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1384]
- Conduction aphasia results from damage to the left superior temporal gyrus and inferior parietal lobe, causing impaired repetition and phonemic paraphasias while speech production and comprehension are less compromised. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1385]
- Global aphasia results from widespread damage affecting inferior frontal and parietal cortices, auditory cortex, insula, and posterior superior temporal cortex, producing severe deficits in comprehension, production, and repetition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1386]
- Within the dual-stream model, Broca's aphasia and conduction aphasia are dorsal stream deficits, while Wernicke's aphasia and transcortical sensory aphasia are ventral stream deficits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1382]
