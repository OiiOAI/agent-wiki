# Dissociation (Neuropsychological)

## Summary

A neuropsychological phenomenon where one or more cognitive abilities are impaired while others remain intact, indicating that these abilities depend on separate neural systems. Gage's case exemplifies dissociation: impaired social conduct was dissociated from intact cognition and behavior including attention, perception, memory, language, and intelligence.

## Key facts

- Dissociation occurs when one or more performances within a general profile of operations are at odds with the rest, as when Gage's impaired character was dissociated from otherwise intact cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p31]
- Double dissociation occurs when damage at site A causes disturbance X but not Y, while damage at site B causes disturbance Y but not X, validating the independence of different cognitive systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p53]
- The Gage-Elliot dissociation profile—impaired decision-making with preserved intelligence, memory, and language—occurs consistently with ventromedial prefrontal damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p32]

## Inferences

- Inference: The existence of consistent dissociation patterns validates that cognitive functions are supported by distinct neural systems rather than a single undifferentiated brain
