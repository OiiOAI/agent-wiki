# Phantasies

## Summary

Phantasies (Phantasien) are psychical structures that Freud identified as playing a central role in hysteria. They are unconscious combinations of things experienced and heard, constructed in accordance with trends that make the memory from which symptoms have emerged inaccessible. Phantasies serve for refining memories, sublimating them, and providing self-exoneration. They are psychical façades constructed to bar the way to primal scene memories.

## Key facts

- Phantasies arise from an unconscious combination, in accordance with certain trends, of things experienced and heard. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p279]
- Phantasies serve the trend towards refining the memories, towards sublimating them, and at the same time serve for self-exoneration. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p275]
- Phantasies are psychical façades constructed in order to bar the way to primal scene memories. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p275]
- Phantasies are made up from things that are heard and made use of subsequently, combining things experienced and things heard, past events and things seen by oneself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p275]
- Phantasies are derived from things heard by children at an early age and only understood later, from the age of six to seven months onwards. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p271]
- In hysteria, memories become inaccessible through phantasies; in obsessional neurosis, perverse impulses; in paranoia, protective fictions penetrate into normal life distorted by compromise.
