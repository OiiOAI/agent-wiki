# Law of the Minimum

## Summary

Justus von Liebig's 'law of the minimum' states that at any given time, the input most important to a system is the one that is most limiting. Adding more of abundant factors does not help if another factor is scarce. Grain will not grow better with more nitrogen if phosphorus is lacking. This concept underlies the idea of layers of limits around every growing entity. Growth depletes some limits while enhancing others, and insight comes from recognizing which factor is limiting and that growth itself changes what becomes limiting.

## Key facts

- Liebig's law: it doesn't matter how much nitrogen is available if phosphorus is short; pouring on more phosphorus won't help if potassium is low. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p128]
- At any given time, the input that is most important to a system is the one that is most limiting. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p129]
- There are layers of limits around every growing plant, child, epidemic, product, company, city, economy, and population. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p130]
- Insight comes from recognizing that growth depletes or enhances limits and changes what is limiting. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p130]
- Any physical entity in a finite environment cannot grow forever—ultimately the choice is not to grow forever but to decide what limits to live within. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p130]
