# Spatial Neglect Syndrome

## Summary

Spatial neglect syndrome is a condition resulting from damage to the posterior parietal cortex, typically on the right side, characterized by impaired awareness of one side of space. Patients may ignore stimuli on the contralateral side of their body, fail to recognize their own limbs, and be unable to perceive or remember objects in the neglected portion of space.

## Key facts

- Lesions of the posterior parietal cortex produce personal neglect syndrome, where patients may not dress, undress, or wash the affected side of their body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p432]
- Some patients with right parietal lesions exhibit denial of their paralyzed limb, asking 'Who put this arm in bed with me?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p432]
- Patients with right posterior parietal damage may ignore the left half of drawings, copying only the petals of a flower on the right side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p432]
- Representational neglect occurs when patients cannot recall landmarks on their left when imagining a familiar scene from memory, but can recall them when their imagined vantage point changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p434]
