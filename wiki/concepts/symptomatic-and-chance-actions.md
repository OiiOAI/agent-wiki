# Symptomatic and Chance Actions

## Summary

Symptomatic and chance actions are unobtrusive behaviors performed 'without thinking there is anything in them,' 'quite accidentally,' or 'just to have something to do.' They express unconscious content the agent does not suspect and does not intend to communicate. Examples include fidgeting, playing with objects, mislaying items, and symbolic gestures like removing wedding rings.

## Key facts

- Symptomatic actions differ from bungled actions by scorning the support of a conscious intention and appearing on their own account, not suspected of having any aim. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2167]
- A young married woman injured her ring-finger while cutting nails on her wedding anniversary, interpreted as expressing her ambivalence about marriage. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2168]
- Freud relates how actress Eleonora Duse, in a drama of adultery, plays with her wedding ring—taking it off and putting it back—before the seducer approaches, ready for the other man. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2178]
- Ernest Jones's analysis of a doctor placing a straight stethoscope between himself and patients showed the act symbolized a barrier against sexual temptation, paralleling Sigurd's sword between himself and Brünhilde. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2172]
- Freud examined a young man with a stain on his trousers and correctly diagnosed the patient was suffering from troubles arising from masturbation, the stain being caused by albumen. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2174]
- Actions performed unintentionally inevitably become sources of misunderstandings in human relations, as the agent does not feel responsible while the second party correctly interprets the unconscious intention. ## Inferences

- Inference: Symptomatic and chance actions allow repressed impulses to find expression while maintaining deniability, serving as 'symptoms' of unconscious conflict.
- Inference: The richer supply of such actions found in neurotics compared to normal people suggests these phenomena exist on a continuum of unconscious expression.
