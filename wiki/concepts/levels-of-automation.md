# Levels of Automation

## Summary

Levels of automation represent a taxonomy describing how human and machine share responsibility for four task aspects: monitoring information, generating options, selecting actions, and implementing tasks. The taxonomy ranges from manual control (human performs all aspects) to full automation (computer operates with no human intervention). Research shows that people have less difficulty with automation that aids gathering and presenting information or carrying out tasks compared to automation that generates options or selects actions.

## Key facts

- Endsley and Kaber identified four task aspects that can be automated: monitoring information, generating options, selecting actions, and implementing tasks. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p191]
- Automation that aids with gathering and presenting relevant information and carrying out tasks causes fewer problems than automation that generates options or selects actions. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p206]
- Operators are most out-of-the-loop with levels of automation employing advanced queuing techniques where operators program sequences of tasks for future implementation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p205]
- When automation aids in or takes over task implementation, overall performance improves; when used to generate options, performance declines. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p205]
- Endsley and Kaber (1999) formulated taxonomic levels of automation (LOA) including low levels such as assistance in implementation and high levels such as supervisory control. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p328]
- Research suggests computer automation of decision-making functions may reduce an operator's awareness of the system and of environmental dynamics. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p328]
- High LOA preceding a malfunction of computer automation led to significantly worse SA and corrective actions in teleoperation experiments. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p328]
- Better SA and corrective actions were found when malfunctions occurred after lower levels of automation that involved the human in decision making. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p328]
- A high LOA yields inadequate mental models for both automatic and controlled cognitive processes during corrective actions when computer automation fails. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p329]

## Inferences

- Inference: The taxonomy can guide designers in selecting which aspects of a task to automate based on the cognitive load and error risk associated with each function.
