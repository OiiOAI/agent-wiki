# Breaking Bad Habits

## Summary

Breaking bad habits requires a five-question framework: (1) What are the triggers? (2) What are the payoffs and costs? (3) What is a good alternative behavior, and why? (4) What unhooking skills are needed? (5) What or who can help? Key principles include starting small, using kind self-talk, and recognizing that new behaviors take much longer than popular myths suggest (often many months or years, not 21 days).

## Key facts

- The brain works by addition, not subtraction; we cannot simply delete old neural pathways but can lay down new ones on top. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p269]
- The five questions for breaking bad habits are: triggers, payoffs and costs, alternative behavior, unhooking skills needed, and what or who can help. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p271]
- Popular claims that it takes 21 days to form a habit have no scientific validity. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p270]
