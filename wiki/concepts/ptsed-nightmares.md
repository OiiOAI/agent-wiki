# PTSD Nightmares

## Summary

PTSD nightmares are characterized by realistic replay of actual traumatic events rather than the metaphorical, associative processing typical of normal dreams. The authors argue these nightmares reflect a breakdown of NEXTUP functioning, specifically caused by failure to suppress noradrenaline during REM sleep, preventing the brain from exploring weak associations needed for adaptive memory processing.

## Key facts

- PTSD nightmares differ from normal dreams in their realistic replay of the actual traumatic event rather than metaphorical representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p208]
- Research by Magdalena Fosse found that less than 2 percent of dream reports met requirements for replay of episodic memories, suggesting realistic replay reflects failure of NEXTUP. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p209]
- Tom Mellman reported that while noradrenaline release dropped 75 percent during sleep in healthy participants, it actually increased 25 percent in those with PTSD. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p210]
- High noradrenaline levels prevent the development of fully functional REM sleep, blocking the normal suppression of episodic memory replay and limiting access to weaker associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p210]
- As many as 90 percent of trauma-exposed individuals with PTSD report nightmares, but only about half experience direct replays of the trauma memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p211]
- Prazosin, an alpha-1-adrenergic antagonist, reduces PTSD nightmare severity by decreasing noradrenaline activity and restoring the neurochemical environment required by NEXTUP. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p213]

## Inferences

- Inference: PTSD nightmares may result from a specific neurobiological failure—the inability to suppress noradrenaline during sleep—which prevents the normal memory processing that occurs through weak associative exploration.
