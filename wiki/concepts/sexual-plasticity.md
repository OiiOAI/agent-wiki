# Sexual Plasticity

## Summary

Sexual plasticity refers to the extraordinary capacity of human sexuality to change and adapt over time, including what types of partners we're attracted to, where we experience sexual pleasure in the body, and what sexual practices appeal to us. Unlike other instincts that are largely hardwired, human sexual desire demonstrates remarkable flexibility and can be shaped by experience, critical periods in development, and neuroplastic changes in the brain.

## Key facts

- Human beings exhibit an extraordinary degree of sexual plasticity compared with other creatures, varying in whom or what they are attracted to. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p104]
- The human libido is not a hardwired, invariable biological urge but can be curiously fickle, easily altered by psychology and the history of sexual encounters. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p104]
- Human beings are drawn to types and have strong preferences; having a 'type' causes us to defer satisfaction until we find what we are looking for. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p104]
- Fetishists desire inanimate objects; a male fetishist can be more excited by a high-heeled shoe with fur trim than by a real woman. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p105]
- The human sexual 'instinct' seems to have broken free of its core purpose, reproduction, and varies to a bewildering extent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p105]
- For millennia most marriages were arranged by parents for practical reasons, and romantic love only began to gain social approval in European aristocracies in the twelfth century. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p106]

## Inferences

- Inference: The variety of human sexual tastes beyond reproduction suggests sexuality has evolved to serve social bonding functions in addition to biological reproduction.
- Inference: The acceptance of romantic love as the basis for marriage is a relatively recent cultural development, indicating that human mating systems are highly malleable.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neurobiological mechanisms by which specific sexual preferences become fixed are not fully understood.
