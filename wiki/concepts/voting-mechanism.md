# Voting Mechanism

## Summary

The voting mechanism is how cortical columns in the Thousand Brains Theory achieve a singular perception despite each column learning different complementary models. Columns broadcast what they think they are sensing via long-range connections, and the most common guesses suppress less common ones until the network settles on one answer.

## Key facts

- Columns broadcast what they think they are observing via long-range connections that extend across brain regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p101]
- When a column is uncertain, its neurons send multiple possibilities simultaneously, and the most common guesses suppress the least common ones until consensus is reached. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p101]
- Voting neurons form a stable pattern representing the object and where it is relative to the observer, and this pattern does not change with movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p102]
- A column does not need to send its vote to every other column—voting works even if long-range axons connect to a small, randomly chosen subset of other columns. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p101]
- When connections between left and right neocortex are cut (to stop seizures), the two sides act as if they have different thoughts and reach independent conclusions, supporting the voting theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p103]

## Inferences

- Inference: The stability of perception during eye movements and finger movements is explained by the stable voting pattern that persists while other column activity changes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The detailed neural mechanisms of how votes are tallied and how competition between possibilities is resolved need further experimental verification.
