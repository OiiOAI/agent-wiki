# Three Ethics Framework

## Summary

Richard Shweder's cultural psychology framework identifying three distinct moral languages or 'ethics' that structure moral thinking across cultures: (1) Ethic of Autonomy—morality based on individuals with rights and preferences; (2) Ethic of Community—morality based on group membership, roles, duties, and hierarchies; (3) Ethic of Divinity—morality based on the body as a temple housing a divine soul, concerned with sanctity, purity, and degradation.

## Key facts

- The ethic of autonomy is based on the idea that people are autonomous individuals with wants, needs, and preferences; it produces moral concepts like rights, liberty, and justice. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p115]
- The ethic of community is based on the idea that people are members of larger entities (families, tribes, nations) that matter and must be protected; it produces concepts like duty, hierarchy, respect, and patriotism. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p115]
- The ethic of divinity is based on the idea that people are temporary vessels housing divine souls; it produces concepts like sanctity, sin, purity, pollution, elevation, and degradation. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p116]
- WEIRD cultures rely almost exclusively on the ethic of autonomy; other cultures draw on all three ethics. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p116]
- Haidt's India experience allowed him to 'feel' the ethics of community and divinity, not just understand them intellectually. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p118-122]

## Inferences

- Inference: The three ethics framework provides the conceptual foundation upon which Haidt built Moral Foundations Theory, with each ethic corresponding to multiple foundations
