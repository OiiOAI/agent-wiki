# Cognitive Miser

## Summary

A cognitive miser is a person who, when willpower is low, hoards mental energy by avoiding compromises and trade-offs. They become prone to taking the easiest path—either choosing the cheapest option or the best quality option without weighing both factors, rather than making difficult trade-offs.

## Key facts

- When willpower is low, people become cognitive misers, looking at only one dimension like price ('just give me the cheapest') or quality ('I want the very best'). [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p98]
- Decision fatigue leaves people vulnerable to marketers who time their sales strategically, as demonstrated at supermarket checkout lines with gossipy tabloids and chocolate bars placed where impulse control is weakest. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p99]
- As decision fatigue set in during car purchases, customers started settling for whatever the default option was, with the average difference totaling more than 1,500 euros per car depending on when choices were offered. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: Chocolate and gossip magazines are strategically placed at checkout counters because they target the moment when shoppers' willpower is most depleted from navigating thousands of product choices.
