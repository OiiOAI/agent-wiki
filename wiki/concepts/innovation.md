# Innovation

## Summary

Innovation in the book's framework is purposeful transformation driven by intuition—the ability to spot exceptions that drive original art, science, and technology. Special Operations developed three methods to accelerate intuition into innovation: (1) Turn an exception into a new rule, (2) Leverage conflict, and (3) Eat your enemy. Einstein doubled down on light's constant velocity to create relativity; Steve Jobs identified exceptional potential in specific gadgets to create the iPhone. The methods work by engaging storythinking, using the tension between a rule and an exception to invent new rules of action.

## Key facts

- Innovation entered English 500 years ago as a synonym for sin—God had made the world perfect, and to introduce the new was to ruin paradise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p97]
- Modern ideation (random association, 'kiss more frogs') produces pseudocreativity—prolific output that is the opposite of innovation, which is purposeful and transformative. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p98]
- Einstein took the exception of light (always flying at constant velocity regardless of origin) and turned it into a new rule: everything everywhere runs on local time, becoming the law of relativity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p100]
- Shakespeare's Hamlet instructs: 'Therefore as a stranger give it welcome'—when something is weird, embrace it and double down, turning the anomaly into a new rule. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p100]
- Steve Jobs' 'reality-distortion field' involved suspending old rules while pushing forward an exception until it became the new rule, as demonstrated when he doubled down on the ROKR's original potential to create the iPhone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p103]
- Leverage conflict means leaning into the tension between a rule and an exception, engaging brain powers of storythinking to invent new rules that join the old rule's why with the exception's what if. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p104]
- Army Special Operations innovated training by replacing fixed obstacle courses with map-based tests where recruits could reach a target however they chose—satisfying both the old rule's why (navigating challenges) and exceptional recruits' what ifs (finding alternate paths). [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p106]
