# Global Neuronal Workspace Theory

## Summary

The global neuronal workspace theory proposes that consciousness is the brain's capacity to make information globally accessible across specialized processors through a network of long-distance connections. The theory synthesizes earlier concepts including Bernard Baars's blackboard metaphor, Donald Broadbent's limited-capacity channel, and the supervisory executive system. Consciousness arises when information ignites a distributed network of prefrontal, parietal, and cingulate regions, allowing flexible routing to language, memory, attention, and motor systems.

## Key facts

- The global workspace broadcasts information flexibly to language processors, enabling the capacity to report conscious experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p209]
- The workspace eliminates the homunculus fallacy by replacing an intelligent observer with distributed unconscious processors receiving broadcast messages. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p210]
- Conscious access creates a bottleneck because only one integrated thought can ignite the workspace at a time, explaining attentional limits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p240]
- The workspace theory predicts five types of unconscious knowledge: preconscious, subliminal, disconnected, diluted, and latent (synaptic). [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p240]
- The theory holds that consciousness evolved to mitigate cortical modularity by enabling information sharing across specialized processors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p213]
- The theory spans pages 13-14 and 161-199, indicating it is the central theoretical contribution of the book. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p161]
- The global workspace allows information to be shared across multiple brain systems including language, attention, memory, and motor planning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p163-167]
- Long-distance projection cells, particularly pyramidal neurons in prefrontal cortex, form the anatomical substrate for global broadcasting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p169-174]
- The theory accounts for unconscious operations in the brain, with unconscious processing preceding and sometimes contradicting conscious decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p190-199]
- Computer simulations implementing the theory replicate key signatures of consciousness including global ignition and gamma-band synchronization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p180-190]
- The theory proposes that consciousness unique to humans may depend on expanded prefrontal cortex and language networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p250-253]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because workspace theory defines consciousness by information integration rather than specific neural substrate, it implies consciousness could theoretically exist in nonbiological systems
- Inference: The theory explains why the same stimulus may or may not become conscious depending on prior brain state (random fluctuations)
