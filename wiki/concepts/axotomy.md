# Axotomy

## Summary

Axotomy is the severing or crushing of an axon, either by cutting or by crushing, which separates the axon into a proximal segment (attached to the cell body) and a distal segment (that has lost this crucial attachment). The consequences of axotomy are numerous and affect both the injured neuron and its synaptic partners.

## Key facts

- Axotomy divides the axon into a proximal segment that remains attached to the cell body and a distal segment that loses this attachment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- The distal axonal stump undergoes Wallerian degeneration, where the neuronal membrane breaks down, the cytoskeleton is disassembled, and the axon is phagocytosed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- The cell body of the damaged neuron undergoes chromatolysis, characterized by swelling and the nucleus moving to an eccentric position. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Synaptic terminals that contact the damaged neuron withdraw, and glial cell processes invade the vacated synaptic site. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- The injured neuron's inputs and targets can undergo transneuronal (retrograde and anterograde) degeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
