# Cohort Analysis

## Summary

Cohort analysis is one of the most important tools of startup analytics. Instead of looking at cumulative totals or gross numbers, it examines the performance of each group of customers that comes into contact with the product independently, with each group called a cohort. This technique provides clear trends and prevents managers from blaming legacy customers or external conditions for poor results.

## Key facts

- Cohort analysis is based on a simple premise: instead of looking at cumulative totals or gross numbers such as total revenue and total number of customers, one looks at the performance of each group of customers that comes into contact with the product independently. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p115]
- At IMVU, cohort analysis showed that despite a fourfold increase in customers using the product five times, the percentage of new customers who paid money remained stuck at around 1 percent after months of work. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p116]
- Cohort analysis allowed IMVU to not blame failure on legacy customers, external market conditions, or any other excuse because each cohort represented an independent report card. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p116]
- Cohort-based reports are the gold standard of learning metrics because they turn complex actions into people-based reports. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p135]
