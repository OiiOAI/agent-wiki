# The Uncanny (Das Unheimliche)

## Summary

The uncanny (unheimlich) is a psychoanalytic concept denoting something frightening that is secretly familiar—the 'un' prefix marking repression of what was once heimlich (homely, known). Freud identifies intellectual uncertainty, the castration complex, the omnipotence of thoughts, and the compulsion to repeat as sources of uncanny feeling. The concept illuminates how repressed material from childhood and primitive animistic beliefs resurface in modern experience as disturbing, uncanny effects.

## Key facts

- The uncanny is defined as 'that class of the frightening which leads back to what is known of old and long familiar'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5483]
- Schelling defined the uncanny as 'everything that ought to have remained secret and hidden but has come to light'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5502]
- The fear of damaging or losing eyes in children is often a substitute for the dread of castration, as demonstrated in the myth of Oedipus's self-blinding. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5493]
- The theme of the 'double' (Doppelgänger) originates from primary narcissism as 'an insurance against the destruction of the ego'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5497]
- The uncanny effect of involuntary repetition (returning to the same place, coincidental numbers) recalls the 'compulsion to repeat' proceeding from drive impulses in the unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5499]
- Jentsch's theory of intellectual uncertainty (being unable to determine if an object is alive or inanimate) is insufficient to explain the uncanny, according to Freud. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5492]
- The uncanny recurs in the female genital organs as 'the entrance to the former Heim of all human beings, to the place where each one of us lived once upon a time'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5505]
- In fiction, uncanny effects are more easily created than in real life because the writer can select a setting admitting realities we do not accept in ordinary experience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5510]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's analysis suggests that the uncanny is fundamentally linked to repressed content returning, distinguishing it from mere unfamiliarity or intellectual uncertainty. The animistic beliefs of primitive cultures and the psychic residues of infancy share a common mechanism with neurotic uncanny experiences.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact boundary between uncanny effects produced by repressed complexes versus those produced by 'surmounted' animistic beliefs may not always be clear, as Freud acknowledges these categories can overlap.
