# Sex Addiction

## Summary

Sex addiction is a controversial diagnostic label applied to compulsive sexual behavior, which Perel examines critically in the context of infidelity. She discusses debates about its validity, noting it borrows criteria from chemical dependency and has spawned an entire treatment industry, while questioning whether it medicalizes behaviors that have complex personal, cultural, and societal roots.

## Key facts

- There is no official diagnosis for sex addiction, yet many researchers and clinicians have defined the disorder by borrowing criteria from clinical definitions of chemical dependency. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p177]
- An entire industry has sprung up in response to sex addiction, including expensive rehab and treatment centers. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p177]
- Perel notes that women are rarely diagnosed with sex addiction; instead, they are seen as 'addicted to love'—described as a no less slippery slope but a more flattering diagnosis. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p178]
- The rise of sex addiction diagnosis is described as a 'fascinating study in the social construction of ills,' echoing age-old fears that too much sex, especially for men, is a slippery slope to deviance. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p178]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel appears to view sex addiction as potentially useful for some individuals to seek help by lifting shame, but problematic when it overshadows broader motives—personal, familial, and societal—that could help men better understand and integrate their sexuality.
