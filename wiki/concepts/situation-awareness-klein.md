# Situation Awareness (Gary Klein's Definition)

## Summary

Gary Klein defines situation awareness (SA) as 'the perception of the reactions that a set of changing events affords.' This definition emphasizes affordances in the situation and views understanding in terms of what can be done rather than merely recalled stimuli. Klein's framework positions SA as the basis for decision making, highlighting the complexity of handling multiple, interacting tasks in dynamic environments.

## Key facts

- Klein defines SA as the perception of reactions to a set of changing events, emphasizing affordances and understanding what can be done rather than merely recalled stimuli. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p68]
- Klein identifies four reasons why SA is important: (1) SA is linked to performance, (2) limitations in SA may result in errors, (3) SA may be related to expertise, and (4) SA is the basis for decision making. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p68]
- The most widely known account of SA is Endsley's (1995) three-level model: Level 1 (detection of relevant elements), Level 2 (comprehension of meaning), and Level 3 (projection of status into the future). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p69]
- Level 1 SA alone is difficult to bound without recourse to Level 2 SA, because potentially an infinite number of objects in an environment may be identified and characterized. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p69]
- What counts as an error in Level 1 SA is a function of the task being performed—for example, being off by 100 feet in altitude has different consequences for a commercial pilot versus a fighter pilot. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p69]
- Level 2 SA concerns meaning and diagnosis, where patterns are detected and situations are synthesized from a diverse mixture of events. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p70]
- Klein considers Level 2 SA as a process of sense-making rather than merely a state, linking it to attention management. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p70]
- Functional categories, based on alternative modes of responding, are more relevant to SA than structural categories (physical features of the environment); fireground commanders categorize fires by response type rather than building structure. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p70]
- A key aspect of Level 2 SA is the detection of leverage points—opportunities for making dramatic changes in a situation—which depend on both the environment and the expertise of the problem solver. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p70]
- SA is not composed of elements; rather, SA defines the elements—what is important depends on task context and the interpretation the person brings. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p85]
- The development of SA is an active process of guided information seeking, rather than a passive receipt and storage of details. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p86]

## Inferences

- Inference: Klein's definition differs from Endsley's in emphasizing affordances and what can be done, rather than purely information processing stages.
- Inference: The emphasis on functional categories suggests that SA research should examine the decision maker's perspective and purposes, not just environmental characteristics.
