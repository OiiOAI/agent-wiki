# Romanian Institution Study

## Summary

A landmark longitudinal study of children raised in Romanian institutions during Ceaușescu's regime (1980s), conducted primarily by Charles Nelson of Harvard. Children experienced severe neglect, sensory and cognitive deprivation, malnutrition, and minimal peer interactions. As adults, they showed decreased total brain size, gray matter, white matter, frontal cortical metabolism, and connectivity between brain regions, while the amygdala was enlarged.

## Key facts

- Children raised in Romanian institutions showed decreased total brain size, gray matter, white matter, frontal cortical metabolism, and connectivity between brain regions as adults. - The Romanian institution kids showed enlarged amygdala as adults. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p209]
- The longer the institutionalization, the worse the prognosis for these children. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p209]
- The study was initiated after Ceaușescu's 1989 overthrow, when thousands of children adopted by Westerners became available for study. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p209]

## Inferences

- Inference: The enlarged amygdala suggests hypervigilance and anxiety as lasting neurological consequences of institutional neglect.
- Inference: The contrast between reduced cortical connectivity and enlarged amygdala suggests a brain adaptation prioritizing threat detection over higher-order processing.
