# Better Angels of Our Nature

## Summary

Steven Pinker's thesis that violence has declined over millennia, presented in his book of the same name. Pinker argues that the 'civilizing process,' involving state monopolization of force, commerce, and an 'escalator of reasoning,' has produced unprecedented reductions in violence, though critics note this 'Long Peace' has been unevenly distributed.

## Key facts

- According to Pinker, violence has declined because states have monopolized force, coupled with the spread of commerce fostering realpolitik self-restraint and recognition that trading partners' well-being matters—this underlies the 'rights revolution' of civil, women's, children's, gay, and animal rights. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p632]
- Critics characterize Pinker's thesis as Eurocentrism: Western countries may be at peace with each other but have made war elsewhere, and parts of the developing world have been continuously at war for decades. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p634]
- When deaths are expressed as a percentage of total population and corrected for duration of events, World War II ranks number one, followed by World War I, the Russian Civil War, Mao, and the Rwandan genocide. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p635]
