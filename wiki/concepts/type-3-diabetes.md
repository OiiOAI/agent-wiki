# Type 3 Diabetes

## Summary

A term increasingly used to describe Alzheimer's disease, reflecting its metabolic origins. Research has shown that 80 percent of people with Alzheimer's have insulin resistance, and having type 2 diabetes equates to a two- to fourfold increased risk of developing Alzheimer's. An estimated 40 percent of Alzheimer's cases may be attributable to hyperinsulinemia alone.

## Key facts

- Insulin resistance is extraordinarily common in people with Alzheimer's disease: 80 percent of people with the disease have insulin resistance. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p135]
- Observational studies have shown that having type 2 diabetes equates to a two- to fourfold increased risk of developing Alzheimer's disease. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p135]
- An estimated 40 percent of Alzheimer's cases may be attributable to hyperinsulinemia alone. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p135]
- A growing chorus of researchers and clinicians are now referring to Alzheimer's as 'type 3 diabetes.'. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p135]
- A research group at Brown University (led by Suzanne de la Monte) coined the term 'type 3 diabetes' to describe Alzheimer's disease, directly characterizing it as being metabolic in origin. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p210]
