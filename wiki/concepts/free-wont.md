# Free Won't

## Summary

'Free won't' is V.S. Ramachandran's term for the human capacity to consciously veto or inhibit an action that the brain has already begun to prepare. It represents a counterinterpretation to the Libet experiment: while we may not have free will to initiate actions, we may have free won't to stop them.

## Key facts

- Ramachandran proposed that while we may not have free will, we have 'free won't'—the ability to consciously choose to veto an action during the lag between brain preparation and execution. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p610]
- The debate about Libet's findings concerns how many of the homunculus's buttons and switches are involved in initiating versus halting a behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p610]
