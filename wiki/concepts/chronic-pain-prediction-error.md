# Chronic Pain as Prediction Error

## Summary

Chronic pain is proposed to be a brain disease with roots in inflammation and prediction error. The theory suggests that chronic pain results when the brain issues unnecessary predictions of pain and ignores prediction error to the contrary, generating authentic pain for no discernable reason—similar to perceiving a bee when looking at a blobby picture. It's possible that the brain received intense nociceptive input in the past, and as the injury healed, the brain didn't get the memo and keeps predicting and categorizing anyway, generating chronic pain. Predictions about inner-body movements may also be turning up the volume for nociceptive input. Over 1.5 billion people suffer from chronic pain, including 100 million in the United States who collectively pay $500 billion per year for treatment.
