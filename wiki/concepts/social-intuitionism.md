# Social Intuitionism

## Summary

Social intuitionism is Jonathan Haidt's theory that moral judgments are primarily based on intuition and emotion, with reasoning serving as post-hoc justification rather than as the primary driver of moral decisions. This challenges the traditional view that humans think their way to moral conclusions.

## Key facts

- Social intuitionism holds that moral decisions are primarily based on intuition and emotion, while reasoning serves to justify decisions already made emotionally. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p494]
- Moral contemplation activates emotional brain regions (amygdala, vmPFC, insula, anterior cingulate) alongside cognitive dlPFC, with different transgressions preferentially activating different emotional subsets. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p494]
- Damage to emotional vmPFC makes moral judgments more pragmatic and coldhearted—people readily sacrifice one to save five, something normal subjects never do. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p496]
- Haidt's concept of 'moral dumbfounding' describes when people have strong moral opinions but cannot articulate why, followed by clumsy rationalization. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p496]
- Emotional brain activation (amygdala, vmPFC, insula) typically precedes dlPFC activation when facing moral quandaries. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p495]
- People become more conservative in social judgments when smelling foul odors or sitting at a dirty desk—visceral states influence moral reasoning. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p496]

## Inferences

- Inference: Social intuitionism suggests that 'moral reasoning' is largely rhetorical—designed to convince others (and ourselves) that our gut reactions are rationally justified. This aligns with findings about post-hoc rationalization in Us/Them contexts (pp. 414-415).
- Inference: The fact that vmPFC damage leads to cold, utilitarian decisions suggests that emotion-based moral intuitionism, while imperfect, anchors some of our most universally agreed-upon moral taboos (like harming kin).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which peripheral bodily states (stress hormones, gut feelings, ambient smells) influence moral reasoning are not fully specified in this chunk.
