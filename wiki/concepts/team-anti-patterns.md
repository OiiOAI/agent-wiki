# Team anti-patterns

## Summary

Team anti-patterns are common mistakes in team formation that lead to unexpected difficulties building and running software systems. The two primary anti-patterns identified are ad hoc team design (including oversized teams, technology-specific teams like COTS or middleware teams, and reactive DBA teams) and shuffling team members (volatile teams assembled for projects then disassembled). These patterns overlook the broader context of interrelationships between teams and can slow delivery while reducing team autonomy.

## Key facts

- The first team anti-pattern is ad hoc team design, including teams that have grown too large, teams created to take care of all COTS software or all middleware, or a DBA team created after a production crash. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- The second common anti-pattern is shuffling team members, leading to extremely volatile teams assembled on a project basis and disassembled immediately afterward. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- The cost of forming new teams and switching context repeatedly gets overlooked in project estimates when shuffling team members. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- A computer will perform the same whether placed in Room A or Room B, but an engineer placed on Team A may perform very differently than if placed on Team B. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- The 'DevOps team' anti-pattern occurs when a DevOps team becomes a hard dependency for application teams instead of helping design self-service capabilities. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
