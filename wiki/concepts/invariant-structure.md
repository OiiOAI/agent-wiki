# Invariant Structure

## Summary

Invariant structure refers to the unchanging aspects of the ambient optic array that persist despite changes in perspective caused by observer movement or illumination changes. These invariants specify the rigid layout of the environment, distinguishing it from perspective structure which varies with viewpoint. The invariant structure becomes evident in the context of changing non-essentials—'essentials become evident in the context of changing nonessentials.'

## Key facts

- Invariant structure consists of what is invariant despite changes in the optic array, and it does not emerge unequivocally except with a flux. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p97]
- The changes in the array come from locomotion, and the nonchanges come from the rigid layout of environmental surfaces—the nonchanges specify the layout. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p97]
- The main invariants of the terrestrial environment are the layout of its surfaces and the reflectances of these surfaces, which tend to persist because substances are sufficiently solid and chemically inert. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p111]
- Invariant structure separates off best when the frozen perspective structure begins to flow during locomotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between perspective structure and invariant structure suggests that perceiving the environment involves extracting persistent information from constantly changing sensory input, a process fundamentally different from sensation-based theories.
