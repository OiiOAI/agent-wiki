# Ego Alteration

## Summary

Ego alteration refers to changes in the ego that occur during defensive struggles, particularly in early childhood. These alterations include reaction-formations, anticathexes, and other defensive mechanisms that become fixed in the ego as regular modes of reaction throughout life. Freud argues that the defensive mechanisms directed against former dangers recur in treatment as resistances against recovery, making the ego treat recovery itself as a new danger. The degree of ego alteration is a key factor determining the success or failure of psychoanalytic treatment.

## Key facts

- Defensive mechanisms become fixated in the ego as regular modes of reaction of the character's nature, repeated throughout life whenever a situation similar to the original one occurs. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7389]
- The defensive mechanisms may become dangers themselves; the dynamic expenditure necessary for maintaining them and the restrictions they entail prove a heavy burden on the psychical economy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7389]
- The defensive mechanisms, by bringing about an ever more extensive alienation from the external world and a permanent weakening of the ego, pave the way for the outbreak of neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7389]
- During work on resistances, the ego withdraws from the agreement on which the analytic situation is founded, disobeys the fundamental rule, and allows no further derivatives of the repressed to emerge. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7391]
- The outcome of analytic treatment depends essentially on the strength and depth of root of the resistances that bring about an alteration of the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7391]
