# Lorenz System

## Summary

The Lorenz system is a chaotic dynamical system used in Active Inference for challenging inference problems and simulating complex behaviors like birdsong. It exhibits itinerant behavior, orbiting one region of space before switching to another. It has been used in hierarchical generative models where slow high-level dynamics parameterize faster low-level dynamics.

## Key facts

- The Lorenz system follows dynamics with parameters: σ (Prandtl number), ρ (Rayleigh number), and β (physical constant), exhibiting chaotic behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p162]
- Lorenz systems have been used to simulate birdsong in multi-agent Active Inference, where high-level states evolve according to slow Lorenz dynamics that parameterize faster Lorenz dynamics at lower levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p163]
- The characteristic itinerancy and apparent autonomy of Lorenz attractors make them well suited for inclusion in models of biological phenomena. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p166]
