# Positional Bargaining

## Summary

Positional bargaining is the most common form of negotiation where each side takes a position, argues for it, and makes concessions to reach a compromise. The authors identify three major drawbacks: it produces unwise agreements because locking into positions prevents reconciling original interests; it is inefficient as extreme positions and small concessions take time; and it endangers ongoing relationships by turning negotiations into contests of will.

## Key facts

- Positional bargaining is the most common form of negotiation where each side takes a position, argues for it, and makes concessions to reach a compromise. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p7]
- When negotiators bargain over positions, they tend to lock themselves into those positions, making it difficult to reconcile original interests. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p7]
- Bargaining over positions creates incentives that stall settlement: starting with an extreme position, holding stubbornly, deceiving about true views, and making small concessions all interfere with reaching settlement promptly. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p8]
- Positional bargaining becomes a contest of will where anger and resentment often result as one side bends to the rigid will of the other while legitimate concerns go unaddressed. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p8]
- The soft style of positional bargaining makes one vulnerable to someone who plays a hard game; in positional bargaining, hard dominates soft. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p9]
