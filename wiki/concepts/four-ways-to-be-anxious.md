# Anxiety: Four Ways to Be Anxious

## Summary

LeDoux synthesizes his framework by describing four scenarios in which anxiety (conscious feeling) can arise: (1) In the presence of an existing or imminent threat, worry about the event and its implications; (2) When noticing body sensations, worry about what they might mean; (3) Thoughts and memories lead to worry about well-being; (4) Thoughts and memories result in existential dread. All forms involve autonoetic (self) consciousness and require cortical processes that enable sensory information and memories, along with consequences of survival circuit activity, to be represented in working memory.

## Key facts

- Anxiety is a conscious feeling that can arise bottom-up from defensive circuit activity or from higher cognitive processes that conceptualize worry. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p359]
- The four scenarios show anxiety involves a particular kind of conscious thought focused on the self and its future. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p359]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because all four scenarios require autonoetic consciousness, treatments that target working memory and self-referential processing (like meditation) may reduce anxiety across scenarios.
