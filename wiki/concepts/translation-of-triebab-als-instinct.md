# Translation of Freud's Trieb as 'Instinct' vs 'Drive'

## Summary

Strachey's translation of Trieb as 'instinct' has been attacked 'with considerable severity' (as Strachey himself acknowledged). Critics argue that Trieb is directionless while 'instinct' implies specific behavioral tendencies; Trieb exerts continuous pressure while 'instinct' is active discontinuously; the vicissitudes of Trieb are modifiable by analysis while 'instinct' is fixed. The near-unanimous modern opinion is that 'instinct' should be reserved for Freud's separate term Instinkt, and Trieb should be translated as 'drive'. Ernest Jones notably objected to 'instinct' and noted that Americans had translated it as 'drive', which he considered a useful term. The RSE has adopted 'drive' as the standard translation. Strachey's reasons for choosing 'instinct' included that 'drive' was not an established English word and that 'instinct' was appropriately vague to cover Freud's varied usage.
