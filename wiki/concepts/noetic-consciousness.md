# Noetic Consciousness

## Summary

Noetic consciousness, as proposed by Endel Tulving, is awareness about facts based on stored internal representations of objects and events. In noetic states, one is aware that something exists but is not personally part of the experience. This contrasts with autonoetic consciousness, which involves self-awareness. LeDoux proposes that nonhuman animals may have a nonverbal form of noetic awareness.

## Key facts

- Noetic consciousness involves awareness of facts based on stored internal representations, without personal involvement. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p292]
- Semantic memories underlie noetic consciousness — they concern facts about the world independent of personal episodes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p291]
- LeDoux proposes nonhuman primates and possibly other mammals may have nonverbal noetic awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p323]
- In noetic awareness an animal could be aware of being in the presence of danger, food, or mates, and have a sense of familiarity, without reflective self-awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p323]

## Inferences

- Inference: Noetic consciousness may be more widespread in the animal kingdom than autonoetic consciousness, since it doesn't require language or full self-awareness.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether nonverbal animals actually have noetic consciousness is difficult to demonstrate scientifically without language-based verbal reports.
