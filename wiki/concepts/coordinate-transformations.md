# Coordinate Transformations

## Summary

The brain performs coordinate transformations between different reference frames (retinal, head-centered, body-centered, hand-centered) through gain modulation mechanisms. This allows reaching movements to be planned correctly regardless of current gaze direction, integrating information across multiple coordinate systems at the same neural stage.

## Key facts

- Parietal neurons carry information about eye position, which can be added to an object's retinal coordinates to compute the relationship between gaze angle and hand target location. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p309]
- By computing summed activity of gain-modulated parietal neurons, downstream reader neurons can infer the gaze-body relationship and update spatial coordinates of arm targets as gaze direction changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p310]
- Eye and hand gain fields have equal and opposite strengths, combining reference frame transformations with movement planning at the same stage of cortical processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p309]
- The transformation between eye-centered and body-centered coordinates requires only a neuronal gain mechanism, and the same type of gain control can be deployed for similar purposes in other circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p312]

## Inferences

- Inference: Along the processing hierarchy, ever more complex features of retinal information can be extracted as body-centered information undergoes further transformation at each level.
- Inference: Translation invariance, an abstraction, emerges when objects are viewed, approached, and touched from different directions, stripping off particular conditions.
