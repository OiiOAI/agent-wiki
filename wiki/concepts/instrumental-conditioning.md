# Instrumental Conditioning

## Summary

Instrumental conditioning involves learning to perform particular behavioral responses to increase or decrease the probability of obtaining rewards or avoiding punishments. It can be fractionated into goal-directed and habitual components mediated by distinct neural substrates.

## Key facts

- Instrumental conditioning involves learning to perform particular behavioral responses to increase or decrease the probability of obtaining outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p253]
- Modern perspectives distinguish between goal-directed learning grounded in action-outcome associations and habitual learning depending on stimulus-response associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p255]
- The dorsomedial striatum is critical for action-outcome learning in instrumental conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p233]
- Reward-prediction error signals in the dorsal striatum distinguish learners from nonlearners during reward-based decision making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p271]
- The dorsolateral striatum supports habit-based behavior while the dorsomedial striatum subserves goal-directed action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p235]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between dorsomedial and dorsolateral striatum function supports a dual-process model of instrumental learning with distinct neural substrates for goal-directed and habitual control.
