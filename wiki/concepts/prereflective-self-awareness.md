# Prereflective Self-Awareness

## Summary

Prereflective self-awareness is the implicit, non-object-directed awareness of one's own ongoing experience that occurs without reflective observation or inference. According to Dan Zahavi's reconstruction of Husserl, prereflective self-awareness is a necessary condition for experiencing temporal phenomena—consciousness must be retentionally and protentionally aware of itself across time. The threefold structure of time-consciousness (primal impression-retention-protention) entails prereflective self-awareness: at the same moment one is aware of something (e.g., a melody), one is implicitly co-aware of one's ongoing experience. This self-awareness is intransitive—it does not present experience as an object. The subjectivity of experience consists essentially in being intransitively self-aware, prior to any reflection. Inner time-consciousness is proposed as equivalent to prereflective self-awareness, and the absolute flow of consciousness is identified as the prereflective self-manifestation of experiences. This self-awareness is streaming (constitutive of flowing experiences) yet standing (an ever-present and unchanging feature of consciousness).
