# Alienation

## Summary

Alienation, philosophically meaning to be separate from one's self, is understood by Deci concretely in terms of behaviors that are pressured and controlled. When people are controlled—rather than acting from their integrated self—they experience alienation. Deci argues that alienation begins as people lose touch with their intrinsic motivation, with the vitality and excitement that characterizes authentic engagement. The phenomenon can be observed when extrinsic rewards transform intrinsically interesting activities into instrumental obligations. Deci's experiments demonstrated that paying people for previously enjoyable activities made them dependent on rewards and diminished their authentic engagement.
