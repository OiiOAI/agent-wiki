# Conceptual Knowledge Organization

## Summary

Hawkins proposes that all knowledge, including abstract concepts like democracy and mathematics, is stored using the same reference frame mechanism that models physical objects. Concepts that cannot be visualized may use reference frames with more than three dimensions. Learning expert knowledge requires discovering useful reference frames in which to organize the facts, which may explain why learning complex subjects is difficult.

## Key facts

- Reference frames can be used to organize knowledge of things we cannot directly sense, such as DNA molecules, by imagining them as if we could see and touch them. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p75]
- Concepts like democracy and mathematics are organized in reference frames that may have more than three dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p80]
- Mathematics can be understood as stored in reference frames where equations are locations and mathematical operations are movements between locations. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p84]
- Becoming an expert requires discovering a useful reference frame for organizing the facts of a field, which may be the most difficult part of learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p84]
- fMRI experiments showed that when people think about birds with varying neck and leg lengths, they mentally 'move' through a maplike space representing those dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p83]

## Inferences

- Inference: onceptual knowledge requires discovering appropriate reference frames, educational methods that explicitly teach frameworks may be more effective than rote memorization.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact number and nature of dimensions used by the brain for abstract concept reference frames remains unknown.
