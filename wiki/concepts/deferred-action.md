# Deferred Action

## Summary

Deferred action refers to the psychological phenomenon whereby experiences from early childhood, which had little effect at the time, become pathogenic when revived in memory after sexual maturity. The memory operates with greater force than the original experience due to the enhanced sexual reactivity of the mature organism.

## Key facts

- Sexual experiences in childhood produce little or no effect at the time but their psychical trace is preserved and can be awakened later with pathogenic force. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p154]
- When awakened after puberty, the memory operates as though it were a contemporary event due to the immense increase in the capacity of the sexual apparatus for reaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p154]
- The period of 'sexual maturity' relevant for deferred action falls earlier than puberty, from approximately the eighth to tenth year. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p177]
- Sexual experiences of childhood produce their effect only to a very slight degree at the time at which they occur; what is far more important is their deferred effect which can only take place at later periods of growth. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1171]
- During the interval between infantile impressions and their reproduction, both the somatic sexual apparatus and the psychical apparatus undergo important development. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1171]
- The influence of earlier sexual experiences leads to an abnormal psychical reaction because the psychical apparatus has developed in the interval. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1171]
- The child received the impression of the primal scene at age 1.5, was only able to understand it at age 4, and only understood it consciously during analysis in his twenties. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5302]
- Deferred action (Nachträglichkeit) explains how scenes from infancy can produce effects long after they occurred, without losing their emotional freshness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5303]
- The Wolf Man's dreams during treatment repeatedly reproduced the content of the primal scene, which Freud regarded as equivalent to recollection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5309]

## Inferences

- Inference: This concept represents the first published description of Nachträglichkeit, which became fundamental to psychoanalytic understanding of how early experiences shape later psychic life.
- Inference: Deferred action explains why the original trauma may be forgotten but still exert pathogenic influence when its effects become amplified by later developmental stages.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact relationship between puberty and the 'sexual maturity' that enables deferred traumatic effects remains somewhat unclear.
