# Problem with Averages

## Summary

The problem with averages arises because they often mask the underlying distribution. Adding Bill Gates to 50 people with average wealth of $54,000 increases the average by over two million percent. In complex systems, distribution becomes increasingly irregular (power law).

## Key facts

- Nassim Taleb warns: 'Don't cross a river if it is (on average) four feet deep.' The river can be shallow for long stretches but twenty feet deep in the middle. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p127]
- Adding Bill Gates (worth ~$59 billion) to 50 people with $54,000 average assets increases average wealth to $1.15 billion. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p127]
- In a complex world, distribution is becoming more irregular: a handful of websites (New York Times, Facebook, Google) garner the majority of visits. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p128]
