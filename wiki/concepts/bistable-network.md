# Bistable Network

## Summary

A bistable network is a neural circuit capable of sustaining activity corresponding to either of two different states, typically one with a low rate of firing and one with a high rate. Persistent firing at a high rate is made possible by strong recurrent excitation, with bistability depending on nonlinear neuronal response properties that make firing rate highly sensitive to input changes at intermediate firing rates but relatively insensitive at low and high rates.

## Key facts

- Bistability is the ability of a network to sustain activity corresponding to either of two different states, typically one with a low rate of firing and one with a high rate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- Persistent firing at a high rate is made possible by strong recurrent excitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- Bistability requires the level of activity of a neuron to depend on its recurrent input in a nonlinear way. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- The nonlinearity is assured by a sigmoidal neuronal response curve that makes firing rate relatively insensitive to changes in input at low firing rates (when mean voltage is well below threshold) and at high firing rates (when rates saturate), but highly sensitive at intermediate firing rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- At an intermediate level of firing, high sensitivity to changes in input renders the excitatory feedback unstable, forcing the network to high or low firing rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- At high or low firing rates, the excitatory feedback becomes stable because of weakened neuronal sensitivity, allowing a stable firing rate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- In a bistable network, low and high firing rates are stable in the presence of small transient input pulses; the firing rate returns to its initial state after the input pulse terminates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
- Larger input pulses can induce transitions from one firing rate to the other in a bistable network. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1666]
