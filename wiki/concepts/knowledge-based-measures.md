# Knowledge-based measures of situation awareness

## Summary

Knowledge-based measures attempt to directly ascertain the subject's internal awareness of the situation. These include structured techniques like SAGAT (Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique), which freezes operator displays at random times during experiments and queries subjects about their knowledge of the environment. Knowledge-based measures can probe all three levels of SA—from basic facts to predictions of future states—and provide insight into the development and maintenance of situation awareness.

## Key facts

- Knowledge-based measures attempt to directly ascertain the subject's internal awareness of the situation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p209]
- SAGAT freezes the operator's displays at random times during experiment runs and queries the subjects about their knowledge of the environment. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p209]
- Knowledge-based measures can isolate components of current situation awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p210]
- A potential limitation of knowledge-based measures is that they may not necessarily predict final performance of the operator. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p210]
- Knowledge-based measures are easier to measure declarative knowledge than procedural knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p210]
- Knowledge-based measures may be intrusive into the operator's task. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p210]
