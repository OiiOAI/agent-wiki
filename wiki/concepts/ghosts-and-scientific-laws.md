# Ghosts and the Nature of Scientific Laws

## Summary

The narrator argues that scientific laws (gravity, logic, mathematics) are 'ghosts'—human inventions that exist only in minds, not in external reality. He contends that dismissing traditional ghosts while believing in scientific laws is inconsistent, as both are mental constructs.

## Key facts

- The narrator claims that before Isaac Newton there was no gravity—laws of nature 'did not exist before Isaac Newton' in any meaningful sense. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p32]
- He states: 'Laws of nature are human inventions, like ghosts. Laws of logic, of mathematics are also human inventions, like ghosts'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p32]
- The narrator attributes mass belief in gravity to 'mass hypnosis. In a very orthodox form known as education'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p32]

## Inferences

- Inference: This argument establishes that all intellectual frameworks—scientific or spiritual—are human creations, suggesting that the distinction between 'rational' and 'mystical' views is less absolute than typically assumed.
