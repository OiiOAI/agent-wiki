# Social Herding

## Summary

Social herding is a cognitive bias where people look to others' behavior when they lack information, assuming others know things they do not. When everyone is doing the same, mutual calibration can result in uninformed actions or the assumption that 'the other guy' is paying attention to fundamentals. This bias played a strong role in the 2008 financial crisis.

## Key facts

- Social herding occurs when people assume others know things they do not, making it sensible to look at the behavior of others. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- If everyone else is doing the same, mutual calibration can result in all members of a group undertaking uninformed actions. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- Social herding presses people to think everything is OK (or not OK) because everyone else is saying so. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p104]
