# Mirror Neurons

## Summary

Mirror neurons are a remarkable population of neurons that discharge both when an individual performs a motor act and when they observe a similar action performed by another. First discovered in the ventral premotor cortex (PMv) and later in parietal area AIP of monkeys, mirror neurons are thought to provide a link between observed actions and the observer's stored knowledge of similar actions they have performed. This system may contribute to action understanding, empathy, and social cognition, and dysfunction may contribute to autism spectrum disorders.

## Key facts

- Mirror neurons, first discovered in PMv and later in parietal AIP, discharge both when the monkey actively grasps and manipulates objects and when it observes similar actions performed by another monkey or the experimenter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
- Mirror neurons typically do not respond when a monkey simply observes a potential target object or when it observes mimed arm and hand actions without a target object. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
- Some parietal mirror neurons can differentiate the ultimate goal of similar observed actions, such as grasping and picking up food to eat versus putting it into a cup. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
- Humans are also endowed with a mirror-like mechanism to match observed actions with actions encoded in their motor system, with activity arising in rostral inferior parietal lobule, IPS, PMv, and posterior sector of inferior frontal gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p840]
- Activation of premotor circuits during nonmotor contexts may contribute to understanding the nature and consequences of observed events in the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p840]
- Dysfunction of the mirror-neuron system in young children may contribute to some of the symptoms of autism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p840]
