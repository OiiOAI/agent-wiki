# Cabinet of Curiosities

## Summary

Inspired by 16th-17th century European Wunderkammern (wonder chambers), this concept encourages sharing your collections, influences, and sources of inspiration. Your tastes form your identity and influence your work, so sharing them helps others understand who you are and what you do—sometimes more than sharing your own work directly.

## Key facts

- In 16th- and 17th-century Europe, wealthy educated people had Wunderkammern or 'cabinets of curiosities'—rooms filled with rare and remarkable objects serving as external display of their thirst for knowledge. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p75]
- Kleon describes cabinets of curiosities as precursors to modern museums, juxtaposing natural and human-made marvels. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p75]
- Writer Jonathan Lethem is quoted: 'I'm basically a curator. Making books has always felt very connected to my bookselling experience, that of wanting to draw people's attention to things that I liked.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p77]
- Public radio personality Ira Glass is cited: 'All of us who do creative work, we get into it because we have good taste. But there is this gap. For the first couple years you make stuff, it's just not that good.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p77]
- DJ Spooky is quoted: 'You're only as good as your record collection.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p77]
- Your influences are worth sharing because they clue people in to who you are and what you do—sometimes even more than your own work. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p77]
- Kleon quotes Michel de Montaigne from 'On Experience': 'In my opinion, the most ordinary things, the most common and familiar, if we could see them in their true light, would turn out to be the grandest miracles.'.
