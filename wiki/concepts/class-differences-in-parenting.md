# Class Differences in Parenting

## Summary

Research by anthropologist Adrie Kusserow comparing parenting in wealthy Manhattan, stable blue-collar, and poor crime-ridden neighborhoods in Queens. Upper-middle-class parenting emphasized 'soft individualism' focused on psychological health and individuation; working-class parenting involved 'hard offensive individualism' focused on maintaining socioeconomic momentum; poor-neighborhood parenting was 'hard defensive individualism' focused on sheltering children from danger.

## Key facts

- In higher-SES strata, parenting tends to be authoritative or permissive; in lower-SES rungs, parenting is typically authoritarian, reflecting themes of protection and preparation for a tough world. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p216]
- Upper-middle-class parents' speech brimmed with metaphors of potential being fulfilled—flowering, blooming, growing, blossoming. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p217]
- Parents in the poor neighborhood used metaphors about not losing what was achieved—standing your ground, keeping up your pride, not letting others get under your skin. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p216]
- Working-class parents' speech about hopes for kids contained images of movement, progress, and athletics—getting ahead, testing the waters, going for the gold. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p216]

## Inferences

- Inference: Class differences in parenting resemble differences between Western countries and developing worlds—higher-SES parents teach and facilitate exploration; lower-SES parents focus on keeping children alive and buffered from threat.
