# Zeigarnik Effect

## Summary

A psychological phenomenon where incomplete tasks dominate attention more than completed ones, which can be mitigated by making a plan for completing the task.

## Key facts

- The Zeigarnik effect describes the ability of incomplete tasks to dominate our attention. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p90]
- If you stop work at 5 p.m. and declare you're done, incomplete obligations will battle for attention throughout the evening. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p90]
- A study by Baumeister and Masicampo found that making a plan for how to complete an incomplete task significantly reduces the Zeigarnik effect. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p90]
- A shutdown ritual captures every task in a common list, reviews tasks, and makes a plan for the next day, releasing the mind from tracking obligations. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p90]
- Zeigarnik observed that waiters remembered complex orders perfectly while customers were present but forgot them the moment they left the table. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p68]
- Open tasks occupy short-term memory until they are done; writing them down removes them even though they are not yet completed. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p68]
- David Allen's 'Getting Things Done' system works by the same principle: getting all tasks into a trusted external system enables a 'mind like water.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p68]
- The Zeigarnik effect can be used deliberately: keeping an unanswered question lingering in the background allows the brain to work on it associatively during unrelated activities like walking or showering. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p69]
