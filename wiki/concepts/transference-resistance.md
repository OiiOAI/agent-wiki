# Transference as Resistance

## Summary

Freud's concept that in psychoanalytic treatment, transference functions as the primary vehicle of resistance rather than merely as a therapeutic tool. The patient transfers unconscious feelings (especially from childhood) onto the analyst, and this transference becomes a powerful resistance because it satisfies the patient's need to maintain repression while avoiding conscious confrontation with pathogenic material.

## Key facts

- Freud observed that transference appears as resistance because it allows the patient to avoid confronting pathogenic material while appearing to engage with the analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3813]
- When associations fail during analysis, telling the patient he is dominated by associations connected to the doctor removes the stoppage, indicating the transference nature of the resistance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3813]
- Freud argued that transference in psychoanalysis becomes the strongest resistance because it exploits material suitable for transfer onto the analyst to avoid deeper pathogenic complexes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3815]
- The longer analysis continues, the more consistently patients use transference distortion because it offers the greatest advantages for avoiding awareness of pathogenic material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3815]
- Every conflict in advanced analysis tends to be fought out in the sphere of transference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3815]

## Inferences

- Inference: This concept revolutionized psychoanalytic technique by requiring analysts to recognize and interpret transference resistances as central to the therapeutic process, rather than viewing them as obstacles to be bypassed.
