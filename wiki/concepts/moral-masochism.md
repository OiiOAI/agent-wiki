# Moral Masochism

## Summary

The third form of masochism distinguished by its loosened connection with sexuality. It manifests as an unconscious sense of guilt requiring punishment, and involves the sexualization of morality and the revival of the Oedipus complex.

## Key facts

- In moral masochism, the suffering itself is what matters, regardless of whether it comes from a loved person or impersonal forces. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6049]
- Moral masochism is unconscious and must be inferred from behavior, while the sadism of the superego usually becomes conscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6052]
- Through moral masochism, morality becomes sexualized once more and the Oedipus complex is revived. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6052]
- Moral masochists create temptations to perform sinful actions which must then be expiated by conscience or by chastisement from the power of Destiny. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6052]
- The first drive renunciation is enforced by external powers and creates the ethical sense, which then demands further renunciation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6053]
