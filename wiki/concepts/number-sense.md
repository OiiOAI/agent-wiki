# Number Sense

## Summary

Number sense refers to the brain's innate ability to estimate quantities, which operates on a logarithmic rather than linear scale. Buzsáki argues that 'natural numbers' are human inventions that must be explicitly learned, contrasting with the preconfigured logarithmic representations present from early development.

## Key facts

- Numbers such as 'threeness' and 'fourness' are explicit abstract symbols, a human invention that must be learned through education. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- The Mundurucu, an Amazonian indigenous group without formal math education, map symbolic and nonsymbolic numbers onto a logarithmic instead of linear scale. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- Western kindergarteners also devote more space to small numbers, imposing a compressed logarithmic mapping (e.g., placing 10 near the middle of the 0-100 segment). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- Children's difficulties with education-based arithmetic may result from their persistent reliance on preexisting logarithmic representations of numerical magnitudes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- Neurological patients with parietal brain damage may choose five as the midpoint of a numerical interval between two and six, biased by the brain's natural tendency to estimate logarithmic values. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p372]
- Problems requiring exact calculation activate frontal cortical regions including language areas, in contrast to the logarithmic estimation used for quantity judgment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p373]
