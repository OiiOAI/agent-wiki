# Episodic-like memory in cuttlefish

## Summary

Research by Christelle Jozet-Alves and colleagues demonstrating that cuttlefish possess a form of memory analogous to episodic memory in humans—specifically 'what-where-when' memory, the ability to remember what food was available, where it was located, and when it would be replenished.

## Key facts

- Christelle Jozet-Alves and her group conducted experiments on cuttlefish memory based on a famous series of experiments showing episodic memory in birds, modeled after Nicola Clayton's work with scrub jays. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p155]
- In the experiment, cuttlefish learned that their preferred food (shrimp) was replenished more slowly (three hours) than crab (one hour), and adjusted their foraging behavior accordingly after delays. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p155]
- After a one-hour delay, cuttlefish went to the crab location; after a three-hour delay, they went for the shrimp, demonstrating they remembered both what food was available and when it would be replenished. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p155]
