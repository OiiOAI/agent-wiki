# Voltage-Gated Sodium Channels

## Summary

Voltage-gated sodium channels are transmembrane proteins that catalyze the rapid influx of Na+ during the rising phase of the action potential. They contain four homologous domains, each with six transmembrane segments, including the voltage-sensing S4 region. Mammals have nine Na+ channel α-subunit genes (Nav1.1–Nav1.9) with distinct expression patterns and functional properties.

## Key facts

- The α-subunit consists of four repeated domains (I-IV), each containing six transmembrane segments (S1-S6), with the S4 region containing positively charged arginine and lysine residues that serve as voltage sensors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p270]
- Na+ channels have activation gates (closed at rest, opened by depolarization) and inactivation gates (open at rest, closed after channel opening), allowing Na+ conduction only during the brief period when both gates are open. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p266]
- Mammalian neurons express Nav1.1, Nav1.2, and Nav1.6 widely, while Nav1.7, Nav1.8, and Nav1.9 are restricted to peripheral sensory neurons; Nav1.1 mutations can cause epilepsy (Dravet syndrome). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p272]
- Movement of S4 regions in domains I-III is associated with activation, while slower movement of the domain IV S4 is associated with inactivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p269]
