# Artificial General Intelligence

## Summary

Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) refers to machines that exhibit human-like intelligence—the ability to rapidly learn new tasks, see analogies between different tasks, and flexibly solve new problems. Hawkins argues that current deep learning approaches cannot achieve AGI and that brain-inspired principles including continuous learning, learning via movement, many models, and reference frames are required.

## Key facts

- AGI is the long-term goal of AI research: creating machines that can rapidly learn new tasks, see analogies, and flexibly solve new problems. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p117]
- Deep learning networks have to be fully trained before deployment and cannot learn new things on the go—training for a new object requires retraining from the ground up. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p116]
- Deep learning AI systems exhibit almost no flexibility—AlphaGo can beat humans at Go but cannot do anything else, like fix a flat tire. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p117]
- Deep learning networks don't possess knowledge—a Go-playing computer doesn't know what Go is, and an image-labeling network has limited knowledge of cats beyond pattern matching. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p119]
- Intelligence cannot be measured by how well a machine performs a single task—intelligence is determined by how a machine learns and stores knowledge about the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p129]

## Inferences

- Inference: The transition from dedicated to universal AI will occur for the same reasons universal computers won out over dedicated computers—cost-effectiveness and ecosystem effects driving rapid improvements.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The timeline for achieving true AGI is unknown, though Hawkins suggests it could occur within the next two to three decades.
