# Errors (Fehlleistungen)

## Summary

In Freud's framework, errors are parapraxes that involve incorrect performance of an action despite the intention to perform it correctly. Freud distinguishes errors from simple ignorance, arguing that errors often represent the expression of repressed thoughts or wishes that cannot achieve direct conscious expression. Errors may take various forms: name substitutions, factual mistakes, temporal displacements in mythological references, and confusions between similar concepts. The underlying principle is that where an error makes its appearance, a repression lies behind it—specifically, an insincerity or distortion ultimately rooted in repressed material.

## Key facts

- Errors are derivatives of repressed thoughts connected with emotionally significant material such as family relationships. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2191]
- Freud distinguishes errors based on repression from those based on genuine ignorance, using the example of confusing two places with the same name. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2192]
- Freud's error of confusing Hasdrubal for Hamilcar (brother's name for father's name) was traced to repressed phantasies about his relationship with his father and half-brother in England. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2192]
- Freud describes an 'error' of taking 'The Medici' instead of a Venice art book for a patient, revealing his unconscious disapproval of the patient's journey. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2193]
- Of all parapraxes, errors seem to have the least rigid mechanism—the disturbing influence determines that a disturbance occurred, but not the particular form the error takes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2193]
