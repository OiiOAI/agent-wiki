# The Surrender Experiment

## Summary

A conscious practice developed by Michael A. Singer in which one surrenders personal preferences and allows life to guide events, rather than resisting what unfolds. The experiment asks whether aligning one's will with natural forces leads to better outcomes than fighting reality to make it conform to personal desires.

## Key facts

- The surrender experiment began around 1972 when Singer decided that if life brought events before him, he would treat them as opportunities to go beyond himself rather than resist them. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p55]
- The core practice involves replacing resistance with acceptance: when the mind says 'Why did it have to rain today?' one replaces it with 'Look how beautiful; it's raining'. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p56]
- Singer describes the experiment as having two distinct steps: first letting go of personal reactions of like and dislike, then looking to see what is being asked by the situation unfolding. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p64]
- The experiment does not mean living without will, but rather having will guided by what life is doing instead of what one wants it to be doing. [raw/books/psychology/The Surrender Experiment_ My Journey into Life's Perfection - Michael A_ Singer.pdf#p13]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept challenges the Western emphasis on control and willpower, suggesting that natural forces have been managing the universe successfully for billions of years without human intervention.
- Inference: Singer's experiment led him from isolation to teaching, business leadership, and prison ministry—outcomes he never planned but accepted as life's guidance.
