# Simple Logic

## Summary

The Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT) by Shane Frederick reveals how people fail to suppress intuitive but wrong answers. MIT students averaged 2.18 correct answers, Princeton 1.63, and Michigan 0.83. High CRT scorers can control impulses and resist the seduction of 'now'.

## Key facts

- Shane Frederick's CRT asks: paddle and ball cost $1.10 where paddle costs $1 more — intuitive answer is 10 cents but correct answer is 5 cents. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p143]
- MIT students averaged 2.18 correct answers on the CRT, Princeton 1.63, and University of Michigan 0.83. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p143]
- People with low CRT scores tend to prefer immediate smaller rewards (instant bird in hand) while high scorers opt for larger delayed rewards. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p143]
