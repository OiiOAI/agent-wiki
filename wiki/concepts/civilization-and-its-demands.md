# Civilization and Its Demands

## Summary

Freud's concept describing how civilization imposes restrictions on human drive satisfaction (particularly sexual and aggressive impulses) through prohibitions and regulations. Every culture must compel a certain degree of drive renunciation, which generates inevitable opposition from individuals. The tension between civilization's requirements and individual drive wishes is a fundamental source of human unhappiness and cultural conflict.

## Key facts

- Every civilization must be built up on coercion and renunciation of drive, and it does not even seem certain that if coercion were to cease the majority of human beings would be prepared to perform the work necessary for acquiring new wealth. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6549]
- There are present in all men destructive, and therefore antisocial and anti-cultural, trends which are strong enough to determine their behaviour in human society. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6549]
- External coercion gradually becomes internalized through the superego, which takes over cultural prohibitions and includes them among its commandments. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6553]
