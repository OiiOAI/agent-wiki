# Posterior Parietal Cortex

## Summary

The posterior parietal cortex (PPC) is critical for providing sensory information about the body and external world for planning and executing motor actions. It contains several distinct functional areas that work in parallel, receiving different combinations of sensory and motor inputs related to guidance of different effectors. The PPC is involved in spatial representation, body schema, working memory for obstacles, and transforms visual information about objects into hand actions through connections with premotor cortex.

## Key facts

- The PPC contains several distinct functional areas working in parallel, each receiving different combinations of sensory and motor inputs for guidance of different effectors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p871]
- Neurons in PPC discharge two to three steps in advance of stepping over an obstacle, suggesting involvement in estimating limb-object position. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p853]
- PPC stores information about obstacle height in working memory, coordinating hindlimb gait modifications with forelimb movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p853]
- VIP neurons in monkeys develop expanded visual receptive fields to incorporate tools after training, as if the tool becomes a functional extension of the hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p875]
- AIP neurons transform visual object properties into hand actions for grasping, with activity during object viewing and grasping movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p872]
