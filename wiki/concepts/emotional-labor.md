# Emotional Labor

## Summary

Emotional labor is the energy that goes into maintaining the public face required in any job and in most human interactions. It includes behaviors like laughing politely at a boss's unfunny joke or putting on a happy face at a function when you want to be elsewhere. The text distinguishes between surface acting (faking emotions) and genuine expression, noting that too great an incongruity between how you really feel and how you pretend to be leads to burnout and negative consequences.

## Key facts

- Emotional labor is the energy that goes into maintaining the public face required in any job, and in fact in most any human interaction. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p170]
- The more you fake your emotions, or surface act, the worse off you're likely to be; too great an incongruity between how you really feel and how you pretend to be becomes such a chore that it leads to burnout. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p170]
- Surface acting is exhausting and can cause disconnection from your core self. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p171]
- Research found that spouses of hoteliers who surface-acted the most hoped their mates would find a different job as a way of rescuing their domestic lives. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p171]
- Hotel employees who truly love delighting guests and seeing to it that they enjoy themselves are probably not surface acting at all. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: Emotional labor at work is most sustainable when aligned with one's values ('walking your why'), transforming what might be exhausting pretense into genuine expression.
