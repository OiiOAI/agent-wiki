# Detachment from the External World in Sleep

## Summary

A psychological characteristic of dreaming wherein the sleeping mind isolates itself from the external world while maintaining some residual connection, as described by Burdach in 1838.

## Key facts

- Burdach wrote that sleep can occur only on condition that the mind is not irritated by sensory stimuli, but the actual precondition is not so much absence of sensory stimuli as absence of interest in them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1332]
- Some sense impressions may actually be necessary in order to calm the mind, as demonstrated by the miller who can only sleep while hearing his mill clack. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1332]
- The mind in sleep distinguishes between sensations; a sleeping man is not aroused by an indifferent word but wakes if called by name. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1333]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud notes that even disregarding these objections, the features of dream life attributed to detachment from the external world do not completely account for dreams' strange character.
