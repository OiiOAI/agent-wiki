# Placebo Effects

## Summary

Placebo effects arise when confident expectations of relief are sufficient to cause real and substantial relief, demonstrating the power of predictive processing over human experience. Remarkably, even honest or open-label placebos—where patients know the pills contain no active ingredient—have proven effective for conditions ranging from irritable bowel syndrome to cancer-related fatigue.

## Key facts

- Confident expectations of imminent pain relief from an administered pill or sham surgical procedure are often sufficient to cause real and substantial relief. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p175]
- Patients receiving placebo surgery for knee pain reported similar amounts of relief as those undergoing actual surgery, substantially more than those receiving less apparently serious placebo interventions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p176]
- In a 2010 study, honest placebos given to IBS patients doubled their rate of improvement, equaling the performance of two prominent active IBS medications. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p177]
- In 2019, honest placebos improved cancer-related fatigue despite patients being told the pills contained no active ingredients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p177]

## Inferences

- Inference: Honest placebos work by activating subterranean expectations through superficial indicators of reliability such as good packaging and professional presentation, because the bulk of the brain's prediction empire is nonconscious [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p178]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Why some individuals are more able than others to benefit from placebo-style interventions requires further investigation
