# Frustration

## Summary

Frustration (or 'frustration') is the deprivation of libidinal satisfaction, described as an external, accidental factor in neurosis causation. Freud argues that people fall ill of neurosis when deprived of the possibility of satisfying their libido, with symptoms serving as a substitute for frustrated satisfaction.

## Key facts

- Freud states that people fall ill of a neurosis if they are deprived of the possibility of satisfying their libido — they fall ill owing to 'frustration' [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5106]. - Symptoms are precisely a substitute for frustrated satisfaction [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5106]. - Frustration is the accidental, external factor in neurosis aetiology, while fixation is the predisposing, internal factor [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5107].
