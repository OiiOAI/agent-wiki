# Revealed Preference Theory

## Summary

Revealed preference theory, developed by Paul Samuelson in the 1930s, provides a method for testing whether a consumer behaves as a utility maximizer without directly observing utility. The Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference (WARP) states that if a consumer chooses x over y in one situation, they cannot be observed choosing y over x in another. This approach defines utility only in relation to observable choice behavior, without requiring auxiliary assumptions.

## Key facts

- WARP was developed in the 1930s by Paul Samuelson, who founded the revealed preference approach that was the heart of the neoclassical revolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p2]
- GARP (generalized axiom of revealed preference) posits that if apples are revealed preferred to oranges, and oranges are revealed preferred to peaches, then apples are indirectly revealed preferred to peaches. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p2]
- Revealed preference theories predict only choice; they use intermediate variables like utility as tools rather than making claims about their internal representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p2]
- The revealed-preference view suppressed interest in the psychological nature of preference because clever axiomatic systems could infer properties of unobservable preference from observable choice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p3]

## Inferences

- Inference: Revealed preference theory represents a methodological innovation that allows economists to test utility maximization by examining only observable behavior rather than requiring direct measurement of internal states.
- Inference: The WARP/GARP framework provides a minimal test that any decision-making model consistent with utility maximization must satisfy.
