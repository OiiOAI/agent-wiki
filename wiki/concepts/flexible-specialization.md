# Flexible Specialization

## Summary

Flexible specialization is an economic paradigm characterized by the use of flexible, multi-purpose equipment and skilled workers to produce small batches of specialized goods. Developed by Michael Piore and Charles Sabel in 'The Second Industrial Divide' (1984), this concept describes how certain regions—particularly in Italy—achieved economic success through networks of small specialized producers rather than mass production.

## Key facts

- Piore and Sabel developed the flexible specialization paradigm in 'The Second Industrial Divide' (1984). [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p394]
- The flexible specialization model emphasizes small-scale production and adaptability over mass production. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p394]
- Machine tool manufacturers tend to be small scale regardless of country due to the inherent low-volume nature of such products. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]

## Inferences

- Inference: Flexible specialization represents an alternative industrial model that emerged in response to the limitations of mass production, particularly viable in regions with strong social trust and cooperative networks.
