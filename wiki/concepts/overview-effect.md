# Overview Effect

## Summary

The overview effect is a transformation experienced by astronauts who travel deep into space and view Earth from distance. They report seeing the planet as a tiny blue sphere floating in blackness, which provides a literal extreme of the 'stepping out' practice for fresh perspective. Edgar Mitchell, lunar module pilot of Apollo 14 and sixth person to walk on the moon, described experiencing the universe as intelligent, loving, and harmonious during his return journey. The effect demonstrates how radically changing one's vantage point can shift emotional and existential perspective.

## Key facts

- Astronauts speak of the 'overview effect' as a transformation experienced after viewing Earth from deep space. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p93]
- Edgar Mitchell was lunar module pilot of Apollo 14 and the sixth person to walk on the moon in 1971. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p93]
- Mitchell described his moment of epiphany: 'On the return trip home, gazing through 240,000 miles of space toward the stars and the planet from which I had come, I suddenly experienced the universe as intelligent, loving, harmonious.'. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p93]
