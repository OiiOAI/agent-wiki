# Episodic memory in animals

## Summary

Research investigating whether animals possess episodic memory—defined by Tulving as including awareness of self and ability to project oneself into past or future—has focused on scrub jays and great apes. Studies by Clayton and colleagues demonstrate that scrub jays fulfill 'what, where, and when' questions about caching events, but whether this represents true episodic memory (with experiential/emotional component) versus semantic knowledge remains debated.

## Key facts

- Scrub jays cache different types of food in different places at different times and selectively retrieve food that degrades before food that stores well. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p323]
- Scrub jays adjust caching strategies to minimize potential stealing, recaching food in private when observed by other birds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p323]
- Clayton calls it 'episode-like memory' because it is unclear whether it is semantic knowledge or experiential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p323]
- Orangutans and bonobos demonstrated future planning of tool use carried from one room to another for use up to fourteen hours later. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p324]
- Episodic memory requires understanding that seeing is knowing and the ability to attribute past mental states to one's earlier self. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p322]

## Inferences

- Inference: Suddendorf argues that although great apes demonstrate future planning, researchers did not measure or control subjects' motivational states, so the findings may not necessarily imply anticipation of a future state of mind.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether scrub jays have true episodic memory with experiential component, or merely update semantic knowledge, remains unresolved.
