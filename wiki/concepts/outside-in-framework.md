# Outside-In Framework

## Summary

The outside-in framework is the dominant paradigm in mainstream neuroscience, which suggests that the brain's task is to perceive and represent the world, process information, and decide how to respond to it. This perception-centered view has deep philosophical roots in British empiricism, starting with Aristotle and elaborated by David Hume, and inevitably assumes the existence of a homunculus or decision-maker within the brain.

## Key facts

- The outside-in framework suggests that the brain's task is to perceive and represent the world, process information, and decide how to respond to it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p14]
- In the empiricism-inspired model, signals enter the brain from the outside, neuronal circuits process and perceive them, and some part of the brain decides whether to generate a motor response. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p8]
- The outside-in framework was strongly influenced by William James and most early psychologists who were influenced by British empiricism and Christian philosophies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p7]
- According to David Hume, all our knowledge arises from perceptual associations and inductive reasoning to reveal cause-and-effect relationships, with nothing existing in the mind without first being detected by the senses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p7]
- The outside-in framework treats the brain as fundamentally a passive device whose main job is to perceive, evaluate sensory inputs, weight their importance, and then decide whether and how to act. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p9]
- The book calls this practice 'neo-phrenology' because it takes human-constructed ideas and looks for brain mechanisms that can explain those ideas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p7]

## Inferences

- Inference: The outside-in framework's dominance may explain why research on perception and action have taken independent paths in neuroscience.
