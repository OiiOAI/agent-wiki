# Savanna Hypothesis

## Summary

Proposed by Gordon Orians, this hypothesis suggests that human aesthetic responses to natural landscapes, particularly trees with spreading forms, are based on innate knowledge of shapes associated with productive human habitats in ancestral African savanna environments.

## Key facts

- Younger subjects (3rd and 5th graders) preferred savanna landscapes over all other natural landscapes tested, while older subjects equally preferred familiar landscapes and savannas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p236]
- People preferred trees with spreading canopies similar to African savanna trees over rounded or columnar shapes, even those raised in areas with different tree dominant shapes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p236]
- Orians proposes that aesthetic responses to tree shapes derive from innate knowledge of productive habitats in ancestral landscapes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p236]
