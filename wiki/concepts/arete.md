# Aretē

## Summary

Aretē is the ancient Greek concept of excellence or virtue, as described in the Homeric epics. It implies a respect for the wholeness of life and a consequent dislike of specialization. The Greek warrior's aretē was 'duty toward himself'—striving after excellence. Phædrus discovers that aretē is the missing philosophical piece that connects Quality, virtue, and dharma. The Sophists were teaching aretē before Plato encapsulated it as the fixed 'Good,' subordinating it to Truth and dialectic, which Phædrus sees as the historical moment when Quality was subordinated to Reason.

- Aretē (ἀρετή) means excellence and implies a respect for the wholeness of life and a consequent dislike of specialization. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p322]
- What moves the Greek warrior to deeds of heroism is 'duty toward himself'—striving after aretē. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p321]
- Phædrus identifies aretē with Quality, virtue, and the Sanskrit dharma. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p322]
