# The Unconscious and Consciousness

## Summary

Freud distinguishes between two kinds of unconscious: the Ucs. (system unconscious), which is inadmissible to consciousness, and the Pcs. (preconscious), whose excitations can reach consciousness after passing through censorship. The unconscious is described as the larger sphere containing the smaller sphere of consciousness; it is the true psychical reality, as unknown to us as the reality of the external world. Consciousness is characterized as a sense organ for the perception of psychical qualities, not merely a reflection of completed processes.

## Key facts

- There are two kinds of unconscious not yet distinguished by psychologists: one (Ucs.) is inadmissible to consciousness, while the other (Pcs.) can reach consciousness through its excitations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1846]
- The unconscious is the larger sphere which includes within it the smaller sphere of the conscious; everything conscious has an unconscious preliminary stage. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1844]
- The unconscious is the true psychical reality; in its innermost nature it is as unknown to us as the reality of the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1844]
- Consciousness functions as a sense organ for the perception of psychical qualities, susceptible to excitation by qualities but incapable of retaining traces of alterations. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1847]
- By perceiving new qualities, consciousness makes a new contribution to directing mobile quantities of cathexis and distributing them in an expedient fashion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1848]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud argues that consciousness should not be overvalued; it is not synonymous with the psychical—complex rational thought processes can occur without consciousness.
- Inference: The Pcs. system acts as a screen between the Ucs. and consciousness, controlling access to voluntary movement and distributing mobile cathectic energy.
