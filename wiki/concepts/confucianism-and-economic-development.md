# Confucianism and Economic Development

## Summary

This chapter examines the dramatic shift in scholarly consensus regarding the relationship between Confucianism and economic modernization. Where early 20th-century scholars like Max Weber argued that Confucianism's emphasis on tradition and adjustment to the world constituted a barrier to capitalist industrialization, by the 1990s the prevailing view had reversed entirely—identifying Confucian cultural values, particularly the emphasis on education and family networks, as key drivers of the East Asian economic miracle.

## Key facts

- Max Weber, writing in 1919, argued that Confucianism resulted in "adjustment to the world" rather than the active mastery of nature promoted by Protestantism, making it incompatible with capitalist industrialization. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p451]
- By the 1990s, the consensus had shifted so that Confucianism was widely viewed as contributing to the East Asian economic miracle, with scholars emphasizing Confucian values like education and work ethic. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p451]
- The Chinese family, which Weber considered the central obstacle to economic progress, came to be seen by later commentators as a source of economic strength through family business networks. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p451]
