# Task-Relevant Maturity

## Summary

Task-relevant maturity is a concept that determines the appropriate management style for a given subordinate in a given situation. It refers specifically to how much experience a subordinate has with the specific task at hand, not their general experience, age, or overall competence. Effective management style varies from close to loose supervision as task-relevant maturity increases.

## Key facts

- Task-relevant maturity refers to how much experience a subordinate has with the specific task at hand, not their general experience or age. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L678-L680]
- The frequency of one-on-ones should depend on task-relevant maturity, with more frequent meetings for subordinates inexperienced in specific situations. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L678-L681]
- The most effective management style in a specific instance varies from very close to very loose supervision as a subordinate's task maturity increases. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L681-L683]
- Monitoring frequency should be based on experience with a specific task and prior performance with it, not on what a subordinate can do in general. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L572-L574]
- Task-relevant maturity (TRM) is a combination of achievement orientation, readiness to take responsibility, and education/training/experience, all specific to the task at hand. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1575-L1577]
- A person's TRM can be high in one job but low in another, and a change in working environment can cause TRM to drop. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1580-L1582]
- When TRM is low, the most effective management style is structured and task-oriented, telling the subordinate what, when, and how. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1590-L1592]
- When TRM is medium, the effective style shifts to individual-oriented with emphasis on two-way communication, support, and mutual reasoning. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1594-L1596]
- When TRM is high, manager involvement should be minimal, consisting primarily of establishing objectives and monitoring performance. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1598-L1600]
- Regardless of TRM level, a manager should always monitor a subordinate's work closely enough to avoid surprises—the difference between delegating and abdicating. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1600-L1602]

## Inferences

- Inference: Task-relevant maturity suggests that the same employee may need very different management approaches depending on which project they are assigned to.
- Inference: This concept provides a framework for deciding when to delegate versus when to closely supervise.
