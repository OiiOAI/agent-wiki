# Hip Extension

## Summary

Hip extension is the backward movement of the thigh away from the torso, which the authors identify as one of the most important physical capabilities for functional movement. Limited hip extension is common due to prolonged sitting and contributes to lower back pain, knee problems, and reduced athletic performance. The Couch Test assesses hip extension capability, and targeted mobilizations can restore range of motion.

## Key facts

- Hip extension is essential for walking, running, standing from a squat, throwing, and powerful forward movement. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L575-L577]
- Limited hip extension causes compensatory movements such as knee stiffness, ankle tracking problems, and "banana back" (excessive lumbar arching). [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L580-L582]
- The psoas and iliacus muscles, when stiff from lack of extension, tug on the spine and create flexion and banana back posture. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L590-L593]
- Glutes (the largest muscles in the body) control the pelvis to prevent forward pitching and banana back; glute weakness is associated with knee injuries, chronic lower back pain, shin pain, and falls among the elderly. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L605-L610]
- The Couch Test assesses both hip extension and quadriceps range of motion through three progressively difficult positions. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L535-L540]
- When hip extension is restricted and the foot turns out to compensate, toe push-off becomes negligible during walking. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L598-L600]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hip extension mobilization may be the single most impactful physical practice for most people due to the prevalence of sedentary behavior.
