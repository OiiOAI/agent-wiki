# Second Messenger

## Summary

Second messengers are intracellular signaling molecules produced in response to receptor activation that diffuse within cells to activate effector enzymes. They mediate slow modulatory synaptic actions that can last from seconds to minutes and are essential for synaptic plasticity.

## Key facts

- The number of substances acting as second messengers is much fewer than the number of transmitters, with approximately 100 transmitters but far fewer well-characterized second messengers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p292]
- Second messengers are divided into intracellular messengers (confined to the cell of origin) and transcellular messengers (which cross the cell membrane to act on neighboring cells). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p292]
- All G protein-coupled receptors consist of a single polypeptide with seven characteristic membrane-spanning regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p293]
- Despite differences in their specific pathways, second-messenger cascades share a common design: receptor activation activates G proteins, which engage primary effectors that produce second messengers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p293]
