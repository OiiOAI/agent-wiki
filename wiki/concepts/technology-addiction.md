# Technology Addiction

## Summary

Technology addiction is a type of behavioral addiction characterized by an inability to limit, regulate, or control one's use of digital technology. It is similar to substance addiction but without the physical substance dependency. Digital technology addiction appeals to the brain's pleasure center, producing dopamine and serotonin releases that create short-term rewards encouraging repeated behavior. Various types include social media addiction, gaming addiction, video consumption addiction, online shopping addiction, and online gambling addiction.

## Key facts

- Technology addiction can be classed as a type of behavioral addiction, placed in the same category as other addictive behaviors such as sex and gambling. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p27]
- Technology addiction is characterized by an inability to limit, regulate, or control one's use of digital technology, and this lack of control is progressive. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p27]
- Every time you use or overuse technology, you get the equivalent of dopamine and/or serotonin release, which experts say is the same high or pleasure you get from gambling, drugs, and other addictive substances. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p30]
- Researchers estimate that people check their smartphones or mobile devices every six minutes on average, with adults checking phones an average of 150 times a day. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p35]
- Internet addiction has been found to create structural changes to the brain and cause chemical alterations, affecting a person's ability to connect with attention, cognitive control, emotional processing, and decision-making. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p36]
- Parts of the brain that deal with a person's ability to pay attention (e.g., dorsolateral prefrontal cortex and orbitofrontal cortex) tend to have reduced amounts of grey matter in individuals who exhibit internet addiction. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p36]
- Common causes of technology addiction include depression, the friend and gang effect, social anxiety and shyness, and expression of other addictions. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p34]
- Effects of technology addiction include: obesity, insomnia, carpal tunnel syndrome, headaches, back pain, loneliness, mood swings, anxiety, guilt, and depression. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p37]
