# Biological Information

## Summary

Biological information refers to the physical instantiation of patterns that carry meaning for living organisms. Mitchell distinguishes between Shannon information (technical measure of uncertainty/complexity) and meaningful information (where physical states inside organisms correlate with and inform action regarding external states). Meaning in biological systems requires interpretation by the organism's internal configuration shaped by natural selection.

## Key facts

- Information is physical—it must be carried in the physical arrangement of matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p62]
- Shannon information measures coding complexity and redundancy but does not refer to what a message might mean. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p64]
- Relative information exists when the physical state inside cells corresponds to or reflects something about the state of the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p65]
- Meaning crucially involves the receiver—symbols do not have meaning in themselves but must be interpreted. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p65]
- For information to have causal power, the possibility must exist that it could have been different—this connects to questions of indeterminacy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p63]
