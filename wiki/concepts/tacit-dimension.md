# Tacit Dimension

## Summary

Michael Polanyi's concept of the Tacit Dimension describes how knowledge has two levels of awareness – focal and subsidiary. Tacit knowledge is implicit, the patterns of categories containing theories, methods, feelings, values and skill that cannot be explicitly articulated but are essential to understanding. Polanyi argued that the knowledge underlying explicit knowledge is more fundamental.

## Key facts

- Polanyi advanced the idea that there are two levels of awareness – focal and subsidiary – and human beings employ both in knowing and understanding. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p96]
- Polanyi termed the integrating power of the mind 'tacit knowing' – it is implicit and the knowledge underlying explicit knowledge is more fundamental; all knowledge is either tacit or rooted in tacit knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p96]
- Polanyi criticized the ideal of objectivity, asserting that science stems from guesswork and intuition and that advances often have the character of 'gestalt' when people suddenly 'see' something that had been meaningless before. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p97]
- Polanyi described science as a 'Republic of Science' in which scientists freely making their own choices are members of a closely knit group with internal tension between conformity and originality. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p98]
- Polanyi extended the principles of the Republic of Science to free societies, arguing that an open society must advance by supporting independent initiatives that self-renew through cultivating originality among followers. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd incorporated Polanyi's tacit dimension into his emphasis on organizational culture, trust, open communications, and tolerance for failure in effective organizations.
