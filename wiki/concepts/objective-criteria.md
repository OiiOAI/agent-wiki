# Using Objective Criteria

## Summary

The fourth principle of principled negotiation is to insist that the result be based on some objective standard rather than the will of either side. Deciding based on will is costly and tends to reward intransigence. Objective criteria such as market value, expert opinion, custom, or law should determine outcomes when interests are opposed. The approach commits to reaching a solution based on principle, not pressure—open to reason but closed to threats.

## Key facts

- Deciding conflicts on the basis of will is costly; no negotiation is likely to be efficient or amicable if you pit your will against theirs. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p43]
- When interests conflict, insist on a result based on some fair standard independent of the naked will of either side, such as market value, expert opinion, custom, or law. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p11]
- Principled negotiation produces wise agreements amicably and efficiently, and agreements consistent with precedent are less vulnerable to attack. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p43]
- Never yield to pressure, only to principle; invite the other side to state their reasoning, suggest objective criteria, and refuse to budge except on this basis. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p47]
