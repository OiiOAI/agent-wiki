# Mindfulness

## Summary

In Langer's framework, mindfulness is the simple process of actively noticing things—discovering novelty and recognizing that one did not know things as well as one thought one knew them. This is contrasted with the popular conception of mindfulness as a meditative or purely mental practice; Langer emphasizes that mindfulness is also a condition of the body and that noticing things affects physical health.

## Key facts

- Langer defines mindfulness as the simple process of actively noticing things, no meditation required, where one notices things not noticed before and recognizes gaps in prior knowledge. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p11]
- Langer argues that mindfulness is also a condition of the body, not solely a condition of the mind. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12]
- The book proposes that mindfulness interventions—such as noticing differences among out-group members—can reduce implicit racial bias by half on the Implicit Association Test. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p17-18]
- Mindfulness research over 40+ years shows that it is literally and figuratively enlivening and good for health. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p89]
