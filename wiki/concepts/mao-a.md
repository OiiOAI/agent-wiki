# MAO-A

## Summary

Monoamine oxidase-A, an enzyme that degrades serotonin and norepinephrine. The low-activity MAO-A variant has been erroneously called the 'warrior gene' in media and courts. Research shows it interacts strongly with childhood abuse to predict antisocial behavior, explaining why it has no main effect on aggression without environmental adversity.

## Key facts

- A Dutch family with an MAO-A gene mutation that eliminates the protein (causing serotonin accumulation) was characterized by varied antisocial and aggressive behaviors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p261]
- Low-activity MAO-A variants predict criminality, but only if coupled with high testosterone levels or with childhood sexual abuse. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p263]
- Prison sentences have been lessened in at least two cases because criminals had the 'warrior gene' variant of MAO-A, despite the effects being tiny and nonspecific. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p261]
- The Dutch family with MAO-A mutation showed not only aggression but also arson and exhibitionism, suggesting the gene does not specifically cause aggression. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p261]
- The MAO-A gene promoter is regulated by stress and glucocorticoids, showing how environment affects gene expression. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p263]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'warrior gene' label is misleading because MAO-A variants don't cause aggression in the absence of environmental adversity.
- Inference: The courtroom use of MAO-A genetics represents dangerous oversimplification of complex gene/environment interactions.
