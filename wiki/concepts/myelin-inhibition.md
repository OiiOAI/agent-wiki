# Myelin Inhibition of Axon Regeneration

## Summary

Fragments of central myelin are potent inhibitors of neurite outgrowth. Three major myelin proteins—Nogo, myelin-associated glycoprotein (MAG), and oligodendrocyte-myelin glycoprotein (OMgp)—bind to common membrane receptors NogoR and PirB, activating intracellular inhibitory pathways. Myelin also contains chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans (CSPG) from glial scars that suppress axon regeneration through PTP-sigma receptors.

## Key facts

- Fragments of central myelin are potent inhibitors of neurite outgrowth; preventing myelin formation enhances sprouting of spinal axon collaterals following injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1343]
- Nogo-A, MAG, and OMgp are myelin proteins that bind to Nogo receptor (NogoR) and PirB, activating intracellular mediators such as Rho and ROCK that inhibit axon growth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1344]
- Chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans (CSPG) are major components of the glial scar that suppress axon regeneration through interaction with receptor tyrosine phosphatase PTP-sigma. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1344]
- Treatment with chondroitinase, which breaks down CSPG sugar chains, promotes axon regeneration and functional recovery in animals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1345]
