# Anterior Insula

## Summary

The anterior insula is a brain region associated with processing negative emotional states and aversive experiences. In neuroeconomics studies of social decision-making, anterior insula activation is associated with responses to unfair treatment and the decision to reject unfavorable offers.

## Key facts

- Anterior insula activation is consistently seen in neuroimaging studies of pain and distress, hunger and thirst, and autonomic arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p73]
- Regions of bilateral anterior insula demonstrated sensitivity to the degree of unfairness of an offer, exhibiting significantly greater activation for a $9:$1 offer than an $8:$2 offer from a human partner. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p73]
- Higher insula response to an unfair offer was related to higher rejection rates of these offers in ultimatum game experiments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p73]
- Higher activation of right anterior insula correlates with greater likelihood of rejecting unfair offers, suggesting AI activation relates to emotional resentment of unfair offers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p212]
- Anterior insula activation is observed both when experiencing disgust and when observing others expressing disgust, supporting its role in empathic responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p257]
- AI activation has been linked to the emotional resistance to accept unfair offers in ultimatum games, representing the neural cost of accepting unfair offers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p214]
- The anterior insula is part of the pain matrix and shows isomorphic responses to self-experienced and observed pain in others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The anterior insula appears to mark social interactions as aversive, discouraging trust of partners who treat one unfairly.
- Inference: Anterior insula appears to be a critical hub for interoceptive awareness and emotional feeling that supports both first-person emotional experience and empathic understanding of others' emotional states
- Inference: The insula's role in multiple functions (pain, disgust, empathy, risk processing) suggests it may be a general-purpose integrator of affective significance rather than emotion-specific
