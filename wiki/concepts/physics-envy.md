# Physics Envy

## Summary

Physics envy is Munger's term for the excessive desire in economics to achieve the kind of precise, formulaic predictions that physics provides. He argues this led to adoption of the hard-form efficient market theory, which produced dangerously wrong conclusions like the idea that corporations should never buy back their own stock.

## Key facts

- Physics envy caused adoption of hard-form efficient market theory, from which conclusions like 'it can never be correct for any corporation to buy its own stock' were logically derived. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p393]
- Munger argues economics should emulate physics' basic ethos of full attribution, but its search for physics-like formulas is almost always wrong in economics. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p394]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger uses this concept to illustrate how the desire for false precision in complex systems like economics leads to worse outcomes than admitting uncertainty.
