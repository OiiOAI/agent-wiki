# BCG Growth-Share Matrix

## Summary

The BCG growth-share matrix, developed by Alan Zakon at Boston Consulting Group, categorized businesses into Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, and Dogs based on market growth and market share. Its real power was transcending financial analysis by changing the framework from capital budgeting to portfolio balance, fundamentally altering organizational power dynamics.

## Key facts

- The BCG matrix changed the framework from capital budgeting to portfolio balance, where some businesses' role was to generate cash while others absorbed it. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p165]
- Technical capital budgeting lent power to people skilled in finance and calculation, while portfolio frameworks redeployed power back to the top. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p165]
- Jack Welch used similar logic when he announced each GE business should be 'No. 1 or No. 2 in your industry' or face being 'fixed, sold, or closed.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p165]

## Inferences

- Inference: The matrix's influence came not from analytical precision but from its ability to change organizational conversations and power structures.
