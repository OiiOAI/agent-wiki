# Homo Symbolicus

## Summary

Homo symbolicus is a virtual species designation proposed by Deacon to refer to all hominid symbol users. Unlike biological species defined by genetic criteria, this nóo-species designation is based on the ability to participate in a common pool of symbolic information. It cuts across fossil phylogenies and includes members who share symbolic inheritance regardless of biological lineage.

## Key facts

- Homo symbolicus members are linked via a common pool of symbolic information that is as inaccessible to other species as are human genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p367]
- The designation refers to a dual inheritance system, where members derive all their symbolic traits from a common pool and contribute to its promulgation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p367]
- Biological correlates of symbolic communication, particularly brain size and internal organization, are evident in fossil skulls and can identify which species must be included in Homo symbolicus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p368]
- The origin of humanness can be defined as the point where tools became the principal source of selection on bodies and brains, which is the diagnostic trait of Homo symbolicus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p372]

## Inferences

- Inference: Homo symbolicus represents a form of information lineage distinct from genetic lineage, demonstrating that evolutionary phylogenies based solely on genes may miss crucial aspects of human evolution.
