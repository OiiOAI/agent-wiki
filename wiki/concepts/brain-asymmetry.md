# Brain Asymmetry

## Summary

The structural and functional differences between the left and right cerebral hemispheres. McGilchrist emphasizes that brain asymmetry is not merely a matter of accommodation but reflects meaningful functional specialization. The brain shows visible anatomical asymmetries including the planum temporale being 30% larger on the left, and a characteristic clockwise twist called Yakovlevian torque.

## Key facts

- The planum temporale is on average some 30 per cent larger on the left than on the right. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p43]
- The normal brain has been twisted about its central axis, wider on the left towards the back and wider on the right towards the front, an effect called Yakovlevian torque. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p43]
- Structure is at some level an expression of function, as accepted since John Hunter and reinforced by D'Arcy Thompson's work. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p43]
- Function is reflected in volume throughout the central nervous system, as illustrated by London cabbies having larger right posterior hippocampi due to navigational experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p44]
- The cerebral hemispheres show a highly consistent right-greater-than-left asymmetry from childhood to adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p55]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistent nature of structural asymmetries across individuals suggests they serve important functional purposes rather than being random.
