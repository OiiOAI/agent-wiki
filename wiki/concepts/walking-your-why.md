# Walking Your Why

## Summary

Walking Your Why is the third movement in the emotional agility framework. After calming mental processes and creating space between thoughts and thinker, individuals can focus on their core values and most important goals. This concept recognizes that recognizing, accepting, and distancing oneself from difficult emotions enables engagement with the 'take the long view' part of oneself that integrates thinking and feeling with long-term values and aspirations.

## Key facts

- Walking Your Why involves focusing on core values and most important goals after uncluttering and calming mental processes. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p18-19]
- Core values provide a compass that guides thousands of daily decision moments, called choice points. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p19]
- People make thousands of decisions every day that should align with their core values. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p19]
- Walking your why means living by your own personal set of values that give you meaning and satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p95]
- Values are described as facets on a diamond—turning one to face you may move another away, but it remains part of the whole. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p100]
- Values allow freedom from social comparisons; by one's own definition of success, one can be comfortable with who one is. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p100]
- Research shows that values help access greater levels of willpower and grit and safeguard from negative social contagion. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p104]

## Inferences

- Inference: Walking one's why requires first identifying what one genuinely cares about, which may differ from what one thinks one should care about.
