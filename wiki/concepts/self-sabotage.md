# Self-Sabotage

## Summary

Self-sabotage is the phenomenon where people unconsciously create the negative circumstances they fear most. Driven by a constricted, fearful attitude, self-sabotage manifests as blaming others for mistakes, avoiding challenges, and creating circumstances that confirm negative beliefs about oneself. Chekhov's case demonstrates that transforming one's attitude can break the self-sabotaging cycle.

## Key facts

- Greene defines self-sabotage as occurring when people with fearful attitudes stop themselves from taking chances, blame others for mistakes, and unconsciously create circumstances they fear. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p252]
- A fearful attitude has a self-fulfilling dynamic: insecurities push people away, making isolation worse, which deepens the negative attitude. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p268]
- Chekhov recognized that his negative emotions from childhood (resentment toward his father) had created a self-imposed prison that he could only escape by changing his attitude. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p262-263]
- Chekhov's path to freedom came through adopting a generous spirit—accepting and understanding others, which liberated his mind from obsessive and petty emotions. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p263]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-sabotage patterns often originate in childhood trauma and are reinforced by the inability to recognize one's own role in creating negative circumstances.
