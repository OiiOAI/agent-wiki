# Depression as Body-Budgeting Disorder

## Summary

Lisa Feldman Barrett proposes that depression is best understood as a disorder of allostasis—a disorder involving mistaken forms of bodily prediction involving energy regulation. The core issue appears to be aberrant precision-weighting, where overly weighted expectations and underweighted new information create a locked-in state resistant to updating.

## Key facts

- Barrett suggests depression involves mistaken forms of bodily prediction involving energy regulation, constituting a disorder of allostasis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p105]
- Chronic depression involves a resistance to updating negative expectations when confronted with good evidence of positive outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p107]
- Barrett describes the state in depression as a locked-in brain where abnormally high precision on prior negative beliefs robs unanticipated positive information of the power to alter the inner model. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p107]
- Healthy controls but not depressed participants rapidly altered their expectations after unexpectedly positive feedback when the test was described as a well-established reliable indicator of ability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p108]

## Inferences

- Inference: The double whammy of poor learning and poor energy regulation makes sense if we suppose the core issue is aberrant precision-weighting [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p106]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise genetic and environmental factors contributing to the precision-weighting abnormalities in depression require further research
