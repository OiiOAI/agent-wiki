# The Wheel on the Suitcase Problem

## Summary

The wheel on the suitcase problem illustrates how implementation does not necessarily follow from invention. It took about 6,000 years between the invention of the wheel and the wheeled suitcase, and three decades after putting a man on the moon before anyone thought to apply wheels to luggage.

## Key facts

- It took close to six thousand years between the invention of the wheel and the brilliant implementation of wheels on suitcases despite billions of hours spent by travelers schlepping heavy luggage. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p240]
- The Mayans had wheels on children's toys but didn't make the leap to practical applications like carts for moving stone slabs for pyramids. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p242]
- The Greeks had an operating steam engine (the aeolipyle) for amusement, but it took the Industrial Revolution to discover this earlier discovery for practical use. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p242]
- David Wooton relates a gap of two centuries between discovery of germs and acceptance of germs as cause of disease, thirty years between germ theory of putrefaction and antisepsis, and sixty years between antisepsis and drug therapy. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p244]
- Taleb calls things 'half-invented' that require a visionary to figure out what to do with a discovery—Steve Jobs exemplified this by putting the computer mouse and graphical interface on the desk. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p242]

## Inferences

- Inference: The implementation gap exists because identifying options and having the wisdom to realize what one has on one's hands requires rationality that doesn't automatically follow from intelligence or discovery.
