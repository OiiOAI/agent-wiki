# Bayesian Brain Hypothesis

## Summary

The Bayesian brain hypothesis holds that the brain is an inference engine that optimizes probabilistic representations of the causes of its sensory input, treating perception as statistical (Bayesian) inference. It is the starting point of the 'low road' to Active Inference. Under this view, the brain does not simply encode sensory stimuli passively but actively constructs probabilistic models of the world that generate predictions about sensory observations. These models encode beliefs about both observable and non-observable (hidden) variables. The hypothesis provides a normative framework for understanding perception, but Active Inference extends it by also treating action, planning, and learning as Bayesian inference problems, and by using variational approximations (variational free energy) that are biologically more plausible than exact Bayesian inference.

## Inferences

- Inference: The Bayesian brain hypothesis treats inference as subjective—the results of inference are not necessarily accurate in any objective sense, because organisms operate with limited computational resources and with generative models that may not correspond to the true generative process.
