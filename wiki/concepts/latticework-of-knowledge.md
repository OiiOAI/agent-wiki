# Latticework of Knowledge

## Summary

The Latticework of Knowledge is a metaphor used by Charles T. Munger to describe how knowledge from diverse disciplines should be interconnected and readily available for application. This approach, central to his Multiple Mental Models framework, emphasizes building a broad, integrated network of understanding that can be drawn upon when making decisions or solving problems.

## Key facts

- Munger's lessons hang together in a coherent 'latticework' of knowledge, available for recall and use when needed. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p5]
- The storyteller serves as a purveyor of complex and detailed information in conveying the latticework of ideas. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p5]

## Inferences

- Inference: The latticework structure implies that pulling on one strand strengthens rather than weakens the overall network, unlike siloed knowledge.
