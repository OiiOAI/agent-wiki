# House-Money Effect

## Summary

The house-money effect describes how people treat money differently depending on its source. Windfall gains (lottery winnings, found money) are spent more frivolously than earned income, leading to riskier financial behavior and poorer outcomes.

## Key facts

- Richard Thaler coined the term "house-money effect" for the tendency to treat windfall money more frivolously than earned income. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p186]
- In Thaler's experiment, 70% of students who had "won" $30 risked it in a gamble, versus only 43% of students choosing between guaranteed $30 or a gamble with equal expected value. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p186]
- Marketing uses the house-money effect through sign-up bonuses, frequent flyer miles, and free credits. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p187]
