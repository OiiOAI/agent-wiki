# Translation of Freud's Besetzung as 'Cathexis'

## Summary

The translation of Freud's German term Besetzung as 'cathexis' by James Strachey has been widely criticized by psychoanalytic scholars. Besetzung, derived from cathechein ('to occupy'), carries multiple meanings in everyday German including occupation, charge, investment, and engagement. Critics argue that Strachey's neologism 'cathexis' loses the power, vitality, polyvalence, and personal meaning of the original term, while imposing military connotations (cf. Besatzung/garrison) that may not have been Freud's primary intent. Freud himself initially disliked the invented word when Strachey introduced it in 1922 but later became reconciled to it, even using it in an article for the Encyclopaedia Britannica.

## Key facts

- Strachey introduced 'cathexis' in 1922, an invention Freud initially disliked but later accepted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7550]
- Underwood (2004) called Strachey's neologism 'the cardinal sin of translation' for inventing a word to convey presumed meaning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7550]
- Critics prefer ordinary English equivalents such as charge, occupation, engagement, investment, filling, and interest. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7550]
- Michael Balint wrote to Strachey in 1959 that Besetzung was 'highly unprecise but stimulating', contrasting it with the exact but non-stimulating 'cathexis'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7550]
- Freud used the term 'cathexis' himself in an article for the Encyclopaedia Britannica in 1926. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7551]

## Inferences

- Inference: The controversy over Besetzung/cathexis reflects a broader tension in translating Freud between preserving ambiguity and creating precision.
- Inference: Strachey's decision to coin a neologism may have been motivated by the polyvalence of the German term, as Meisel & Kendrick (1985) suggested.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which military connotations were central to Freud's usage remains debated (Grubrich-Simitis disputes the 'charge' electrical connotation).
