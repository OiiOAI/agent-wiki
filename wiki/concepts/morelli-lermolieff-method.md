# Morelli/Lermolieff method

## Summary

An art criticism method developed by Ivan Lermolieff (pseudonym of physician Giovanni Morelli) that revolutionized art authentication by focusing on minor, overlooked details such as fingernails, earlobes, and halos rather than the overall impression of a work. Freud identified this technique as closely related to the methodology of psychoanalysis.

## Key facts

- Ivan Lermolieff was a pseudonym for Giovanni Morelli, an Italian physician who died in 1891 with the rank of Senator of the Kingdom of Italy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4325]
- The Morelli method insists on diverting attention from general impression and main features, instead laying stress on the significance of minor details. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4325]
- Freud characterized Morelli's method as closely related to the technique of psychoanalysis, which similarly divines secret and concealed things from despised or overlooked details. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4325]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud drew an explicit parallel between art criticism and psychoanalysis, suggesting that both disciplines uncover hidden truths by attending to details that are typically neglected or overlooked by conventional approaches.
