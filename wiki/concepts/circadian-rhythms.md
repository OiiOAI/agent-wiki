# Circadian Rhythms

## Summary

Circadian rhythms are 24-hour internal cycles managed by the body clock, regulating sleeping and eating patterns, hormone production, temperature, alertness, mood, and digestion. These rhythms evolved to work in harmony with Earth's rotation and are set primarily by external cues, particularly daylight. Light is the most important time setter for body clocks, with blue light during daytime being beneficial but the same wavelength from electronic devices at night being harmful.

- A circadian rhythm is a 24-hour internal cycle managed by the body clock that regulates sleeping and eating patterns, hormone production, temperature, alertness, mood, and digestion. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p20]
- Circadian rhythms are ingrained within us, being the product of millions of years of evolution. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p21]
- Melatonin, a hormone that regulates sleep, is produced in the pineal gland which responds to light. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p21]
- Peak sleeping time occurs around 2-3 a.m., mirrored by afternoon sleepiness twelve hours later. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p22]
- Blue light is not inherently bad; it is only badly timed light. Daylight is full of blue light and during the day it suppresses melatonin and improves alertness. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p23]
- Professor Russell Foster states that long-term acting against the circadian clock can lead to serious health problems. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p24]
