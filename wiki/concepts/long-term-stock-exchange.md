# Long-Term Stock Exchange

## Summary

The Long-Term Stock Exchange (LTSE) is a proposed new stock exchange designed to trade stocks of companies organized to sustain long-term thinking. Eric Ries proposed this as a solution to the intense short-term pressure from public markets that prevents established companies from investing consistently in innovation. Companies on the LTSE would report using innovation accounting on internal entrepreneurship efforts, and trading would have higher transaction costs to minimize day trading while allowing greater corporate governance freedom for long-term investments.

## Key facts

- The Long-Term Stock Exchange is designed to trade stocks of companies organized to sustain long-term thinking, addressing intense pressure from public markets for short-term profitability and growth targets. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p261]
- Companies on the LTSE would report using innovation accounting on their internal entrepreneurship efforts, including revenue from products that did not exist a few years earlier. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p261]
- Trading on the LTSE would have much higher transaction costs and fees to minimize day trades and massive price swings, in exchange for greater corporate governance freedom for management to pursue long-term investments. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p261]
- Executive compensation in LTSE companies would be tied to the company's long-term performance. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p261]
