# P3 wave

## Summary

The P3 wave (also P300) is a slow positive voltage deflection in the EEG that appears approximately 270-500 milliseconds after stimulus onset and serves as a signature of conscious perception. It reflects the global ignition of prefrontal and parietal cortices when information crosses the threshold for awareness. The P3 is distinct from earlier visual potentials (P1, N1) that are present even for subliminal stimuli.

## Key facts

- The P3 wave emerges around 270ms after stimulus onset and peaks between 350-500ms, representing a late slow wave unique to conscious access. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p157]
- P3 amplitude reflects the amplification of brain activity when conscious perception occurs—it is absent or minimal on unconscious trials even when identical physical stimuli are presented. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p164]
- The P3 wave originates from simultaneous activation of a large circuit with nodes in occipital, parietal, and prefrontal lobes, indicating brain-wide ignition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p168]
- Consciousness cannot experience two ignitions simultaneously—when the first P3 wave is large and long, the second is suppressed, explaining attentional limitations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p158]
- During masking experiments, the P3 wave appears discontinuously at a threshold of ~50ms delay, reflecting the nonlinear phase transition of conscious perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p166]

## Inferences

- Inference: The P3 wave's sensitivity to conscious access makes it useful as an objective neural marker for distinguishing conscious from unconscious processing in experimental settings.
