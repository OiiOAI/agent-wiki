# Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex

## Summary

The ventromedial prefrontal cortex (orbital and medial sectors of the prefrontal lobe) is identified as a critical brain region where reasoning/decision-making and emotion/feeling 'intersect.' Damage to this region consistently produces impairments in personal and social decision-making while preserving intelligence, language, memory, and basic motor function. This region works in concert with the amygdala and somatosensory cortices to integrate body signals with cognitive processes.

## Key facts

- The ventromedial prefrontal region is damaged in Phineas Gage and in modern patients like Elliot, producing similar deficits in social behavior and decision-making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p52]
- Bilateral prefrontal damage that includes the ventromedial sector is consistently associated with impairments of reasoning/decision-making and emotion/feeling, against an otherwise largely intact neuropsychological profile. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p80]
- The prefrontal cortices work in concert across many levels of neuronal organization, with both 'high-level' and 'low-level' brain regions from prefrontal cortices to hypothalamus and brain stem cooperating in the making of reason. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p14]
- Serotonin-2 receptors are concentrated in the ventromedial prefrontal cortex and amygdala, and high receptor levels correlate with socially cooperative behavior in monkeys. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p76]
- The ventromedial prefrontal cortex is identified as the critical neural system for somatic-marker signaling and is coextensive with the system critical for secondary emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p201]
- The prefrontal cortices receive signals from all sensory regions where images constituting thoughts are formed, including somatosensory cortices representing past and current body states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p201]
- The prefrontal cortices receive signals from bioregulatory brain sectors including neurotransmitter nuclei in the brain stem (dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin), basal forebrain (acetylcholine), amygdala, anterior cingulate, and hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p202]
- The ventromedial prefrontal cortices send signals to autonomic nervous system effectors and can promote chemical responses associated with emotion, as demonstrated by Walle Nauta. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p204]
- Damage to the ventromedial region compromises the ability to acquire and deploy somatic markers, resulting in 'acquired sociopathy' with impaired decision-making but rarely violence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p198]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ventromedial prefrontal cortex likely holds and manipulates representations of body states that become conscious as feelings, and these representations serve to 'mark' decision options with positive or negative valence.
- Inference: The ventromedial prefrontal cortex is in a privileged position because it receives signals about existing factual knowledge, innate biological regulatory preferences, and previous/current body states modified by that knowledge and those preferences.
