# Principle of Participation

## Summary

The Principle of Participation is Rawls's application of the equal liberty principle to political procedure. It requires that all citizens have an equal right to take part in and determine the outcome of the constitutional process. The principle requires equal representation (one elector one vote), fair value of political liberty (so that those with lesser means can still effectively participate), and access to public office. Constitutional mechanisms that limit majority rule are justified insofar as they protect other liberties.

## Key facts

- The principle of participation requires that all citizens have an equal right to take part in, and to determine the outcome of, the constitutional process that establishes the laws with which they are to comply. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p206]
- The principle requires that measures be taken to preserve the fair value of political liberty, including keeping property and wealth widely distributed and providing public monies to encourage free public discussion. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p210]
- Historically, one of the main defects of constitutional government has been the failure to insure the fair value of political liberty through necessary corrective steps. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p210]

## Inferences

- Inference: The principle of participation reflects the transfer of the equal representation idea from the original position to the constitution as the highest-order system of social rules for making rules.
