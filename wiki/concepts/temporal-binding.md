# Temporal Binding

## Summary

Temporal binding is the neurological mechanism by which independently operating neural mechanisms subserving sensory and interoceptive stimuli are linked together through temporal conjunction—synchronous activation of spatially disparate neurons that creates unity of perception from fragmented sensory inputs. The 40 Hz oscillation is proposed as the global signal that implements this binding.

## Key facts

- Temporal binding proposes that neurons firing together wire together and bind together through temporal coherence. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p121]
- Temporal coherence is the mechanism that underlies perceptual unity, binding together independently derived sensory components called cognitive binding. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p121]
- 40 Hz gamma oscillations synchronize spatially disparate cortical columns during visual processing, binding components of a singular cognitive object. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p123]
- The 40 Hz coherent activity reflects resonant properties of the thalamocortical system endowed with intrinsic 40 Hz oscillatory activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p124]
- Synchronous activation of retinal ganglion cells entering the optic nerve is close to synchronous on to the thalamus, despite varying axon distances. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p127]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of temporal binding addresses how the brain creates singular perceptual entities from multiple specialized neurons, each representing only fragments of reality.
