# Bottling

## Summary

Bottling is a default coping mechanism where individuals push unwanted emotions aside and get on with things. Bottlers tend to shove away uncomfortable feelings because they view them as weaknesses or distractions. Research shows that attempting to minimize or ignore thoughts and emotions only serves to amplify them—a phenomenon demonstrated by the 'white bear' effect. Bottling leads to emotional leakage where suppressed emotions surface in unintended ways. Men are more likely to bottle than women.

## Key facts

- Bottlers push emotions to the side and get on with things, often believing being less than bright is a sign of weakness. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p42-43]
- Daniel Wegner's research showed that trying not to think about white bears actually made people think about them more. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p44]
- Suppressed emotions inevitably surface in unintended ways, a process psychologists call emotional leakage. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p44]
- Bottling can have a negative effect on relationships as partners may feel the bottler doesn't care. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p45]
- Research shows bottling increases other people's blood pressure even if they don't know the bottler is bottling. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p45]
