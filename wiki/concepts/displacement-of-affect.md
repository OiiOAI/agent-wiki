# Displacement of Affect

## Summary

Displacement of affect is a psychological mechanism described by Freud in which an emotional charge detaches from its original unconscious ideational content and becomes attached to a different, often more accessible idea. In the Rat Man case, Freud illustrated how self-reproach attaches to a conscious but trivial content rather than to the unknown unconscious material that actually generated it.

## Key facts

- When there is a dissociation between an affect and its ideational content, the layman says the affect is exaggerated, but the analytic physician says the affect is justified but belongs to some other unconscious content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3297]
- The known ideational content has only got into its actual position owing to a false connection, much as police arrest a wrong person when they cannot catch the right murderer. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3297]
- In displacement of affect, the affect cannot be destroyed by logical argument because it belongs to an unconscious content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3297]
- The mechanism in obsessional neurosis differs from hysteria: instead of being forgotten, the trauma is deprived of its affective cathexis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3312]
