# Negativity Bias

## Summary

Negativity bias is the psychological phenomenon where negative events have greater impact than positive events of equal magnitude. This bias appears to be evolutionarily selected because detecting threats (predators, toxins, cheaters) was more critical for survival than detecting benefits.

## Key facts

- Negative stimuli raise blood pressure, cardiac output, and heart rate, and grab attention more effectively than positive stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p131]
- People detect angry faces faster than happy faces in neutral crowds, reflecting evolutionary prioritization of threat detection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p131]
- Students judged that a person would need to save 25 lives at personal risk to be forgiven for murder, showing extreme negativity to immoral acts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p131]
- Rozin and Royzman identified four components of negativity bias's adaptive value: potency of negative events, their complexity, their sudden onset, and their contagiousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p131]
