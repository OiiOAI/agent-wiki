# Causal Diagrams

## Summary

Causal diagrams are dot-and-arrow pictures that summarize scientific knowledge about causal relationships. Dots represent variables of interest and arrows represent known or suspected causal relationships, indicating which variables 'listen to' which others. They are a core tool of the Causal Revolution.

## Key facts

- Causal diagrams consist of dots representing variables and arrows representing causal relationships between them. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L77]
- A variable X is defined as a cause of Y if Y 'listens' to X and determines its value in response. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L111]
- Causal diagrams can be read to extract simplifying assumptions about the data-generating process. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L110]
- Pearl considers causal diagrams superior to mathematical equations or assumption lists due to their transparency. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L109]
- Causal diagrams are the computational core of the causal inference engine, enabling machines to answer questions from all three rungs of the Ladder of Causation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L290-L305]
- The 'surgery' operation on causal diagrams involves removing all arrows pointing to an intervened variable to model the effect of deliberate actions. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L395-L420]
- The structure of causal diagrams often enables estimation of causal and counterfactual relationships even when mathematical details are left unspecified. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L440-L450]
- Once a causal diagram is established, the same estimand can be applied to new data without repeating the entire analysis, demonstrating robustness. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L450-L460]
- Causal diagrams are childlike in simplicity—humans can communicate knowledge easily with dot-and-arrow diagrams, suggesting brains may use similar representations. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L380-L395]

## Inferences

- Inference: Causal diagrams are the 'language of knowledge' component of Pearl's dual-language approach to causal inference, paired with the symbolic 'language of queries.'
- Inference: The book argues that causal diagrams succeed because they carry causal information—when constructed by asking about direct causes rather than mere associations, they enable causal reasoning capabilities.
