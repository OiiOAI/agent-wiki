# Predictive Modeling

## Summary

The brain learns a predictive model of the world, continuously predicting what sensory inputs will occur next. This prediction is intrinsic and never stops, serving an essential role in learning. When predictions match reality, the model is accurate; when they mispredict, attention is drawn to the error and the model is updated. The neocortex makes thousands of parallel predictions simultaneously.

## Key facts

- The brain continuously predicts what its inputs will be; prediction is an intrinsic property that never stops. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p42]
- When the brain's predictions are verified, it means the brain's model of the world is accurate. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p42]
- A mis-prediction causes attention to be drawn to the error and triggers an update to the model. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p42]
- We are unaware of most predictions unless the input does not match, at which point the mismatch becomes conscious. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p42]
- The brain learns its model of the world by observing how its inputs change over time, requiring active movement and exploration. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p44]
- In 2010, Hawkins discovered that predictions are represented inside neurons as dendrite spikes, not as signals sent to other neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p54]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since predictions are stored within neurons rather than transmitted, this explains why we are consciously unaware of most predictions—only errors reach conscious awareness.
