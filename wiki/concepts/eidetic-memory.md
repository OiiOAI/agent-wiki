# Eidetic Memory Technique

## Summary

A memory system based on vivid, stable visual imagery that S. developed and refined throughout his life. Initially relying on direct visual persistence, he later developed techniques including: (1) enlarging images for better perception, (2) abbreviating images to symbolic forms, (3) 'semanticizing' meaningless sounds by attaching meaning to syllables through associations. These methods allowed him to memorize unlimited material including foreign languages, mathematical formulas, and nonsense syllables.

## Key facts

- S. initially recalled tables of numbers by continuing to 'see' them exactly as they appeared on the board, requiring only 35-40 seconds to imprint a 20-number table. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p25]
- S. distributed images along mental routes or streets, 'placing' images at houses, gates, and store windows, then 'walking' along these routes to recall series forward or backward. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p41]
- S. developed abbreviated images where instead of remembering a full scene, he selected one symbolic detail—for example, for 'horseman' he would image only a foot in a spur. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p51]
- S. could memorize incomprehensible Latin phrases like 'Ibi bene ubi patria' by breaking them into syllables and attaching meaningful associations to each. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p53]
- S. reproduced a nonsense mathematical formula exactly after seven minutes of study and could still recall it precisely fifteen years later. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p49]
- The technique of 'semanticizing' sounds involved basing images on phonetic qualities while using synesthetic reactions to guarantee accurate recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p53]

## Inferences

- Inference: S.'s technique differed fundamentally from conventional mnemonic devices—he relied on figurative, synesthetic processing rather than logical organization of material.
