# Anxiety (Freudian theory)

## Summary

Freud's theory of anxiety evolved significantly across his works. In this 1926 revision, Freud proposed that anxiety is not a product of transformed libido but rather an affective signal produced by the ego to warn of impending danger. Anxiety reproduces the trauma of birth and serves to prepare the ego for situations of helplessness. Different forms of anxiety correspond to different developmental stages and danger situations: loss of object (early infancy), castration anxiety (phallic phase), and fear of the superego (latency period).

## Key facts

- Anxiety is primarily located in the ego, not in the id; it is an affective state that can only be felt by the organized ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6357]
- Anxiety serves as a signal to the pleasure-unpleasure agency, preparing the ego to inhibit or avoid dangerous processes in the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6344]
- Realistic anxiety differs from neurotic anxiety: the former responds to known external dangers, while the latter responds to unknown internal drive dangers. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6379]
- Anxiety can be produced automatically in traumatic situations analogous to birth, or can be intentionally reproduced by the ego as a warning signal. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6355]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanism by which the ego produces the anxiety signal remains unspecified in Freudian metapsychology.
