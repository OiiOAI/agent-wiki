# Buttonhook Turn

## Summary

The buttonhook turn was a maneuver demonstrated by the YF-16 during the lightweight fighter fly-off that convinced pilots it was superior to the YF-17. The maneuver allows an aircraft to snap from one direction to another almost instantaneously, enabling pilots to force pursuers to overshoot. The ability to perform fast transients became the foundation of Boyd's research into 'quickness' or 'agility' as a combat quality separate from energy-maneuverability.

## Key facts

- Pilots described the YF-16 as 'born to turn and burn—the most nimble little banking and yanking aircraft the world had ever seen.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p301]
- The buttonhook turn changed energy dumping from a desperation maneuver into a 'tactic of choice,' since the F-16 could recover energy so quickly through 'dumping and pumping.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p302]
- Boyd was puzzled that E-M data and computer modeling predicted a much closer contest than the unanimous pilot preference for the YF-16. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p301]
- Following the fly-off, Boyd researched 'asymmetric fast transients' in a briefing called 'New Conception for Air-to-Air Combat,' returning to concepts of 'quickness' he had first considered during F-86 studies. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p303]

## Inferences

- Inference: The buttonhook turn revealed a limitation in the Energy-Maneuverability theory that Boyd openly acknowledged, showing his willingness to pursue new observations rather than defend existing work.
