# Two-Minute Rule

## Summary

The Two-Minute Rule states that any action which will take less than two minutes to complete should be done immediately at the moment it is defined, rather than being added to a list for later. This prevents small tasks from accumulating and clogging the workflow system, while also providing immediate psychological relief.

## Key facts

- If an action will take less than two minutes, it should be done at the moment it is defined. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p72]
- The Two-Minute Rule was identified as one of the few exceptions to GTD's modular approach that must be embraced fully. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p14]
- If an actionable item can be completed in two minutes or less, it should be done immediately when first encountered. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p182]
- The rationale for the two-minute rule is that it marks the point where tracking an item takes longer than simply doing it. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p182]
- The two-minute rule should be applied primarily when processing new input such as in-tray items, interactions, or hallway conversations. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p184]
- At least 30 percent of actionable emails typically require less than two minutes to respond and dispatch. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p184]

## Inferences

- Inference: The two-minute rule reduces cognitive load by eliminating the need to track trivial tasks.
