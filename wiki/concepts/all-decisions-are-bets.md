# All Decisions Are Bets

## Summary

The concept that every choice we make—accepting a job, buying a house, ordering dinner, or raising a child—is fundamentally a bet. Each decision involves choosing among alternatives, risking resources (time, money, happiness, health), assessing probabilities of outcomes, and betting that the chosen path will yield better results than rejected alternatives. Not choosing is itself a bet against the paths not taken.

## Key facts

- Every decision commits us to a course of action that eliminates acting on other alternatives, creating opportunity cost. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L530-L535]
- In most decisions, we are betting against future versions of ourselves that we are not choosing. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L550-L553]
- Even a set salary is not guaranteed—you could get laid off, hate the job and quit, or the company could fail. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L518-L521]
- Hiring an employee, like offering a bet, is not a riskless choice—betting on hiring the wrong person can have huge costs. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L538-L541]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book argues that treating decisions as explicit bets forces us to acknowledge uncertainty and inventory potential upside and downside, improving decision quality.
