# Fairness in Economic Transactions

## Summary

Research by Thaler, Knetsch, and Kahneman on fairness norms in economic transactions revealed that people distinguish between losses and gains in evaluating fairness: current prices, wages, and rents serve as reference points that should not be violated. Raising prices to exploit increased demand (e.g., snow shovels after a blizzard) is considered unfair even though it is economically rational; cutting wages in a labor market downturn is considered unfair even when market conditions warrant it.

## Key facts

- In a survey, 82% of respondents rated a hardware store's price hike from $15 to $20 for snow shovels after a major snowstorm as 'unfair' or 'very unfair,' even though the price increase responded to increased demand—because the pre-storm price was the reference point. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p396]
- 83% of respondents rated cutting a loyal employee's wage from $9 to $7 per hour as unfair, even though the market wage for the same work was $7, because the existing wage was the reference point. However, 73% considered it acceptable to hire a new employee at $7—the same market wage was acceptable for a new hire because no existing relationship was being violated. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p396]
- Companies facing threats to their profits are permitted by fairness norms to pass costs to others (workers, consumers), but they are not expected to sacrifice profits to avoid imposing costs. This 'dual entitlement' principle explains why wage cuts during a downturn are tolerated but price increases during a supply crisis are condemned. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p397]
- Buyers who discover that a seller has lowered prices since their purchase reduce future purchases by up to 15%, averaging $90 in lost revenue per affected buyer—because the original price became a reference point and the price reduction feels like a loss relative to that reference. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p398]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dual entitlement principle and loss aversion in fairness perceptions explain why price stability is economically valuable even when price flexibility would be efficient: the disruption of reference points creates economic harm beyond the immediate transaction.
