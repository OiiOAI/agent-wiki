# Feature-Positive Effect

## Summary

The feature-positive effect is the tendency to notice and value what is present while neglecting what is absent. Absence is harder to detect than presence. This explains why prevention campaigns emphasizing positive actions work better, why fraud escapes checklists, and why we don't appreciate peace or health until they're gone.

## Key facts

- In number series, detecting the number four in every series (presence) was easier than noticing no number six (absence)—absence is harder to detect. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p207]
- "Smoking causes lung cancer" is more powerful than "Not smoking leads to life free of lung cancer.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p207]
- Auditors miss artistic fraud (Enron, Madoff, rogue traders) because it doesn't appear on checklists—only positive features are checked. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p207]
- Nobel Prizes are awarded for confirming hypotheses but never for falsifying them, even though falsification is equally valuable. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p208]
