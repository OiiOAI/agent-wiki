# Deming Wheel

## Summary

The Deming Wheel (also called the Deming Circle) was created by W. Edwards Deming in 1950 during a JUSE-sponsored seminar in Japan. It consists of four steps: Design, Produce, Sell, and Research (Redesign), emphasizing constant rotation with quality as the aim.

## Key facts

- Deming modified the Shewhart cycle at a Japanese Union of Scientists and Engineers (JUSE) sponsored eight-day seminar in 1950, converting his original straight line of Design-Produce-Sell to a circle with a fourth step added: Redesign through marketing research. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- The four steps of the Deming Wheel are: Design the product (with appropriate tests), Make it and test it, Put it on the market, and Test it in service through market research. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Deming stressed the importance of constant interaction among design, production, sales, and research, with quality of product and service as the aim. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- The Japanese called this the 'Deming wheel' (or Deming Circle). [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
