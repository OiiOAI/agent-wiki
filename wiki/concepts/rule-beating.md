# Rule Beating

## Summary

Rule beating means taking evasive action to get around the intent of a system's rules—abiding by the letter but not the spirit of the law. It becomes a problem when it leads to large distortions and unnatural behaviors. Examples include government departments spending budget at year-end to avoid cuts, Vermont's land parcels just over ten acres to avoid Act 250, and European cassava imports replacing restricted corn. The solution is to understand rule beating as useful feedback and revise, improve, or better explain the rules.

## Key facts

- Rule beating produces the appearance of rules being followed while achieving outcomes contrary to the rules' intent. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p167]
- Drivers obey speed limits when police are nearby. Development avoids habitat where endangered species are documented. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p167]
- Rule beating is usually a response to overrigid, deleterious, unworkable, or ill-defined rules from above. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p168]
- The way out: understand rule beating as useful feedback and revise, improve, rescind, or better explain the rules. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p168]
- Rules to govern a system can lead to rule-beating—perverse behavior that gives the appearance of obeying the rules or achieving the goals, but that actually distorts the system. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p225]
- The remedy is to design or redesign rules to release creativity not in the direction of beating the rules, but in the direction of achieving the purpose of the rules. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p225]
