# Constructions in Analysis

## Summary

In this 1937 technical paper, Freud distinguishes between interpretations (which apply to single elements of material) and constructions (which lay before the patient a piece of his early history that he has forgotten). Freud compares the analyst's work to that of an archaeologist reconstructing destroyed structures, noting that the analyst works under more favorable conditions since what is forgotten is still alive and present somewhere. A patient's 'Yes' to a construction requires indirect confirmations—new memories completing the construction—while 'No' often points to incompleteness rather than error.

## Key facts

- A construction is appropriate when laying before the subject of analysis a piece of his early history that he has forgotten, whereas interpretation applies to single elements such as associations or parapraxes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7409]
- The analyst works under more favorable conditions than the archaeologist because what is being recovered is not something destroyed but something still alive—perhaps for another reason as well. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7410]
- All of the essentials are preserved in the psychical object whose early history the analyst seeks to recover; even things that seem completely forgotten are present somehow and somewhere. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7411]
- A plain 'Yes' from a patient is not unambiguous; it can be genuine recognition, meaningless, or even hypocritical, serving the resistance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7413]
- A patient's 'No' is typically an expression of resistance and points to incompleteness; the construction has not yet told him everything. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7413]
