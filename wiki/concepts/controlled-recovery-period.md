# Controlled Recovery Period

## Summary

A Controlled Recovery Period (CRP) is the book's term for daytime napping, reframing traditional 'naps' as strategic recovery opportunities. The mid-afternoon window (1-3 p.m.) aligns with natural circadian dips and is ideal for either a 30-minute rest or full 90-minute cycle. Early evening (5-7 p.m.) offers a secondary window limited to 30 minutes maximum. Even without actual sleep, 30 minutes of disconnection provides benefits. CRPs supplement nocturnal sleep cycles as part of a 24/7 recovery approach.

## Key facts

- CRPs are reframed from traditional 'naps' as strategic recovery opportunities during natural circadian dips. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p65]
- The mid-afternoon window (1-3 p.m.) is when circadian rhythm spikes upward producing increased urge to sleep that coincides with high need. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p66]
- A NASA study found that a twenty-six-minute nap improves pilot performance by 34 percent and alertness by 54 percent. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p68]
- A University of Düsseldorf study showed that even very short naps enhance memory processing. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p67]
- Caffeine should be taken beforehand so it takes effect towards the end of the CRP; caffeine takes about twenty minutes to affect the body. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p68]
- Evening CRPs (5-7 p.m.) should be limited to thirty minutes maximum to avoid interference with nocturnal sleep. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p72]
- It doesn't matter if you don't actually enter a sleep state—closing eyes and disconnecting provides benefits. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p70]
- Hunter-gatherer communities studied by Professor Carol Worthman show polyphasic sleep is the norm in traditional societies. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p66]
- Littlehales introduced probably the world's first training-ground recovery room at Manchester United's Carrington facility. [raw/books/health/Sleep_ The Myth of 8 Hours, the Power of N - Nick Littlehales [Littlehales, Nick].pdf#p9]

## Inferences

- Inference: Regular breaks every 90 minutes during the day help maintain concentration and prevent afternoon slumps.
- Inference: Sleep should be viewed as a 24-hour recovery process rather than just nocturnal sleep.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Optimal CRP timing may vary for different chronotypes.
