# Cognitive Engineering

## Summary

Cognitive engineering is an approach to designing information technology systems that respects and supports human intuition and decision-making expertise. Rather than treating users as passive recipients of computer-generated recommendations, cognitive engineering begins by determining the types of strategies and expertise that decision makers employ, then designs computer support around these requirements. The field seeks to establish methods for advocating for the needs of decision makers as part of the development of advanced computer support systems.

## Key facts

- Cognitive engineering begins by determining the types of strategies and expertise that decision makers employ to accomplish a task, then designs computer support around these requirements. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p329]
- Specialists in cognitive engineering work to establish methods for advocating for the needs of decision makers as part of the development of advanced computer support systems. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p329]
- The cognitive engineering approach contrasts with designing systems that treat operators as the weak link who should follow prescribed procedures. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p327]
