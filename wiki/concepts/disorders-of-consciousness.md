# Disorders of Consciousness

## Summary

Disorders of consciousness include vegetative state/unresponsive wakefulness syndrome (wakeful but apparently unconscious), minimally conscious state (behavioural signs of consciousness come and go), and locked-in syndrome (fully conscious but totally paralyzed). These conditions highlight the dissociation between wakefulness and awareness, and present urgent diagnostic challenges. Brain-based measures like PCI and fMRI can detect covert consciousness in patients who cannot communicate behaviourally.

## Key facts

- In vegetative state/unresponsive wakefulness syndrome, patients cycle through sleep and wakefulness but show no behavioural signs of conscious awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p44]
- Locked-in syndrome preserves full consciousness despite total paralysis, often from brainstem damage affecting motor control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p49]
- Jean-Dominique Bauby, locked-in after a brain haemorrhage, wrote the entire book 'The Diving Bell and the Butterfly' using limited eye movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p49]
- Adrian Owen's 2006 fMRI study showed a behaviourally unresponsive patient could communicate by imagining playing tennis versus walking through a house. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p50]
- Analysis suggests 10-20% of vegetative state patients might retain covert consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p51]

## Inferences

- Inference: These disorders demonstrate that consciousness cannot be reliably inferred from behaviour alone, necessitating brain-based measurement approaches.
