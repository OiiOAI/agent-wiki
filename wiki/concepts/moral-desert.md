# Moral Desert

## Summary

Rawls argues against distribution according to moral desert, contending that justice as fairness rejects this principle because it would not be chosen in the original position and there is no way to define the requisite criterion. He distinguishes moral desert from legitimate expectations, arguing that what people are entitled to is not proportional to nor dependent upon their intrinsic worth.

## Key facts

- Justice as fairness rejects distribution according to moral desert; such a principle would not be chosen in the original position. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p284]
- The notion of distribution according to virtue fails to distinguish between moral desert and legitimate expectations. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p284]
- It is one of the fixed points of our moral judgments that no one deserves their place in the distribution of natural assets. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p285]
- The effort a person is willing to make is influenced by natural abilities and skills and the alternatives open to them. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p285]
- The concept of moral worth is secondary to those of right and justice and plays no role in the substantive definition of distributive shares. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p286]
