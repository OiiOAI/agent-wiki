# Safe-to-fail Probes

## Summary

Safe-to-fail probes are small-scale experiments designed to test hypotheses in complex systems while limiting potential negative consequences. The concept is central to Cynefin's approach to the Complex domain, where outcomes cannot be predicted in advance.

## Key facts

- Dave Snowden advocates deploying Innovation Teams alongside Business Recovery Teams during crises to capture novel practice. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p132]
- Steve Holt describes how Goldratt used intuition and abductive reasoning to develop coherent ideas, then tested them with safe-to-fail probes. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p215]
- In diabetes management, safe-to-fail experiments include taking one glucose tablet instead of three to raise blood sugar and monitoring the change. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p195]
- Trent Hone documents how Nimitz used parallel, safe-to-fail experimentation to develop the Combat Information Center in WWII. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: Safe-to-fail probes represent a fundamental shift from traditional planning approaches that assume predictability.
