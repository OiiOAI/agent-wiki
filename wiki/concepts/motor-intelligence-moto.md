# Motor Intelligence (Moto)

## Summary

Motor intelligence (abbreviated moto) is a brain mechanism that generates new actions through A → B causation rather than logical A = B computation. It runs on synapses and operates with feedback, allowing it to act with low or no information, taking initiative and imagining unprecedented futures. Unlike logical thinking, moto is largely insentient—humans are not aware of the action sequences neurons use for tasks like drumming rhythms or writing letters.

## Key facts

- Motor intelligence (moto) generates new actions through A → B causation rather than A = B computation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p205]
- Moto runs on a nonlogical brain machine: the synapse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p205]
- Moto operates with feedback, allowing it to work with low (even no) information, taking initiative and imagining futures with no precedent. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p206]
- Moto runs on feedback, allowing it to act smart in ways logic cannot, often generating new art, science, and technology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p206]
- Moto is largely insentient—humans are not aware of the action sequences neurons use for tasks like drumming rhythms, writing letters, or slicing bread. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p206]
- Moto's bias toward action maintains psychological momentum that helps the brain overcome setbacks and push through uncertainty. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p206]

## Inferences

- Inference: Moto evolved to process exceptions and unknown causes, which is why it cannot be fully simulated by computers that require known algorithms.
- Inference: While moto operates unconsciously, it can be consciously trained through story and deliberate practice.
