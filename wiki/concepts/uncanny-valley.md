# Uncanny Valley

## Summary

The uncanny valley is a concept introduced by Masahiro Mori in 1970 describing how as a robot appears more humanlike, it elicits increasingly positive reactions until a point where it appears strikingly human in some ways but falls short in others—at which point reactions turn rapidly to revulsion and fear. Hiroshi Ishiguro's Geminoid robots exemplify this phenomenon, with visitors commonly reporting fear. Real-world physical robots struggle with the uncanny valley while advances in virtual world technology using generative adversarial networks can now create photorealistic but fake human faces.

## Key facts

- Masahiro Mori proposed in 1970 that as a robot begins to look humanlike, it will elicit increasingly positive and empathetic reactions until it passes a certain point where it appears strikingly human but falls short in others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p243]
- At the uncanny valley, reactions turn rapidly to revulsion and fear, only recovering as resemblance becomes even closer to indistinguishability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p243]
- A 2009 study found that the most common feeling experienced by visitors meeting a Geminoid was fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p243]
- Generative adversarial neural networks can now create photorealistic faces of people who never actually existed by mixing features from large databases of actual faces. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p245]
