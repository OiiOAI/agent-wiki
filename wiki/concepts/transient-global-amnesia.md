# Transient Global Amnesia

## Summary

Transient global amnesia is a benign condition, often associated with migraine headaches, in which patients are suddenly deprived of recently added autobiographical memories. Patients retain core consciousness and core self for current events but lose access to personal historical provenance and personal future, making the present incomprehensible. They constantly repeat anxious questions about where they are and what they are doing.

## Key facts

- In transient global amnesia, patients are deprived of records recently added to autobiographical memory, losing access to events from seconds before through hours or days prior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p203]
- Patients retain core consciousness for events and objects in the here and now, including core consciousness for the fact that some knowledge is no longer present. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p203]
- Without an updated autobiography, the here and now becomes incomprehensible, leading patients to repeatedly ask: Where am I? What am I doing here? How did I come here?. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p203]
- Patients with transient global amnesia have suspended extended consciousness and autobiographical self, with preservation of core consciousness and core self—a perfect contrast to epileptic automatism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p204]
- Transient global amnesia underscores the limitation of core consciousness: without provenance and motive for current actions, the present is merely a puzzle. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p203]

## Inferences

- Inference: The condition demonstrates that autobiographical memory must be continuously reactivated to support extended consciousness, and that core consciousness alone cannot provide meaning to present experiences.
