# Somatosensory Cortex Columnar Organization

## Summary

The somatosensory cortex is organized into vertical columns or slabs, each 300 to 600 μm wide, spanning all six cortical layers. Neurons within a column receive inputs from the same local area of skin and respond to the same class of touch receptors, forming elementary functional modules.

## Key facts

- Each cortical column receives thalamic input primarily in layer IV from one body part. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p500]
- Pyramidal neurons in layer V provide the principal output from each column. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p501]
- Columns within S-I are arranged somatotopically with a complete representation of the body in each of the four areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p501]
- More cortical columns are devoted to the fingers than to the entire trunk, reflecting the importance of tactile information from these regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p503]
