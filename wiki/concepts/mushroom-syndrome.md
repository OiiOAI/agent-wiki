# Mushroom Syndrome

## Summary

The Mushroom Syndrome is a metaphor describing people who, like mushrooms competing for space, constantly grow worries instead. When there is nothing to worry about, they find something. This is identified as a learnt destructive habit that exhausts both the person and those around them, leaving the sufferer in chronic stress. The cure involves recognising the pattern and overwriting it with rational Autopilots.

## Key facts

- In the mushroom analogy, when mushrooms compete for space, one inevitably takes over, and when picked, the next largest fills the gap—the story continues indefinitely. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p149]
- The Mushroom Syndrome describes a person who cannot stop growing worries and finds something to worry about even when there is nothing to worry about, a learnt destructive habit. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p149]
- Recognising that you suffer with the Mushroom Syndrome means you can stop this Gremlin behaviour from continuing and remove it by overwriting it with a programme that rationalises what is going on. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p149]
- Useful Autopilots to replace the Mushroom Syndrome Gremlin include: humans learn to live with concerns but not allow worry; worrying never does any good; worrying is an option and we can choose not to worry; and relaxing is a powerful worry remover. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p149]
