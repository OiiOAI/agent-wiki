# Capital Budgeting Critique

## Summary

Formal capital budgeting theory—calculating present values of future cash flows—is largely ignored by corporations because the largest risk in long-term investments is that people proposing them are incompetent or lying. The case of Project T illustrates how managers manipulate projections to win 'battles for corporate resources' rather than pursuing genuine value creation.

## Key facts

- Only a few companies actually use formal capital budgeting as recommended, instead letting managers shape projects through discussions with senior management. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p160]
- Project T's manager Bradley cut and pasted charts to show a five-year payback instead of seven years before a board presentation. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p162]
- Bradley stated, 'Strategic planning is a battle for corporate resources. It is a battle I intend to win.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p162]
- The quality of strategy work is limited by the amount of honesty and integrity in the system. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p163]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boards tend to discount complex financial analyses and rely on intuition because they know projections are often manipulated by ambitious managers.
