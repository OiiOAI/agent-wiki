# The Inverse Turkey Problem

## Summary

The Inverse Turkey Problem describes how empirical evidence underestimates the benefits of antifragile systems because positive Black Swan events are absent from past data. While the classic turkey problem misses negative rare events, the inverse problem misses positive ones.

## Key facts

- In antifragile cases with positive asymmetries, sample track records underestimate long-term averages and hide qualities rather than defects. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p295]
- In fragile cases with negative asymmetries, sample track records underestimate long-term averages and hide defects rather than display qualities. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p295]
- Harvard Business School professor Gary Pisano made the elementary inverse-turkey mistake by citing biotech companies' lack of profits as evidence against biotech investment, without recognizing that rare events dominate in Extremistan. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p295]
- Harvard professor Kenneth Froot made the opposite mistake with reinsurance companies, failing to recognize that catastrophic events tend to be absent from past data due to their rarity. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p296]

## Inferences

- Inference: Conventional statistical methods fail to account for asymmetry, leading both academics and practitioners to systematically misinterpret the risk-reward profiles of antifragile versus fragile systems.
