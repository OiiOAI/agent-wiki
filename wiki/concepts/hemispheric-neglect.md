# Hemispheric Neglect

## Summary

A condition typically resulting from right-hemisphere stroke damage, in which patients ignore or are unaware of the left side of space and their own left body. This provides key evidence for the right hemisphere's role in providing a unified, holistic experience of the world.

## Key facts

- Following right-hemisphere stroke, patients may fail to acknowledge anyone standing to their left, the left half of a clock face, or the left page of a newspaper, and may even neglect to wash or dress the left half of their body. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p71]
- The problem is not due to blindness as ordinarily understood; temporarily disabling the left hemisphere through transcranial magnetic stimulation improves the neglect. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p71]
- The mirror-image of neglect does not occur after left-hemisphere stroke because the still-functioning right hemisphere supplies a whole body and whole world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p72]
- The left hemisphere has difficulty disengaging; patients become 'stuck' to items in the right hemifield, exhibiting 'stickiness' of attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p73]

## Inferences

- Inference: The asymmetry of neglect (occurring after right but not left hemisphere damage) suggests the right hemisphere has a fundamentally different relationship to the whole of experience than the left.
