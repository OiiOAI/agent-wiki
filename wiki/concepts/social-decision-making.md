# Social Decision Making

## Summary

Social decision making refers to choices made in the context of direct or indirect social interactions, where outcomes depend on the decisions of others. Game Theory provides tools for studying these decisions, revealing systematic deviations from purely self-interested behavior.

## Key facts

- Social decision making involves outcomes that depend on the concomitant decisions of others, requiring inference about others' values and probabilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p309]
- In the Ultimatum Game, low offers of less than 20% of the total amount are rejected about half of the time, contrary to self-interest predictions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p312]
- In the Trust Game, despite predictions that trustees will never honor trust, most investors send some money and trust is typically reciprocated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p313]
- In the Prisoner's Dilemma Game, mutual cooperation occurs about 50% of the time, especially in early rounds, despite the Nash equilibrium being mutual defection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p314]
- Human brains may have evolved in part to support complex social interactions and the decisions they require. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p309]
- Social decision making is a relatively understudied field where the neuroeconomic approach can make important contributions through games embedded in actual, consequential social interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p326]
- Anterior insula activation in response to unfair offers was more active when playing with another human than with a computer, and reliably predicted rejection decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p320]
- The anterior insula was also activated by unreciprocated cooperation in an iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, and connectivity between anterior insula and lateral OFC predicted subsequent defection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p320]
- Providing general personality information about partners before a Trust Game led to reduced caudate activity in response to actions of morally positive or negative partners, but not neutral ones, demonstrating top-down influence on social learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p325]

## Inferences

- Inference: People's choices in economic games do not conform to a model driven purely by self-interest, suggesting important roles for reciprocity, fairness, and social emotions.
- Inference: Relative neocortex size correlates positively with social group size across primates, suggesting social complexity drove brain evolution.
- Inference: Social decision making recruits both basic reward mechanisms and affective processing areas adapted for complex social interactions.
- Inference: Neural data can constrain behavioral economics models by providing information about the underlying processes driving social preferences.
