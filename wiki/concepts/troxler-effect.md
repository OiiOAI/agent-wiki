# Troxler Effect

## Summary

The Troxler effect is a visual phenomenon where a stationary visual scene fades from awareness when gaze is fixed steadily on a target. Named after Ignaz Troxler, this effect demonstrates that perception requires action - specifically eye movements - and provides evidence for the book's argument that perception depends on action.

## Key facts

- Named after Ignaz Troxler, a Swiss physician who first demonstrated the effect in 1804. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p57]
- When gaze is fixed steadily on a stationary target, the surrounding visual scene disappears after just a few seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p57]
- The moment eye movements are made, sight returns, demonstrating that action is required for perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p57]
- One explanation of the Troxler effect is neuronal 'adaptation' in the retina or visual system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p57]
