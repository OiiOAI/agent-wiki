# German Works Councils

## Summary

German works councils (Betriebsräte) are worker elected bodies that play a critical role in workplace governance, particularly in monitoring training quality and maintaining trust relationships with management. They complement business associations in ensuring training standards and provide guarantees to apprentices regarding credential quality.

## Key facts

- Works councils carry out monitoring activities to provide guarantees to apprentices on the quality and marketability of training. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- The low distrust in German training transactions results from the close and high-trust relations between company management and works councils. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- Works councils and unions complement the activities of business associations, particularly in medium and large companies. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- Most monitoring activities are carried out by works councils rather than by unions, distinguishing their role in the training system. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p433]
- Works councils were regarded suspiciously by management, which wanted to preserve its prerogatives, and by socialist parties and trade unions, which sought transformation of the capitalist system. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p432]
- The only workers' associations to give works councils unqualified support in the studied period were those connected with the antidemocratic Wirtschaftsfriedliche movement. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p432]

## Inferences

- Inference: The chapter indicates that works councils function as institutional trust mechanisms that reduce monitoring costs and transaction costs in workplace training, creating a stable framework for human capital investment.
