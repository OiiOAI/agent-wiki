# Taste Receptor Cell

## Summary

Taste receptor cells are specialized epithelial cells in taste buds that detect chemical compounds in food and trigger neural signals to the brain. Different subsets of taste cells detect sweet, bitter, salty, sour, and umami qualities through distinct transduction mechanisms. Sweet, bitter, and umami are detected by G protein-coupled receptors (T1R and T2R families), while salty and sour involve ion channels.

## Key facts

- Humans and other mammals can distinguish five different taste qualities: sweet, bitter, salty, sour, and umami. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p727]
- Bitter, sweet, and umami tastants interact with G protein-coupled receptors, whereas salty and sour tastants involve specific ion channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p729]
- Sweet compounds are detected by a complex of two G protein-coupled receptors, T1R2 and T1R3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p729]
- Umami taste is detected by a complex of two G protein-coupled receptors, T1R1 and T1R3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p729]
- Bitter tastants are detected by approximately 30 G protein-coupled receptors called T2Rs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p731]
- Each taste bud contains approximately 100 taste receptor cells along with supporting cells and basal stem cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p728]
- T1R and T2R receptors are expressed in different, non-overlapping taste cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p782]
