# Cerebellar Microcircuit

## Summary

The cerebellar cortex contains a highly regular, repeating microcircuit composed of five main neuron types organized into three layers. The granular layer contains granule cells and Golgi cells; the Purkinje cell layer contains Purkinje neurons; and the molecular layer contains basket and stellate cells. Two afferent systems—climbing fibers from the inferior olive and mossy fibers from spinal cord and brain stem—process information differently and converge on Purkinje cells.

## Key facts

- The cerebellar cortex contains approximately 100 billion granule cells, the most numerous neurons in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p918]
- Purkinje cell axons provide the entire output of the cerebellar cortex, projecting to deep nuclei and releasing the inhibitory transmitter GABA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p918]
- Climbing fibers originate in the inferior olivary nucleus and produce complex spikes in Purkinje cells through powerful excitatory synapses wrapping around proximal dendrites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p919]
- Each Purkinje neuron receives synaptic input from only a single climbing fiber, but each climbing fiber contacts 1 to 10 Purkinje cells arranged in parasagittal strips. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p919]
- Mossy fibers convey sensory and corollary discharge information and affect Purkinje cells via granule cells and parallel fibers, with each mossy fiber influencing up to 1 million parallel fiber synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p919]
- Parallel fibers run perpendicular to Purkinje cell dendritic trees and produce simple spikes in Purkinje cells through small excitatory postsynaptic potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p920]
- Purkinje cells fire simple spikes at rates up to 100 per second at rest and up to several hundred spikes per second during active movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p920]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The functional role of molecular layer interneurons (basket and stellate cells) remains poorly understood despite being described over 60 years ago.
