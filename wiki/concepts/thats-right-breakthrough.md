# That's Right Breakthrough

## Summary

The 'that's right' breakthrough is a psychological moment in negotiation when the counterpart fully embraces the negotiator's understanding of their perspective. It is more powerful than 'yes' because it represents genuine buy-in rather than compliance, and is triggered by summarizing the counterpart's worldview including both rational and emotional elements.

## Key facts

- 'That's right' signals that negotiations can proceed from deadlock and breaks down barriers impeding progress. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p93]
- A good summary combines rearticulating meaning plus acknowledgment of underlying emotions (paraphrasing + labeling = summary). [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p92]
- 'You're right' is a disaster compared to 'that's right' because it indicates the counterpart hasn't internalized the idea and will return to previous behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p95]
- The 'that's right' breakthrough usually occurs invisibly to the counterpart and represents a subtle epiphany. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p91]
- In the Schilling kidnapping, Abu Sabaya's 'that's right' caused him to drop his $10 million war damages demand entirely. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p93]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'that's right' breakthrough works because it engages the psychological principle of unconditional positive regard, making the counterpart feel truly understood rather than simply persuaded.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact psychological mechanism for why 'that's right' creates more lasting behavioral change than 'yes' is not fully explained in the source.
