# media-age assassin

## Summary

A media-age assassin is an individual who attacks a public figure primarily seeking attention, fame, and identity rather than political or ideological goals. Gavin de Becker argues that these attackers are motivated by the desire to become 'somebody' through a violent act that will guarantee them media coverage.

## Key facts

- De Becker defines media-age assassins as attackers motivated by 'attention and publicity' rather than ideology or politics. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p157]
- These assassins often study and emulate previous attackers, creating predictable patterns of pre-attack behavior. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p160]
- De Becker draws parallels between assassins and daredevils, noting that both derive all their worth from one act and 'create their own emergencies.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p162]
- The author cites Arthur Bremer's diary showing escalating intimacy with his target, moving from 'the President' to 'Nixy-boy,' demonstrating how stalkers come to 'know' their victims. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p161]
- Bremer worried about his 'ratings' in his journal: 'If something big in Nam flares up, [my attack] won't get more than three minutes on network T.V. news.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: The author's comparison of assassins to daredevils suggests both share a fundamental psychological need for recognition and identity that their ordinary lives failed to provide.
