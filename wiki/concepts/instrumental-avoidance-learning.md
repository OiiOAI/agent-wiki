# Instrumental Avoidance Learning

## Summary

Instrumental avoidance learning allows organisms to perform novel actions that successfully escape or avoid harm. Unlike innate defensive reactions, avoidance is learned through consequences. When avoidance becomes habitual through extensive repetition, it becomes an automatic stimulus-response process that can persist even when no longer appropriate.

## Key facts

- Instrumental avoidance learning allows organisms to acquire novel behaviors that enable escape from or avoidance of harm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p108]
- Escape from threat involves learning to terminate the conditioned threat stimulus, motivated by conditioned negative reinforcement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p113]
- Avoidance becomes self-perpetuating because the person or animal never experiences the threat, preventing extinction of the underlying association. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p115]
- When avoidance becomes habitual, the CS automatically triggers the response even if it is no longer associated with the threat. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p115]
