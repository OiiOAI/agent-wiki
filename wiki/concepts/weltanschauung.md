# Weltanschauung

## Summary

Freud defines a Weltanschauung as an intellectual construction solving all problems of existence uniformly on the basis of one overriding hypothesis. In Lecture XXXV, he argues that psychoanalysis cannot construct its own Weltanschauung but must accept the scientific one, which he characterizes by its limitation to what is knowable and rejection of revelation. He positions psychoanalysis as an extension of scientific method into the mental realm.

## Key facts

- Freud defines a Weltanschauung as an intellectual construction solving all problems of existence on the basis of one overriding hypothesis that leaves no question unanswered. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6983]
- Freud argues that psychoanalysis is unfit to construct its own Weltanschauung and must accept the scientific one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6983]
- The scientific Weltanschauung assumes uniformity of explanation but only as a programme to be fulfilled in the future, marked by limitation to what is knowable and rejection of sources other than research. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6984]
- Freud identifies religion as the primary opponent of science, distinguishing it from art (harmless) and philosophy (similar to science but clinging to illusion of coherent picture without gaps). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6985]
- Freud traces religion's power to its threefold function: satisfying thirst for knowledge, providing consolation, and laying down ethical precepts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6986]
- Freud describes religious belief as determined by the situation of childhood, the mnemic image of the father overvalued in childhood being exalted into a deity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6987]
- Freud characterizes religious ethics as deriving from childhood situations where parents' prohibitions and demands persist as conscience, with God ruling by the same system of rewards and punishments. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6988]
- Freud compares religion to the neurosis which individual civilized humans pass through from childhood to maturity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6992]
- Freud critiques Marxism as having created a prohibition of thought as ruthless as religion's, with Marx's writings taking the place of sacred books as revelation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7002]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's characterization of Bolshevism as developing 'illusions' similar to religion's, promising Paradise on earth, reflects his skepticism about revolutionary social change without corresponding changes in human nature.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges his understanding of Marxism may be inadequate, noting particular difficulty with concepts like dialectical development of social forms.
