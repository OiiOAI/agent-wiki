# Mobilizations

## Summary

Mobilizations are techniques designed to counter the effects of mono-positioning and lack of movement by taking joints through different ranges, unsticking compressed soft tissue, and ingraining new movement patterns. Unlike traditional stretching which targets muscles through passive tension, mobilizations engage multiple systems including connective tissue, joints, and the nervous system. The contract-relax (PNF) technique is commonly used, involving contracting a muscle for a few seconds then relaxing to train brain control of the position.

## Key facts

- Mobilizations are techniques designed to counter the effects of mono-positioning and lack of movement by taking joints to different places and unsticking compressed soft tissue. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L225-L228]
- Mobilizations engage the brain, signaling that accessing certain positions is safe, unlike passive stretching which only pulls on tissues. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L250-L255]
- The contract-relax technique (from proprioceptive neuromuscular facilitation) involves contracting a muscle for a few seconds then relaxing to train brain control of a position at end range. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L258-L262]
- Isometrics are exercises that involve contracting muscles without moving joints. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L262-L264]
- The recommended equipment for mobilizations includes a lacrosse-size ball, foam roller, exercise band, and PVC pipe or broomstick. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L278-L285]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mobilizations are presented as more effective than stretching because they address the nervous system's protective responses to movement.
