# NEXTUP (Dream Function Theory)

## Summary

NEXTUP is the authors' comprehensive theory of dream function, proposing that the brain uses sleep—including dreaming—to explore weak, previously unconsidered associations to ongoing concerns in order to extract new knowledge and better prepare for the future. The theory integrates findings from neuroscience, sleep research, learning, and memory, and proposes that dreaming serves different but interrelated functions across sleep stages.

## Key facts

- NEXTUP proposes that dreaming extracts new knowledge from existing information through discovery and strengthening of unexpected, previously unexplored associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p268]
- The reduction (in N2) or absence (in REM) of brain noradrenaline during sleep facilitates the search for weak associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p268]
- Dreams are not meant to solve ongoing concerns directly, but to explore them and their possible solutions to better understand what they mean for the dreamer. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p268]
- Reduced (in N2) or absent (in REM) levels of serotonin create states in which the brain is biased toward accepting dream associations as meaningful and useful. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p269]
- Dreams tend to select ongoing concerns that have emotional salience and contain unresolved questions whose answers would be of future use. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p269]
- Dreams don't replay events from our life as they occurred; instead, they tell stories about the event by bringing together fragments of episodic and semantic memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p270]
- The conscious experiencing of dreams creates narratives that allow exploration of possible scenarios and produces emotional feelings critical for evaluating these scenarios. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p270]
- Dreams do not need to be recalled after awakening to serve NEXTUP's function of memory evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p270]

## Inferences

- Inference: NEXTUP integrates multiple findings about sleep-dependent memory processing with the phenomenology of dreaming to provide a unified framework for understanding why we dream.
- Inference: The theory explains why dreams often feel meaningful yet their connections to waking concerns are usually obscure—the associations explored are weak and previously unconsidered.
