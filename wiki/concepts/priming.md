# Priming

## Summary

Priming refers to the automatic, non-conscious influence of recently perceived concepts, words, objects, or situations on subsequent thoughts and behaviors. Research demonstrates that exposure to concepts (even subliminally) can prime related behaviors and attitudes without conscious awareness. The priming literature shows that seemingly irrelevant contextual factors can significantly influence judgments and actions.

## Key facts

- The Florida effect study showed that students primed with elderly-related words walked significantly slower down a hallway. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p72]
- Priming effects work bidirectionally: walking slowly primes elderly concepts just as elderly concepts prime slow walking. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p73]
- Studies showed that priming with money concepts increased self-reliance and decreased helpfulness to others. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p75]
- The office kitchen study found that donations to an honesty box tripled when posters showed watching eyes compared to flower posters. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p77]
- Priming with death-related concepts increases attractiveness of authoritarian ideas, possibly providing existential security. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p76]

## Inferences

- Inference: Priming demonstrates that System 1 processes information outside conscious awareness and influences behavior without conscious intent.
