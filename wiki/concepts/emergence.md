# Emergence

## Summary

Emergence describes the arising of large-scale, collective patterns of behavior in complex systems through self-organization. The enactive approach reformulates emergence as 'dynamic co-emergence,' where part and whole co-emerge and mutually specify each other. Emergence involves circular causality: local-to-global determination (novel macrolevel structures emerge from component interactions) and global-to-local determination (global structures constrain local interactions through changes in control parameters). Dynamic co-emergence goes beyond standard dissipative structures because autonomous systems endogenously create and maintain the constraints guiding energy/matter flows.

## Key facts

- An emergent process belongs to an ensemble of elements, arises spontaneously from local interactions, and does not belong to any single element. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p68]
- Emergence has two aspects: local-to-global determination and global-to-local determination whereby global structures constrain local interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p69]
- Circular causality refers to the two-sided or double determination in emergence, as when a tornado both arises from and sucks particles into a configuration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p69]
- In dynamic co-emergence, the whole not only arises from the parts but the parts also arise from the whole. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p72]
- The parts do not exist in advance as independent entities; part and whole co-emerge and mutually specify each other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p72]
- Self-organization involves no 'self' or agent inside the system doing the organizing; the system organizes itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p69]

## Inferences

- Inference: Dynamic co-emergence best describes the sort of emergence seen in autonomy: a membrane and reaction network co-emerge through their integrative, metabolic relation.
