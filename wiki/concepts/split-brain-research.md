# Split-Brain Research

## Summary

Studies of patients in whom the corpus callosum was severed as treatment for epilepsy, conducted by Roger Sperry, Michael Gazzaniga, and Joseph Bogen. These studies revealed that each cerebral hemisphere has its own consciousness capable of functioning independently of the other, demonstrating that even consciousness is not a unitary process. The non-dominant hemisphere can perform tasks the dominant hemisphere cannot, showing hemispheric specialization.

## Key facts

- Split-brain studies by Sperry, Gazzaniga, and Bogen found that each hemisphere had a consciousness able to function independently of the other. - One patient reading with his left hand (controlled by right hemisphere) found the book boring and commanded the left hand to put it down, even though the left hemisphere could read and did not feel bored. - The dominant hemisphere sometimes commented on the performance of the nondominant hemisphere, manifesting false confidence about problems it could not solve.
