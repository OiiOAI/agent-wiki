# Rate Coding

## Summary

Rate coding is a neural coding mechanism discovered by Edgar Adrian and Yngve Zotterman in 1925 where the frequency of action potentials (number of action potentials per second) varies with the strength and duration of the stimulus. Stronger stimuli evoke larger receptor potentials that generate a higher frequency of action potentials. The intensity of a stimulus is represented in the brain by all active neurons in the receptor population.

## Key facts

- The frequency of firing, the number of action potentials per second, varies with the strength of the stimulus and its duration; stronger stimuli evoke larger receptor potentials that generate a greater number and a higher frequency of action potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p443]
- The intensity of a stimulus is represented in the brain by all active neurons in the receptor population, depending on the fact that individual receptors in a sensory system differ in their sensory thresholds or affinity for particular molecules. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p443]
