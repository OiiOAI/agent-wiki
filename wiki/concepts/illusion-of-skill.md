# Illusion of Skill

## Summary

The illusion of skill is the mistaken belief that success in uncertain domains (entrepreneurship, investing, corporate leadership) stems primarily from talent and effort rather than luck. Serial entrepreneurs succeed less than 1% of the time. Kahneman found zero correlation between investment advisors' year-to-year rankings—rewarding luck, not skill.

## Key facts

- Serial entrepreneurs account for less than 1% of everyone who starts a company—not because they retire but because they fail at succeeding. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p205]
- Research found that in 40% of cases, the weaker CEO leads the stronger company—only 10 percentage points above pure chance. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
- Kahneman found zero correlation between investment advisors' rankings year-to-year—performance was pure coincidence. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
- Warren Buffett wrote: "A good managerial record is far more a function of what business boat you get into than it is of how effectively you row.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
