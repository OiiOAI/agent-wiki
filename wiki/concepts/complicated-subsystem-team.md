# Complicated-subsystem team

## Summary

A complicated-subsystem team is responsible for building and maintaining a part of the system that depends heavily on specialist knowledge, to the extent that most team members must be specialists to understand and make changes. The goal is to reduce the cognitive load of stream-aligned teams working on systems that include or use the complicated subsystem. Examples include video processing codecs, mathematical models, real-time trade reconciliation algorithms, and face-recognition engines.

## Key facts

- A complicated-subsystem team is responsible for building and maintaining a part of the system that depends heavily on specialist knowledge. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- The decision to create a complicated-subsystem team is driven by team cognitive load, not by a perceived opportunity to share the component. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Organizations should expect only a few complicated-subsystem teams compared to the number of component teams in a traditional structure. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Examples of complicated subsystems include video processing codecs, mathematical models, real-time trade reconciliation algorithms, transaction reporting systems, and face-recognition engines. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- A complicated-subsystem team should be mindful of the current development stage, providing high collaboration during early phases and reduced interaction during later stages when the subsystem has stabilized. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
