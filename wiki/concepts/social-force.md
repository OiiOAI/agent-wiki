# Social Force

## Summary

The social force is an invisible force field that affects and binds a group of people through shared sensations, creating an intense feeling of connection. It exists both inside us and outside us simultaneously—when we experience the bodily sensations of this force, we believe others are feeling the same. The larger the group, the more intense the effect. Certain leaders with charisma amplify this energy through the group. The social force can shift from joy and excitement to anger and violence in the presence of an enemy.

## Key facts

- The social force exists inside us and outside us at the same time, creating a shared energy between self and others. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p500]
- The larger the group, the more intense the effect of the social force, with increased strength and vitality as well as communal warmth. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p500]
- If the leader is charismatic and bursting with energy, the social force filters through the group or gathered masses more intensely. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p500]
- Humans are drawn to the social force, manifested in love of partisan crowds, stadiums, choirs, parades, carnivals, concerts, religious assemblies, and political conventions. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p500]
- Prolonged isolation causes people to feel disconnected from reality and sense their personalities are disintegrating, as the eyes of others validate our sense of self. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p501]
- The social force is merely a more complex version of what all social animals experience, including primates that imitate those higher in rank as a show of inferiority. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p501]

## Inferences

- Inference: The social force explains why humans are fundamentally social creatures who cannot thrive in isolation, and why they seek belonging to groups throughout their lives.
