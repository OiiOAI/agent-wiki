# Resilience

## Summary

Resilience is defined as the ability to 'bounce back in the face of adversity'—to remain functional and recover from major setbacks like breakups, bereavement, or catastrophe without descending into dysfunction. The book uses a 'tempered steel' metaphor: just as steel must be heated and cooled in a specific sequence to gain both hardness and springiness, humans need psychological 'tempering' to become strong without becoming brittle. Multiple practices from the book contribute to resilience, but the Focus on Positive (lovingkindness) practice is specifically highlighted as directly building resilience through stress reduction, improved vagal tone, increased social connection, and positive emotion cultivation. Research cited shows FOP practice increases telomere length (longevity marker), reduces PTSD symptoms, and improves immune function.

## Key facts

- Resilience is defined as the ability to bounce back from major emotional setbacks without descending into dysfunction. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p184]
- The 'tempered steel' metaphor describes how resilience combines strength with flexibility—bending without breaking. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p185]
- Focus on Positive (lovingkindness) practice was shown to increase telomere length in women, a key longevity indicator. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p186]
- Studies showed FOP practice reduced PTSD symptoms in veterans even three months after training ended. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p186]
- A meta-analysis of 30 happiness studies found a strong relationship between positive emotions and longevity, comparable to smoking vs. not smoking. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p187]
