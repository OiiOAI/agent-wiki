# Intuition in Expert Decision Making

## Summary

Intuition in expert decision making refers to the rapid recognition of key patterns that indicate the dynamics of a situation, based on accumulated experience. Research by Klein shows that experienced decision makers cannot always articulate how they recognized a situation, leading them to attribute their expertise to supernatural abilities like ESP. Intuition has a biological basis and depends on implicit memory rather than conscious deliberation.

## Key facts

- Intuition depends on the use of experience to recognize key patterns that indicate the dynamics of the situation. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p56]
- Bechara, Damasio, Tranel, and Damasio (1997) found that intuition has a biological basis in the brain's emotional processing systems. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p56]
- Because patterns can be subtle, people often cannot describe what they noticed or how they judged a situation as typical or atypical. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p56]
- Experienced decision makers may confuse intuition with ESP because they are not aware of the pattern-matching process they are using. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p57-58]
- Research by Wilson and Schooler (1991) shows that people perform worse at some decision tasks when asked to analyze the reasons for their preferences. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p59]
- Intuition is not an inborn trait but grows out of experience through pattern recognition and implicit memory. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p58-59]

## Inferences

- Inference: The difficulty experts have describing their intuition suggests that training should include scenario-based learning and story sharing rather than relying on explicit verbalizable rules.
