# Piriform Cortex

## Summary

The piriform cortex is the largest area of the olfactory cortex, receiving direct projections from mitral and tufted relay neurons of the olfactory bulb. It is considered the major olfactory cortical area and projects to various limbic structures including the amygdala and hypothalamus. Pyramidal neurons activated by a particular odorant are distributed broadly across the piriform cortex.

## Key facts

- The piriform cortex is the largest area of the olfactory cortex and receives direct projections from mitral and tufted relay neurons of the olfactory bulb. - Mitral and tufted cell axons form excitatory glutamatergic synapses with pyramidal neurons in the piriform cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p771]
- Pyramidal neurons activated by a particular odorant are scattered broadly across the piriform cortex, an arrangement different from the highly organized input in the olfactory bulb. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p771]
- The piriform cortex projects back to the olfactory bulb, providing centrifugal modulation of olfactory processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p771]
