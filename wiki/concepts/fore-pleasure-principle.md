# Fore-Pleasure Principle

## Summary

Freud's fore-pleasure principle states that a small amount of pleasure from the joke-work itself can lift stronger internal inhibitions or repressions, allowing much greater suppressed pleasure to emerge. This explains why tendentious jokes produce such intense enjoyment—the fore-pleasure acts as an incentive bonus that enables the breakthrough of repressed impulses.

## Key facts

- A suppressed urge cannot normally breakthrough; but when another urge releases pleasure through the joke, this second development of pleasure permits the suppressed urge to gain sufficient strength to overcome inhibition. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2758]
- The enjoyment obtained from tendentious jokes is incomparably greater than the pleasure from the joke technique alone; the suppressed purpose seems to pass through undiminished. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2759]
- The fore-pleasure principle corresponds to an arrangement holding in many widely separated departments of mental life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2759]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fore-pleasure mechanism in jokes parallels similar structures in sexual response (where preliminary pleasure enables final satisfaction) and in aesthetic experiences.
