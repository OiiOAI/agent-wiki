# Life/Dinner Principle

## Summary

The life/dinner principle, proposed by Dawkins and John Krebs, explains asymmetries in evolutionary arms races between predators and prey or parasites and hosts. The rabbit runs faster than the fox because the rabbit is running for its life while the fox is only running for its dinner. This principle accounts for why hosts do not always evolve complete resistance to manipulation by parasites.

## Key facts

- The life/dinner principle states that there is an asymmetry in the cost of failure: a rabbit runs faster than a fox because the rabbit is running for its life while the fox is only running for its dinner. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p291]
- Each individual cuckoo nestling is descended from a long line of ancestral cuckoo nestlings, every one of whom must have succeeded in manipulating its foster parent, whereas individual foster parents have ancestors who never encountered a cuckoo.
