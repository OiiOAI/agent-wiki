# Classical Conditioning in Aplysia

## Summary

Classical conditioning is an associative form of learning where a neutral stimulus (conditioned stimulus) becomes associated with a meaningful stimulus (unconditioned stimulus). In Aplysia, Kandel and colleagues demonstrated classical conditioning of the gill-withdrawal reflex in 1983, showing that the neural signals from innocuous and noxious stimuli must occur in a precise temporal sequence.

## Key facts

- In 1983, Kupfermann, Carew, and Kandel succeeded in reliably producing classical conditioning of the gill-withdrawal reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p204]
- In classical conditioning, neural signals from innocuous (conditioned) and noxious (unconditioned) stimuli must occur in a precise sequence. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p213]
- When the siphon is touched just before the tail is shocked, predicting the shock, the synapse between sensory and motor neurons is strengthened much more than in sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p213]
- Classical conditioning recruits both homosynaptic and heterosynaptic changes in the neural circuit. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p217]
