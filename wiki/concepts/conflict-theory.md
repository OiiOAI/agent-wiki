# Conflict Theory

## Summary

Conflict theory emerged as an alternative to structural functionalism, dealing with social conflict rather than equilibrium. Unlike consensus theories, conflict theories emphasize the dominance of some social groups by others, see social order as based on manipulation and control by dominant groups, and view social change as occurring rapidly and disorderly as subordinate groups overthrow dominant groups. The first major effort was Lewis Coser's book on the functions of social conflict (1956). Ralf Dahrendorf wrote the most influential conflict theory work "Class and Class Conflict in Industrial Society" (1959), embedding his theory in the Marxian tradition. However, conflict theory was criticized for lacking a sound basis in Marxian theory and often appearing as a mirror image of structural functionalism.
