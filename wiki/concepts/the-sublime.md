# The Sublime

## Summary

The Sublime refers to confrontation with something unknowable and beyond human capacity to conceptualize—death being the ultimate example. The term derives from Latin meaning 'up to the threshold.' Experiencing the Sublime produces both fear and awe, reminding individuals of their smallness in relation to something vaster and more powerful. This experience serves as an antidote to complacency and the petty concerns of daily life, temporarily releasing one from habitual patterns and banality.

## Key facts

- Death represents the ultimate threshold and mystery that humans cannot solve or verbalize, exemplifying the Sublime. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p585]
- The word 'Sublime' comes from Latin meaning 'up to the threshold.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p585]
- Contemplating the infinity of space and the smallness of Earth produces a sensation of the Sublime. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p586]
- Thinking about the origin of life and the billions of years involved exceeds our capacity to conceptualize them, leaving us with a sublime sensation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p586]
- Encountering places where normal compass points are scrambled—a vastly different culture or vast landscapes—physically confronts us with what dwarfs us. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p586]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Sublime experience provides a 'foretaste of death itself' that shakes us from smugness.
