# Strategies for Establishing Authority

## Summary

Greene outlines six key strategies for establishing authentic authority: (1) Find your authority style through authenticity—identifying natural archetypes like Deliverer, Founder, Visionary Artist, Truth Seeker, Quiet Pragmatist, Healer, or Teacher; (2) Focus outwardly through the attitude of listening, earning respect, and seeing leadership as a tremendous responsibility; (3) Cultivate the third eye through vision—elevating perspective above daily chaos to see larger patterns and foresee trends; (4) Lead from the front by working harder than everyone else and setting the highest standards; (5) Stir conflicting emotions through aura—mixing qualities to create fascination and respect; (6) Never appear to take, always to give—avoiding any perception of taking resources, rights, or privileges from followers.

## Key facts

- Six strategies for establishing authority: (1) Find your authority style through authenticity, (2) Focus outwardly through listening and earning respect, (3) Cultivate vision by elevating perspective, (4) Lead from the front with hard work and high standards, (5) Stir conflicting emotions through aura, (6) Never appear to take, always to give. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p565]
- Authority archetypes include: the Deliverer (determined to deliver people from evil), the Founder (establishing new orders, sensing trends), the Visionary Artist (creating new styles), the Truth Seeker (intolerant of lies), the Quiet Pragmatist (fixing broken things), the Healer (unifying people), and the Teacher (getting people to learn from mistakes). [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p565]
- Focusing outwardly involves: honing listening skills, reading between lines of what people say, attuning to their moods, earning respect rather than feeling entitled to it, and feeling visceral connection to the group's fate. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p566]
- Vision requires disconnecting from emotions roiling the group, entertaining enemy perspectives, listening to outsiders, and gaming out possible trends and how things could go wrong. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p568]
- Leading from the front means being seen working as hard as or harder than everyone else, sharing sacrifices, and setting limits that are fair—done from the beginning, as first impressions are critical. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p569]
- Creating aura through mixed signals—showing qualities slightly contrary to the dominant persona—causes people to pause in categorizations and think about who you really are, creating fascination. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p570]
- Taking something people assumed they possessed creates basic insecurity and calls into question the leader's legitimacy, stirring doubts about what else they will take and whether they have been fooling everyone. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p571]

## Inferences

- Inference: The strategy of 'never appear to take, always to give' explains why Elizabeth I's personal frugality and reduction of court expenses were so politically effective—they reinforced her legitimacy and prevented the Entitlement Curse from developing.
