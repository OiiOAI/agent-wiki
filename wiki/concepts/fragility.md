# Fragility

## Summary

Fragility is the negative counterpart of antifragility—systems, objects, or structures that lose from volatility, disorder, and uncertainty. Technically, fragility corresponds to negative convexity effects. Fragile things are concave in their response to stressors and are sensitive to perturbations in the left tail of probability distributions.

## Key facts

- Fragility is technically defined as negative convexity effects—sensitivity to perturbations in the left tail of the probability distribution. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p533]
- Fragile Type 2 systems exhibit limited gains but larger losses, with increasing uncertainty causing mostly negative outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p547]
- Fragility increases nonlinearly as a function of uncertainty—small increases in model error can lead to catastrophic underestimation of tail risks. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p575]

## Inferences

- Inference: Anything that is not antifragile is either fragile or robust; there is no third category.
