# Oral Phase

## Summary

The oral or 'cannibalistic' phase is the earliest recognizable sexual organization, during which the original attachment of sexual excitation to the nutritional drive dominates. The sexual aim could only be cannibalism—devouring. This phase leaves permanent marks on the usages of language (people speak of an 'appetizing' love object, describe persons they are fond of as 'sweet'). Anxiety belonging to this phase manifests as fear of death and may attach to anything pointed out to the child. The patient expressed this phase through his fear of 'being eaten by the wolf' (later translated as fear of being copulated with by his father) and through his initial refusal to eat anything except sweet things.

- The patient was told about an uncle who had refused to eat in the same way and had wasted away to death, and that his own winding-sheet had been prepared when he was three months old during a serious illness. - The fear of 'being eaten by the wolf' was a regression from a higher stage expressing the cannibalistic oral aim. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5358]

## Inferences

- Inference: The patient's disturbance of appetite was the first of his neurotic illnesses, rooted in processes in the sphere of sexuality, with his observation of copulation possibly having accelerated sexual maturing.
- Inference: The patient later overcompensated for eating, as though to guard himself against the threat of death that had been evoked to make him eat again.
