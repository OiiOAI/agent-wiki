# Stigma

## Summary

Goffman's Stigma (1963) examines the gap between virtual social identity (what a person ought to be) and actual social identity (what a person actually is). Anyone with a gap between these identities is stigmatized. Goffman distinguished between discredited stigma (differences known or evident to the audience) and discreditable stigma (differences neither known nor perceivable). Goffman came to argue that we are all stigmatized at some time or in some setting.

## Key facts

- Stigma focuses on the dramaturgical interaction between stigmatized people and normals. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p271]
- Virtual social identity is what a person ought to be, while actual social identity is what a person actually is. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p271]
- Discredited stigma exists when the actor assumes differences are known or evident to audience members, such as a paraplegic or someone who has lost a limb. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p271]
- Discreditable stigma exists when differences are neither known by audience members nor perceivable by them, such as someone who has had a colostomy or a homosexual passing as straight. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p271]
- Goffman came to argue that we are all stigmatized at some time or in one setting or another, with examples including Jews passing in Christian communities and fat persons in groups of normal weight. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p271]
