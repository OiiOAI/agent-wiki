# Pleasure Principle

## Summary

The pleasure principle is described as governing the course of mental events, seeking to keep the quantity of excitation low and to avoid unpleasure or achieve pleasure. Freud argues that while the pleasure principle dominates mental life, it is opposed by certain forces and circumstances, and can be overridden by the compulsion to repeat and by the reality principle.

## Key facts

- Freud defines the pleasure principle as governing 'the course of those events...invariably set in motion by an unpleasurable tension, and that it takes a direction such that its final outcome coincides with a lowering of that tension'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5587]
- The pleasure principle is replaced by the reality principle under the influence of the ego's self-preservative drives, though the pleasure principle 'long persists' in the sexual drives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5590]
- Freud acknowledges that 'strictly speaking it is incorrect to talk of the dominance of the pleasure principle over the course of mental processes' because most mental processes are not accompanied by pleasure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5589]
- The compulsion to repeat, operating in traumatic neuroses and childhood play, represents the 'demonic' power that can override the pleasure principle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5602]
- The pleasure principle dominates the operation of the mental apparatus from the start, but its program is at loggerheads with the whole world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6612]
- Satisfaction of needs dammed up to a high degree produces intense happiness, but such satisfaction is only possible as an episodic phenomenon. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6612]
- Men can derive intense enjoyment only from contrast and very little from a state of things, limiting possibilities of happiness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6612]
- The pleasure principle under the influence of the external world changes into the more modest reality principle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6613]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'beyond the pleasure principle' Freud introduces is not a rejection of the principle but rather an acknowledgment of a more primitive drive force (death drives) that can override it.
- Inference: Dreams generally serve the pleasure principle through wish-fulfillment, but traumatic dreams may serve the prior function of binding unprocessed excitations.
