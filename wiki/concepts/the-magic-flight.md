# The Magic Flight

## Summary

When the trophy has been won against the opposition of its guardian, or when the hero's wish to return has been resented by gods or demons, the final stage becomes pursuit. This flight may be complicated by magical objects tossed behind to delay pursuers—protective interpretations, symbols, or rationalizations that absorb the started Hound of Heaven's power.

## Key facts

- The magic flight is characterized by objects left behind to speak for the fugitive and delay pursuit, as in the tale of children whose huts, trees, and temple answered their pursuer's calls. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p156]
- The magic objects tossed behind by the fleeing hero—protective interpretations, principles, symbols, rationalizations—delay and absorb the power of the Hound of Heaven. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p157]
- The Welsh tale of Gwion Bach shows transformation flight: hare fleeing greyhound, fish pursued by otter, bird hunted by hawk, finally becoming a grain swallowed by a hen. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p155]
