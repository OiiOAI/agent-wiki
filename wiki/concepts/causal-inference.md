# Causal Inference

## Summary

Causal inference is the scientific discipline that enables researchers to move beyond associations in data to understand and quantify cause-and-effect relationships. It provides mathematical tools including causal diagrams and the do-operator to distinguish between observation (seeing) and intervention (doing).

## Key facts

- Causal inference provides a mathematical language to articulate causal relationships that were previously considered unscientific or unanswerable. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L40-L41]
- The do-operator P(Y | do(X)) enables scientists to estimate the effect of intervening on X on outcome Y, which classical statistics cannot express. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L82]
- Causal inference allows prediction of intervention effects without actually enacting them. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L86-L87]
- The discipline can address counterfactual questions such as 'Would Joe have lived had he not taken the drug?'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L91-L93]

## Inferences

- Inference: Causal inference represents a paradigm shift from traditional statistics because it explicitly models the data-generating process rather than merely summarizing data patterns.
