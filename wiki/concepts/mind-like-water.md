# Mind Like Water

## Summary

Mind Like Water is a karate metaphor describing a state of perfect readiness and appropriate responsiveness. Just as water responds totally appropriately to the force and mass of a pebble thrown into a pond, then returns to calm, a person with Mind Like Water responds appropriately to inputs without overreaction or underreaction, then returns to equilibrium. This state is accessible to everyone and is increasingly necessary for dealing with complexity in modern life.

## Key facts

- Mind Like Water is a metaphor from karate defining the position of perfect readiness. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p43]
- When a pebble is thrown into still water, it responds appropriately to the force and mass, then returns to calm—the same response expected of a mind like water. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p43]
- Water can overwhelm but is not overwhelmed; can be still but is not impatient; can be forced to change course but is not frustrated. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p43]
- Anything that causes you to overreact or underreact can control you. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p43]
- The martial artist Craig Lambert describes a similar state in rowing called 'swing'—a frictionless state where the boat carries the rower rather than the rower forcing the boat. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p43]
