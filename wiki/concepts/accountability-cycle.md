# The Accountability Cycle

## Summary

The Accountability Cycle is one of the Three Commitments, emphasizing that taking complete ownership of outcomes by holding no one but oneself responsible drives success. The cycle involves acknowledging reality, owning the problem, looking for solutions, assuming one is part of the solution, and acting. The book contrasts accountable managers who adapt to market changes with victims who blame others. Finding an accountability partner (mentor, peer, or coach) significantly increases success rates, with those sending weekly progress reports to friends being 76.7 percent more likely to achieve goals.

## Key facts

- Accountability is described as the most important of the three commitments. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p148]
- The accountability cycle involves: acknowledging what is happening, owning the problem, looking for solutions, assuming you are part of the solution, and acting. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p149]
- Accountable people see outcomes as information to frame better actions for better outcomes. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p151]
- An accountability partner provides frank objective feedback, creates ongoing expectations for progress, and can provide expertise. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p151]
- Ericsson's research confirmed that elite performers seek out teachers and coaches while amateurs rarely engage in supervised training. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p151]
- The book states it is never too soon or too late to get a coach. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p152]
