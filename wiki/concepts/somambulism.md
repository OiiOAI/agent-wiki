# Somnambulism

## Summary

Somnambulism (deep hypnosis with amnesia) is described by Freud as the decisive criterion for effective hypnotic treatment. In this state, the hypnotized subject's consciousness is sharply cut off from ordinary consciousness, resulting in amnesia upon waking. Only with somnambulism can the physician deny the reality of pains with complete confidence, making it the optimal state for therapeutic suggestion. However, Freud notes the chief deficiency of hypnotic therapy is that it cannot be dosed—the depth of hypnosis attainable does not depend on the physician's procedure but on the patient's chance reaction.

## Key facts

- Somnambulism means that the state of consciousness brought about in hypnosis is cut off from the ordinary one sufficiently sharply for the memory of what occurred during hypnosis to be absent after waking. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p120]
- In somnambulism the physician can deny the reality of the pains that are present with the greatest decision, which he is as a rule unable to do if he knows the patient will later contradict him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p120]
- The chief deficiency of hypnotic therapy is that it cannot be dosed; the degree of hypnosis attainable does not depend on the physician's procedure but on the chance reaction of the patient. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p120]
- Somnambulistic subjects may without fear be allowed to speak, walk about and work, and the physician obtains the most far-reaching psychical influence over them by questioning them under hypnosis. ## Inferences

- Inference: The emphasis on somnambulism as the optimal hypnotic state for treatment foreshadows Freud's later focus on accessing unconscious material through psychoanalysis.
