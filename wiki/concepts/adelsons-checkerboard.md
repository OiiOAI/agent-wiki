# Adelson's Checkerboard

## Summary

Adelson's Checkerboard is a visual illusion demonstrating how context shapes colour and brightness perception. Squares A and B appear different despite being identical shades of grey—the brain's visual system uses contextual information about shadows to infer brightness, and this prior knowledge influences conscious perception automatically and irresistibly.

## Key facts

- Squares A and B in Adelson's Checkerboard are the same shade of grey, but appear different due to contextual shadow information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p91]
- The brain's visual system has inscribed deep in its circuitry knowledge that objects in shadow appear darker. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p91]
- Knowing that A and B are the same colour does not prevent them from appearing different—the effect is automatic and irresistible. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p91]
