# Sleep Apnea

## Summary

Sleep apnea is a common sleep disorder affecting approximately 5% of adults in which the upper airway repeatedly collapses during sleep, causing brief awakenings to restore breathing. The airway collapse occurs because muscle tone falls during sleep and relaxation of airway dilator muscles (such as the genioglossus) exceeds the structural support available. This fragmented sleep results in daytime sleepiness, impaired cognition, and increased cardiovascular risk. Treatment includes continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP), surgery, dental devices, or weight loss.

## Key facts

- During sleep, muscle tone falls and relaxation of airway dilator muscles such as the genioglossus can cause collapse of the airway in susceptible individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]
- Airway obstructions cause oxygen desaturation and hypercapnia, activating chemosensory systems in the medulla that activate neurons in the parabrachial nucleus promoting awakening. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]
- Sleep apnea causes daytime sleepiness, impaired vigilance, and difficulty with cognitive tasks requiring learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]
- Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) devices deliver pressurized air via the nose to inflate and open the airway during sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1140]

## Inferences

- Inference: Some residual cognitive impairment after CPAP treatment may result from neuronal injury caused by repeated episodes of hypoxia during untreated sleep.
