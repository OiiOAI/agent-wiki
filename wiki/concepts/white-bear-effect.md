# The White Bear Effect

## Summary

The white bear effect, discovered by Dan Wegner, describes how attempting to suppress a thought (like thinking about a white bear) makes that thought more likely to return. Langer's research found this effect only occurs in people who have a preconceived notion about what the object is—suggesting that by introducing variability or choices, people can gain more control over their thoughts.

## Key facts

- Wegner found that when instructed not to think about a white bear, people could not suppress the thought—it came back to mind. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p188]
- Langer's students found that when people were shown four different-looking white bears (thin, fat, old, young) before being told not to think about a white bear, they had no difficulty following the instruction. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p188]
- Langer argues that rather than trying not to think about something, such as whether cancer is incurable, we would be better off thinking about it differently—reframing from different angles to achieve new personal control. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p188]

## Inferences

- Inference: This finding suggests we can control our thoughts more than we realize, and that the key is not suppression but rather reframing or introducing variability in how we think about things.
