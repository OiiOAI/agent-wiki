# Secondary emotions

## Summary

Secondary emotions are emotional responses that develop once individuals begin experiencing feelings and forming systematic connections between categories of objects and situations and primary emotions. They require both limbic system structures and prefrontal/somatosensory cortices, involving the activation of acquired dispositional representations that embody individual experience.

## Key facts

- Secondary emotions occur when the brain forms systematic connections between categories of objects and situations and primary emotions, based on individual experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p154]
- Secondary emotions require structures in the limbic system as well as prefrontal and somatosensory cortices, broadening the network beyond what primary emotions need. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p154]
- Prefrontal acquired dispositional representations respond automatically to signals from processed images, embodying knowledge about how situations have been paired with emotional responses in individual experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p156]
- Secondary emotions are piggy-backed on the machinery of primary emotions; nature did not select independent mechanisms for both types. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p158]
