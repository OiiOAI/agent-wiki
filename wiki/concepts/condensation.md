# Condensation (Dream)

## Summary

Condensation (Verdichtung) is one of the four mechanisms of the dream-work. It results in the manifest dream having a smaller content than the latent dream thoughts. Freud identifies three ways condensation occurs: omission of certain latent elements, passage of only fragments of some complexes into the manifest dream, and fusion of latent elements that share something in common into single manifest elements.

## Key facts

- Condensation is brought about by (1) total omission of certain latent elements, (2) only a fragment of some complexes passing into the manifest dream, and (3) latent elements with something in common being combined and fused. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4942]
- Condensation creates composite figures that may combine features of several people, emphasizing what they have in common. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4942]
- Condensation sometimes produces a blurred and vague image, like superimposing several photographs on the same plate. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4942]
- Condensation is traceable to a mechanical or economic factor rather than directly to the dream censorship, though the censorship profits from it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4943]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mechanism of condensation in dreams parallels Francis Galton's technique of composite photography, which Freud used as an analogy.
