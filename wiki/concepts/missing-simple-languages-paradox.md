# The Missing Simple Languages Paradox

## Summary

The missing simple languages paradox is a key thesis of the book: if language were merely a complex form of communication, we would expect to find simpler versions in other species. But no such simple symbolic languages exist in nature, nor can other species readily learn them even with extensive human training. This paradox suggests that language complexity is not the barrier—what prevents nonhuman species from having language is the symbolic threshold, not grammatical complexity.

## Key facts

- A simple language with only five or ten vocabulary items and toddler-level syntax would satisfy the core criteria of language, yet no such communication system exists in nonhuman species. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p41]
- Despite many intelligent species capable of learning complex associations, no nonhuman species can readily learn simple languages, suggesting the barrier is not complexity but symbolic reference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p41]
- The paradox cannot be explained by complexity alone, by intelligence differences, by articulatory limitations, or by built-in grammar predispositions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p42]

## Inferences

- Inference: The absence of simple languages suggests that language evolution required crossing the symbolic threshold first, after which complexity could increase incrementally. [Based on: p.45]
