# Edinger's Ladder

## Summary

Ludwig Edinger's influential but now superseded theory proposing that the vertebrate forebrain evolved through sequential addition of new structures, with older reptilian structures at the base and newer mammalian structures layered on top.

## Key facts

- Edinger noticed that hindbrain and midbrain were similar across vertebrates, but the forebrain varied in size and complexity in what appeared to be progression from fish to mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p187]
- Edinger proposed that the basal ganglia dominated the forebrain in fish, amphibians, and reptiles, with cortex and other areas added later in mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p188]
- Starting in the 1970s, Harvey Karten, Glenn Northcutt, and others discovered homologues of neocortex in reptiles and birds, contradicting the sequential building-block theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p190]
- Fish and amphibians were found to have a striatum, contradicting Edinger's claim that it only appeared with reptiles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p190]
- Ann Butler and William Hodos noted that evolution creates diversity through divergence, not by accumulation of features, and does not create superior or inferior organisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p191]

## Inferences

- Inference: Edinger's ladder model reflected the scala naturae (ladder of nature) thinking rather than Darwin's branching tree model of evolution.
