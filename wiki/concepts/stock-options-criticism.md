# Stock Options Criticism

## Summary

Munger strongly criticizes the use of stock options as executive compensation, arguing they create perverse incentives and represent both an expense and dilution that should be accounted for properly. He views the theory that options have no cost as contributing to corporate excesses and describes the failure to expense options as accounting fraud approved by Congress.

## Key facts

- Giving stock options to a CEO in his sixties to incentivize loyalty is described as demented. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
- The stock option system may give extraordinary rewards to people who did nothing and nothing to those who deserve a lot. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
- Berkshire Hathaway does not use stock options except where they inherit them. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
- Black-Scholes works for short-term options but is insane for long-term options if you know something about value. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
- At Costco, Black-Scholes valued $60 strike options higher than $30 strike options, which Munger calls insane. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
- Munger states that a stock option is both an expense and dilution, and argues not expensing them is insane. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
- Munger describes the 88 senators who voted to maintain not expensing stock options as stupid and dishonorable. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p127]
