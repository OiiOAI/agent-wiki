# Authority-Misinfluence Tendency

## Summary

Man was born to follow leaders in dominance hierarchies, and this tendency causes miscognition when leaders are wrong or their ideas are misunderstood. Milgram's famous obedience experiments demonstrated this tendency. Munger provides examples from military, medical, and business contexts where authority figures caused disasters.

## Key facts

- Milgram's experiment showed that a man posing as an authority figure could trick ordinary people into giving what appeared to be massive electric shocks to innocent fellow citizens. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p589]
- A pilot misinterpreted a minor shift by a general in the copilot's seat as a direction, crashed the plane, and became a paraplegic. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p587]
- A physician's prescription 'Tho drops, twice a day, rear' led a nurse to direct a patient to put eardrops in his anus. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p587]
- Warren Buffett always acts like an overquiet mouse around his pilots to avoid triggering Authority-Misinfluence Tendency. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p588]

## Inferences

- Inference: Authority-Misinfluence Tendency explains why copilot training now includes simulator exercises where copilots must ignore obviously foolish orders from captain pilots—ordinary people in these situations often allow planes to crash despite clear knowledge of the error.
