# Primary Process

## Summary

Primary process refers to the uninfluenced mode of psychical functioning described in Freud's 1895 Project. In primary process, cathexes flow freely according to facilitation and quantity, without inhibition by the ego. Characteristics include: displacement of cathexis along associative pathways, condensation (combination of multiple ideas), hallucinatory satisfaction of wishes, and disregard for reality testing. Primary processes dominate during dreams, the transference, and psychoneuroses.

## Key facts

- Freud defined psychical primary processes as: 'Wishful cathexis to the point of hallucination [and] complete generation of unpleasure which involves a complete expenditure of defence.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p399]
- Primary processes occur when the ego is unloaded, as during sleep: 'The precondition of psychical primary processes' is 'the ideal state of inertia, rid of his store of Qη.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p361]
- Primary processes follow 'the compulsion to associate': 'Two cathexes that are present simultaneously must, so it seems, be brought into connection.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p363]
- Primary processes are 'biologically suppressed in the course of ψ development' but 'are daily presented to us during sleep' and 'have the greatest similarity to dream processes in the psychoneuroses.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p361]
- Primary processes involve the free displacement of Qἠ and are characterized by the absence of negation, doubt, or degrees of certainty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p351]
- The secondary process involves the ego binding Qἠ, permitting only small displacements for experimental purposes rather than full discharge. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p360]
- The secondary process requires two conditions: a cathected ego and a process that is not the motor one (i.e., not leading to discharge). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p362]
- Consciousness attaches to a comparatively quiet lingering of cathexis rather than to intense current of Qἠ. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p343]

## Inferences

- Inference: Primary process represents a return to earlier modes of psychical functioning, comparable to the auto-erotic current in sexual development.
- Inference: The concept of primary process underlies Freud's theory of the unconscious as operating according to different principles than conscious thought.
- Inference: The distinction between primary and secondary processes became foundational to psychoanalytic theory, persisting throughout Freud's work under various formulations
- Inference: Secondary processes 'ride upon' primary processes, suggesting a developmental and functional relationship between the two modes
