# Gene Pool

## Summary

The gene pool is the total set of genes in a breeding population available to be passed on to offspring. Dawkins uses this concept to explain how genes persist over evolutionary time. Each gene competes with its alleles for positions on chromosomes of future generations, making genes direct competitors with their own alleles while other genes form part of the environment.

## Key facts

- The gene pool is defined as the total set of genes in a breeding population available to be passed on to offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p52]
- A gene may be regarded as a unit that survives through a large number of successive individual bodies as paths constantly cross and recross down the generations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p50]
- Genes are competing directly with their alleles for survival, since their alleles in the gene pool are rivals for their slot on the chromosomes of future generations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p63]
- The gene pool functions as the modern equivalent of the primeval soup, providing the environment in which genes make their living. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p72]
- Evolution is the process by which some genes become more numerous and others less numerous in the gene pool. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p72]
