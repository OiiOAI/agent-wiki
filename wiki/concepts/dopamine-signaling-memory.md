# Dopamine Signaling in Memory

## Summary

Dopamine acts as a key modulatory neurotransmitter in the hippocampus for converting short-term to long-term explicit memory. Dopamine release in the hippocampus is recruited voluntarily (top-down) by the cerebral cortex, contrasting with the bottom-up serotonin recruitment in implicit memory. This dopaminergic signal marks the importance of a stimulus for long-term storage.

## Key facts

- Dopamine is recruited to switch a short-term homosynaptic change into a long-term heterosynaptic change in the mouse hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p303]
- Like serotonin in Aplysia, dopamine prompts a receptor in the hippocampus to activate an enzyme that increases cyclic AMP. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p303]
- In spatial memory, dopamine appears to be recruited voluntarily from the top down: the cerebral cortex activates cells that release dopamine, and dopamine modulates activity in the hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p324]
- Dopamine modulates the amount of the prion-like CPEB protein (CPEB-3) in the mouse hippocampus, similar to how serotonin modulates CPEB in Aplysia. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p324]
- There is an age-related loss of synapses that release dopamine in the hippocampus, contributing to age-related memory loss. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p337]
