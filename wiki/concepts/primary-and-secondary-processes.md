# Primary and Secondary Processes

## Summary

Primary and secondary processes represent two modes of mental functioning Freud developed from his 1895 'Project for a Scientific Psychology.' Primary process (unconscious) operates on wish fulfillment, displacement, and condensation; secondary process (preconscious/conscious) governs reality-oriented thinking and postpones gratification.

## Key facts

- The 'Project for a Scientific Psychology' (1895) first articulated the distinction between primary and secondary processes, based on the Helmholtzian physiological tradition and the neuron doctrine. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1265]
- Primary process is characterized by wish fulfillment, free movement of cathexis (psychic energy), and lack of negation, while secondary process introduces reality testing and delay of gratification. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1266]
- Freud stated that much of the seventh chapter of The Interpretation of Dreams and later metapsychological studies only became fully intelligible after the publication of the 'Project.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1265]
- The primary psychical process admits only thoughts that involve wish fulfillment; it seeks free discharge of excitation and aims to establish perceptual identity with the experience of satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1834]
- The secondary psychical process develops later, inhibits primary process discharge through cathexis by the second system, and redirects activity toward establishing 'thought identity' rather than perceptual identity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1834]
- The dream-work's characteristic processes—condensation, formation of intermediate ideas resembling compromises, loose associations based on homonyms and verbal similarities, and tolerance of contradictions—are features of the primary process. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1831]
- A normal train of thought is submitted to abnormal psychical treatment (condensation, compromise formation, superficial associations, regression) only if an unconscious wish derived from infancy and in a state of repression has been transferred onto it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1832]
- The unpleasure principle regulates excitation in the second system as much as in the first; the second system can only cathect an idea if it can inhibit any development of unpleasure proceeding from it—this is the key to repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1835]
- Primary processes are present in the mental apparatus from the first, while secondary processes unfold during the course of life and may not achieve complete domination until the prime of life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1838]
- The core of our being, consisting of unconscious wishful impulses, remains inaccessible to the understanding and inhibition of the preconscious due to the belated appearance of secondary processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1838]
- The unconscious wishes exercise a compelling force upon all later mental trends, a force which those trends are obliged to fall in with or may endeavour to divert and direct to higher aims. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1838]
- Primary processes appear wherever ideas are abandoned by the preconscious cathexis and can become charged with uninhibited energy from the unconscious striving to find an outlet. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1839]
- The irrational processes in the psychical apparatus are the primary ones; they are not intellectual errors but modes of functioning freed from inhibition. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1839]

## Inferences

- Inference: The secondary process corrects primary process activity because condensation and compromise structures obstruct the path toward thought identity; the secondary process therefore seeks to avoid these features.
- Inference: Primary processes represent a theoretical fiction—no psychical apparatus exists with primary processes only—yet they model the earliest stage of mental functioning.
- Inference: The relationship between primary and secondary processes explains why dreams and hysterical symptoms share similar mechanisms: both involve regression to primary process functioning when repression removes preconscious cathexis.
