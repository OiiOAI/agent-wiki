# Taboo

## Summary

In Freud's analysis, taboo denotes prohibitions and restrictions imposed on certain persons, places, things, or conditions that are believed to possess a dangerous magical power (mana). Taboo restrictions are distinct from religious or moral prohibitions: they have no stated grounds and are of unknown origin, yet are enforced with compulsive strictness.

## Key facts

- Taboo is a Polynesian word whose meaning diverges in two contrary directions: 'sacred' or 'consecrated' on one hand, and 'uncanny', 'dangerous', 'forbidden', or 'unclean' on the other. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4135]
- Taboo restrictions are distinct from religious or moral prohibitions because they are not based upon any divine ordinance and fall into no system that declares generally why certain abstinences must be observed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4135]
- Wundt described taboo as the oldest human unwritten code of laws, generally supposed to be older than gods and dating back to a period before any kind of religion existed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4135]
- According to Freud, the essence of taboo is a prohibited action for which a strong inclination exists in the unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4147]
- The two fundamental and oldest taboo prohibitions are: not to kill the totem animal and to avoid sexual intercourse with members of the totem clan of the opposite sex. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4147]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's comparison of taboo with obsessional neurosis suggests he viewed certain unconscious psychological mechanisms as operating similarly across primitive societies and neurotic individuals.
- Inference: The apparent contradiction between the 'sacred' and 'dangerous' aspects of taboo reflects an underlying emotional ambivalence toward tabooed objects and persons.
