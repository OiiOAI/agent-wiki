# Perspective Structure

## Summary

Perspective structure is the aspect of the ambient optic array that changes with every displacement of the point of observation—the shorter the displacement, the smaller the change. This contrasts with invariant structure which persists. Perspective structure specifies locomotion and rest, with flowing perspective structure indicating locomotion and arrested perspective structure indicating rest. Each stationary point of observation has a unique arrested perspective.

## Key facts

- Perspective structure changes with every displacement of the point of observation—the shorter the displacement, the smaller the change, and the longer the displacement, the greater the change. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p97]
- An arrested perspective structure in the ambient array specifies a fixed position relative to the environment (rest), while a flowing perspective structure specifies an unfixed position (locomotion). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p99]
- The particular instance of flow specifies the particular path of locomotion—the difference between beginning and end of optical change is specific to the difference between beginning and end positions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p99]
- Perspective structure and invariant structure are concurrent and reciprocal—one implies the other, like two sides of a coin. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p100]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concurrent nature of perspective and invariant structure explains how the same optical information can specify both the observer's movement and the environment's rigid layout simultaneously.
