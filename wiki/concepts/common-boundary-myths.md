# Common Boundary Myths

## Summary

The authors identify eight common myths about boundaries that Christians often believe. These include beliefs that setting boundaries is selfish, a sign of disobedience, will hurt others, or means one is angry. The authors provide biblical responses to each myth.

## Key facts

- Myth #1: 'If I set boundaries, I'm being selfish'—The authors distinguish selfishness (fixation on own wishes excluding responsibility to love others) from stewardship (taking care of the life God has given us). [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p107-110]
- Myth #2: 'Boundaries are a sign of disobedience'—The authors state that internal 'no' nullifies external 'yes,' and God cares more about hearts than outward compliance. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p111-113]
- The authors state: 'If we can't say no, we can't say yes' because motivation matters—an internal 'no' compromises the 'yes.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p112]
- Myth #3: 'If I set boundaries, I will be hurt by others'—The authors note that boundaries are a litmus test for relationship quality; those who can respect boundaries will love one's separateness. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p113-116]
- The authors emphasize 'bonding first, boundaries second'—one needs supportive relationships before setting boundaries with difficult people. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p115-116]
- Myth #4: 'If I set boundaries, I will hurt others'—Boundaries are defensive tools that prevent treasures from being taken at the wrong time; saying no to adults doesn't cause injury. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p116-117]
- Myth #5: 'Boundaries mean that I am angry'—Anger is a signal that boundaries have been violated; the more biblical one's boundaries, the less anger experienced. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p117-121]
- The authors note that boundary-injured people often experience 'old anger'—years of nos that were never voiced, respected, or listened to. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p119]
- Myth #6: 'When others set boundaries, it injures me'—Difficulty accepting others' boundaries may indicate an idolatrous relationship or projection of one's own injuries. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p121-124]
- Myth #7: 'Boundaries cause feelings of guilt'—The authors distinguish between gifts (no strings attached, requiring only gratitude) and loans (conditional). [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p124-126]
- Myth #8: 'Boundaries are permanent and I'm afraid of burning my bridges'—Boundaries are subject to the owner and can be renegotiated when appropriate. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p126-127]

## Inferences

- Inference: The authors frame boundary-setting as a spiritual discipline aligned with biblical freedom rather than rebellion.
