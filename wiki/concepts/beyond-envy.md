# Beyond Envy

## Summary

Greene presents five strategies for transforming envy into something positive: (1) Move closer to what you envy—see behind the façade and recognize the disadvantages that come with success; (2) Engage in downward comparisons—look at those with less to stimulate gratitude; (3) Practice Mitfreude—actively try to feel others' joy as a form of empathy, coined by Nietzsche; (4) Transmute envy into emulation—redirect comparing into a spur to excellence by believing in your capacity to improve; (5) Admire human greatness—acknowledge achievements without feeling pain from comparison.

## Key facts

- Mitfreude, coined by Nietzsche, means 'rejoicing with'—actively feeling others' joy as a form of empathy rather than experiencing schadenfreude. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p355]
- Having a sense of purpose or calling immunizes against envy because self-worth comes from within rather than from external comparison. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p356]
- The comparison mechanism cannot be eliminated but can be redirected into something productive and creative—wanting to raise oneself up rather than tear others down. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p356]
- Aeschylus wrote: 'For not many men . . . can love a friend who fortune prospers without envying; and about the envious brain cold poison clings.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p357]
