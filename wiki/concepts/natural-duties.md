# Natural Duties

## Summary

Moral requirements that apply to individuals without regard to voluntary acts and have no necessary connection with institutions or social practices. Unlike obligations, their content is not defined by the rules of particular arrangements. They hold between persons irrespective of institutional relationships and are owed to persons generally. Examples include the duty not to harm or injure another, the duty not to cause unnecessary suffering, and the duty of mutual aid. The fundamental natural duty is the duty of justice.

## Key facts

- Natural duties apply to us without regard to our voluntary acts; they have no necessary connection with institutions or social practices; their content is not defined by the rules of these arrangements. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p114]
- It is no defense to say we have made no promise not to be cruel or to come to another's aid; a promise not to kill is normally ludicrously redundant. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p114]
- Natural duties hold between persons irrespective of their institutional relationships; they obtain between all as equal moral persons. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p114]
- The fundamental natural duty is the duty of justice: to support and comply with just institutions that exist and apply to us, and to further just arrangements not yet established when this can be done without too much cost. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p115]
- The natural duty of justice binds citizens generally and requires no voluntary acts to apply; it is more fundamental than the principle of fairness which binds only those who have voluntarily assumed obligations. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p115]

## Inferences

- Inference: Even if principles of natural duty are derived from a contractarian point of view, they do not presuppose an act of consent or any voluntary act in order to apply.
