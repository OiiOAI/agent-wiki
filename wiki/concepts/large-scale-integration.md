# Large-Scale Neural Integration

## Summary

Large-scale integration is the process by which widely distributed brain regions achieve coordinated activity to support coherent conscious experience. Cognitive neuroscience increasingly recognizes that moment-to-moment consciousness is associated with dynamic, large-scale neural activity rather than any single brain region. The key variable for understanding large-scale integration is the nature of dynamic links among neural components, investigated through collective variables describing emergent patterns of integration. Phase synchrony between neuronal populations defines transient patterns of integration corresponding to the duration of present moments of experience. The integration-relaxation dynamics occur at the 1 scale (hundreds of milliseconds), with large-scale patterns being metastable—constantly shifting between unstable regions without settling. This integration is proposed as the neural basis for the temporal structure of consciousness, with each transient pattern of coherence containing a trace of its predecessor and being attracted along a particular trajectory.

## Key facts

- Large-scale integration is the key variable for understanding how coherent neural activities operate from moment to moment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p322]
- Neural synchrony patterns define a temporal frame of momentary and transient neural integration corresponding to the duration of the present moment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p322]
- A neural assembly engages widely distributed brain regions through reciprocal connections at the same and different network levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p323]
- Phase synchrony occurs at various scales—short-range within local networks and long-range over widely separated brain regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p324]
- Large-scale integration requires not only establishment of dynamic links but also their active uncoupling (desynchrony) for the next cognitive act. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p325]
- Metastability means the system constantly shifts from one unstable region to another without settling down to any one of them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p329]
- Whether local neural processes participate in conscious states depends on whether they participate in coherent synchronous large-scale assemblies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p324]

## Inferences

- Inference: Large-scale integration is necessary but not minimally sufficient for consciousness—bodily processes of emotion and motor activity also play crucial roles.
