# Nutritional Psychiatry

## Summary

Nutritional psychiatry is an emerging field that studies the relationship between diet, micronutrients, and mental health, positioning nutrition as a primary or adjunct treatment for psychiatric disorders. It contrasts with the dominant biomedical paradigm, which attributes mental disorders to disordered brain biochemistry and treats them almost exclusively with medication. The field investigates micronutrient supplementation, dietary patterns (such as the Mediterranean diet), the gut-brain axis, and epigenetic effects of nutrition on brain function.

## Key facts

- The field of integrative mental health, which includes nutritional psychiatry, is described as being in its infancy but attracting growing numbers of mental health professionals given the inadequacy of the pharmaceutical approach. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p11]
- A 2018 report to the New Zealand government stated: 'We can't medicate our way out of the epidemic of mental distress.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p42]
- Dr. E. Fuller Torrey and Judy Miller documented that mental illness prevalence rates tripled from 1 to 3 per 10,000 between 1750 and 1960, and the WHO now reports over 2,000 per 10,000—shockingly, one in five people. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p31]
- The DSM diagnostic categories quadrupled from 128 in 1952 to 541 by 2013, reflecting both expanded definitions and cultural changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p33]
- Psychiatrist Allen Frances (2013) stated: 'There has been no real advance in diagnosis since DSM-III in 1980, and no real advance in treatment since the early 1990s.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p42]
