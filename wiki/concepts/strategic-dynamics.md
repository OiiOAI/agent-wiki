# Strategic Dynamics

## Summary

Strategic dynamics refers to the exploitation of waves of change—largely exogenous shifts in technology, cost, competition, politics, and buyer perceptions that can upset existing competitive positions. Waves of change create new high ground and level what had been high ground. A leader's job is to understand the likely evolution and channel resources toward positions that will become valuable and defensible as dynamics play out. The challenge is not forecasting but understanding the past and present.

## Key facts

- Waves of change are largely exogenous—they are mostly beyond the control of any one organization and result from myriad shifts in technology, cost, competition, politics, and buyer perceptions. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p69]
- Exogenous waves of change create new high ground and level what had been high ground—erasing old advantages and enabling new ones. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p69]
- To exploit a wave of change requires understanding the likely evolution and channeling resources toward positions that will become valuable and defensible as dynamics play out. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p69]
- The challenge is not forecasting but understanding the past and present—evidence lies in plain sight waiting for deeper meanings to be read. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p69]
