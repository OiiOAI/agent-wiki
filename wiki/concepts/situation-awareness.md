# Situation Awareness

## Summary

Situation Awareness is identified in the book's table of contents as 'the Key to User-Centered Design'. This suggests the book positions Situation Awareness as a critical outcome or goal of effective User-Centered Design, particularly in the context of system interfaces and decision support.

## Key facts

- Situation Awareness is identified as 'the Key to User-Centered Design' in Chapter 1. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#L88]
- The formal definition of SA is 'the perception of the elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, the comprehension of their meaning, and the projection of their status in the near future'. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p33]
- Level 1 SA involves perception of status, attributes, and dynamics of relevant elements in the environment. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p34]
- Level 2 SA (comprehension) involves synthesizing disjointed Level 1 elements and comparing that information to goals. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p36]
- Level 3 SA (projection) involves predicting what elements will do in the future based on understanding of the current situation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p38]
- In one study, 76% of SA errors in aviation involved Level 1 SA problems (not perceiving needed information). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p36]
- SA varies widely between individuals; as much as a 10-fold difference in SA has been noted between pilots. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p49]
