# Situation Awareness

## Summary

Situation Awareness is identified in the book's table of contents as 'the Key to User-Centered Design'. This suggests the book positions Situation Awareness as a critical outcome or goal of effective User-Centered Design, particularly in the context of system interfaces and decision support.

## Key facts

- Situation Awareness is identified as 'the Key to User-Centered Design' in Chapter 1. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#L88]
- The formal definition of SA is 'the perception of the elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, the comprehension of their meaning, and the projection of their status in the near future'. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p33]
- Level 1 SA involves perception of status, attributes, and dynamics of relevant elements in the environment. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p34]
- Level 2 SA (comprehension) involves synthesizing disjointed Level 1 elements and comparing that information to goals. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p36]
- Level 3 SA (projection) involves predicting what elements will do in the future based on understanding of the current situation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p38]
- In one study, 76% of SA errors in aviation involved Level 1 SA problems (not perceiving needed information). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p36]
- SA varies widely between individuals; as much as a 10-fold difference in SA has been noted between pilots. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p49]
- SA emerged as a major area of study in the late 1980s within human factors research. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p8]
- SA originated from the aerospace industry and cockpit design challenges. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p8]
- SA has been applied to domains including air traffic control, medicine, control rooms, ground transportation, maintenance, space, and education. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p8]
- SA research expanded from system design to include training, selection, and cognitive process research. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p8]
- SA is defined as 'the perception of the elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, the comprehension of their meaning and the projection of their status in the near future' (Endsley, 1988). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p24]
- SA is most frequently defined in operational terms, where for a given operator it is defined in terms of the goals and decision tasks for that job. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- The Endsley model shows SA as a stage separate from decision making and performance, depicted as the operator's internal model of the state of the environment. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p27]
- SA and decision making are distinct but interrelated; it is possible to have perfect SA yet make an incorrect decision, and conversely to make good decisions with poor SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p27]
- SA is derived from environmental perception through visual, aural, tactile, olfactory, or taste receptors, as well as from system displays and communication with others. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p29]
- SA has been studied in diverse domains including aviation, air traffic control, power plants, advanced manufacturing, education, driving, train dispatching, maintenance, weather forecasting, and medicine. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p24]
- Jones and Endsley (1996) found that 76% of SA errors in pilots could be traced to problems in perception, 20% to comprehension problems, and the remainder to projection failures. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p24]
- The information gap refers to the huge gap between tons of data produced and disseminated and the operator's ability to find necessary bits and process them to arrive at actual information required for decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p23]
- Temporal dynamics play an important role in SA formulation; operators constrain parts of the situation based on how soon elements will impact their goals and tasks. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p26]
- SA is a multidimensional construct and not a unitary one, varying across different elements of the situation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p46]
- Pew defines a situation as a set of environmental conditions and system states with which the participant is interacting, characterized uniquely by a set of information, knowledge, and response options. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p52]
- Awareness requirements include current state of the system, predicted state in the near future, information and knowledge required for current activities, activity phase, prioritized current goals, and information needed to support anticipated future contexts. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p53]
- Five dimensions of awareness must be considered: spatial, mission-goal, system, resource, and crew awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p54]
- The ideal SA is defined by experts evaluating requirements at leisure or after the fact, including information and knowledge requirements. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p55]
- The achievable ideal SA is the subset actually available for the crew member to acquire, assuming well-designed information resources and accounting for expected human cognitive abilities. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p55]
- Actual SA is inferred from measurement or observation and is a subset of achievable ideal SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p55]
- The difference between achievable ideal and actual SA creates space for evaluating individual differences and developing training opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p56]
- Endsley (1995) defined SA as 'the perception of the elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, the comprehension of their meaning, and the projection of their status in the near future'. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p91]
- SA is separate from, but provides critical inputs to, decision making that underlies all operator actions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p91]
- Even the best trained, most experienced controllers or pilots can make poor decisions if they have incomplete or inaccurate SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p91]
- The maintenance of SA is critical to operator performance in complex systems. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p91]
- SA is the continuous extraction of information about a dynamic system or environment, the integration of this information with previously acquired knowledge to form a coherent mental picture, and the use of that picture in directing further perception of, anticipation of, and attention to future events. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p225]
- Three aerospace environments are prominent for SA: geographical space around the aircraft (hazards, traffic, weather, terrain), internal aircraft systems (especially automation), and responsibility for tasks confronting pilot, crew, and automated agents. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p225]
- Geometry plays a key role in geographical hazard awareness, Boolean logic in system awareness, and checklists and queues in task awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p225]
- The first commonality underlying SA preservation is the dissociation between the contents of SA and the responses or actions taken on the basis of those contents. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p225]
- The second commonality is the dissociation between the rapidly evolving state of SA and the more enduring characteristics of long-term memory. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p225]
- The third commonality is the distinction between the product of SA (contents of working memory and long-term working memory) and the processes (attention, mental models) necessary to attain it. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p225]
- SA is defined as "the perception of the elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, the comprehension of their meaning and the projection of their status in the near future" (Endsley, 1988, p. 789). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p315]
- SA is a phenomenon mediated by controlled cognitive processes as opposed to automatic cognitive processes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p315]
- The quality of decisions is contingent on an understanding of situation parameters and their relations, and the acquisition and application of accurate and timely information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p316]
- Aviation accidents often provide evidence of faulty decision-making processes resulting from inadequate situation assessment and awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p316]
- In two documented accidents (Air Illinois Flight 710, 1983 and GP Express Flight 861, 1992), flightcrews' inability to form accurate situation models led to loss of SA and subsequent crashes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p318]
- Experts have an overall sense of what is happening in a situation and can judge prototypicality, seeing the big picture. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experts appear less likely to fall victim to information overload compared to novices. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- The RPD model presents situation awareness as emerging from recognition of a situation, including relevant cues, plausible goals, actions to consider, and expectancies. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experts identify 'leverage points' as the central aspect of situation dynamics when explaining situations to others. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experts perceive a situation as the patterns and relationships that grew out of the past and will grow into the future, not just the cues that exist at the moment. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p196]
- The ability to decenter—seeing the world through others' eyes—is part of situation awareness; experienced tank platoon leaders considered cues not present, imagining how adversaries approached and what they were seeing. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p197]
- Metaphors and analogues direct thinking by framing situation awareness, identifying appropriate goals, and flagging relevant pieces of information. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p262]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book's title 'Designing for Situation Awareness' and its placement as a key chapter topic suggests Situation Awareness is the primary outcome that good User-Centered Design should achieve.
- Inference: The three-level model provides a framework for understanding both the nature of SA errors and measurement approaches targeting each level.
- Inference: The distinction between SA and decision making has implications for measurement, suggesting that behavior and performance measures are only indirect indices of operator SA.
- Inference: SA requirements shift with changing contexts, requiring continuous reassessment as situations evolve
- Inference: Because SA is foundational to decision making, interventions that improve SA (such as better displays or communication protocols) may have cascading safety benefits
- Inference: The domain-specific nature of SA means that different operational environments (flight deck, tower cab, en route radar) require identification of unique SA components
- Inference: Situation awareness is central to pilot performance across three distinct domains, suggesting SA measurement must be tailored to each domain's specific metrics.
- Inference: The four commonalities suggest that SA is a multi-faceted cognitive construct requiring multiple assessment approaches.
- Inference: Since SA is critical to aviation safety and depends on controlled cognitive processes, over-reliance on automation during training may reduce operators' ability to maintain SA in posttraining situations.
- Inference: Situation awareness enables experts to filter relevant information from noise, avoiding information overload that affects novices who lack the experiential framework to prioritize cues.
