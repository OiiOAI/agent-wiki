# Figurative Thinking and Poetry

## Summary

Figurative thinking, while helpful for concrete imagery, proved a particular hindrance for S. when reading poetry. The book argues that poetry evokes ideas rather than images—the images in a poem merely clothe meaning, and understanding poetry requires grasping figurative meaning, not literal imagery. S.'s tendency to generate vivid literal images for each word created chaos when reading poetry, preventing comprehension of the poem's actual intent.

## Key facts

- S. found poetry the most difficult thing to read because each expression gave rise to an image that conflicted with other evoked images. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p131]
- Luria argues that to understand poetry, one must grasp the figurative meaning suggested by an image, not the literal sense—the images merely clothe meaning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p131]
- When reading Tikhonov's poem about an old man stomping grapes, S. produced unrelated images: a castle in Rezhitsa, a familiar river, furniture from home—fragmentary images that made no sense. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p132]
- S. described his impression of poetry as 'rather like what you'd get if you accidentally overheard a conversation—fragments of images that made no sense'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p133]
- S. reported that even when reading Pushkin, whom he considered a 'simple' poet, the images that appeared did not prevent him from following the poem but could hardly be said to have helped him. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p138]

## Inferences

- Inference: Luria suggests that normal development involves transitioning from concrete, graphic thinking to verbal-logical thinking where images remain peripheral and are replaced by 'accepted ideas about the meaning of words.' S. was unable to make this transition fully.
