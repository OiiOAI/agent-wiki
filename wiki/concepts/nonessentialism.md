# Nonessentialism

## Summary

Nonessentialism is the undisciplined pursuit of more. Nonessentialists think they must do everything, that it's all important, and that they can do both. They react to what's most pressing, say yes to people without thinking, and try to force execution at the last moment. This leads to feeling out of control, being unsure if the right things got done, and feeling overwhelmed and exhausted.

## Key facts

- The paradox of success has four predictable phases: clarity of purpose leads to success, which leads to a reputation as a 'go to' person with increased opportunities, which leads to diffused efforts, which undermines the very clarity that led to success. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p16]
- The pursuit of success can be a catalyst for failure; success can distract from focusing on the essential things that produce success in the first place. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p17]
- Nonessentialism is driven by three false assumptions: 'I have to,' 'It's all important,' and 'I can do both.'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p34]
