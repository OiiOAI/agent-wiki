# Miraculous Childhood

## Summary

Miraculous childhood is the mythological theme wherein heroes are endowed with extraordinary powers from the moment of birth or even conception. This accords with the view that herohood is predestined rather than simply achieved. The child of destiny must face a long period of obscurity—extreme danger, impediment, or disgrace—while being fostered by helpers ranging from animals to divine beings.

## Key facts

- The tendency in legend-making is to endow the hero with extraordinary powers from the moment of birth, or even the moment of conception, showing the whole herolife as a pageant of marvels with the great central adventure as its culmination. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p252]
- The child of destiny must face a long period of obscurity—a time of extreme danger, impediment, or disgrace. He is thrown inward to his own depths or outward to the unknown, where angel appears, helpful animal, fisherman, hunter, crone, or peasant may foster him. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p256]
- Herakles strangled a serpent sent against his cradle by the goddess Hera; Maui snared and slowed the sun to give his mother time to cook her meals; the baby Buddha sat fixed in a yogic trance beneath a tree while the shadow did not move all afternoon. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The miraculous childhood theme serves to establish the hero's connection to transcendent or divine powers before his earthly work begins, justifying his later role as world-transformer.
