# Separating People from the Problem

## Summary

The first principle of principled negotiation addresses the 'people problem' in negotiation. Human beings have strong emotions, different perceptions, and difficulty communicating clearly. The principle addresses three categories: perception (understanding how the other side sees things), emotion (recognizing and dealing with feelings), and communication (ensuring clear and effective exchange). The key is to deal with the people as human beings and the problem on its merits.

## Key facts

- Negotiators are people first, not abstract representatives; they have emotions, values, different backgrounds, and are unpredictable. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p14]
- The relationship between parties tends to become entangled with their discussions of substance, and positional bargaining aggravates this entanglement. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p14]
- The people problem falls into three categories: perception, emotion, and communication. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p15]
- Understanding the other side's point of view empathetically is one of the most important skills a negotiator can possess, requiring putting yourself in their shoes. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p16]
- Allowing the other side to let off steam is often an effective way to deal with negative emotions, as people obtain psychological release through recounting their grievances. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p19]
- Active listening improves not only what you hear but also what they say; the cheapest concession you can make is to let them know they have been heard. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p21]
