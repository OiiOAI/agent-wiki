# Bottleneck Theory

## Summary

The bottleneck theory explains why life cycles involve squeezing through a single-celled bottleneck (fertilized egg) at the beginning and end of each generation. Bottlenecking fosters the evolution of discrete, unitary organisms through three mechanisms: 'back to the drawing board' (allowing radical design changes), 'orderly timing-cycle' (providing a calendar for embryological events), and 'cellular uniformity' (ensuring genes share identical cellular environments).

## Key facts

- A bottlenecked life cycle means that every organism begins as a single cell (fertilized egg) and ends as single cells (sperms or eggs), with the body serving as a temporary vessel between these bottlenecks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p300]
- The first benefit of bottlenecking is the 'back to the drawing board' effect: each generation makes a clean start, inheriting design ideas in DNA form but not physical organs, allowing radical design changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p302]
- The second benefit is the 'orderly timing-cycle': a stereotyped growth cycle provides a clock by means of which embryological events may be triggered at particular times. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p303]
- The third benefit is 'cellular uniformity': in bottlenecked reproduction, all cells in a daughter plant contain the same genes, ensuring they collaborate in manufacturing efficient survival machines. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p304]
- Bottlenecked life cycles and discrete organisms evolved together and reinforce each other. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p306]

## Inferences

- Inference: The bottleneck theory explains why 'single' organisms like humans are the ultimate embodiment of many genetic mergers—parasite genes that entered the germ line would have been squeezed through the same bottleneck as host genes.
