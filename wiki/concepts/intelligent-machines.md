# Intelligent Machines

## Summary

Intelligent machines, as envisioned by Jeff Hawkins, are systems that work on brain principles including continuous learning, learning via movement, multiple models with voting, and reference frames for knowledge storage. They require embodiment (sensors and movement), some old-brain equivalent for basic behaviors and goals, and a neocortex equivalent for learning.

## Key facts

- Intelligent machines need embodiment—sensors and the ability to move them to learn a model of the world—though embodiment can be physical (robot) or virtual (web crawler). [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p141]
- The neocortex equivalent must be attached to something that already has sensors and behaviors—it does not create completely new behaviors but learns to string existing behavioral primitives together. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p144]
- Intelligent machines will need goals and motivations, which must be specifically designed and embedded—they do not arise automatically from intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p146]
- Silicon-based neocortex equivalents could potentially operate a million times faster than biological neurons, but knowledge acquisition cannot be sped up proportionally due to the need for physical interaction with the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p147]
- Unlike humans, intelligent machines can be cloned—once trained, their learned connections can be transferred to copies without requiring relearning. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p151]

## Inferences

- Inference: The flexibility of machine intelligence lies not just in software but in the ability to create any connections without the energy and space constraints that force biological brains to prune unused wiring.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The practical limits of how much artificial neocortex capacity can improve intelligence, and what a thousand-times-larger brain would enable, remain unknown.
