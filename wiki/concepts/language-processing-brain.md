# Language Processing in the Brain

## Summary

Language processing in the brain involves multiple interconnected cortical regions, with the left hemisphere being dominant in most people. Broca's area controls speech production while Wernicke's area processes auditory input for comprehension. These regions are connected by the arcuate fasciculus.

## Key facts

- Broca's area controls the production of speech and lies near the region of motor cortex that controls mouth and tongue movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p17]
- Wernicke's area processes auditory input for language and is important for understanding speech, located near the primary auditory cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p17]
- Wilder Penfield and George Ojemann confirmed language areas by electrically stimulating the exposed cortex of awake patients during brain surgery. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p19]
- Deaf signers and hearing speakers share common language processing areas, demonstrating that auditory input is not necessary for left hemisphere language capabilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p20]
- Modern fMRI studies reveal that many more cortical areas are activated in language than traditional lesion studies suggested. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p19]
