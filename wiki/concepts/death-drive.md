# Death Drive

## Summary

The death drive is one of two fundamental classes of drives proposed by Freud, working essentially in silence to lead the living creature toward death. These drives manifest outwardly as destructive or aggressive impulses due to the combination of unicellular elementary organisms. The death drives and life drives (Eros) are present in living beings in regular mixtures or fusions, with 'defusions' also liable to occur. Life consists in the manifestations of their conflict or interaction.

## Inferences

- Inference: The death drive concept extends Freud's earlier theoretical framework by providing a biological basis for the destructive and aggressive aspects of human nature.
- Inference: The death drive, when directed outward, serves Eros by allowing the organism to destroy other things instead of destroying itself; the two drives are 'alloyed' together in varying proportions.
- Inference: sadism, the death drive is strongly alloyed with erotism; in masochism, destructiveness directed inward is united with sexuality.
- Inference: The concept of the death drive provides a biological justification for aggressive and destructive impulses, explaining them as fundamental to organic life rather than as aberrations.
- Inference: Freud suggests that internal psychical conflicts correspond to external struggles that ceased as aggressiveness was internalized during civilization's development.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The death drive is inferred primarily from theoretical grounds and Freud acknowledges it is not entirely proof against theoretical objections.
