# Cerebral Cortex Layers and Columns

## Summary

The neocortex is organized into six distinct layers (I-VI) and vertical columns that run from white matter to the pial surface. Each layer has different inputs, outputs, and neuron types. Columns spanning the layers contain neurons with similar response properties and form the fundamental computational modules of the neocortex.

## Key facts

- The neocortex contains six layers numbered from the outer pial surface to the white matter: molecular layer (I), external granular (II), external pyramidal (III), internal granular (IV), internal pyramidal (V), and multiform layer (VI). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p396]
- Layer IV is the main recipient of sensory input from the thalamus and is most prominent in primary sensory areas; the primary motor cortex has essentially no layer IV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p397]
- Layers II and III contain pyramidal neurons that mediate intracortical communication, projecting locally and to other cortical areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p397]
- Layer V pyramidal neurons give rise to major output pathways projecting to subcortical structures including the spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p397]
- Columns are the fundamental computational modules of the neocortex; neurons within a column have similar response properties and form local processing networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p399]
