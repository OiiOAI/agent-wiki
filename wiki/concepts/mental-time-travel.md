# Mental Time Travel

## Summary

Mental time travel refers to the ability to mentally return to a specific place and time in the past, a capability unique to humans. Endel Tulving coined the term and argued that episodic memory enables us to 'roam at will over what has happened as readily as over what might happen.' The hippocampus enables mental time travel by linking together cell assemblies from different brain areas that were active during a past event. When we access an episodic memory, we can pull up contextual information that transports us back. However, the brain has no direct way of perceiving location or exact clock time—it tracks time by capturing changes in the world around us.

## Key facts

- Endel Tulving called episodic memory a form of 'mental time travel,' enabling us to roam over what has happened as readily as over what might happen. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p37]
- The hippocampus enables mental time travel by storing links to the different cell assemblies that were active during a past event. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p44]
- Context cues (places, smells, music, emotional states) can trigger mental time travel by reactivating past contexts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p48]
- Music is exceptionally effective at eliciting mental time travel and can help patients with Alzheimer's disease recall past events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p48]
- Mental time travel recruits past and future versions of oneself as decision buddies, using the same neural pathways engaged when remembering the past, including the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1500-L1505]
- Research by Jeremy Bailenson and Laura Carstensen showed that subjects viewing age-progressed versions of themselves in virtual reality allocated $178.10 to retirement (vs. $73.90 for those seeing present-self), demonstrating future-self as effective decision buddy. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1565-L1570]
- Techniques for mental time travel include: 10-10-10 questioning, backcasting from positive outcomes, premortems from negative outcomes, Ulysses contracts, and future-self visualization tools. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1595-L1610]
- When we view the past and future, we engage deliberative mind, improving rationality compared to in-the-moment decisions driven by emotional reactions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1548-L1550]
- Mental time travel involves two-directional cognitive projection: forward (prospection/future imagination) and backward (autobiographical memory). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2105-L2120]
- The hippocampus plays a key role in both remembering the past and imagining the future. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2105-L2120]
- Daniel Gilbert's research suggests humans have a 'simulation generator' that constructs future scenarios using past experiences. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2105-L2120]
- Duke introduces 'Night Jerry' (future-focused) and 'Morning Jerry' (past-focused) perspectives as a framework for decision-making. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2180-L2190]
- Mental contrasting, as studied by Gabriele Oettingen, involves pairing positive future visions with obstacle identification. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2230-L2240]

## Inferences

- Inference: Organizations like Prudential, AARP, and Merrill Lynch have developed apps showing clients age-progressed versions of themselves to improve retirement planning decisions.
- Inference: Mental time travel is presented as a trainable skill that can be improved through deliberate practice and structured techniques.
