# Mental Time Travel

## Summary

Mental time travel refers to the ability to mentally return to a specific place and time in the past, a capability unique to humans. Endel Tulving coined the term and argued that episodic memory enables us to 'roam at will over what has happened as readily as over what might happen.' The hippocampus enables mental time travel by linking together cell assemblies from different brain areas that were active during a past event. When we access an episodic memory, we can pull up contextual information that transports us back. However, the brain has no direct way of perceiving location or exact clock time—it tracks time by capturing changes in the world around us.

## Key facts

- Endel Tulving called episodic memory a form of 'mental time travel,' enabling us to roam over what has happened as readily as over what might happen. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p37]
- The hippocampus enables mental time travel by storing links to the different cell assemblies that were active during a past event. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p44]
- Context cues (places, smells, music, emotional states) can trigger mental time travel by reactivating past contexts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p48]
- Music is exceptionally effective at eliciting mental time travel and can help patients with Alzheimer's disease recall past events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p48]
