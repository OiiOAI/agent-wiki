# Transference in Dreams

## Summary

Transference in dream formation refers to the process whereby repressed unconscious ideas obtain access to the preconscious by establishing a connection with preconscious ideas and transferring their intensity onto them. This concept, derived from neurosis analysis, explains why trivial recent impressions appear in dreams and why such impressions are preferred—they offer fresh material with few existing associations, making them suitable as 'covers' for unconscious content.

## Key facts

- An unconscious idea is incapable of entering the preconscious on its own; it must establish a connection with an idea belonging to the preconscious, transferring its intensity and getting itself 'covered' by it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1801]
- The unconscious prefers to weave connections around preconscious impressions that are either indifferent (attracting no attention) or have been rejected (attention withdrawn). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1801]
- Recent indifferent impressions are preferred because they have had no opportunity to form many associations and thus face less resistance from censorship. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1801]
- The day's residues serve two purposes in dream formation: they borrow driving force from the unconscious, and they offer indispensable points of attachment for unconscious transference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1802]
