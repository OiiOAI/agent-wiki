# Predatory Imminence Theory

## Summary

Predatory imminence theory, proposed by Michael Fanselow, explains defensive behavior in terms of the momentary imminence of a predator. The prey organism's goal is to exit from the predatory sequence as soon as possible, and the relevant defensive behaviors differ depending on where the predator and prey are in the sequence.

## Key facts

- According to predatory imminence theory, the prey organism's goal is to exit from the predatory sequence as soon as possible. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p97]
- The preencounter stage is the baseline condition where no predator has been detected; the encounter stage involves freezing as the dominant response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p97]
- The circa-strike stage occurs immediately before or after predator contact, where fighting or fleeing becomes the relevant response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p97]
- Fanselow proposed that threats activate a defensive motivational state that lowers thresholds for each defensive response, with freezing having the lowest threshold. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p98]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether defensive motivational states are truly nonconscious in animals, as Fanselow argues, cannot be definitively proven without verbal self-report.
