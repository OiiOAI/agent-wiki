# Arousal

## Summary

Arousal is the brain's fundamental mechanism generated mainly in the brainstem and hypothalamus that determines which neural signals will be transmitted and how strongly. It arises from post-synaptic neuromodulation, which is distinct from synaptic transmission. Arousal adjusts how 'loudly' different neural channels communicate, affecting whether the same stimulus evokes a response. Arousal determines the difference between coma, vegetative state, and fully responsive wakefulness. It is accompanied by feelings and qualitative features, not merely quantitative 'levels'.

## Key facts

- Arousal is generated mainly in the brainstem and hypothalamus and arouses the forebrain by modulating neurotransmission. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p125]
- Synaptic transmission is targeted, binary, and rapid; neuromodulation is messy, slow-acting, long-lasting, and grades the probability that neurons will fire. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p121]
- Neuromodulators spread diffusely through the brain, washing over swathes of networks to regulate the overall 'state' of the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p121]
- The state of the cortex affects the differential strengths of message-passing within its channels—the state adjusts how 'loudly' the different channels communicate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p121]
- Cortical arousal can be modulated both upwards and downwards, to the point of suppressing transmission completely as happens during sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p122]
- Arousal states inscribe lessons more deeply in the channels of our brains; you are more likely to remember a journey when anxiously trying to find an unfamiliar destination than when on autopilot. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p123]
- The shift from vegetative wakefulness to affective arousal depends upon the integrity of the PAG. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p126]
- Affective arousal enables volition—this is what the PAG adds to automatic, vegetative functioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p127]
