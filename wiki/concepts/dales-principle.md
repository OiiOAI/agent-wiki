# Dale's Principle

## Summary

Dale's Principle, proposed by Henry Dale in the 1930s, states that an action potential releases the same type of neurotransmitter from all axon terminals of a neuron. This gives each neuron a distinctive neurochemical profile.

## Key facts

- Dale's Principle states that an action potential releases the same type of neurotransmitter from all of the axon terminals of a neuron. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p707]
- Henry Dale proposed this rule in the 1930s, and it forms the core of each neuroscientist's sense of well-being. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p707]
- This principle means each neuron has a distinctive neurochemical profile—e.g., a 'neurotransmitter A-type' neuron whose targets all have type A receptors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p707]
