# Symptom-formation

## Summary

Symptom-formation is the process by which repressed drive impulses find substitutive expression, allowing the ego to avoid anxiety while obtaining a disguised satisfaction. In conversion hysteria, symptoms involve motor paralyses, contractures, or sensory disturbances that replace blocked excitatory processes. In phobias, symptoms serve to avoid danger situations through restriction and avoidance. In obsessional neurosis, symptoms evolve from defensive prohibitions to increasingly satisfying substitutive formations, eventually compromising the ego's autonomy.

## Key facts

- Symptoms are created to remove the ego from a danger situation whose presence has been signalled by anxiety generation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6346]
- In conversion hysteria, symptoms may replace disturbed excitatory processes, concentrating the energy of the entire process into one part. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6332]
- The secondary gain from illness makes symptoms increasingly indispensable to the ego, operating as resistances during analytic treatment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6321]
