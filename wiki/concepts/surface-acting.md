# Surface Acting

## Summary

Surface acting is the practice of faking emotions to match organizational or social expectations—for example, smiling at customers while feeling frustrated inside. It is contrasted with genuine emotional expression and is associated with burnout, exhaustion, and negative consequences for both individuals and organizations. Research on hotel employees found that surface acting at work spills over into marital conflict at home.

## Key facts

- Surface acting refers to faking your emotions to match what is expected of you in a work or social setting. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p170]
- Surface acting leads to burnout and all sorts of related negative consequences at work, both for you and for your organization, in part because it's just so exhausting. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p170]
- Surface acting at work can seep into your personal life, leaving you depleted and disconnected from your core self. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p170]
- Research found that spouses of hoteliers who surface-acted the most hoped their mates would find a different job as a way of rescuing their domestic lives. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: Surface acting is distinguished from deep acting (genuinely trying to feel the emotions you express), and the text implies that walking your why reduces the need for surface acting.
