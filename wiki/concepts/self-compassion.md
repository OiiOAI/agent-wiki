# Self-Compassion

## Summary

Self-compassion involves treating oneself with the same kindness and support one would offer a good friend. It means acknowledging mistakes or imperfections without harsh self-recrimination. Research shows self-compassion is the antidote to shame—while shame focuses on character as bad, self-compassion allows one to acknowledge wrongdoing while still accepting one's fundamental worth. Self-compassion is associated with better health behaviors, stronger immune function, and greater social connection.

## Key facts

- Self-compassion means forgiving oneself for mistakes or imperfections to move on to more productive things. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p60]
- Self-compassion is not about lying to oneself but about looking at oneself from a broad and inclusive perspective that doesn't deny reality. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p63]
- People who express self-compassion at the beginning of a difficult experience like divorce are doing better nine months later than those who beat themselves up. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p62]
- Self-compassionate people are no less ambitious but don't fall apart when they don't meet goals. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p64]
- Self-compassion is associated with health behaviors like eating right, exercising, sleeping well, and managing stress. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p64]
- Self-compassion means acknowledge your pain and respond with kindness. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p142]
- Self-compassion is radically different from obeying or struggling against pain; we simply acknowledge our pain and treat ourselves kindly. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p142]
- Hundreds of scientific studies published in top psychology journals have demonstrated the many benefits of self-compassion for health, well-being, and happiness. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p142]
- Self-pity sounds like 'I can't bear it any longer, why me, it's not fair, no one else has to go through this' and never helps, only making things worse. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p142]
- The brain changes through addition, not subtraction, so self-compassion involves laying down new neural pathways rather than trying to delete harsh self-talk. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p146]
- Research shows harsh, critical coaching may get positive results short-term but leads to demotivated people and poor performance long-term, while kind, supportive coaching is far more effective. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p144]
- Self-compassion involves acknowledging how hard and painful it is to be human. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p219]
- The Giving Support exercise involves imagining traveling back in time to visit a younger version of yourself and offering the support they needed. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p222]
- People should not go directly back to a traumatic event but rather to sometime after it occurred hours, days, or weeks afterward. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p223]
- Echoes of the past will continue to arise, but we can get better at noticing old patterns and responding effectively when they occur. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p225]
