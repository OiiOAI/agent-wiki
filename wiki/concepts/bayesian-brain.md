# Bayesian Brain

## Summary

The Bayesian brain hypothesis holds that brain circuits compute prior probability distributions and send predictive messages to sensory neurons in an effort to dampen incoming signals. Perception involves comparisons between predicted and actual distributions, resulting in computations of posterior probability. This unconscious process underlies all perception and action. The brain is fundamentally engaged in asking questions of itself about its own states and minimising the difference between predicted and actual outcomes.

## Key facts

- Bayes's theorem says that given a hypothesis followed by some evidence, you must revise the probability of the hypothesis by considering its likelihood in conjunction with its prior probability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p162]
- Brain circuits literally compute prior probability distributions and send predictive messages to sensory neurons in an endless effort to dampen the incoming signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p163]
- Perception literally involves comparisons between predicted and actual distributions, resulting in computations of posterior probability—the resultant inferences are what perception actually is. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p163]
- Perception is an endeavour to self-generate the incoming sensory signals and thereby explain them away. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p163]
- The predictive brain is 'lazy'—vigilant for every opportunity to achieve more by doing less. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p163]
- The fundamental driving force behind volitional behaviour of all life forms is that they are obliged to minimise their own free energy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p164]
- Self-organising systems are 'self-evidencing'—they model the world in relation to their own viability and then seek evidence for their models. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p161]
