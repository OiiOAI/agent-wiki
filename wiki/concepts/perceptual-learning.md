# Perceptual Learning

## Summary

Perceptual learning is a form of implicit learning that improves the ability to discriminate sensory attributes through repeated practice without conscious awareness. In vision, it involves plasticity in cortical connections that enhances detection of contours, orientations, and spatial differences. A key feature is task-specificity: training on one perceptual task does not transfer to other tasks.

## Key facts

- Perceptual learning is a variety of implicit learning that does not involve conscious processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p607]
- Perceptual learning involves repeating a discrimination task many times and does not require error feedback to improve performance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p607]
- Training on one perceptual task does not transfer to other tasks, demonstrating task-specificity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p607]
- Several areas of visual cortex, including the primary visual cortex, participate in perceptual learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p607]
- With practice, subjects improve their ability to detect shorter contours and V1 neurons become correspondingly more sensitive to shorter contours. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p609]
- Henry completed the Gollin test without errors after four trials, and his accuracy was slightly better than that of ten control participants. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p187]
- An hour later, Henry identified the same picture fragments in fewer trials without conscious memory of having seen the test before. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p187]
- When given the same test thirteen years later, Henry's identification was even more accurate, demonstrating long-term retention of perceptual learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p188]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perceptual learning occurs because the functional properties of visual cortex neurons remain mutable throughout life, despite earlier claims that adult visual cortex is fixed.
- Inference: The specificity of perceptual learning suggests that early stages of visual processing are responsible, where receptive fields are smallest and orientation tuning is sharpest.
- Inference: Perceptual learning relies on intact visual cortex rather than medial temporal-lobe structures, explaining why Henry could improve on the task without declarative awareness.
