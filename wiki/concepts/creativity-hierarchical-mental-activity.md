# Creativity as Hierarchical Mental Activity

## Summary

Creativity is best viewed as a sequence of generation-and-verification cycles leading to novel and useful solutions. At the brain level, complex higher-order mental activities appear to be based on distributed networks built upon component neural systems that are reconfigured dynamically in relation to task demands. This hierarchical functional organization makes evolutionary sense because it is more efficient for the brain to reconstitute component systems to serve multiple complex activities than to create separate systems for each. Higher-order mental activities that appear different at the behavioral level (e.g., creativity and planning) are frequently built on largely shared cortical infrastructures at the neural level.

## Key facts

- The creative process is best viewed as a sequence of generation-and-verification cycles leading to novel and useful solutions (Campbell, 1960; Eysenck, 1993; Simonton, 1989). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p425]
- Decision making plays a role in both the generation and verification aspects of creativity, but to different extents and in different ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p425]
- Successful problem solving requires flexible navigation between focused and defocused states of cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p426]
- There appears to be no unitary brain 'module' for creativity; complex higher-order mental activities are based on distributed networks dynamically reconfigured in relation to task demands. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p426]
- A hierarchical functional organization for complex systems is evolutionarily efficient because it is more efficient for the brain to reconstitute component systems to serve multiple complex activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p426]
- Decision making is one of many component processes in creativity, just as it is in reasoning, planning, and problem solving. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p427]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hierarchical view of creativity as involving shared neural infrastructure with other higher cognitive functions suggests that studying component processes can reveal commonalities among mental activities while remaining responsive to evolutionary considerations for cortical organization.
