# Intuitive vs. Rational Thinking

## Summary

The distinction between intuitive (System 1, fast, automatic) and rational (System 2, slow, deliberate) thinking. Intuition excels for practiced activities and repeated decisions within one's circle of competence; rational analysis is necessary for complex novel problems. Willpower depletion impairs rational thinking.

## Key facts

- The intuitive mind is swift, spontaneous, and energy-saving; rational thinking is slow, demanding, and energy-guzzling (in the form of blood sugar). [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p221]
- For practiced activities like playing an instrument or reading balance sheets, trust intuition; for complex novel problems, use rational analysis. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p221]
- We think like lawyers, crafting justifications for predetermined conclusions, rather than scientists seeking objective facts. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p221]
