# Multitasking and Task Switching

## Summary

The belief that humans can multitask is a delusion. When people think they are doing several things at once, their brains are actually rapidly switching between tasks. This switching incurs significant cognitive costs including performance drops, increased errors, reduced creativity, and diminished memory.

## Key facts

- MIT neuroscientist Earl Miller states that the human brain can only produce one or two thoughts in conscious mind at once. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p43]
- The 'switch cost effect' means that every task switch requires the brain to reconfigure, causing slower performance even when the distraction lasts only seconds. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p44]
- An HP study found that technological distraction (emails and phone calls) caused a drop in worker IQ by an average of 10 points—twice the effect of smoking cannabis. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p45]
- A Carnegie Mellon study found that students who received intermittent text messages performed 20 percent worse on tests than those with phones switched off. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p47]
- Average American workers are distracted roughly once every three minutes. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p46]
- The average CEO of a Fortune 500 company gets only 28 uninterrupted minutes per day. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p46]
- Office workers spend 40 percent of their work time wrongly believing they are multitasking. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p46]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cognitive costs of switching mean that reported screen time understates actual productivity loss.
- Inference: Even having a phone switched on and receiving texts constitutes switching and incurs cognitive costs.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise magnitude of IQ drop from distraction may vary across different study populations.
