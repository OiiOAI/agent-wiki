# Overdetermination

## Summary

Overdetermination refers to the principle that hysterical symptoms and dream elements are produced not by single causes but by the convergence of multiple unconscious factors. Freud illustrated this through Dora's dreams and symptoms, showing how each element could represent several simultaneous meanings from different layers of unconscious material.

## Key facts

- Freud stated that 'in the world of reality... an accumulation and conjunction of mental activities—in a word, overdetermination—is the rule'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2345]
- A literary work would sacrifice this element to censorship, simplifying and abstracting; but reality exhibits complication of motives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2345]
- Dora's supervalent thought about her father contained multiple determinants including jealousy of Frau K. based on Dora's unconscious homosexual love. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2347]
- The jewel case in Dora's dream represented multiple meanings: female genitals, sexual wetness, the gift from Herr K., and condensation of infantile and contemporary sources.
