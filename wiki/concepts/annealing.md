# Annealing (Metallurgical)

## Summary

A metallurgical technique involving heating and controlled cooling of metal to increase crystal size and reduce defects. The heat causes atoms to randomly wander through higher energy states; cooling allows them to find better configurations. Taleb uses this as a metaphor for political and social systems, where controlled exposure to stress allows discovery of better equilibrium states.

## Key facts

- Annealing in metallurgy involves heating and controlled cooling to increase crystal size and reduce defects: heat causes atoms to wander randomly through higher energy states while cooling gives them chances to find better configurations. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p140]
- Mathematicians use simulated annealing as a computer optimization method, leveraging randomness to escape local optima and find global solutions. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p140]
- Political annealing: ancient Athenians chose assembly members by lot to protect against degeneracy; computer simulations by Pluchino and colleagues showed adding randomly selected politicians improves parliamentary functioning. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p141]
- Voltaire considered government tempered with political assassination; regicide acts as tapping the barometer to make it work—creating necessary reshuffling. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p141]

## Inferences

- Inference: The annealing metaphor suggests that periodic disruption of political and social systems prevents calcification and allows emergence of better configurations, explaining why long periods of 'stability' become dangerous.
