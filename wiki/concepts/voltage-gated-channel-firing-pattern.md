# Voltage-Gated Channel Regulation of Firing Pattern

## Summary

Neuronal firing patterns are determined by the expression and functional state of various voltage-gated ion channels (Kv1, A-type K+, HCN, Cav3), which can be modulated by synaptic input, activity, and disease states.

## Key facts

- Kv1 channels prevent second action potential firing by increasing spike threshold; blocking these channels with α-dendrotoxin changes firing from strong adaptation to maintained repetitive firing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p232]
- A-type K+ channels generate transient outward current that slows depolarization approach to threshold when the cell is held at hyperpolarized potentials that remove inactivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p232]
- Thalamocortical relay neurons fire spontaneously in bursts driven by Ih current through HCN channels followed by Ca2+ influx through Cav3 channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p233]
- Suprachiasmatic nucleus neurons generate pacemaker potentials driven by persistent Na+ current and Na+ leak through NALCN channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p233]
- Steady hyperpolarization from inhibitory synaptic input can profoundly affect spike train patterns by modulating voltage-gated Ca2+ and K+ channel availability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p233]
- Activity-induced changes in neuronal excitability may involve homeostatic feedback where increased network activity leads to decreased individual neuron excitability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: Dysfunction of regulatory pathways controlling voltage-gated ion channels may underlie some forms of epilepsy and hyperexcitability associated with conditions like neuropathic pain.
