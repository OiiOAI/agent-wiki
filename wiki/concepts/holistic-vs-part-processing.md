# Holistic vs. Part-Based Processing

## Summary

The right hemisphere perceives things as unified wholes (Gestalt) before they are broken into parts, while the left hemisphere builds up understanding from parts. This fundamental difference underlies many higher cognitive functions including language, spatial awareness, and social cognition.

## Key facts

- The link between the right hemisphere and holistic or Gestalt perception is one of the most reliable and durable generalisations about hemisphere differences. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p74]
- The right hemisphere sees the whole before whatever it is gets broken up into parts; its holistic processing is not based on summation of parts. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p74]
- Patients with right-hemisphere damage lose overall coherence and integrity in drawings, unable to grasp the whole; one patient asked to draw an elephant 'draws only a tail, a trunk and an ear'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p76]
- Split-brain subjects have complete inability to relate the shape of something seen to something felt with the right hand, but perform perfectly with the left hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p75]
- Patients with right-hemisphere damage rely on their intact left hemisphere, which starts with pieces and puts them together to get the overall picture, whereas those with left-hemisphere damage prefer a global approach.
