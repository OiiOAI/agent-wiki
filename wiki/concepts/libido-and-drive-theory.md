# Libido and Drive Theory

## Summary

Freud's drive theory evolved significantly, moving from the distinction between ego drives and sexual drives to a dual-drive theory of Eros (sexual drives) and the aggressive/destructive drives. The drives are characterized by source, aim, and object. The theory emphasizes the conservative nature of drives, which seek to restore an earlier state of things, and introduces the concept of the death drive (Thanatos).

## Key facts

- A drive is distinguished from a stimulus by arising from sources of stimulation within the body, operating as a constant force, and the subject cannot avoid it by flight. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6929]
- A drive's source is a state of excitation in the body, its aim is the removal of that excitation; the drive presses in a particular direction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6929]
- The theory proposes two essentially different classes of drives: the sexual drives (Eros) and the aggressive drives whose aim is destruction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6935]
- In sadism and masochism, two excellent examples of a mixture of Eros and aggressiveness are seen; every drive impulse examined consists of similar fusions of these two classes of drives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6936]
- The drives reveal an effort to restore an earlier state of things; a drive arises when a previously attained state is upset, bringing about phenomena described as a compulsion to repeat. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6938]
- The death drive seeks to do away with life and re-establish the inorganic state; from the concurrent and opposing action of Eros and the death drives proceed the phenomena of life which are brought to an end by death. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6939]
- The self-destructive drive may be an expression of a death drive which cannot fail to be present in every vital process. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6939]
- Libidinal organizations pass through phases: oral, sadistic-anal, and phallic, before the genital phase established after puberty. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6931]
- Abraham distinguished two stages in the sadistic-anal phase: an earlier dominated by destructive trends of destroying and losing, a later characterized by trends of keeping and possessing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6932]
- Anal erotism undergoes multiple transformations: interest passes to objects as gifts, then to gold and money, and contributes to the cathexis of baby and penis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6933]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conservative characteristic of drives may not belong to all drives without exception, and even Eros may seek to restore earlier states when striving to create greater unities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all the aggressiveness that has returned from the external world is bound by the superego and turned against the ego, or whether part carries on as free destructive drive in the ego and id.
