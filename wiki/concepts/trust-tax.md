# Trust Tax

## Summary

The Trust Tax is Covey's concept describing the hidden cost incurred when trust is low in relationships or organizations. When trust is diminished, speed decreases while costs increase—analogous to paying a tax on every transaction and interaction. This 'tax' manifests as bureaucracy, politics, defensive communication, micromanagement, and second-guessing, all of which drain organizational energy and resources.

## Key facts

- The formula for low trust impact is: Down Trust leads to Down Speed and Up Cost. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p39]
- Low-trust environments create hidden agendas, politics, interpersonal conflict, win-lose thinking, and defensive communication—all reducing the speed of trust. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p23]
- The book describes varying trust tax levels: an 80% tax represents nonexistent trust with dysfunctional environments; a 40% tax represents low trust with common 'CYA' behavior and hidden agendas. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p49]
- Professor John Whitney of Columbia Business School states that 'Mistrust doubles the cost of doing business.'. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p45]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Trust Tax concept quantifies what would otherwise be invisible costs in organizations, making the case for trust as a measurable business metric.
