# Sleep Stages

## Summary

Human sleep consists of three stages of non-REM sleep (N1, N2, and N3, representing progressively deeper sleep) alternating with REM sleep approximately every 90 minutes. N3, also called slow-wave or deep sleep, shows large synchronized brain waves. REM periods increase in length through the night while N3 decreases and may disappear by mid-night.

## Key facts

- The five sleep stages were originally defined by Rechtschaffen and Kales in 1968, with later revisions combining stages 3 and 4 into current N3. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p60]
- In N1 sleep, slow eye movements (SEMs) take 2-4 seconds to move back and forth, about a tenth the speed of REM. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p66]
- Dream reports are obtained from all sleep stages: approximately 75-80 percent from REM, 50-60 percent from N2, and close to 50 percent from N3. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p67]
- Different sleep stages contribute differently to memory processing: N2 for motor skills, N3 for verbal memory, REM for emotional memory and problem solving. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p89]
