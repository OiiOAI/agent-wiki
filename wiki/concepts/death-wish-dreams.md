# Death Wish Dreams

## Summary

Dreams of the death of persons dear to the dreamer are analyzed by Freud as expressions of unconscious wishes. He distinguishes between cases where the dreamer feels no grief (concealing another wish) and cases where the dreamer feels deep pain. The latter are traced to childhood hostile impulses toward siblings as rivals, and toward parents as sexual rivals.

## Key facts

- When dreams of a loved one's death produce no grief, the dream conceals a wish to see that person, with grief being absent because the wish was not behind the dream. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p248]
- Children are completely egoistic and feel their needs intensely, striving ruthlessly to satisfy them especially as against their brothers and sisters who are rivals. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p250]
- A child's idea of being 'dead' has nothing much in common with adults' concept apart from the word; to children, being dead means approximately the same as being 'gone' or not troubling the survivors. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p254]
- The death wish against parents dates back to earliest childhood and is confirmed with certainty beyond all doubt in the case of psychoneurotics when subjected to analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud traces hostility toward siblings to the situation when a new baby arrives and the elder child feels supplanted, with the wish that the rival may disappear being expressed as death wishes.
- Inference: The sexual theory underlying parent death dreams holds that a girl's first affection is for her father and a boy's for his mother, making the same-sex parent a rival whose elimination would be advantageous.
