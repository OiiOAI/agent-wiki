# Five Intertwined Errors

## Summary

Rumelt identifies five intertwined errors in human judgment and behavior that contributed to man-made disasters including the 2008 financial crisis: (1) engineering overreach, (2) smooth-sailing fallacy, (3) risk-seeking incentives, (4) social herding, and (5) the inside view.

## Key facts

- Engineering overreach occurs when designers build systems whose failure modes exceeded their ability to comprehend or analyze. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- The smooth-sailing fallacy is where people assume that a lack of recent tremors means there is no risk. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- Risk-seeking incentives occur when people profit greatly if things go well and other people take losses when things go poorly. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- Social herding occurs when everyone does the same, resulting in uninformed actions. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- The inside view is the tendency to ignore related data and believe 'this case is different.'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- These five errors also contributed to the Johnstown Flood, Hindenburg burning, Hurricane Katrina aftermath, and BP Gulf oil spill. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
