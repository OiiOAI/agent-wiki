# Inner Authority

## Summary

Inner Authority is the cultivated voice of the higher self that serves as conscience and guide for personal conduct. It balances the lower self—the primitive, animal part of human nature that craves quick pleasures, turns inward, and broods over petty matters. The Inner Authority dictates a code of behavior: contributing to culture rather than merely consuming; cultivating what is unique about oneself; focusing and prioritizing; adhering to highest work standards; maintaining self-discipline. This concept parallels the outer authority that leaders establish over groups, but applies to self-mastery. By following Inner Authority, individuals gain cohesion and force, freedom from anxiety about living below their potential, and true independence from needing external guidance or leadership.

## Key facts

- Inner Authority serves as the voice of the higher self, already present but weak, requiring cultivation to increase its frequency and volume in daily decision-making. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p573]
- The lower self—more primitive and animal—naturally becomes indolent, craves quick pleasures, turns inward, and broods over petty matters, requiring effort to tame. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p573]
- Inner Authority dictates: responsibility to contribute to culture (not merely consume); cultivating uniqueness; focusing and prioritizing; adhering to highest work standards; developing self-discipline and proper work habits. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p573]
- Leonardo da Vinci exemplified Inner Authority through his motto 'ostinato rigore' (relentless rigor)—going beyond commissions, poring over every detail without needing external motivation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p575]
- Following Inner Authority provides cohesion and force, freedom from anxiety about living below one's potential, and the ability to indulge the lower self occasionally without becoming its prisoner. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p575]
- Those operating according to Inner Authority become their 'own mother and father, own leader, truly independent,' needing no external comfort or guidance. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p575]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of Inner Authority suggests that personal excellence and self-mastery are prerequisites for outer authority—leaders who cannot govern themselves cannot effectively govern others.
