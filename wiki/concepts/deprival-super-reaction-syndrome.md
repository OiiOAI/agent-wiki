# Deprival Super-Reaction Syndrome

## Summary

A powerful psychological response where people react disproportionately to losing something they already have, making 'takeaways' extremely difficult to negotiate. This syndrome helps explain gambling behavior and consumer resistance to product changes.

## Key facts

- Deprival super-reaction syndrome explains why people go broke by refusing to stop pursuing a losing investment—they cannot bear to lose what is already committed. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p247]
- Changing Coca-Cola's flavor would trigger deprival super-reaction syndrome because consumers have deeply conditioned preferences for the original taste. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p298]
- The syndrome makes most gamblers irrational and is responsible for much poor decision-making in both gambling and business. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p298]
- Deprival super-reaction syndrome relates to gambling and New Coke—people react disproportionately when they perceive they will lose something they had. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p252]
- The more you put into something, the more the consistency principle makes you think 'now it has to work, if I put in just a little more, then it'll work.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p259]
- People go broke because they can't stop, rethink, and say 'I can afford to write this one off and live to fight again.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p259]
- Changing a successful flavor would trigger in consumers the standard Deprival super-reaction syndrome that makes 'takeaways' so hard to get in any type of negotiation. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p312]
- A large flavor change would allow a competitor, by copying the old flavor, to take advantage of both the hostile consumer super-reaction to Deprival and the huge love of the original flavor created by previous work. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p312]
- Biological creatures ordinarily prefer effort minimization in routine activities and don't like removal of long-enjoyed benefits. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p351]

## Inferences

- Inference: The syndrome explains sunk cost fallacy and why cutting losses is psychologically harder than taking initial risk—losses trigger disproportionate emotional response.
