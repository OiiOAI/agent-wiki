# Unconscious Operations

## Summary

The brain processes that occur without conscious awareness, including automatic motor control, subliminal perception, masked priming, unconscious intention, error detection, and mathematical intuition. The book catalogs extensive evidence that much of cognition—including value-based decisions, attention, and even complex problem-solving—operates unconsciously.

## Key facts

- Unconscious operations are discussed across pages 10-11, 13-14, 17-21, 38-43, and 47-89, making this one of the most extensively covered topics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p47]
- Key unconscious operations include masked priming, subliminal perception, automatic pilot functions, and unconscious motor control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p50-56]
- Pioneers of unconscious research include Freud, Helmholtz, James, Pierre Janet, and Théodule Ribot. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p49-52]
- Unconscious processing can be accessed through subliminal priming with masked words and images. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p56-71]
- The brain can perform unconscious inference, as described in Helmholtz's work on perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p50-51]
- Unconscious mathematics includes statistical learning and unconscious preparation of decisions that later appear conscious. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p79-88]
- Unconscious error detection operates through the medial frontal cortex, sometimes before conscious awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p85-87]

## Inferences

- Inference: The taxonomy of unconscious states includes preconscious (accessible but not attended), subliminal (below threshold), and unconscious (actively suppressed) categories
- Inference: Unconscious operations form the 'automatic pilot' that allows efficient processing while consciousness is reserved for novel or effortful tasks
