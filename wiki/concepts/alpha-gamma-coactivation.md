# Alpha-Gamma Co-activation

## Summary

Alpha-gamma co-activation refers to the simultaneous activation of alpha motor neurons (which contract extrafusal muscle fibers) and gamma motor neurons (which contract intrafusal muscle fibers). This mechanism maintains muscle spindle sensitivity during voluntary movements by preventing spindle unloading when the muscle shortens, allowing spindles to continue signaling changes in muscle length throughout the movement.

## Key facts

- Alpha-gamma co-activation occurs when gamma motor neurons are activated simultaneously with alpha motor neurons during voluntary movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p769]
- When only alpha motor neurons are stimulated, spindle Ia fibers pause firing because the muscle shortens and unloads the spindle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p769]
- Gamma motor neuron activation contracts the intrafusal fibers, keeping the central sensory region under tension and maintaining spindle firing during muscle contraction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p769]
- During slow voluntary finger movements, Ia sensory fibers increase their firing rate even as the muscle shortens, demonstrating alpha-gamma co-activation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p775]
- Alpha-gamma co-activation stabilizes spindle sensitivity and allows the stretch reflex to compensate for unexpected load changes during movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p775]
