# Hypotheses

## Summary

Phædrus developed the observation that the number of rational hypotheses explaining any given phenomenon is infinite. This led him to question the validity of scientific method itself, since if hypotheses grow faster than experiments can test them, then not all hypotheses can ever be tested, making conclusive knowledge impossible.

## Key facts

- Phædrus coined a law that 'The number of rational hypotheses that can explain any given phenomenon is infinite.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p95]
- If all hypotheses cannot be tested, then the results of any experiment are inconclusive and the entire scientific method falls short of its goal of establishing proven knowledge. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p95]
- The time spans of scientific truths appear to be an inverse function of the intensity of scientific effort, suggesting more hypotheses shorten truth's lifespan. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p96]
- Scientific method itself appears to be causing the growth of hypotheses, meaning as you try to move toward unchanging truth, you actually move away from it. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p96]

## Inferences

- Inference: Phædrus's observation about infinite hypotheses represents a nihilistic critique of scientific rationality that troubled him deeply.
