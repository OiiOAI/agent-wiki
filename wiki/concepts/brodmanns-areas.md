# Brodmann's Areas

## Summary

The 52 distinct functional areas of the human cerebral cortex identified by Korbinian Brodmann based on cytoarchitectonics—the distinctive shapes of cells and variations in their layered arrangement. This map remains a foundational tool in neuroscience, with specific areas now known to correspond to particular functions such as motor control, somatosensory processing, vision, and hearing.

## Key facts

- Brodmann identified 52 discrete functional areas in the human cerebral cortex based on cell structure and layer arrangement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p64]
- Area 4 is the motor cortex responsible for voluntary movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p65]
- Areas 1, 2, and 3 constitute the primary somatosensory cortex receiving sensory information primarily from skin and joints. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p65]
- Area 17 is the primary visual cortex receiving sensory signals from the eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p65]
- Areas 41 and 42 constitute the primary auditory cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p65]
