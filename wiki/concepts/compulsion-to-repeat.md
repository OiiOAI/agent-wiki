# Compulsion to Repeat

## Summary

The compulsion to repeat describes the patient's tendency to reproduce forgotten and repressed material not as memory but as action. During psychoanalytic treatment, the patient repeats pathological attitudes, inhibitions, and character traits instead of remembering them. Freud observed that the greater the resistance, the more extensively acting out replaces remembering. This compulsion extends beyond the analytic situation to the patient's entire life during treatment. The management of this compulsion through the transference—allowing it expression within the analytic setting while preventing destructive actions outside it—is central to psychoanalytic technique.

## Key facts

- The patient reproduces forgotten material as action rather than memory, repeating instead of remembering without knowing he is repeating. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3867]
- The compulsion to repeat replaces the impulsion to remember, extending beyond the analytic relationship to all activities and relationships during treatment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3868]
- The analyst uses the transference as a 'playground' where the compulsion to repeat can expand freely and reveal pathogenic material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3871]
- The unconscious mind is dominated by a 'compulsion to repeat' proceeding from drive impulses, which is 'powerful enough to overrule the pleasure principle'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5499]
- This compulsion is 'responsible for a part of the course taken by the analyses of neurotic patients'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5499]
- Whatever reminds us of this inner compulsion to repeat is perceived as uncanny, such as repeatedly returning to the same place or encountering the same numbers. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5499]

## Inferences

- Inference: This clinical observation of the compulsion to repeat was later generalized by Freud in Beyond the Pleasure Principle (1920) to a fundamental drive-level principle.
- Inference: The compulsion to repeat represents a fundamental drive mechanism that operates below the level of conscious experience and seeks to reinstate earlier states of being, which may explain why certain repetitive experiences produce the distinctive quality of uncanniness.
