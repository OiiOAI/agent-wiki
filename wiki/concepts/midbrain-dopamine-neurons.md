# Midbrain Dopamine Neurons

## Summary

Midbrain dopamine neurons in the ventral tegmental area and substantia nigra pars compacta encode reward prediction errors as specified by temporal difference learning theory. Their phasic responses to rewards and reward-predicting stimuli provide a teaching signal for learning about rewards and may serve as a 'retina of the reward system,' identifying motivational value regardless of sensory properties.

## Key facts

- 75-80% of midbrain dopamine neurons show phasic activations with latencies under 100ms to temporally unpredicted food and liquid rewards, coding reward value. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p325]
- Dopamine responses adapt to predicted reward distributions, coding prediction errors scaled by standard deviation rather than mean, reflecting risk. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p326]
- More than one-third of dopamine neurons show slow, moderate activations during delay periods that vary monotonically with reward risk (standard deviation), separate from phasic prediction error responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p327]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dopamine reward signal may qualify as a 'retina of the reward system' by providing abstract information about motivational value regardless of sensory modality.
- Inference: Dopamine appears to have both an informational function (phasic prediction errors) and a permissive function (tonic levels enabling various brain processes).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact relationship between phasic dopamine signals and actual synaptic dopamine release in target areas involves non-linearities that complicate interpretation.
