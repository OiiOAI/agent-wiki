# Life's Work

## Summary

Life's work refers to the individual purpose or calling that provides direction and integration in life. It is intimately connected to one's individuality and what makes a person unique—their particular skills, gifts, and inclinations. Finding this higher sense of purpose gives the integration and direction that humans crave, channeling the many sides of one's character into a coherent path. Examples include Martin Luther King Jr., who fused the practical with the spiritual, and Leonardo da Vinci, who combined interests in art, science, architecture, and engineering.

## Key facts

- Each human individual is radically unique due to the one-of-a-kind configuration of DNA, the particular way brains are wired, and unique life experiences. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p458]
- This uniqueness has a purpose, contributing to cultural diversity just as species diversity sustains ecosystems. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p458]
- Signs of uniqueness appear in childhood as primal inclinations that speak like a voice, but become drowned out by parents, peers, and teachers as people get older. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p459]
- Life's work can be discovered by looking for moments of visceral attraction in earliest years when one was unusually fascinated by particular subjects, objects, or activities. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p463]
- Ingrid Bergman expressed this when talking of early fascination with performing: 'I didn't choose acting. It chose me'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p464]
- Operating with a high sense of purpose is described as a 'force multiplier' where all decisions and actions have greater power because they are guided by a central idea and purpose. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p461]
- With a sense of purpose, boredom transforms into embrace of tedium for future benefits, insecurity becomes focus on work quality rather than self-worth, anxiety becomes productive emotion, and depression is reduced. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p459-460]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of life's work suggests that fulfillment comes from aligning one's activities with innate inclinations rather than pursuing external rewards like money, status, or attention.
