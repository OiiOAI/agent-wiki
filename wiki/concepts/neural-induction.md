# Neural Induction

## Summary

Neural induction is the process by which ectodermal cells are directed to adopt a neural fate and form the neural plate. Classical experiments by Spemann and Mangold identified the 'organizer' region in the dorsal blastopore lip that could induce a secondary nervous system when transplanted. Modern understanding reveals that neural induction operates through de-repression: the default fate of ectoderm is neural, but BMP signaling promotes epidermal fate. Organizer cells secrete BMP inhibitors (noggin, chordin, follistatin) that block BMP, allowing neural differentiation.

## Key facts

- The default fate of ectodermal cells is neural differentiation; BMP signaling from ectodermal cells suppresses neural fate and promotes epidermal differentiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1157]
- Organizer-derived BMP inhibitors including noggin, chordin, follistatin, and variant BMP proteins bind to BMPs and block their signaling, promoting neural differentiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1158]
- Dissociated ectodermal cells acquire neural properties in the absence of cell-to-cell signaling, demonstrating that neural fate is the default state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1157]
- Transplanted dorsal lip cells from the organizer induced a complete secondary neural axis in host embryos, demonstrating the inductive capacity of this tissue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1156]
- Spemann and Mangold demonstrated neural induction by transplanting dorsal blastopore lip tissue, which induced a complete secondary nervous system in host embryos. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1219]
- Dissociated ectodermal cells spontaneously become neural without added factors, demonstrating neural is the default fate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1220]
- BMP proteins are the ectodermal signals that repress neural fate; BMP inhibitors from the organizer overcome this repression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1220]

## Inferences

- Inference: FGF signaling is also necessary for neural induction, indicating that multiple pathways cooperate to establish neural fate.
