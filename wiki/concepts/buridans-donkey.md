# Buridan's Donkey

## Summary

A thought experiment named after medieval philosopher Jean de Buridan: a donkey equally hungry and thirsty, placed at equal distance between food and water, will die of hunger or thirst because it cannot decide which to approach first. The metaphor illustrates how systems stuck in dangerous impasses require random disruption to escape—a pure case where absence of randomness equals guaranteed failure.

## Key facts

- A donkey equally famished and thirsty caught at equal distance between food and water would unavoidably die of hunger or thirst, but can be saved by a random nudge in either direction. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p139]
- When systems are stuck in dangerous impasses, randomness and only randomness can unlock them; absence of randomness equals guaranteed death. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p139]

## Inferences

- Inference: Buridan's donkey demonstrates that perfect balance can be worse than imbalance when both options are equivalent—the system requires stochastic perturbation to function at all.
