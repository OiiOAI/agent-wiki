# Fourth-Generation Warfare

## Summary

Fourth-generation warfare (4GW) is a concept developed by Marine Corps officers including G.I. Wilson, building on John Boyd's ideas, describing a new form of conflict that emerged from 'Islamic traditions' where the distinction between war and peace becomes blurred and adversaries use low-technology or no-technology approaches to defeat high-tech forces through subversion rather than direct confrontation.

## Key facts

- In early 1989, a group including Colonel G.I. Wilson wrote about four generations of warfare: massed troops (first), massed firepower (second), blitzkrieg/time-driven (third), and something new (fourth). [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p415]
- The October 1989 article 'The Changing Face of War: Into the Fourth Generation' was published simultaneously in both the Marine Corps Gazette and the Army's Military Review. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p415]
- The article predicted fourth-generation warfare might emerge from 'Islamic traditions' with terrorists moving freely within society while 'actively seeking to subvert it.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p415]
- The article stated the 'distinction between war and peace will be blurred to the vanishing point,' a concept G.I. Wilson discussed extensively with John Boyd. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p415]
- The Pentagon ignored the 1989 article, but it caused great interest and debate within the Marine Corps and Army special-operations community. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p415]
- After September 11, 2001, the 1989 article was rediscovered and reprinted in the November 2001 Marine Corps Gazette because it was 'frighteningly prescient' in describing how terrorists might operate. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p439]
- One of the websites devoted to Boyd suddenly received as many as 1,600 hits per day after 9/11, with many visitors accessing the 1989 fourth-generation warfare article. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p439]

## Inferences

- Inference: The accuracy of the fourth-generation warfare predictions over a decade before 9/11 demonstrated that Boyd and his students understood the trajectory of future conflict better than the official military establishment.
