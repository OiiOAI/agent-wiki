# Organizational Strategy

## Summary

An Organizational Strategy involves creating an organization chart that defines all positions needed to fulfill the Strategic Objective, regardless of current company size. Each position has a Position Contract—a summary of results to be achieved, accountabilities, and evaluation standards. The organization should be structured around functions, not personalities.

## Key facts

- Most companies organize around personalities rather than around functions, and the result is almost always chaos. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p167]
- A Position Contract is a summary of the results to be achieved by each position, the work accountable for, a list of standards for evaluation, and a signature line. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p175]
- A Position Contract is not a job description—it is a contract between the company and an employee summarizing the rules of the company's game. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p175]
- Accountability literally means 'stand up and be counted.'. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p175]
- The Organization Chart flows down from the Strategic Objective, which flows down from the Primary Aim. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p183]

## Inferences

- Inference: Position Contracts create accountability by making explicit what each person is responsible for achieving.
- Inference: The Organization Chart serves as a blueprint for the Franchise Prototype, enabling systematic replacement of owners with employees.
