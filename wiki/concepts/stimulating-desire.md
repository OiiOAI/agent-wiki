# Stimulating Desire

## Summary

Stimulating desire is the art of making oneself or one's work into an object that others covet. Key strategies include creating mystery and ambiguity, associating with transgressive or taboo elements, and leveraging social proof through competitive desire—making others see that desirable people or things are already desired by others.

## Key facts

- Chanel surrounded herself with mystery, never revealing her impoverished childhood or her creative process, and allowing rumors to spread about herself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p174]
- She associated her designs with something transgressive—masculine elements that remained feminine, flouting gender roles while combining freedom with sex appeal. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p174]
- She encouraged piracy of her designs, believing that ubiquity would stimulate desire in women of all classes who wanted to participate in the Chanel mystique. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p173]
- Human desire is never individual—we want what others want, and seeing others desire something drives up its value. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p182]
- Effective objects of desire should be new, unfamiliar, and exotic, with open-ended messages that allow people to project their own fantasies. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p182]
