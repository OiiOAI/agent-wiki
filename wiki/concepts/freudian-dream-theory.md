# Freudian Dream Theory

## Summary

Freudian dream theory, as presented in Sigmund Freud's 1899 'The Interpretation of Dreams,' proposed that dreams give expression to repressed wishes (often sexual) and serve to protect sleep. The theory included mechanisms of censorship and disguise through dreamwork (condensation, displacement, considerations of representation, and secondary revision). Modern scientific research has found little empirical support for these claims.

## Key facts

- Freud proposed that dreams were 'the guardians of sleep,' protecting sleep from being disturbed by repressed unconscious material. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p47]
- Freud argued that every dream was an attempt at wish fulfillment, requiring psychic energy from a repressed wish. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p48]
- Critics noted that Freud's theory was neither testable nor falsifiable, and many types of dreams (especially nightmares) showed no evidence of censorship or distortion. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p49]
- Innumerable dream studies have led to the conclusion that there is little or no empirical support for Freud's proposed dream functions of wish fulfillment and guardian of sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p51]
