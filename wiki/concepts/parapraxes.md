# Parapraxes

## Summary

Parapraxes (German: Fehlleistungen, literally 'faulty acts') are common mental phenomena including slips of the tongue, misreading, mishearing, forgetting of names and intentions, mislaying objects, and bungled actions. Freud's psychoanalytic investigation of these phenomena demonstrated that they are not random chance events but have psychological meaning and serve as gateways to understanding unconscious mental processes.

## Key facts

- Parapraxes include slips of the tongue (Versprechen), slips of the pen (Verschreiben), misreading (Verlesen), mishearing (Verhören), forgetting (Vergessen), mislaying (Verlegen), and losing (Verlieren). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4730]
- Parapraxes are not merely chance events or physiological disturbances but have a psychical sense. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4731]
- The sense of parapraxes is expressed through the slip itself, which may reveal the true intention behind the disturbance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4731]
- Parapraxes arise from the concurrent action or mutual opposition of two different intentions, one of which may be unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4731]
- Physiological factors such as fatigue, excitement, illness, or distraction facilitate parapraxes but are not their primary cause. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4730]
- Slips of the tongue can express the precise opposite of what was intended, as in the president who 'declared the sitting closed' when intending to open it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4731]
- Composite words in slips of the tongue can reveal a convergence of two different intended speeches. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4731]
