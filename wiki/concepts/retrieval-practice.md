# Retrieval Practice

## Summary

Retrieval practice is the act of trying to remember information before looking it up or receiving feedback. It is far more effective for learning than passive review because it confronts learners with gaps in their knowledge. When we try to answer a question before knowing how, we later remember the answer better, even if the initial attempt failed.

## Key facts

- Only the actual attempt to retrieve information will clearly show if we have learned something or not. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p82]
- When we try to answer a question before we know how to, we will later remember the answer better, even if our attempt failed. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p85]
- Putting effort into retrieving information makes us much more likely to remember it long-term, even if we fail to retrieve it without help in the end. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p85]
- Even without any feedback, trying to remember something ourselves leaves us better off than not trying. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p85]
