# Dream Interpretation: Technique and Theory

## Summary

Freud's 'Remarks on the Theory and Practice of Dream Interpretation' (1923) and 'Some Additional Notes on Dream Interpretation as a Whole' (1925) systematized the technical procedures and theoretical limits of dream interpretation in clinical psychoanalysis. Freud described four technical approaches (chronological, element-focused, day-residue-focused, and free association), distinguished 'dreams from above' (day residues reinforced by unconscious material) from 'dreams from below' (unconscious wishes forcing entry into waking life), and argued that interpretation success depends entirely on the tension of resistance between the awakened ego and the repressed unconscious. Freud also addressed the ethical question of responsibility for dream content, arguing that the morally objectionable material revealed in dream interpretation belongs to the analyst's own id and must be accepted as part of the self.

## Key facts

- Freud described four technical approaches to dream interpretation: (a) chronological, following elements in order; (b) starting from a striking element; (c) beginning with day residues; (d) letting the dreamer choose freely where to begin. - Freud distinguished 'dreams from below'—provoked by unconscious repressed wishes forcing entry into waking life—from 'dreams from above'—day thoughts reinforced by unconscious material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5993]
- Freud argued that the success of dream interpretation depends entirely on the tension of resistance between the awakened ego and the repressed unconscious; high resistance may render dreams uninterpretable. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5992]
- Freud argued that one must hold oneself morally responsible for the evil impulses of one's dreams, as they are part of one's own being; the ego must accept that the repressed id is not alien to it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6013]
- Freud expressed a provisional favorable opinion toward telepathy (thought transference), noting that if telepathic messages exist, they could reach a person during sleep and appear as dreams; he cited unfulfilled fortune-teller prophecies as evidence of unconscious wish communication.
