# Cognitive Hierarchy Theory

## Summary

Cognitive hierarchy theory is a behavioral game theory framework explaining strategic choice as resulting from limited iterated reasoning. It assumes players differ in how many steps of strategic thinking they engage in, with zero-step players choosing randomly, level-k players assuming others engage in k-1 steps, and a logit choice function introducing stochasticity. This model explains deviations from Nash equilibrium predictions in games like the p-beauty contest.

## Key facts

- Cognitive hierarchy theory assumes that iterated reasoning is limited and heterogeneous across players due to evolutionary constraints, working memory limits, and adaptive overconfidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p195]
- In the p-beauty contest game, Nash equilibrium predicts all players choose 0, but empirical data shows many players choose p times 50 (one step of reasoning). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p196]
- Cognitive hierarchy predictions fit data better than Nash equilibrium predictions in games like the p-beauty contest, with typical τ (temperature) parameters between 1 and 3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p197]
- In mixed-strategy games like the work-shirk game, cognitive hierarchy predictions are closer to first-period human and monkey data than Nash equilibrium predictions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p197]
- The LUPI lottery game field data shows that first-week behavior matches cognitive hierarchy predictions closely, with τ ≈ 2.89. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p198]

## Inferences

- Inference: The success of cognitive hierarchy models across laboratory and field settings suggests bounded rationality is a robust feature of human strategic reasoning.
