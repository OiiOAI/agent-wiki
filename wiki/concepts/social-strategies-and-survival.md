# Social strategies and survival

## Summary

Damasio discusses how social strategies and ethical structures evolved as means to cope with suffering in species whose brains developed enhanced capacities for memory and anticipation. He notes that while some social behaviors in nonhuman species may have neural controls that are innate, the most elaborate social conventions in humans arose culturally and were transmitted through learning. The development of these strategies required three capacities: large memory capacity for objects and events, capacity for novel combinations of memorized representations, and capacity to memorize these new creations.

## Key facts

- Social strategies likely evolved as a means to cope with the suffering experienced by individuals whose capacity to remember the past and anticipate the future had attained remarkable development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p282]
- The most elaborate social conventions and ethical structures by which humans live must have arisen culturally and been transmitted likewise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p281]
- Humans possess some innate mechanisms for social behavior that are likely the basis for some ethical structures, but the most elaborate structures arose culturally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p281]
