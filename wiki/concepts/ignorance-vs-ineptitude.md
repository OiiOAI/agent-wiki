# Ignorance vs. Ineptitude

## Summary

Gorovitz and MacIntyre identified two reasons for failure: ignorance (lack of knowledge) and ineptitude (knowledge exists but is not applied correctly). Gawande argues that medicine has shifted from primarily facing ignorance to primarily facing ineptitude, making checklists and systems to ensure proper knowledge application essential.

## Key facts

- Ignorance means we err because science has given us only partial understanding of the world and how it works. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p16]
- Ineptitude means the knowledge exists but we fail to apply it correctly—this is the skyscraper built wrong and collapses, the warning signs missed. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p16]
- Through the 1950s, medicine was governed primarily by ignorance with little known about causes or treatments of major diseases. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p16]
- Studies show at least 30 percent of stroke patients, 45 percent of asthma patients, and 60 percent of pneumonia patients receive incomplete or inappropriate care. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p17]
- Heart attack patients receiving cardiac balloon therapy should have it done within 90 minutes of hospital arrival, but in 2006 fewer than 50 percent of hospitals achieved this. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p17]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction matters because failures of ignorance can be forgiven but failures of ineptitude are seen as negligence, creating different incentive structures for improvement.
