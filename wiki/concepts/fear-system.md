# FEAR System

## Summary

The FEAR system is an emotional operating system that generates defensive responses to threatening or potentially harmful stimuli. It produces a range of responses from active avoidance to passive freezing, and can be activated both by innate danger signals and learned threat cues.

## Key facts

- Rats exhibit innate fear responses to cat odor without prior exposure to predators, demonstrating hardwired fear circuitry. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p17]
- A single exposure to cat odor can inhibit play behavior in rats for up to five successive days through classical conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p17]
- Cat odor is a species-typical fear stimulus restricted to prey species, while pain is a species-general fear stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p18]
- The FEAR system can generate conditioned responses to environmental cues that have been associated with unconditioned fear stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p33]
- The FEAR system is a neural circuit for generating defensive responses to threat. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p205]
- The amygdala and periaqueductal gray are key structures in the FEAR system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p205]
- Benzodiazepines reduce anxiety by acting on FEAR circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: The FEAR system likely involves the amygdala as a key structure, consistent with extensive neuroscience literature
- Inference: The persistence of fear responses after single conditioning trials suggests robust neural plasticity in fear circuits
