# Language of Thought

## Summary

Dehaene proposes that the human brain may be unique in its ability to combine mental representations using a sophisticated 'language of thought'—a compositional system that allows recursive, nested structures of symbols. This capacity to compose elementary concepts into novel combinations may underlie uniquely human abilities including sophisticated self-consciousness.

## Key facts

- Dehaene suggests the human brain may be unique in its ability to combine mental representations using a sophisticated 'language of thought' that allows nested or recursive structures of symbols. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p309-p310]
- In agreement with Noam Chomsky, Dehaene argues language evolved as a representational device rather than primarily as a communication system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p310]
- The human global neuronal workspace may be unique in its capacity to formulate conscious thoughts combining concepts from utterly different domains: 'taller than Tom,' 'left of the red door,' 'not given to John.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p310]
- The recursive function of human language may serve as a vehicle for complex nested thoughts such as 'He thinks that I do not know that he lies,' which seem vastly beyond the competence of primate cousins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p311]
- Human prefrontal neurons have the largest dendritic trees among all primates, potentially making the prefrontal cortex more agile in collecting and integrating information from processors elsewhere in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p310]

## Inferences

- Inference: Human uniqueness in consciousness may reside in the anterior prefrontal cortex (Brodmann area 10), which is larger in Homo sapiens than in any other ape and may be absent or minimal in macaque monkeys [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p311].
- Inference: Von Economo neurons discovered in Broca's area and the midline anterior cingulate may be unique to humans and great apes, potentially contributing to the broadcasting of conscious messages in the human brain [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p311].
