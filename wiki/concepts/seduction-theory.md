# Seduction Theory

## Summary

Freud's seduction theory posited that hysteria and obsessional neurosis are caused by memories of sexual experiences in early childhood—specifically, passive sexual experiences for hysteria and active sexual aggression for obsessional neurosis. These memories remain unconscious until puberty when they are activated and produce pathological effects through deferred action.

## Key facts

- The specific aetiology of hysteria is a passive sexual experience before puberty, occurring between infancy and age eight to ten years. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p163]
- A precocious sexual experience produces little effect at the time but its psychical trace is preserved; when awakened at puberty, the memory operates as though it were a contemporary event. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p154]
- For obsessional neurosis, the specific cause involves active sexual acts performed with pleasure in childhood, rather than passive experiences. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p169]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud later acknowledged this seduction theory contained an error—he could not distinguish between patients' phantasies and real recollections. This recognition eventually led to the discovery of infantile sexuality.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The lower age limit for pathogenic sexual trauma extended to one and a half to two years, which Freud himself found extraordinary.
