# Workspace Theory of Consciousness

## Summary

The workspace theory, proposed by Bernard Baars and later developed by Dehaene and Naccache, posits that consciousness arises from a global workspace where information is integrated and broadcast throughout the brain. Conscious experience serves to handle novel situations requiring integration of multiple information sources for deliberate action.

## Key facts

- Baars proposed that most brain activity is unconscious, but a small fraction becomes conscious through integration into a global workspace. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p119]
- Dehaene and Naccache argued that conscious thought handles novel situations requiring deliberate action and integration of multiple information types. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p119]
- Human subjective experience is integrated, bringing together information from multiple senses and memory into an overall scene. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p119]
- Inner speech provides one mechanism for broadcasting information through the brain, creating loops between thought construction and reception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p120]

## Inferences

- Inference: The integration function of consciousness may have evolved specifically for handling situations where habitual responses fail and novel solutions are required.
