# Schizophrenia

## Summary

Schizophrenia is a persistent and devastating mental disorder affecting about 1% of the population worldwide, characterized by positive symptoms (delusions, hallucinations), negative symptoms (social withdrawal, flattening of affect), and cognitive symptoms (poor attention, working memory deficits). It has a genetic component involving multiple genes interacting with environmental factors, unlike simple single-gene neurological disorders.

## Key facts

- Schizophrenia strikes about 1 percent of the population worldwide and seems to affect men slightly more frequently and more severely than women. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p361]
- An additional 2 to 3 percent of the general population has schizotypal personality disorder, often considered a milder form of the disease without psychotic behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p361]
- Unlike neurological diseases caused by single defective genes, psychiatric illnesses arise from the interaction of several genes with the environment; each gene exerts a relatively small effect but together creates a genetic predisposition. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p347]
- Antipsychotic drugs that block D2 receptors help only with the positive symptoms of schizophrenia but do not significantly affect negative or cognitive symptoms. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p365]
- Genetically engineered mice with an overabundance of D2 receptors in the striatum have deficits in working memory, consistent with Carlsson's dopamine hypothesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p366]
