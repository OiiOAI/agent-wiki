# Tip-of-the-Tongue (TOT)

## Summary

The tip-of-the-tongue (TOT) state is a common blocking experience where a person is certain they know a word or name but cannot retrieve it, experiencing a feeling of imminence. Research shows that people in TOT states often know partial information such as the first letter, number of syllables, and grammatical properties of the blocked word. The ugly sisters hypothesis (that related words cause TOTs) has been largely discredited; instead, weak activation of phonological representations due to infrequent use appears to be the primary cause.

## Key facts

- College students experience roughly one or two TOTs each week, compared to about two to four per week in elderly adults. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p103]
- Expressions using 'the tongue' to describe TOT states exist in 45 of 51 languages surveyed, with 'on the tip of the tongue' being most common. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p104]
- People in TOT states often know the first letter of the blocked word, less frequently know the final letter, and even less often know the middle letters. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p105]
- The ugly sisters hypothesis (that similar-sounding words cause TOTs) has been undermined by tightly controlled studies showing that providing similar-sounding ugly sisters had no effect on TOT incidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p107]
- TOTs are especially likely to occur for words with few phonological neighbors and for infrequently used words. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p108]
- Older adults produce fewer ugly sisters than younger adults but also call up less partial information during TOTs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p109]
