# Scratch Your Own Itch

## Summary

The principle of 'scratching your own itch' means creating products or services that solve problems you yourself have. The authors argue this is the easiest and most straightforward path to creating great products because you design what you know and can immediately assess quality. The book cites examples including James Dyson inventing the cyclonic vacuum, Vic Firth creating drumsticks while in the Boston Symphony Orchestra, Bill Bowerman creating Nike's waffle sole in his workshop, and Mary Kay Wagner knowing her skin-care products were excellent because she used them herself.

## Key facts

- The authors recommend creating something you want to use because it lets you design what you know and immediately assess whether what you're making is any good. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p28]
- 37signals builds products it needs to run its own business, creating Highrise to track contacts and conversations. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p28]
- James Dyson invented the cyclonic bagless vacuum after experiencing suction problems with his own vacuum cleaner. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p28]
- Vic Firth created his drumstick company after finding commercial sticks inadequate for his work as a timpanist for the Boston Symphony Orchestra; the company now produces over 85,000 drumsticks daily with a 62 percent market share. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p28]
- Bill Bowerman created Nike's famous waffle sole by pouring rubber into his family's waffle iron. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p28]
- Mary Kay Wagner knew her skin-care products were great because she used them herself before founding Mary Kay Cosmetics. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p29]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-directed product development naturally leads to products with market fit because the creator is the first and most demanding customer.
