# False Memory Syndrome

## Summary

False memory syndrome refers to the phenomenon where individuals develop detailed but inaccurate memories of autobiographical events, particularly traumatic experiences like childhood sexual abuse. The False Memory Syndrome Foundation was established in 1992. Research by Elizabeth Loftus, Ira Hyman, and others demonstrated that false memories can be experimentally implanted in 20-40% of participants through suggestive procedures. The recovered memories controversy of the 1990s raised questions about the reliability of memories recovered during therapy.

## Key facts

- The False Memory Syndrome Foundation was formed in 1992 by alarmed middle-aged adults, mainly parents involved in conflicts with adult daughters who had recovered memories of childhood sexual abuse. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p166]
- Elizabeth Loftus's 'lost in the mall' study demonstrated that approximately one-quarter of participants falsely remembered being lost in a shopping mall as a child after suggestive interviews. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p167]
- Ira Hyman's research found that 20 to 40% of participants produced some memory of false events in later interviews, with more than half describing them as 'clear' recollections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p167]
- People who produced false memories of childhood experiences scored higher on scales measuring vividness of visual imagery than individuals with more accurate recollections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p168]
- When Hyman instructed people to try to imagine an event if they could not recall it initially, more false memories were found compared with allowing participants to sit quietly and think. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p168]
- Dream interpretation procedures led the majority of participants to claim memories of suggested early experiences including abandonment and getting lost, with nothing of the sort found in control groups. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p168]
- Stephen Porter and colleagues successfully implanted false childhood memories of serious animal attacks, outdoor accidents, and harm perpetrated by another child in approximately one-third of college students. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p169]
- The False Memory Syndrome Foundation was dissolved on December 31, 2019 because 'the need for the FMS Foundation has diminished dramatically over the years.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p185]

## Inferences

- Inference: The decline in false memory syndrome cases may be attributable to increased awareness among psychotherapists of the dangers of suggestive memory recovery techniques.
- Inference: Visual imagery vividness appears to be a vulnerability factor for false memory creation, as it makes false memories feel more authentic.
