# Grandiosity

## Summary

Grandiosity is a psychological condition in which a person's self-perception becomes disproportionately enlarged relative to reality. Individuals experiencing grandiosity perceive themselves as superior, exceptional, and worthy of special attention, losing their sense of proportion and realistic self-assessment. This condition exists on a spectrum, from low-grade everyday versions common to nearly all people, to high-grade clinical presentations in certain leaders and public figures. Grandiosity affects perception of one's own abilities, the difficulty of tasks, and the predictability of others' responses, leading to overconfidence, poor decision-making, and difficulty learning from mistakes.

## Key facts

- Grandiosity reverses normal realistic proportions—self becomes larger and greater than anything else around it. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p373]
- The root of the word grandiosity means 'big' or 'great'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p373]
- Grandiosity causes people to overestimate their own skills and abilities and underestimate obstacles. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p374]
- Low-grade grandiosity is a trait embedded in human nature stemming from our deep need to feel important and superior to others. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p373]
- Grandiosity has its roots in the earliest years of life according to psychoanalyst Heinz Kohut (1913–1981). [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p375]
- In early infancy, we had no sense of separate identity and believed we had omnipotent magical powers to control the mother. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p375]
- Modern factors increasing grandiosity include childhood pampering, lack of respect for authority, technology creating unrealistic expectations, and social media. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p377]
- Social media spreads the grandiosity virus by creating the illusion of attention and adoration from thousands or millions of people. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p377]
- Grandiose people exhibit low levels of empathy, poor listening, and tend to see people as extensions of themselves—tools to be used. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p379]
- The greatest protection against grandiosity is maintaining a realistic attitude and accepting one's limitations. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p381]
