# Mental Models

## Summary

Mental models are principles that help with memory prediction. Naval recommends collecting models from evolution, game theory, Charlie Munger, Nassim Taleb, and Benjamin Franklin. Key mental models include inversion (eliminating what won't work), compound interest, basic math, and falsifiability.

## Key facts

- The best mental models come through evolution, game theory, and Charlie Munger. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p88]
- Nassim Taleb has great mental models around Black Swans and extreme probabilities. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p91]
- Mental models are compact ways to recall your own knowledge. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p89]
- Inversion means not trying to say what will work, but trying to eliminate what won't work. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p89]
- Mental models are defined as mechanisms whereby humans generate descriptions of system purpose and form, explanations of system functioning and observed system states, and predictions of future states. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p41]
- Mental models help determine what information is important to attend to. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p42]
- Mental models enable higher levels of SA (comprehension and projection) without straining working memory. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p42]
- Mental models also provide default information (expected characteristics) that helps form higher levels of SA even when data is missing or incomplete. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p42]
- Less experienced individuals need significantly more mental processing time to develop SA and may overload working memory, leading to significant SA gaps. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p44]
- Munger states you must know the big ideas in the big disciplines and use them routinely—all of them, not just a few. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p129]
- He uses the analogy: 'To the man with a hammer, every problem looks like a nail.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p156]
- Munger estimates eighty to ninety important models carry about ninety percent of the freight in making someone worldly wise. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p156]
- The most reliable models come from hard science and engineering, followed by biology/physiology. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p161]
- Munger describes the elementary psychology of misjudgment as 'terribly important' with about twenty little principles. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p163]
- He advocates using decision trees and the mathematics of permutations and combinations (Fermat/Pascal) routinely in life. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p157]
- Poetry professors are described as 'so unwise in a worldly sense' because they don't have enough models in their heads. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p156]
- You must know the big ideas in the big disciplines and use them routinely, all of them, not just a few. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p136]
- Most people are trained in one model (economics) and try to solve all problems in one way. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p136]
- You need a different checklist and different mental models for different companies. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p136]
- You can't learn the 100 big ideas the way many students do (cramming for grades then emptying them like a bathtub) and expect to succeed in life. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p136]
- Munger says there is no rule that you can't add another model even fairly late in life. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p136]
- Long-term memory stores in the form of mental models or schemata play a major role in dealing with limitations of working memory (Endsley, 1988, 1995c). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p35]
- Mental models help direct limited attention in efficient ways, provide a means of integrating information without loading working memory, and provide a mechanism for generating projection of future system states. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p35]
- The situation model (SA) is the current state of the mental model, capturing not only parameter values but also understanding of how elements relate in terms of system form and function. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p35]
- Critical cues may be matched to prototypical schemata to indicate prototypical situations that provide instant situation classification and comprehension, potentially with attached action scripts. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p35]
- While schemata may facilitate situation assessment by reducing working memory demands, they can also lead to significant problems with biasing in selection and interpretation of information. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p36]
- Approximately 18% of SA errors are traced to mental model problems: poor/insufficient models (7%), incorrect mental models (6.5%), and overreliance on default values (4.6%). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p36]
- Sarter and Woods (1991) stated that adequate mental models are a prerequisite for achieving SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p36]
- Experienced pilots could report on relevant SA information for 5 to 6 minutes following simulation freezes without the memory decay expected from working memory, supporting the model of working memory as an activated subset of long-term memory. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p33]
- Expert weather forecasters depend on mental models that enable them to spot negative cues—things that should have happened but didn't—only by reviewing data themselves. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p310]
- Mental models are casual accounts for how things work in a situation, while stories are specific accounts using mental models to explain particular circumstances. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p365]
- Information technology can damage mental models by locking thinking into predefined categories set up in advance, preventing learning and adaptation. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p319]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger's approach to mental models reflects his broader interdisciplinary education and his view that wisdom comes from synthesizing knowledge across fields rather than specializing narrowly
