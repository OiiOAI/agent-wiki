# Consciousness (Neuroscience)

## Summary

In the Thousand Brains Theory, consciousness is viewed as a physical phenomenon tied to the brain's ability to learn models and continuously form moment-to-moment memories of thoughts and actions. Awareness requires memory formation; qualia are properties of the brain's model of the world; and machines built on brain principles would likely be conscious.

## Key facts

- Awareness, the sense of presence, requires that we form moment-to-moment memories of our actions and thoughts—we are unaware of what we don't remember. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p131]
- The ability to jump back in time to replay recent thoughts and slide forward again to the present gives us our sense of presence and awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p132]
- Qualia are subjective internal experiences and are properties of the brain's model of the world, not purely properties of the physical world—as shown by the dress illusion where different people see different colors. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p134]
- Some qualia like color may be learned via movement—similar to how we learn objects, the brain learns reference frames for surface orientations that correspond to perceived colors. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p134]
- Not all qualia are learned—pain is innate, mediated by special pain receptors and old-brain structures, not the neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p135]
- Fear of death and sorrow for loss are created by the old brain through hormones and chemicals, and are not required ingredients for consciousness—intelligent machines would not care if shut down unless designed with equivalent fears. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p137]

## Inferences

- Inference: An intelligent machine that learns models using brain principles and remembers internal states would likely be conscious, as consciousness appears to be a physical phenomenon explainable by neuroscience.
- Inference: Machines would not need to be moral patients in the way humans are, since they would not have the emotional machinery that makes us fear death.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether storing and recalling brain states is sufficient for consciousness, or whether additional properties are needed, is debated.
- Uncertain: The neural mechanisms underlying why pain 'hurts' in the way it does remains unexplained.
