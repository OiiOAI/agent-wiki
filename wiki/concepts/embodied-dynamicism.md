# Embodied Dynamicism

## Summary

Embodied dynamicism emerged in the 1990s as a critical stance toward computationalism. It combines dynamic systems theory with an embodied approach to cognition. The central metaphor is the mind as an embodied dynamic system in the world, rather than as a neural network in the head. Cognitive processes are understood to emerge from nonlinear and circular causality of continuous sensorimotor interactions involving brain, body, and environment. The cognitive unconscious is reconceived as embodied and embedded processes that cannot be made experientially accessible, extending throughout the body and into the material, social, and cultural environment.

- The embodied approach holds that cognition is the exercise of skillful know-how in situated and embodied action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p22]
- The cognitive unconscious consists of embodied and embedded cognition and emotion processes that cannot be made experientially accessible. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p23]
