# Catecholamine biosynthesis

## Summary

Catecholamines (dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine) are synthesized from the essential amino acid tyrosine through a five-enzyme biosynthetic pathway. Tyrosine hydroxylase catalyzes the rate-limiting first step. Dopamine β-hydroxylase is the only membrane-associated enzyme, bound to the inner surface of aminergic vesicles, making norepinephrine the only transmitter synthesized within vesicles.

## Key facts

- The catecholamine biosynthetic pathway contains five enzymes: tyrosine hydroxylase, pteridine reductase, aromatic amino acid decarboxylase, dopamine β-hydroxylase, and phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p343]
- Tyrosine hydroxylase converts tyrosine to L-DOPA and is rate-limiting for the synthesis of both dopamine and norepinephrine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p343]
- Dopamine β-hydroxylase is membrane-associated and bound to the inner surface of aminergic vesicles, making norepinephrine the only transmitter synthesized within vesicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p344]
- Phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase converts norepinephrine to epinephrine and is present only in the adrenal medulla; only a small number of neurons use epinephrine as a transmitter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p344]

## Inferences

- Inference: The membrane-bound nature of dopamine β-hydroxylase explains why norepinephrine synthesis occurs within vesicles while other neurotransmitters are synthesized in the cytoplasm.
- Inference: The specificity of phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase to adrenal medulla explains why most neurons produce only dopamine or norepinephrine, not epinephrine.
