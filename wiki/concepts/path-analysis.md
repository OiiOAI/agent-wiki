# Path Analysis

## Summary

A statistical method developed by Sewall Wright in 1920 for estimating causal effects from correlational data by combining qualitative causal assumptions (represented in path diagrams) with quantitative data. The method involves tracing paths between variables and multiplying path coefficients, enabling researchers to distinguish direct from indirect effects and to estimate the magnitude of unmeasured causal factors.

## Key facts

- Wright developed path analysis in 1920, the first mathematical method for bridging correlation (rung one) and causation (rung two) on the Ladder of Causation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L560-L580]
- Path coefficients represent the amount of variability in the target variable accounted for by the source variable, or equivalently the results of hypothetical interventions. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L575-L590]
- The rule for computing correlations involves tracing paths connecting two variables and multiplying the coefficients encountered along the way. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L575-L590]
- Path analysis requires scientific thinking and cannot be reduced to mechanical routines, unlike many statistical methods, which explains its limited adoption. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L595-L615]
- Sociologists renamed path analysis as structural equation modeling (SEM) and later automated it with LISREL, causing it to become a rote method disconnected from causal interpretation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L620-L640]
- Wright (1920) introduced path diagrams as a method for causal analysis. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3433-L3433]
- Blalock (1964), Duncan (1966), and Goldberger (1972) introduced Wright's path analysis ideas to social science. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3438-L3439]
- Freedman's 1987 critique challenged path analysts to explain how interventions are modeled, causing leading researchers to retreat from causal interpretation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3440-L3440]
- A revealing discussion among twelve scholars on path analysis is documented in the Journal of Educational Statistics. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3440-L3440]
- The fate of path analysis in economics and social science is narrated in Pearl (2000) Chapter 5 and Bollen and Pearl (2013). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3437-L3437]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wright's contribution was unique because the information leading to causal conclusions resided in two incompatible mathematical languages—diagrams and data—requiring their integration.
