# Planning

## Summary

The process of deciding on an abstract sequence of operations intended for achieving a goal. Planning is subserved by the prefrontal cortex and involves both successive refinement (top-down hierarchical) and opportunistic (data-driven, bottom-up) models. Key brain structures include the prefrontal cortex, basal ganglia, motor cortex, and parietal lobes.

## Key facts

- Plans range from short-term motoric (planning key presses) to long-term cognitive (steps for landing an airplane), and include hierarchical subplans down to the level of motor action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p381]
- Successive refinement models propose planning is a top-down hierarchical process that controls the order of operations, while opportunistic models propose data-driven processing that operates concurrently at multiple levels of abstraction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p383]
- Patients with frontal lobe lesions demonstrate difficulty in developing and executing plans, with failures including problems in both top-down and bottom-up plan development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p397]
- Tower-type tasks (Tower of Hanoi, Tower of London) reveal that planning difficulty increases with the number of competing alternative choices at each step. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p391]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prefrontal cortex is crucial for sustaining information processing over long periods and representing sequential event knowledge.
- Inference: Different PFC subregions process different aspects of planning: left PFC focuses on specific features of individual events, right PFC mediates integration across events.
