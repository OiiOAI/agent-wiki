# Inner Choice

## Summary

Inner choice refers to the psychological principle that commitments are most effective in changing self-image and future behavior when individuals accept personal inner responsibility for their actions. This occurs when people believe they chose to perform a behavior in the absence of strong outside pressures such as large rewards or threats.

## Key facts

- Social scientists determined that we accept inner responsibility for a behavior when we think we have chosen to perform it in the absence of strong outside pressures. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p91]
- Freedman's experiment showed that boys given no strong threat against playing with a robot (only an instruction that it was 'wrong') avoided the robot six weeks later, while boys given severe threats chose the robot 77% of the time when the threatener was absent. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p92]
- A large reward may get someone to perform an action, but it won't get them to accept inner responsibility for the act, and consequently they won't feel committed to it. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p91]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text suggests that effective child-rearing should avoid heavy bribing or threatening, instead using barely sufficient reasons that allow children to take personal responsibility for desired behaviors.
