# Secondary Revision

## Summary

Secondary revision is the fourth factor in dream construction, operating after condensation, displacement, and considerations of representability. It is a psychical force that creates apparent coherence and connectedness in dream content, fusing disparate elements into a whole that makes sense and appears without contradiction. This secondary processing gives dreams their seemingly logical appearance and makes it difficult for dreamers to recognize the true origin of dream elements.

## Key facts

- A psychical force creates apparent connectedness in dreams, submitting material produced by the dream-work to secondary revision. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p401]
- Secondary revision fuses together elements in a dream of disparate origin into a whole which shall make sense and be without contradiction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p401]
- The dream-work cannot actually create speeches; speeches appearing in dreams are fragments extracted from real speeches made or heard by the dreamer. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p374]
- Speeches in dreams have a structure similar to breccia, where blocks of various kinds of stone are cemented together by a binding medium. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p375]
- Secondary revision is the psychical agency which approaches the content of dreams with a demand that they must be intelligible, subjecting them to a first interpretation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1750]
- The interpolations made by secondary revision are easy to recognize because they are often reported with hesitation, introduced by 'as though', and are not particularly vivid. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1737]
- Secondary revision fills in the gaps in the dream structure with shreds and patches, making the dream lose its appearance of absurdity and disconnectedness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1737]
- Some dreams subjected to secondary revision have a meaning that is far removed from their true significance; they are dreams which might be said to have been already interpreted once, before being submitted to waking interpretation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1738]
- Secondary revision prefers to take possession of a ready-made daydream from the dream thoughts and introduce it into the dream content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1739]

## Inferences

- Inference: Secondary revision functions analogously to waking consciousness, attempting to impose order and meaning on raw psychological material, which may explain why dreams often seem comprehensible upon first recall.
- Inference: Secondary revision is functionally identical to normal waking thought, differing only in the material it works upon. Freud explicitly identifies it with preconscious thought that establishes order, relations, and intelligibility in perceptual material.
- Inference: The function of secondary revision is responsible for the range in dream quality between confusion and clarity. Clear parts have been successfully revised; confused parts are where revision failed.
- Inference: Secondary revision was the only factor in dream formation observed by most writers on the subject, who overestimated its importance and credited it with the entire achievement of creating dreams.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether secondary revision creates entirely new contributions to dreams, or only reorganizes existing material. Freud notes that in extreme cases it makes new creations, but usually employs material from dream thoughts.
