# Projection

## Summary

A common defense mechanism used by both men and women to avoid anxiety. Projection is when you perceive the source of your anxiety to have the insecurity rather than yourself.

## Key facts

- Projection is defined as a common defense mechanism used by both men and women to avoid anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p269]
- Projection is when you perceive the source of your anxiety to have the insecurity rather than yourself. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p269]
- For instance, an Indian man who is insecure about his race will project onto the women he meets that they are racist and don't like him because he's Indian. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p269]
- Women project as well; a woman who is low self-esteem and perceives you to be too attractive for her will project onto you that insecurity and get mad at you for only wanting her for sex. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p269]
- This is the reason why less attractive women may reject you more often and harsher than more attractive women. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p269]
- Projection is an abuse of a psychical mechanism commonly employed in normal life; it is a normal mechanism when we remain conscious of our own internal change, but becomes paranoia when we forget it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p280]
- The purpose of paranoia is to fends off an idea incompatible with the ego by projecting its substance into the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p279]
- In paranoia, the content and affect of the incompatible idea are retained but projected into the external world; hallucinations, when they arise, are hostile to the ego but support the defence. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p283]
- The determining element of paranoia is the mechanism of projection involving the refusal of belief in the self-reproach. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p285]
- Projection serves the purpose of dealing with emotional conflict: unconscious hostile impulses are ejected from internal perception into the external world and detached from the self. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4172]
- The survivor denies having harbored hostile feelings against the dead loved one; instead, the soul of the dead harbors them and seeks to put them into action during mourning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4172]
- Under conditions not yet sufficiently established, internal perceptions of emotional and thought processes can be projected outwards in the same way as sense perceptions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4175]
- The concept of demons may have been derived from the important relation of the living to the dead, with ambivalence giving rise to both fear of demons and veneration of ancestors from the same root. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4176]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of projection appears here for the first time in Freud's work, though it was made public in the second paper on the neuropsychoses of defence (1896b) with much less detail.
- Inference: Freud's analysis suggests that belief in spirits and demons represents a collective application of the projection mechanism, externalizing internal psychological conflicts.
