# Cultural Group Selection

## Summary

An evolutionary process proposed by Atran and Henrich in which cultural variants (ideas, practices, religions) spread based on their effects on group cohesion and success, rather than based purely on genetic fitness. Unlike genetic evolution, cultural evolution can proceed rapidly through learning and transmission. This theory explains how religions evolved from morally capricious hunter-gatherer gods to morally concerned deities that promote cooperation and suppress free riding. Religious groups with more effective binding practices outcompeted less effective groups.

## Key facts

- Scott Atran and Joe Henrich argue that cultural evolution of religion was driven largely by competition among groups. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p277]
- Groups that were able to use gods to promote cooperation had advantages over groups that failed to do so, causing their ideas to spread. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p277]
- Moralistic high gods evolved along with agriculture in the last 10,000 years to condemn selfish and divisive behaviors. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p277]
- Richard Sosis found that religious communes survived significantly longer than secular communes (39% vs 6% after 20 years), with survival correlating to costly sacrifices demanded. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p279]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cultural group selection may explain the global success of world religions—they spread not through genetic replacement but through adoption of more effective cooperative practices.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which cultural group selection operated independently versus in concert with genetic evolution remains debated.
