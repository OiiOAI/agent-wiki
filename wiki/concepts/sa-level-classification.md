# SA Level Classification

## Summary

SA level classification involves using psychophysiological data to train computerized classifiers to distinguish between good and bad situation awareness. Based on training data where SA levels are determined by subjective and performance measures, classifiers such as linear discriminant analysis, Bayes classifiers, and neural networks can be trained to identify SA levels from physiological data. Wilson and colleagues demonstrated 86-90% accuracy in mental workload classification, suggesting this approach may be applicable to SA.

## Key facts

- Classification approaches require selecting data recorded during periods of good and bad SA based upon subjective and performance criteria, then dividing into training and test subsets. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p183]
- Various classifiers have been used including linear discriminant analysis, Bayes classifiers, and artificial neural network classifiers. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p183]
- Wilson and Fisher (1995) achieved 86% average accuracy in determining which of seven task categories and two difficulty levels subjects were experiencing using psychophysiological data. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p183]
- Data recorded during flight could correctly identify flight segment with accuracies better than 90%. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p183]
- At least 85% overall accuracy was achieved in determining mental workload level during simulated air traffic control using psychophysiological data. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p183]
- The weights assigned to data features by classifiers can be examined to determine important features for each operator and provide insight into underlying mechanisms. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p183]

## Inferences

- Inference: The successful application of classification techniques to mental workload suggests similar approaches may prove successful for SA classification given the similarities between these constructs.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: It is not yet demonstrated that data from different sessions can be used to achieve comparable accuracy, which would be necessary to apply these procedures to the work environment.
