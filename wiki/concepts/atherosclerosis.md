# Atherosclerosis

## Summary

Atherosclerosis is a slow-moving disease where LDL particles penetrate the endothelium and become oxidized, triggering an inflammatory cascade that leads to plaque formation. It begins in adolescence, with fatty streaks visible in some sixteen-year-olds. The process requires three prerequisites: significant lipoprotein burden (apoB), LDL oxidation, and background inflammation. Globally, heart disease and stroke represent the leading cause of death.

## Key facts

- Atherosclerosis begins when LDL particles penetrate the endothelial barrier into the subendothelial space and become oxidized. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p127]
- Oxidized LDL causes endothelial harm, leading to greater retention of LDL and more damage in a vicious cycle. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p127]
- Macrophages consume oxidized LDL and become foam cells, which gather to form fatty streaks. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p128]
- Autopsy data shows as many as a third of sixteen-to-twenty-year-olds already had actual atherosclerotic lesions or plaques in their coronary arteries. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p128]
- Heart disease and stroke represent the leading cause of death globally, killing an estimated 2,300 people every day in the United States. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p119]
- Atherosclerosis requires three prerequisites: significant lipoprotein burden or apoB, LDL oxidation or modification, and high level of background inflammation. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p133]
