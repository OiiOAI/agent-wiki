# Metaphor

## Summary

Metaphor is a fundamental cognitive mechanism that bridges language with embodied experience. McGilchrist argues that only the right hemisphere has the capacity to understand metaphor, and that metaphor is essential to understanding the world because it is the only way understanding can reach outside the system of signs to life itself. Metaphor carries us across the gap that language itself creates, connecting the denotative left-hemisphere world back to lived experience.

## Key facts

- Metaphor is the crucial aspect of language whereby it retains its connectedness to the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p183]
- Only the right hemisphere has the capacity to understand metaphor. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p170]
- Metaphor functions like money - it is only an intermediary but takes on some of the life of the things it represents. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p170]
- Metaphor (subserved by the right hemisphere) comes before denotation (subserved by the left) - this is a historical truth. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p172]
- Metaphorical language is a reflection of metaphorical thought; eliminating metaphor would eliminate philosophy. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p173]
- A metaphor asserts a common life that is experienced in the body of the one who makes it, and the separation is only present at the linguistic level. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p172]

## Inferences

- Inference: The gap across which metaphor carries us is one that language itself creates. Metaphor is language's cure for the ills entailed on us by language.
- Inference: Metaphor's power is lost when it is paraphrased or replaced, because whatever had been extralingual, unconscious, and therefore potentially new and alive in the collision of two entities gets reconstructed only in terms of what is familiar.
