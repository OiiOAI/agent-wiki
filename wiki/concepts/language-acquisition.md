# Language Acquisition

## Summary

Language acquisition in children follows a universal pattern regardless of culture. Infants begin learning language earlier than previously thought, with well-developed neural architecture for language processing present at birth. A critical period exists for phonetic learning, and social interaction plays a crucial role in early language development.

## Key facts

- Regardless of culture, all children initially exhibit universal patterns of speech perception and production that do not depend on the specific language they hear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1373]
- By the end of the first year, infants have learned which phonetic units convey meaning in their language and can recognize likely words, even though they do not yet understand them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1373]
- At 6 months of age, infants can discriminate all phonetic units used in all languages, but by the end of the first year they fail to discriminate phonetic changes they successfully recognized 6 months earlier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1374]
- American infants exposed to Mandarin Chinese at 9 months learned the phonemes only through interaction with live native speakers; infants exposed to identical material through television or audiotape showed no learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1375]
- Infants are sensitive to statistical properties of speech, including distributional frequency patterns of sounds and transitional probabilities between adjacent syllables. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1377]
- Fetuses learn prosodic cues in utero by listening to their mother's speech, demonstrating preference for the mother's voice and native language rhythm at birth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1376]
- The degree to which parentese is used in language spoken to infants at 11 and 14 months is strongly correlated with child language development at 24 and 36 months. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1378]

## Inferences

- Inference: The requirement for social interaction in early phonetic learning suggests that language evolution capitalized on infants' innate ability to recognize social cues.
