# Liberty/Oppression Foundation

## Summary

The sixth moral foundation proposed by Haidt, evolved in response to the adaptive challenge of living with would-be dominators in small groups. Drawing on Christopher Boehm's 'reverse dominance hierarchy' theory, the foundation explains why humans evolved to unite against oppressors and why both libertarians and left-wing revolutionaries rely heavily on liberty-based moral arguments. The foundation concerns proportionality fairness and resistance to domination, distinct from equality-based fairness.

## Key facts

- The Liberty/oppression foundation evolved to meet the challenge of resisting would-be alpha males who try to dominate groups. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p192]
- Boehm's reverse dominance hierarchy theory explains how humans transitioned from natural hierarchy to enforced egalitarianism by banding together to restrain dominators. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p191]
- Liberals use the Liberty foundation in service of underdogs and powerless groups everywhere, leading to sacralization of equality. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p196]
- Conservatives sacralize 'liberty' rather than 'equality,' uniting them politically with libertarians against government overreach. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p196]
- The foundation explains why proportionality (people should get what they deserve) differs psychologically from equality, requiring separate theoretical treatment. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p189]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Liberty foundation operates in tension with the Authority foundation—people recognize legitimate authority but are vigilant for signs that leaders have crossed into self-aggrandizement and tyranny
