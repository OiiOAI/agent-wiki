# Projection

## Summary

Freud's first published use of the technical term 'projection' in the context of paranoia, describing it as a method of repression where the self-reproach is repressed by erecting distrust of other people as a defensive symptom. Unlike obsessional neurosis where self-doubt protects against self-reproaches, in paranoia the subject withdraws acknowledgement of self-reproach through projecting it onto others.

## Key facts

- In paranoia, the self-reproach is repressed by erecting the defensive symptom of distrust of other people, described as projection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1087]
- Through projection, the subject withdraws his acknowledgement of the self-reproach and is deprived of a protection against the self-reproaches which return in his delusional ideas. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1087]
- Freud traces the term to his earlier study of paranoia sent to Fliess on January 24, 1895. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1087]

## Inferences

- Inference: The comparison with obsessional neurosis suggests that projection in paranoia represents a fundamentally different defensive strategy that leaves the ego more vulnerable rather than protected.
