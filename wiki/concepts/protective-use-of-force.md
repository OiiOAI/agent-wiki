# Protective Use of Force

## Summary

The protective use of force is employed when dialogue is not possible and force is necessary to protect life or individual rights. Its intention is to prevent injury or injustice without passing judgment on the person or behavior. The assumption behind protective force is that people behave injuriously due to some form of ignorance that can be addressed through education rather than punishment.

## Key facts

- The protective use of force is applied when two parties cannot communicate due to unwillingness or imminent danger, with the intention to prevent injury or injustice. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p248]
- The intention behind protective force is to prevent injury or injustice, without passing judgment on either the person or the behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p249]
- The corrective process for protective force is education, not punishment, based on the assumption that harmful behavior results from ignorance including lack of awareness of consequences, inability to see alternative ways to meet needs, belief in the right to punish, or delusional thinking. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p249]
- When exercising protective force, thinking is solely directed toward protecting from danger without blaming or condemning. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p249]
