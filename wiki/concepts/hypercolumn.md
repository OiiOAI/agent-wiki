# Hypercolumn

## Summary

A hypercolumn is a repeating functional unit of approximately 1 mm diameter in the primary visual cortex containing a full cycle of orientation columns (180°), one cycle of left- and right-eye ocular dominance columns, and blobs and interblobs. Hypercolumns are thought to contain all functional and anatomical cell types necessary to analyze a particular point in visual space.

## Key facts

- Across the cortical surface there is a regular clockwise and counterclockwise cycling of orientation preference with the full 180° cycle repeating every 750 μm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p618]
- Left- and right-eye dominance columns alternate with a periodicity of 750 to 1,000 μm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p618]
- A hypercolumn roughly 1 mm in diameter contains an orientation hypercolumn, one cycle of ocular dominance columns, and blobs and interblobs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p621]
- Columnar organization minimizes the distance required for neurons with similar functional properties to communicate and allows them to share inputs from discrete pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p621]

## Inferences

- Inference: The clustering of neurons into columns economizes brain volume and maximizes processing speed by reducing the number of neurons required for analyzing different attributes.
