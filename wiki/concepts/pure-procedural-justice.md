# Pure Procedural Justice

## Summary

A form of procedural justice where there is no independent criterion for the right result; instead there is a correct or fair procedure such that the outcome is correct or fair provided the procedure has been properly followed. Contrasted with perfect procedural justice (which has an independent criterion and a procedure guaranteed to achieve it) and imperfect procedural justice (which has an independent criterion but no guaranteed procedure).

## Key facts

- Pure procedural justice obtains when there is no independent criterion for the right result: instead there is a correct or fair procedure such that the outcome is likewise correct or fair, provided the procedure has been properly followed. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p87]
- The distinctive feature of pure procedural justice is that the procedure for determining the just result must actually be carried out; there is no independent criterion by reference to which a definite outcome can be known to be just. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p89]
- The basic structure is the subject of pure procedural justice: only against the background of a just basic structure can one say that the requisite just procedure exists. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p89]
- The principle of fair equality of opportunity insures that the system of cooperation is one of pure procedural justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p90]
- The great practical advantage of pure procedural justice is that it is no longer necessary to keep track of the endless variety of circumstances and the changing relative positions of particular persons. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p90]
- The problem of distributive justice is the choice of a social system designed so that the resulting distribution is just however things turn out. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p252]
- Without an appropriate scheme of background institutions, the outcome of the distributive process will not be just—background fairness is lacking. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p252]
- In a competitive price system surrounded by just background institutions, the distribution that results is a case of background justice on the analogy with the outcome of a fair game. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p278]
- No attempt is made to define the just distribution on the basis of information about the preferences and claims of particular individuals—this knowledge is regarded as irrelevant from a suitably general point of view. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p278]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rawls applies pure procedural justice to distributive shares: the justice of a distribution is founded on the justice of the scheme of cooperation from which it arises and on answering the claims of individuals engaged in it.
- Inference: The reliance on pure procedural justice presupposes that the basic structure satisfies the two principles of justice, and no attempt to achieve distributive justice through pure procedural means can succeed without just surrounding institutions.
