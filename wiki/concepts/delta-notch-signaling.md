# Delta-Notch Signaling

## Summary

Delta-Notch signaling is an evolutionarily conserved pathway that regulates neural cell fate decisions through lateral inhibition. When a cell begins expressing Delta ligand, it activates Notch in neighboring cells, suppressing their neuronal differentiation and promoting glial fate. Proneural bHLH transcription factors (Mash1, neurogenins) promote neuronal fate and also activate Delta expression, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies initial small differences into all-or-none fate decisions. Numb protein antagonizes Notch and promotes neuronal differentiation.

## Key facts

- Delta-Notch signaling uses lateral inhibition: cells with higher Notch become glial, cells with higher Delta become neuronal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1241]
- Delta binding causes proteolytic cleavage of Notch cytoplasmic domain, which enters the nucleus and activates Hes bHLH repressors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1241]
- Proneural bHLH proteins (Mash1, neurogenin-1/2) promote neuronal fate and activate Delta, creating positive feedback that amplifies fate differences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1242]
- Numb protein antagonizes Notch signaling and is segregated into neuronal daughter cells during asymmetric division. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1243]
