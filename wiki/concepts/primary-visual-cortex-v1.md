# Primary Visual Cortex (V1)

## Summary

The primary visual cortex (V1), also known as the striate cortex or Brodmann's area 17, is the first cortical area to receive visual input from the lateral geniculate nucleus of the thalamus. It is characterized by a highly organized columnar architecture that systematically represents visual space (retinotopy) while simultaneously encoding stimulus orientation, ocular dominance, and color properties through distinct columnar systems.

## Key facts

- V1 receives direct input from the lateral geniculate nucleus through the optic radiation and is identical to Brodmann's area 17. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]
- V1 is organized into visuotopic maps where the visual field is systematically represented across the cortical surface, with the smallest receptive fields at the fovea. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- Cells with similar functional properties are organized into columns extending from the cortical surface to the white matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- Ocular-dominance columns reflect alternating inputs from left and right eyes, with each full cycle called a hypercolumn (~750-1000 μm). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- Orientation columns cycle through all orientations (180°) every ~750 μm, with pinwheel structures at singularities of orientation preference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p557]
- Cytochrome oxidase blobs within V1 contain color-selective neurons with poor orientation selectivity, specialized for processing surface color rather than edges. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p557]
- A cortical computational module roughly 1 mm square contains one orientation hypercolumn, one cycle of ocular-dominance columns, and blobs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p559]

## Inferences

- Inference: The columnar organization allows V1 to efficiently process multiple visual attributes (orientation, depth, color, motion) in parallel for each location in visual space.
