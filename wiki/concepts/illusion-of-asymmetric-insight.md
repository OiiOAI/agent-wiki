# Illusion of Asymmetric Insight

## Summary

The illusion of asymmetric insight is a cognitive bias identified by psychologist Emily Pronin, where people believe they understand others better than others understand them. Pronin's word-completion experiments demonstrated that people deny their own word choices reveal anything about them while readily interpreting strangers' identical word choices as deeply meaningful.

## Key facts

- Emily Pronin's experiments showed that people believe their own word completions are random while seeing deep meaning in strangers' identical completions. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p32]
- Pronin found that people think they know others better than others know them, leading to overconfidence in judging strangers. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p33]
- This conviction leads people to 'talk when they would do well to listen' and to be impatient when others feel misunderstood. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p33]
