# Central Dogma (Molecular Biology)

## Summary

The central dogma of molecular biology, formulated by Francis Crick in the early 1960s, describes the flow of genetic information: DNA determines RNA sequence, which determines amino acid sequence, which determines protein shape and function.

## Key facts

- Francis Crick proposed that DNA nucleotide sequence determines RNA sequence, which determines amino acid sequence, which determines protein shape and function. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p731]
- A sequence of 3 nucleotides (a codon) codes for a single amino acid; 61 codons code for 20 amino acids, with redundancy. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p730]
- One type of gene specifies one type of protein. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p731]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery of DNA structure by Watson and Crick (with significant contribution from Rosalind Franklin) enabled understanding of the molecular basis of inheritance.
