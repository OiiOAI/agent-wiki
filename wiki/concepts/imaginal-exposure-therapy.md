# Imaginal Exposure Therapy

## Summary

Imaginal exposure therapy is the most effective treatment for persistent traumatic memories and PTSD. Patients are repeatedly exposed to stimuli associated with their traumas and recall vivid images of incidents. This allows habituation—the reduction of physiological response to repeated stimuli—which suppression of memories prevents. The approach contrasts with avoidance or suppression, which paradoxically increase intrusive memories.

## Key facts

- Therapeutic approaches to counter persistence in trauma survivors almost invariably focus on allowing patients to reexperience the traumatic event within the safe confines of the therapy setting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p234]
- In the early 1980s, Terrence Keane reported that exposure therapy reduced levels of anxiety and intrusive memories in Vietnam veterans. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p234]
- Studies comparing imaginal exposure therapy to supportive counseling found exposure therapy produced the greatest reductions in intrusive memories, flashbacks, and related PTSD symptoms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p234]
- Testimony therapy, where trauma survivors retell and relive their experiences in a collective context, reduced intrusive memories in traumatized Bosnian refugees. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p234]
- Repeated reexperiencing of a traumatic memory in a safe setting allows habituation—reduced physiological response—which suppression prevents, explaining why suppression backfires. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p233]
