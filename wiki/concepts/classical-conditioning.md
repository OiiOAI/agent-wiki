# Classical Conditioning

## Summary

Classical conditioning is an associative learning process where a neutral conditioned stimulus (CS) becomes associated with an unconditioned stimulus (US) when the CS reliably predicts the US. In Aplysia gill-withdrawal reflex conditioning, activity-dependent facilitation occurs when the CS (siphon touch) precedes the US (tail shock) by about 0.5 seconds, producing greater presynaptic facilitation than sensitization alone.

## Key facts

- In Aplysia classical conditioning, a weak siphon touch (CS) is paired with tail shock (US) such that the CS precedes the US within approximately 0.5 seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1318]
- Activity-dependent facilitation occurs because Ca2+ influx during the CS primes adenylyl cyclase to respond more strongly to subsequent serotonin release from the US. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1318]
- If the CS and US occur in reverse order (US precedes CS), no conditioning occurs, demonstrating that prediction, not mere temporal association, drives learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1318]
