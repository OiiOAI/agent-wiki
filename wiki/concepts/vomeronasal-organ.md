# Vomeronasal Organ

## Summary

The vomeronasal organ is an accessory olfactory structure present in many mammals (but not humans) that detects pheromones. It contains two families of chemosensory receptors (V1R and V2R) that signal through different G proteins and project to the accessory olfactory bulb, which in turn projects to the medial amygdala and hypothalamus.

## Key facts

- The vomeronasal organ is present in many mammals although not in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p772]
- The vomeronasal organ has two different families of chemosensory receptors, the V1R and V2R families, with more than 100 members each in the mouse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p772]
- V1R and V2R receptors have seven transmembrane domains typical of G protein-coupled receptors, but V2R receptors have a large extracellular domain at the N-terminal end. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p772]
- Vomeronasal neurons project to the accessory olfactory bulb, which projects to the medial amygdala and hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p772]
- The V1R and V2R families are localized in two different zones of the vomeronasal organ that express different G proteins (Gαi2 and Gαo). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p773]
