# Hysterical Phantasies

## Summary

Freud's concept that hysterical symptoms are formed from unconscious phantasies—particularly sexual phantasies—which have been repressed and converted into somatic or mental symptoms. The paper advances the additional thesis that many hysterical symptoms express not one but two unconscious sexual phantasies, one masculine and one feminine, reflecting the innate bisexual disposition of the subject.

## Key facts

- Every hysterical attack investigated has proved to be an involuntary irruption of daydreams into motor expression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3043]
- Hysterical symptoms are nothing other than unconscious phantasies brought into view through conversion, and insofar as they are somatic, they are often drawn from the same sexual sensations and motor innervations that originally accompanied the phantasy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3045]
- The content of the hysteric's unconscious phantasies corresponds completely to the situations in which satisfaction is consciously obtained by perverts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3045]
- Many hysterical symptoms express not a single unconscious phantasy but two—one masculine and one feminine—so that the symptom corresponds to a union of two libidinal phantasies of opposite sexual character, reflecting the subject's innate bisexual disposition. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3047]
- The giving up of the habit of masturbation is undone in hysterical symptoms, restoring the original primary sexual satisfaction as a pathological approximation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3045]
- Hysterical symptoms arise as a compromise between two opposite affective and drive impulses, of which one attempts to bring to expression a component drive or constituent of the sexual constitution, and the other attempts to suppress it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3046]

## Inferences

- Inference: The theory that hysterical symptoms express both masculine and feminine unconscious phantasies represents a significant advance beyond the earlier formula that symptoms are merely compromises between libidinal and repressing impulses.
