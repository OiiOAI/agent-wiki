# Digital Detox

## Summary

A digital detox is the practice of abstaining from using digital technology for a period of time, moving focus away from technology temporarily to concentrate on real-world life, face-to-face social interactions, and personal well-being. It follows the same fundamental idea as medical detoxification—removing toxic influences. Signs that someone needs a digital detox include inability to concentrate, staying up late to play games, feeling anxious without phone access, and constantly checking social media metrics.

## Key facts

- A digital detox involves abstaining from using any form of digital technology including phones, tablets, computers, TV, social media, and the internet. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p67]
- FOMO (fear of missing out) is a fear of missing out on the best events or trends, compelling people to seek constant connectivity and check their devices constantly. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p68]
- The book states 'Comparison is the biggest thief of your innermost joy in life.'. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p70]
- Using smartphones prior to bedtime interferes with the quantity and quality of sleep, with significant effects on children. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p71]
- The mere presence of a smartphone can reduce the quality of conversation with other people—this is dubbed the 'iPhone Effect.'. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p72]
- The book recommends limiting tech use to bare minimum—only as necessary—during specific times including before bed, when waking up, during meetings, when spending time with loved ones, and during meals. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p74]
- Research suggests that reducing social media usage by 30 minutes each day can improve quality of life by reducing depression symptoms and loneliness. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p75]
- A study showed that after a 24-hour digital detox, participants slept better due to reduced blue light emissions allowing the brain to better signal melatonin release. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p80]
