# Imprinting

## Summary

Imprinting is a widespread phenomenon studied primarily in birds, where particular combinations of external stimuli (sound and visual cues) become permanently embedded in neural circuits to define specific constructs such as 'mother.' It exemplifies how attractor-like circuit properties during critical periods can create lasting neural representations.

## Key facts

- Imprinting is a critical period phenomenon where particular sensory combinations come to define intrinsic central connectivity, such that a particular sound and visual clue in conjunction mean 'mother' forever. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p201]
- There is a sensitive period during which repetition of stimulus sequences will be embedded; after this period passes, even the real mother will never mean mother. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p201]
- The attractor-like properties of neural circuits are very strong during the critical period—a duckling will imprint to a shoe if repeatedly presented, following it instead of its quacking mother. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p202]
- Once the mother construct is embedded, it can be activated in full by a single sensory component—mother's quack alone means 'mother: go to her.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p203]
