# Peer-Group Syndrome

## Summary

A common obstacle in group decision-making where peers hesitate to express differing opinions, instead waiting for consensus to develop. This leads groups to 'wander around' feeling each other out. The solution is the 'peer-plus-one' approach, where a senior person is brought in to provide direction and confidence.

## Key facts

- The peer-group syndrome occurs when people are reluctant to come out with an opinion in the presence of their peers due to fear of going against the group. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1075-L1077]
- In peer-group syndrome, members wait for a consensus to develop before anyone risks taking a position, and when consensus emerges, one member states it as a group opinion rather than a personal position. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1077-L1079]
- The peer-plus-one approach involves bringing in a senior manager who acts as a 'godfather' and repository of knowledge about how decisions should be made. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1095-L1097]
- Self-confidence to overcome peer-group syndrome comes from the realization that nobody has ever died from making a wrong business decision or being overruled. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1090-L1092]
- Junior people hang back due to fear of being overruled and losing face in front of their peers, more than fear of sanctions or job loss. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1100-L1102]
