# Jealousy in Romantic Relationships

## Summary

This chunk explores jealousy as an intrinsic response to infidelity, examining its modern clinical framing (PTSD vocabulary), its function as proof of love, its capacity to enhance eroticism, and debates about whether it is evolutionarily innate or socially constructed. It distinguishes jealousy from envy, discusses the concept of compersion in nonmonogamous relationships, and presents clinical approaches to reframing jealous thoughts rather than suppressing them.

## Key facts

- Polly's response to discovering her husband Nigel's affair included both trauma and jealousy, which Esther Perel distinguishes as separate responses. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p87]
- Proust called jealousy 'the demon that cannot be exorcised,' and Perel notes it has acquired modern vocabulary such as 'trauma,' 'intrusive thoughts,' 'flashbacks,' and 'attachment injury.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p87]
- Ayala Malach Pines writes that 'jealousy is the shadow of love' because it affirms that we value our partner and relationship. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p88]
- The etymology of 'jealousy' traces to the Greek word zelos, meaning zeal, which Perel connects to having something to fight for rather than remaining a victim. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p88]
- Sexologist Jack Morin identifies 'longing—the desire for what is not present' as the first of his Four Cornerstones of Eroticism. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p89]
- Envy relates to something you want but do not have, whereas jealousy relates to something you have but are afraid of losing; envy is a tango between two people while jealousy requires three. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p91]
- Anthropologist Helen Fisher's fMRI studies show romantic love literally activates the same brain areas as cocaine or nicotine addiction. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p92]
- Evolutionary psychologist David Buss posits jealousy is 'an exquisitely tailored adaptive mechanism' that served ancestral interests. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p95]
- Compersion is a term used in polyamorous communities to describe happiness at seeing one's partner enjoy sexual contact with someone else. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p95]
- Perel suggests that jealous thoughts can be reframed as proof of love and aliveness rather than purely pathological symptoms requiring elimination. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p94]
