# Dream Symbolism in Freudian Theory

## Summary

Dream symbolism in Freudian psychoanalysis refers to the indirect representation of latent dream thoughts through symbols, particularly sexual content. Freud recognized that symbols are not peculiar to dreams but are characteristic of unconscious ideation, appearing in folklore, myths, legends, linguistic idioms, and jokes. The most common symbols include elongated objects (sticks, umbrellas) for male genitalia, and hollow objects (boxes, cases, rooms) for female genitalia.

## Key facts

- Long, elongated objects such as sticks, tree-trunks, umbrellas (with opening comparable to erection), knives, daggers, and pikes represent the male organ. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p354]
- Rooms in dreams usually represent women ('Frauenzimmer'), with various ways in and out representing bodily orifices. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p354]
- Steps, ladders, or staircases, or walking up or down them, are representations of the sexual act due to the rhythmical pattern shared with copulation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p356]
- Children in dreams often stand for the genitals, and both men and women refer to their genitals affectionately as their 'little ones'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p357]
- Snakes, fishes, snails, cats, and mice represent sexual symbols, with snakes being most important for the male organ. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p357]
- Dream symbols are not peculiar to dreams but are characteristic of unconscious ideation, appearing in folklore, myths, legends, linguistic idioms, and proverbial wisdom. - The symbolic relation seems to be a relic and mark of former identity, as things symbolically connected today were probably united in prehistoric times by conceptual and linguistic identity. ## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's view that symbolism in dreams derives from unconscious thinking that is shared with other cultural productions suggests that dream interpretation can draw upon linguistic, mythological, and folkloric parallels.
- Inference: The plastic nature of psychical material means symbols frequently have more than one meaning, requiring context for correct interpretation.
