# Brain Opioids and Social Behavior

## Summary

Endogenous brain opioids play a critical role in social bonding and attachment. Opioid systems mediate social comfort, reduce separation distress, and facilitate social recognition. Oxytocin and opioids work together to regulate social attachment behaviors, with opioids being released during social grooming and touch, promoting positive social feelings.

## Key facts

- Endogenous opioids are key mediators of social bonding and attachment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p204]
- Opioid blockade and social comfort in chicks demonstrates the social bonding function of endogenous opioids. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p206]
- Low doses of oxytocin facilitate social recognition in rats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p207]

## Inferences

- Inference: The opioid theory of autism suggests that dysregulation of brain opioid systems may contribute to social deficits observed in autism spectrum disorders, based on evidence that naltrexone (an opioid antagonist) shows therapeutic effects in some autistic children.
- Inference: Brain opioids may have evolved as a biochemical substrate for both social attachment and the relief of separation distress, linking these two important social-emotional functions.
