# Phobia in Children

## Summary

Phobias in children, as illustrated by Little Hans's horse phobia, are characterized by the secondary displacement of anxiety onto specific objects. The anxiety itself originates from unconscious conflicts, particularly Oedipal wishes, and becomes attached to objects that are symbolically connected to the original conflict. Children's phobias often involve diffuse but strictly conditioned fears that reflect condensation of multiple unconscious meanings. The objects of phobias (like horses for Hans) serve as representatives or substitutes for the actual unconscious conflicts.

## Key facts

- The phobia extended beyond horses to include carts, furniture vans, and buses—all heavily loaded vehicles, connected to the meaning of pregnancy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3234]
- Hans was afraid not only of horses falling down but also of biting horses, horses starting to move, and horses that looked big and heavy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3234]
- The material for the disguises which Hans's fear adopted was collected from his daily impressions of the Head Customs House across the street. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3235]
- Phobias in children are characterized by their secondary nature—the relation between the anxiety and its objects is secondary, not primary. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3235]
- The horse in Hans's phobia represented his father, and the horse's falling down represented both his father's death and childbirth. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3234]
- Hans's phobia showed how unconscious thoughts from the past emerge into consciousness through displacement and symbolization. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3217]
