# Criminals from a Sense of Guilt

## Summary

Freud introduced the concept of 'criminals from a sense of guilt' (Täter aus Schuldgefühl) based on psychoanalytic observations that some individuals commit misdeeds because they already possess an oppressive sense of guilt, and the deed provides relief by attaching this guilt to something concrete. The misdeed arises from pre-existing guilty feeling rather than the guilt arising from the deed.

## Key facts

- The sense of guilt is present before the misdeed and does not arise from it; conversely, the misdeed arises from the sense of guilt. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4732]
- This obscure sense of guilt derives from the Oedipus complex and is a reaction to the two great criminal intentions of killing the father and having sexual relations with the mother. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4732]
- Parricide and incest with the mother are the two great human crimes pursued and abhorred in primitive communities. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4732]
- Children are often 'naughty' on purpose to provoke punishment, and are quiet and contented after they have been punished, revealing guilty feeling which induced them to seek punishment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4732]
- Nietzsche described this phenomenon in 'On the Pale Criminal' in Thus Spoke Zarathustra, where the pre-existence of the feeling of guilt and the utilization of a deed to rationalize this feeling glimmer before us. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4733]

## Inferences

- Inference: This concept suggests that for some criminals, punishment may serve psychological functions beyond deterrence, potentially providing relief from unconscious guilt rather than merely serving retributive or deterrent purposes
