# Sound Localization

## Summary

Sound localization is the process by which animals determine the spatial origin of sounds. In the horizontal plane, localization relies on interaural time differences (for low frequencies) and interaural intensity differences (for high frequencies). Mammals additionally use spectral filtering by the head and ears to localize sounds in the vertical plane. The neural pathways for localization include the superior olivary complex, inferior colliculus, and superior colliculus.

## Key facts

- Sound sources at different positions in the horizontal plane affect the two ears differentially: sound arrives earlier and is more intense at the ear nearer the source. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p734]
- The maximal interaural delay in humans is approximately 600 microseconds; in small birds the greatest delay is only 35 microseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p734]
- Humans can resolve the location of a sound source directly ahead to within approximately 1 degree, corresponding to an interaural time difference of 10 microseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p734]
- Spectral filtering in mammals allows sounds to be localized in the vertical plane and with a single ear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p734]
- Among vertebrates only mammals have dorsal cochlear nuclei and only mammals have been shown to make use of spectral cues for sound localization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p741]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Jeffress model proposes that delay lines allow coincidence detection of inputs from the two ears to form a map of interaural time differences.
- Inference: Sound localization abilities develop during a sensitive period in early life and can be altered by modified sensory experience.
