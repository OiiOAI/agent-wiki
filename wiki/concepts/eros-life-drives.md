# Eros (Life Drives)

## Summary

Eros is Freud's term for the life-preserving drives, encompassing sexual drives and the drive toward combination and preservation of living substance. In 'Beyond the Pleasure Principle,' Freud develops a dual-drive theory contrasting Eros (life drives) with Thanatos (death drives), building on Weismann's distinction between mortal soma and immortal germ-plasm.

## Key facts

- Freud equates Eros with 'the preserver of all things' and describes it as working toward the combination of organic substances into 'ever-larger unities'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5621]
- Sexual drives are described as 'conservative in a higher degree' than ego/death drives because they preserve life itself for longer periods and are 'peculiarly resistant to external influences'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5620]
- Freud draws an analogy between Weismann's morphological distinction of mortal soma vs immortal germ-plasm and his own dynamic distinction of death drives vs life/sexual drives. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5624]
- The germ cells behave in a 'completely narcissistic' fashion, retaining their libido for themselves against their later constructive activity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5630]

## Inferences

- Inference: Eros as conceptualized here encompasses not only genital sexuality but the broader biological drive toward survival, combination, and reproduction.
- Inference: Freud connects his psychoanalytic concept of libido with biological concepts of life preservation, using the Greek term Eros to emphasize the philosophical dimension.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the sexual drives (Eros) themselves exhibit the 'conservative' character of returning to earlier states, or whether this quality applies more clearly to the death drives.
