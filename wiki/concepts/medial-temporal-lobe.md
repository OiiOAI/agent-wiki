# Medial Temporal Lobe

## Summary

The medial temporal lobe is a brain region including the hippocampus, entorhinal cortex, perirhinal cortex, parahippocampal cortex, and amygdala that is critical for long-term declarative memory formation. Henry Molaison's bilateral removal of medial temporal lobe structures created the most studied case of human amnesia.

## Key facts

- MRI scans revealed that Henry was missing the front half of each hippocampus, the entorhinal cortex, perirhinal cortex, parahippocampal cortex, and most of the amygdala—about 5 centimeters of tissue extending front to back. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p81]
- The MRI showed that approximately two centimeters of hippocampus still remained in each half of Henry's brain, but the pathways carrying information to it had been destroyed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p81]
- The entire medial temporal lobe lesion extended just over five centimeters from front to back, far less than the eight centimeters William Scoville had estimated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p81]
- An important MRI finding was that beyond the hippocampus, some medial temporal-lobe tissue was left behind on both sides—the back part of the parahippocampal gyrus including perirhinal and parahippocampal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p82]

## Inferences

- Inference: Henry's residual medial temporal-lobe tissue may have enabled his occasional surprising memories, such as drawing the floor plan of his house and recognizing complex color pictures up to six months later.
