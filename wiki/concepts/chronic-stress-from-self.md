# Chronic Stress from Self

## Summary

Chronic stress arising from self is usually based on Gremlin activity—learnt unhelpful behaviours such as poor coping strategies or learnt destructive beliefs. The chapter identifies common patterns including creating one's own misery through behaviours that alienate others, making red-herring excuses that prevent facing real problems, and the Mushroom Syndrome where individuals compulsively grow worries instead of mushrooms.

## Key facts

- Chronic stress from yourself is usually based on Gremlin activity, including poor coping strategies and destructive beliefs such as seeing yourself as less than others. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p149]
- The Mushroom Syndrome describes people who cannot stop growing worries, finding something to worry about even when there is nothing to worry about—a learnt destructive habit that exhausts both the person and those around them. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p149]
- Conflicting drives from within commonly occur when a maternal Chimp drive battles with a Human fulfilment desire, such as a working mother feeling she is failing both at work and with family. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p150]
- Beating yourself up and feeling guilty are two very useless and destructive Gremlins that never have anything constructive to offer. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p151]
- Asking 'how' questions rather than 'why' questions is recommended because 'how' questions are planning questions that tend to be constructive, while 'why' questions are backward-looking and unconstructive. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p151]
