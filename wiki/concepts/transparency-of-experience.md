# Transparency of Experience

## Summary

The transparency of experience thesis holds that in perceptual experience, we are aware only of the objects and properties presented by that experience, not of intrinsic mental features. Thompson distinguishes extreme transparency claims (we are never aware of experience itself) from moderate claims (we are not usually aware but can with effort attend to features of experience). The extreme version is criticized as neglecting prereflective self-consciousness.

## Key facts

- Extreme transparency of awareness: We are not aware of (intrinsic mental features of) our experience, but only of the objects and properties presented by that experience. Extreme transparency of attention: We cannot attend to (intrinsic mental features of) our experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p280]
- Moderate transparency of awareness: We are not usually aware of (intrinsic mental features of) our experience, but only of the objects and properties presented. Moderate transparency of attention: We can (with effort) attend to (intrinsic mental features of) our experience, but not by turning our attention away from what that experience presents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p280]
- The extreme transparency claims neglect the prereflective self-consciousness constitutive of subjective experience: in seeing, I am explicitly aware of the object but also implicitly aware of my seeing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p281]
