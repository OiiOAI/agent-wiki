# Wish Fulfilment

## Summary

Wish fulfilment is the fundamental principle that Freud identified as governing both dreams and neurotic symptoms. Dreams are wish fulfilments of repressed thoughts; symptoms are wish fulfilments that must satisfy both the repressed and the repressing thought, making them compromises. This principle extends to hysterical attacks and acute delusional insanity.

## Key facts

- Dreams are wish fulfilments; hysterical attacks are wish fulfilments; every neurotic event is wish fulfilment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p304]
- The distinction between dreams and symptoms is that a symptom must be something else besides wish fulfilment of the repressed thought—it must also be the wish fulfilment of the repressing thought. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p304]
- A symptom is the wish fulfilment of the repressing thought when it is a punishment, a self-punishment, or the final replacement of self-gratification. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p304]
- The first motive for symptom-formation is, chronologically, libido; thus symptoms, like dreams, are the fulfilment of a wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p283]
- Symptoms serve the purpose of keeping the incompatible idea away from the ego; this purpose is served by the fact that the ego accepts the symptom. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p283]
