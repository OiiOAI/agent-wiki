# Informational Interview

## Summary

An informational interview is a structured conversation with an expert to learn about a new field or passion. The three-act conversation plan involves preparation before the meeting, getting the best out of the conversation through rapport-building, genuine interest, and open-ended questions, and finally thanking and reciprocating to maintain ongoing relationships.

## Key facts

- The three ground rules for conversations with experts are: aim to build rapport without selling yourself, come across as an interested inquirer rather than an interrogator, and ask open-ended questions to get responders to go deeper. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p95]
- Nigel Toon, CEO of Graphcore, emphasizes making experts feel like they are the teacher, which encourages them to share more information openly. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p95]
- A study by Kashdan and Roberts found that people were rated as warmer and more attractive if they showed real curiosity in an exchange. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p95]
- Closed-ended questions with yes-or-no answers neither encourage conversation nor spark new ideas. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p96]
- The book provides sample thank-you letter templates for following up after conversations with experts. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p96]
