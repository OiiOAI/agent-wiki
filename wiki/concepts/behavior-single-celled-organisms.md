# Behavior in Single-Celled Organisms

## Summary

Single-celled organisms including bacteria and protozoa exhibit genuine behavioral responses despite lacking nervous systems. These include taxic behaviors (approach/withdrawal movements), learning and memory, fluid regulation, thermoregulation, and even social behaviors through biofilms. LeDoux argues these behaviors demonstrate that survival responses predate nervous systems.

## Key facts

- Bacteria exhibit approach and avoidance behaviors using running and tumbling movements controlled by flagella. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p48]
- Bacteria can form internal molecular representations of environmental conditions and use them to predict future conditions—suggesting learning and memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p51]
- Bacteria form biofilms—collections of cells stuck together that communicate electrically to coordinate feeding and reproduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p50]
- Protozoa like paramecia use taxic behaviors to engage with useful and harmful stimuli in their daily lives. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p44]
- Learning and memory do not require a nervous system and are present even in unicellular organisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p51]
