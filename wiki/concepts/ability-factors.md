# Ability Factors

## Summary

Ability Factors are the five elements that determine how easy or hard a behavior is to perform: Time (do you have enough time?), Money (do you have enough money?), Physical (are you physically capable?), Mental Effort (does it require creative or mental energy?), and Routine (does it fit into your current routine or require adjustments?). Fogg describes these as links in an 'Ability Chain'—the chain is only as strong as its weakest link. The Discovery Question identifies which factor is causing difficulty, while the Breakthrough Question asks how to make the behavior easier using skills, tools, or making the behavior tiny.

## Key facts

- The five Ability Factors are: Time, Money, Physical capability, Mental effort, and Routine fit. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p96]
- The Ability Chain is only as strong as its weakest Ability Factor link. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p96]
- The three approaches to making a behavior easier are: increase skills, get tools and resources, and make the behavior tiny. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p98]
