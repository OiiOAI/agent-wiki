# Phobias

## Summary

Phobias are anxiety states organized around specific objects or situations, distinguished from obsessions by their monotonous anxiety affect and typical symptoms. Freud classified phobias into common phobias (fear of storms, snakes, illness) and locomotive phobias (agoraphobia), with a psychical mechanism distinct from obsessional neurosis.

## Key facts

- Phobias differ from obsessions in that the affect is always anxiety and the symptoms are typical rather than specialized. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p131]
- Common phobias involve fear of things that normally evoke some anxiety (storms, darkness, snakes, illnesses); locomotive phobias include agoraphobia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p131]
- The psychical mechanism of phobias involves no substitution or repressed idea but rather an irreducible tendency to anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p131]
- Phobias belong to anxiety neurosis and are regularly accompanied by other symptoms of that neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: Phobias represent a somatic anxiety tendency that becomes linked to specific ideational content, making them dependent on the arousal of that content for anxiety manifestation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The complete elucidation of the relationship between somatic anxiety and phobic ideation remained an unsolved problem in this work.
