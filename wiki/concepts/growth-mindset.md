# Growth mindset

## Summary

Carol Dweck's concept of the growth mindset describes an orientation toward learning characterized by actively seeking and welcoming feedback, including negative feedback, as a path to improvement. The fixed mindset, by contrast, focuses on preserving a positive self-image and avoids challenges that might produce failure. Ahrens applies this framework to academic writing: students praised for being 'talented' rather than for effort tend to develop fixed mindsets and avoid the challenges necessary for genuine expertise. A growth mindset is necessary but not sufficient; the slip-box provides a practical system that creates the feedback loops through which growth mindset translates into measurable improvement.

## Key facts

- Dweck's research shows that the most reliable predictor of long-term success is having a 'growth mindset' that actively seeks and welcomes feedback. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p53]
- Highly gifted students who receive praise for what they are (talented) rather than for what they do tend to develop a fixed mindset and avoid new challenges. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p53]
- The linear writing model provides very few feedback opportunities spread over long intervals, making it difficult to apply a growth mindset in practice. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p54]
- The circular slip-box approach implements multiple small feedback loops—checking understanding when writing literature notes, spotting contradictions when linking permanent notes—that enable improvement multiple times daily. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p54-55]

## Inferences

- Inference: Growth mindset alone does not produce expert writers; it must be paired with a system that generates frequent, concrete feedback—precisely what the slip-box's note-linking and contradiction-spotting mechanisms provide.
