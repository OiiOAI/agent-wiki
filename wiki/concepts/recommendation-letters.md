# How to Write a Recommendation Letter

## Summary

Recommendation letters are used for graduate admission, academic jobs, or scholarly prizes. Effective recommendations follow the VSN-C framework, honor Grice's maxims, write the amount the community expects, be unambiguous, tell stories about the candidate, use titles and surnames, and avoid volunteering to be called. Different purposes require different focuses: research potential for graduate admission, vision and progress for jobs, recent achievements for business, and impact and inspiration for prizes.

## Key facts

- When recommending, you establish that the candidate has a Vision and characteristics needed to do something with that Vision. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p343]
- For graduate admission, focus on evidence of research potential rather than undergraduate activities unrelated to research. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p346]
- For academic jobs, emphasize that the candidate is progressing on a series of steps toward a Vision with important steps just ahead. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p346]
- For business jobs, focus on recent achievement and what the candidate can do for the employer, not what happened before entering the workforce. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p347]
- For prizes, explain how the candidate has influenced you personally, as this assures decision makers the choice will be considered wise by at least one person. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p348]
- Use the candidate's title and surname; avoid given names and especially diminutive names or nicknames. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p345]
- Avoid ending letters with 'Do not hesitate to call me' as some readers assume this is code for lack of enthusiasm. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p345]
- If you agree to recommend someone, ask for talking points to help write an effective letter. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p348]
