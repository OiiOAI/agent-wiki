# Problem Formation

## Summary

Problem formation examines how problems arise and are perpetuated. The authors identify three main ways problems are mishandled: (A) denying a problem exists when action is necessary; (B) attempting change regarding unchangeable or nonexistent difficulties; (C) committing errors in logical typing by attempting first-order change when second-order change is needed, or vice versa. The authors distinguish between difficulties (undesirable situations that may be resolvable or must simply be lived with) and problems (impasses created through the mishandling of difficulties).

## Key facts

- Problems are impasses created and maintained through the mishandling of difficulties, not the difficulties themselves. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- The first way of mishandling is denying a problem exists when action is necessary. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- The second way is attempting change regarding a difficulty that is unchangeable or nonexistent. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- The third way is committing errors in logical typing—attempting first-order change when second-order change is needed, or vice versa. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Problem formation can occur on any level of human functioning: individual, dyadic, familial, sociopolitical. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
