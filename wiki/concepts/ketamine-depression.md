# Ketamine in Depression Treatment

## Summary

Ketamine is an NMDA glutamate receptor antagonist that has shown promise as a rapidly acting antidepressant. Unlike traditional antidepressants that take weeks to work, ketamine can produce antidepressant effects within 2 hours when administered by intravenous infusion, with effects lasting approximately 7 days.

## Key facts

- Ketamine blocks the NMDA glutamate receptor and produces rapid antidepressant effects within 2 hours. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1562]
- The therapeutic effects of ketamine last approximately 7 days, after which second and third doses may continue to be effective. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1562]
- At higher doses, ketamine produces psychotropic effects including euphoria, dissociation, depersonalization, and hallucinations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1562]

## Inferences

- Inference: Ketamine represents the first antidepressant drug that does not exert its primary action on monoamine neurotransmission.
