# Cortical control over vocalization

## Summary

A gradual superimposition of cortical motor control on visceral motor systems for vocalization has made possible human speaking abilities. In most mammals, cortical areas control mouth and tongue movements for grooming and eating but are not directly involved in call production. In primates, increased forebrain size gives cortical projections a favorable competitive bias, allowing them to outcompete local connections and persist in brain stem motor nuclei. In humans, this process likely extended to visceral motor nuclei controlling the larynx and breathing.

## Key facts

- The only cortical areas involved in call production in primates are located in the medial frontal cortex (anterior cingulate cortex), which are intermediate between limbic cortex and neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p263]
- In monkeys, cortical projections connect directly with facial, trigeminal, and hypoglossal motor nuclei, but not with visceral motor nuclei like the nucleus ambiguus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p267]
- Monkeys lack direct cortico-ambiguus connections, and cortical motor damage does not eliminate a monkey's ability to vocalize. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p269]
- Primate forebrain expansion provides a 'vertical rendition of the displacement process' responsible for connectional changes, with more numerous cortical axons displacing less numerous local connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p268]
- Human cortical projections have probably also invaded nuclei controlling visceral muscle systems, including the nucleus ambiguus (larynx) and those controlling breathing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p268]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Direct evidence of such a change in the human brain's motor output to the larynx is not yet available, though circumstantial evidence is abundant.
