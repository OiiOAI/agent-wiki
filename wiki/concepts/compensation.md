# Compensation

## Summary

Compensation is the standard approach to learning disabilities that works around the problem rather than correcting it, based on the now-outdated theory that once brain cells die or fail to develop, they cannot be restored. Examples include using audiotapes for reading difficulties, giving extra time on tests, or color-coding main points.

## Key facts

- The accepted theory of the time held that once brain cells die or fail to develop, they cannot be restored. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- Compensations work around the problem: people with trouble reading listen to audiotapes, those who are 'slow' get more time on tests, those who have trouble following an argument are told to color-code main points. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- Barbara found the compensation program too time-consuming. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- Her thesis showed that most children treated with compensations at the OISE clinic were not really improving. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- She had so many deficits that it was sometimes hard to find healthy functions that could work around her deficits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- PowerPoint presentations are described as 'the ultimate compensation for a weak premotor cortex'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p51]
