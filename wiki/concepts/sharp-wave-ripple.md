# Sharp Wave Ripple

## Summary

Sharp wave ripples are spontaneously occurring, highly synchronized hippocampal population events that emerge when the brain is disengaged from the environment, such as during non-REM sleep, eating, drinking, or grooming. They consist of two components: a large-amplitude sharp wave reflecting strong firing coordination among CA3 pyramidal neurons, and a fast ripple oscillation (140-200 Hz) generated by the tug-of-war between excitatory and inhibitory neurons in CA1. Sharp wave ripples represent the highest excitatory gain in the hippocampal system, making them especially suitable for transmitting information to the neocortex.

## Key facts

- Sharp wave ripples are produced by tens of thousands of neurons in the hippocampus, subicular complex, and entorhinal cortex, firing over just 30-100 ms when the brain is disengaged from the environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p200]
- Sharp wave ripples consist of two components: the sharp wave (large-amplitude negative deflection in the apical dendritic layer of CA1) and the ripple (140-200 Hz fast oscillatory synchronization). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p201]
- During sharp wave ripples, excitation makes a two- to threefold gain over inhibition, representing the highest excitatory gain in the hippocampal system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p203]
- Sharp wave ripples contain compressed sequences of neuronal firing that can occur in forward or reverse order relative to the animal's prior experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p205]
- Forward replay events occur predominantly before running and may serve planning functions, while reverse replay events dominate after running and may serve memory consolidation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p206]
- Sharp wave ripples are found in the same form and shape in every mammalian brain investigated to date, suggesting conserved functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p201]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sharp wave ripples may serve as the biological substrate for memory consolidation by replaying learned information during offline brain states.
- Inference: The palindromic (forward and reverse) nature of replay sequences may allow the brain to both predict future outcomes and evaluate past events.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact physiological mechanisms that determine whether a forward or reverse replay event occurs remain incompletely understood.
