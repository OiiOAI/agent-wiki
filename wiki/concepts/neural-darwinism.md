# Neural Darwinism

## Summary

Deacon's framework for understanding neural information processing as a Darwinian process operating at multiple timescales. At the cellular level, neural circuits are created through competition and selection among patterns of neural activity. Moment-to-moment cognition involves spontaneous generation of alternative patterns that compete for neural substrate recruitment, with winners becoming recognition processes and responses. This design logic—overproduction, variation, competition, selection—appears at every level of brain organization, from synaptic modification to higher cognitive functions. The framework explains both how consciousness is generated and why subjective experiences of intention and self-determination are genuine rather than epiphenomenal.

## Key facts

- Deacon proposes that at every step, the design logic of brains is Darwinian: overproduction, variation, competition, selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p492]
- Consciousness is described as 'messy, anything but streamlined and efficient' because vast numbers of alternative patterns compete, mostly culled by selective processes due to lack of correlation with sensory and mnemonic information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p493]
- This 'neural microevolution' (or microgenesis) is the generator of representations: the process to which sensory input patterns are represented and with respect to which they are re-cognized as icons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p493]
- Deacon notes that different neuronal computations may compete during development and within each cognitive activity, producing functional redundancy and fractional representation in distributed networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p493]
- Unlike mechanistic views suggested by digital computer metaphors, the Darwinian model explains why subjective experiences of being originators of thoughts and actions are genuine: because the operations are not prespecified and neural circuits are created by patterns of signal processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p494]
- Deacon states 'We are what we experience ourselves to be. Our self-experience of intentions and will are not epiphenomenal illusions. They are what we should expect an evolutionlike process to feel like!'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p494]
- Gerald Edelman has also called this Neural Darwinism and others have called the process Darwin machines, building on evolutionary theory and Dawkins's concept of memes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p493]
- Edelman's Neural Darwinism frames global brain functions on a Darwinian-like theory of neuronal group interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Edelman hypothesized that the initial nonspecificity of connections provides the total initial variety upon which selection processes (in the form of learning) can later act. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Deacon argues that initial connectional nonspecificity accounts for only a small fraction of the variety of information available to selection/learning processes, with the rest supplied 'on line' by continuous, spontaneous, nonspecific sources including metabolic noise effects and continuous axodendritic growth processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Deacon argues that initial connectional nonspecificity and axonal competition principally influences cognitive processes by introducing global biases in neural computations resulting from global patterns of regional parcellation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Darwinian model suggests that mental disorders involving conflicting intentions may have a direct neural correlate in competing signal processing pathways.
- Inference: Because evolution is an 'origination process' capable of creating adaptive structural information where none previously existed, minds genuinely author their own responses rather than executing predetermined programs.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Deacon notes that a detailed discussion of the Darwinian nature of neural information processing would take the discussion too far from the book's theme of symbolic processes.
