# Mitigated Free Will

## Summary

Mitigated free will is the view that biological causation of behavior is compatible with moral responsibility, provided the capacity for rational deliberation remains sufficiently intact. Believers in this stance accept that brain damage or severe biological impairment can eliminate responsibility, but hold that ordinary biological influences on behavior do not excuse moral accountability.

## Key facts

- If someone had their entire frontal cortex destroyed, you probably shouldn't hold them responsible for their actions, because their rationality is grossly impaired when deciding their own courses of action. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p607]
- Mitigated free will proponents distinguish between 'causation' (every behavior having been caused) and 'compulsion' (a subset of behaviors being really caused by something that compromises rational, deliberative processes). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p609]
- The stance of assigning aptitude to biology and effort to free will, or impulse to biology and resisting it to free will, is the most permeating and destructive among views of mitigated free will. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p614]

## Inferences

- Inference: Being a child molester is as much a product of biology as is being a pedophile; the distinction some make between 'you can't choose to be a pedophile but you can choose not to act on it' reflects folk psychology rather than rigorous analysis.
- Inference: The column of 'homuncular grit' (effort, resisting impulse) is itself a property of the physical universe and biology, just as natural ability is.
