# Chemotaxis

## Summary

Chemotaxis is the directed movement of organisms toward or away from chemical stimuli. In bacteria like E. coli, chemotaxis involves comparing signal concentrations over time to bias movement toward food sources. The system involves sensory receptors, signal integration, and motor outputs that control flagellar rotation, demonstrating how single-celled organisms make strategic decisions about movement.

## Key facts

- E. coli move by rotating flagella—counterclockwise rotation propels them forward while clockwise rotation causes tumbling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p56]
- E. coli compare chemical concentrations over time rather than instantaneously to determine direction of movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p56]
- The bacterial chemotaxis system integrates multiple signals including food concentration, temperature, pH, and cell density. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p61]
- Paramecia respond to mechanical disturbance through membrane receptors that generate electrical signals similar to those used by nerve cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p57]
