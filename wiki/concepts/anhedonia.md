# Anhedonia

## Summary

Anhedonia refers to the reduced ability to experience pleasure and is a cardinal feature of major depressive disorder. Research suggests it may reflect reduced sensitivity to reward-related cues and deficits in reward anticipation rather than consummatory processes.

## Key facts

- Anhedonia is a core symptom of depression characterized by loss of pleasure and reduced hedonic capacity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p281]
- Depressed and anhedonic participants fail to distinguish between options yielding large versus small rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p289]
- Anhedonic symptoms are negatively correlated with activation in the ventral striatum, dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, and amygdala in response to positive stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p290]
- Blunted responsiveness to reward-related cues may lead to reduced motivational drive to pursue pleasurable activities, contributing to depressive symptoms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p290]
- Reduced hedonic capacity and low scores on self-report measures of sensitivity to reward-related cues characterize both currently depressed and remitted depressed individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p290]

## Inferences

- Inference: Ventral striatal dysfunction may lead to deficits in reward anticipation and reward-based learning, contributing to anhedonia.
- Inference: Whether anhedonia is more closely tied to deficits in reward anticipation or consumption remains an important question for future research.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact cause of anhedonia in depression remains unclear, particularly what causes the associated dysfunction in striatal and orbitofrontal regions.
