# Autism as Prediction Breakdown

## Summary

Autism is speculated to be a disorder of prediction. If you couldn't predict at all, ever, your body budget would be screwed up, you'd have difficulty integrating sensory input, impaired statistical learning, and trouble learning concepts. You might be anxious all the time because sensations are unpredictable. People with autism who can describe their experiences report lack of prediction and overwhelming prediction error. Temple Grandin describes struggling to form concepts as a child, categorizing dogs from cats by size. Naoki Higashida describes how he scans memory to find an experience closest to what's happening now. Some researchers believe autism is primarily caused by dysfunction of the control network, while others see problems with oxytocin affecting the interoceptive network.

## Key facts

- Autism is speculated to be a disorder of prediction. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p237]
- Temple Grandin describes her lack of prediction and overwhelming prediction error, noting that sudden loud noises hurt like a dentist's drill. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p237]
- Naoki Higashida describes how he scans memory to find an experience closest to what's happening now, trying to recall what he said the last time. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p237]
- Some researchers believe autism is primarily caused by dysfunction of the control network, producing a model of the world too specific to each situation. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p237]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Barrett explicitly states this is a speculation: 'All I'm saying is: the possibility is intriguing that autism is a disorder of prediction.' Autism is enormously complex and cannot be summed up in a few paragraphs.
