# Five Whys

## Summary

The Five Whys is a root cause analysis technique developed by Taiichi Ohno as part of the Toyota Production System, adapted by Eric Ries for the Lean Startup methodology. It involves asking 'Why?' five times consecutively to trace a problem from its immediate symptom back to its fundamental human cause, enabling proportional investments in prevention that automatically regulate organizational speed.

## Key facts

- The Five Whys was developed by Taiichi Ohno, father of the Toyota Production System, as a systematic problem-solving tool. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p214]
- The Five Whys requires making proportional investments at each of five levels of the problem hierarchy, with smaller investments for minor symptoms and larger investments for more painful ones. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p215]
- The Five Whys acts as a natural speed regulator: the more problems encountered, the more investment in solutions, and as investments pay off, teams naturally speed up again. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p217]
- The Five Whys ties the rate of progress to learning, not just execution, and should be used whenever teams encounter technical faults, business result failures, or unexpected customer behavior changes. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p217]
- A simplified version of the Five Whys uses two rules: be tolerant of all mistakes the first time, and never allow the same mistake to be made twice. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p219]
