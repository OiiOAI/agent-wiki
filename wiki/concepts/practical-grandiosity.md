# Practical Grandiosity

## Summary

Practical grandiosity is a reality-based form of the grandiose energy that fuels ambition and achievement. Unlike fantastical grandiosity, which is based on fantasies and inflated self-perception, practical grandiosity channels energy into actual work, skill development, and goal achievement. The energy impels people to develop and hone their abilities, and self-esteem comes from real accomplishments rather than subjective fantasies. This concept offers five principles for channeling grandiose energy productively: coming to terms with grandiose needs, concentrating energy through deep focus, maintaining dialogue with reality through feedback, seeking calibrated challenges just above skill level, and occasionally letting the grandiose energy loose.

## Key facts

- Practical grandiosity is based not on fantasy but on reality, channeling energy into work and desire to reach goals. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p388]
- In practical grandiosity, the desire for attention is under control and subordinate to the work itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p388]
- The five basic principles for practical grandiosity are: come to terms with grandiose needs, concentrate the energy, maintain dialogue with reality, seek calibrated challenges, and let loose the grandiose energy. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p388-391]
- Concentrating energy means focusing deeply on a single project to enter a state of flow where ideas come at odd hours. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p389]
- Calibrated challenges are those just above your skill level—challenging enough to energize but not so ambitious as to overwhelm. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p390]
- Letting loose grandiose energy means occasionally entertaining greater challenges to test yourself, like developing a new skill or writing a novel. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p391]
- Winston Churchill exemplified productive grandiosity that inspired troops during World War II. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p391]
