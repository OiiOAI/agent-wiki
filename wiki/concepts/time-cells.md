# Time Cells

## Summary

Time cells are hippocampal and entorhinal neurons whose activity correlates with elapsed duration in memory tasks. However, evidence suggests these neurons encode both time and distance, challenging the view of dedicated time-tracking mechanisms.

## Key facts

- Most hippocampal and entorhinal neurons responded to both time and distance; they fired reliably at the same distance or the same time from the beginning of a run. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p261]
- A minority of neurons was relatively selective for time spent on a treadmill while an equally small fraction was better correlated with distance. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p261]
- The proper term for these neurons should be 'duration cells' because cell assemblies track duration rather than absolute time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p261]

## Inferences

- Inference: Time cells are better understood as velocity-controlled oscillators that track both distance and duration equivalently, rather than as dedicated timekeepers.
