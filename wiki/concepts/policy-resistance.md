# Policy Resistance (Fixes that Fail)

## Summary

Policy resistance occurs when multiple actors each try to pull a system stock toward their own goals, resulting in a standoff where the system stays where no one wants it. Each actor monitors the system state and responds to discrepancies from their goal, but their actions are countered by others. The result is that despite energetic efforts, nothing changes, and everyone expends energy maintaining an undesirable state. The solution is to align subsystem goals through an overarching goal that lets all actors break out of their bounded rationality.

## Key facts

- Policy resistance occurs when various actors try to pull a system stock toward various goals, producing resistance to any new policy. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p141]
- Examples include failed farm programs, wars on drugs, investment tax credits that don't stimulate investment, and decades of job creation that don't keep unemployment low. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p141]
- The alternative to overpowering policy resistance is to 'let go' and redirect energy to understanding the system and finding mutually satisfactory goals. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p143]
- The most effective approach is finding a way to align various goals, usually through an overarching goal that allows all actors to break out of bounded rationality. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p144]
- Policy resistance occurs when various actors try to pull a system state toward various goals, creating mutual opposition. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p222]
- Any new effective policy pulls the system state farther from the goals of other actors, producing additional resistance. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p222]
- The remedy for policy resistance is to let go: bring in all actors and use the energy formerly spent on resistance to seek mutually satisfactory ways for all goals to be realized. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p222]
- An alternative remedy is to seek redefinitions of larger and more important goals that everyone can pull toward together. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p222]
