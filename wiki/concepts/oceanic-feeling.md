# Oceanic Feeling

## Summary

A term attributed to Romain Rolland describing a sensation of eternity, limitless boundlessness, and union with the external world. Freud examines this feeling in 'Civilization and Its Discontents,' questioning whether it represents the original source of religious sentiment. While acknowledging that many people experience this feeling, Freud suggests it is not primary but rather a residue of the original ego feeling from early infancy, when the infant does not distinguish itself from the external world. He argues that the true source of religious need lies in the feeling of infantile helplessness and the longing for paternal protection, not in this oceanic sense. Freud views the oceanic feeling as an intellectual perception accompanied by feeling-tone, potentially serving as a consolation that disavows the threat of the external world.

## Key facts

- The oceanic feeling is described as a sensation of eternity, of something limitless and unbounded, a feeling of being one with the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6601]
- Freud traces the oceanic feeling back to an early phase of ego feeling, when the infant does not distinguish its ego from the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6606]
- Freud argues the true source of religious needs is infantile helplessness and longing for paternal protection, not the oceanic feeling. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6608]
- The concept originated from Romain Rolland, who described it as a purely subjective fact not equivalent to article of faith. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6601]
