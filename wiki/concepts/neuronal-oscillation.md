# Neuronal Oscillation

## Summary

Neuronal oscillation refers to rhythmic variations in membrane voltage across a neuron's enveloping membrane, similar to traveling sinusoidal waves in calm water. These oscillations range from less than 1 Hz to more than 40 Hz and remain in the local vicinity of the neuron. On the crests of these oscillations, larger action potentials may be evoked. Oscillatory switching of electrical activity allows neurons to switch between hearing and ignoring incoming signals, enabling simultaneity of neuronal activity—the most pervasive mode of brain operation.

## Key facts

- Neuronal oscillations are voltage variations across the cell membrane that range from less than 1 Hz to more than 40 Hz, conferring temporal agility to the system. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p15]
- The peaks and valleys of neuronal electrical oscillations dictate the waxing and waning of a cell's responsiveness to incoming synaptic signals, determining whether the cell responds to or ignores an incoming signal. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p16]
- Simultaneity of neuronal activity, brought about by intrinsic oscillatory electrical activity, resonance, and coherence, is at the root of cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p18]

## Inferences

- Inference: Neuronal oscillation provides the means for simultaneity of activity to occur in a predictable manner, serving as the electrical glue that allows the brain to organize itself functionally during development.
