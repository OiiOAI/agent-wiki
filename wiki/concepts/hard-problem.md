# Hard problem of consciousness

## Summary

The 'hard problem' of consciousness, as described by David Chalmers, asks why and how physical processing gives rise to such a rich inner life at all. It is widely agreed that experience arises from a physical basis, but there is no good explanation of why and how it so arises. Seth addresses this by proposing that the real problem approach offers genuine hope of reconciling the physical with the phenomenal—dissolving, not solving the hard problem.

## Key facts

- David Chalmers describes the hard problem: 'It is widely agreed that experience arises from a physical basis, but we have no good explanation of why and how it so arises. Why should physical processing give rise to such a rich inner life at all?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
- Philosophy has provided a range of options from panpsychism to eliminative materialism in response to the hard problem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
- The real problem approach offers genuine hope of reconciling the physical with the phenomenal—dissolving, not solving the hard problem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p255]
