# McDonaldization

## Summary

McDonaldization is George Ritzer's concept describing the process by which the principles of the fast-food restaurant (efficiency, calculability, predictability, control through technology, and the irrationality of rationality) are coming to dominate more sectors of society and the world.

## Key facts

- McDonaldization is based on Max Weber's ideas about the rationalization of the West but focuses on the fast-food restaurant rather than the bureaucracy as the model. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p485]
- The five basic dimensions of McDonaldization are efficiency, calculability, predictability, control through technology, and the irrationality of rationality. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p485]
- Over half of McDonald's restaurants are outside the United States, and well over half of profits come from overseas operations. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p487]
- The irrationality of rationality includes dehumanization where both employees and customers suffer from the dehumanizing effects of McDonaldized systems. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p487]
- Other nations have developed indigenous variants of fast-food institutions including Tim Hortons in Canada, Mos Burger in Japan, and Russkoye Bistro in Russia. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p487]

## Inferences

- Inference: McDonaldization represents an extension and intensification of Weberian rationalization into the late twentieth and twenty-first centuries.
- Inference: The globalization of McDonaldized institutions represents a form of cultural convergence driven by capitalist expansion.
