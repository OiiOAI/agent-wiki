# Human Nature

## Summary

Human nature is described in the book as the collection of forces that push and pull at us from deep within, stemming from the particular wiring of our brains, the configuration of our nervous system, and the way humans process emotions—all of which developed over approximately five million years of evolution as a social animal. The book identifies envy, grandiosity, irrationality, shortsightedness, conformity, aggression, and passive aggression as core forces of human nature.

## Key facts

- Human nature stems from brain wiring, nervous system configuration, and emotional processing that evolved over five million years. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p14]
- Humans evolved complex emotions (joy, shame, gratitude, jealousy, resentment) that could be read immediately on faces for communication. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p14]
- Humans remain highly susceptible to the moods and emotions of others, unconsciously imitating, wanting what others have, and getting swept up in viral feelings. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p14]
- The book identifies envy, grandiosity, irrationality, shortsightedness, conformity, aggression, and passive aggression as core forces of human nature. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p15]
- Social media has heightened emotional permeability, intensified status comparison and envy, and amplified tribal tendencies. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p17]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book argues that technology and progress have not changed human nature but have merely altered the forms of irrationality, making ancient psychological insights still relevant.
