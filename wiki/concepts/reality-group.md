# Reality Group

## Summary

The Reality Group is a functional, high-performing organizational model characterized by a tight relationship to reality—emphasis on getting things done, maximizing resources, and adapting to a competitive, changing environment. In contrast to dysfunctional groups that breed factions, politics, and mediocrity, reality groups channel members' higher nature, creating powerful upward momentum. The model contrasts with dysfunctional cultures where members become confused about roles, pursue self-interest, and allow bad actors to thrive, ultimately producing incompetent leaders and repeated failures.

## Key facts

- Dysfunctional groups produce incompetent lieutenants and grandiose leaders because the culture itself is at fault, not individual actors. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p527]
- The Reality Group maintains tight relationship to reality, defined by: purpose (getting things done, making things, solving problems); resources (labor, strengths, finances); environment (competitive and constantly changing). [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p528]
- Five key strategies for creating a Reality Group: (1) Instill a collective sense of purpose, (2) Assemble the right team of lieutenants, (3) Let information and ideas flow freely, (4) Infect the group with productive emotions, (5) Forge a battle-tested group. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p528]
- Historical examples of Reality Groups include Napoleon's battalions, early IBM under Thomas Watson, FDR's initial cabinet, John Ford's film team, and the Chicago Bulls under Phil Jackson. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p528]
- Purpose must be clearly stated and publicized—not vague or implied—and emphasize excellence rather than making money as the goal itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p529]
- Leaders should select lieutenants based on competence and character, not charm, and choose people with complementary skills. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p530]
- Napoleon's success came from personally reviewing concise reports from field marshals, lieutenants, and foot soldiers, giving him multiple lines of unfiltered perspective. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p531]
- Phil Jackson found it more productive to instill calmness in his team rather than excitement, helping players execute the game plan without overreacting to ups and downs. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p532]
- A battle-tested group is developed by giving members challenging tasks and shorter deadlines to gauge their resilience under stress. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p533]
- Being in a high-functioning Reality Group can make individuals healthy and whole, developing greater empathy, confidence, and connection to reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p534]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Reality Group concept directly explains why replacing individual leaders or lieutenants in dysfunctional organizations fails—the dysfunctional dynamic persists and corrupts new members.
