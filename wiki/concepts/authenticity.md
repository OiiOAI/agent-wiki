# Authenticity and Inauthenticity

## Summary

Authenticity refers to direct engagement with the world as it really is, unmediated by conceptual frameworks, abstractions, or re-presentations. Inauthenticity results from the left hemisphere's tendency to substitute its simplified models and categories for the reality they are meant to represent. The pre-Socratics began with an 'intellectual sense of wonder at the sheer fact of existence' and a conviction that normal ways of construing the world are profoundly mistaken—an awareness of radical inauthenticity.

- Inauthenticity results from the left hemisphere substituting its simplified models for reality, a 're-presentation' rather than 'presentation.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p372]
- The pre-Socratics began with a conviction that normal ways of construing the world are profoundly mistaken—an awareness of radical inauthenticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p372]
- The decline of the West correlates with a progressive entrenchment of inauthentic, left-hemisphere modes of understanding. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p412]
- Authenticity means acting on integrated values rather than on introjected or externally imposed values. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p208]
- When controls of society are merely introjected, people may either comply or defy, but neither compliance nor defiance represents authenticity. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p208]
- To defy what authority says just because authority says it is to be irresponsible, but in a profound sense, to comply with authority just because it is authority is also to be irresponsible. - Responsibility requires that people act autonomously in relating to the world around them and behave authentically on behalf of some general good. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p209]
- People were responsible because they were able to unhook themselves from the controls around them and avoid the dynamics of compliance and defiance. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p209]
