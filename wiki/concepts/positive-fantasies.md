# Positive Fantasies

## Summary

Positive fantasies are free-flowing thoughts and images about a desired future that arise from wishes and desires. Oettingen's research demonstrates that unlike positive expectations based on past experience, positive fantasies detached from reality actually impede goal achievement. The pleasurable act of fantasizing creates a 'mental attainment' effect—fooling the mind into believing the goal has already been achieved—which relaxes the body (reducing systolic blood pressure) and saps the energy needed to take real action. Positive fantasies do serve useful functions: providing short-term pleasure, helping people endure difficult waiting periods, and enabling imaginative exploration of future options.

## Key facts

- Positive fantasies are free-flowing thoughts and images about the future that arise from wishes and desires, distinct from expectations based on past experience. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p16]
- In a study of 25 obese women in a weight-loss program, women who had strong positive fantasies about slimming down lost 24 pounds less than those who fantasized more negatively. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p18]
- Positive fantasies reduce systolic blood pressure, indicating a relaxing effect that undermines motivation. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p43]
- The mental attainment effect describes how fantasizing fools the mind into thinking the goal has already been achieved, reducing motivation to take actual action. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p49]
- In a study with 164 female college students, those who positively fantasized about wearing high heels showed lower systolic blood pressure after the exercise, while those who also generated negative thoughts showed no change. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p43]
- 83 male graduate students who frequently experienced positive fantasies about finding a job sent out fewer applications, received fewer offers, and earned less money over a two-year period. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p20]

## Inferences

- Inference: Positive fantasies appear to function through 'mental attainment,' where the mind is fooled into experiencing the wish as already fulfilled, creating a relaxed state that reduces motivation for real action.
