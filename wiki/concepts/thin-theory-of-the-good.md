# Thin Theory of the Good

## Summary

The thin theory of the good is a restricted account of goodness used to derive the principles of justice. Its purpose is to secure premises about primary goods required to arrive at the principles of justice, without prejudicing the prior place of the concept of right. Once principles are established, the full theory of the good can be developed.

## Key facts

- The thin theory is used to define primary goods and the interests of persons in the original position. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p353]
- The thin theory's purpose is to secure the premises about primary goods required to arrive at the principles of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p353]
- The thin theory explains the rational preference for primary goods and explicates the notion of rationality underlying the choice of principles in the original position. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p355]
- The thin theory must distinguish itself from the full theory to avoid circular argumentation, since the list of primary goods serves as a premise from which the principles of right are derived. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p386]
