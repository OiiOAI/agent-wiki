# Parallel Processing in Vision

## Summary

The visual system employs parallel processing strategies at all levels, from retina to cortex. Features such as form, color, motion, and depth are processed in discrete, specialized neural pathways that remain segregated yet interact. This distributed processing requires binding mechanisms to achieve unified visual perception.

## Key facts

- The retina extracts approximately 20 local features including local contrast, orientation, color, and movement through parallel channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p544]
- Parallel streams are sent along axons of retinal ganglion cells, forming the optic nerves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p544]
- At least two parallel and interacting pathways mediate object recognition and guidance of movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p544]
