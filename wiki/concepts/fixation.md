# Fixation

## Summary

Fixation is the concept that portions of the libido may remain attached to (or 'lag behind' at) earlier stages of development. These fixed points serve as regressive destinations when the libido is frustrated, and stronger fixations make the individual more susceptible to neurosis by facilitating regression.

## Key facts

- Freud defines fixation as 'the lagging behind of a part trend at an earlier stage' of libidinal development [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5103]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5103]
- Fixation represents the predisposing, internal factor in the aetiology of the neuroses, while frustration represents the accidental, external one [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5107]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5107]
- The stronger the fixations on its path of development, the more readily will the function evade external difficulties by regressing to the fixations [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5103]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5103]
- Fixation of the libido in the adult falls into two parts: the inherited constitution and the disposition acquired in early childhood [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5123]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5123]
- Fixation occurs when mental development comes to a sudden standstill and terminates at some point, because an overwhelming event has forced the libido into positions which are then clung to. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7037]

## Inferences

- Inference: Fixation and regression are two sides of the same coin: fixation represents the static point where libido remains, while regression represents the dynamic process of returning to that point [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5103]
- Inference: Fixation represents a failure to progress through psychosexual stages, creating vulnerability to regression under stress.
