# Olfactory Receptor Gene Families

## Summary

Mammals possess multiple families of olfactory chemosensory receptors. The main odorant receptor (OR) family contains approximately 350 genes in humans and 1000 in mice, all G protein-coupled receptors with seven transmembrane domains. A smaller TAAR family recognizes volatile amines. The vomeronasal organ contains V1R and V2R families (over 100 members each) for pheromone detection, with V2R having large extracellular domains. Each olfactory sensory neuron expresses only one receptor gene, enabling combinatorial coding of thousands of odors.

## Key facts

- Humans have approximately 350 different odorant receptors, whereas mice have approximately 1,000. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p732]
- Each olfactory sensory neuron expresses only one odorant receptor gene and therefore one type of receptor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p733]
- V1R and V2R vomeronasal receptor families in mice have more than 100 members each, with V2R having large extracellular N-terminal domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p738]
- Trace amine-associated receptors (TAARs) in mice recognize volatile amines present in predator and conspecific urine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p732]
- Insect odorant receptors have little sequence similarity to mammalian odorant receptors and may not be G protein-coupled receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p740]

## Inferences

- Inference: The rapid evolutionary diversification of olfactory receptor families reflects the need to detect ecologically relevant chemical signals.
- Inference: dependent evolution of insect and mammalian chemosensory receptors demonstrates convergent solution to chemical detection.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanism by which odorant receptors guide axons to specific glomeruli remains unsolved.
