# Hot-Cold Empathy Gap

## Summary

The hot-cold empathy gap is the inability, during a cool and rational moment, to appreciate how one will behave during the heat of passion and temptation. It was identified by economist George Loewenstein and explains why people set unrealistic rules for themselves when they cannot anticipate the strength of future desires.

## Key facts

- George Loewenstein coined the term 'hot-cold empathy gap' to describe the inability to appreciate how one will behave when in a heated emotional state versus a calm one. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p137]
- In one experiment, men answered questions about sexual behavior while in a cold state (by computer) and then answered the same questions while in a hot state (masturbating), with much higher acceptance of problematic behaviors in the hot state. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p139]
- Stanley observed that the Rear Column's men, who seemed civilized at home in England, became savage in Africa when depleted by fever and hardship, illustrating how heat depletes self-control. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p137]
