# Neurotransmitter Removal

## Summary

Neurotransmitter removal from the synaptic cleft is critical for terminating synaptic transmission and preventing receptor desensitization. Mechanisms include diffusion, enzymatic degradation (e.g., acetylcholinesterase for ACh), and reuptake by specific plasma membrane transporters driven by sodium gradients.

## Key facts

- At cholinergic synapses, acetylcholine is cleared primarily by enzymatic degradation to choline and acetate by acetylcholinesterase, with choline then recaptured by a high-affinity transporter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p418]
- Glutamate, GABA, glycine, and biogenic amines are removed by specific transporters at the plasma membrane of nerve terminals and glial cells, driven by the Na+ gradient. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p418]
- Neuropeptides are removed relatively slowly from the synaptic cleft by diffusion and proteolysis by extracellular peptidases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p418]
- Cocaine blocks reuptake of dopamine, norepinephrine, and serotonin; selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) are widely used antidepressants. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p422]
