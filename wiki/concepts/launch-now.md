# Launch Now

## Summary

The concept advocates launching products as soon as they do what needs to be done rather than waiting for completion of everything on the development list. The authors argue that leftovers can be done later and often better with real user feedback, while long development cycles kill momentum and morale. They cite their own launch of Basecamp without billing capability, the Camper shoe store that opened before construction finished, and Crate and Barrel's first store that displayed merchandise on the actual crates and barrels it came in.

## Key facts

- The authors argue that once a product does what it needs to do, it should be launched, and leftovers can be done later, often better with user feedback. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p73]
- 37signals launched Basecamp without billing capability because the product billed in monthly cycles, giving them a thirty-day gap to solve that problem. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p73]
- Camper opened a San Francisco store before construction finished, calling it 'Walk in Progress' and letting customers draw on walls; the most popular message was 'Keep the store just the way it is.'. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p73]
- The founders of Crate and Barrel didn't wait for fancy displays when opening their first store, instead turning over the crates and barrels the merchandise came in. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p73]

## Inferences

- Inference: Early launch creates real user feedback that is more valuable than internal planning and enables iterative improvement.
