# The Train of Knowledge Analogy

## Summary

The narrator introduces an analogy comparing knowledge to a long railroad train. Classic Knowledge is the engine and all the boxcars—the static, structured knowledge taught by the Church of Reason. Romantic Quality is not a part of the train but the leading edge of the engine, the cutting surface where the train moves along the track. The real train of knowledge isn't static but always going somewhere on a track called Quality. At the leading edge there are no subjects, no objects—only Quality ahead. This analogy illustrates why traditional dualistic reason causes stuckness: examining a stopped, subdivided train misses what makes it a train at all.

## Key facts

- Classic Knowledge is the engine and boxcars of the train; Romantic Quality is the leading edge of the engine, a two-dimensional surface without which the train couldn't go anywhere. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p241]
- The real train of knowledge is always going somewhere on a track called Quality; classic knowledge is only the collective memory of where that leading edge has been. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p242]
- At the cutting edge of this instant is always the totality of everything there is; the past cannot remember the past and the future cannot generate the future. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p242]

## Inferences

- Inference: The analogy explains why stuckness occurs: when examining the train (reality) while it is stopped and subdivided, one loses access to the Quality that determines where the train is going.
