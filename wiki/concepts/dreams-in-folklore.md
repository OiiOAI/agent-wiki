# Dreams in Folklore

## Summary

A section discussing how folk traditions interpret dream symbols in ways consistent with psychoanalysis. Folkloric dreams of treasure are interpreted as representing faeces, following the psychoanalytic finding that in childhood faeces are highly prized (coprophilic drives) before being repressed and becoming objects of contempt. The interest in faeces transfers to gold in adulthood. Defaecation dreams represent both sexual need (impotence toward the partner) and death wishes against the bedfellow disguised as wish to mark treasure. The Devil and spirits appear as bestowers of treasure and seducers, personifying repressed unconscious drive life.

## Key facts

- Psychoanalysis taught that in the earliest period of childhood faeces is a highly prized substance in relation to which coprophilic drives find satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3906]
- With repression of coprophilic drives, faeces fall into contempt and serve conscious purposes as means of expressing disdain and scorn. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3906]
- The most important residue of former esteem for faeces is that all the interest the child had in faeces transfers in adults onto gold. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3906]
- In folklore dreams, gold is unambiguously seen as a symbol of faeces; the pile of faeces serves as a sign to mark where treasure is to be found. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3907]
- Extensible objects are ordinarily symbols of erection; thread refusing to get long enough in dreams indicates failed erection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3911]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dreams of assumption to Heaven where the dreamer wants to return to earth but the thread is not long enough combine sexual need (failed erection) with death wishes.
- Inference: Defaecation dreams function as impotence dreams for men who can no longer satisfy a woman, or whose partner no longer satisfies them.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The connection between spun thread and evacuated faeces has no counterpart in other psychoanalytic symbolizations, where thread has other meanings.
