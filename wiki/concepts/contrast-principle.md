# Contrast Principle

## Summary

The contrast principle is a perceptual mechanism affecting how we perceive differences between sequentially presented items—we judge the second item as more different from the first than it actually is. This principle operates across sensory domains and is exploited by compliance professionals to make prices, products, or requests appear more or less favorable depending on what precedes them.

## Key facts

- The contrast principle states that if a second item is fairly different from the first, we will tend to see it as more different than it actually is. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p18]
- Placing one hand in cold water and one in hot water, then both in lukewarm water, produces dramatically different sensations—cold hand feels hot, hot hand feels cold. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p18]
- College students rated potential blind dates as less attractive after watching Charlie's Angels compared to those who watched a different show, due to comparison with media beauty standards. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p18]
- Retail clothiers instruct salespeople to sell expensive items first so subsequent cheaper items appear less costly by comparison—a $95 sweater seems reasonable after a $495 suit. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p19]
- Real estate agents use 'setup' properties—run-down houses at inflated prices—to make actual properties seem comparatively more attractive. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p20]
- Automobile dealers add options sequentially after negotiating the base car price, making each hundred-dollar add-on seem trivial against the fifteen-thousand-dollar total. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p20]

## Inferences

- Inference: The principle works virtually undetectable because the comparison context appears natural rather than deliberately arranged by the seller.
