# Culture of Curiosity

## Summary

A culture of curiosity is the organizational glue that binds team members together along curiosity journeys. It creates an environment that celebrates discovery and questions, where curiosity becomes a habit. Nurturing such a culture requires embracing three key contradictions: stretching beyond comfort zones while feeling comfortable, humility requiring boldness, and celebrating cohesion while incorporating diverse perspectives.

## Key facts

- Psychology professor Todd Kashdan found that while 84 percent of respondents said their employer encourages curiosity, 60 percent report barriers that discourage its expression. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p112]
- Excessive cohesiveness can lead to groupthink, where pressure to follow others overrides the ability to consider other opinions. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p112]
- Mary Katrantzou uses a democratic approach of questioning everything, which she says leads to meaningful conversations. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p113]
- Jon Wiley states that Google supports curiosity even when chasing answers is not fruitful, and emphasizes not punishing people for going down wrong paths. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p114]
- Ceylan Shevket describes surrounding the team with people from different specialities who all learn from each other in a collaborative environment. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p114-115]
- Colonel John Hunt consciously assembled a diverse team including a student, statistician, physicians, a journalist, and Sherpa guides for the 1953 Everest expedition. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p117-118]
