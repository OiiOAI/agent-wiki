# Rough-and-Tumble Play

## Summary

Rough-and-tumble play (RAT play) is a fundamental emotional system in the mammalian brain that generates joy and social interaction. Research in rats demonstrates that play is an intrinsic function of the nervous system, requiring specific somatosensory inputs from specialized 'play skin' zones. The PLAY system involves brain circuits in the thalamus, midbrain, and cortex, with opioids, acetylcholine, and glutamate promoting play while serotonin, norepinephrine, and GABA reduce it.

## Key facts

- Rough-and-tumble play in juvenile rats can be quantified by counting 'pins' (one animal on its back with another on top) and dorsal contacts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p151]
- Prior social isolation systematically increases roughhousing play in juvenile rats, while social satiation reduces it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p150]
- Play motivation is an endogenous urge of the brain; anesthetizing play skin zones reduces pinning but not dorsal contact solicitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p154]
- Tickling rats at the nape of the neck, where animals normally solicit play, induces 50 KHz chirping ('laughter') that animals seek out. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p153]
- Low doses of morphine increase play while opiate antagonists reduce it, demonstrating opioids specifically modulate play. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p155]
- When one animal receives morphine and the other naloxone, the morphine-treated animal always becomes the play winner. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p151]
- Muscarinic cholinergic blockade with scopolamine markedly reduces play, while nicotinic agonists reduce play. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p155]
- Psychostimulants such as amphetamines, which invigorate exploratory activities, markedly reduce play behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p156]
- Decortication reduces pinning to about half of control levels but does not eliminate play solicitation, showing cortex is not essential for play motivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p155]
- Damage to nonspecific thalamic nuclei including the parafascicular complex specifically reduces play while leaving food-seeking intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p154]
- Play appears to have an evolutionary protective function: the initial protest response (crying) may help parents find lost offspring, followed by a despair phase that conserves energy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Music that produces 'chills' may activate separation-distress circuits through audiovocal signals resembling primal cries of being lost. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p148]
- Rough-and-tumble play is identified as a fundamental brain process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p212]
- Males typically engage in more rough-and-tumble play than females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p212]
- Social deprivation reduces play behavior in rats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p212]

## Inferences

- Inference: Play and exploratory systems appear to be distinct and at times mutually exclusive in the brain
- Inference: Play may serve to exercise the behavioral potentials of emotional circuits in the relative safety of a positive affective state
- Inference: Laughter in humans may be homologous to the pinning position in rat play, both serving as signals for victory within playful social encounters

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: No specific 'play transmitter' has been identified; combinations of pharmacological agents have failed to create a 'ludic cocktail'
- Uncertain: The extent to which human enjoyment of sports emerges from primal PLAY circuits remains unresolved
