# Organizational Learning Dominance

## Summary

Organizational Learning Dominance is a concept identified in Boyd's work as the major theme across all levels of organization and war—the capability to evolve, adapt, and learn better and faster than opponents.

## Key facts

- A major theme throughout Boyd's work for all levels of organization and war is the capability to evolve, adapt, to learn, or organizational learning dominance. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]
- R. Evan Ellis labeled this concept 'Organizational Learning Dominance, The Emerging Key to Success in the New Era of Warfare' in Comparative Strategy, 18:191-202, 1999. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]
- Boyd argues that verifying existing beliefs and expectations and modifying them in a timely manner is crucial for survival. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]
- The way to play the game of interaction and isolation is to spontaneously generate new mental images that match an unfolding world of uncertainty and change. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p287]

## Inferences

- Inference: Organizational Learning Dominance provides a framework for understanding why Boyd emphasized learning, adaptation, and the continuous updating of schemata as essential for military effectiveness.
