# Social Comparison Bias

## Summary

Social comparison bias is the tendency to withhold assistance from people who might outdo you, even at your own expense. Stephen Garcia described a Nobel laureate who prevented a promising young colleague from applying for a job. Guy Kawasaki notes that B-players hire C-players to feel superior, leading to 'bozo explosion.'

## Key facts

- Stephen Garcia described a Nobel laureate who prevented a promising young colleague from applying for a job at 'his' university. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p161]
- Guy Kawasaki (Apple) says: 'A-players hire people even better than themselves. B-players hire C-players so they can feel superior to them, and C-players hire D-players.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p162]
- Isaac Barrow gave up his professorship when shown the research of 25-year-old Isaac Newton during the plague of 1666-67. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p162]
