# Natural Direct Effect (NDE)

## Summary

The Natural Direct Effect (NDE) measures the effect of an exposure on an outcome that goes through all pathways except the specific mediator of interest. It compares outcomes when X is set to one value versus another, while holding the mediator at the value it would have taken under the baseline exposure level for each individual. Unlike the controlled direct effect, NDE allows the mediator to vary naturally across individuals.

## Key facts

- The NDE compares outcomes when X is changed while letting M take its 'natural' value for each individual. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2830-L2845]
- NDE requires counterfactual definitions because the mediator's natural value depends on the individual and the treatment status. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2835-L2845]
- The formula for NDE involves comparing outcomes across two different treatment conditions while keeping the mediator at its counterfactual value. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2840-L2848]
