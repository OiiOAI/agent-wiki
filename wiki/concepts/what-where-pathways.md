# What and Where Pathways

## Summary

The brain has two parallel visual processing pathways: the 'what' pathway for object identification and the 'where' pathway for spatial location and movement. Similar dual pathways exist for touch and hearing. Hawkins proposes that these pathways differ not in their neural circuitry but in what their reference frames are anchored to: what columns attach reference frames to objects, while where columns attach reference frames to the body.

## Key facts

- The brain has two vision systems: the what visual pathway for identifying objects and the where visual pathway for spatial location. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p78]
- If the where pathway is disabled, a person can identify objects but cannot reach for them; if the what pathway is disabled, they can reach but cannot identify objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p78]
- What and where columns look similar with similar cell types, layers, and circuits, yet they perform different functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p78]
- Hawkins proposes that what columns attach reference frames to external objects while where columns attach reference frames to the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p79]
- The difference between what and where regions may be as simple as whether inputs come from sensory sources (creating object-centered reference frames) or from body position sensors (creating body-centered reference frames). [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p79]

## Inferences

- Inference: what and where pathways differ only in reference frame anchoring, this supports Mountcastle's hypothesis that all cortical columns use the same universal algorithm.
