# Symbols of Authority

## Summary

Research demonstrates that symbols of authority—titles, clothing, and trappings—can trigger automatic compliance even in the absence of genuine authority. Studies show that uniforms, business attire, and titles significantly increase compliance rates, and people consistently underestimate this influence.

## Key facts

- A man introduced as a Cambridge "professor" was perceived as 2.5 inches taller than when introduced as a "student" in an Australian study. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p199]
- In a hospital study, 95 percent of nurses followed a phone order from someone claiming to be a doctor, even though the order violated hospital policy, prescribed an unauthorized drug, and prescribed double the maximum safe dosage. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p202]
- A man in a security guard uniform received 92 percent compliance versus 42 percent when in street clothes for identical requests. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p203]
- 3.5 times as many pedestrians followed a suited man jaywalking versus one in work clothes. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p204]
- Drivers waited significantly longer before honking at a luxury car stopped at a green light than at an economy car, with 50 percent never honking at the luxury car. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: The consistent underestimation of authority symbol effects may account for much of their success as compliance devices—they work forcefully but unexpectedly.
