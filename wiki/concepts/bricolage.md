# Bricolage

## Summary

Bricolage is a form of trial and error close to tweaking—trying to make do with what you've got by recycling pieces that would otherwise be wasted. François Jacob introduced this concept into science to explain how natural systems exploit optionality through experimentation with small errors and large potential gains.

## Key facts

- François Jacob introduced into science the notion of options or option-like characteristics in natural systems through trial and error under the variant called bricolage. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p233]
- Nature exploits optionality: about half of all embryos undergo spontaneous abortion—easier than designing the perfect baby by blueprint. Nature has an option and uses it. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p233]
- Trial and error presents small errors and large gains, which Taleb calls 'convex tinkering'—low-cost mistakes with known maximum losses and large potential payoff. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bricolage explains how evolution produces sophisticated biological systems without intelligence, through the combination of optionality, selection filters, and randomness—a process far more powerful than directed design.
