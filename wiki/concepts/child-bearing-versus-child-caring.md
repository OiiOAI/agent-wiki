# Child-bearing vs. Child-caring Distinction

## Summary

Dawkins's distinction between child-bearing (bringing new individuals into the world) and child-caring (investing resources in existing individuals) represents what he considers an important but often misunderstood distinction in evolutionary theory. While these activities often co-occur in practice (particularly in mammals and birds), they are genetically and strategically distinct decisions that may require different optimal strategies and may compete with each other for an individual's limited resources.

## Key facts

- Child-bearing refers to decisions about whether to take steps to bring new individuals into the world (reproduction), while child-caring refers to decisions about whether to invest resources in existing individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p142]
- An individual survival machine must make two quite different sorts of decisions: caring decisions ('There is a child; its degree of relatedness to me is so and so; shall I feed it?') and bearing decisions ('Shall I reproduce?'). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p142]
- Caring and bearing are bound to compete with each other for an individual's time and other resources—a parent may have to choose 'Shall I care for this child or shall I bear a new one?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p142]
- A pure caring strategy cannot be evolutionarily stable—if all individuals devoted themselves to caring for existing children without bringing new ones into the world, mutant individuals who specialize in bearing would invade the population. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p142]
- From the perspective of selfish genes, there is no genetic distinction in principle between caring for a baby brother and caring for a baby son—both infants are equally closely related to you. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p143]

## Inferences

- Inference: The child-bearing vs. child-caring distinction explains why parental care and reproduction have historically been muddled—because bearing and caring so often go together in mammals and birds, people have mistakenly grouped them together as 'reproduction' rather than recognizing them as separate strategic domains.
