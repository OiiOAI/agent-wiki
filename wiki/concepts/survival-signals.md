# Survival Signals

## Summary

Survival signals are specific behavioral strategies used by predators to establish control over victims. The book identifies seven key signals: forced teaming, charm and niceness, too many details, typecasting, loan sharking, unsolicited promises, and discounting the word 'no.'

## Key facts

- Forced teaming involves using 'we' language to create a false sense of shared purpose or predicament, making cooperation difficult to refuse without appearing rude. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p42]
- The author states that charm is almost always a directed instrument with motive—'to charm is to compel, to control by allure or attraction.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p43]
- Too many details is a deception technique where liars continue adding details because the truth doesn't sound credible to them. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p44]
- Typecasting involves a slight insult designed to make the target prove the characterization wrong, thereby engaging with the manipulator. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p45]
- Loan sharking describes offering unwanted help to create a sense of debt, making it harder to ask the person to leave. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p45]
- The unsolicited promise is identified as a reliable danger signal because it reveals the speaker perceives the listener's doubt. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p46]
- Discounting the word 'no' indicates a person seeking control or refusing to relinquish it, and the author advises that 'no' is a complete sentence requiring no negotiation. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p47]

## Inferences

- Inference: These seven survival signals appear to function as a system of victim selection that predators use to test and evaluate potential targets before escalating to violence.
