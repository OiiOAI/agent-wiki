# Ascending Arousal System

## Summary

The ascending arousal system is a neural network in the brainstem and hypothalamus that maintains wakefulness. It comprises multiple neuronal populations including glutamatergic neurons in the dorsolateral pons (parabrachial and pedunculopontine nuclei), cholinergic neurons in the basal forebrain, and GABAergic neurons in the lateral hypothalamus. These neurons project to the thalamus, hypothalamus, basal forebrain, and cerebral cortex, releasing neurotransmitters including glutamate, acetylcholine, GABA, dopamine, norepinephrine, serotonin, histamine, and orexin to promote cortical arousal and wakefulness.

## Key facts

- The crucial components of the ascending arousal system are glutamatergic neurons in the dorsolateral pons, supramammillary hypothalamus, and basal forebrain; cholinergic neurons in the dorsolateral pons and basal forebrain; and GABAergic neurons in the lateral hypothalamus and basal forebrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- Lesions of the parabrachial nucleus and pedunculopontine tegmental nucleus cause coma, as do large bilateral lesions of the basal forebrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- The ascending arousal system is inhibited during sleep by GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus and parafacial zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- Von Economo identified the upper brainstem and posterior hypothalamus as critical regions for wakefulness control based on encephalitis lethargica cases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1131]
- In 1936, Bremer found that transection of the cat's brain stem at the midbrain level produced a continuous sleep-like state, whereas transections separating the medulla from the spinal cord did not. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1090]
- Damage to the ascending arousal system or its projections in the thalamus and hypothalamus leads to coma. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1091]
- The ascending arousal system includes neurons in the locus ceruleus (noradrenergic), dorsal and median raphe nuclei (serotonergic), pedunculopontine and laterodorsal tegmental nuclei (cholinergic), and tuberomammillary nucleus (histaminergic). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1091]
- At the junction of the midbrain and diencephalon, projections from the ascending arousal system split into two major branches: a dorsal branch terminating in the thalamus and a ventral branch traveling through the lateral hypothalamic area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1092]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mutual inhibition between sleep-promoting ventrolateral preoptic neurons and arousal system neurons forms a flip-flop switch similar to an electrical circuit, producing rapid transitions between sleep and wake states.
- Inference: Modulatory pathways containing orexin and monoamines are needed to maintain full and sustained wakefulness, particularly under adverse conditions.
