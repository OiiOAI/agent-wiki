# Intense Ideology Warning

## Summary

Munger warns against extremely intense ideology, which 'cabbages up' one's mind. He notes this occurs with religious and political ideology, particularly when young people drift into it and never escape. The 'iron prescription' for avoiding overintensity is not feeling entitled to have an opinion unless one can state the arguments against one's position better than opponents.

## Key facts

- Munger warns: 'You want to be very careful with intense ideology. It presents a big danger for the only mind you're ever going to get.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p439]
- Munger describes intense ideology in the worst TV preachers: 'They have different, intense, inconsistent ideas about technical theology, and a lot of them have minds reduced to cabbage.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p438]
- Munger's 'iron prescription': 'I feel that I'm not entitled to have an opinion unless I can state the arguments against my position better than the people who are in opposition.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p439]
- Munger recalls Dean Acheson's attribution to William the Silent of Orange: 'It's not necessary to hope in order to persevere,' noting this is probably too tough for most people. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p439]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger views intense ideology as a form of self-inflicted cognitive damage, noting that announcing loyalty to a cult-like group and shouting orthodox ideology literally 'pounds in' destructive mental patterns.
