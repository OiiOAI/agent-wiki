# Frisson Prosthesis

## Summary

The Frisson prosthesis is a device developed by the MIT Media Lab's Fluid Interfaces Group that artificially induces aesthetic chills—those distinctive shivery feelings paired with piloerection or goose bumps that occur during moments of high emotion. The device delivers a wave of coldness traveling down the wearer's back to mimic the unfolding of an affective chill.

## Key facts

- The Frisson prosthesis includes three thermal way stations or Peltier elements, a control board, and a vibrotactile unit. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p111]
- Artificially inducing the stream of sensation should nudge the brain into thinking there has been a sudden resolution of some important and emotionally salient uncertainty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p111]
- Aesthetic chills occur when the brain identifies something as critical new information that resolves important uncertainties, making it a physiological echo of the aha moment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p110]
- Preliminary results showed effects on reported emotional intensity when using the Frisson prosthesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p112]

## Inferences

- Inference: The reverse engineering principle underlying the Frisson prosthesis is sound and resembles the facial feedback hypothesis—if you manipulate bodily states, this acts as evidence that nudges the predictive brain [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p112]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The long-term effects of regular Frisson prosthesis use on emotional regulation have not been studied
