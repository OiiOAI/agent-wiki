# Proper mass

## Summary

The concept of 'proper mass' is a classic theory concerning the relation of brain structure to function, which assumes that larger brain structures are more powerful analyzers or more spacious storage devices than smaller ones. This 'bigger-is-more-powerful' perspective has been applied at both regional and whole brain levels of analysis.

## Key facts

- Proper mass is usually invoked along with the assumption that larger brain structures are more powerful analyzers or more spacious storage devices than smaller ones. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p238]
- The regional interpretation of brain size differences was the basis for phrenology, proposed by Franz Josef Gall at the turn of the nineteenth century. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p238]

## Inferences

- Inference: The proper mass concept requires modification when considering developmental processes, because sizes of individual brain structures are not determined in isolation but through peripheral-central matching and competitive effects.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact degree to which proper mass explains functional differences remains uncertain for humans specifically.
