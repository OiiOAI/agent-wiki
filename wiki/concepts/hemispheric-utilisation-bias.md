# Hemispheric Utilisation Bias

## Summary

A phenomenon where one or other hemisphere may predominate, its particular cognitive and perceptual style more greatly influencing our experience of the world, even over very long periods. Individuals can have characteristic and consistent biases towards one hemisphere, associated with differing degrees of arousal and activation.

## Key facts

- Hemispheric utilisation bias is a phenomenon where one or other hemisphere predominates, with its particular cognitive and perceptual style influencing experience over extended periods, not just milliseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p307]
- Subjects with characteristic left-hemisphere bias showed enhanced tendency for the left hemisphere to prioritise the right visual field, causing irrelevant information to interfere with left visual field tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p307]
- Subjects with characteristic right-hemisphere bias showed no such competitive effects when conditions were reversed, suggesting a more even distribution of concern in the right hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p307]

## Inferences

- Inference: Having a utilisation bias in favour of the left hemisphere intensifies the left's unequal concern, whereas a bias in favour of the right does not upset its even-handedness.
