# Excitotoxicity

## Summary

Excitotoxicity is the process by which excessive stimulation of excitatory amino acid receptors (particularly glutamate receptors) leads to neuronal damage and death. It is implicated in seizure-related brain damage and involves excessive intracellular calcium accumulation, activation of calcium-dependent enzymes, free radical production, and eventual cell death through both necrotic and apoptotic mechanisms.

## Key facts

- Excitotoxicity results from excessive stimulation of glutamate receptors causing excessive intracellular Ca2+ accumulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Excessive intracellular Ca2+ activates calcium-dependent enzymes including phosphatases, proteases, and lipases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Lipid peroxidation produces free radicals that damage cellular proteins and lead to cell death. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The immature brain is particularly vulnerable to excitotoxicity due to greater electrotonic coupling, less effective potassium buffering by immature glia, and decreased glucose transport across the blood-brain barrier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Excitotoxicity preferentially affects interneurons expressing calcium-permeable AMPA receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
