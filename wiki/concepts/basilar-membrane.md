# Basilar Membrane

## Summary

The basilar membrane is an elastic structure in the cochlea that separates the scala media from the scala tympani and performs mechanical frequency analysis. Its mechanical properties vary continuously along its length, being narrow and stiff at the base (responsive to high frequencies) and wide and flexible at the apex (responsive to low frequencies). Sound produces traveling waves on the membrane, with each frequency peaking at a characteristic position.

## Key facts

- The basilar membrane is more than five times as broad at the cochlear apex as at the base, and is thinner and floppier at the apex but thicker and tauter at the base. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p710]
- Sound stimulation produces traveling waves on the basilar membrane that progress from the cochlear base toward the apex, peaking at a position determined by the stimulus frequency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p710]
- Low-frequency sounds produce basilar membrane motion near the apex, mid-frequency sounds excite the middle region, and high frequencies up to 20 kHz excite the base. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p711]
- The arrangement of vibration frequencies along the basilar membrane constitutes a tonotopic map where the logarithm of best frequency is roughly proportional to distance from the cochlear apex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p711]
- The basilar membrane acts as a mechanical frequency analyzer by distributing stimulus energy to hair cells arrayed along its length. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p711]
- The concept of tonotopic frequency analysis on the basilar membrane was first articulated by Hermann von Helmholtz in the 19th century. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p711]

## Inferences

- Inference: The frequency selectivity observed in vivo is far sharper than predicted by passive basilar membrane mechanics alone, indicating that active processes in outer hair cells are essential for the ear's true frequency resolution.
