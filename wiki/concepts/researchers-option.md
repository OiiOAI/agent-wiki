# Researcher's Option

## Summary

The researcher's option describes how academic researchers possess free optionality analogous to bankers—they capture upside (publications, prestige, grants) while truth bears the downside (failed predictions, retracted findings). This manifests through cherry-picking statistical relationships that confirm beliefs while discarding disconfirming results. In large datasets, false correlations grow convexly faster than true signals, making Big Data particularly dangerous. John Ioannides calculated that over 80% of observational study results are spurious, yet these populate academic journals. The tragedy is compounded because funding for replication studies is nearly impossible to obtain—trying to reject existing studies makes no one a hero.

## Key facts

- Researchers get the upside while truth gets the downside; the researcher can cherry-pick statistics to confirm beliefs and stop once the desired result is achieved. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p515]
- In large datasets, large deviations are vastly more attributable to noise than to information, and false correlations grow much faster than true relationships. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p515]
- John Ioannides calculated that more than 80% of observational study results are spurious, yet these populate scientific journals. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p516]
- Taleb and Stan Young found a genetics study in The New England Journal of Medicine claiming significance from data that were no better than random; their complaint to the journal had no effect. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p516]
- With 200 completely unrelated random variables, finding a 30% correlation is nearly inevitable—but entirely spurious. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p516]
- Funding for replication studies is nearly impossible to obtain; trying to reject existing studies makes no one a hero and cannot advance a researcher's career. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p517]
- Modernity provides too many variables but too little data per variable, and spurious relationships grow much faster than real information because noise is convex while signal is concave. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p517]

## Inferences

- Inference: The researcher's option explains the replication crisis in psychology and social sciences—researchers have structural incentives to find publishable results rather than truth.
- Inference: Big Data accelerates rather than solves the knowledge problem because increasing variables exponentially increases false correlations faster than true signals.
