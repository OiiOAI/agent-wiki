# Stock

## Summary

A stock is the foundation of any system—a store, quantity, or accumulation of material or information that has built up over time. Stocks are the elements of a system that can be seen, felt, counted, or measured at any given time. Stocks change over time through flows (inflows and outflows). Stocks generally change slowly even when flows change suddenly, acting as delays, buffers, or shock absorbers.

## Key facts

- A stock is the memory of the history of changing flows within the system. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p33]
- Stocks change over time through the actions of flows, which are filling and draining, births and deaths, purchases and sales, growth and decay. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p34]
- As long as the sum of all inflows exceeds the sum of all outflows, the level of the stock will rise. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p38]
- Stocks allow inflows and outflows to be decoupled and to be independent and temporarily out of balance with each other. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p41]
- Stocks generally change slowly, even when the flows into or out of them change suddenly; they act as delays or buffers in systems. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p39]

## Inferences

- Inference: The tendency of stocks to change slowly explains why systems often appear stable even when conditions that will eventually cause change are already in place.
