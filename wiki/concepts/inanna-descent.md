# The Inanna Descent

## Summary

The Sumerian myth of Inanna's descent to the nether world represents one of the oldest recorded accounts of the passage through the gates of metamorphosis. Inanna must pass through seven gates, at each being stripped of a garment or ornament, until she stands naked before the throne of her sister Ereshkigal. There she is turned into a corpse and hung from a stake. This myth illustrates the hero's necessary surrender of all defenses and attachments to achieve transformation.

## Key facts

- Inanna descended to the nether world governed by her sister Ereshkigal, abandoning heaven, earth, lordship, and ladyship. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p85]
- At each of seven gates, the gatekeeper removed part of Inanna's clothing and regalia, until she stood naked before the throne. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p86-88]
- At the command of the judges of the nether world, the Anunnaki, Inanna was turned into a corpse hung from a stake. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p88]
- Inanna and Ereshkigal represent the one goddess in two aspects—light and dark—and their confrontation epitomizes the whole sense of the difficult road of trials. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p89]
- The hero must put aside his pride, virtue, beauty, and life, bow or submit to the absolutely intolerable, then discovers he and his opposite are one flesh. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p89]
