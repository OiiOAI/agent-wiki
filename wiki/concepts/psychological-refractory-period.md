# Psychological Refractory Period

## Summary

The Psychological Refractory Period (PRP) refers to the systematic delay in executing the second task when two tasks are presented simultaneously or sequentially at short intervals. PRP experiments reveal a central bottleneck in information processing where only the central component of each task cannot be carried out simultaneously, while perceptual and motor components can proceed in parallel.

## Key facts

- When two tasks are presented simultaneously or at short intervals, a systematic delay in executing the second task is observed, known as the Psychological Refractory Period. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p35]
- PRP experiments model decision making as a noisy integrator that accumulates evidence until it exceeds a threshold, with all variance in response time attributed to this integration process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p34]
- Perceptual and motor stages are reliable in time and can be carried out in parallel with stages of another task, while the central stage establishes a serial bottleneck. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p40]
- Notation manipulations affect a pre-bottleneck parallel stage, distance manipulations affect the serial central stage, and response complexity manipulations affect a post-bottleneck parallel stage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p39]
- fMRI studies show delayed activity in prefrontal cortex during PRP paradigms, suggesting a frontal network is responsible for the central bottleneck. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p44]

## Inferences

- Inference: The PRP paradigm provides a powerful experimental methodology for understanding the internal structure of cognitive tasks by examining task interference.
- Inference: The decomposition of tasks into perceptual, central, and motor stages based on PRP predictions converges with decompositions based on response-time variability analysis.
