# Depression and Cultural Factors

## Summary

Depression rates vary dramatically between cultures (up to 12-fold) and are linked to cultural stability and social interconnectedness. Urbanisation, globalisation and destruction of local cultures have led to rising mental illness in the developing world. Depression is being experienced more often, at younger ages, and with more severe episodes in younger birth cohorts.

## Key facts

- Depression rates differ by as much as 12-fold between cultures, apparently linked to the degree of stability and interconnectedness within a culture. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p596]
- Urbanisation, globalisation and destruction of local cultures has led to a rise in the prevalence of mental illness in the developing world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p597]
- Depression is being experienced more often, at younger ages, with more severe and more frequent episodes in younger birth cohorts, and in the USA has doubled since the Second World War. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p597]

## Inferences

- Inference: The left hemisphere's assault on embodied culture and traditional communities has contributed to rising rates of depression by undermining social integration.
