# Chemical Synapse

## Summary

Chemical synapses are specialized junctions where neurotransmitters released from presynaptic terminals diffuse across a synaptic cleft to activate receptors on the postsynaptic cell, enabling signal transmission with amplification and synaptic delay.

## Key facts

- At chemical synapses, the presynaptic and postsynaptic neurons are separated by a synaptic cleft of 20-40 nm, with no cytoplasmic continuity between cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p248]
- Chemical synaptic transmission involves the release of neurotransmitter from synaptic vesicles at active zones, triggering exocytosis upon presynaptic action potential arrival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p248]
- Chemical transmission produces a synaptic delay of at least 0.3 ms, usually 1-5 ms or longer, compared to the virtually absent delay at electrical synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p242]
- A key advantage of chemical transmission is amplification: one synaptic vesicle releasing thousands of transmitter molecules can open thousands of ion channels in the target cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p249]
- Neurotransmitters act on two major classes of receptors: ionotropic receptors (ligand-gated ion channels) that produce fast synaptic actions, and metabotropic receptors that activate second-messenger cascades producing slower modulatory effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p251]
- Chemical synapses have a synaptic cleft of 20-40 nm, wider than the normal 20 nm nonsynaptic intercellular space, with no cytoplasmic continuity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p230]
- Chemical synaptic transmission depends on diffusion of neurotransmitter across the synaptic cleft to bind receptors on the postsynaptic membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p235]
- The synaptic delay at chemical synapses is significant, at least 0.3 ms but usually 1-5 ms or longer, due to multiple biochemical steps. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p230]
- Chemical synapses can amplify signals so that a small presynaptic nerve terminal can alter the response of large postsynaptic cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p228]
- Chemical synaptic transmission is unidirectional, from presynaptic to postsynaptic cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p230]
- Neurotransmitters are released from synaptic vesicles at active zones, specialized regions of the presynaptic membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p235]

## Inferences

- Inference: The focused release of neurotransmitter at active zones in conventional chemical synapses differs from more diffuse release patterns in autonomic neurons innervating smooth muscle, suggesting functional specialization based on synaptic architecture.
- Inference: The biochemical complexity of chemical synapses allows for modulatory mechanisms that electrical synapses lack.
- Inference: Signal amplification at chemical synapses enables one presynaptic neuron to affect large postsynaptic populations.
