# Dream-Work

## Summary

The dream-work (Traumarbeit) comprises the psychological transformations applied to dream thoughts to produce the manifest dream content. It includes: (1) condensation (Verdichtung), the process by which psychical intensity is transferred en bloc from one idea to another; (2) considerations of representability, transforming abstract thoughts into sensory images suitable for visual representation; (3) dramaticization, converting thoughts into perceptual scenes; and (4) secondary revision, where the preconscious processes the dream content to make it coherent and intelligible.

## Key facts

- The dream-work transforms thoughts into sensory images through condensation, regard for representability in sensory images, and (though not invariably) demand for rational, intelligible exterior structure. - Condensation involves intensities becoming capable of discharge en bloc and passing from one idea to another, forming ideas with great intensity; this is responsible for the bewildering impression of dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1831]
- The dream-work treats ideas through condensation, compromise formation, and superficial associations based on homonyms and verbal similarities—processes of the primary psychical process. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1831]
- Secondary revision is the preconscious processing of dream content to make it consecutive and intelligible, treating the dream like any other perceptual content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1812]
- The considerations of representability are brought into connection with the selective attraction exercised by visually recollected scenes touched upon by dream thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1787]
- The dream-work transforms the latent dream thoughts into the manifest dream content, involving condensation, displacement, dramatization, and secondary revision. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1881]
- Condensation brings together multiple dream thoughts or combines elements having something in common into composite structures. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1883]
- Dream displacement transfers psychical intensity, significance, or affective potentiality from thoughts to which it properly belongs onto others that have no claim to such emphasis. - The dream-work is not creative—it develops no phantasies of its own, makes no judgements, and draws no conclusions beyond condensation and displacement. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1897]

- The dream-work includes regression from thoughts to perceptual images, condensation, displacement, and secondary revision. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2796]
- Displacement in dreams occurs when cathectic energy passes uninhibited from important ideas to unimportant ones, giving dreams their alien, incomprehensible character. ## Inferences

- Inference: The dream-work cannot occur on preconscious material alone; it requires the involvement of unconscious wishes that provide the driving force for these transformations.
- Inference: The dream-work operates by the same mechanisms found in hysterical symptom formation, condensation and displacement being invariable characteristics of both.
- Inference: Freud proposes that unconscious thinking operates by the same rules as dream-work, characterized by condensation without regard to logical relations and displacement unconstrained by conscious judgments of importance.
- Inference: The censorship during sleep operates analogously to critical reason in waking life—both inhibit direct expression of unconscious material.
- Inference: The dream-work achieves condensation by seeking ambiguous words that allow multiple latent thoughts to converge, similar to joke technique.
- Inference: The archaic features of the dream-work (antithetical meaning, reversals) parallel features of ancient languages, suggesting dream-work recreates primitive modes of expression.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that the attempt to determine the exact stages at which dream-work transformations occur 'has not yet been seriously undertaken.'
