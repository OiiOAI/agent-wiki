# Profit Squeeze

## Summary

The Profit Squeeze describes how competitive pressure gradually reduces profit margins over time. When businesses achieve high profitability (typically above 20 percent), competitors copy their methods and drop prices to attract customers, forcing everyone to reduce prices to stay competitive. This creates a constant need for innovation to maintain profit margins.

## Key facts

- When businesses become more profitable, competitive pressure sets in and prices drop to attract more customers, squeezing profits. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p153]
- When profit margins are big (in excess of 20 percent), competitors sniff out what you're doing and start to duplicate it and look for ways to do it better, faster, and cheaper. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p153]
- Even if you figure out a big leap in profitability, it's only a matter of time before competitors do the same thing. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p153]
- The only way to keep big margins is to milk them for all they're worth when you have them and keep innovating to find new ways to bump up profitability. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: Constant innovation is required not just to grow profits but to maintain current profit levels against competitive pressure.
