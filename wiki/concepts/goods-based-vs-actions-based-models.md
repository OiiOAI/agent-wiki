# Goods-based vs. actions-based models of economic choice

## Summary

Two competing theoretical frameworks for understanding economic choice: goods-based models propose that choices are made in an abstract space of goods before action selection, while actions-based models propose that choices are embedded within sensory-motor processes of action selection.

## Key facts

- Goods-based models assume choices are made in an abstract space of goods, with action selection following as a separate process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p457]
- Actions-based models propose that values are attached to each possible course of action and economic choice unfolds as action selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p457]
- Goods-based models appear in principle more efficient because they are modular, separating the computationally challenging process of planning movements from the evaluation of options. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p458]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that OFC neurons encode value independently of sensory-motor contingencies supports the goods-based model, since this abstract representation would be useful for efficient modular decision making.
