# The Ego and the Id

## Summary

The structural model of the psyche distinguishes three agencies: the id (the reservoir of drives and the original psychical constitution), the ego (the organized, reality-testing portion that mediates between id, superego, and external world), and the superego (the critical, prohibiting agency). The ego is viewed as having a special capacity for self-observation and can split during certain functions.

## Key facts

- The id is the storehouse of drive excitations and the original psychical constitution. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6850]
- The ego can take itself as an object, can treat itself like other objects, can observe itself, criticize itself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6894]
- The ego can be split; it splits itself during a number of its functions temporarily and its parts can come together again. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6894]
- The superego is distinguished from the ego as an observing, judging, and threatening agency. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6894]
- Pathology reveals normal structures that would otherwise escape notice; mental patients are 'split and broken structures' that illuminate the normal lines of cleavage in psychical life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6894]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud draws an analogy between the ego's capacity to split and crystallography: just as crystals break along predetermined lines of cleavage, the ego can be split by pathological processes into parts that normally remain united.
