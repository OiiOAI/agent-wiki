# Habit Values

## Summary

Habit values represent generalized propensities to emit particular responses acquired from a history of reinforcement but divorced from any representation of the specific reinforcing goal. Habits fail both the contingency degradation test and the outcome devaluation test. Neural evidence implicates dorsolateral striatum in habit learning, with lesions of this region preventing the emergence of habitual control.

## Key facts

- Habitual actions fail both the contingency degradation test and the outcome devaluation test, indicating they are not controlled by knowledge of action-outcome contingencies or the current value of the goal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p372]
- Overtrained lever-presses in thirsty rats reinforced by saccharin persisted even after saccharin was devalued by pairing with illness, demonstrating insensitivity to outcome value changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p373]
- Lesions of dorsolateral striatum in rats abolish the onset of habitual behavior, rendering lesioned animals permanently goal-directed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p381]

## Inferences

- Inference: Habits are classically envisioned to arise from stimulus-response associations that lack any representation of the rewarding outcome and are instead valued by being 'stamped in' by a history of reinforcement
