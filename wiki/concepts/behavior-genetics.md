# Behavior Genetics

## Summary

Behavior genetics is the academic discipline that evaluates how genetic factors contribute to behavioral tendencies. Cross-fostered identical-twin studies suggest approximately 50% of basic human behavioral tendencies (as reflected in personality factors) can be attributed to genetic factors while about 50% can be attributed to learning. Animal husbandry demonstrates that behavioral tendencies are as capable of being genetically transmitted as external bodily features.

## Key facts

- Recent estimates of heritability for many human behaviors from cross-fostered identical-twin studies suggest approximately 50% of basic human behavioral tendencies can be attributed to genetic factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p28]
- Study after study of inbreeding has indicated that virtually any behavioral tendency in animals can be enhanced or diminished as successfully as bodily characteristics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p28]
- Different strains of mice differ markedly in their tendencies to exhibit aggressive and fearful behaviors, demonstrating genetic influence on emotional traits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p28]
- Knockout preparations (animals with single-gene deletions) have shown that elimination of DNA for a single brain enzyme can have devastating effects on cognitive/memory abilities, while elimination of another enzyme produces violent and hypersexual animals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p28]
- Huntington's disease arises from excessive CAG trinucleotide repeats on chromosome 4, with repeats of more than 42 (vs normal 11-24) causing neurodegeneration in the basal ganglia accompanied by emotional lability and cognitive disintegration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p29]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that emotional characteristics can be selectively strengthened or weakened by breeding, as well as by cross-rearing, indicates that both nature and nurture contribute to emotional phenotypes.
- Inference: The emerging genetic analyses suggest that a menagerie of genetically altered animals will provide information about biological underpinnings of psychobehavioral traits, though methodological problems remain to be overcome.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Investigators have typically not been able to identify the precise genes and gene products that have been selected when temperamental traits are inherited.
- Uncertain: The extent to which emotional characteristics can be modified by experience versus being genetically fixed is still being determined.
