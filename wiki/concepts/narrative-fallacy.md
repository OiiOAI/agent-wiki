# Narrative Fallacy

## Summary

A cognitive bias that leads people to perceive past events as more predictable than they actually were, by constructing coherent and compelling narratives that explain outcomes while overlooking the role of chance and alternative scenarios. The narrative fallacy creates the illusion that we understand the past and can therefore predict the future.

## Key facts

- The narrative fallacy arises inevitably from our continuous effort to make sense of the world by constructing coherent stories about the past that overlook the role of luck and alternative possible outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p257]
- Nassim Taleb introduced the concept of the narrative fallacy to describe how flawed narratives of the past shape our view of the world and our expectations for the future. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p257]
- The narrative fallacy favors explanations that are simple, concrete rather than abstract, and give a large role to talent, stupidity, and intention while downplaying the role of luck. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p257]
- The narrative fallacy contributes to overconfidence in predictions because coherent stories of the past create the illusion that the future is also predictable and controllable. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The narrative fallacy explains why business success books are so popular despite having limited predictive value: coherent stories of success and failure appeal to our need for causal explanations while underweighting the role of luck and regression to the mean.
