# Evo-devo

## Summary

Evo-devo (evolutionary developmental biology) is the 'new synthesis' between developmental and evolutionary biology that emerged in the 1970s-1990s. Its core idea is that all important changes in evolution are alterations in development. Research in evo-devo spans cell biology, comparative embryology, paleontology, and molecular genetics to explain how developmental processes become modified during evolution, producing changes in animal morphologies and body plans.

## Key facts

- Evo-devo proposes that all important changes in evolution are alterations in development, contrasting with the Modern Synthesis which substituted genetics for embryology as the motor for evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p191]
- A central finding is that evolution is driven by robust and flexible developmental processes: robustness is the capacity not to change when conditions change, while flexibility is the capacity to change in relation to changing conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p191]
- Structures previously thought to be analogous (like insect and vertebrate eyes) have been found to be related at deeper levels of genetic and developmental conservation, revealing homologies of process rather than just structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p191]
- Hox genes provide a striking example of robustness and flexibility: they have been conserved in evolution while also undergoing duplication and diversification, leading to wide variety of bodily compartments and appendages. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p195]
