# The OK Plateau

## Summary

The OK Plateau is a concept identified by Joshua Foer describing when people stop improving once they reach what they deem to be acceptable performance. Using typing as an example, Foer notes that despite years of practice typing millions of words, most people do not progress beyond a certain speed because they have switched off learning and gone on automatic pilot. The book identifies this as a common ceiling of achievement that blocks extraordinary results when it applies to one's ONE Thing.

## Key facts

- Joshua Foer identified the OK Plateau where people stop improving once they reach acceptable performance. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p182]
- The example given is typing—if practice alone mattered, people would progress to 100 words per minute over their careers. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p182]
- People switch off learning when they hit an acceptable level, going on automatic pilot. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p182]
- Accepting the OK Plateau is not acceptable when it applies to one's ONE Thing. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p182]
