# Validation

## Summary

Validation in DBT is the process of communicating to another person that their thoughts, feelings, and behaviors make sense and are understandable within their current situation or life context. Validation is essential for building relationships of trust and intimacy. There are six levels of validation, from listening without reaction to connecting the person's experience to past experiences or biological/psychological vulnerabilities.

## Key facts

- Validation of others' feelings, beliefs, experiences, and actions is essential in building any relationship of trust and intimacy. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p155]
- The six levels of validation range from simply being present and listening, through reflecting back what was said, to identifying the person's experiences as valid based on their history or situation. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p156]
- When recovering from invalidation, DBT teaches self-validation skills such as acknowledging that your emotions make sense given your history, and using Wise Mind to see multiple perspectives. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p158]
- Validation means finding the kernel of truth in another person's perspective or situation and verifying the facts. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p179]
- Validation means acknowledging that a person's emotions, thoughts, and behaviors have causes and are therefore understandable. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p179]
- Validation does not necessarily mean agreeing with the other person and does not mean you like it. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p179]
- Validation improves relationships by showing we are listening and understand, and reduces pressure to prove who is right, negative reactivity, and anger. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p179]
- The six levels of validation include: Pay Attention, Reflect Back, 'Read Minds', Understand, Acknowledge the Valid, and Show Equality. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p180]
