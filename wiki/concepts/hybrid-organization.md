# Hybrid Organization

## Summary

An organizational structure that balances mission-oriented (decentralized) and functional (centralized) forms. Grove's Law states all large organizations with a common business purpose end up in hybrid form. Examples include Intel, armies, and universities. The hybrid form enables both local responsiveness and economies of scale.

## Key facts

- Grove's Law states: All large organizations with a common business purpose end up in a hybrid organizational form. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1325-L1327]
- The mission-oriented organization is completely decentralized, where each unit pursues its mission with little tie-in to other units. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1305-L1307]
- The totally functional organization is completely centralized, where each function (like merchandising or personnel) operates across all locations. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1310-L1312]
- Alfred Sloan summed up decades of experience at General Motors by saying, 'Good management rests on a reconciliation of centralization and decentralization.'. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1315-L1317]
- The only exception to hybrid organization is conglomerates, which are typically organized in totally mission-oriented form because they do not have a common business purpose. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1330-L1332]
- The most important task of a hybrid organization is the optimum and timely allocation of its resources and the efficient resolution of conflicts arising over that allocation. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1340-L1342]
