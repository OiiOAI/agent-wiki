# Prospects for Memory Research

## Summary

Kandel reflects on where memory research is heading: from cellular and molecular mechanisms to systems properties of memory. He identifies three questions he would tackle if starting anew: (1) understanding unconscious processing of sensory information and how conscious attention guides memory stabilization, (2) the relation of unconscious to conscious mental processing and how brain imaging could connect psychoanalysis to brain anatomy, and (3) developing molecular sociobiology by applying molecular biology to understand social behaviors through research on C. elegans feeding patterns, Drosophila courtship, and mirror neurons.

## Key facts

- A major conceptual shift needed is from studying elementary processes (single proteins, genes, cells) to studying systems properties (mechanisms of many proteins, complex neural circuits, functioning of whole organisms). [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p428]
- Cori Bargmann found that the only difference between social and solitary C. elegans variants is one amino acid in a shared receptor protein; transferring the receptor makes solitary worms social. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p430]
- Rizzolatti discovered mirror neurons in the premotor cortex that fire both when a monkey performs an action and when it watches another monkey perform the same action, possibly providing insight into imitation, empathy, and human social interaction. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p431]
- Kandel states his psychiatric training and interest in psychoanalysis 'lie at the very core of my scientific thinking' and have provided perspective influencing 'almost every aspect of my work'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p431]

## Inferences

- Inference: The future of memory research will require imaging techniques that can resolve activity of individual neurons and neuronal networks, and will focus more on nonhuman primates and human beings.
