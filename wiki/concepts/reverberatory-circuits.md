# Reverberatory Circuits

## Summary

Reverberatory circuits are proposed neural mechanisms for short-term memory where neurons in a closed ring excite each other in sequence, maintaining activity around and around. This could allow sustained firing for a period before fading. Layer 6 pyramidal neurons that project back to the thalamus may be involved in such circuits, as both thalamic neurons and these cortical neurons have few axon collaterals that spread sideways, potentially providing the isolation needed.

## Key facts

- Reverberatory circuits involve neurons in a closed ring where each neuron excites the next, keeping activity going around and around. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p238]
- Transient changes in synaptic strength decay in complicated ways, partly as fast as 50 milliseconds and partly more slowly, with decay times ranging from fractions of a second to a minute. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p239]
- Transient synaptic changes were originally discovered in the fifties at neuromuscular junctions and later in the hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p239]
- The layer 6 pyramidal neurons projecting back to the thalamus and thalamic neurons have very few axon collaterals that spread sideways, probably allowing partial isolation for reverberatory activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p240]
- The back pathway from layer 6 to the LGN has very many axons, perhaps five or ten times as many as the major forward connection from the LGN to layer 4. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p240]

## Inferences

- Inference: Layer 6 neurons may be intimately involved in maintaining reverberatory circuits that embody very short-term memory, a key aspect of consciousness.
- Inference: The activity of LGN neurons is reduced in slow-wave sleep, which could prevent sufficient signals to sustain reverberatory activity, making the animal unconscious.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether transient synaptic changes that are Hebbian exist remains to be investigated.
- Uncertain: Whether reverberatory circuits for short-term memory exist in only particular places or more widely is unknown.
