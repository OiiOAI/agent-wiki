# Rejection-then-retreat

## Summary

The rejection-then-retreat technique (also called the door-in-the-face technique) is a compliance strategy where a requester first makes an excessively large request that is likely to be refused, then retreats to a more reasonable smaller request. This technique leverages the reciprocity rule by creating a sense of obligation in the target, who perceives the concession as a favor deserving reciprocation.

## Key facts

- The rejection-then-retreat technique was validated through three experiments that all demonstrated its effectiveness in producing compliance. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p252]
- The rejection-then-retreat procedure was shown to be more than four times more effective than simply asking for the smaller request only, based on bottom-line compliance measures (Miller et al., 1976). [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p252]
- The technique works by triggering the reciprocity rule—the target feels obligated to reciprocate the perceived concession made by the requester. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p252]
