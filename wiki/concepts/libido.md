# Libido

## Summary

Libido is an expression from the theory of the emotions denoting the energy of drives related to love. Freud uses the concept to explain group cohesion, arguing that emotional ties (libido) rather than rational factors constitute the essence of the group mind. The libido theory allows Freud to connect group formation with sexual drives and the life drives (Eros) more broadly.

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's use of 'Eros' here connects his libido theory with Plato's philosophy and broader philosophical discussions of love.
- Inference: Freud's teleological explanation for feminine repression connects to his broader evolutionary and biological framework, which some critics have found problematic.
- Inference: The concept of libido as a quantifiable psychic energy that can be dammed up, displaced, and sublimated is central to psychoanalytic theory of mental functioning.
- Inference: The diphasic onset of sexual life (early childhood efflorescence, latency, puberty) is described as unique to humans and connected to hominization, suggesting evolutionary significance for the prolongued childhood period.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges the gap in understanding why specifically sexual excitations play such a prominent role in neurosis causation, suggesting this requires biological rather than psychological explanation.
