# Social Reward Circuitry

## Summary

Social reward circuitry refers to brain systems, particularly the striatum (including nucleus accumbens and caudate), ventral tegmental area, and ventral striatum, that are activated by social stimuli and social behaviors in ways similar to primary and secondary non-social rewards. Research shows that social rewards like mutual cooperation, charitable donations, and punishment of unfair behavior activate overlapping circuitry with monetary rewards, supporting the hypothesis of a common neural currency for socially-preferred and other rewards.

## Key facts

- Mutual cooperation with a human partner activates the ventral striatum more than identical monetary gains from cooperation with a computer partner. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p214]
- The dorsal striatum (caudate nucleus) is strongly activated in costly punishment of defectors compared to symbolic costless punishment in trust games. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p214]
- Activation in ventral and dorsal striatum during charitable donation decisions positively correlates with the money that goes to the charity, across both forced and voluntary donation conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p215]
- The same dollar bargaining offer elicits higher striatal activation when it represents a fair share (50%) compared to when it represents a small share (15%). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p226]

## Inferences

- Inference: Social preferences may be encoded as genuine preferences because they activate the same neural reward circuitry as primary rewards, suggesting social rewards have intrinsic value beyond instrumental considerations
- Inference: The overlap between self-rewards and social rewards suggests a common neural currency for valuing both self-interested and other-regarding outcomes
