# Population Coding

## Summary

Population coding is a neural coding scheme where information about stimulus properties is distributed across populations of active neurons. The intensity of a stimulus is represented by all active neurons in a receptor population. For example, the color yellow is synthesized by specific combinations of activity in red, green, and blue cone cells. Perceived hue can be represented mathematically in a three-dimensional vector space where the strengths of activation of each receptor class combine to yield a unique sensation. This approach extends to high-dimensional representations of stimuli across large neural populations.

## Key facts

- The intensity of a stimulus is represented in the brain by all active neurons in the receptor population, depending on the fact that individual receptors differ in their sensory thresholds or affinity for particular molecules. - High-dimensional multineuronal representation of stimuli across large populations of neurons is analyzed by plotting the firing rates of each neuron in a coordinate system with multiple axes such as modality, location, intensity, and time. - Population vectors extracted from the pooled activity of M1 neurons during reaching movements predict the continually changing trajectory of arm motions 100 to 150 milliseconds into the future. - In the population vector approach, each neuron's activity is represented by a vector pointing in its preferred direction, with length varying as a function of discharge rate during reaches in each direction. - The population vector algorithm provides a unique signal about each reaching movement by pooling the heterogeneous single-neuron activity of the recorded M1 population.
