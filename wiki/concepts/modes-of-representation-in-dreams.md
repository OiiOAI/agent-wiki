# Modes of Representation in Dreams

## Summary

Modes of representation in dreams refer to the techniques the dream-work employs to translate abstract ideas and verbal expressions into visual dream imagery. These include: literalizing figures of speech (e.g., 'hurling invectives' becomes hurling animals), representing abstract concepts plastically, translating concepts into spatial or visual imagery, exploiting word sounds for symbolic meaning, and using ambiguity in words to create switching points between different meanings.

## Key facts

- The dream-work literalizes figures of speech: 'Monkey' and animal names used as invectives become actual animals hurled at the dreamer. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p363]
- The dream-work represents abstract ideas plastically; 'impressions on children' was represented by a child with a deformed skull. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p364]
- The dream-work exploits word sound rather than spelling; 'Ähren' (ears of corn) and 'Ehren' (honour) were used together to represent 'Kuss in Ehren' (honourable kiss). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p364]
- The dream-work uses ambiguity of words as switching points where different meanings of a word can be employed in dream content and dream thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p367]
- The dream-work can represent proper names by far-fetched associations; 'Stanniol' led to Stannius, author on fish nervous systems. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p369]
