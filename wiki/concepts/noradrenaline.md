# Noradrenaline

## Summary

Noradrenaline (also called norepinephrine) is a neuromodulator released during the fight-or-flight response that enhances memory formation by increasing the contrast of memories and promoting long-term changes in neural connections. It is released throughout the brain in response to threats.

## Key facts

- Noradrenaline (also known as norepinephrine) is a well-studied neuromodulator that influences how humans learn and remember. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p96]
- During the fight-or-flight response, adrenaline is pumped out by adrenal glands raising heart rate, blood pressure, and breathing rate, and noradrenaline is released all over the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p96]
- Psychologist Mara Mather showed that emotional arousal ratchets up attention stakes, making people better at perceiving salient things that are important or stand out. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p96]
- Noradrenaline jacks up the contrast of memories, highlighting significant details while pushing other information into the background. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p96]
- Over a few hours after an emotionally intense event, noradrenaline triggers a cascade in active cell assemblies, turning on genes that manufacture proteins to tighten connections between neurons so the memory remains robust over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p96]
