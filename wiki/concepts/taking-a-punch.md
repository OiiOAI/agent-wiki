# Taking a Punch

## Summary

Experienced negotiators often lead with extreme anchors to knock counterparts off their game. Voss teaches that preparation and psychological readiness allow you to welcome the extreme anchor and learn from it rather than flee to your maximum. Effective responses include deflecting with 'No' variations, pivoting to terms, using calibrated questions, and employing strategic umbrage (controlled anger at the proposal, not the person).

## Key facts

- Experienced negotiators often lead with ridiculous offers (extreme anchors); if unprepared, you'll lose your moorings and immediately go to your maximum. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p174]
- Researchers Ames and Wazlawek found that people receiving strategic umbrage rated themselves as overassertive even when the counterpart didn't think so. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p178]
- Anger expressions increase negotiator advantage and final take, but must be real and under control; fake anger backfires and reduces cognitive ability in both parties. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p178]
- When angry at a ridiculous offer, take a breath and channel it at the proposal, not the person, saying 'I don't see how that would ever work.'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p178]
- Pivoting to terms means detouring from price haggles to nonmonetary issues that make any final price work, either directly or obliquely by asking 'What else would you be able to offer?'. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p175]
