# Developmental Sociopathy

## Summary

Developmental sociopaths (psychopaths) are individuals who from early development show abnormally high thresholds for emotional responses. They appear unflappable, report being unfeeling and uncaring, and repeatedly commit crimes in cold blood. Damasio suggests their impairment may arise from dysfunction in the same neural systems impaired in Phineas Gage, involving abnormal circuitry and chemical signaling from early development.

## Key facts

- Developmental sociopaths have such high emotional thresholds that they appear unflappable, report being unfeeling and uncaring, and repeat crimes in cold blood to everyone's disadvantage including their own. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p198]
- It is possible that developmental sociopathy arises from dysfunction within the same overall system impaired in Phineas Gage, at cortical or subcortical level, but from abnormal circuitry and chemical signaling beginning early in development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p198]
- Acquired sociopathy describes behavioral changes in patients with adult-onset prefrontal damage, though they differ from developmental sociopaths in that acquired sociopaths are rarely violent. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p198]

## Inferences

- Inference: Understanding the neurobiology of sociopathy might lead to prevention or treatment, and could illuminate how social factors interact with biological ones.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural circuits involved in developmental versus acquired sociopathy have not been fully characterized.
