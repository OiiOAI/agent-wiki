# Crisis Management Teams (Worst Teams Example)

## Summary

Crisis management teams, typically established by manufacturing companies handling dangerous materials, are presented as examples of poorly functioning teams. Klein and Thordsen observed several teams established by nuclear weapons manufacturers and found their incompetence surprising, with leaders lacking background in crisis management and exercising poor judgment about reaction times.

## Key facts

- Crisis management teams fall apart because their heads are corporate executives with no background in crisis management, going through training only a few times a year. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p291]
- In one training scenario, the crisis manager issued an order to increase security at sector H, then was amazed his order had not been obeyed thirty-one minutes later. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p292]
- Klein calculated it would take at minimum two hours to double the security staff, yet the crisis manager expected compliance in thirty-one minutes. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p292]
- The crisis manager tried to micromanage events, relying on photographs rather than leaving decisions to people on the spot. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p293]
- In one exercise, the security director and assistants were overloaded while the public relations unit sat without tasks for hours, showing confusion about roles and functions. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p295]
