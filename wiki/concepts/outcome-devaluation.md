# Outcome Devaluation

## Summary

Outcome devaluation is a behavioral procedure used to determine whether an action is goal-directed. Animals are trained to perform an action for an outcome, then the value of that outcome is reduced either by outcome-specific satiety or by taste aversion learning. If responding is goal-directed, the animal reduces performance of the action associated with the devalued outcome relative to actions earning valued outcomes.

## Key facts

- Outcome devaluation tests whether performance of an action depends on the current value of its outcome and the contingency between action and outcome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p71]
- Animals with intact goal-directed systems reduce responding on the devalued action in extinction tests, demonstrating sensitivity to outcome devaluation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p71]
- Lesions of the prelimbic cortex, insular cortex, or nucleus accumbens core abolish sensitivity to outcome devaluation while sparing other forms of learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p75]

## Inferences

- Inference: Outcome devaluation performance depends on dissociable neural circuits: prelimbic cortex for acquisition, insular cortex for retrieval of value, and nucleus accumbens core for performance.
- Inference: The basolateral amygdala encodes changes in incentive value during devaluation but is not involved in retrieving that value for performance.
