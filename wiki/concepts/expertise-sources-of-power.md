# Expertise (Sources of Power)

## Summary

Expertise involves the accumulation of experience that allows experts to see things novices cannot. The two primary sources of power are pattern matching (intuition) and mental simulation, supplemented by fine discrimination abilities and awareness of limitations.

## Key facts

- Experts can see patterns that novices do not notice, anomalies (events that did not happen), the big picture, the way things work, opportunities and improvisations, events from the past or future, and differences too small for novices to detect. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p188]
- Experts can also see their own limitations, enabling them to manage around these constraints. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p188]
- Pattern matching (intuition) gives experts the ability to detect typicality and notice anomalies that violate patterns. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p188]
- Mental simulation covers the ability to see events that happened previously and events likely to happen in the future. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p188]
- CPR instructors who had never performed CPR on actual victims failed to recognize paramedic expertise when shown on video, while actual paramedics and novices recognized it. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: The accumulation of experience does not weigh people down; it 'lightens them up' by enabling more rapid and accurate pattern recognition and mental simulation.
