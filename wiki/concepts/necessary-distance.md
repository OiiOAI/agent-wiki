# Necessary Distance

## Summary

McGilchrist introduces 'necessary distance' as the psychological achievement that enabled the flowering of Greek civilization. This is not alienation or detachment that separates, but rather a standing back that enables empathy and connection. It is fundamental to the creative process and makes possible both art and philosophy, allowing the mind to cognize itself. The concept is connected to enhanced frontal lobe function and is prerequisite to self-consciousness.

## Key facts

- Necessary distance is achieved through enhanced frontal lobe function and enables empathy and connection rather than separating from the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p372]
- This distance enabled drama, which allows us to feel powerfully with and thus know ourselves in others, and others in ourselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p379]
- The tragedy of Prometheus represents the paradox of self-consciousness: the beginnings of the mind coming to know and understand its own nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p381]
- Drama reconciled opposites that could never be reconciled in explicit philosophical discourse through the implicit power of myth. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p379]
