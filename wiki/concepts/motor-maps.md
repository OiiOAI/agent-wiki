# Motor Maps

## Summary

Motor maps are representations in the brain's motor cortex that control body movements. Research on Braille readers and piano learners demonstrated that these maps are plastic and expand with practice. A key finding was the 'Friday-Monday effect': rapid dramatic expansion during training (visible on Friday brain maps) that regressed to baseline by Monday, followed by slower permanent changes that only appeared after six months. The speed of reading correlated better with stable Monday maps than with dramatic Friday maps.

## Key facts

- TMS mapping showed that motor maps for Braille reading fingers were larger than maps for other index fingers and for non-Braille readers' index fingers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p205]
- Friday maps showed rapid dramatic expansion during training but returned to baseline by Monday; after six months, Friday maps were still increasing but less dramatically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p205]
- Monday maps didn't begin to change until six months into training, then increased slowly and plateaued at ten months; Braille speed correlated better with Monday maps. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p205]
- Pascual-Leone proposed that fast Friday changes strengthen existing neuronal connections and unmask buried pathways, while slower Monday changes suggest formation of new structures like sprouting of new connections and synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p206]
- After two months off, Braille readers' maps were unchanged from the last Monday mapping two months before, showing stable long-term plastic change. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p206]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Friday-Monday effect suggests that sustained, consistent practice over months is necessary for permanent skill acquisition, explaining why 'cramming' produces rapid but temporary improvement.
