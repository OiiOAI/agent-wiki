# Planning Is Guessing

## Summary

The concept argues that long-term business planning is a fantasy because too many factors are outside anyone's control, and that plans should be called what they really are: guesses. The authors contend that turning guesses into plans puts blinders on you, that timing of planning is wrong (most information comes during action, not before), and that plans become fossils in file cabinets. They recommend deciding what to do this week rather than this year and making decisions right before acting.

## Key facts

- The authors argue that long-term business planning is a fantasy because too many factors are out of control, including market conditions, competitors, customers, and the economy. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p18]
- The authors recommend calling plans what they really are: guesses, including business plans, financial plans, and strategic plans. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p18]
- The authors argue that the most information is available when doing something, not before, yet plans are written before you've begun, which is the worst time for big decisions. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p18]
- The authors recommend giving up guesswork, deciding what to do this week not this year, and making decisions right before acting. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p18]
