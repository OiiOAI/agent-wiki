# Mediation Fallacy

## Summary

The Mediation Fallacy is the mistake of conditioning on a mediator when attempting to estimate the total effect of X on Y. This error opens back-door paths and can reverse the direction of apparent effects. Barbara Burks identified this problem in 1926, and it was rediscovered by William Kruskal in the context of the Berkeley Admissions Paradox. R.A. Fisher also fell victim to this fallacy in 1936.

## Key facts

- The Mediation Fallacy is conditioning on a mediator instead of holding it constant. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2765-L2775]
- Conditioning on a mediator opens back-door paths through colliders. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2765-L2775]
- Barbara Burks identified this problem in 1926, describing it as conditioning on effects of causes. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2650-L2660]
- R.A. Fisher fell victim to the same fallacy in 1936. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2775-L2780]
- The Mediation Fallacy is demonstrated in Pearl (1998) in 'Graphs, causality, and structural equation models'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3893-L3894]
- Cole and Hernán (2002) also demonstrated the fallacy of conditioning on a mediator. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3893-L3894]
- The fallacy involves inappropriate conditioning on a mediator. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3893-L3894]
- Fisher fell for the Mediation Fallacy as recounted in Rubin (2005). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3894-L3895]
