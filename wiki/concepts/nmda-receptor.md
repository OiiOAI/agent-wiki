# NMDA Receptor

## Summary

The NMDA receptor is an ionotropic glutamate receptor in the hippocampus that acts as a molecular coincidence detector. It responds only to extraordinarily rapid trains of stimuli and is required for long-term potentiation. When a postsynaptic neuron is stimulated repeatedly, AMPA receptor activation depolarizes the cell membrane, causing an ion channel in the NMDA receptor to open and allow calcium to flow into the cell.

## Key facts

- Watkins and Graham Collingridge found that glutamate acts on two different types of ionotropic receptors in the hippocampus: the AMPA receptor and the NMDA receptor. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p294]
- The AMPA receptor mediates normal synaptic transmission and responds to an individual action potential in the presynaptic neuron; the NMDA receptor responds only to extraordinarily rapid trains of stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p294]
- When AMPA receptors generate a powerful synaptic potential depolarizing the cell membrane by 20-30 millivolts, the NMDA receptor ion channel opens, allowing calcium to flow into the cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p294]
- Roger Nicoll and Gary Lynch discovered that calcium influx acts as a second messenger triggering long-term potentiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p294]
- The NMDA receptor acts as a coincidence detector, allowing calcium ions to flow through only if it detects the coincidence of presynaptic glutamate release and postsynaptic depolarization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p295]
- In 1949, psychologist D.O. Hebb predicted that some kind of neural coincidence detector would be present in the brain during learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p295]
