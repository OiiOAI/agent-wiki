# Neomercantilism

## Summary

Neomercantilism is a modern economic critique that shares with Marxist and Keynesian traditions an emphasis on state importance as an economic actor, but focuses on more modest objectives such as promoting high-technology industries in competitive global markets.

## Key facts

- Neomercantilists concede that global competition produces beneficial economic efficiencies and that economies should be export oriented and outward looking. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p364]
- Unlike Marxists who sought total state control or Keynesians who sought massive government intervention for full employment, neomercantilists argue only that the market alone is insufficient to produce technological leadership. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p364]

## Inferences

- Inference: Neomercantilism represents a middle-ground position between pure free markets and extensive state control, accepting market mechanisms while arguing for strategic state involvement in technology development.
