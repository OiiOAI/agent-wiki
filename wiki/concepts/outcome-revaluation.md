# Outcome Revaluation

## Summary

Outcome revaluation is a behavioral procedure used to test whether instrumental actions are sensitive to changes in the current value of their associated outcomes. Methods include specific satiety (feeding to saturation) or taste aversion learning (pairing food with illness). Actions that decrease their performance after revaluation are classified as goal-directed.

## Key facts

- After training rats on two actions earning different foods, devaluing one outcome by feeding to satiety or pairing with illness selectively reduces performance of the action associated with the devalued outcome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p370]
- Outcome revaluation tests the second criterion for goal-directed action: that performance is sensitive to the current value of the goal outcome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p370]
- Valentin et al. (2007) used fMRI to show that human OFC activity during action selection is modulated by the value of the associated outcome following devaluation by feeding to satiety. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p383]
