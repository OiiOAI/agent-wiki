# Free Energy Principle

## Summary

The free energy principle (FEP) is a first-principles theory originating in statistical physics that states that any system that resists the dispersion of its states must minimize free energy. Applied to living organisms with a Markov blanket, it implies that organisms must minimize the surprise of their sensory observations to maintain their existence. Active Inference is the specific application of the FEP to brain, mind, and behavior, focusing on how the imperative to minimize free energy manifests as perception, action, planning, and learning. The FEP is distinguished from Active Inference in that it covers a wider range of biological phenomena and timescales, including evolutionary, cellular, and cultural levels, while Active Inference focuses on the behavioral and neural implementation of free energy minimization.

## Key facts

- The free energy principle states that any self-organizing system that maintains its boundaries must minimize its free energy, which in the context of living organisms equates to minimizing surprise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p14]
- Active Inference inherits from the free energy principle which equates existence with self-evidencing and self-evidencing with an enactive sort of inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p7]
- The book distinguishes between the FEP (covering phenomena from evolutionary to cellular and cultural timescales) and Active Inference (focused on neural and behavioral implementation of free energy minimization). [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p14]
- Under the FEP, the persistence of a Markov blanket implies that the distribution of blanket states remains constant over time, which is achieved by dynamics that descend a Hamiltonian interpretable as negative log evidence or surprise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p47]
- The book notes that the free energy concept in Active Inference targets nonequilibrium steady-state systems that are open, in contrast to equilibrium thermodynamics in standard statistical physics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p51]

## Inferences

- Inference: The FEP provides the ultimate justification for why organisms must minimize surprise—because they must resist the dispersion of their states (entropy increase dictated by the second law of thermodynamics) in order to maintain their existence.
