# Free Energy Principle

## Summary

The free energy principle (FEP) is a first-principles theory originating in statistical physics that states that any system that resists the dispersion of its states must minimize free energy. Applied to living organisms with a Markov blanket, it implies that organisms must minimize the surprise of their sensory observations to maintain their existence. Active Inference is the specific application of the FEP to brain, mind, and behavior, focusing on how the imperative to minimize free energy manifests as perception, action, planning, and learning. The FEP is distinguished from Active Inference in that it covers a wider range of biological phenomena and timescales, including evolutionary, cellular, and cultural levels, while Active Inference focuses on the behavioral and neural implementation of free energy minimization.

## Key facts

- The free energy principle states that any self-organizing system that maintains its boundaries must minimize its free energy, which in the context of living organisms equates to minimizing surprise. - Active Inference inherits from the free energy principle which equates existence with self-evidencing and self-evidencing with an enactive sort of inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p7]

- Under the FEP, the persistence of a Markov blanket implies that the distribution of blanket states remains constant over time, which is achieved by dynamics that descend a Hamiltonian interpretable as negative log evidence or surprise. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p47]

- The Free Energy Principle brings together concepts from biology, physics, statistics, neuroscience, engineering, and machine learning, and applies to all features of living systems from single bacteria to brains. - For a living system to exist there must be a boundary between that thing and everything else, and living systems actively maintain their boundaries over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p190]
- Living systems exist out of equilibrium with their environment and temporarily fend off the second law of thermodynamics by occupying states they expect to be in. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p191]
- Sensory entropy cannot be directly detected or measured by a system, which is why free energy—a measurable approximation—is used instead. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p192]
- Free energy is mathematically equivalent to sensory prediction error, meaning that minimizing free energy is the same as minimizing prediction error in predictive processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p192]
- The FEP licenses the idea that living systems have or are models of their environment, specifically models of the causes of their sensory signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p192]

- Minimizing free energy does not mean retreating into a dark room; complex organisms need to allow some things to change for other things to stay the same. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p193]

## Inferences

- Inference: The FEP provides the ultimate justification for why organisms must minimize surprise—because they must resist the dispersion of their states (entropy increase dictated by the second law of thermodynamics) in order to maintain their existence.
- Inference: The beast machine theory can be understood through the lens of the FEP as flowing from a fundamental constraint on what it means to be alive, on what it means to exist.
- Inference: Minimizing long-term prediction error means seeking out new sensations that reduce uncertainty about what would happen next, making organisms curious sensation-seeking agents.
- Inference: onsciousness is homeostatic, and homeostasis is reducible to physical laws, then so are the phenomena of consciousness [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p142-143]
- Inference: The Free Energy Principle reduces mental and neurological processes to a single computable mechanism, which Solms considers its great appeal.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The book references Friston (2019) for details on the underlying physics of self-evidencing, indicating the full physics treatment is beyond the scope of this book.
- Uncertain: The FEP has been criticized for being difficult to falsify experimentally, though proponents argue it serves as a framework motivating more specific testable theories.
- Uncertain: There are acknowledged gaps between the broad brushstrokes Solms has delineated and the practical implementation of the mechanisms they describe.
