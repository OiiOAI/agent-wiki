# The Choice Point

## Summary

The choice point is a central concept in ACT where a person recognizes they have a choice when difficult thoughts and feelings arise. They can respond with toward moves (effective, life-enhancing behaviors) or away moves (ineffective, life-diminishing behaviors). The concept was co-created by Russ Harris with colleagues Joe Ciarrochi and Ann Bailey to simplify the ACT model.

## Key facts

- The choice point is a diagram showing that when life is difficult and painful thoughts and feelings show up, a person potentially has a choice between toward moves and away moves. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p27]
- The choice point concept was co-created by Russ Harris with colleagues Joe Ciarrochi and Ann Bailey. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p28]
- People only have the choice presented at the choice point if they have developed good unhooking skills; without them, the default setting is to get hooked and pulled into away moves. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p28]
- The less ability to unhook, the less choice a person has; thoughts and feelings tend to dominate and dictate what they can and cannot do. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p28]
