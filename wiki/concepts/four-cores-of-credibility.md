# Four Cores of Credibility

## Summary

The Four Cores of Credibility are the foundational elements that create personal and organizational credibility necessary for establishing trust: Integrity (character), Intent (motive/agenda), Capabilities (competence), and Results (track record). All four cores are vital and interdependent. Integrity and Intent represent character dimensions while Capabilities and Results represent competence dimensions. These cores determine whether others will trust us and whether we can trust ourselves.

## Key facts

- Integrity includes making and keeping commitments to yourself, standing for something, and being open. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p97]
- Intent is defined by motive, agenda, and behavior. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p106]
- Capabilities enable individuals to perform with excellence and inspire trust when specifically matched to the task. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p123]
- Results matter to credibility and give people clout, influence, and classification as a producer. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p142]
- Research shows participants rate their own credibility at 86.6% while others rate them at 56.2% on average. [raw/books/business/The SPEED of Trust _ The One Thing that Ch - Covey, Stephen M.R.pdf#p159]
