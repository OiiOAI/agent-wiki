# Deserved Trust

## Summary

Munger identifies trust as arguably the most important theme in the book: deserved reliance upon the character, values, and integrity of those you live and work with. He and Warren Buffett are known for steadfast commitment to unblemished integrity, believing ethical practices pay because they are good. Ben Graham's lesson emphasizes that even smart people can fool you, so trust is essential.

## Key facts

- Munger states: 'Capitalism works best when there is trust in the system.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p429]
- Elbert Gary, Chairman of US Steel from 1901 to 1927, stated: 'Ethical practices aren't good because they pay; they pay because they are good.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p429]
- Ben Graham taught: 'No matter how smart you are, there are smart people out there who can fool you if they really want to. So, be sure you can trust the smart people you work with.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p429]
- Munger advises that in your own life, 'what you want to maximize is a seamless web of deserved trust.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p449]
- The Mayo Clinic operates on total trust between reliable people, without much fancy procedure—if lawyers introduced more process there, more patients would die. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p449]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger distinguishes between 'earned' and 'blind' trust, emphasizing that seeking people who deserve trust through demonstrated integrity is different from naive trust in credentials or authority.
