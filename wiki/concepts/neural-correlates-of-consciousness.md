# Neural Correlates of Consciousness

## Summary

Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCC) are the minimal neuronal mechanisms jointly sufficient for any one specific conscious percept. The NCC approach, pioneered by Francis Crick and Christof Koch, identifies specific patterns of neural activity responsible for particular conscious experiences. Limitations include difficulty distinguishing true NCCs from prerequisites or consequences, and the fundamental problem that correlation is not explanation.

## Key facts

- The gold-standard definition of an NCC is 'the minimal neuronal mechanisms jointly sufficient for any one specific conscious percept'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p32]
- The NCC approach was pioneered by Francis Crick and Christof Koch in the late 1980s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p32]
- Binocular rivalry provides an example where conscious perception changes while sensory input remains constant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p33]
- A fundamental limitation of NCCs is that correlations are not explanations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p34]
- NCCs are usually interpreted as relating to specific brain regions but need not be—the definition relates to mechanisms that could be implemented across various regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p33]
