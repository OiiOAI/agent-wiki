# Epilepsy

## Summary

Epilepsy is a disease with a long history dating back to prehistoric times, characterized by recurrent seizures caused by excessive electrical activity in the brain. Henry Molaison developed epilepsy at age ten, initially experiencing petit mal (absence) seizures and later grand mal (tonic-clonic) seizures. His condition worsened over time, leading to the experimental surgery in 1953. The medical understanding of epilepsy advanced through the work of John Hughlings Jackson, Harvey Cushing, and Wilder Penfield, who developed surgical treatments including temporal lobectomy.

## Key facts

- Epilepsy is a disease characterized by excessive electrical activity in the brain causing seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p9]
- Henry developed epilepsy at age ten, with petit mal seizures initially, progressing to grand mal seizures at age fifteen. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p5]
- During petit mal seizures, Henry became mentally absent for a few seconds without convulsions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p5]
- Grand mal (tonic-clonic) seizures involve stiffening followed by rhythmic convulsions and loss of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p6]
- John Hughlings Jackson proposed that seizures start in a single brain area and progress orderly to other areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p7]
- Victor Horsley performed the first surgery for epilepsy in 1886. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p7]
- Epilepsy is the chronic condition of recurrent seizures, which are temporary disruptions of brain function resulting from abnormal, excessive neuronal activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1168]
- Approximately 3% of all individuals living to age 80 are diagnosed with epilepsy, with highest incidence in young children and the elderly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1168]
- Focal seizures originate within a small group of neurons known as a seizure focus, and symptoms depend on the location of the focus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1171]
- Primary generalized seizures begin without an aura or focal seizure and involve both hemispheres from onset. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1169]
- The EEG represents the collective behavior of cortical neurons, reflecting field potentials from ensembles of cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1170]
