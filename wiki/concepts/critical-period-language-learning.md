# Critical Period for Language Learning

## Summary

There is a critical or sensitive period for language learning, typically ending around puberty. Children learn language more naturally and efficiently than adults. Evidence comes from studies of immigrants showing that second-language proficiency declines with age of arrival. Early language learning creates neural commitment to the acoustic patterns of the native language, which can interfere with learning a second language later.

## Key facts

- Children learn language more naturally and efficiently than adults, despite adults having superior cognitive skills in general. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1411]
- Evidence for the critical period comes from Chinese and Korean immigrants to the United States, whose performance at identifying grammatical errors declined with age of arrival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1411]
- Early exposure to a specific language results in neural circuitry tuned to detect the phonetic units and prosodic patterns of that language, which can interfere with second language learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1411-1412]
- The second half of the first year appears to be a sensitive period for speech learning; exposure to a new language at this time can lead to learning if provided through live human interaction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1409]
