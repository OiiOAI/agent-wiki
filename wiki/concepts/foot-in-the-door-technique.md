# Foot-in-the-Door Technique

## Summary

The foot-in-the-door technique secures compliance with large requests by first obtaining agreement to small, trivial ones. The technique exploits commitment and consistency, as agreeing to a small request changes self-perception, making subsequent larger requests consistent with this new self-image. This technique was systematically employed by Chinese Communist handlers in Korean War POW camps decades before social scientists formally documented it.

## Key facts

- The foot-in-the-door technique uses small initial commitments to pave the way for subsequent larger compliance requests. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p70]
- Freedman and Fraser's California study showed 76% of those who agreed to display a small 'BE A SAFE DRIVER' sign later agreed to host a large 'DRIVE CAREFULLY' billboard on their lawn. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p71]
- Even signing a petition for an unrelated cause (state beautification) increased lawn sign compliance by 50%, demonstrating generalization across commitment topics. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p71]
- The Chinese started with trivial anti-American statements from POWs, then escalated to essays, self-criticisms, and broader collaboration, using each commitment to justify the next. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p69]
- Signed essays won prizes in Chinese camp essay contests only if they contained pro-Communist content, creating incentive to gradually shift positions. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p76]
- The technique works because signing a petition changes self-perception—people see themselves as public-spirited citizens who agree to requests from strangers. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p72]
- Cialdini states he is 'rarely willing to sign a petition anymore' after understanding how even trivial commitments influence self-image. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p72]

## Inferences

- Inference: The technique's effectiveness with unrelated topics (beautification petition → safe driving sign) suggests the mechanism operates through general self-concept change rather than topic-specific consistency.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The long-term persistence of foot-in-the-door effects and whether they fade over time without reinforcement is not addressed.
