# Involuntary Ideas in Dreams

## Summary

Thoughts or images that emerge in sleep without deliberate intention, considered by Freud to be a central feature of dream formation and closely related to the emergence of immoral and absurd dream content.

## Key facts

- Schleiermacher asserted that the act of falling asleep is accompanied by the appearance of involuntary ideas or images. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1347]
- Involuntary ideas in the moral sphere contradict our usual attitude of mind, whereas others merely strike us as strange. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1347]
- Volkelt noted that ideas which entered waking consciousness almost unnoticed and perhaps never recalled announce their presence through dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1347]

## Inferences

- Inference: The emergence of involuntary ideas in dreams represents material suppressed or unused during waking life, providing access to psychological depths normally inaccessible while awake.
