# Exposure

## Summary

In psychology, exposure means deliberately getting in contact with difficult stuff so that you can learn more effective ways of responding to it. This difficult stuff may be in the external world (people, places, situations, activities, events) or the internal world (thoughts, emotions, memories). Exposure is described as the single most powerful intervention in the entire field of psychology, with nothing having larger effects on human behavior. In terms of neuroplasticity, exposure enables laying new neural pathways on top of old ones, so when old pathways fire up and difficult thoughts and feelings reappear, new learning kicks in and one can unhook. The playful techniques in the book (unhooking, making room) are forms of exposure to internal experience. The aim is not for unpleasant images or memories to disappear but to free oneself up to do toward moves and focus on what matters.
