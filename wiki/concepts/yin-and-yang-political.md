# Yin and Yang (Political)

## Summary

Haidt applies the Chinese philosophical concept of yin and yang to political ideologies, arguing that liberals and conservatives are complementary opposing forces necessary for healthy political life. Drawing on John Stuart Mill and Bertrand Russell, Haidt suggests that a party of order or stability (conservatives) and a party of progress or reform (liberals) are both necessary elements of a healthy state. The framework is used to show that each political ideology has genuine moral wisdom that the other lacks.

## Key facts

- John Stuart Mill said that liberals and conservatives are like yin and yang: 'A party of order or stability, and a party of progress or reform, are both necessary elements of a healthy state of political life.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p317]
- Bertrand Russell explained that 'each party to this dispute—as to all that persist through long periods of time—is partly right and partly wrong' and that 'every community is exposed to two opposite dangers: ossification through too much discipline and reverence for tradition, on the one hand; on the other hand, dissolution, or subjection to foreign conquest.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p317]
- Liberals are experts in care; they are better able to see the victims of existing social arrangements, and they continually push to update social arrangements and invent new ones. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p337]
