# Performance Appraisal

## Summary

The most important form of task-relevant feedback a supervisor provides, assessing performance and delivering that assessment face-to-face. The fundamental purpose is to improve the subordinate's performance. Key principles include assessing both skill level and motivation, and the 'three L's': Level, Listen, and Leave yourself out.

## Key facts

- Performance reviews are the single most important form of task-relevant feedback a supervisor can provide and represent the most formal type of institutionalized leadership. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1620-L1622]
- The fundamental purpose of performance appraisal is to improve the subordinate's performance, both by addressing skill gaps and intensifying motivation. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1625-L1627]
- The three L's of delivering a review are: Level (be totally frank), Listen (employ all sensory capabilities to ensure the message is received), and Leave yourself out (keep your own insecurities out of it). [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1650-L1652]
- A subordinate has finite capacity to deal with facts and suggestions; the review should target key areas rather than overwhelming with too many messages. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1655-L1657]
- Any outcome that includes a commitment to action is acceptable in a performance review; people do not need to agree, only to commit to pursue the decided course of action. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1690-L1692]
- The most important priority should be improving performance of top performers (stars), not just marginal employees, because stars account for disproportionately large share of work. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1710-L1712]
