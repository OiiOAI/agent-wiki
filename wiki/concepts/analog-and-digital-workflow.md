# Analog and Digital Workflow

## Summary

Kleon advocates for alternating between analog and digital tools in creative work. The analog station—free of electronics—is for generating ideas through hands-on manipulation, while the digital station is for editing and publishing.

## Key facts

- Kleon has two desks: an analog desk with markers, pens, pencils, paper, index cards, and newspaper, and a digital desk with laptop, monitor, scanner, and drawing tablet. [raw/books/productivity/Steal Like an Artist_ 10 Things Nobody Tol - Kleon, Austin [Kleon, Austin].pdf#p45]
- For Newspaper Blackout, Kleon scanned pieces into a computer and printed them on quarter sheets, then spread them across the office to rearrange them—analog to digital and back. [raw/books/productivity/Steal Like an Artist_ 10 Things Nobody Tol - Kleon, Austin [Kleon, Austin].pdf#p44]
- The computer is good for editing ideas and getting them ready for publishing, but not for generating ideas due to the temptation to delete. [raw/books/productivity/Steal Like an Artist_ 10 Things Nobody Tol - Kleon, Austin [Kleon, Austin].pdf#p44]
- Lynda Barry said, 'In the digital age, don't forget to use your digits!'. [raw/books/productivity/Steal Like an Artist_ 10 Things Nobody Tol - Kleon, Austin [Kleon, Austin].pdf#p41]
