# Exposure Therapy

## Summary

Exposure therapy is a behavioral treatment for anxiety disorders involving repeated encounters with feared objects, situations, or stimuli. Based on the principle of extinction, it prevents avoidance and allows new learning that the stimulus is not dangerous. Variants include systematic desensitization (gradual exposure with relaxation), flooding (intense prolonged exposure), and prolonged exposure therapy. Research suggests exposure therapy achieves approximately 70 percent success rates but can be enhanced by procedural refinements including spaced extinction trials, multiple contexts, removal of safety behaviors, and pharmacologically enhanced extinction.

## Key facts

- Systematic desensitization was introduced by Joseph Wolpe in the late 1950s and used repeated gradual exposures to imagined threats with relaxation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p368]
- Exposure therapy helps roughly 70 percent of those treated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p369]
- Isaac Marks concluded in 1987 that exposure alone was sufficient and the various supplements added to it (relaxation, breathing, belief change) were redundant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p373]
