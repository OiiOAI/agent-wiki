# Extended Amygdala

## Summary

The extended amygdala is a group of structures related to but somewhat different from the amygdala proper. It includes the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST) and is proposed to control reactions and actions based on uncertain threats. Unlike the amygdala, which processes specific and certain threat cues via extensive sensory inputs, the BNST is more extensively connected with cortical areas involved in cognitive processing including the hippocampus, ventromedial prefrontal cortex, insula cortex, and orbitofrontal cortex. The BNST may coordinate defensive circuits and risk-assessment circuitry, balancing which system dominates behavioral control depending on the degree of uncertainty.

## Key facts

- The bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST) is part of the extended amygdala and plays a key role in processing uncertain threats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p160]
- The BNST has many of the same output connections as the central amygdala, linking to circuits controlling freezing, autonomic responses, and endocrine function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p160]
- Unlike the amygdala with extensive sensory inputs, the BNST has stronger connections with cortical areas involved in cognitive processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p160]
- The BNST may coordinate defensive circuits involving the amygdala and accumbens with septohippocampal risk-assessment circuitry. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p161]
- Damage to the amygdala does not reliably interfere with performance in anxiety tests that involve uncertainty, while the BNST often plays a role in such tests. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p159]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between amygdala (certain threats) and BNST (uncertain threats) may explain why anxiety (uncertainty-based) involves different neural substrates than fear (certainty-based).
- Inference: The BNST can partially take over defensive functions normally controlled by the amygdala if the latter is damaged.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact subregional organization and functional specificity within the BNST continues to be clarified.
