# Psychology of Human Misjudgment

## Summary

The Psychology of Human Misjudgment is one of Charles T. Munger's key talks, later expanded into the eleventh talk in Poor Charlie's Almanack. This talk examines the psychological biases and mental shortcuts that lead humans to make systematic errors in judgment, forming a crucial component of Munger's multidisciplinary approach to understanding human behavior.

## Key facts

- The Psychology of Human Misjudgment is presented as a special edition that Charlie created especially for the book. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p7]
- The talk centers on fundamental truth, human accomplishment, human foibles, and the arduous path to wisdom. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p6]
- Munger identifies 25 psychological tendencies that cause misjudgment, listed and discussed in his talk. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p469]
- The grand general principle of social psychology states that cognition is ordinarily situation-dependent so that different situations often cause different conclusions, even when the same person is thinking in the same general subject area. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p468]
- Munger was influenced by Robert Cialdini's book 'Influence,' which he sent to all his children and for which he gave Cialdini a share of Berkshire Class A stock. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p463]
- Munger criticizes academic psychology textbooks for not adequately addressing intertwined psychological tendencies and for lacking discussion of antidotes to psychological-driven folly. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p461]
- Munger uses inversion (collecting instances of bad judgment and pondering ways to avoid them) as a key method for developing his psychological framework. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p458]
- Munger uses 'to track analysis'—first, what are the factors that really govern the interests rationally considered, and second, what are the subconscious influences where the brain is forming conclusions that often malfunction when approaching rationality. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p71]
- Munger delivered a famous talk titled 'The Psychology of Human Misjudgment' at the Harvard Faculty Club in 1994. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p90]
- The second type of analysis evaluates psychological factors that cause subconscious conclusions, many of which are wrong. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p71]
- Munger applies psychological analysis to investments just as one would work out a bridge problem by evaluating real interests and real probabilities. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p71]
- Munger identifies 25 standard causes of human misjudgment, combining psychological tendencies that often work together. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p497]
- Munger places 'Reward and Punishment Super Response Tendency' first because he believes he always underestimated its power. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p499]
- The grand general principle of social psychology states that cognition is ordinarily situation-dependent, causing different situations to produce different conclusions from the same person. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p496]
- Munger was motivated to study psychology partly by curiosity about how destructive cults converted normal people over a single weekend. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p488]
- Robert Cialdini's book 'Influence' significantly helped Munger develop his psychological framework. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p491]
- Munger donated Berkshire stock to Cialdini to thank him for his contributions. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p491]
- The framework identifies 25 psychological tendencies including Reward-Super Response, Liking/Loving, Disliking/Hating, Doubt-Avoidance, Inconsistency-Avoidance, and Social-Proof tendencies. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p513]
- Munger criticizes psychology textbooks for omitting coverage of envy/jealousy and for not coherently presenting the Lollapalooza Effect of multiple tendencies combining. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p535]
- Munger provides practical antidotes for each tendency, including Darwin's method for countering confirmation bias and Buffett's rule of avoiding open-outcry auctions. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p607]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that Munger created a special edition of this talk for the book suggests it is one of his most important contributions to understanding decision-making.
- Inference: Munger synthesized psychological insights from non-clinical sources including Cialdini's applied social psychology, Darwin's evolutionary observations, and his own investment and legal experience, rather than relying solely on academic psychology.
- Inference: The 25 tendencies include both innate evolutionary traits (like social hierarchy behavior) and culturally-acquired patterns, reflecting Munger's view that both nature and nurture contribute to misjudgment.
- Inference: Munger developed his psychological framework over decades, primarily through self-study and general reading rather than formal academic training, which may explain its practical, non-traditional structure.
- Inference: The 'lollapalooza tendency' refers to extreme results that occur when multiple psychological tendencies act in confluence, which Munger considers particularly dangerous.
- Inference: The Psychology of Human Misjudgment framework was influenced by Munger's multidisciplinary approach, combining insights from psychology, economics, evolution, and practical business experience.
