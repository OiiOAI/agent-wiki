# Psychology of Human Misjudgment

## Summary

The Psychology of Human Misjudgment is one of Charles T. Munger's key talks, later expanded into the eleventh talk in Poor Charlie's Almanack. This talk examines the psychological biases and mental shortcuts that lead humans to make systematic errors in judgment, forming a crucial component of Munger's multidisciplinary approach to understanding human behavior.

## Key facts

- The Psychology of Human Misjudgment is presented as a special edition that Charlie created especially for the book. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p7]
- The talk centers on fundamental truth, human accomplishment, human foibles, and the arduous path to wisdom. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p6]
- Munger identifies 25 psychological tendencies that cause misjudgment, listed and discussed in his talk. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p469]
- The grand general principle of social psychology states that cognition is ordinarily situation-dependent so that different situations often cause different conclusions, even when the same person is thinking in the same general subject area. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p468]
- Munger was influenced by Robert Cialdini's book 'Influence,' which he sent to all his children and for which he gave Cialdini a share of Berkshire Class A stock. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p463]
- Munger criticizes academic psychology textbooks for not adequately addressing intertwined psychological tendencies and for lacking discussion of antidotes to psychological-driven folly. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p461]
- Munger uses inversion (collecting instances of bad judgment and pondering ways to avoid them) as a key method for developing his psychological framework. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p458]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fact that Munger created a special edition of this talk for the book suggests it is one of his most important contributions to understanding decision-making.
- Inference: Munger synthesized psychological insights from non-clinical sources including Cialdini's applied social psychology, Darwin's evolutionary observations, and his own investment and legal experience, rather than relying solely on academic psychology.
- Inference: The 25 tendencies include both innate evolutionary traits (like social hierarchy behavior) and culturally-acquired patterns, reflecting Munger's view that both nature and nurture contribute to misjudgment.
