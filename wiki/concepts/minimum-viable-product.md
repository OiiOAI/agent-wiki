# Minimum Viable Product

## Summary

The Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is that version of the product that enables a full turn of the Build-Measure-Learn loop with a minimum amount of effort and the least amount of development time. The MVP lacks many features that may prove essential later, but its purpose is to start the process of learning as quickly as possible. It is not about building a crappy product; it's about maximizing learning per dollar spent and time elapsed.

## Key facts

- The MVP enables a full turn of the Build-Measure-Learn loop with minimum amount of effort and least amount of development time. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p74]
- Unlike prototypes or concept tests, the goal of an MVP is to test fundamental business hypotheses, not just answer product design or technical questions. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p87]
- The goal of the MVP is to begin the process of learning, not end it. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p87]
- Early adopters accept—in fact prefer—an 80 percent solution; they don't need a perfect solution to capture their interest. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p89]
- The video MVP for Dropbox drove the beta waiting list from 5,000 to 75,000 people overnight. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p93]
- The Concierge MVP involves personally serving early customers one-on-one to learn what works before investing in automation. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p93-96]
- The term minimum viable product has been in use since at least 2000 as part of various approaches to product development. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p275]
- An MVP is defined as any version of a product that can begin the process of learning, using the Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p275]
- Frank Robinson of PMDI refers to a version of the product that is the smallest needed to sell to potential customers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p275]
- Steve Blank uses the term 'earlyvangelist' to emphasize the evangelical powers of early customers who adopt an MVP. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p275]
