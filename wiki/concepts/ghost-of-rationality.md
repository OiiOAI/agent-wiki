# Ghost of Rationality

## Summary

The ghost of rationality is Phædrus's term for what underlies all of technology, modern science, and Western thought. He pursued this ghost throughout his life, eventually finding it and 'thrashing it good.' The ghost declares that the ultimate purpose of life—to keep alive—is impossible, yet this remains life's ultimate purpose, creating a circular meaninglessness that pervades modern existence.

## Key facts

- The ghost that Phædrus pursued was the ghost that underlies all of technology, all of modern science, all of Western thought. It was the ghost of rationality itself. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p71]
- The ghost declares that the ultimate purpose of life, which is to keep alive, is impossible, but that this is the ultimate purpose of life anyway, so that great minds struggle to cure diseases so that people may live longer, but only madmen ask why. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p71]
- One lives longer in order that he may live longer. There is no other purpose. That is what the ghost says. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p71]
- The cause of current social crises, Phædrus believed, is a genetic defect within the nature of reason itself. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p97]
