# Ethology

## Summary

The scientific study of animal behavior in natural habitats, developed in Europe in contrast to American behaviorism. Ethologists emphasized behavioral variety across species and the need to 'interview animals in their own language' (using their sensory modalities). Founded by Konrad Lorenz, Niko Tinbergen, and Karl von Frisch.

## Key facts

- Ethology emerged in Europe emphasizing behavioral variety and species-specific adaptations, unlike behaviorism's focus on universal laws. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p86]
- Ethologists studied animals in natural habitats, unlike behaviorists who used lab conditions ('studying rat social behavior in a cage is like studying dolphin swimming in a bathtub'). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p86]
- Sensory triggers of behavior include auditory (vocalizations), visual (postures, displays), olfactory (pheromones), and species-specific modalities like infrared sensing. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p87]
- The dominant sensory modality for each species has the most direct access to its limbic system—olfaction for rodents, vision for primates. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p87]
