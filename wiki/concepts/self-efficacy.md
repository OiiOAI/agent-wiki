# Self-Efficacy

## Summary

Self-efficacy is one's belief in their ability to influence an outcome. Research shows it significantly impacts exercise adoption and maintenance. The book argues that mini habits are a 'self-efficacy-generating machine' because daily successes from tiny requirements build confidence. Unlike motivation-based approaches that can fail repeatedly and crush self-efficacy, mini habits guarantee daily wins.

## Key facts

- Self-efficacy is defined as your belief in your ability to influence an outcome. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p71]
- A two-year randomized trial showed baseline self-efficacy had a significant impact on exercise adoption and maintenance. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p71]
- Albert Bandura clarified that 'Expectation alone will not produce desired performance if the component capabilities are lacking.'. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p71]
- Mini habits are described as a 'self-efficacy-generating machine' where daily successes train you to have high self-efficacy, starting with zero requirement. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p71]
- The author notes that repeated failures train an expectation of failing, which crushes self-efficacy and makes it hard to believe next time will be different. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p72]

## Inferences

- Inference: By guaranteeing daily success with mini habits, even people with depression, weak willpower, and repeated failures can rebuild self-efficacy through consistent small victories.
