# Body Budgeting

## Summary

Body budgeting is a concept from Lisa Feldman Barrett describing how the brain tracks and anticipates the use and replenishment of key resources (water, salt, glucose) for maintaining bodily life and functioning. Both the feeling of thirst and the feeling of having quenched thirst each reflect anticipatory processing—when we drink water, we feel less thirsty immediately even though it takes twenty minutes for water to reach the bloodstream.

## Key facts

- Lisa Feldman Barrett uses the metaphor of body budgeting to describe how the brain tracks and anticipates the use and replenishment of key resources including water, salt, and glucose for maintaining bodily functioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
- When we feel thirsty and take a drink, we immediately feel less thirsty even though water takes around twenty minutes to reach the bloodstream—the brain delivers the sensation of 'quenched thirst' right away because the sensation of thirst was activated in advance too. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
- Both the feeling of thirst and the feeling of having quenched thirst each reflect anticipatory processing rather than direct responses to physiological states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
- A human brain's sensory and motor neurons communicate through intermediaries called association neurons, which endow the nervous system with decision-making ability. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p246]
- The brain's control network, which helps select actions, is composed of association neurons and is always engaged, actively selecting actions, even when the experience of being in control is absent. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p247]
- The law defines deliberate choice as whether you feel in control of your thoughts and actions, but this fails to distinguish between ability to choose and subjective experience of choice—two are not the same in the brain. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p247]
- Emotions are not temporary deviations from rationality; instances of emotion are no more out of control than thoughts, perceptions, beliefs, or memories. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p247]
