# Body Budgeting

## Summary

Body budgeting is a concept from Lisa Feldman Barrett describing how the brain tracks and anticipates the use and replenishment of key resources (water, salt, glucose) for maintaining bodily life and functioning. Both the feeling of thirst and the feeling of having quenched thirst each reflect anticipatory processing—when we drink water, we feel less thirsty immediately even though it takes twenty minutes for water to reach the bloodstream.

## Key facts

- Lisa Feldman Barrett uses the metaphor of body budgeting to describe how the brain tracks and anticipates the use and replenishment of key resources including water, salt, and glucose for maintaining bodily functioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
- When we feel thirsty and take a drink, we immediately feel less thirsty even though water takes around twenty minutes to reach the bloodstream—the brain delivers the sensation of 'quenched thirst' right away because the sensation of thirst was activated in advance too. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
