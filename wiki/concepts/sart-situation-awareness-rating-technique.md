# SART (Situation Awareness Rating Technique)

## Summary

SART (Situation Awareness Rating Technique) is a multidimensional self-rating scale developed through knowledge elicitation from experienced aircrew. It measures SA across 10 constructs organized into three domains: Attentional Demand, Attentional Supply, and Understanding. SART is one of the most thoroughly tested subjective SA techniques, demonstrating sensitivity to task difficulty and operator experience, though concerns remain about test-retest reliability and potential confounding with workload measurement.

## Key facts

- SART was developed without using an a priori definition of SA; instead, the definition was derived from information gathered from experienced aircrew. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p133]
- The 10 SART constructs were identified through knowledge elicitation techniques and were governed by factors including elicitation frequency, principal coordinate clustering, and intercorrelations. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p133]
- The three SART domains are: Attentional Demand (instability, variability, complexity of situation), Attentional Supply (arousal, spare mental capacity, concentration, division of attention), and Understanding (information quantity, information quality, familiarity). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p134]
- The 3-D SART uses a continuous 100-millimeter line with endpoints low (0 mm) to high (100 mm). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p134]
- Current 10-D SART implementations employ a 7-point rating scale for each of the 10 generic constructs. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p134]
- SART ratings can be combined using the algorithm SA(calc) = Understanding - (Demand - Supply). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p135]
- SART showed sensitivity to task difficulty and operator experience in a study where RAF pilots rated a videotaped air combat sequence. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p136]
- A disadvantage of SART is that critics argue including workload elements within the SA scale confounds the measure of SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p137]
- SART is a multidimensional, immediate, and absolute rating technique for assessing SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p250]
- Twelve out of 20 experiments using subjective SA ratings employed the SART technique. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p250]
- SART was the most reliably sensitive SA measure in the current database based on meta-analytic results. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p250]

## Inferences

- Inference: The SART developers' decision to derive constructs from aircrew rather than theory suggests an empirical, user-centered design approach.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The chapter notes that the necessity and sufficiency of the 10 general constructs has not been clearly established, leaving room for scale refinement.
