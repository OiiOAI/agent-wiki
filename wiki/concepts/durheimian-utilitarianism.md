# Durkheimian utilitarianism

## Summary

Durkheimian utilitarianism is Haidt's proposed moral framework that combines utilitarian calculations of collective welfare with Durkheim's insights about the essential role of group solidarity, sacredness, and ritual in human flourishing. It addresses what Haidt sees as utilitarianism's blind spot regarding groups.

## Key facts

- Haidt argues that utilitarianism tends to be 'high systemizers who focus on individuals and have difficulty seeing groups,' missing Durkheim's insights about how groups bind and sustain human well-being. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p415]
- The framework is pluralist rather than monist, following Berlin in believing there are multiple and sometimes conflicting goods that cannot be reduced to a single dimension. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p415]
- Haidt endorses rule utilitarianism (creating systems and rules that produce the greatest total good over the long run) rather than act utilitarianism (maximizing utility in each individual case). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p415]
- The framework addresses the critique that Durkheimian societies can be oppressive to outsiders (such as gay people in conservative religious communities) by advocating pluralism. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p413]

## Inferences

- Inference: Durkheimian utilitarianism represents Haidt's attempt to create a policy framework that can incorporate conservative insights about community and binding values alongside liberal concerns for individual welfare.
