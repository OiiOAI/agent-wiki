# Opponent Brain Systems for Reward and Punishment Learning

## Summary

Four main hypotheses exist for how the brain implements opponent systems for reward and punishment learning. Hypothesis 1 proposes that dopamine dips encode negative prediction errors. Hypothesis 2 proposes a separate population of dopamine neurons encoding punishments. Hypothesis 3 proposes serotonin as the opponent neuromodulator. Hypothesis 4 proposes that insular and amygdala circuits mediate punishment learning. Causal evidence from drug and lesion studies provides mixed support across these hypotheses.

## Key facts

- Dopamine neuron firing rates parametrically encode reward prediction error, with bursts for positive RPE and pauses for negative RPE. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p294]
- Parkinson's disease patients ON dopaminergic medication show improved reward learning but impaired punishment learning, while OFF medication they show the reverse pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p297]
- Tourette syndrome patients (hyperdopaminergic, treated with antidopaminergics) show the opposite double dissociation from PD patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p297]
- Presymptomatic Huntington disease patients show reduced punishment learning but intact reward learning, consistent with specific dorsal striatum involvement in punishment avoidance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p298]
- Computational analyses of Huntington disease data revealed that the dorsal striatum may be implicated in selecting between actions leading to negative outcomes rather than in learning per se. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p298]
- Insular lesions specifically impair punishment learning while sparing reward learning, demonstrating a critical role for the anterior insula in punishment processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p299]

## Inferences

- Inference: The double dissociation between PD (dopamine-low, worsened by medication withdrawal) and TS (dopamine-high, worsened by treatment) supports the hypothesis that dopamine dips are necessary for punishment learning, while dopamine bursts are necessary for reward learning.
- Inference: The finding that insular lesions impair punishment learning but not reward learning suggests that Hypothesis 4 (insular/amygdala circuits) has a necessary role distinct from dopamine-based systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The evidence for serotonin as an opponent neuromodulator is mixed; some studies found specific roles in punishment learning while others found no effect or even reward-specific effects.
- Uncertain: The extent to which a distinct population of dopamine neurons positively encodes punishments (Hypothesis 2) versus DA dips encoding negative prediction errors (Hypothesis 1) remains debated.
