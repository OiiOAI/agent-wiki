# Functional Connectivity

## Summary

Functional connectivity refers to the correlation of neural activity between brain regions over time, estimated using fMRI. If two regions show correlated activity, they may be sharing information or participating in the same process. Resting-state functional connectivity measures correlations during task-free periods, revealing large-scale brain networks including the default mode network.

## Key facts

- Functional connectivity measures the correlation of BOLD activity between voxels or regions over time to understand how brain regions interact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p166]
- The default mode network includes the posterior medial cortex, lateral parietal cortex, and medial prefrontal cortex, and is active during resting states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p166]
- Resting functional connectivity can be linked indirectly to behavior by examining how it differs in disease or cognitive differences between people. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p167]
- Task-based functional connectivity is calculated after removing BOLD responses evoked by stimuli to isolate communication between regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p167]
