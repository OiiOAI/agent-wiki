# Cruel Optimism

## Summary

A concept identified by historian Lauren Berlant and applied to the attention crisis by Ronald Purser. It describes the tendency to offer upbeat individual solutions to problems with deep systemic causes. While appearing optimistic, it is 'cruel' because its solutions are inadequate and lead to self-blame when they inevitably fail, deflecting attention from the social and political changes that might actually work.

## Key facts

- Ronald Purser explained that cruel optimism takes a big problem with deep cultural causes and offers 'people, in upbeat language, a simplistic individual solution' that usually fails. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p162]
- Purser cited Stanford research showing top causes of stress are systemic factors: lack of health insurance, layoff threats, low autonomy, long hours, and unjust demands — not personal thinking failures. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p163]
- Purser criticized meditation-as-solution narratives as 'bullshit,' saying 'Tell it to Hispanic women working three jobs with four kids' — noting they come 'from a privileged position.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p163]
