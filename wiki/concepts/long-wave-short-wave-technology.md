# Long Wave and Short Wave (Technology)

## Summary

Technologies advance in waves: long waves extending over a century or more, and shorter waves driven by reductions in costs of delivering new benefits. The long wave of electricity began in 1820 with Oersted's discovery and continued through motors, power stations, electric cable cars, home electricity, appliances, computers, transistors, integrated circuits, microprocessors, home computers, the Internet, and mobile phones. Short waves occur when costs of new technology become low enough to permit commercialization—for example, LEDs appearing in calculators in 1970 and replacing incandescent bulbs in the mid-2000s. Strategists should know the nature of the long wave being considered, with the predictable technological future being about five to seven years off, beyond which a portfolio approach is needed.

## Key facts

- In 1820 Oersted saw that electric current could move physical objects, beginning the long wave of electricity that continued through motors, power stations, electric cable cars, home electricity, appliances, computers, transistors, integrated circuits, microprocessors, home computers, the Internet, and mobile phones. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p192]
- Short waves occur when costs of doing something new become low enough to permit commercialization. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p193]
- Philips developed dim LEDs in the early 1960s, calculators displayed them in 1970, and they replaced incandescent bulbs in the mid-2000s. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p194]
- The predictable technological future seems to be about five to seven years off; beyond that, strategists must take a portfolio approach. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p193]
- Fiber-optic cables were initially limited by signal losses to less than one mile; the US Department of Defense was the first buyer because fiber-optic was immune to electromagnetic-pulse waves. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p194]

## Inferences

- Inference: Long waves are observable but hard to predict; the 1967 Herman Kahn futures forecasting exercise failed to predict pocket computers and worldwide networks.
