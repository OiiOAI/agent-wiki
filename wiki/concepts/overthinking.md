# Overthinking

## Summary

Overthinking is the tendency to analyze routine tasks to death, undermining intuitive expertise. Wilson and Schooler's jelly experiment showed that requiring detailed justifications warped rankings. The solution: for practiced activities and repeated decisions, trust intuition; for complex novel problems, use rational reflection.

## Key facts

- The centipede who analyzed the best route step-by-step never moved and starved—illustrating overthinking paralysis. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p197]
- Jean Van de Velde lost the 1999 British Open with a triple-bogey on the final hole despite having a three-shot lead—classic overthinking. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p197]
- Students rating jelly while justifying choices had completely warped rankings compared to those simply tasting. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p198]
- For practiced activities and questions answered thousands of times, detailed reflection undermines intuitive ability. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p198]
