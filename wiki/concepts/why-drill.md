# The Why Drill

## Summary

The Why Drill is a questioning technique used to identify the core values behind desired habits. By repeatedly asking 'why?' until answers become circular, one finds the fundamental motivation. This ensures habits are sourced from personal values rather than external pressure, which would create internal resistance.

## Key facts

- The Why Drill involves asking 'why?' multiple times to identify the source of a desired habit until reaching the core value. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p87]
- The best habit ideas are sourced straight from life values, not from peer pressure or others' expectations. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p87]
- Trying to change based on another person or society's opinion creates massive internal resistance. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p87]
- The author gives an example where wanting to get up at 6 AM traced back to external pressure ('society looks down on me') rather than internal values, making it lower priority than writing. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p88]

## Inferences

- Inference: Habits built on authentic personal values will have lower resistance and higher long-term adherence than habits motivated by external expectations.
