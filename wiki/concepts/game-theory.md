# Game Theory

## Summary

Game Theory provides mathematical models for understanding decisions in social interactions. It offers rigorous tasks like the Ultimatum Game, Trust Game, and Prisoner's Dilemma that reveal systematic deviations from classical rational predictions, incorporating factors like reciprocity and fairness.

## Key facts

- Game Theory provides rigorous mathematical models for understanding situations where decision makers interact with one another. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p310]
- Classical Game Theory predicts Nash equilibria from which no player can increase their own payoff unilaterally, but observed behavior often deviates substantially from these predictions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p310]
- The Ultimatum Game measures strategic thinking in bargaining, with responders typically rejecting low offers despite the rational prediction to accept any positive offer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p312]
- The Dictator Game measures pure altruism, as the proposer sacrifices personal gain without possibility of rejection by the responder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p312]
- The Trust Game models reciprocal exchange of favors, revealing that most investors send money despite rational prediction of zero investment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p313]
- Coordination games like Matching Pennies provide insights into how individuals assess others' preferences and use Theory of Mind processes for strategic decision making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p315]
- Game theory was established as a field by John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern with their 1944 book 'Theory of Games and Economic Behavior'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- The original reviews that greeted 'Theory of Games' are quoted in the book's sixtieth anniversary edition. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- At least eleven Nobel laureates in economics have been cited for work connected to or influenced by game theory, including Harsanyi, Nash, Selten, Aumann, Schelling, Hurwicz, Maskin, Myerson, Roth, Shapley, and Tirole. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Colin Camerer's work combining neuroscience and game theory is referenced, including his TEDx Talk on monkeys and game theory. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
