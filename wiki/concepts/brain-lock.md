# Brain Lock

## Summary

Brain Lock is a neuroplasticity-based treatment developed by Jeffrey M. Schwartz for obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and related conditions. The treatment is based on the concept that OCD involves a 'lock' between three brain regions—the orbital frontal cortex, cingulate gyrus, and caudate nucleus—and teaches patients to 'unlock' this circuit through a four-step approach: relabel, refocus, reattribute, and reluminate. The method leverages brain plasticity to replace pathological obsessive-compulsive circuits with healthier ones by actively redirecting attention to positive activities.

## Key facts

- Brain Lock is a plasticity-based treatment developed by Jeffrey M. Schwartz for OCD and everyday worries, obsessions, compulsions, and bad habits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p175]
- The treatment works by changing the OCD circuit, unlocking the link between the orbital cortex and the cingulate, and normalizing the functioning of the caudate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p177]
- The four-step Brain Lock method includes relabeling (recognizing obsessions as OCD symptoms), refocusing (focusing on a positive activity), reattributing (understanding the cause is faulty brain circuitry), and reluminating (using the insight to motivate change). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p177]
- Brain Lock was the first treatment to use PET brain scans to both understand a disorder and develop a psychotherapy for it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p175]
- Brain scans showed that after treatment, the three parts of the brain that had been 'locked' and firing together in a hyperactive way began to fire separately in a normal way. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p182]
- Eighty percent of Schwartz's patients improved when using the Brain Lock method in combination with medication. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p182]
- The treatment does not aim to 'break' bad habits but to replace bad behaviors with better ones. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p177]
- The goal is to 'change the channel' to a new activity for fifteen to thirty minutes when having an OCD symptom. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p181]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Brain Lock treatment parallels Taub's Constraint-Induced (CI) approach to strokes by imposing a constraint (not acting on compulsions) and giving massed practice (thirty-minute segments of intensive focus).
- Inference: The technique works by growing new brain circuits that give pleasure and trigger dopamine release, which rewards the new activity and consolidates new neuronal connections, eventually competing with the older pathological network.
