# Separation of Tasks

## Summary

Separation of tasks is an Adlerian principle that distinguishes between what is one's own responsibility and what belongs to others. The key criterion is determining who ultimately receives the consequences of a decision. This framework prevents interpersonal relationship troubles caused by either intruding on others' tasks or having one's own tasks intruded upon. Once tasks are separated, individuals can provide assistance without controlling outcomes.

## Key facts

- All interpersonal relationship troubles are caused by intruding on other people's tasks or having one's own tasks intruded upon. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p146]
- The test for whose task something is: 'Think, Who ultimately is going to receive the result brought about by the choice that is made?'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p146]
- Separating tasks from others means one does not intrude on other people's tasks while still being ready to assist when requested. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p147]
- Adlerian psychology distinguishes between noninterference (not caring) and protecting (knowing but not intruding). [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p146]
- Separation of tasks means calmly delineating where one's own tasks end and another person's tasks begin, and not intervening in other people's tasks or allowing even a single person to intervene in one's own tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p154]
- Intervening in other people's tasks and taking on other people's tasks turns one's life into something heavy and full of hardship. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p153]
- With families there is less distance, so it is all the more necessary to consciously separate the tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p150]
- The act of believing is also the separation of tasks—one believes in one's partner, but how that person acts with regard to expectations and trust is other people's tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p150]

- Inference: This principle provides a practical framework for setting boundaries without abandoning relationships.
