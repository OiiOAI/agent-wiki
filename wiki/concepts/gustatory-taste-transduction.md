# Gustatory Taste Transduction

## Summary

The five basic taste qualities (sweet, bitter, salty, sour, umami) are detected by distinct receptor types in taste receptor cells. Sweet and umami use G protein-coupled T1R family receptors (T1R2+T1R3 for sweet, T1R1+T1R3 for umami), while bitter uses the T2R family (approximately 30 receptors). Salty taste uses epithelial sodium channels (ENaC), and sour uses the proton channel Otopetrin-1. Each taste modality is encoded by dedicated taste cells connected via labeled lines to specific behavioral responses.

## Key facts

- Sweet tastants are detected by a heteromeric receptor composed of T1R2 and T1R3 subunits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p745]
- Umami taste is detected by a combination of T1R1 and T1R3 subunits, with purine nucleotides acting as positive allosteric modulators. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p746]
- Bitter tastants are detected by approximately 30 T2R G protein-coupled receptors with high-affinity binding in the micromolar range. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p748]
- Sour taste is detected by Otopetrin-1, a proton-selective channel also involved in vestibular gravity sensing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p748]
- Appetitive salt concentrations are detected by ENaC in dedicated salt taste receptor cells, while high salt activates bitter and sour cells causing aversion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p748]
- Different taste modalities are detected by distinct subsets of taste cells expressing different receptor types connected via labeled lines to specific behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p748]
