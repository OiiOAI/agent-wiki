# Senility as Mindful Response Hypothesis

## Summary

Langer proposed the controversial hypothesis that senility might be a mindful response to an overly routinized environment—that bizarre thoughts and behaviors in 'senile' individuals could represent novel, mindful thinking that provides biological adaptive benefits, potentially including longevity. A 1984 study found those labeled senile lived significantly longer than those with only heart disease, though the paper was rejected by a major journal.

## Key facts

- Langer proposed that senility might be a mindful response to an overly routinized environment, with some bizarre behaviors potentially being mindful relief from redundancy. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p177]
- In 1984, Langer with colleagues Beck, Janoff-Bulman, and Timko found that those labeled senile lived significantly longer than those who only had heart disease. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p177]
- The paper was rejected by a major scientific journal with the reason that the 'journal didn't publish research in progress.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p177]
- Langer argued: 'Everything in science is in progress. There are no final answers; we are always learning new things about the mindful body.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p177]

## Inferences

- Inference: The rejection suggests that scientific institutions often resist findings that contradict established beliefs, even when the findings are empirically derived.
