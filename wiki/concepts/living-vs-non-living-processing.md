# The Living vs. The Non-Living

## Summary

A distinction whereby the right hemisphere shows affinity for living things and the left hemisphere for mechanical, non-living things and tools. This may relate to the right hemisphere's holistic processing (living things are unified wholes) and its empathic capacity, versus the left hemisphere's interest in manipulation and use.

## Key facts

- The right hemisphere is more concerned with living individuals than man-made objects, while the left hemisphere alone codes for non-living things. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p85]
- The left hemisphere has a particular affinity for words and concepts for tools, man-made things, and mechanisms; it codes for tools and machines even in left-handers. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p86]
- Left-hemisphere damage renders the sufferer incapable of using a hammer and nail or a key and padlock, whereas right-hemisphere damage leaves the ability to use simple tools unaltered. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p86]
- Food and musical instruments sort with the living rather than the non-living, presumably because of the intimate way they take part in the life of the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p86]
- The body as such is a right-hemisphere entity, whereas body 'parts' are the province of the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p86]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The evidence for strict living/non-living segregation is noted as potentially complicated by confounding factors in studies.
