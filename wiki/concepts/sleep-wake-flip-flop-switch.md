# Sleep-Wake Flip-Flop Switch

## Summary

The sleep-wake flip-flop switch is a neural circuit composed of mutually inhibitory neurons that produces rapid and complete transitions between sleep and wake states. The switch comprises sleep-promoting GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral preoptic nucleus (VLPO) and wake-promoting neurons in the ascending arousal system. When one side of the switch becomes active, it inhibits the other side, producing a stable state. This arrangement prevents intermediate drowsy states that would be behaviorally disadvantageous, as an animal would be vulnerable while transitioning.

## Key facts

- The ventrolateral preoptic neurons receive inhibitory inputs from neurons throughout the arousal system, creating mutually inhibitory connections between sleep-promoting and wake-promoting systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- Mutually inhibitory connections between VLPO neurons and arousal system neurons create properties similar to an electrical flip-flop switch, producing rapid transitions between sleep and wake states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- A similar flip-flop switch between GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray and subceruleus neurons governs transitions between REM and non-REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- Most animals spend nearly their entire day either clearly awake or asleep, with very little time spent in transitional states, due to the rapid state transitions produced by the flip-flop switch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]

## Inferences

- Inference: Narcolepsy can be understood as a destabilization of the flip-flop switch, where loss of orexinergic excitation allows inappropriate transitions into sleep or REM sleep states.
