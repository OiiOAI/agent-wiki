# Wrong Causal Model

## Summary

Using incorrect analogies for diagnosis leads to strategic failure. The case of smartphones illustrates this: Microsoft, Nokia, and analysts predicted iPhone failure using the PC industry analogy, missing that the smartphone market would be driven by consumer demand for Web-enabled devices rather than enterprise standardization.

## Key facts

- Microsoft CEO Steve Ballmer predicted no chance the iPhone would get significant market share, preferring 60-70 percent of phones running Windows Mobile. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p141]
- Nokia's chief strategist Anssi Vanjoki predicted the iPhone would remain a niche product like the Macintosh. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p141]
- PC clone competition arose because IBM made errors in design and intellectual property protection, a situation that did not repeat in smartphones. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p142]
- Business demand in the phone market was already sated by BlackBerry; it was consumer demand for Web-enabled smartphones that exploded. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p143]

## Inferences

- Inference: The trick to using analogy well is access to more than one or two additional situations and understanding how the logic maps to the current context.
