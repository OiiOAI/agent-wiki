# Protein Synthesis in Neurons

## Summary

Protein synthesis in neurons occurs similarly to other secretory cells, with proteins synthesized in the cell body from mRNA. Secretory and membrane proteins are translated in conjunction with the rough endoplasmic reticulum and modified in the Golgi complex before being transported to their destinations.

## Key facts

- Secretory and membrane proteins are synthesized and modified in the endoplasmic reticulum, with most polypeptides translocated across the rough ER membrane during synthesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p135]
- Proteins from the endoplasmic reticulum are carried in transport vesicles to the Golgi complex where they undergo modifications including N-linked and O-linked glycosylation, phosphorylation, and sulfation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p137]
- Post-translational modifications include phosphorylation, ubiquitination, glycosylation, and acylation, which regulate protein function, localization, and degradation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p136]
- Some protein synthesis occurs in dendrites, including mRNA for actin, MAP2, and the alpha-subunit of Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase, important for local synaptic modifications. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p134]
