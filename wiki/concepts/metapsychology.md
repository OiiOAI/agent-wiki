# Metapsychology

## Summary

Metapsychology is Freud's term for the comprehensive psychological description of mental processes in their dynamic, topographical, and economic aspects. Freud proposed that when all three perspectives are applied to a psychical process, this constitutes a metapsychological presentation. The dynamic view examines the forces involved (drives, cathexes, anticathaxes); the topographical view locates processes within the systems of the mind (Ucs, Pcs, Cs); the economic view traces the vicissitudes of amounts of excitation. The metapsychological papers, including 'Drives and their Vicissitudes,' 'Repression,' and 'The Unconscious' (all 1915), represent Freud's most theoretical and systematic works, providing the foundational framework for psychoanalytic theory.
