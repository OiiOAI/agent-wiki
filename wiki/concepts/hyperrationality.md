# Hyperrationality

## Summary

Hyperrationality is a mental disturbance in which the victim attempts to handle all decisions using purely rational and analytical reasoning, eventually leading to paralysis by analysis. Klein uses vision as a metaphor: rational analysis is like foveal vision—powerful for fine discrimination but insufficient for orientation and useless at night.

## Key facts

- Hyperrationality is defined as the attempt to handle all decisions and problems on a purely rational basis, relying on only logical and analytical reasoning. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p314]
- Rational thinking requires decomposition (breaking tasks into units), decontextualization (finding context-independent elements), calculation, and description open to public scrutiny. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p316]
- Rational thinking has limitations: there are no natural 'basic elements' for decomposition, rules are ambiguous about when antecedent conditions are met, and calculations are hard to initialize. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p317]
- Formal methods run into combinatorial explosions when considering many factors and working out all permutations. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p318]
- Cherniak showed that a belief system containing only 138 logically independent propositions would overwhelm the time resources of a supercomputer running for twenty billion years to check for consistency. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p320]
- Logic is indifferent to truth—it roots out inconsistencies but does not consider whether beliefs are true. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p322]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hyperrationality becomes problematic when experiential sources of power (intuition, pattern recognition, mental simulation) are ignored.
