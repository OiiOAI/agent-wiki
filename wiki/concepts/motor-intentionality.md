# Motor Intentionality

## Summary

Motor intentionality is the kind of bodily intentionality that characterizes habitual actions and bodily skills—what Dreyfus calls absorbed skillful coping. Rather than having a subject-object structure where one represents an independent object and acts upon it, motor intentionality is 'at its object rather than positing it.' Consciousness in skillful action is characterized by 'I can' rather than 'I think.'

## Key facts

- Merleau-Ponty calls this kind of bodily intentionality 'motor intentionality,' explaining that in grasping something we direct ourselves toward it and thus our action is intentional, but the action does not refer to the thing by representing its objective and determinate features; it refers to it pragmatically in the light of a contextual motor goal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p245]
- 'Consciousness is in the first place not a matter of 'I think that' but of 'I can'' (Merleau-Ponty 1962, p. 137). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p307]
- The intentional arc subtending the life of consciousness integrates sensibility and motility, perception and action, and is neither purely first-personal nor purely third-personal, neither mental nor physical; it is an existential structure prior to and more fundamental than these abstractions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p246]
