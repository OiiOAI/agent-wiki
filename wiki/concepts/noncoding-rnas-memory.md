# Noncoding RNAs in Memory

## Summary

Noncoding RNAs including microRNAs (miRNAs) and PIWI-interacting RNAs (piRNAs) are regulated by neuronal activity and contribute to long-term memory consolidation through transcriptional and posttranscriptional regulation of gene expression.

## Key facts

- miRNA-124 normally constrains serotonin-induced synaptic facilitation by inhibiting translation of CREB-1 mRNA; serotonin inhibits miRNA-124 synthesis, enabling CREB-1-mediated transcription. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1370]
- piRNA-F increases in response to serotonin, leading to methylation of the CREB-2 promoter, reducing CREB-2 gene transcription and consolidating long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1371]
- Serotonin regulates both piRNA and microRNA in a coordinated fashion: serotonin rapidly decreases miRNA-124 levels while, after a delay, increasing piRNA-F levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1371]

## Inferences

- Inference: The coordinated regulation of miRNAs and piRNAs by serotonin provides a mechanism for fine-tuning gene expression during memory consolidation, with miRNAs acting rapidly and piRNAs acting with a delay.
