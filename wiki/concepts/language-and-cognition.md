# Language and Cognition

## Summary

The cognitive neuroscience perspective that language is key to the complex, rich, and unique nature of human cognitive mental life, enabling abstract conceptual thought, hierarchical relational reasoning, inner speech for deliberation, and cultural transmission of knowledge.

## Key facts

- Lev Vygotsky made famous the concept of inner speech, noting it enables children to develop concepts, achieve identity, and construct their world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p234]
- Daniel Dennett stated that language 'lays down tracks on which thoughts can travel' and that adding language creates a mind so different from one without it that calling both 'minds' is a mistake. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p235]
- Edmund Rolls noted that syntax enables humans to plan actions and evaluate consequences by anticipating many steps ahead without actually performing the actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p236]
- Only humans can flexibly use sounds or symbols to spontaneously indicate things about present, past, and/or future, and use syntax to plan cooperative action. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p236]
- Oren Kolodny and Shimon Edelman proposed that language arose as an exaptation—cobbled together by coupling existing neural mechanisms for communication, serial cognition, and tool use. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p239]
- Cecilia Heyes proposed that human-unique cognitive gadgets like mind reading, imitation, and language emerge through social learning and culture, not genetics alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p238]

## Inferences

- Inference: Language may have evolved both as a biological adaptation and as a cultural tool, with social transmission amplifying cognitive capacities beyond what genetics alone would allow.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The relative contributions of biological evolution versus cultural evolution to human language and cognition remain unresolved.
