# Computational Phenotyping

## Summary

Computational phenotyping is the process of characterizing subjective behavior in terms of the computations that generate it, by fitting Active Inference models to observed behavioral data. This approach enables the identification of parameters representing prior beliefs that subjects would have to hold for their behavior to be Bayes optimal.

## Key facts

- Computational phenotypes may be used in combination with other measures to establish links between neuroimaging findings and function, or used alone in forecasting behaviors following therapeutic intervention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p180]
- Computational phenotyping holds promise in computational psychiatry, neuropsychology, and neurology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p180]
- The approach enables testing hypotheses about the model the brain uses and phenotyping individuals based on prior beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p180]
