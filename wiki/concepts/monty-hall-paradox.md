# Monty Hall Paradox

## Summary

The Monty Hall paradox is a famous probability puzzle named after the host of the TV game show "Let's Make a Deal." The annotated bibliography in Chapter 6 of "The Book of Why" traces its history in probability textbooks and its connection to Bayesian reasoning.

## Key facts

- The Monty Hall paradox appears in many introductory books on probability theory including Grinstead and Snell (1998, p. 136) and Lindley (2014, p. 201). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3663-3664]
- The equivalent "three prisoners dilemma" was used to demonstrate the inadequacy of non-Bayesian approaches in Pearl (1988, pp. 58–62). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3665-3666]
- Tierney (July 21, 1991) and Crockett (2015) document the story of Marilyn vos Savant's column on the Monty Hall paradox and the embarrassing comments she received from so-called experts. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3667-3668]
- Tierney's article tells what Monty Hall himself thought of the controversy over his puzzle. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3668-3669]
