# Navy Accountability Model

## Summary

Munger's endorsement of the Navy's approach where captains are accountable for outcomes regardless of fault—a ship going aground ends one's naval career even with no negligence. He advocates for similar no-fault accountability in civil contexts to minimize system failures.

## Key facts

- The Navy system: if a captain turns the ship over to a competent first mate who grounds it through no fault of the captain's, the captain's naval career is over despite no personal negligence. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p270]
- Napoleon liked luckier generals—Navy practice doesn't care about fault, only outcomes; nobody's interested in your fault, it's just a rule for the good of all. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p271]
- Munger favors no-fault rules because civilization works better with them, even though such rules are anathema to law schools that prefer due process. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p271]
