# Hebbian Learning

## Summary

Hebbian learning, proposed by Donald Hebb in 1949, explains how connections between neurons become altered in their relative signal transduction 'strength' in response to the patterning of signals. The theory posits that 'neurons that fire together, wire together.' Hebbian learning rules are widely used in neural net computing paradigms and apply both to processes modifying synaptic strengths during learning and to synapse preservation versus loss during early development, lending support to Darwinian theories of neural information processing.

## Key facts

- Hebb (1949) proposed that connections could become altered in their relative signal transduction 'strength' in response to the patterning of signals carried on these connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Many neural net computing paradigms employ Hebbian learning rules and demonstrate their effectiveness as a substrate for learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Hebbian learning theory applies not only to processes modifying synaptic strengths during learning, but also to those determining which synapses are preserved and which are lost during early development. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- The continuity of mechanisms at these two levels of information generation lends support for a more global Darwinian theory of the logic of neural information processing, in which early wiring and later learning processes are considered two ends of the same spectrum. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- In 1949, Donald Hebb published 'Organization of Behavior,' proposing that when an axon of cell A is near enough to excite cell B and repeatedly takes part in firing it, some growth process occurs such that A's efficiency in firing B is increased. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p198]
- Hebb's key insight was that it was not sufficient that a synapse should be strengthened merely because it was active—he wanted a mechanism that only worked when two activities were associated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p198]
- Such a mechanism, or one somewhat resembling it, is now called 'Hebbian.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p198]
- In Hopfield networks, if two units have the same output, the weights of the reciprocal connections between them are made equal to +1; if opposite outputs, weights are made equal to -1. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p198]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether there are transient synaptic changes that are truly Hebbian (requiring both pre- and postsynaptic activity) remains to be investigated.
