# Female Sexual Desire After Marriage

## Summary

This chunk examines why married women often experience a diminishment of sexual desire within marriage while potentially experiencing intense desire in affairs. Drawing on researcher Marta Meana's findings and author Dalma Heyn's concept of 'erotic silence,' it explores how the roles of wife and mother can conflict with the self-absorption required for erotic pleasure, and how affairs can function as acts of self-reclamation.

## Key facts

- Researcher Marta Meana challenges the assumption that women's sexuality is primarily dependent on relational connectedness, suggesting women may be 'just as turned on as men by the novel, the illicit, the raw, the anonymous.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p155]
- Meana posits that 'women choose good relationships over sexual pleasure' when forced to prioritize. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p155]
- Dalma Heyn uses 'erotic silence' to describe a 'deadening of pleasure and vitality' that happens to some women after marriage, explaining that 'a woman's sexuality depends on her authenticity and self-nurturance.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p157]
- Heyn notes that marriage and motherhood demand selflessness that is 'at odds with the inherent selfishness of desire,' making it harder for women to feel 'spontaneous, sexually expressive, and carefree.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p157]
- Meana and colleague Karen E. Sims identify three 'core themes' dragging on female sexual desire: institutionalization of relationships, overfamiliarity replacing individuality, and the desexualizing nature of roles like mother and house manager. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p157]
- Sims and Meana conclude that 'female sexual desire requires a balance between opposing impulses... of comfort and freedom, of security and risk, of intimacy and individuality.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p157]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel implies that the 'erotic silence' phenomenon explains why some women who seem sexually disinterested at home can become intensely sexual with lovers—the context and self-narrative matter more than the specific partner.
