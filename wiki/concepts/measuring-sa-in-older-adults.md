# Measuring SA in Older Adults

## Summary

Measuring SA acquisition in older adults requires consideration of age-related changes in physiological, perceptual, and cognitive systems that may impact validity. Physiological measures (heart rate, EEG, eye tracking) and reaction time measures must account for normative age changes. Domain-specific assessment techniques may better capture older experts' capabilities than domain-general tests.

## Key facts

- Physiological measures of SA using heart rate, eye blinks, and saccade latencies can be impacted by age-related health conditions and normative changes such as reduced maximal heart rate. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p307]
- A simple reaction time measure for a 60-year-old is approximately 20% slower than for a 20-year-old, requiring evaluation of whether performance differences reflect general slowing rather than process-specific effects. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p307]
- Standardized domain-general tests may penalize older experts and better predict SA in younger than older adults due to greater dissociation between general and domain-specific skills with age. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p308]
- Indirect memory tests show much smaller aging effects than direct memory tests, potentially offering less biased SA assessment. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p309]

## Inferences

- Inference: When comparing SA across age groups, adjusting for baseline rates or using age-based norms can reduce measurement biases from physiological and cognitive age changes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The appropriate balance between absolute and relative standards for SA assessment depends on whether the goal is evaluating minimum competency or comparing age-related variation.
