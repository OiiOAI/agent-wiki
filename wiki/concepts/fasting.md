# Fasting

## Summary

Fasting encompasses various eating patterns that involve periods of not eating, including time-restricted feeding (16:8, 18:6), alternate-day fasting, and prolonged fasting. While fasting triggers beneficial physiological mechanisms including lowered insulin, autophagy activation, and mTOR inhibition, it also carries risks including muscle loss and reduced activity levels. The author recommends caution and precision in using fasting-based interventions.

## Key facts

- Fasting causes insulin to drop dramatically due to no incoming calories triggering an insulin response. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p347]
- Fasting activates autophagy, the cellular recycling process, and activates FOXO cellular repair genes. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p347]
- The original 16/8 time-restricted feeding model came from mouse studies where a 16-hour fast for a mouse is akin to a multiday fast for humans. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p348]
- A 2020 clinical trial by Ethan Weiss found no weight loss or cardiometabolic benefits in 116 volunteers on a 16/8 eating pattern. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p348]
- Alternate-day fasting studies found that subjects lost more lean mass than those simply eating 25% fewer calories every day. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p349]
- Fasting can reset crashed metabolism in severely metabolically dysfunctional patients when no other dietary intervention has worked. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p352]
- Due to muscle loss risks, fasting should be considered only for patients in extremis when no other options exist. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p352]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The author's recommendation against frequent prolonged fasting is based on limited human trial data and clinical observation rather than extensive RCTs.
