# Strategy Kernel

## Summary

The kernel of a strategy contains three essential elements: (1) a diagnosis that defines or explains the nature of the challenge, (2) a guiding policy for dealing with the challenge, and (3) a set of coherent actions designed to carry out the guiding policy. This structure acts as a guide on reasoning and a check against generating bad strategy.

## Key facts

- A diagnosis simplifies the often overwhelming complexity of reality by identifying certain aspects of the situation as critical. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p37]
- A guiding policy is an overall approach chosen to cope with or overcome the obstacles identified in the diagnosis—it is like a signpost marking the direction forward. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p37]
- Coherent actions are feasible coordinated policies, resource commitments, and actions designed to carry out the guiding policy. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p15]
- A good diagnosis names or classifies the situation, linking facts into patterns and suggesting that more attention be paid to some issues and less to others. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p37]
- An especially insightful diagnosis can transform one's view of the situation, bringing a radically different perspective to bear. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p37]

## Inferences

- Inference: Without a diagnosis, one cannot evaluate alternative guiding policies; without working through to at least the first round of action, one cannot be sure the guiding policy can be implemented.
