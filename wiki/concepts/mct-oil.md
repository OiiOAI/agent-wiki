# MCT Oil

## Summary

Medium-chain triglycerides (MCTs) are a rare dietary fat abundant in coconut oil, palm oil, goat's milk, and human breast milk. Upon consumption, MCTs go straight to the liver to be converted to ketones, elevating blood ketone levels even in a non-fasted state. Of the 14 grams of fat in a typical tablespoon of coconut oil, 62 to 70 percent is pure MCT. MCT oil may be particularly useful for people with Alzheimer's disease who develop a sweet tooth as their brains struggle for energy.

## Key facts

- MCTs are abundant in coconut oil, palm oil, goat's milk, and human breast milk, and upon consumption go straight to the liver to be converted to ketones. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p212]
- Of the 14 grams of fat in a typical tablespoon of coconut oil, 62 to 70 percent is pure MCT, most of which is lauric acid. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p212]
- In a non-ketotic, non-fasted state, the brain could potentially derive 5 to 10 percent of its fuel from MCT-derived ketones. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p212]
- MCT oil and ketone supplements may provide serious cognitive benefits but are not ideal for weight loss since they prevent the body from using its own fat. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p213]
