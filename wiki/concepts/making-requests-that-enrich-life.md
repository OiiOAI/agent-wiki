# Making Requests That Enrich Life

## Summary

The fourth component of NVC addresses expressing what we would like to request of others to enrich life. Key principles include using positive action language (stating what we want rather than what we don't want), being specific and concrete rather than vague or abstract, and distinguishing requests from demands. When requests are heard as demands, others see only two options: submission or rebellion.

## Key facts

- We should express what we are requesting rather than what we are not requesting, as negative requests confuse and provoke resistance. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p107]
- Requests should be worded in the form of concrete actions that others can undertake, avoiding vague, abstract, or ambiguous phrasing. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p109]
- Requests are received as demands when others believe they will be blamed or punished if they do not comply, leading them to see only two options: submission or rebellion. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p121]
- A request becomes a demand if the speaker then criticizes, judges, or lays a guilt trip when the request is not complied with. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p122]
- We demonstrate a genuine request by showing empathy toward the other person's needs when they don't comply. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p123]
- Since the message sent is not always the message received, we should ask for a reflection to verify accurate understanding. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p115]
- The objective of NVC is not to change people to get our way, but to establish relationships based on honesty and empathy that will fulfill everyone's needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p124]
