# Moral Tribes

## Summary

Joshua Greene's framework distinguishing two types of moral dilemmas: 'Me versus Us' (tragedy of the commons) and 'Us versus Them' (tragedy of commonsense morality). Greene argues that different cognitive processes are suited to each: rapid intuitive processing works for cooperation within groups, while deliberative reasoning is needed for navigating moral conflicts between groups with incompatible value systems.

## Key facts

- The tragedy of the commons involves getting individuals in groups to not be selfish—a problem solvable through cooperation and social species' evolved mechanisms. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p521]
- In the tragedy of commonsense morality, each group has tightly reasoned structures justifying their approach as morally correct, making intergroup conflicts often reducible to cultural disagreements about whose 'right' is righter. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p521]
- When facing Me-versus-Us moral dilemmas of resisting selfishness, rapid intuitions are good, honed by evolutionary selection for cooperation in a sea of green-beard markers. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p524]
- When doing moral decision making during Us-versus-Them scenarios, intuitions should be kept as far away as possible; instead, one should think, reason, question, and take their perspective. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p524]
