# Latency Period

## Summary

Freud's concept that traumatic experiences (individual or collective) are followed by a period of apparent normalcy before symptoms emerge. Applied to Jewish religious history, this explains how Mosaic monotheism disappeared after Moses' murder yet re-emerged centuries later through the Prophets.

## Key facts

- In Jewish religious history, there was a long period after defection from Mosaic religion during which no sign of the monotheist idea was detectable—this is the latency period. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7235]
- The latency period in neurosis is explained as occurring either at puberty (when drives intensify) or later when the ego's defensive alterations prove hindrances to new life tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7244]
- Freud proposes that the tradition of Moses persisted in obscure form through centuries, eventually forcing its way into later revisions and acquiring decisive influence on the thoughts and actions of the people. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7237]

## Inferences

- Inference: The latency period in Jewish religion parallels the traumatic neurosis model—Moses' murder was the trauma, the Kadesh compromise and Yahweh worship the defence, and the Prophets' revival the delayed emergence.
