# Oxytocin and Brain Unlearning

## Summary

Oxytocin, called the 'commitment neuromodulator,' is released when lovers connect and during parenting. Neuroscientist Walter Freeman proposes that oxytocin melts down existing neuronal connections that underlie existing attachments, allowing new attachments to form. Unlike dopamine which induces excitement, oxytocin induces a calm, warm mood that increases tender feelings and attachment, and may trigger trust.

## Key facts

- Oxytocin is released when lovers connect and make love, in humans during orgasm, and when couples parent and nurture their children. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p127]
- Oxytocin appears to attach children to parents, and the neurons that control its secretion may have a critical period of their own. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p127]
- Oxytocin is called an amnestic hormone because of its ability to wipe out learned behavior, allowing new attachments to be formed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p128]
- Freeman proposes that oxytocin melts down existing neuronal connections that underlie existing attachments, so new attachments can be formed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p128]
- Whereas dopamine induces excitement and triggers sexual arousal, oxytocin induces a calm, warm mood that increases tender feelings and attachment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p128]
- Studies show that oxytocin triggers trust; people who sniff oxytocin are more prone to trust others with their money. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p128]

## Inferences

- Inference: Oxytocin's role as an 'eraser' of old neural patterns explains why falling in love can feel like a 'loss of identity' - massive neural reorganization is occurring.
- Inference: The combination of dopamine (novelty/excitement) and oxytocin (attachment/unlearning) creates the neurochemical foundation for romantic love and long-term bonding.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: There is some dispute over whether oxytocin is solely responsible for this burst of learning and unmaking of existing attachments.
- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which oxytocin affects human bonding require more research.
