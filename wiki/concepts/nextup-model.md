# NEXTUP model of dreaming

## Summary

NEXTUP stands for Network Exploration to Understand Possibilities. The model proposes that the human brain needs to dream as part of offline memory processing during sleep. Rather than simply consolidating memories, the brain extracts meaning and patterns from daily experiences, and dreaming represents the conscious manifestation of this process.

## Key facts

- NEXTUP stands for Network Exploration to Understand Possibilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p9]
- The model is described as showing why the human brain needs to dream and offering new answers to what dreams are, where they come from, what they mean, and what they are for. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p9]
- The authors propose that dreams are psychologically and neurologically important and meaningful experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p8]
- NEXTUP proposes that the brain uses downtime to identify ongoing concerns or incomplete processes for later processing during sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p138]
- The default mode network (DMN) is active during mind wandering and appears to identify concerns for later sleep-dependent processing, providing a mechanism for determining what's NEXTUP. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p139]
- In REM sleep, weak associations are preferentially activated due to the shutdown of noradrenaline, allowing the brain to explore unexpected connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p136]
- During nonREM sleep, only strong associations are activated, searching for more obviously related episodic memories from the recent past rather than weak, remote associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p136]
- REM dreams are longer and more vivid, emotional, and bizarre than nonREM dreams, with fewer episodic memory sources and more semantic memory sources. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p141]
- NEXTUP suggests that dreams create narrative simulations allowing the brain to react to concerns, with the brain tracking both how the dreamer responds to dream scenarios and how the dream world reacts to the dreamer. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p161]
- The normal sequence of sleep stages across the night reflects the brain's need to seek out weaker and weaker associations, with nonREM decreasing and REM increasing as the night progresses. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p142]

## Inferences

- Inference: The NEXTUP model positions dreaming as a functional byproduct of memory processing, not merely random neural firing.
- Inference: NEXTUP may explain why recurrent dreams persist—once a metaphor for a concern is established, the brain returns to it when similar concerns are tagged for processing.
- Inference: The model predicts that smartphones and constant media consumption may interfere with DMN tagging during waking, potentially contributing to insomnia.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which NEXTUP determines which memories are worth exploring remain incompletely characterized.
