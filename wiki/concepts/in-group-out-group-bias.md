# In-Group Out-Group Bias

## Summary

In-group out-group bias is the tendency to favor members of one's own group over outsiders. Henri Tajfel's experiment found strangers allocated to groups at random (by coin toss) still preferred members of their own group. Stereotypes and prejudices stem from the out-group homogeneity bias.

## Key facts

- Henri Tajfel found strangers allocated to groups by coin toss still found each other more agreeable than members of other groups, despite being strangers with no real connection. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p175]
- We perceive people outside our own group to be more similar than they actually are (out-group homogeneity bias), leading to stereotypes and prejudices. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p176]
- The goal of military training is to forge soldiers together as 'brothers' — pseudo-kinship that can lead to laying down life for a random group. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p176]
