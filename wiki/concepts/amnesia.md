# Amnesia

## Summary

Amnesia is the inability to establish lasting memories that are available for conscious retrieval. The word originates from Greek 'amnesia,' meaning forgetfulness or loss of memory. Amnesic patients cannot turn immediately present experiences into lasting memories. This condition may be permanent or temporary and typically stems from brain insult such as encephalitis, stroke, head injury, or surgery. Henry Molaison developed permanent amnesia following bilateral medial temporal-lobe resection. The condition involves loss of the ability to encode, store, or retrieve information in long-term memory, while short-term memory and other cognitive abilities may remain intact.
