# False-Consensus Effect

## Summary

The false-consensus effect is the tendency to overestimate how much others share our beliefs and attitudes. Lee Ross found students willing to wear sandwich boards assumed 62% of others would agree, while those who refused assumed 67% of others would find it too stupid.

## Key facts

- Lee Ross found students willing to wear sandwich boards assumed 62% of others would volunteer, while those who refused assumed 67% would find it too stupid — both imagined themselves in the popular majority. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p171]
- The false-consensus effect has evolutionary value: whoever seemed confident and convincing attracted disproportionate resources and increased chances of passing on genes. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p172]
