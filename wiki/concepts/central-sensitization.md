# Central Sensitization

## Summary

Central sensitization is a prolonged increase in the excitability of dorsal horn neurons following repeated C-fiber activation, similar to long-term potentiation in memory circuits. It involves NMDA receptors and results in allodynia (pain from innocuous stimuli) and hyperalgesia (exaggerated pain responses).

## Key facts

- Central sensitization involves activation of NMDA-type glutamate receptors on dorsal horn neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p528]
- Repeated C-fiber firing causes a progressive enhancement of dorsal horn neuron responses termed 'wind-up'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p528]
- Peripheral nerve injury activates microglia, which release mediators that enhance neuronal excitability and reduce GABAergic inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p530]
- Central sensitization contributes to neuropathic pain and mechanical allodynia following nerve injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p530]
