# Immanent Purposiveness

## Summary

Immanent purposiveness is the constitutive purposiveness of autopoietic systems that is neither determined by something external (extrinsic) nor a nonrelational property of something internal (intrinsic in a misleading sense), but rather a property the whole system possesses because of its organizational structure. It manifests in two complementary modes: (1) identity—the production and maintenance of a dynamic self despite material change, and (2) sense-making—the active interpretation of the environment in terms of significance for survival. This concept resolves Kant's dilemma by naturalizing what Kant called 'natural purposes' while avoiding both vitalism and the reduction of life to mechanism.

## Key facts

- Immanent purposiveness is neither a property determined by something outside the system nor a nonrelational property of something internal, but a constitutive property of the whole system due to its organization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p146]
- Weber and Varela argue that autopoiesis entails immanent purposiveness in two modes: identity (self-production) and sense-making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p147]
- The organism's concern to continue existing—Spinoza's conatus—is the immanent purposiveness of life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p152]
