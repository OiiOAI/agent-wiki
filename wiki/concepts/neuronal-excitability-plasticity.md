# Neuronal Excitability Plasticity

## Summary

Neuronal excitability is not fixed but can change in response to synaptic input, activity levels, environment, injury, or disease through modulation of voltage-gated ion channel expression, localization, and functional state.

## Key facts

- Channel phosphorylation via second messenger pathways can produce transient changes in channel functional properties that modulate cell excitability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p234]
- Increased activity of a neuronal network can lead to decreased excitability of individual neurons through homeostatic feedback mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p234]
- Structural changes such as altered axon initial segment length or migration relative to the soma can affect excitability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p234]
- Homeostatic changes in excitability likely involve intracellular calcium signaling pathways controlling transcription or trafficking of specific ion channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p234]
