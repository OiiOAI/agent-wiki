# STICC Framework

## Summary

STICC is an acronym for Situation, Task, Intent, Concerns, and Calibration—a script for giving clear directions. Based on Karl Weick's approach, it provides a structured way to communicate executive intent so subordinates can understand not just what to do but why, enabling them to adapt effectively.

## Key facts

- SITUATION: Describing the events or changes prompting action ensures everyone sees the problem the same way and understands why this communication matters. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p253]
- TASK: The statement should be fairly brief initially, with elaboration following once everyone understands the big picture and intent. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p253]
- INTENT: This explains why the staff needs to perform the task and describes the vision of what the end result should be. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p253]
- CONCERNS: This optional step tells staff what they need to monitor more closely and points out potentially tricky parts of the task. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p253]
- CALIBRATION: This key step makes the leader available for questions to ensure team members understand their roles, requiring waiting and eye contact rather than rushing to the next task. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p253]

## Inferences

- Inference: The STICC framework addresses the common problem where subordinates nod in meetings but leave unclear about what they're supposed to do.
