# Pituitary-Adrenal Stress Response

## Summary

The pituitary-adrenal stress response is a major neuroendocrine system activated by physical and psychological stressors. It involves release of corticotrophin releasing factor (CRF) from the paraventricular nucleus of the hypothalamus, triggering ACTH release from the anterior pituitary, which stimulates cortisol secretion from the adrenal cortex. This system helps mobilize energy for coping with stress but can become dysregulated in depression and chronic stress, leading to harmful effects on brain cells including the hippocampus.

## Key facts

- Stress is defined in psychobiology as anything that activates the pituitary-adrenal system (the ACTH-cortisol axis). [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- The pituitary-adrenal response is initiated when stress releases CRF from the paraventricular nucleus (PVN) of the hypothalamus, triggering ACTH release from the anterior pituitary and subsequent cortisol release from the adrenal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- Cortisol feeds back onto brain tissue via specific receptors in the hippocampus and on CRF neurons of the PVN, exerting an inhibitory effect to regulate stress response intensity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- In many individuals with depression, the negative feedback mechanism fails—stress responses do not diminish normally once stressful episodes end. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- The dexamethasone suppression test (DST) evaluates feedback patency by injecting synthetic cortisol and observing whether endogenous cortisol levels are suppressed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- Sustained excessive cortisol secretion can deplete metabolic resources of hippocampal neurons and eventually kill certain brain cells, impairing cognitive abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- A second major limb of the stress response operates via a hypothalamic pathway descending to the spinal cord, activating epinephrine and norepinephrine release from the adrenal medulla via sympathetic efferents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p68]
- ACTH secretion is entrained to REM sleep, increasing with each successive REM period through the night, preparing the body for waking activities by promoting catabolism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p77]
- Depressed individuals often exhibit excessively high ACTH and cortisol secretions during REM sleep, contributing to early morning awakening. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p78]

## Inferences

- Inference: The coupling of ACTH release to REM sleep suggests that stress hormones may contribute to the emotional intensity of dreams.
- Inference: The failure of cortisol feedback in depression provides a biological basis for understanding why depressed individuals experience prolonged stress responses.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise molecular mechanisms by which sustained stress exposure leads to hippocampal neuron death remain under investigation.
