# Censorship in Dreams

## Summary

Dream censorship is the psychical force that prevents unacceptable thoughts from reaching consciousness directly, modifying them through distortion. Freud draws an explicit parallel between this internal censorship and external political or social censorship, arguing that both operate by softening, alluding, or disguising objectionable material.

## Key facts

- Freud compares dream censorship to political censorship, stating that the writer 'must beware of the censor and on its account he must soften and distort the expression of his opinion.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1412]
- The 'second agency' that commands the approaches to consciousness exercises censorship by modifying thoughts that seek admission. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1414]
- Hysterical identification is discussed as related to dream censorship, with both operating through unconscious inference and resemblance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1419]

## Inferences

- Inference: The analogy to political censorship suggests that dream censorship serves a protective function, preventing the conscious mind from being confronted with unacceptable unconscious material.
