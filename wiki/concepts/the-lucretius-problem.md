# The Lucretius Problem

## Summary

A mental defect where people believe the tallest mountain they have observed represents the maximum possible height, or where risk managers use the worst historical event as an estimate for the worst possible future event. The Lucretius problem demonstrates human tendency to fight the last war rather than prepare for the next one, missing the fact that past worst-case events exceeded the worst-case estimates of their time.

## Key facts

- The Lucretius problem is the mental defect where people believe the tallest mountain they have seen is the largest that can possibly exist, extrapolating from past observations to future possibilities. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p69]
- Risk managers use the worst historical recession, war, move in interest rates, or unemployment point as an exact estimate for the worst future outcome, never noticing that these 'worst-case events' exceeded the worst case at the time. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p69]
- The Fukushima nuclear reactor was built to withstand the worst past historical earthquake, with builders not imagining anything much worse—ignoring that the worst past event had no precedent. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p70]
- Alan Greenspan apologized to Congress with 'It never happened before'—but nature, unlike Fragilista Greenspan, prepares for what has not happened before. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p70]
- 'If humans fight the last war, nature fights the next one'—the body is more imaginative about the future than we are, overshooting in response to exposures. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p70]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Lucretius problem represents a fundamental cognitive bias that undermines risk management and leads to fragile systems that are unprepared for genuinely novel events.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether training or institutional reform can effectively address the Lucretius problem or if it represents a permanent feature of human cognition.
