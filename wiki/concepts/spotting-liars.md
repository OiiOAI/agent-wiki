# Spotting Liars

## Summary

Techniques for identifying liars in negotiation include the Pinocchio Effect (liars use more words and third-person pronouns), analyzing pronoun usage (heavy 'I/me/my' indicates lower authority), and watching for incongruence between words and body language per the 7-38-55 rule.

## Key facts

- Liars use more words than truth tellers and use far more third-person pronouns to distance themselves from the lie. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p157]
- Liars speak in more complex sentences in an attempt to win over suspicious counterparts, dubbed the Pinocchio Effect. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p157]
- Pronoun usage reveals authority: heavy 'I/me/my' suggests the person is not the real decision maker, while 'we/they/them' may indicate a decision maker keeping options open. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p158]
- Incongruence between tone/body language and words signals potential lying or discomfort requiring further investigation through labels. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p155]
