# Signal-Dependent Noise Model

## Summary

A model proposed by Harris and Wolpert (1998) that explains movement trajectories as selected to minimize endpoint variance, where motor noise increases with the magnitude of the neural control signal.

## Key facts

- The model proposes that signal-dependent noise causes faster movements (requiring larger control signals) to be more variable, explaining the well-known speed-accuracy tradeoff. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p97]
- The model predicts that optimal movement minimizing endpoint variance has a bell-shaped velocity profile, consistent with observed human and animal movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p97]
- Movement trajectories measured in saccadic eye movements, hand paths for point-to-point movements, and obstacle-avoidance tasks are all consistent with predictions of this model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p97]
