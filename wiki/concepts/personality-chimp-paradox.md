# Personality (in The Chimp Paradox)

## Summary

Personality is defined as ingrained behaviours and responses that are predictable in a person. In the Chimp Paradox model, personality is a mixture of the Human, Chimp, and Computer, each having separate personalities that can be constructive or destructive. The real personality, or the true self, is the Human; the Chimp merely hijacks the person and presents something often not wanted. Both nature (genetic package) and nurture (life experiences) influence personality development through the Human, Chimp, and Computer.

## Key facts

- Personality is broadly defined as ingrained behaviours and responses that are predictable in a person. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p85]
- The personality displayed to the world is a mixture of the Human, Chimp, and Computer. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p85]
- The real personality that is truly you is the Human; the Chimp merely hijacks you and presents something that is often not what you want. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p87]
- The Chimp's personality has nothing to do with the person; it is a machine that was given to work with. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p85]
- Nature is defined as the genetic package given at birth, inherited from parents. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p86]
- Nurture is defined as the experience life gives, and the reaction to and interpretation of that experience. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p86]
- Most Chimps are dominated by nature with some input from nurture, whereas most Humans are dominated by nurture with some input from nature. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p86]
