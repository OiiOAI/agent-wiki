# Long-term depression (cerebellar)

## Summary

Long-term depression (LTD) at synapses between parallel fibers and Purkinje cells is a form of synaptic plasticity thought to underlie cerebellar motor learning. Climbing fiber activity produces prolonged, long-lasting effects on the synaptic efficacy of parallel fibers. Concurrent stimulation of climbing fibers and parallel fibers depresses subsequent Purkinje cell responses to the same parallel fibers, and this depression can last from minutes to hours.

## Key facts

- Climbing fibers can selectively induce long-term depression in the synapses between Purkinje neurons and parallel fibers that are activated concurrently with the climbing fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1028]
- Concurrent stimulation of climbing fibers and parallel fibers depresses Purkinje cell responses to subsequent stimulation of the same parallel fibers but not to parallel fibers not stimulated with climbing fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1028]
- The resulting depression from concurrent stimulation can last for minutes to hours. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1028]
- During an inaccurate movement the climbing fibers respond to specific movement errors and depress the synaptic strength of parallel fibers involved with those errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1029]
- LTD is typically induced by prolonged low-frequency stimulation (1 Hz for 15 minutes), which produces a relatively modest postsynaptic depolarization and small Ca2+ influx compared to LTP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1565]
- NMDA receptor-dependent LTD requires activation of the calcium-dependent phosphatase calcineurin, which has a higher affinity for Ca2+ compared to CaMKII. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1565]
- Activated calcineurin triggers dephosphorylation of GluA1 and phosphorylation of GluA2 subunits of AMPA receptors, leading to endocytosis of AMPA receptors and reduction of EPSP size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1565]
- Mice with inhibited phosphatase 2A show normal learning when first tested in the Morris maze but decreased ability to learn a new platform location when the hidden platform is moved, suggesting LTD is needed for behavioral flexibility. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1566]

## Inferences

- Inference: This mechanism allows the cerebellum to progressively improve motor accuracy by selectively weakening synapses that contribute to errors.
- Inference: Learning in the cerebellum is implemented through complementary synaptic changes in both the cerebellar cortex and deep nuclei.
