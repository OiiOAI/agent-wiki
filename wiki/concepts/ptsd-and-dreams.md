# PTSD and Dreams

## Summary

PTSD involves disrupted sleep-dependent memory processing, including failures to weaken emotional responses to traumatic memories and to integrate them with older memories. PTSD nightmares often replay actual trauma memories, representing a failure of NEXTUP function.

## Key facts

- PTSD is a disorder of sleep-dependent memory evolution that reflects specific failures of sleep to weaken emotional responses to traumatic memories and promote their integration with older memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p205]
- In PTSD, the realistic replay of episodic trauma memories in dreams represents a failure of NEXTUP, which normally avoids reactivating source memories to find weakly related associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p207]
- While healthy individuals show 75% drop in noradrenaline during sleep, PTSD patients show 25% increase, preventing normal suppression of episodic memory replay in REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p208]
- Having replicative nightmares in the aftermath of trauma is a predictor of who develops PTSD, and repeated trauma replays are associated with more severe PTSD symptoms. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p207]
- About 90% of trauma-exposed individuals with PTSD report nightmares resembling the trauma, but only about half experience actual replays of trauma memories; others show distorted or metaphorical representations. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p208]

## Inferences

- Inference: Breakdown of NEXTUP functioning during REM sleep may be the ultimate reason some trauma memories fail to evolve adaptively and why PTSD develops.
