# Mirror Box Therapy

## Summary

Mirror box therapy is a treatment for phantom limb pain and other chronic pain conditions developed by V.S. Ramachandran. The patient places their intact limb into a box with a mirror that creates the visual illusion that the missing or painful limb is moving normally. This visual feedback helps 'unlearn' phantom paralysis and pain by providing the brain with confirmation that the limb can move without pain.

## Key facts

- The mirror box is the size of a large cake box, divided into two compartments with a vertical mirror facing the good hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p192]
- When the patient looks into the mirror, they see the reflection of their intact hand appearing to be their amputated hand, creating the illusion of movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p193]
- Philip Martinez, a patient with phantom elbow pain and paralysis, experienced permanent relief after four weeks of mirror box practice, with his phantom arm and pain completely gone. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p194]
- About half of patients using the mirror box have lost their phantom pain, unfrozen their phantoms, and regained control over them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p194]
- fMRI brain scans show that as patients improve with mirror therapy, motor maps for their phantoms increase, map shrinkage reverses, and sensory and motor maps normalize. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p194]
- Mirror box therapy has been extended to treat reflex sympathetic dystrophy, with patients who had the condition for only two months getting better within a month of therapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p200]
- Patients who had reflex sympathetic dystrophy for longer than two years did not improve, possibly because motor maps for the affected limb had wasted away. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p200]
- G.L. Moseley combined mirror therapy with visualization exercises, reducing or eliminating pain in some patients with chronic pain after twelve weeks of therapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p201]
- Ramachandran and Eric Altschuler showed that mirror therapy is effective on non-phantom problems, such as paralyzed legs of stroke patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p202]
- Ramachandran called the mirror box the first successful 'amputation of a phantom limb.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p194]
