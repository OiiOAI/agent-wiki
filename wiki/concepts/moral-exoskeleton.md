# Moral Exoskeleton

## Summary

Haidt describes religions as 'moral exoskeletons'—sets of norms, relationships, and institutions that work primarily on the elephant (intuitions) to influence behavior. If you live in a religious community, you are enmeshed in these external structures. For atheists living in looser communities with less binding moral matrices, they may have to rely more on internal moral compasses. Haidt warns that relying solely on internal compasses may be a 'recipe for anomie,' using Durkheim's term for what happens to societies that lose their shared moral order.
