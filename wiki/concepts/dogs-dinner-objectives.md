# Dog's Dinner Objectives

## Summary

Dog's dinner objectives occur when a strategic plan contains a long scrambled list of things to accomplish—often mislabeled as 'strategies' or 'objectives'—rather than focusing on one or a few pivotal objectives. Such lists usually grow out of planning meetings where stakeholders make suggestions without prioritization.

## Key facts

- A long list of 'things to do,' often mislabeled as 'strategies' or 'objectives,' is not a strategy—it is just a list of things to do. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p30]
- A mayor's planning committee's strategic plan contained 47 'strategies' and 178 action items, with action item number 122 being 'create a strategic plan.'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p30]
- Good strategy works by focusing energy and resources on one, or a very few, pivotal objectives whose accomplishment will lead to a cascade of favorable outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p30]
