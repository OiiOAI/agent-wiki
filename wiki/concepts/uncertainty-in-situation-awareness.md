# Uncertainty in Situation Awareness

## Summary

Uncertainty plays a role throughout the decision process affecting SA at all levels. The book identifies five sources of Level 1 uncertainty: missing information, reliability/credibility of data, incongruent/conflicting data, timeliness of data, and ambiguous/noisy data. Confidence in SA (a correlate of uncertainty) critically links SA and decision making. The relationship between actual SA quality and confidence determines whether operators act boldly or conservatively, with the dangerous case being poor SA combined with high confidence.

## Key facts

- Uncertainty exists at four levels: data uncertainty (u1), comprehension uncertainty (u2), projection uncertainty (u3), and decision uncertainty (ud). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p133]
- Missing information is one of the most frequent sources of uncertainty in military scenarios where the enemy actively works to deny or hide relevant information. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p134]
- Operators learn not only which sensors are more reliable than others, but also when these systems are reliable and the factors that lead them to be unreliable. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p134]
- When operators have poor SA but high confidence in that erroneous picture, they are most likely to act boldly and incorrectly, making this the most dangerous situation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p137]
- People actively manage uncertainty through strategies including searching for more information, relying on defaults, conflict resolution, thresholding, bet-hedging, and narrowing options. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p138]

## Inferences

- Inference: The qualitative nature of uncertainty (how data is missing, why sensors are unreliable) is itself information that feeds decision making.
