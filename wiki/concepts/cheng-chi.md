# Cheng and Ch'i

## Summary

Cheng and Ch'i are complementary Sun Tzu concepts fundamental to strategy. Cheng (orthodox/expected) are maneuvers the enemy can comprehend—visible formations, direct attacks. Ch'i (unorthodox/unexpected) are surprising elements—the flank attack from nowhere, the baited retreat. General Griffith explained: Cheng = distraction, Ch'i = decision. Neither by itself has these effects. Key principle: 'Engage with the cheng, win with the ch'i.' In business, cheng = expected (working as advertised, quality), while ch'i = the magical surprise element that delights customers (miryoku teki hinshitsu).
