# Stochastic Resonance

## Summary

A phenomenon where adding random noise to a weak signal makes that signal more detectable by bringing it above the threshold of perception. Applied metaphorically by Taleb, this demonstrates that controlled randomness can improve system performance, contradicting the intuition that noise is purely harmful. The concept supports antifragility by showing that exposure to disorder can enhance rather than degrade outcomes.

## Key facts

- Stochastic resonance: adding random noise to background makes you hear sounds (like music) with more accuracy by raising weak signals above the threshold of detection. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p139]
- Weak SOS signals too faint for remote receptors become audible in the presence of background noise—randomness allowing signal detection that nothing else can achieve. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p139]
- Chaotic systems can be stabilized by adding randomness: a doctoral student demonstrated balls jumping chaotically under steady vibration became orderly when random shocks were added. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p140]

## Inferences

- Inference: Stochastic resonance provides physical evidence for Taleb's broader argument that randomness is not merely tolerable but essential for optimal functioning across systems.
