# Receptive vs. Detached Attention

## Summary

McGilchrist contrasts two modes of attention: the empathic, receptive gaze of the right hemisphere versus the detached, focused attention of the left hemisphere. The right hemisphere's gaze is intrinsically empathic and acknowledges the inevitability of 'betweenness'—the mutual transformation that occurs in looking. The left hemisphere's detached stance, while enabling certain kinds of knowledge, ultimately leads to a disengaged relationship with reality. The focused but detached attention of the surgeon may easily mimic that of the torturer; only knowledge of intention changes how we understand the act. This detachment, when not acknowledged, makes the betweenness of sight particularly 'cold'.
