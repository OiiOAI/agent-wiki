# Difficult Decisions

## Summary

A ten-step process for handling difficult decisions or dilemmas: (1) Acknowledge you have a dilemma, (2) Do a commonsense cost-benefit analysis, (3) Recognize there is no perfect solution, (4) Recognize there is no way not to choose, (5) Acknowledge today's choice, (6) Take a stand for your values, (7) Make time to reflect, (8) Name the story when the mind pulls you back, (9) Open up and make room for anxiety, (10) Practice self-compassion.

## Key facts

- Most 'values conflicts' are actually time-management conflicts; values remain the same regardless of how much time is invested. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p263]
- The greater the dilemma, the less likely commonsense methods will be helpful, because if one option was obviously better, there would be no dilemma. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p265]
- Whatever your dilemma, you are already making a choice; each day you do not quit your job, you are choosing to stay. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p266]
- If the dilemma remains unsolved, the process at least allows you to live by your values and treat yourself kindly. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p268]
