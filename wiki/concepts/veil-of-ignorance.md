# Veil of Ignorance

## Summary

The veil of ignorance is a key feature of the original position in justice as fairness. It deprives all parties of knowledge of those contingencies which set men at odds and allows them to be guided by their prejudices. Specifically, no one knows their place in society, class position or social status, their fortune in the distribution of natural assets and abilities (intelligence, strength, etc.), their conception of the good or special psychological propensities. This ensures that principles of justice cannot be tailored to the circumstances of one's own case and represents reasonable restrictions on arguments for principles of justice.

## Key facts

- The veil of ignorance ensures that no one is advantaged or disadvantaged in the choice of principles by the outcome of natural chance or the contingency of social circumstances. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The parties do not know their place in society, class position or social status, nor their fortune in the distribution of natural assets and abilities, intelligence, strength, and the like. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The parties are assumed not to know their conceptions of the good or their special psychological propensities. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The veil of ignorance excludes knowledge of those contingencies which set men at odds and allows them to be guided by their prejudices. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p28]
- The veil of ignorance rules out principles that would be rational to propose only if one knew certain things that are irrelevant from the standpoint of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p28]
- The parties do not know their place in society, class position, or social status; nor does anyone know his fortune in the distribution of natural assets and abilities, intelligence and strength, and the like. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p132]
- The parties do not know their conception of the good, the particulars of their rational plan of life, or even the special features of their psychology such as aversion to risk or liability to optimism or pessimism. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p132]
- The parties do not know the particular circumstances of their own society — its economic or political situation, or the level of civilization and culture it has achieved. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p132]
- The veil of ignorance excludes all but the vaguest knowledge of likelihoods, giving parties strong reasons for being wary of probability calculations. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p149]
- The veil of ignorance nullifies the effects of specific contingencies which put men at odds and tempt them to exploit social and natural circumstances to their own advantage. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p133]
- Without the veil of ignorance, no one is in a position to tailor principles to his advantage. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p135]

## Inferences

- Inference: The veil of ignorance is arrived at naturally by representing the desired restrictions on arguments for principles of justice.
- Inference: The veil of ignorance makes it possible for a unanimous agreement to be reached, since everyone is equally rational and similarly situated, and would be convinced by the same arguments.
