# Writing a paper step by step

## Summary

Ahrens outlines an eight-step workflow that begins with capturing fleeting ideas and ends with manuscript editing. The steps are: (1) make fleeting notes, (2) make literature notes, (3) make permanent notes, (4) add permanent notes to the slip-box with links, (5) develop topics bottom-up from the growing slip-box, (6) decide on a topic and collect relevant notes, (7) turn notes into a rough draft, and (8) edit and proofread. The key innovation is that steps 1–5 happen continuously and in parallel across multiple projects, so that by the time a writing decision is made, the material is already developed and interconnected.

## Key facts

- Fleeting notes should be captured immediately whenever an idea arises, using any medium (notebook, phone, napkin), and processed within a day. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p27]
- Literature notes should be very short, selective, in your own words, and stored with bibliographic details in a reference system—not in the slip-box. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p27-28]
- Permanent notes are written one per idea, in full sentences, with explicit references, as if writing for someone else, and are the only notes that enter the slip-box. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p28]
- When adding a permanent note to the slip-box, Luhmann placed it behind the most closely related existing note and added links to other relevant notes anywhere in the system. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p28]
- Topics are developed bottom-up by looking at where note chains have built up, not by brainstorming or selecting from a predetermined topic list. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p28-29]
- The draft is written by translating collected notes into continuous text while simultaneously building the argument—notes are not simply copied but reworked. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p29]
- In reality, all steps happen simultaneously across multiple projects in different stages; the system's strength is managing multiple concurrent lines of thought. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p29-30]

## Inferences

- Inference: The workflow is circular rather than linear: writing, reading, and note-taking are not sequential phases but simultaneous activities that reinforce each other, with the slip-box serving as the central hub connecting them.
