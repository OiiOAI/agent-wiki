# Proximate Objectives

## Summary

Proximate objectives are strategic goals that are close enough in perspective to be actionable and achievable, rather than distant aspirations. Chapter 7 of Good Strategy, Bad Strategy explores how identifying proximate objectives is a key component of good strategy, using examples including Kennedy's moon landing goal and various organizational case studies.

## Key facts

- Proximate objectives are goals that are close enough to be actionable rather than distant aspirations. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#L194-L195]
- Kennedy's moon landing goal serves as an example of a proximate and strategic objective. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#L196]
- The chapter examines why what is proximate for one organization may be distant for another. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#L204-205]
- A proximate objective names a target that the organization can reasonably be expected to hit, even overwhelm. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p47]
- President Kennedy's moon landing objective was proximate because engineers knew how to design and build rockets and spacecraft and much of the technology had already been developed. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p47]
- The War on Drugs is not a proximate objective because it is not feasible within the present legal and law-enforcement framework. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p47]
- An important duty of any leader is to absorb a large part of ambiguity and pass on to the organization a simpler problem—one that is solvable. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p48]
- The more uncertain and dynamic the situation, the more proximate a strategic objective must be—the essential logic is that of taking a strong position and creating options. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p49]
- A proximate objective is a task, not a performance goal, that has a reasonable chance of being successfully accomplished within a short period. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p293]
- There is nothing that motivates an army or company better than winning; by tackling an important objective and overcoming it, leadership sets the stage for the next battle. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p293]
- Think of strategy as a series of proximate objectives rather than a long-term vision. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p293]
- An eighteen-month horizon is often suggested, facilitating emphasis on action and helping achieve agreement by making strategy a fast-paced cycle. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p293]

## Inferences

- Inference: Proximate objectives cascade down hierarchies—high-level proximate objectives create goals for lower-level units, which create their own proximate objectives in a cascade of problem solving at finer levels of detail.
- Inference: The proximate objectives concept provides a practical framework for maintaining organizational momentum while preserving strategic flexibility.
