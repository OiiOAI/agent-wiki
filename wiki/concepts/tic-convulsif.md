# Tic Convulsif

## Summary

Tic convulsif is a neurosis with symptomatic similarity to hysteria, sharing with it the mechanism of counterwill. Charcot distinguished it from hysteria only by its persistence, while genuine hysterical tic disappears sooner or later.

## Key facts

- Tic convulsif has symptomatic similarity with hysteria so that its whole picture may occur as a part-manifestation of hysteria. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p154]
- Guinon reported that some patients arrived at their spasms and grimaces because an antithetic idea had put itself into effect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p155]
- Charcot found only one distinguishing mark between tic and hysteria: that hysterical tic disappears sooner or later while genuine tic persists. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p154]
- Coprolalia, the involuntary utterance of foul words in tics, may originate from the patient's fear of losing control over sounds combined with counterwill. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p155]
