# Localization of Function

## Summary

Localization of function is the principle that specific brain regions are associated with specific mental functions. While certain functions can be traced to particular brain areas, higher cognitive functions are highly distributed across interconnected regions rather than localized to single points.

## Key facts

- Broca and Wernicke provided the first evidence for localization of cognitive abilities through studies of language disorders (aphasia). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Edgar Adrian, Wade Marshall, and Philip Bard established a precise body map in the cerebral cortex by systematically probing the cat body surface. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p19]
- Functional specialization is a key organizing principle in the cerebral cortex, extending even to individual columns of cells within an area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p19]
- Hughlings Jackson showed that different motor and sensory functions could be traced to specific parts of the cerebral cortex through studies of focal epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p9]

## Inferences

- Inference: Localization of function does not mean that a single brain region is solely responsible for a complex behavior; rather, critical nodes can be identified within distributed networks.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise degree to which higher cognitive functions are localized versus distributed remains an active area of research.
