# Mirror Recognition and Self-Awareness in Amnesia

## Summary

H.M.'s ability to recognize himself in mirrors without shock or surprise, despite not remembering anything since his late twenties, raised questions about how self-image is maintained with profound amnesia. His intact fusiform gyrus and prefrontal cortex networks likely allowed him to perceive his own face as familiar through repeated exposure, enabling continuous updating of his mental self-image despite explicit memory deficits.

## Key facts

- When Henry looked in the mirror, he never expressed shock or lack of recognition; he was comfortable with the person he saw looking back at him. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p234]
- When asked what he thought about how he looked, Henry answered with characteristic understated humor: 'I'm not a boy.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p234]
- Henry's factual knowledge of his appearance was full of gaps—he believed he had dark brown hair even after being partially gray and described himself as 'thin but heavy.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p235]
- The fusiform gyrus, specialized for processing faces, was preserved in Henry's brain, and areas in the prefrontal cortex become active when people view their own face. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p235]
- Henry unconsciously became familiar with new information through repeated exposures day after day, exemplifying slow learning different from healthy people's acquisition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p236]

## Inferences

- Inference: Henry's comfort with his changing appearance likely stemmed from implicit familiarity mechanisms rather than explicit autobiographical memory, suggesting different neural substrates for self-recognition versus self-narrative.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise contribution of fusiform versus prefrontal cortex activity to Henry's mirror recognition could not be determined from behavioral evidence alone.
