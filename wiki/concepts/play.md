# Play

## Summary

Play is essential to the Essentialist approach because it expands the mind in ways that allow exploration. Play broadens the range of options available, acts as an antidote to stress, and has a positive effect on the executive function of the brain. Stuart Brown states: 'Nothing fires up the brain like play.'

## Key facts

- Stuart Brown, founder of the National Institute for Play, states: 'Play leads to brain plasticity, adaptability, and creativity. Nothing fires up the brain like play.'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p81]
- Play expands the mind in three specific ways: it broadens the range of options available, it is an antidote to stress, and it has a positive effect on the executive function of the brain. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p82]
- Key breakthroughs have taken place in times of play: Columbus conceived the world was round, Newton saw gravity in an apple tree, Watson and Crick discovered the double helix. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p83]
- Play doesn't just help us explore what is essential. It is essential in and of itself. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p85]
