# Haptic Metaphor

## Summary

The psychological phenomenon where physical tactile sensations influence abstract judgments and decision-making, demonstrating that the brain processes literal and metaphorical sensations through shared neural circuits. Heavy objects make things seem more 'serious,' rough textures make social interactions seem less 'smooth,' hard seats make people perceive others as more rigid.

## Key facts

- Job candidates evaluated on heavy clipboards are judged as more 'serious' than those on lighter clipboards, demonstrating that physical weight influences abstract assessment of gravity. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p580]
- Sitting in a hard chair makes people perceive individuals as more stable and unemotional and less flexible in economic game play. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p580]
- Holding a warm cup of coffee makes people perceive individuals as having a 'warmer' personality; cold objects produce colder social judgments. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p580]

## Inferences

- Inference: These findings suggest that abstract thought is not purely cerebral but is grounded in bodily experience through neural reuse of sensory circuits for metaphorical processing.
