# Professorial Email Sorting

## Summary

Professorial email sorting is a strategy inspired by famous academics at MIT who default to not responding to emails. The philosophy holds that it's the sender's responsibility to convince the receiver that a reply is worthwhile. Three rules help sort messages: do not reply if the message is ambiguous, if it's not a question or proposal that interests you, or if nothing really good would happen if you responded and nothing really bad would happen if you didn't.

## Key facts

- Famous academics at MIT share a fascinating approach to email: their default behavior when receiving a message is to not respond. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p149]
- The philosophy driving this behavior is that it's the sender's responsibility to convince the receiver that a reply is worthwhile. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p149]
- Messages that don't make a clear case for why a meeting makes sense and minimize the effort needed from the receiver are unlikely to generate a reply from these professors. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p150]
- The professorial approach breaks a key email convention: replies are assumed regardless of relevance or appropriateness. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p150]
- Tim Ferriss wrote: 'Develop the habit of letting small bad things happen. If you don't, you'll never find time for the life-changing big things.'. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p150]
