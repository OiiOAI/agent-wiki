# Gold-Plating

## Summary

Gold-plating refers to the practice of adding excessive, non-essential systems and capabilities to military aircraft, driving up cost while degrading actual combat performance. Pierre Sprey coined and popularized this term to describe how the Air Force loaded heavy, expensive items onto fighter aircraft that had nothing to do with the fundamental mission of shooting down enemy aircraft.

## Key facts

- Sprey defined gold-plating as adding heavy items not necessary to kill another aircraft, such as nose wheel steering, tail hooks, and maintenance ladders. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p227]
- He wrote a famous letter to General Ferguson detailing how the Air Force had exercised no design discipline on the F-X. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p227]
- The Air Force wanted to add so many heavy items to the F-X that performance was seriously degraded. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p227]
