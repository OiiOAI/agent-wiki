# Brain-Body Allometry

## Summary

Brain-body allometry is the systematic mathematical relationship between brain size and body size across species. Snell's 1892 work established that mammalian brain and body weights scale according to a power function of approximately 2/3, resembling a surface-to-volume relationship. This regularity provides a baseline for identifying outliers, though Deacon emphasizes that the same allometric deviation can result from different developmental processes.

## Key facts

- Otto Snell showed in 1892 that mammalian brains and bodies scale according to the power function 2/3. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p165]
- Alexander Brandt proposed in 1867 that brain size might scale with metabolism and that sensory-motor systems should relate to body surface rather than total size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p165]
- Basal metabolism scales to the 3/4 power of body mass in mammals, not 2/3 as initially predicted from surface-to-volume heat dissipation assumptions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p167]
- Within-species brain/body allometry is flatter than between-species allometry because brain size is less variable than body size within species. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p177]
- The regularity of brain-body scaling provides a frame of reference for identifying species that deviate from predicted brain size for their body size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p166]
