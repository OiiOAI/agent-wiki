# Paid Engine of Growth

## Summary

The paid engine of growth describes companies that grow by investing revenue from customers into advertising or sales to acquire new customers. Growth rate is determined by the margin between customer lifetime value (LTV) and cost per acquisition (CPA). Companies can grow faster by either increasing LTV or driving down CPA. Over time, competition tends to bid up CPA, requiring differentiated monetization abilities.

## Key facts

- In the paid engine, each customer pays money over their lifetime as a customer; after variable costs are deducted, this is called customer lifetime value (LTV). [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p201]
- If an advertisement costs $100 and causes fifty new customers to sign up, the cost per acquisition (CPA) is $2.00; if LTV exceeds CPA, the product will grow. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p201]
- IMVU developed techniques for collecting online payments from customers without credit cards by allowing billing to mobile phones or cash by mail, enabling them to afford higher acquisition costs than competitors. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p203]
- Over time, any source of customer acquisition tends to have its CPA bid up by competition, requiring a differentiated ability to monetize a certain set of customers to grow long-term. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p203]
