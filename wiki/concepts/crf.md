# Corticotropin Releasing Factor (CRF)

## Summary

CRF is a neuropeptide that activates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis stress response. In the brain, it promotes stress, anxiety, and fear, in apparent opposition to the anti-stress effects of opioid peptides.

## Key facts

- CRF neurons are concentrated in paraventricular and supraoptic nuclei, projecting to the anterior pituitary to control ACTH release during stress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p65]
- CRF promotes the general stress response with many specific emotional repercussions, while B-endorphin promotes an anti-stress response and calming of negative affective arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p65]
- High concentrations of CRF terminals are found on NE cell group A6 (locus coeruleus), where CRF promotes a general form of brain arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p65]
