# Biosocial Theory

## Summary

Biosocial theory is DBT's explanation of why some people find it challenging to manage their emotions and actions. It describes how emotional vulnerability (biological) interacts with an invalidating social environment to produce difficulties in emotion regulation. The theory emphasizes that it is the transactions between the person and the social environment that matter.

## Key facts

- Emotional vulnerability is biological—some people are born more sensitive to emotional stimuli, experience emotions more often, have more intense emotions, and have emotions that last longer. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p38]
- An invalidating social environment tells you your emotions are invalid, weird, wrong, or bad, and may ignore emotional reactions or command change without coaching on how to do it. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p39]
- The theory emphasizes that it is the transactions between the person and the social environment that count—each reciprocally influences the other. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p39]
