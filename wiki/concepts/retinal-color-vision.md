# Color Vision in Retina

## Summary

Color vision begins in the retina with three cone photoreceptor types (L, M, S) containing distinct visual pigments with different spectral sensitivities. Retinal circuits compare signals from these cone types to encode wavelength information independently of intensity, using opponent mechanisms.

## Key facts

- The human retina contains three cone types with distinct visual pigments: L (long-wave, ~560 nm peak), M (medium-wave, ~530 nm peak), and S (short-wave, ~420 nm peak). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p572]
- The fovea contains only cones, densely packed with no rods; S cones are absent from the central fovea and constitute only ~10% of all cones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p573]
- Midget bipolar cells in the central retina receive input from single cones, connecting to P-type ganglion cells that carry red-green opponent signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p583]
- Small bistratified ganglion cells receive S-ON input and combined L+M-OFF input, creating blue-yellow opponent responses transmitted to koniocellular LGN layers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p538]
- Four P-cell types encode red-green opposition: L-ON/M-OFF, L-OFF/M-ON, M-ON/L-OFF, and M-OFF/L-ON, with surrounds composed of mixed L and M cones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p538]
- Protanopia and deuteranopia (red-green color blindness) result from loss of L or M pigment genes on the X chromosome, affecting ~1% of males each; they are caused by unequal recombination during meiosis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p539]

## Inferences

- Inference: The L and M pigment genes are 96% identical and located in tandem on the X chromosome, explaining the high frequency of red-green color blindness through unequal crossing over.
