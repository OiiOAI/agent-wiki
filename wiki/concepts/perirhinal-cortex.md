# Perirhinal Cortex

## Summary

The perirhinal cortex is a brain region in the temporal lobes that supports familiarity-based recognition memory. It becomes active when trying to match current perceptual input to stored representations, and decreasing activity upon repeated exposure reflects neural efficiency and memory formation. Wilder Penfield discovered that stimulating this area could elicit déjà vu.

## Key facts

- Rebecca Burwell found that stimulating the perirhinal cortex at high frequency made rats treat old boring pictures as new and interesting, while low frequency stimulation made them treat never-seen pictures as old. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p113]
- The perirhinal cortex may be responsible for the natural sense of familiarity experienced when visiting a place or seeing a person seen before. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p113]
- The perirhinal cortex integrates information from different senses to help build semantic memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p118]
- When thinking about a concept for the first time, activity spikes in the perirhinal cortex as if trying to match the input to a template not yet created; subsequent encounters show less activity because elections are resolved faster. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p118]
