# Semantic Memory in Amnesia

## Summary

Studies of H.M. revealed a critical distinction between semantic memory (general knowledge about the world) and episodic memory (personal experiences). While his semantic memory for preoperative information remained intact and stable for decades, his ability to acquire new semantic knowledge was profoundly impaired, demonstrating that medial temporal-lobe structures are necessary for forming new declarative memories but not for retaining preoperatively acquired semantic information.

## Key facts

- Henry's performance on IQ subtests (Vocabulary, General Information, Similarities, General Comprehension) remained consistent across 48 years of testing from 1953 to 2000. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p244]
- In 1975 experiments, Henry could not learn the definitions of eight novel vocabulary words (quotidian, manumit, hegira, anchorite, minatory, egress, welkin, tyro) after 20 trials, while controls learned them in fewer than 6 trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p246]
- Henry correctly identified 93% of pre-1950s words as real words but only 50% of post-1950s words, compared to controls who scored 92% and 77% respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p248]
- On a famous-names task, Henry scored 88% for 1930s–1940s celebrities but only 53% for 1960s–1980s celebrities, compared to controls' 80%. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p249]
- Despite overall impairment, Henry could provide accurate semantic information about some post-1953 famous individuals, including Julie Andrews ('famous for her singing, on Broadway'), Lee Harvey Oswald ('assassinated the president'), and Mikhail Gorbachev ('famous for making speeches, head of the Russian parliament'). [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p257]
- Henry showed normal word-completion priming with pre-1953 words but zero priming with post-1953 words like granola and hacker. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p252]
- The crossword puzzle experiment showed that Henry could learn new information only when it could be linked to preexisting preoperative knowledge (schema-based learning). [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p261]

## Inferences

- Inference: Henry's preserved ability to provide semantic information about some post-1953 celebrities demonstrated that limited declarative semantic learning can occur without hippocampal function when information can be anchored to existing schemas.
- Inference: The distinction between intact word-completion priming for familiar words and absent priming for novel words indicates that different priming mechanisms operate at different levels of information processing.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all new semantic information must first enter as episodic memory before becoming semantic knowledge (standard consolidation view) versus potentially entering directly as semantic knowledge remains debated.
