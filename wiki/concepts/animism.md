# Animism

## Summary

The first complete system of thought developed by humans, consisting of the doctrine of souls and spiritual beings. Animism represents a psychological theory in which primitive man transposes the structural conditions of his own mind into the external world. Freud connects animism to the narcissistic stage of libidinal development.

## Key facts

- Animism is the doctrine of souls and of spiritual beings in general; it was probably the first and most consistent of the three great pictures of the world: animistic, religious, and scientific. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4185]
- Primitive peoples believe that human individuals are inhabited by similar spirits; these souls can leave their bodies and migrate into other human beings. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4185]
- The animistic phase corresponds to narcissism both chronologically and in its content; the religious phase corresponds to the stage of object choice. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4196]
- Animism is a system of thought which gives a psychologically complete explanation of the world; spirits and demons are only projections of man's own emotional impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4198]

## Inferences

- Inference: Man's first theoretical achievement—the creation of spirits—arose from the same source as the first moral restrictions (taboo observances): the emotional ambivalence toward primal objects.
