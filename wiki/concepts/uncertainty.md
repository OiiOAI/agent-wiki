# Embracing Uncertainty

## Summary

Duke argues that comfort with uncertainty is essential for better decision-making. She advocates saying 'I'm not sure' as a more accurate representation of reality, noting that acknowledging uncertainty is the first step toward getting closer to objective truth. Good decision-makers understand they can almost never know exactly how something will turn out.

## Key facts

- Duke states that getting comfortable with 'I'm not sure' is a vital step to being a better decision-maker, requiring making peace with not knowing. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L412-L415]
- Neuroscientist Stuart Firestein's book Ignorance: How It Drives Science champions recognizing the limits of knowledge, with physicist James Clerk Maxwell quoted: 'Thoroughly conscious ignorance is the prelude to every real advance in science.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L418-L423]
- Duke states that good poker players and good decision-makers share comfort with an uncertain and unpredictable place, embracing uncertainty and trying to figure out how unsure they are rather than needing to be sure. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L426-L431]
- A great decision is defined as the result of a good process that includes accurately representing one's own state of knowledge, which is some variation of 'I'm not sure.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L423-L426]

## Inferences

- Inference: Accepting uncertainty allows for better calibration of probabilities rather than black-and-white thinking about outcomes.
