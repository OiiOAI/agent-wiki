# Pre-incident Indicators

## Summary

Pre-incident indicators are detectable factors that occur before the outcome being predicted. The book emphasizes that everything a person does is created twice—once in the mind and once in execution—making ideas and impulses predictive of action.

## Key facts

- The author notes that gross movements get attention but are rarely the indicators that matter most; subtle movements often carry greater predictive significance. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p19]
- The author gives the example that stepping on the first rung of a ladder is a pre-incident indicator of reaching the top; stepping on the sixth is even more significant. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p19]
- Threats to kill reveal an idea one step toward the outcome; introducing a weapon is another step; purchasing the weapon earlier is yet another. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p19]
- Useful PINs for workplace violence include: inflexibility, weapons interest, chronic anger/depression, hopelessness, identification with other perpetrators, and co-worker fear. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p104]
- TIME syndrome consists of Threats, Intimidations, Manipulations, and Escalations used by problem employees. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p103]
- The Scriptwriter is a problem employee type who is inflexible, invests others with worst motives, and writes scripts for interactions where he is a victim. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p102]
- People who experience strong anger are at increased risk of heart attack, superseding risk factors like smoking, high blood pressure, and high cholesterol. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p104]
- After nationally publicized workplace violence stories, several more incidents typically follow within weeks, as perpetrators identify with previous attackers. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p106]
- Park Dietz assembled ten behaviors common to modern assassins: displayed some mental disorder; researched the target; created a diary; obtained a weapon; communicated inappropriately with a public figure; displayed exaggerated self-image (grandiosity, narcissism); exhibited random travel; identified with a stalker or assassin; had ability to circumvent ordinary security; made repeated approaches to a public figure. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p164]
- De Becker's office considers over 150 pre-incident indicators beyond those covered by Dietz's ten behaviors. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p164]
- John Hinckley and Robert Bardo shared more than thirty striking similarities in their pre-attack behaviors, including writing letters to actresses, reading Catcher in the Rye, criss-crossing the country, stalking multiple public figures, and bringing Catcher in the Rye to the attack. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p163]
