# Strains of Commitment

## Summary

The strains of commitment refer to the consideration that parties in the original position must weigh whether they will be able to honor their agreement under all relevant and foreseeable circumstances. Since the original agreement is final and made in perpetuity, parties cannot enter into agreements that may have consequences they cannot accept. They must consider whether they can stick by their commitment even if the worst possibilities prove to be the case, otherwise they have not acted in good faith.

## Key facts

- The parties consider the strains of commitment: they cannot enter into agreements that may have consequences they cannot accept and will avoid those they can adhere to only with great difficulty. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p168]
- Since the original agreement is final and made in perpetuity, there is no second chance, and the question of the burden of commitment is especially acute. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p168]
- The two principles of justice have an advantage regarding strains of commitment because parties protect their basic rights and insure themselves against worst eventualities, running no chance of having to acquiesce in a loss of freedom.
