# Gestalt Principles of Visual Organization

## Summary

Gestalt psychology established that perception is an active, constructive process governed by innate organizational laws. Key principles include similarity (similar elements grouped together), proximity (closer elements grouped), and good continuation (smooth, continuous contours preferred).

## Key facts

- Gestalt psychologists argued that the visual system processes sensory information according to computational rules inherent in the neural system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p544]
- The principle of good continuation underlies contour saliency, whereby smooth contours pop out from complex backgrounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p545]
- Figure-ground segmentation relies on geometric principles as well as cognitive influences such as attention and expectation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p545]
