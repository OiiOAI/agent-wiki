# Availability Heuristic

## Summary

The availability heuristic causes people to assess the frequency or probability of events by the ease with which examples come to mind. The easier it is to recall or imagine something, the more frequent or probable it is judged to be. This leads to systematic biases: events made salient by media coverage, vivid personal experiences, or recent occurrences are overestimated, while common but dull events are underweighted. Availability cascades occur when media coverage of a minor risk spirals into public panic and policy action, as seen with Love Canal and Alar.

## Key facts

- Kahneman and Tversky defined availability as 'the process of estimating frequency by the ease with which instances come to mind.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p171]
- Zero examples suffice to produce an availability judgment; people immediately know that XUZONLCJM allows far more possible words than TAPCERHOB without generating specific examples. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p171]
- People asked to list 12 examples of their assertive behavior rated themselves as less assertive than those listing 6, because the difficulty of generating examples dominated the ease of recall, not the quantity. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p174]
- Participants who smiled (cheek contraction) evaluated themselves as more assertive than those who frowned (forehead contraction) when listing the same number of assertive behaviors, because facial expression affects cognitive ease. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p175]
- Slovic and colleagues found that stroke deaths were estimated as less frequent than accident deaths despite actually being twice as common, because accidents receive more media coverage. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p181]
- Terrorist deaths are perceived as far more likely than traffic deaths despite being orders of magnitude rarer, because terrorist events are highly available through vivid media imagery. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p189]

## Inferences

- Inference: The availability heuristic is an instance of substitution: the difficult question 'how probable is this?' is replaced by the easier question 'how easily does this come to mind?' System 1 performs this substitution automatically.
