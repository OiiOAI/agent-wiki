# Primate Evolution and Economic Behavior

## Summary

Research examining how the evolutionary history of primates informs our understanding of economic decision-making, demonstrating that different primate species possess different cognitive specializations and decision-making strategies that reflect their unique evolutionary paths and feed ecologies.

## Key facts

- There are 264 extant monkey species, all inhabiting different environments, eating different things, coming from different genera, and possessing different cognitive specializations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p83]
- Cotton-top tamarins (Saguinus oedicpus) and common marmosets (Callithrix jacchus) – extremely closely related New World monkey species – exhibit robust differences in discounting behavior, with marmosets valuing future rewards more than tamarins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p83]
- Capuchins (New World monkeys) split from the Old World primate line about 35-40 million years ago, making them distantly related to humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- Adam Smith argued that animal behavior was not relevant to economics because animals lacked the capacity to master trade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p83]

## Inferences

- Inference: Choice experiments involving rodents and birds are silent on questions regarding the evolutionary history of human choice behavior, making primate studies essential for understanding the neural architecture underlying economic decisions.
- Inference: Capuchins serve as a cognitive model of human cognition (rather than neuroscientific model) because they share with humans large brains relative to body size, large social groups, tolerance, and tool-use abilities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific neural substrates underlying different cognitive specializations across primate species remain to be fully characterized.
