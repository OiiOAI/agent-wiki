# Blue-Sky Objectives

## Summary

'Blue-sky objectives' is identified as one of the common 'strategy sins' that Rumelt critiques in Good Strategy/Bad Strategy, alongside fluff and failure to face the problem.

## Key facts

- James Roche identifies strategy 'sins' as including fluff, blue-sky objectives, and not facing the problem. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p3]
- A good strategy defines a critical challenge and builds a bridge between that challenge and action, between desire and immediate objectives that lie within grasp. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p30]
- If the leader's strategic objectives are just as difficult to accomplish as the original challenge, there has been little value added by the strategy. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p30]
- An example of blue-sky objective: 'build school leadership teams that share common beliefs, values, and high expectations' without diagnosing why leadership is weak. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p30]

## Inferences

- Inference: Blue-sky objectives represent aspirational but unrealistic or ungrounded strategic goals that lack connection to actionable plans or real obstacles.
