# Individual Effects of Conformity

## Summary

Four main effects occur when individuals enter groups: the desire to fit in (shaping appearances and adopting group ideas/values), the need to perform (becoming actors who exaggerate agreement and display strengths while minimizing flaws), emotional contagion (picking up others' moods and emotions, with anxiety and fear being most contagious), and hypercertainty (feeling more confident about decisions due to group validation that neutralizes doubting and reflective mechanisms).

## Key facts

- The desire to fit in operates through appearances (dressing as others do) and more importantly through adopting the ideas, beliefs, and values of the group. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p504]
- The longer one is in a group, the stronger and more insidious the conformity effect, with different parts of thinking slowly trimmed away to match others. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p504]
- In the group setting, people become actors who mold what they say and do so others accept and like them, with the mask fusing into personality over time. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p505]
- Emotional contagion makes anxiety and fear the most contagious emotions, followed by joy, excitement, tiredness, apathy, intense anger, hatred, and desire. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p506]
- Emotional contagion has a self-fulfilling dynamic: if three people feel anxious, there must be a good reason, and the fourth feels compelled to adopt the emotion. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p507]
- Hypercertainty neutralizes the doubting, reflective mechanism, compelling individuals to feel confident about what the group decides regardless of actual merit. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p507]

## Inferences

- Inference: These individual effects operate unconsciously, making people believe they have chosen their beliefs independently when they have actually been shaped by group membership.
