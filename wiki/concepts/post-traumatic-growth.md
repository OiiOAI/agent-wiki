# Post-Traumatic Growth

## Summary

A psychological phenomenon in which people harmed by past events surpass themselves, representing the opposite of post-traumatic stress disorder. Taleb identifies this as a manifestation of antifragility in human psychology—evidence that humans can benefit from and grow through exposure to adversity, stressors, and trauma.

## Key facts

- Post-traumatic growth is the opposite of post-traumatic stress syndrome, by which people harmed by past events surpass themselves—this is the antifragile psychological response to adversity. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p64]
- Taleb admits to never having thought of post-traumatic growth despite years studying volatility, indicating domain dependence even in the expert. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p64]
- Popular culture has awareness of post-traumatic growth through the expression 'it builds character,' as do ancient Mediterranean classics and grandmothers. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p64]
- Psychologists who studied post-traumatic growth show data for it but don't fully grasp the concept, lapsing into the language of 'resilience' instead of recognizing the deeper antifragility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p71]

## Inferences

- Inference: Post-traumatic growth suggests that psychological antifragility exists in humans, supporting the broader claim that antifragility is a fundamental property of living systems rather than merely a metaphor.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether post-traumatic growth can be reliably distinguished from resilience in empirical studies, given Taleb's observation that even researchers studying it conflate it with resilience.
