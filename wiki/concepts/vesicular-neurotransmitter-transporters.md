# Vesicular neurotransmitter transporters

## Summary

Vesicular transporters concentrate neurotransmitters into synaptic vesicles using proton gradients generated by vacuolar-type H+-ATPases. These transporters can concentrate neurotransmitters up to 100,000-fold relative to cytoplasmic concentrations. Different vesicular transporters are specific for different transmitter classes (monoamines, acetylcholine, GABA, glutamate).

## Key facts

- Vesicular transporters use the H+ electrochemical gradient created by V-ATPase hydrolysis of ATP to concentrate transmitter molecules into vesicles against their concentration gradient. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p347]
- Vesicular transporters can concentrate neurotransmitters up to 100,000-fold relative to their concentration in the cytoplasm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p347]
- Transmitter uptake into vesicles by transporters is energy-dependent and extremely rapid, enabling vesicles to be quickly refilled during periods of rapid nerve firing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p347]
- Reserpine and tetrabenazine inhibit uptake of amine transmitters by binding to the vesicular monoamine transporter and have played a historic role in developing the biogenic amine hypothesis of depression.
