# Nutrients versus medications for ADHD

## Summary

Both multinutrient supplements and stimulant medications are used to treat ADHD, but they work differently. Stimulants show larger and more powerful changes in core ADHD symptoms in the short term (within hours), while multinutrient effects are subtler but grow over time and often improve sleep, mood, and reduce aggression—effects not typically seen with stimulant medication. Multinutrients have a better side effect profile with no rebound effect.

## Key facts

- Multinutrients will not work as quickly as stimulant medication for quick impact within a few hours. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p123]
- For symptoms like explosive rage, improvements are routinely seen in just a few days with broad-spectrum multinutrients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p123]
- The side effect profile for multinutrients is better, with no substantial effects reported on sleep or appetite, unlike many ADHD medications. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p123]
- There is no rebound effect associated with taking multinutrients, an effect often observed with medications as the drug wears off. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p123]
- Teachers did not note improvements in core ADHD symptoms such as attention with multinutrients, whereas research with stimulant medication has repeatedly found strong effects based on teacher ratings. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p124]
