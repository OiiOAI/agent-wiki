# Epigenetic changes in memory

## Summary

Epigenetic changes in chromatin structure are important for long-term explicit memory storage. These include histone acetylation, which opens chromatin and allows gene transcription, and DNA methylation, which can repress gene expression. CREB-binding protein (CBP) functions as a histone acetylase, and mutations in CBP underlie Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome. Histone deacetylase inhibitors can enhance memory and recover lost memory capabilities.

## Key facts

- Histone acetylation by CREB-binding protein (CBP) disrupts repressive chromatin structure and opens up promoter regions of target genes, allowing RNA polymerase II to initiate transcription. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1567]
- Mutations in CBP underlie Rubinstein-Taybi syndrome, characterized by mental retardation and skeletal abnormalities, and mice with truncated CBP show reduced histone acetylation and impaired late-phase LTP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1567]
- Contextual fear conditioning causes an increase in methylation of the BDNF gene in the CA1 region of the hippocampus, leading to changes in BDNF expression, and this requires NMDA receptor activation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1567]
- Inhibitors of histone deacetylase (HDAC) can enhance memory and recover memory capabilities lost following neuronal death in a mutant mouse line with significant hippocampal degeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1567]
- Early prenatal and postnatal environmental influences, including maternal behavior, can lead to persistent DNA methylation changes in genes encoding hormones, growth factors, and receptors important for learning and emotional states, and these changes can be passed to offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1569]
