# Central Pattern Generator

## Summary

A central pattern generator (CPG) is a neural network in the spinal cord capable of producing rhythmic, coordinated motor patterns (such as locomotion) independent of sensory feedback. CPGs generate both the rhythm underlying locomotion and the spatiotemporal pattern of muscle activation, with separate circuit components for rhythm generation and pattern formation. They are highly conserved across vertebrates and invertebrates.

## Key facts

- CPGs can generate both rhythm and pattern independent of sensory inputs, as demonstrated in decerebrate and spinal cats walking on treadmills after dorsal root section. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p838]
- The lamprey swimming CPG consists of interconnected glutamatergic excitatory neurons that drive motor neurons and commissural inhibitory interneurons for left-right alternation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p840]
- Quadrupedal CPGs contain flexor and extensor modules that control sequential muscle actions around joints through inhibitory and excitatory interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p840]
- V0 commissural neurons mediate left-right alternation, with V0D controlling walking gait and V0V controlling trot gait. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p841]
- CPGs in mammals are modular, with separate circuits for rhythm generation (Shox2ON and Hb9 neurons) and pattern generation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p840]
