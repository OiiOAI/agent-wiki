# Population Thinking

## Summary

Population thinking, inspired by Darwin's approach to biology, holds that a category (like a species or an emotion) is a population of unique members who vary from one another, with no fingerprint at their core. Barrett applies this to emotions: the category 'Anger' contains diverse instances (shouting, seething quietly, teasing, widening eyes) that vary more than the classical view can explain. Just as no species member perfectly represents the species average, no instance of anger must include an 'average anger pattern.'

## Key facts

- Population thinking proposes that a category is a population of unique members who vary from one another, with no fingerprint at their core, describable only in abstract statistical terms. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p29]
- Emotion categories like 'Anger' and 'Fear' have tremendous variety—anger varies from shouting to seething quietly to planning revenge. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p29]
- The category 'Cookie' contains diverse instances (crisp, chewy, sweet, savory) deemed equivalent for the purpose of being a snack, without looking the same or using the same recipe. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p49]
- Population thinking is based on variation, whereas essentialism is based on sameness; the two ideas are fundamentally incompatible. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p180]
- To Darwin, each species was a conceptual category—a population of unique individuals who vary from one another, with no essence at their core. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p179]
- The ideal dog doesn't exist; it's a statistical summary of many diverse dogs. No features are necessary, sufficient, or even typical of every individual. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p179]
- Origin is a profoundly anti-essentialist book, yet it is baffling that where emotion is concerned, Darwin reversed his greatest achievement by writing Expression. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p180]
