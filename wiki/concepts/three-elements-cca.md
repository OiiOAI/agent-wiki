# The Three Elements (CCA)

## Summary

The three core components of mindfulness meditation identified in the Basic Mindfulness system: Concentration (the ability to direct and sustain attention), Sensory Clarity (the ability to perceive fine details of experience), and Acceptance (an attitude of openness and nonjudgment toward whatever arises).

## Key facts

- Concentration means being able to train your attention on whatever object you choose and sustain it over time. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p51]
- Sensory clarity means having a high resolution of the details of whatever object you are focusing on. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p51]
- Acceptance means having an attitude of openness, curiosity, and nonjudgment with whatever is happening in the moment. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p51]
- If even one of these elements is a little bit present, you can have a decent meditation. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p49]
- The three elements interact to make meditation deeper, more powerful, and more effective. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p49]
