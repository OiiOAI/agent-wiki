# Tonic Dopamine

## Summary

Tonic dopamine refers to sustained, baseline levels of dopamine that operate on slower timescales than phasic signals, affecting high-affinity D2-type dopamine receptors. Niv and colleagues proposed that tonic levels of striatal dopamine represent the net rate of rewards, providing a normative explanation for dopamine's role in determining response vigor. This contrasts with phasic dopamine's role in reward prediction errors.

## Key facts

- Dopamine neurons operate in both phasic (short-lived bursts) and tonic (sustained baseline) modes, with phasic signals affecting D1-type low-affinity receptors and tonic signals affecting D2-type high-affinity receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p332]
- Niv and colleagues suggested that tonic levels of striatal dopamine represent the net rate of rewards, providing the opportunity cost of time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p332]
- Injections of 6-hydroxydopamine into the nucleus accumbens profoundly reduce the rate of instrumental responding, implicating dopamine in vigor of ongoing behavior rather than learning processes alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p332]
