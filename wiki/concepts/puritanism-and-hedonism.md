# Puritanism and Hedonism

## Summary

American culture exhibits a fundamental ambivalence around sexuality, oscillating between repressive puritanism and excessive hedonism. The Puritan legacy emphasizes that sex is dirty and should be confined to marriage, associating female sexuality with sin. Meanwhile, hedonistic culture promotes unlimited sexual freedom and commodified pleasure through media and pornography. These opposing attitudes create a profound disconnect between what we are encouraged to want and what we are allowed to have. Europeans by contrast view adolescent sexuality as a normal developmental stage, and their teen birth rates are eight times lower than in America despite—or because of—this approach.

## Key facts

- Despite unprecedented sexual freedom in America, the practice of policing sexuality has continued from Puritan days. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p87]
- The European counter-slogan to American abstinence-only messaging is 'Safe Sex or No Sex,' viewing teen sexuality as normal development. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p89]
- Teenagers in Europe engage in sexual activity an average of two years later than American teenagers, and teen birth rates are eight times less in Europe. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p89]
- Both taboo-ridden sexuality and excess-driven sexuality lead to psychic dissociation from the physical act of sex. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p89]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel suggests that the saturation of sexual imagery in American media, rooted in market economics rather than sexual enlightenment, leaves the underlying 'city on a hill' morality unchallenged—the idea that sex is fundamentally dirty remains culturally entrenched.
