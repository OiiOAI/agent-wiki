# Pareto's Principle

## Summary

Pareto's Principle states that a minority of causes, inputs, or effort usually lead to a majority of the results, outputs, or rewards. Joseph M. Juran named this unequal distribution phenomenon after Vilfredo Pareto, who observed that 80% of land in Italy was owned by 20% of the people. The book extends this to the extreme by identifying the single most important factor among the vital few.

## Key facts

- Vilfredo Pareto wrote a mathematical model showing 80% of land was owned by 20% of the people in 19th century Italy. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p29]
- Joseph M. Juran identified that a handful of flaws produces the majority of defects and named this Pareto's Principle. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p29]
- Richard Koch defined the principle as minority of causes leading to majority of rewards. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p30]
- Keller extends Pareto to the extreme: identifying 20% of 20% of 20% to find the essential ONE Thing. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p31]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 80/20 ratio is illustrative rather than exact; the principle is about unequal distribution regardless of specific percentages.
