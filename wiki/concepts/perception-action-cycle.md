# Perception-Action Cycle

## Summary

The perception-action (PA) cycle is the cybernetic processing of information that adapts the organism to its environment, involving circular processing with feed-forward and feedback connections between neural structures. At its biological base, the cycle consists of homeostatic mechanisms involving the autonomic nervous system and hypothalamus. At intermediate levels, the cycle involves limbic structures supporting emotional and value-assessing mechanisms. At the cortical level, the PA cycle incorporates prefrontal cognitive components, with the prefrontal cortex playing an integrative, coordinating, and commanding role.

## Key facts

- The PA cycle is the cybernetic processing of information that regulates the environmental adaptation of the organism through circular processing with feed-forward and feedback connections. - At the lowest level, the PA cycle involves the vegetative and visceral structures of the hypothalamus and the autonomic nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p96]
- At intermediate levels, the PA cycle involves limbic structures including the amygdala, orbitofrontal cortex, insula, and nucleus accumbens supporting emotional and value-assessing mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p96]
- The prefrontal cortex plays an integrative, coordinating, and commanding role in the PA cycle, contributing predictive and preadaptive control through top-down executive functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p96]
