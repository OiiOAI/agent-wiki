# Hamiltonian Relatedness in Hymenoptera

## Summary

Hymenoptera (ants, bees, wasps) have an unusual sex determination system where males develop from unfertilized eggs (haploid) and females from fertilized eggs (diploid). This creates unusual relatedness coefficients: a mother is 50% related to sons (normal), but a son is 100% related to his mother (since he received all her genes). Sisters share on average 75% of genes (because they share all paternal genes plus half of maternal genes), making them more closely related to full sisters than a mother would be to her own daughters. This asymmetry underlies Hamilton's explanation for the evolution of worker sterility.

## Key facts

- In Hymenoptera, males develop from unfertilized eggs and contain only a single set of chromosomes (all from their mother), while females develop from fertilized eggs and have the normal double set from mother and father. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p210]
- All sperm from a given male hymenopteran are genetically identical because they are derived from a single haploid cell by mitosis, unlike in mammals where each sperm is a unique 50% sample of the father's genome. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p211]
- A female hymenopteran has both queen-making and worker-making genes; which set is expressed depends on environmental factors like food during development rather than genetic differences. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p211]
- Because a worker bee shares all genes from her father but only half from her mother, she is on average 75% related to her sisters (25% shared through mother plus 50% through identical father) but only 50% related to any potential daughters. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p210]
- Hamilton realized that workers may be more closely related to the queen's offspring (their siblings) than the queen herself is, explaining why workers' genes might benefit more from helping rear siblings than from reproducing themselves—this is a key explanation for the evolution of worker sterility. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p210]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 3:1 relatedness ratio for sisters vs. 1:2 for mother-daughter creates the conditions for the 'tangled loop' of hymenopteran genetics, where genes for worker behavior can spread because workers are more related to sisters than they would be to their own offspring.
