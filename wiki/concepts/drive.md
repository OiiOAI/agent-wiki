# Drive

## Summary

In Freud's metapsychology, a drive (Trieb) is defined as 'a concept on the frontier between the mental and the somatic, as the psychical representative of the stimuli originating from within the organism and reaching the mind.' Drive stimuli differ from external stimuli by arising from within the organism, operating as constant force rather than momentary impact, and being impossible to escape by flight. Drives have four characteristics: pressure (motor factor), aim (satisfaction through removal of stimulation), object (thing through which aim is achieved), and source (somatic process in an organ). Freud classified primal drives into ego or self-preservative drives and sexual drives, later adding the death drive in Beyond the Pleasure Principle (1920).

## Key facts

- A drive is 'a concept on the frontier between the mental and the somatic, as a measure of the demand made upon the mind for work in consequence of its connection with the body.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4501]
- The four essential characteristics of a drive are: pressure (its motor factor), aim (satisfaction), object (the thing through which satisfaction is achieved), and source (the somatic process in an organ). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4501-4502]
- The object of a drive is what is most variable about it and is not originally connected with it; it may be changed any number of times and may even be part of the subject's own body. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4502]
- Freud distinguishes between primal drives, which cannot be further dissected according to their sources, and more specialized drive motives that may admit of further analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4503]
- Drives are the true motive forces behind the advances that have led the nervous system to its present high level of development. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4500]
