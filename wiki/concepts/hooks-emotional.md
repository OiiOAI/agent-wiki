# The Four Common Hooks

## Summary

The Four Common Hooks are self-defeating patterns that prevent emotional agility: Thought-Blaming (blaming thoughts for actions without recognizing personal agency), Monkey-Mindedness (incessant internal chatter jumping from topic to topic), Old Outgrown Ideas (holding onto beliefs and behaviors from past circumstances that no longer apply), and Wrongheaded Righteousness (persisting in wanting to be proven right at the expense of other values). These patterns trap people in rigid responses and prevent flexible engagement with present circumstances.

## Key facts

- Hook #1 Thought-Blaming occurs when speakers blame thoughts for their actions, but thoughts in isolation do not cause behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p36]
- Hook #2 Monkey-Mindedness describes the internal chatterbox that leaps from topic to topic and often includes bossy, judgmental language with words like 'must,' 'can't,' and 'should'. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p36-37]
- Monkey mind is obsessed with the push of the past and the pull of the future, taking one out of the moment. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p37]
- Hook #3 Old Outgrown Ideas involves holding onto thoughts and behaviors that were functional in the past but no longer serve current circumstances. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p37]
- Hook #4 Wrongheaded Righteousness involves hanging on to the need for justice, vindication, or proof of being right, which can steal years from one's life. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p38]
