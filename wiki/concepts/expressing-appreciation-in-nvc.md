# Expressing Appreciation in NVC

## Summary

NVC distinguishes conventional compliments, which are often life-alienating judgments, from genuine appreciation expressed purely for celebration. Appreciation in NVC has three components: the actions contributing to well-being, the specific needs fulfilled, and the pleasurable feelings from those needs being met. Appreciation should be received without feelings of superiority or false humility.

## Key facts

- Compliments are often judgments—however positive—of others and constitute life-alienating communication. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p277]
- When appreciation is used to influence others, recipients may work harder initially but productivity drops once they sense the manipulation. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p278]
- When NVC is used to express appreciation, it is purely to celebrate, not to get something in return—the sole intention is to celebrate the way our lives have been enriched by others. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p278]
- The three components of NVC appreciation are: the actions contributing to well-being, the particular needs fulfilled, and the pleasurable feelings from fulfilling those needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p279]
- NVC appreciation can be received without feelings of superiority or false humility when one is aware that the power to enrich life for others comes from a source available to everyone. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p281]
- Despite unease in receiving appreciation, most people yearn to be genuinely recognized and appreciated. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p283]
