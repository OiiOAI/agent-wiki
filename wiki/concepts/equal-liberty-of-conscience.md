# Equal Liberty of Conscience

## Summary

Equal Liberty of Conscience is a fundamental liberty protected by Rawls's first principle of justice. Rawls argues that parties in the original position, not knowing their religious or moral convictions, must choose principles that secure the integrity of their religious and moral freedom. Equal liberty is the only principle such parties can acknowledge, as gambling with liberty by permitting dominant doctrines to suppress others would show they do not take their convictions seriously.

## Key facts

- Parties cannot take chances with their liberty by permitting the dominant religious or moral doctrine to persecute others, even if belonging to the majority is probable. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p193]
- Liberty of conscience is limited only by the common interest in public order and security, and only when there is a reasonable expectation that not doing so will damage the public order. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p198]
- The limitation of liberty must be based on evidence and ways of reasoning acceptable to all, appealing to what everyone can accept without presupposing particular metaphysical doctrines. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p199]
