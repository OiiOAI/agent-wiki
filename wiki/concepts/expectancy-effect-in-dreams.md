# Expectancy Effect in Dreams

## Summary

The expectancy effect describes how lucid dreamers can influence dream content not by forcing things to appear but by expecting them to appear. Rather than trying to will objects into existence, experienced lucid dreamers imagine the desired object or scene and then turn around expecting to see it behind them. This technique works because the dreaming brain tracks the dreamer's expectations and uncertainties, incorporating them into the unfolding dream narrative. The effect only works if the dreamer is genuinely convinced they can do it.

## Key facts

- Lucid dreamers can use the expectancy effect to make objects or people appear by imagining the desired scene and then turning around while expecting to see it, rather than trying to force materialization. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p256]
- The expectancy effect works only if the dreamer is genuinely convinced they can do it; any doubts cause the dreaming brain to track those uncertainties and take the dream in unexpected directions. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p256]
- Experienced lucid dreamers use techniques like spinning their dream body or rubbing their hands to prolong lucid dreams when they sense the dream is ending and visual aspects start to fade. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The expectancy effect demonstrates that even in lucid dreams, the dreaming brain—not the conscious dreamer—is the primary generator of dream content, but the dreamer can influence what associations are explored through their expectations.
