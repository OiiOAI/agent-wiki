# Brain as Closed System

## Summary

The closed-system hypothesis proposes that the brain operates as a self-referential, closed system in two senses: physically separated from inquiry by bone, and functionally self-referential, able to know universals only through specialized sense organs. Sensory input modulates rather than informs this intrinsic system. The significance of incoming sensory information depends on the pre-existing functional disposition of the brain. This explains how the brain can generate images during dreaming without sensory input.

## Key facts

- The brain operates as a self-referential, closed system in at least two senses: separated from direct inquiry by bone, and mostly self-referential, able to know universals only by means of specialized sense organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p13]
- The significance of incoming sensory information depends on the pre-existing functional disposition of the brain; sensory cues earn representation via their impact upon pre-existing functional states. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p14]
- The brain does not depend on continuous input from the external world to generate perceptions—sensory input modulates rather than informs the intrinsic system. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p13]

## Inferences

- Inference: Brain function has two distinct components: a private or closed system responsible for qualities such as subjectivity and semantics, and an open component responsible for sensory-motor transformations dealing with relations between the private component and the external world.
