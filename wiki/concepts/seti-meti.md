# SETI and METI

## Summary

SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence) attempts to detect signals from intelligent beings elsewhere in the galaxy. METI (Messaging Extraterrestrial Intelligence) involves actively sending strong signals to make humanity's presence known. Hawkins argues for METI, suggesting it is safe because any civilization capable of reaching Earth would likely have overcome existential risks and would not be dangerously aggressive.

## Key facts

- Earth's radar, radio, and TV broadcasts are too weak to be detected by existing SETI technology from other planets unless they are very close. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p200]
- METI opponents fear that making humanity's presence known could lead to invasion, enslavement, or infection by advanced alien civilizations. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p201]
- Hawkins argues METI risks are built on improbable assumptions: that aliens can travel interstellar distances, would spend thousands of years doing so, need something from Earth, cannot detect life independently, and would choose to harm rather than help. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p201]
- Hawkins suggests that intelligent beings elsewhere would have faced similar existential risks and likely evolved beyond dangerously aggressive behavior to survive. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p201]
- Hawkins concludes we have nothing to fear from METI, analogizing it to an entrepreneur sharing ideas rather than being secretive. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p202]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hawkins proposes that successful communication with extraterrestrials requires creating long-lasting signals that persist millions of years, not relying on ongoing transmission.
