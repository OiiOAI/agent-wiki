# Generalized Synchrony

## Summary

Generalized synchrony refers to the capacity of agents to synchronize their internal states when they have similar generative models. In multi-agent Active Inference, this enables primitive theory of mind where internal states of one creature come to resemble those of another. This has been demonstrated in synthetic birdsong studies showing turn-taking behavior.

## Key facts

- When two creatures have similarly structured generative models, their internal states can synchronize, enabling a primitive kind of theory of mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p163]
- In birdsong simulations, if one bird hears the song it is predicting, there is no need to generate it; if it predicts a song not heard, it must start singing to resolve prediction error. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p169]
- Turn-taking in birdsong emerges from alternation between attention to sensory data and attenuation during vocal action, with two agents leading to reciprocal alternation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p170]
