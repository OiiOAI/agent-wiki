# Aircraft Accident Investigation Methodology

## Summary

Aircraft accident investigation involves collecting and analyzing data from multiple sources including the aircraft wreckage, accident site, crew records, ATC radar recordings, Cockpit Voice Recorders (CVR), Digital Flight Data Recorders (DFDR), and training/personnel records. Investigators assess the situation awareness of critical personnel by examining whether relevant information was available, perceived, comprehended, and used appropriately to anticipate future states.

## Key facts

- Data sources for accident investigation must be internally consistent, logical, and sequentially correct. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p118]
- DFDRs contain 11 to over 100 parameters recording airplane flight surfaces, engines, systems, pilot controls, and flight path from the preceding 25 hours. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p118]
- CVRs record sounds, conversation, alerts, and warnings within the cockpit from the preceding 30 minutes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p118]
- ATC facilities record communications between pilots and controllers and radar information revealing precise location, airspeed, and altitude of aircraft. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
- Investigators can use post hoc analysis to establish the sequence of events and then attempt to reconstruct the situation awareness of crew involved. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
