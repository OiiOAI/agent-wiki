# REM Sleep Mechanisms

## Summary

REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep is a distinct vigilance state characterized by cortical arousal (similar to waking EEG), muscular paralysis (atonia), rapid eye movements, and vivid dreaming. The executive mechanisms for REM are located in the lower brain stem, caudal to waking mechanisms, suggesting REM may represent an evolutionarily ancient form of arousal that was later superseded by neocortical waking systems. REM sleep is intimately involved with emotional processing and memory consolidation.

## Key facts

- REM sleep exhibits cortical arousal (more energized than waking EEG), flaccid muscular paralysis (atonia), and rapid eye movements accompanied by vivid dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p71]
- The executive mechanisms for REM sleep are situated more caudally (primitively) in the brain stem than the ARAS waking mechanisms, suggesting REM is evolutionarily older than waking consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p75]
- Norepinephrine (NE) and serotonin (5-HT) neurons cease firing completely during REM sleep, while dopamine (DA) cells show relatively little change across vigilance states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp#p79]
- PGO (pons-geniculate-occipital) spikes are massive neural discharges that sweep through the brain during REM sleep, accompanied by REMs, bodily jerks, and muscular tremors that break through motor inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p74]
- Atonia during REM is produced by glycine inhibition exerted on motor neurons controlling anti-gravity muscles, located just below the locus coeruleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p74]
- Animals with lesions of the atonia mechanism act out their dreams, exhibiting species-typical emotional behaviors (snarls, running movements) during REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p78]
- REM sleep is rudimentary or absent in reptiles and fish, brief in birds, and fully developed only in mammals, suggesting it emerged in early mammalian evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p76]
- The echidna (spiny anteater), an ancient monotreme mammal, apparently exhibits no REM sleep despite having a developed neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p76]
- REM sleep deprivation can counteract depression as effectively as antidepressant drugs, possibly by blocking REM-related hormone secretions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p78]
- The basic rest-activity cycle (BRAC) determines the average rate at which animals cycle through sleep stages, directly related to metabolic rate—approximately 20 minutes in cats and 90 minutes in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p73]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cessation of NE and 5-HT firing during REM may explain the hallucinatory quality of dreams, similar to the perceptual effects of LSD which blocks serotonin.
- Inference: REM sleep may have originally mediated a primitive form of emotional arousal that was superseded by neocortical waking mechanisms as mammals evolved.
- Inference: The therapeutic effect of REM deprivation on depression may work by suppressing cortisol and other stress-related hormones that normally peak during REM.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise function of REM sleep in brain homeostasis remains debated, with theories ranging from memory consolidation to emotional processing to neural rejuvenation.
- Uncertain: Whether the dreaming state (subjective experience) can be fully dissociated from the REM physiological state is uncertain—frontal lobectomy patients lack dream reports but maintain normal REM EEG.
