# Winner-Take-All

## Summary

Winner-take-all is a proposed neural mechanism for decision-making where many neurons compete with each other but only one (or a few) win, firing more vigorously or in some special way while all others are forced to fire more slowly or not at all. This nonlinear process can be implemented in neural networks by making excitatory neurons stimulate an inhibitory neuron that in turn inhibits all the excitatory ones.

## Key facts

- Winner-take-all is a mechanism in which many neurons compete with each other but only one (or a few) win, firing more vigorously or in some special way while all others are suppressed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p237]
- Making an equitable compromise can be a linear process but making a sharp decision is a highly nonlinear one. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p237]
- In artificial neural networks, winner-take-all can be implemented by making each excitatory neuron stimulate an inhibitory neuron that in turn inhibits all excitatory neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p237]
- The neuron that can triumph best over general inhibition is likely to emerge the winner. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p237]
- Competitive learning uses a winner-take-all mechanism where one unit suppresses others since it best expresses the significance of the input, with only the winning unit and its neighbors adjusting their incoming weights.
