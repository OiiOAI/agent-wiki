# CREB and Memory Genes

## Summary

CREB (cAMP Response Element-Binding protein) is a transcription factor that activates genes necessary for long-term memory formation. Kandel discovered the memory-suppressor gene CREB-2; blocking this gene enhances both synaptic strengthening and memory. The late phase of LTP requires CREB activation and new protein synthesis.

## Key facts

- Cyclic AMP recruits protein kinase A and other protein kinases, which leads to the activation of CREB and the turning on of effector genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p303]
- Blocking CREB-2 and similar memory-suppressor genes enhances both long-term potentiation in the hippocampus and spatial memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p303]
- In Aplysia, blocking the expression of the CREB-2 memory-suppressor gene enhances both the strengthening and the increase in number of synapses associated with long-term facilitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p303]
- Explicit memory in the mammalian brain requires several gene regulators in addition to CREB, unlike implicit memory in Aplysia. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p304]
