# Ordinary Unconsciousness

## Summary

Ordinary unconsciousness is the normal state of most humans, characterized by identification with thought processes, emotions, reactions, desires, and aversions. It manifests as an almost continuous low-level unease, discontent, boredom, or nervousness that most people accept as normal. People often use alcohol, drugs, sex, food, work, television, or shopping as anesthetics to remove this basic unease. The book distinguishes between ordinary unconsciousness (continuous low-level discomfort) and deep unconsciousness (acute suffering when the ego is threatened or major challenges arise).

## Key facts

- Ordinary unconsciousness is defined as being 'identified with your thought processes and emotions, your reactions, desires, and aversions'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p76]
- It manifests as 'an almost continuous low level of unease, discontent, boredom, or nervousness—a kind of background static'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p77]
- People often use substances or activities as anesthetics to remove this basic unease, creating compulsive or addictive patterns. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p77]
- Ordinary unconsciousness turns into deep unconsciousness when resistance becomes intensified through challenge or threat to the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p77]
- The book uses the analogy that this unease is like a continuous background hum that one is not aware of until it stops. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p77]
