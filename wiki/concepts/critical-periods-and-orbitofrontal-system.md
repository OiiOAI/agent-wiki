# Critical Periods and the Orbitofrontal System

## Summary

Critical periods are windows in brain development when specific functions undergo rapid plasticity. The orbitofrontal system, which matures around 10-18 months, is crucial for emotional attachment and regulation. Children deprived of maternal interaction during these periods may develop difficulties with emotion regulation and relationships, as seen in studies of institutionalized children.

## Key facts

- At twenty-six months, a child's plastic change is at its height; the right hemisphere has just completed a growth spurt and the left hemisphere is beginning its own. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p231]
- A critical period from approximately ten to eighteen months shapes brain circuits allowing infants to maintain attachments and regulate emotions through development of the right orbitofrontal system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p232]
- The right orbitofrontal system allows us to read people's facial expressions and understand and control our own emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p232]
- During World War II, René Spitz studied infants in foundling homes where one nurse cared for seven babies; these infants stopped developing intellectually, were unable to control emotions, and showed 'turned-off' states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p233]
- Mr. L. lost his mother only a few months after completing orbitofrontal development, so others had to help him use and exercise this system, or it would weaken. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p233]
- Children losing their mother at this young age suffer two devastating blows: losing their mother to death and the surviving parent to depression, often learning to 'autoregulate' by turning off emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p233]
