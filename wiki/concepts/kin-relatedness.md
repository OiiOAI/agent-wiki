# Kin Relatedness

## Summary

Kin relatedness (r) is the probability that two individuals share genes by common descent. Calculated by genealogical distance, relatedness ranges from 1.0 (identical twins) through 0.5 (siblings, parent-child) to 0.125 (first cousins). The relatedness coefficient determines the genetic benefit of altruistic acts: a gene for altruism spreads when rB > C.

## Key facts

- Full siblings have relatedness of 0.5 because half the genes possessed by one will be found in the other (by the logic of meiosis). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p121]
- Parent-child relatedness is exactly 0.5, achieved through equal genetic contribution to sex cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p122]
- The generation distance method: count steps to common ancestor and back, then multiply (1/2)^g for each path, summing across all common ancestors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p123]
- First cousins have two common ancestors and generation distance of 4, giving relatedness of 2 × (1/2)^4 = 1/8 = 0.125. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p123]
- A third cousin (r = 1/32 ≈ 0.03) is near the baseline probability of sharing a random gene with any individual in the population. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p123]
- When relatedness is uncertain (e.g., possibly half-brothers, possibly full brothers), the effective relatedness is the weighted average based on probability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: Animals cannot calculate relatedness consciously but can evolve behavioral rules that statistically correlate with genetic relatedness (e.g., staying near birthplace increases chance of helping kin).
- Inference: The 'certainty' of relatedness matters beyond the coefficient itself: parents are more certain of children's kinship than siblings are of each other, explaining asymmetries in altruism.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether animals have psychological mechanisms that estimate relatedness better than random, possibly through phenotype matching or familiar association.
