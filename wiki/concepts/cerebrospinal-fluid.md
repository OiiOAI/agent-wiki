# Cerebrospinal Fluid

## Summary

Cerebrospinal fluid is a clear, colorless fluid that bathes the brain and spinal cord, providing mechanical cushioning, buoyancy, and a reservoir for metabolic waste removal. It is primarily secreted by choroid plexuses and circulates through the ventricular system and subarachnoid space before being absorbed into venous blood.

## Key facts

- The total volume of CSF is approximately 140 mL, with the lateral and third ventricles containing approximately 12 mL, the spinal subarachnoid space approximately 30 mL, and the remaining 100 mL in the subarachnoid space and major cisterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1625]
- CSF is absorbed through arachnoid granulations and villi into venous blood via a one-way bulk flow mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1625]
- The rate of formation of CSF in adults is 0.35 mL per minute or approximately 500 mL per day, so the entire volume of CSF is turned over three or four times a day. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1625]
- CSF flows from the lateral ventricles through the interventricular foramina of Monro into the third ventricle, then through the cerebral aqueduct of Sylvius, and through the foramina of Magendie and Luschka into the subarachnoid space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1625]
- By its buoyant action the CSF allows the brain (average weight 1400 g) to float, thereby reducing its effective weight in situ to less than 50 g. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1625]

## Inferences

- Inference: The circulation pathway through multiple foramina suggests redundant mechanisms for CSF flow, which may protect against obstruction.
