# Prenatal Testosterone

## Summary

Testosterone secreted by the fetal testes around eight weeks postconception has organizational effects on the brain, causing lifelong changes in structure and function. It acts by being converted to estrogen in the brain, which then binds to intracellular estrogen receptors. Without testosterone and anti-Müllerian hormone, fetal mammalian brains automatically feminize.

## Key facts

- Around eight weeks postconception, human fetal gonads start secreting steroid hormones, with testosterone plus anti-Müllerian hormone masculinizing the brain. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p220]
- Surprisingly few testosterone effects in the brain result from testosterone binding to androgen receptors; instead it is converted to estrogen and binds to intracellular estrogen receptors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p220]
- Fetuses make alpha-fetoprotein, which binds circulating estrogen, preventing maternal and fetal estrogen from masculinizing female fetal brains. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p220]
- In Goy's studies with rhesus monkeys, testosterone exposure throughout pregnancy produced daughters who did more rough-and-tumble play, were more aggressive, and displayed male-typical mounting behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p224]

## Inferences

- Inference: Unless there is testosterone and anti-Müllerian hormone around, fetal mammalian brains automatically feminize, suggesting female is the default state.
