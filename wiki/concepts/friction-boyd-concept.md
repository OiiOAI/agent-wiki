# Friction (Boyd's concept)

## Summary

Boyd developed friction as both a threat and a weapon, building on Clausewitz's concept and Sun Tzu's insights. He identified two complementary ways to manipulate friction: minimizing one's own friction through initiative and harmony of response, and maximizing the opponent's friction through variety and rapidity of action.

## Key facts

- Boyd identified four key qualities of successful operations: initiative, harmony, variety, and rapidity. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p285]
- Friction, uncertainty and ambiguity are closely related threats and potential weapons in Boyd's framework. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p285]
- To minimize friendly friction, one must act and react quicker than one's opponent through initiative at lower levels within a chain-of-command. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p285]
- To maximize enemy friction, one should plan to attack with a variety of actions executed with the greatest possible rapidity. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p285]
- Variety in actions across dimensions, areas, forces, tactics, method, timing or rhythm precludes the opponent from recognizing a pattern. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p285]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's manipulation of friction concept extends Clausewitz by adding the deliberate weaponization of friction against adversaries rather than viewing it merely as an obstacle to overcome.
