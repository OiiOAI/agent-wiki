# Small Batches

## Summary

Small batches is a fundamental lean manufacturing principle that advocates completing work in minimal quantities rather than large batches. The concept, illustrated through the envelope-stuffing example where completing one envelope at a time proves faster than batch processing, has been adopted by startups to accelerate the Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop and achieve validated learning more quickly.

## Key facts

- Single-piece flow (batch size of one) completes work faster than large batches despite appearing inefficient due to avoiding time spent sorting, stacking, and moving piles of half-complete work. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p172]
- Small batches detect quality problems much sooner; if letters don't fit envelopes or envelopes are defective, this is discovered immediately rather than at the end of a large batch. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p173]
- Toyota adopted small-batch production because the Japanese car market was too small for economies of scale that powered American mass production, and capital was unavailable for massive machine investments. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p173]
- At IMVU, features were designed, developed, and shipped one at a time, with an average of fifty product changes per day released to customers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p176]
- IMVU called its automated defect prevention system its 'product's immune system' because it detected business consequences and automatically invoked an andon cord equivalent. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p177]
- Wealthfront practices true continuous deployment including more than a dozen releases to customers every day in an SEC-regulated environment. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p178]
