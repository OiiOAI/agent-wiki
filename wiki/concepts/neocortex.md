# Neocortex

## Summary

The neocortex ('new outer layer') is the newest part of the brain, found only in mammals. It occupies about 70 percent of human brain volume and is the organ of intelligence, responsible for vision, touch, hearing, language, and abstract thinking. Despite performing diverse functions, its regions look remarkably similar at the cellular level, suggesting a common underlying algorithm.

## Key facts

- The neocortex means 'new outer layer' and all mammals, and only mammals, have a neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p23]
- The human neocortex occupies about 70 percent of brain volume and would be about the size of a large dinner napkin if ironed flat. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p23]
- The neocortex is responsible for all capabilities associated with intelligence, from vision and touch to language and abstract thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p24]
- The neocortex does not control behavior directly; none of its cells connect directly to muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p24]
- The neocortex is divided into dozens of regions that perform different functions but look similar at the cellular level. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p25]
- The complex circuitry under one square millimeter of neocortex contains roughly 100,000 neurons, 500 million synapses, and several kilometers of axons and dendrites. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p29]
- The neocortex is at the front and top of the forebrain, expanded greatly in mammals and making up three-quarters of the human brain's mass. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p126]
- The cortex has a modular, repeating columnar structure organized into six layers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p128]
- Each cortical column receives inputs from thalamus and other brain regions, integrates information from neighboring columns, and sends outputs to other cortical areas and the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p128]
- In mammals, many cortical areas are devoted to vision, with information processed through a hierarchy from V1 to V2, V3, V4, and many other visual areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p130]
- Higher-order features are extracted through successive cortical areas: neurons become responsive to lines, then shapes, then larger objects, and even specialize for types of objects like faces. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neocortex likely evolved the strategy of duplicating a single circuit rather than evolving many different circuits, allowing for flexibility in what each region learns.
- Inference: The neocortex's hierarchical architecture enables increasingly abstract representations of the world.
