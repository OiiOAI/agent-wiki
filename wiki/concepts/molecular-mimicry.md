# Molecular Mimicry

## Summary

Molecular mimicry occurs when foreign antigens (such as those from pathogens or food proteins like gluten) have molecular markings similar to the body's own cells. This can lead to an immune system attack on both the foreign substance and the body's own tissues. Transglutaminase enzymes, found throughout the body including in the thyroid and brain, have molecular markings very similar to gliadin antigens, potentially explaining the link between gluten consumption and autoimmune conditions affecting multiple organ systems.

## Key facts

- Molecular mimicry may occur when pathogens attempt to better fit into the host environment, meaning the body's immune system creates antibodies that can also attack similar-looking tissues. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p244]
- Transglutaminase enzymes have molecular markings very similar to gliadin antigens, so in susceptible persons, eating gluten may lead the body to attack both gliadin and transglutaminase enzymes. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p244]
- Dysfunction of transglutaminase enzymes has been implicated in Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, and ALS. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p244]
- The prevalence of celiac disease in patients with autoimmune thyroid disease was two- to fivefold compared to healthy controls. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p245]
