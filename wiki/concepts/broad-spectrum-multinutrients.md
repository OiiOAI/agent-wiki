# Broad-Spectrum Multinutrients

## Summary

Broad-spectrum multinutrients (also called broad-spectrum multinutrient formulas) refer to supplements containing a full range of dietary minerals and vitamins—in balance, at appropriate doses—alongside amino acids and antioxidants. The term was coined to distinguish from single-nutrient supplementation. The authors argue that studying nutrients one at a time (the 'magic bullet' approach) was the wrong methodology, and that broad-spectrum multinutrient formulas have consistently produced positive results in clinical trials for mental health conditions, whereas single-nutrient studies rarely changed clinical practice.

## Key facts

- Broad-spectrum multinutrients contain approximately 30 dietary minerals and vitamins, along with some amino acids and antioxidants, in a balanced formula. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p18]
- The two specific products most studied by the authors are EMP (originally called EMPower, later EMPowerplus and EMPowerplus Advanced) and DEN (Daily Essential Nutrients by Hardy Nutritionals). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p18]
- Every clinical trial using broad-spectrum multinutrients to treat psychiatric symptoms has shown positive results; approximately 80% of participants experience some benefit, with about 50% being much to very much improved. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
- In a study of adults with ADHD published in the British Journal of Psychiatry (April 2014), after eight weeks, twice as many responded in the high-dose multinutrient group compared to placebo; hyperactivity and impulsivity dropped into the normal range. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
- At a one-year follow-up of the ADHD study, those who stayed on micronutrients maintained or showed further improvements, while those who stopped or switched to medications saw symptoms worsen. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
- In two studies—one with 358 adults and one with 120 children with bipolar symptoms—micronutrients reduced symptoms by an average of 50%, sustained for six months with simultaneous reduction in medication use. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
- Nutrients given to survivors after natural disasters or traumatic events showed that well-nourished individuals recovered better; even after a year, those who received nutrients did better than those who did not. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p26]
- The authors explicitly state that there is nothing in their research suggesting that only EMP and DEN benefit mental health, and they advocate for more high-quality formulas to be studied by independent scientists. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p19]
