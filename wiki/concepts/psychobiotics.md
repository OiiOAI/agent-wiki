# Psychobiotics

## Summary

Psychobiotics are probiotics that influence the brain and behavior through the gut-brain axis. Research in mice has shown that certain probiotic strains like Lactobacillus rhamnosus and Lactobacillus reuteri can reduce depressive behaviors and improve social behavior, potentially through vagus nerve communication and effects on neurotransmitters including GABA and serotonin.

## Key facts

- Mice fed Lactobacillus rhamnosus probiotic showed increased anti-anxiety GABA receptors in parts of the brain compared to control mice. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p277]
- The effect of Lactobacillus rhamnosus on behavior was absent in mice with severed vagus nerves, suggesting the mechanism was direct microbial communication with the brain. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p277]
- Mice born to mothers fed a high-fat, high-sugar diet showed autism-like social behavior symptoms and had nine times less Lactobacillus reuteri; supplementing with L. reuteri corrected these behavioral deficits. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p278]
- Lactobacillus reuteri was present in 30 to 40 percent of the population in the 1960s but is now found in only 10 to 20 percent, likely due to diminished intake of fermented foods, fiber, ultra-processed foods, and antibiotic use. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p278]

## Inferences

- Inference: The decline of beneficial gut bacteria may be contributing to increases in autism and related conditions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific mechanisms by which psychobiotics influence brain function in humans are still being studied, and optimal probiotic formulations remain uncertain.
