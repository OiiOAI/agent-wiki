# Thousand Brains Theory

## Summary

The Thousand Brains Theory, proposed by Jeff Hawkins, is a framework for understanding how the neocortex creates intelligence. It posits that the neocortex contains approximately 150,000 cortical columns, each of which learns a complete model of objects and the world using reference frames. Rather than a single unified model, perception emerges from a consensus among many independent models.

## Key facts

- The theory was formally proposed by Jeff Hawkins after a breakthrough in understanding in 2016. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p16]
- Each cortical column learns its own complete model of the world, leading to thousands of parallel models rather than one unified model. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p10]
- Perception emerges from a democratic consensus among cortical columns, which may even involve dispute between different models. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p10]
- The theory builds on Vernon Mountcastle's proposal that all cortical columns perform the same basic function. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p35]
- The theory proposes that reference frames are the universal framework that Mountcastle hypothesized but could not identify. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p59]
- Each cortical column is a complete sensory-motor system capable of learning models of complete objects, not just simple features. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p96]
- Knowledge about any particular object is distributed among thousands of complementary models in different cortical columns. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p98]
- Cortical columns vote to reach consensus on what object they are sensing, which explains our singular perception despite multiple models. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p99]
- The theory replaces the traditional view of the neocortex as a hierarchy of feature detectors with a model of distributed object recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p93]
- The neocortex uses reference frames to learn the structure of objects, including shape, how they change, and spatial relationships. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Thousand Brains Theory implies that consciousness and unified perception are emergent properties arising from consensus among many semi-autonomous columns, rather than from a central processor.
- Inference: The Thousand Brains Theory explains why V1 and V2 are relatively large brain regions—complete object recognition occurs there, not just feature detection.
- Inference: The theory provides a solution to the binding problem by showing how different sensory inputs are unified through voting rather than convergence to a single location.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact proportion of hierarchical learning within single columns versus between cortical regions remains unclear.
- Uncertain: How attention is implemented through the thalamus requires further research.
