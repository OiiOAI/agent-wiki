# Micronutrients and Brain Metabolism

## Summary

Micronutrients (minerals and vitamins) are the essential cofactors required for brain metabolism, including the synthesis of neurotransmitters such as serotonin and dopamine. The brain uses 20–40% of the body's nutrients and energy while constituting only about 2% of body weight. Neurotransmitter production depends on enzymes, and every enzyme requires multiple micronutrient cofactors to function. For example, converting tryptophan to serotonin requires iron, phosphorus, calcium, and vitamin B6; the breakdown pathways require additional B vitamins and trace minerals. Without adequate micronutrient supply, brain metabolic pathways become slow and inefficient.

## Key facts

- A quart of blood passes through the brain every minute—15 to 20% of total blood volume—despite the brain being only about 2% of body weight. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p50]
- The brain uses 20 to 40% of all the nutrients and energy consumed, even though it is only about 2% of body weight. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p51]
- Converting tryptophan to serotonin requires four micronutrients: three minerals (iron, phosphorus, calcium) and one vitamin (B6). The serotonin breakdown pathway requires niacin, riboflavin, and molybdenum. Tryptophan breakdown requires five minerals (calcium, iron, potassium, zinc, copper) and three B vitamins. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p53]
- The mitochondria produce ATP (adenosine triphosphate), the energy molecule essential for life, and every step of this process is dependent on dietary nutrients as cofactors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p54]
- Methylation—the transfer of methyl groups to DNA, hormones, and neurotransmitters for repair and regulation—requires zinc, folate, vitamin B2, vitamin B6, and vitamin B12 as cofactors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p55]
- Human cells grown in cultures lacking folate displayed the same amount and types of DNA breaks that occurred with radiation exposure. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p56]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since tryptophan-to-serotonin conversion alone requires eleven distinct micronutrients (four vitamins and seven minerals), and similar complexity applies to all other neurotransmitter pathways, no single nutrient can optimize brain function on its own. This supports the book's argument against 'magic bullet' single-nutrient approaches.
