# Post-Marxist Theory

## Summary

Post-Marxist theories are the most recent varieties of neo-Marxian theory that reject many basic premises of Marx's original theory and earlier neo-Marxian theories while maintaining sufficient affinities with Marxism to be considered part of the neo-Marxian tradition. They emerged from both external changes (end of the Cold War, collapse of world communism) and internal intellectual shifts (poststructuralism, postmodernism, analytical Marxism).

## Key facts

- Post-Marxist theories emerged from two sets of factors: external changes including the end of the Cold War and collapse of world communism, and internal changes including new theoretical currents like poststructuralism and postmodernism. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p208]
- Analytical Marxism is criticized by Aronson as 'Marxism without Marxism'—pure theories lacking practice, surviving as one voice among many in a theoretical landscape. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p217]
- Many neo-Marxian theorists are unhappy with post-Marxist developments, attacking analytical Marxists for eliminating the issue of history and postmodern Marxists for political quietism and cynical defeatism. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p218]

## Inferences

- Inference: The emergence of post-Marxist theories reflects a broader fragmentation of grand theoretical projects in the late twentieth century, as the failure of real-world communism discredited the integrated theory-and-practice model that Marxism represented.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The boundary between neo-Marxism and post-Marxism is contested; some practitioners maintain they are still Marxists while critics argue the designation has become meaningless.
