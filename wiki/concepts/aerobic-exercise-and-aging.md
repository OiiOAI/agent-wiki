# Aerobic Exercise and Aging

## Summary

Aerobic exercise protects against cognitive and emotional decline during aging by increasing blood volume, regulating fuel, encouraging neuronal activity and neurogenesis. The aging brain is more vulnerable to damage, so exercise has more pronounced effects than on young adults. Exercise sparks connections and growth in brain networks, increases neurotrophins like BDNF and VEGF, and improves the brain's ability to compensate. Studies show that six months of walking increased brain volume in frontal and temporal lobes, making brains look 2-3 years younger.

## Key facts

- Starting at about age 40, humans lose on average 5% of overall brain volume per decade. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p181]
- In a 6-month study, sedentary 60-79 year olds who walked on treadmills showed increased brain volume in frontal and temporal lobes on MRI scans; their brains looked 2-3 years younger. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p183]
- The Nurses' Health Study found women with highest energy expenditure had 20% lower chance of cognitive impairment; benefits were seen with walking just 1.5 hours per week. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p179]
- Exercise increases blood volume, regulates fuel, and encourages neuronal activity and neurogenesis in aging brains. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p181]
- Exercise counters the natural decline of dopamine, the key neurotransmitter in motivation and motor systems. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p191]
- People who feel lonely are twice as likely to develop Alzheimer's, and studies show exercise reduces depression and is better than Zoloft at preventing relapse. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p185]

## Inferences

- Inference: The aging brain being more vulnerable to damage means interventions have more pronounced effects than on young adults, suggesting it's never too late to benefit from exercise.
