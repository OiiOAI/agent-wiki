# Binocular Disparity and Stereopsis

## Summary

Binocular disparity is the difference in position of corresponding retinal images between the two eyes, providing a powerful cue for depth perception. Cortical neurons selective for specific ranges of disparity compute depth from the slight differences in the two eyes' views of the world.

## Key facts

- Binocular disparity results from the separation of the two eyes; images of objects in the plane of fixation fall on corresponding retinal points, while objects nearer or farther fall on noncorresponding points. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]
- Binocular neurons in visual cortical areas are selective for narrow ranges of disparity: some are tuned to the fixation plane (tuned excitatory/inhibitory), others respond only to near or far objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]
- Disparity capture occurs when unambiguous depth assignment in one image region influences depth calculation in regions with insufficient information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]
- Random-dot stereograms demonstrate global disparity analysis: coherent depth perception emerges from disparity between otherwise incoherent dot patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p552]
- Neurons in area V2 respond to illusory edges formed by binocular disparity cues, demonstrating sensitivity to global disparity information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p553]

## Inferences

- Inference: The computation of depth requires matching features between the two eyes, which is a global calculation that can propagate disparity information across the visual field.
