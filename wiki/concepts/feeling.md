# Feeling

## Summary

A feeling is the private, mental experience of an emotion, as opposed to emotion itself which has publicly observable components. Feelings are poised at the threshold separating being from knowing, having a privileged connection to consciousness. An organism may represent a feeling nonconsciously without ever knowing it is occurring.

## Key facts

- The term feeling should be reserved for the private, mental experience of an emotion, while emotion designates the collection of publicly observable responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p43]
- You cannot observe a feeling in someone else although you can observe a feeling in yourself when you perceive your own emotional states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p43]
- The basic mechanisms underlying emotion do not require consciousness; even the occurrence of a feeling is conceivable without the organism knowing of its occurrence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p43]
- Feelings are poised at the very threshold that separates being from knowing and thus have a privileged connection to consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p43]
- We often realize suddenly that we feel anxious or uncomfortable, pleased or relaxed, but the particular state of feeling has begun before the moment of knowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p37]
- Consciousness must be present if feelings are to influence the subject having them beyond the immediate here and now. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p37]

## Inferences

- Inference: Feelings serve as the bridge between nonconscious emotional processes and conscious self-awareness, making subjective experience possible.
