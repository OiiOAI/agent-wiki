# Six-Level Model

## Summary

The six-level model provides altitude-based horizons for reviewing one's work, from ground level (current actions) up to life purpose. Each level should enhance and align with those above it: Ground (current actions), Horizon 1 (current projects), Horizon 2 (areas of focus and accountability), Horizon 3 (one- to two-year goals), Horizon 4 (long-term visions), and Horizon 5 (life purpose and principles).

## Key facts

- The six levels from bottom up are: Ground (current actions), Horizon 1 (current projects), Horizon 2 (areas of focus and accountability), Horizon 3 (one- to two-year goals), Horizon 4 (long-term visions), and Horizon 5 (life). [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p269]
- Each level should enhance and align with the ones above it. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p269]
- Horizon 2 is the level of current job responsibilities and areas of life to maintain at appropriate standards. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p275]
- From Horizon 3 up, factors of the future and one's direction and intentions are primary. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p277]

## Inferences

- Inference: Working from the bottom up is suggested because without a sense of control at the implementation level, trying to manage from the top down often creates frustration.
