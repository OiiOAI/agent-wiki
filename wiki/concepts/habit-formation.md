# Habit Formation

## Summary

Ravikant emphasizes that habits shape who we are, and transformation comes from consistent daily practice. He advocates making health the number one priority and doing something every day rather than optimizing which specific activity to perform.

## Key facts

- We are trained in habits from childhood—potty training, when to cry, how to smile—these become behaviors we integrate. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p147]
- You become your habits. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p147]
- To have peace of mind, you have to have peace of body first. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p147]
- When you really want to change, you just change. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p148]
- A Duke University study concluded that about 45% of our behavior is from habit. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p23]
- Neural pathways are communication channels in the brain that habits 'look like' physically; as habits become ingrained, associated neural pathways thicken. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p25]
- The 2009 study in the European Journal of Social Psychology found the average time for behavior to become habit was 66 days, with a range of 18 to 254 days. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p27]
- The 21-day habit myth was possibly started by Dr. Maxwell Maltz, a plastic surgeon who found amputees took about 21 days to adjust to limb loss, which the author argues is not comparable to forming habits. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p27]
- Stress has been shown to increase habitual behavior; UCLA and Duke studies found that when stressed, people tend to repeat their usual behaviors rather than make new decisions. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p26]
- Professor Wendy Wood states: 'People can't make decisions easily when stressed, are low in willpower or feeling overwhelmed. When you are too tired to make a decision, you tend to just repeat what you usually do.'. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p26]
- Researchers concluded that missing a day did not physiologically derail a habit, but psychologically missing a day can be a problem if you let it. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p28]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since habits take a long time to form and stress makes us rely on them more, having good habits provides a protective effect against negative spirals during difficult times.
