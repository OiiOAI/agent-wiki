# Neuroactive Peptides

## Summary

Neuroactive peptides are short polymers of amino acids processed from larger polyprotein precursors in the Golgi apparatus and packaged in large dense-core vesicles (70-250 nm diameter). Unlike small-molecule neurotransmitters, they are synthesized only in the cell body and cannot be locally recycled at nerve terminals.

## Key facts

- Neuropeptides are derived from secretory proteins synthesized in the cell body, processed in the Golgi apparatus, and packaged in large dense-core vesicles transported to terminals by axoplasmic flow. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p415]
- More than 50 short peptides are produced by neurons or neuroendocrine cells, including substance P, enkephalins, beta-endorphin, and vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p415]
- Multiple neuroactive peptides can be encoded by a single mRNA translated into one polyprotein precursor, which is cleaved by specific proteases at basic residues to yield different peptide products. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p416]
- After exocytosis, large dense-core vesicle membrane is not recycled locally; new vesicles must be transported from the cell body, and there is no uptake mechanism for neuropeptides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p417]
