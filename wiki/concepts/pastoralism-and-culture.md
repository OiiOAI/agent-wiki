# Pastoralism and Culture

## Summary

Nomadic herding societies in harsh, dry environments where theft of herds represents the primary vulnerability. This generates correlates including militarism, desert monotheism, and cultures of honor. Pastoralist cultures show high rates of warrior classes, military trophies, death-in-battle guarantees of afterlife, economic polygamy, and authoritarian parenting.

## Key facts

- Desert monotheism disproportionately occurs among pastoralists because deserts teach tough, singular things approached with deep fatalism. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p292]
- Pastoralism fosters cultures of honor because rustlers can steal herds but not crops, making theft the central vulnerability. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p291]
- If herders let someone take their camel without retaliation, tomorrow they will take the rest of the herd plus wives and daughters. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p292]

## Inferences

- Inference: Pastoralist roots may explain the Southern American culture of honor, where Scottish, Irish, and northern English herders disproportionately settled.
