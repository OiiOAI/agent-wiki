# Developmental Clock

## Summary

The developmental clock is a cell-intrinsic timing mechanism that determines how many cell divisions occur before cells become committed to final fates. Set early in embryogenesis, the clock reflects the rate of production or accumulation of signaling molecules and appears to differ between species but not within individuals of a species. This mechanism explains the mathematical regularity in brain-body allometries across species.

## Key facts

- The developmental clock is set early in embryogenesis and determines the number of cell divisions before cells become committed to final fates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p201]
- The clock appears to differ between species but not between cells within an individual, explaining why different species follow common growth trajectories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p203]
- Xenotransplantation experiments demonstrate that pig neurons develop on a pig schedule even when growing in rat brains, indicating species-specific clock settings. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p205]
- Human brain maturation is appropriate for our large brain size but prolonged compared to other primates of our body size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p204]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact molecular mechanisms setting the developmental clock have not been fully identified.
