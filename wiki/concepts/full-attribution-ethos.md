# Full-Attribution Ethos

## Summary

A principle borrowed from hard science requiring that one must know all big ideas in disciplines more fundamental than one's own, never make explanations in less fundamental ways when more fundamental explanations are available, and always attribute ideas to their source disciplines.

## Key facts

- Under the full-attribution ethos, one must know all big ideas in all disciplines more fundamental than one's own. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p406]
- Boltzmann's constant is named for Boltzmann because he derived it from basic physics more fundamentally than the discoverer who found it in a less fundamental way. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p406]
- Munger calls Mankiw's approach of grabbing without attribution 'Kiplingism,' after Kipling who 'took what he might require.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p414]
- Munger describes not using full attribution as 'like running a business with a sloppy filing system' that reduces power. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p414]
