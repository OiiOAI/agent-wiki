# Passive Aggression

## Summary

Passive aggression is indirect assertion of power and control while maintaining the appearance of non-aggression. Greene describes it as a strategy used by those who fear direct confrontation but need to assert themselves. The chapter outlines six common strategies: the Subtle-Superiority Strategy (chronic lateness with excuses), the Sympathy Strategy (playing the perpetual victim), the Dependency Strategy (creating dependence through false attentiveness), the Insinuating-Doubt Strategy (undermining through faint praise and comments), the Blame-Shifter Strategy (reversing criticism), and the Passive-Tyrant Strategy (impossible-to-please behavior that keeps others off-balance).

## Key facts

- Passive aggression involves indirect strategies that create desired effects while maintaining plausible deniability of aggressive intent. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p612]
- Chronic passive aggressors often had domineering or neglectful parents, and passive aggression became their childhood strategy for gaining attention while avoiding punishment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p613]
- The Subtle-Superiority Strategy involves chronic lateness or forgetfulness, always accompanied by logical excuses and insincere apologies, to subtly communicate superiority. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p613]
- The Sympathy Strategy involves perpetually positioning oneself as a victim to elicit favors, attention, and care from others. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p615]
- The Dependency Strategy involves excessive attentiveness early in relationships to create dependence, which is then exploited for power and control. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p616]
- The Insinuating-Doubt Strategy uses faint praise, ambiguous comments, and backhanded compliments to undermine targets' self-esteem. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p617]
- The Blame-Shifter Strategy reverses criticism by appearing hurt or silent, potentially conjuring up past wrongs to make the accuser feel guilty. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p619]
- The Passive-Tyrant Strategy involves being moody, inconsistent in praise and criticism, keeping subordinates in a state of uncertainty and hypervigilance. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p620]

## Inferences

- Inference: The chapter implies that passive aggression is more prevalent than overt aggression because most people fear direct confrontation but still need to assert control. It also notes that actively aggressive types like Rockefeller often employ passive-aggressive tactics as additional weapons in their arsenal.
