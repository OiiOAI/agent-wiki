# Memory without Generalization

## Summary

A paradoxical condition observed in S. where exceptional memory for particulars coexisted with profound difficulty forming general concepts. Despite remembering every word, number, and detail perfectly, S. could not organize material into categories, detect logical patterns, or understand abstractions. This resulted in what Jerome Bruner termed 'memory without record'—a failure to convert encounters with particulars into instances of the general.

## Key facts

- S. could not identify that among words he was asked to recall were categories like 'birds' or 'liquids'—he noticed semantic relationships only after reading off all words in a series. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p67]
- When shown a number table that progressed in a simple logical order (1, 2, 3, 4, etc.), S. failed to notice the pattern and simply memorized each number individually. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p69]
- S. stated he would not have noticed alphabetical arrangement of letters in a similar series and definitely would not have recognized logical patterns earlier. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p69]
- Bruner described S.'s condition as 'memory without record'—a capacity to retain particulars but inability to convert them into general instances. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p6]
- S. had difficulty understanding metaphors, metaphors, and figurative expressions because he could not 'get behind the surface images' of words. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p9]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between S.'s exceptional figurative memory and poor logical organization suggests these cognitive functions operate somewhat independently.
- Inference: S.'s inability to generalize may explain his difficulty with reading comprehension, as he could not extract essential meaning from textual details.
