# Complemental Series

## Summary

The complemental series is a concept describing how instances of neurotic illness fall into a series where two factors (sexual constitution and experience, or fixation and frustration) are represented in varying proportions. Extreme cases exist at each end, with most cases showing a mixture.

## Key facts

- Freud introduces the 'complemental series' to describe how cases of neurotic illness fall into a series where the two factors — sexual constitution and experience, or fixation of the libido and frustration — are represented in such a manner that if there is more of the one there is less of the other [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5108]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5108]
- At one extreme are cases where people would have fallen ill 'in any case, whatever they had experienced' due to their libidinal development; at the other extreme are cases where people 'would certainly have escaped falling ill' if their lives had not brought them into particular situations [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5108]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5108]
- Freud compares this to the question of whether a baby comes about through being begotten by its father or conceived by its mother — both determinants are equally indispensable [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5108].
