# The Three Caskets Theme

## Summary

A recurring literary and mythological theme in which a man chooses between three women (appearing as sisters, daughters, or caskets), with the third being the best. Freud interprets this as originally representing the choice of Death, later wishfully transformed into the choice of Love.

## Key facts

- The theme of choosing between three women appears in The Merchant of Venice (lead, silver, gold caskets), King Lear (Cordelia vs. Goneril and Regan), and the Judgement of Paris (Aphrodite vs. Hera and Athena). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4011]
- Caskets are symbols of women, of what is essential in woman, and therefore of a woman herself. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4010]
- In dreams, dumbness is a common representation of death; hiding and pallor are also symbols of death in dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4012]
- The third sister in the theme originally represents the Goddess of Death (Atropos, the third Moira), who was later replaced by the Goddess of Love through wishful reversal. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4014]
- The original myth was distorted by wish-fulfilment: choice replaces necessity (destiny), and the figure of terror is replaced by the fairest and most desirable of women. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4017]
