# Libido Regression in Psychosis

## Summary

Freud's theory that psychosis involves a regression of libido to earlier developmental stages (narcissism, auto-erotism) following detachment from external objects. This regression manifests as megalomania and the 'end of the world' delusion, representing an attempt at reconstruction after the internal catastrophe of libidinal withdrawal.

## Key facts

- Freud proposed that in psychosis, the detachment of libido from the external world is followed by its attachment to the ego, constituting megalomania—a return to the stage of narcissism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3780]
- Schreber's 'end of the world' delusion represented the projection of an internal catastrophe—his withdrawal of love from the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3779]
- The delusional formation in psychosis is not merely pathological but represents an attempt at recovery, a process of reconstruction after the libidinal catastrophe. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3779]
- Freud distinguished paranoia from dementia praecox (paraphrenia) by the different locations of dispositional fixation and different mechanisms for the return of the repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3784]
- In paranoia, the regression extends to narcissism; in dementia praecox, it extends to complete abandonment of object love and return to infantile auto-erotism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3785]

## Inferences

- Inference: This theory unified the understanding of psychosis and neurosis within the libido framework, showing that both involve conflicts between ego and libido, but differ in the degree and direction of regression.
