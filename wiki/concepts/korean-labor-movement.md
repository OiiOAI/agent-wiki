# Korean Labor Movement

## Summary

The Korean labor movement gained political activity during the turmoil following President Park Chung Hee's assassination in 1979 and again in agitation against the military regime of President Chun Doo Hwan in 1987. The movement initiated approximately 3,000 strikes in summer 1987 alone, leading to wage increases of thirty-seven percent in 1987-1988 and broader political liberalization.

## Key facts

- Labor unions became politically active following the assassination of President Park Chung Hee in 1979. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]
- The Korean labor movement initiated approximately 3,000 strikes in the summer of 1987. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]
- These strikes had an important effect in inducing Roh Tae Woo to break with Chun and accept calls for direct elections for the presidency. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]
- Wages rose some thirty-seven percent in the two years 1987-1988 alone following labor unrest. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]

## Inferences

- Inference: The labor movement's success in 1987 marked a turning point in Korean political development, demonstrating that collective action could challenge authoritarian rule.
- Inference: The explosion of labor demands in the late 1980s reflected pent-up grievances from years of repressed labor organizing during the authoritarian period.
