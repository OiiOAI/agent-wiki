# Infant Exile and Return

## Summary

Infant exile and return is a prominent mythological theme found in legend, folk tale, and myth wherein the hero is cast out in infancy—often in a basket, jar, or cave—and later returns to fulfill his destiny. Examples include Sargon of Agade, Chandragupta, Charlemagne, Pope Gregory the Great, and Abraham. The motif serves to combine themes of divine origin, humble origins, and destined triumph.

## Key facts

- King Sargon of Agade was born of a lowly mother, his father unknown, set adrift in a basket of bulrushes on the waters of the Euphrates, discovered by Akki the husbandman whom he was brought up to serve as gardener—later becoming king and emperor, renowned as the living god. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p253]
- Chandragupta was abandoned in an earthen jar at the threshold of a cowshed; when playing 'High King in the Judgment Seat,' he commanded that offenders have their hands and feet cut off, which then immediately returned to place. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p253]
- Pope Gregory the Great was born of noble twins who committed incest at the devil's instigation; his penitent mother set him to sea in a little casket, where after adventures he was elected Pope. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p254]
- Charlemagne was persecuted as a child by his elder brothers and fled to Saracen Spain; after signal services, converting the king's daughter, and secret marriage arrangements, he returned to France, overthrew his persecutors, and triumphantly assumed the crown. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p254]
- The popular Hebrew legend of Abraham's birth tells how his mother abandoned him in a cave, where God sent Gabriel to sustain him with milk from his finger until he could walk and speak. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: The infant exile motif serves multiple functions: it explains how a divine or destined hero could emerge from obscurity, it creates sympathy for the hero by establishing humble origins, and it justifies eventual power by showing the gods or destiny protected the child.
