# Vertebrate Neuro-Bauplan

## Summary

The common organizational plan of the vertebrate nervous system consisting of central and peripheral components, with the central nervous system containing three brain zones (hindbrain, midbrain, forebrain) that are conserved across all vertebrates despite variations in size and complexity.

## Key facts

- The canonical vertebrate nervous system consists of two components: central (brain and spinal cord) and peripheral (network of nerves connecting CNS with body tissues). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p184]
- Within each half-brain of all vertebrates are three broad zones: hindbrain (vegetative functions), midbrain (primitive sensory-motor reflexes), and forebrain (complex behaviors including cognition and emotion). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p185]
- Functions come about by circuits—ensembles of neurons in one area connected by axons to ensembles in other areas, forming functional networks; this wiring pattern is evolutionarily conserved across vertebrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p185]
- Hox genes control development of hindbrain and spinal cord, while different homeobox gene suites control midbrain and forebrain development. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p186]
- Protostomes like fruit flies, bees, and cockroaches also have brains with three major zones subject to anterior-posterior patterning by hox genes, suggesting a common neural heritage from the PDA. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p186]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conservation of three-zone brain organization across both deuterostomes (vertebrates) and protostomes suggests this pattern dates back to their common ancestor.
