# Boundary Conflicts in Friendships

## Summary

The authors identify four types of friendship boundary conflicts: Compliant/Compliant, Compliant/Aggressive Controller, Compliant/Manipulative Controller, and Compliant/Nonresponsive. They provide a boundary checklist for resolving each type.

## Key facts

- In Compliant/Compliant conflicts, neither friend does what they really want because both are afraid of telling the truth to each other. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p146-147]
- The Compliant/Aggressive Controller conflict involves the compliant feeling controlled and resentful while the aggressive controller feels irritated at being nagged. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p147-148]
- The authors note that compliant people volunteer their power to aggressive controllers; 'The compliant controls the aggressive controller by pleasing her, hoping it will appease her and cause her to change.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p148]
- In Compliant/Nonresponsive conflicts, one friend does all the work while the other coasts; Marsha/Tammy is given as an example where Marsha always initiates and Tammy never calls. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p151-152]
- The authors provide a boundary checklist: (1) identify symptoms, (2) identify roots, (3) identify the boundary conflict, (4) determine who needs to take ownership, (5) identify what is needed, (6) determine how to begin, (7) set boundaries, (8) see what happens next. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p147-152]
- The authors caution: 'Don't let anyone fool you. All friendships need to be based on attachment, or they have a shaky foundation.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p153]

## Inferences

- Inference: The authors suggest that external commitments alone (marriage, work, church) cannot sustain friendships without genuine attachment.
