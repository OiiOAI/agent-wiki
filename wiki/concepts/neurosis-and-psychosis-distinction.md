# Neurosis and Psychosis Distinction

## Summary

Freud's 1924 analysis distinguishing neurosis from psychosis: in neurosis the ego suppresses drive impulses in service of reality, while in psychosis the ego withdraws from reality in service of the id. Both involve two steps, with the second being a repair attempt supported by the id's desire for power.

## Key facts

- In a neurosis, the ego suppresses a piece of the id in dependence on reality; in a psychosis, the ego withdraws from a piece of reality in service of the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6071]
- In neurosis, a piece of reality is avoided by flight; in psychosis, it is remodelled. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6073]
- Normal healthy behavior combines features of both reactions—disavowing reality no more than a neurosis, while exerting itself to alter reality as a psychosis does. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6073]
- Both neurosis and psychosis are expressions of rebellion on the part of the id against the external world. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6073]
- The world of phantasy serves as a storehouse from which both neurosis and psychosis draw materials for wishful constructions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6075]

## Inferences

- Inference: Healthy functioning is alloplastic (altering external reality) rather than merely autoplastic (changing internal perception).
