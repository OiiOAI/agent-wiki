# Analog Exploration

## Summary

Analog exploration is the practice of capturing parts of what a curiosity journey might be like in an environment that resembles the actual environment one will enter. It enables faster, cheaper preparation through a three-stage process: imagining and planning on paper, conducting experiments in controlled environments, and approximating reality with deliberate practice in natural settings.

## Key facts

- Analog exploration is a form of personalized risk assessment designed to recognize problems and figure out how to prevent or cope with them. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p125]
- The three purposes of analog exploration are: acting as a testing ground for physical demands or mental strength, building reputation and meeting like-minded people, and making one stronger and more confident. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p125]
- The premortem method involves imagining that a project has failed and focusing on unpacking what could have gone wrong. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p125-126]
- The come-back-from-the-future approach starts with a clear endpoint and works backward to unpack everything that will make the goal possible. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p126-127]
- Claudia Pasquero conducts experiments in controlled lab conditions with total control over parameters to study biological elements. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p127-128]
- Felicity Aston trained her team in the Oman desert because unmappable terrain forced real-time decision-making similar to Arctic conditions. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p130-131]
