# Rostromedial Prefrontal Cortex in Social Dominance

## Summary

The rostromedial prefrontal cortex (RMPFC) plays a crucial role in representing dominance relationships and encoding opponent-specific prediction errors during competitive social interactions. Model-based analyses show it tracks the dominance status of opponents and encodes signed competitive prediction errors in a dynamic fashion throughout competitive tasks.

## Key facts

- The RMPFC, together with TPJ and medial STS, is generally more engaged when subjects compete against other humans compared to computers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p209]
- Model-based analyses highlighted that RMPFC encoded opponent-specific prediction errors and monitored the probability of winning against each player dynamically. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p209]
- The RMPFC activity encoded a signed competitive prediction error that did not reflect winning or losing per se, the identity of the opponent, or interactions of these two factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p209]
- tDCS applied over the RMPFC caused a causal imbalance in learning rates, with more weight placed on victories and less on defeats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p210]

## Inferences

- Inference: The RMPFC's role in encoding competitive prediction errors suggests it may be involved in representing the mental states of others and/or the dominance relationship emerging between two individuals.
