# Dream Censorship

## Summary

The psychical censorship is a mechanism that suppresses dreams from direct expression, forcing them into disguised forms. Freud identifies it as the agency that stands in closer relation to consciousness than the agency it criticizes, acting as a screen between unconscious processes and consciousness. The censorship explains why dreams are not recognizable as wishes and why they exhibit peculiarities and absurdities. It operates by demanding modifications that enable dreams to reach consciousness while concealing their unconscious wish origins.

- The existence of two psychical agencies was inferred because dream formation required hypothesizing one agency that submits the other's activity to criticism involving exclusion from consciousness. - The critical agency (later identified with the preconscious) stands in closer relation to consciousness than the agency it criticizes, functioning as a screen between the latter and consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1780]

- The censorship between unconscious and preconscious acts as a 'watchman' of mental health, and during sleep it relaxes its activities, allowing suppressed impulses to find expression. - During sleep the preconscious wish to sleep protects by shutting the door upon the power of movement, so that unconscious impulses remain harmless even when expressed. ## Inferences

- Inference: The censorship explains why trivial or recent impressions often appear in dreams—they have fewer associations and thus fewer points of contact with repressed material that would trigger censorship.
- Inference: Dream censorship explains why we often find our own dreams strange and puzzling—the material reaching consciousness has been transformed to bypass the censor.
- Inference: Freud's mention of 'ego' and 'egoism' in the context of censorship suggests this is an early articulation of the ego's role in resisting unacceptable unconscious impulses.
- Inference: The resistance encountered in dream interpretation is a manifestation of the same censorship that produces dream distortion.
- Inference: Dream censorship represents a general reduction of mental censorship during sleep, allowing repressed material to find expression in distorted forms.
- Inference: The censorship acts analogously to frontier control commissions that examine suspicious content for forbidden materials.
