# Entity Praise vs. Process Praise

## Summary

Carol Dweck's research demonstrated that praising children for being smart ('You must be so smart') produces different outcomes than praising them for effort ('You must have worked so hard'). Process praise leads to greater resilience, enjoyment of challenges, and intrinsic valuing of accomplishment, while entity praise causes effort to seem suspect and beneath the praised individual.

## Key facts

- When children are praised for working hard, they tend to work harder the next time, show more resilience, enjoy the process more, and become more likely to value the accomplishment for its own sake rather than for the grade. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p611]
- When you praise kids for being smart, effort begins to seem suspect, beneath them—after all, if you're really so smart, you shouldn't have to work hard; you glide, you don't sweat and grunt. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p611]
- The distinction between 'You're so smart' and 'You work so hard' falls on either side of one of the deepest lines drawn by believers in mitigated free will: the belief that one assigns aptitude and impulse to biology and effort and resisting impulse to free will. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p611]
