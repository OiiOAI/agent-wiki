# Truthseeking Groups

## Summary

Truthseeking groups (or decision groups) are teams assembled to make better decisions through collective deliberation while minimizing individual biases. Duke outlines characteristics of effective truthseeking groups including: accountability mechanisms that improve accuracy, explicit rules of engagement based on norms like CUDOS, diversity of viewpoints to counter groupthink, and processes for managing dissent. The groups aim to achieve better outcomes than individuals working alone by systematically considering alternative perspectives and explicitly accounting for uncertainty. Duke contrasts truthseeking groups with other group types that optimize for cohesion, morale, or consensus at the expense of accuracy.

## Key facts

- Truthseeking groups aim to improve decision accuracy through collective deliberation while minimizing individual biases. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2120-L2140]
- Effective truthseeking groups require accountability mechanisms that encourage accurate rather than self-serving reasoning. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2120-L2140]
- Diversity of viewpoints is essential to counter groupthink and confirmatory thought. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2120-L2140]
- Duke cites research on Heterodox Academy showing that lack of political diversity in social psychology may bias research conclusions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2120-L2140]
- Structured dissent channels, like the State Department's Dissent Channel, provide mechanisms for raising concerns without career risk. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2120-L2140]
