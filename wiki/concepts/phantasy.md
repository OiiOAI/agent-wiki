# Phantasy

## Summary

Phantasy (or imagination) is a mental realm withdrawn from the reality principle where abandoned sources of pleasure continue to exist. Freud argues that neurotic symptoms are partly constructed from phantasies, and that 'primal phantasies' may represent a phylogenetic endowment where the individual reaches beyond his own experience into primaeval experience.

## Key facts

- Freud describes phantasy as a mental realm 'withdrawn from the reality principle' where abandoned sources of pleasure are granted further existence, free from the claims of reality [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5132]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5132]
- Freud introduces 'primal phantasies' including observation of parental intercourse, seduction, and castration threat, suggesting these may be a phylogenetic endowment [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5131].
