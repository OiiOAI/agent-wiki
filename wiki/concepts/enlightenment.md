# Enlightenment

## Summary

Enlightenment is described as the state of wholeness, of being 'at one' and therefore at peace with oneself and life. Buddha defined it simply as 'the end of suffering.' Tolle describes it as rising above thought while still being able to use the mind when needed. It is not a superhuman accomplishment but the natural state of felt onedness with Being. Enlightenment ends the 'dreadful enslavement to incessant thinking' and dissolves the illusion of separateness. The book distinguishes between 'living knowledge' (experiential) and mere beliefs, emphasizing that enlightenment must be realized through direct experience rather than intellectual understanding.

## Key facts

- Buddha's definition of enlightenment is 'the end of suffering'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p26]
- Enlightenment is described as a state of wholeness, being 'at one' and at peace. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p28]
- Enlightenment is the end of 'the dreadful enslavement to incessant thinking'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p28]
- The obstacle to enlightenment is identification with the mind, which causes thought to become compulsive. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p28]
- Enlightenment is not a superhuman accomplishment but the natural state of felt oneness with Being. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p25]
