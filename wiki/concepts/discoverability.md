# Discoverability

## Summary

Discoverability is the degree to which a piece of content can be found in a search. In the context of note-taking, it refers to how easy it is to discover and access the specific points in notes that are most immediately useful. Forte identifies discoverability as the element most often missing from people's notes, noting a paradox where more notes collected can paradoxically make them less discoverable.

## Key facts

- Discoverability refers to 'the degree to which a piece of content or information can be found in a search of a file, database, or other information system.'. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p104]
- Discoverability is identified as the element most often missing from people's notes. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p104]
- A paradox exists where the more notes are collected, the more time and effort review takes, and the less discoverable they become. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p105]
