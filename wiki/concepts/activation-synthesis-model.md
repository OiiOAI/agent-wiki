# Activation-synthesis model of dreaming

## Summary

The activation-synthesis model, proposed by Harvard psychiatrists Allan Hobson and Robert McCarley in 1977, holds that dreams result from wild neural firings in various regions of the brain. The authors note that Santiago Ramón y Cajal anticipated this model almost seventy years earlier, writing about dreams arising from irregular neural activity during sleep.

## Key facts

- Hobson and McCarley's activation-synthesis model proposed that dreams result from wild neural firings in various regions of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p46]
- Santiago Ramón y Cajal anticipated the activation-synthesis model approximately seventy years earlier. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p46]
- De Sanctis distinguished between brain structures responsible for starting dreams and others responsible for elaborating their contents, a distinction that reappeared in Hobson and McCarley's 1977 model. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p35]

## Inferences

- Inference: The activation-synthesis model was anticipated by both Cajal and De Sanctis in earlier centuries.
