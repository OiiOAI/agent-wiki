# Social Union

## Summary

A form of human association characterized by two features: (1) persons share final ends, and (2) they value their common institutions and activities as good in themselves. Examples include families, friendships, games, science, and art.

## Key facts

- Human beings have in fact shared final ends and they value their common institutions and activities as good in themselves; they need one another as partners in ways of life that are engaged in for their own sake. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p463]
- Whether individuals have a shared end depends upon the more detailed features of the activity to which their interests incline them as these are regulated by principles of justice; there must be an agreed scheme of conduct in which the excellences and enjoyments of each are complementary to the good of all. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p465]
- A well-ordered society is itself a form of social union of social unions: both characteristic features are present—the successful carrying out of just institutions is the shared final end of all the members of society, and these institutional forms are prized as good in themselves. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p466]

## Inferences

- Inference: The collective activity of justice is the preeminent form of human flourishing, for it is by maintaining these public arrangements that persons best express their nature and achieve the widest regulative excellences of which each is capable.
