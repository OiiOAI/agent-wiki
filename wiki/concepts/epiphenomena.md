# Epiphenomena

## Summary

Epiphenomena are causal illusions where two things appear together and observers mistake correlation for causation. Taleb uses this concept to explain why we wrongly believe universities cause wealth, or that education drives economic growth, when in fact the reverse is true.

## Key facts

- In epiphenomenal situations, you see A and B together and believe A causes B, or B causes A, depending on cultural framework. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p252]
- We see high academic research in wealthy countries and believe research generates wealth, without checking the sequence of events. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p252]
- Clive Granger's method examines sequences of events to establish causation—who came first, A or B—helping debunk fake causation. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p253]
- Greed is an epiphenomenon: it has existed throughout history but systemic fragility is newer; blaming greed for crises mistakes correlation for causation. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p253]
