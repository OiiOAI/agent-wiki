# Coupling

## Summary

Coupling is the idea that behaviors are linked to very specific circumstances and conditions rather than being determined solely by an individual's character or internal traits. Gladwell applies this concept to explain phenomena including suicide (tied to available means), crime concentration (tied to specific geographic locations), and police encounters.

## Key facts

- Behaviors are coupled to specific contexts, meaning the same person may behave differently depending on environmental triggers and available options. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p156]
- When British town gas (which contained carbon monoxide) was phased out in favor of natural gas, suicide rates in England fell dramatically—demonstrating that suicidal behavior is coupled to available means. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p155-157]
- Of 515 people who tried to jump from the Golden Gate Bridge but were restrained, only 25 subsequently killed themselves by other means, showing their suicide decision was coupled to that specific location. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p158]
- Crime is concentrated in a tiny fraction of geographic locations—approximately 3-5% of street segments account for over 50% of crime in cities studied. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p161-162]

## Inferences

- Inference: The coupling concept suggests that changing the environment or removing specific triggers can significantly alter behavior outcomes without changing the underlying individual.
