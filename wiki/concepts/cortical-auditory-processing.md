# Cortical Auditory Processing

## Summary

The auditory cortex processes complex acoustic features through multiple areas organized in hierarchical and parallel streams. Primary auditory cortex (A1) contains tonotopic maps and processes sound frequency, while higher areas encode more complex features including pitch, harmonics, and vocalizations.

## Key facts

- Primary auditory cortex (A1, Brodmann area 41) receives input from the ventral division of the medial geniculate nucleus and is tonotopically organized. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p715]
- A1 neurons encode sound frequency along one dimension with isofrequency contours along the perpendicular dimension. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p715]
- Auditory cortex neurons respond to nonpreferred stimuli with transient onset firing but maintain sustained firing throughout preferred stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p714]
- Stimulus selectivity increases progressively along the ascending auditory pathway, with cortical neurons becoming highly selective in multidimensional acoustic space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p714]
- The auditory cortex has separate processing streams for different sound features including pitch, harmonics, and behaviorally relevant vocalizations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p716]
