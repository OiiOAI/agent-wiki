# Mesolimbic and Mesocortical Dopamine System

## Summary

The brain's reward circuitry arising from the ventral tegmental area projecting to the nucleus accumbens (mesolimbic) and prefrontal cortex (mesocortical). Dopamine is more about anticipation of reward than the reward itself, and codes for discrepancy from expectation. It fuels goal-directed behavior and motivation.

## Key facts

- Dopamine is released not just when reward is obtained but especially when a cue signals potential reward—dopamine is about anticipation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p73]
- Anticipatory dopamine release peaks with greatest uncertainty about whether reward will occur (50% probability produces more than 25% or 75%). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p75]
- If dopamine release doesn't occur after a reward cue, the animal won't perform the required behavior—dopamine fuels goal-directed action. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p76]
- Dopamine neurons code for discrepancy from expectation—get more reward than expected, big burst; less, a decrease. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p71]
- Humans invent pleasures far more intense than natural rewards, causing unnaturally strong habituation and escalating cravings. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p72]
- Dopamine release for 'winning' an auction inhibits dopamine for losing, unlike lottery winnings—auction loss = social subordination. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p70]
- Cooperation in economic games activates dopamine more than betrayal, despite betrayal being more profitable. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p69]
