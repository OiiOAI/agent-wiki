# The Two Worlds of the Hemispheres

## Summary

McGilchrist argues that the two cerebral hemispheres bring into being two fundamentally different but both vital ways of experiencing the world. The left hemisphere creates a world of precision, fixity, manipulation, and clear boundaries - suited for grasping and using things. The right hemisphere maintains connection with the whole, context, the implicit, and lived experience - suited for engagement and 'betweenness'. These two stances toward reality are expressed throughout philosophy and are fundamentally incompatible yet both necessary.

## Key facts

- The left hemisphere is characterised by division - manipulation and use require clarity and fixity which require separation and division; it is the hemisphere of 'either/or'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p199]
- The left hemisphere is bound to see language as consisting of bits strung together, as the words are strung together by syntax. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p173]
- For the left hemisphere, language can come to seem cut off from the world, to be itself the reality; and reality comes to seem made up of bits strung together. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p173]
- The right hemisphere maintains its broader remit and the richness of experience in all its variety. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p184]
- The left hemisphere's relationship with the world is one of reaching out to grasp and therefore to use it; the right hemisphere's appears to be one of reaching out just that, without purpose. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p186]
- The most fundamental observation about the observable universe is that there are forces that tend to coherence and unification, and forces that tend to incoherence and separation. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p187]

## Inferences

- Inference: Philosophy in the West is essentially a left-hemisphere process - verbal, analytic, requiring abstracted, decontextualised, disembodied thinking - yet some philosophers have been compelled to articulate the right-hemisphere world.
