# Population Vector

## Summary

The population vector is a computational method developed by Apostolos Georgopoulos to decode movement direction from the distributed activity of motor cortex neurons. Each neuron contributes a vector pointing in its preferred direction, weighted by its firing rate, and the sum of these vectors predicts the actual movement direction.

## Key facts

- Georgopoulos found that individual motor cortex neurons have a preferred direction of movement and respond in a graded fashion to directions between preferred and opposite directions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p900]
- Vectorial addition of all single-cell contributions produces a population vector that corresponds closely to the actual movement direction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p901]
- Neurons in all parts of the arm motor map contribute to the motor command for each reaching movement, with the pattern of activity changing continuously as a function of intended direction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p901]
- Andrew Schwartz demonstrated that population vectors predict the instantaneous direction and speed of arm movements approximately 100 ms in the future. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p902]

## Inferences

- Inference: The population vector coding scheme suggests that motor commands are generated by widely distributed neural populations rather than by dedicated labeled lines for specific movements.
