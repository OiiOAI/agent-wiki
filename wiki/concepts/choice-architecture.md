# Choice Architecture

## Summary

Choice architecture refers to how choices are designed and presented, which influences behavior without forcing decisions. Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein demonstrated how subtle design changes can significantly impact outcomes—for example, organ donation rates varying from 12% in Germany (opt-in) to nearly 100% in Austria (opt-out). Four key tweaking strategies include: (1) switching up environment so values-aligned choices are easiest, (2) piggybacking new behaviors onto existing habits, (3) precommitment with if-then strategies for anticipated obstacles, and (4) mental contrasting (offsetting positive visions with potential challenges).

## Key facts

- Germany's organ donation rate is 12% (opt-in) while Austria's is nearly 100% (opt-out), demonstrating the power of choice architecture. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p125]
- People eat 90 to 97 percent of what is on their plate regardless of size; using smaller plates can reduce food intake proportionally. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p128]
- Piggybacking new behaviors onto existing habits transfers new actions into habitual behaviors more successfully. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p129]
- Mental contrasting—imagining the future while assessing present reality—links obstacles with plans for overcoming them. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p130]
- Studies show that women who imagined transformative weight loss lost fewer pounds than those who imagined potential temptations. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: Designing one's environment in advance reduces reliance on willpower during moments of temptation.
