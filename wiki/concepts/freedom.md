# Freedom

## Summary

Freedom in Adlerian psychology is achieved through recognizing that one is not living to satisfy others' expectations, and that others are not living to satisfy one's own. True freedom comes from understanding separation of tasks and accepting that one cannot control how others behave or think. The book positions freedom as connected to courage—the courage to be disliked, to make one's own choices, and to accept responsibility for one's life.

## Key facts

- Adlerian psychology states: 'You are not living to satisfy other people's expectations, and neither am I. It is not necessary to satisfy other people's expectations.'. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p118]
- The book identifies freedom as the next topic for discussion, linked to the concept of courage. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p128]
- If one is not living one's life for oneself, no one else will live it—that responsibility cannot be delegated. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p118]
