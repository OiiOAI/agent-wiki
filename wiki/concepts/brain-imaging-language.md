# Brain Imaging of Language

## Summary

Three metabolic imaging techniques have been used to study language function in the brain: regional cerebral blood flow (rCBF), positron emission tomography (PET), and functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI). These reveal that language functions are widely distributed across multiple brain regions and organized hierarchically according to processing time scales.

## Key facts

- For rCBF imaging, inert radioactive gas dissolved in blood indicates where blood flow has increased during language tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p318]
- For PET imaging, a radioactive analogue of glucose is injected to measure cumulative metabolism in different brain regions during language tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p318]
- Mindless repetition only minimally recruits classic language areas, while interpretation of words involves both auditory and prefrontal regions but apparently not traditional Broca's area. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p319]
- Word analysis tasks activate the ventral prefrontal cortex, while motor activity and word analysis independently produce intense activation of the cerebellum on the opposite side of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p320]
- Brain imaging shows hierarchic organization correlating with both time and the hierarchic-segmental organization of sentences, with segregation according to physical form or manipulation of the signal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p323]
- The classic language areas are not unitary modules but complicated clusters of areas, each with different component functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p323]
