# Misreading

## Summary

Misreading is a parapraxis where the reader's preparedness or expectations alter the perceived text, often correcting it to fit desires or defenses. Freud describes cases where distressing information is unconsciously misread into something more palatable.

## Key facts

- Misreadings often result from the reader's preparedness, which alters the text to what they are expecting or occupied with. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2098]
- A misreading can involve correcting text to fit repudiation or wish fulfillment when the original text contains something distressing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2099]
- The reader's profession or present situation determines the outcome of misreading. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2098]
- In some cases the text is first correctly understood before being unconsciously corrected. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2099]

## Inferences

- Inference: Misreadings demonstrate that perception is not passive but influenced by unconscious desires and expectations.
