# Three-Act Pitch Structure

## Summary

A story structure Kleon recommends for pitches, proposals, and requests. The first act is the past—where you've been and what you want. The second act is the present—where you are now and how you've used resources. The third act is the future—where you're going and how the listener can help you get there. Like a Choose Your Own Adventure book, this turns the listener into the hero who decides how it ends.

## Key facts

- A good pitch is set up in three acts: First act is the past—where you've been, what you want, how you came to want it, what you've done so far. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p98]
- Second act is the present—where you are now in your work and how you've worked hard and used up most of your resources. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p98]
- Third act is the future—where you're going and how exactly the person you're pitching can help you get there. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p98]
- Like a Choose Your Own Adventure book, this story shape turns your listener into the hero who gets to decide how it ends. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p98]
- John Gardner's basic plot formula: 'A character wants something, goes after it despite opposition (perhaps including his own doubts), and so arrives at a win, lose, or draw.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p98]
