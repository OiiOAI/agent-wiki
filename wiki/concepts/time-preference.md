# Time Preference

## Summary

Rawls rejects pure time preference as irrational for individuals and unjust for society. For rational persons, impartial concern for all parts of life means mere difference in temporal position is not a rational ground for valuing something more or less. Similarly, in the original position, parties have no reason to favor earlier over later periods since any decision made now equally authorizes future generations to discount the present. Rejecting time preference is compatible with using interest rates to ration investment funds and with accounting for uncertainties and changing circumstances.

## Key facts

- In the case of an individual, pure time preference is irrational: it means the person is not viewing all moments as equally parts of one life. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p269]
- In the case of society, pure time preference is unjust: it means the living take advantage of their position in time to favor their own interests at the expense of future generations. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p269]
- The parties in the original position have no reason to give any weight to mere position in time since there is no ground for using today's discount of the future rather than the future's discount of today—the situation is symmetrical. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p269]
- The rejection of pure time preference is compatible with using an interest rate to ration limited funds for investment and with taking uncertainties and changing circumstances into account. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p269]
