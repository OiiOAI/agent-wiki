# Egocentric Bias

## Summary

Egocentric bias refers to memory distortions that favor the self. This includes claiming more credit for positive events than warranted, exaggerating past difficulties to enhance current accomplishments, and deprecating past selves to maintain favorable views of the present self. The self's role in encoding and retrieval, combined with the motivation to view oneself positively, creates conditions for self-enhancing memory biases.

## Key facts

- When married and dating couples were asked to recall how much they contributed to decisions, one spouse might claim 80% credit while the other claims 40%, with both being inaccurate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p198]
- College students were highly accurate for grades of A (89% correct) but extremely inaccurate for grades of D (29% correct) when trying to recall high school transcripts. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p200]
- Graduate students who recorded anxiety levels before comprehensive examinations exaggerated their pre-exam anxiety levels a month later, particularly those who passed the exam. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p200]
- A substantial majority of college students and middle-aged adults rate their present selves, but not past selves, as above average relative to their peers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p201]
- Self-enhancing biases are reflected in several maneuvers: selective recall, exaggerating past difficulties, and deprecating past selves that surround the present self in a comforting glow of positive illusions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p201]
