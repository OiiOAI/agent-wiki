# Four-Criteria Model

## Summary

The four-criteria model provides a framework for choosing actions in the moment. The criteria, applied in order, are: context (what can be done where you are with available tools), time available (how much time before the next commitment), energy available (mental/physical capacity), and priority (relative importance among remaining options).

## Key facts

- The four-criteria model for choosing actions in the moment, in order, is: context, time available, energy available, and priority. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p258]
- Context is the first criterion: what could be done where you are with the tools you have. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p258]
- Organizing action reminders by context prevents unnecessary reassessments about what to do. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p259]
- The four-criteria model assumes next actions have already been determined and are available on appropriately organized lists. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p263]
