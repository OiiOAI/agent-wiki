# Sense of Guilt

## Summary

The sense of guilt (Schuldgefühl) is presented as one of the most important problems in the development of civilization. Freud traces its origins to the Oedipus complex and the primal parricide, arguing that civilization can only achieve its aims through an ever-increasing reinforcement of guilt. The sense of guilt exists in two forms: fear of external authority and fear of the internalized superego.

## Key facts

- The sense of guilt is the expression of the conflict due to ambivalence, the eternal struggle between Eros and the drive to destruction or death. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6662]
- Whether one has killed one's father or has abstained from doing so, one is bound to feel guilty, for the sense of guilt is an expression of the conflict due to ambivalence. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6662]
- The community, like the individual, evolves a superego under whose influence cultural development proceeds, and the cultural superego sets up strict ideal demands whose violation is met with fear of conscience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6669]
- When a drive trend undergoes repression, its libidinal elements are turned into symptoms and its aggressive components into a sense of guilt. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6668]

## Inferences

- Inference: The sense of guilt produced by civilization often remains unconscious or appears as malaise and dissatisfaction for which people seek other motivations.
- Inference: Remorse differs from the sense of guilt, as it presupposes conscience already existed before the deed took place.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the sense of guilt arising from repressed aggression specifically, rather than from any frustrated drive, is the more accurate formulation.
