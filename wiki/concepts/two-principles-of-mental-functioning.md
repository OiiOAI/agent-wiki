# The Two Principles of Mental Functioning

## Summary

A 1911 paper by Freud introducing the fundamental distinction between the primary process (governed by the pleasure principle) and the secondary process (governed by the reality principle). The pleasure principle dominates in infancy and the unconscious, where wishes are hallucinated into fulfillment. The reality principle develops when the non-occurrence of expected satisfaction leads the psychical apparatus to form conceptions of real external circumstances and seek actual alterations. This development necessitates functions like attention, judgement, and postponed motor discharge (thinking). The paper also discusses how the sexual drives remain longer under pleasure principle dominance through auto-erotism and the latency period.

## Key facts

- The pleasure principle directs mental activity toward gaining pleasure and avoiding unpleasure, and governs primary processes including dreams and repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3919]
- The reality principle was introduced when hallucinatory satisfaction failed; the psychical apparatus began forming conceptions of real external circumstances and endeavoring to make real alterations in them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3919]
- The reality principle proves to be a momentous step that safeguards rather than replaces the pleasure principle, sacrificing momentary pleasure for assured later pleasure. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3922]
- Sexual drives remain longer under the pleasure principle due to auto-erotism and the latency period, making many people never fully able to withdraw from it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3922]
- Thinking is essentially experimental acting with small quantities of cathexis and less expenditure, requiring conversion of freely displaceable cathexes into 'bound' cathexes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3921]

## Inferences

- Inference: The paper represents Freud's first extensive theoretical discussion since the 'Project for a Scientific Psychology' (1895) and Chapter VII of The Interpretation of Dreams (1900), bringing forward quasi-neurological hypotheses into purely psychological terms.
- Inference: The paper foreshadows the structural model (id, ego, superego) by distinguishing between ego drives and sexual drives and their different relationships to the reality principle.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which the conversion from freely displaceable to bound cathexes occurs are described cursorily due to insufficient knowledge.
