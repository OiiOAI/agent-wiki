# Adverse Childhood Experiences Study

## Summary

The Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE) Study is a standard assessment that identifies trauma in childhood by asking about ten categories of adverse experiences including physical abuse, cruelty, and neglect. Research using this framework has demonstrated strong correlations between childhood trauma and numerous health and attention problems in later life.

## Key facts

- The ACE Study asks whether a child has experienced any of ten bad things in their childhood, including factors like physical abuse, cruelty, and neglect. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p190]
- Children who had experienced four or more types of trauma were 32.6 times more likely to have been diagnosed with attention or behavior problems than children who had not experienced any trauma. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p190]
- Dr. Nicole Brown found that childhood trauma tripled the development of ADHD symptoms. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p190]
- A British Office of National Statistics study found that if there's a financial crisis in the family, a child's chances of being diagnosed with attention problems go up 50 percent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p190]
