# Counterwill

## Summary

Counterwill is a psychical mechanism whereby antithetic ideas against an intention or expectation operate to prevent the desired outcome. Freud developed this concept in his 1892–93 case of successful hypnotic treatment of lactation inability, where the patient's counterwill against feeding her baby manifested as vomiting, loss of appetite, and agitation. The concept relates to the broader mechanism by which unconscious ideas can produce somatic symptoms.

## Inferences

- Inference: Counterwill represents an early conceptualization of unconscious opposition to one's own conscious intentions, foreshadowing Freud's later concepts of resistance and repression in psychoanalysis.
- Inference: Counterwill is fundamentally connected to the dissociation of consciousness characteristic of hysteria.
- Inference: The mechanism of counterwill is supremely characteristic of hysteria but also occurs in tic convulsif.
- Inference: Counterwill represents a manifestation of ambivalence, where two opposing emotional tendencies conflict in the unconscious mind.
