# Humour

## Summary

A 1927 paper by Freud on humour, in which he develops a structural (metapsychological) account of how the humorous attitude functions. Drawing on his later theory of the superego, Freud proposes that humour involves the superego addressing the ego with kindly words of comfort, enabling the ego to view the world as a game unworthy of serious concern. Humour represents a triumph of narcissism and the pleasure principle over the unkindness of real circumstances.

## Key facts

- Humour involves the humorist withdrawing the psychical accent from the ego and transposing it to the superego, which then permits the ego to appear tiny and its interests trivial. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6693]
- The grandeur in humour lies in the triumph of narcissism, the victorious assertion of the ego's invulnerability; the ego refuses to be distressed by the provocations of reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6691]
- Humour is not resigned but rebellious; it signifies the triumph not only of the ego but also of the pleasure principle, which is able to assert itself against the unkindness of real circumstances. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6692]
- Humour is the contribution made to the comic through the agency of the superego, paralleling how jokes are the contribution of the unconscious to the comic. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6694]
- The superego, normally a severe master, condescends to enabling the ego to obtain a yield of pleasure through humour, showing that we have still much to learn about the nature of the superego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6694]

## Inferences

- Inference: Humour belongs to the series of methods which mental life constructs to evade the compulsion to suffer, along with neurosis, madness, intoxication, self-absorption and ecstasy.
