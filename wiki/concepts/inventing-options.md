# Inventing Options for Mutual Gain

## Summary

The third principle of principled negotiation is to invent options for mutual gain before deciding what to do. The authors identify four major obstacles to inventing options: premature judgment, searching for a single answer, assuming a fixed pie, and thinking that solving their problem is their problem. Solutions include separating inventing from deciding through brainstorming, broadening options rather than looking for a single answer, looking for mutual gains, and making their decision easy.

## Key facts

- Four major obstacles inhibit inventing options: premature judgment, searching for the single answer, assuming a fixed pie, and thinking that 'solving their problem is their problem.'. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p31]
- To invent creative options, separate the act of inventing from judging; brainstorm without criticism to generate many ideas. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p32-33]
- Agreement is often made possible precisely because interests differ, and differences can be dovetailed to create mutual gain. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p39]
- Look for items that are of low cost to you and high benefit to them, and vice versa—differences in interests, priorities, beliefs, forecasts, and attitudes toward risk make dovetailing possible. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p41]
