# Beauty

## Summary

Beauty arises in the stillness of presence. The book describes that beauty is not recognized by the mind, which can only label and categorize. True perception of beauty requires that the mind be still and the observer be present. Beyond external beauty, there exists an ineffable inner essence or 'holiness' that reveals itself when one is present. The mind, when left to itself, creates ugliness and 'monstrosities,' as evidenced in modern urban landscapes. Many people are so imprisoned in their minds that they cannot truly see nature's beauty—they only perform mechanical mental labeling. The wider the gap between perception and thought, the more depth one has as a human being.

## Key facts

- Mind can neither recognize nor create beauty; only through presence can beauty or sacredness be perceived. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p96]
- Many people 'are so imprisoned in their minds that the beauty of nature does not really exist for them'. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p97]
- The mind, when left to itself, creates 'monstrosities' and ugliness, as seen in urban landscapes. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p97]
- Beyond external forms, there is 'something ineffable, some deep, inner, holy essence' that reveals itself only when present. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p96]
- The wider the time gap between perception and thought, the more depth there is to one as a human being. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p96]
