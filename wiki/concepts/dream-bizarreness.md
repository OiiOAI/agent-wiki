# Dream Bizarreness

## Summary

Dream bizarreness encompasses elements that are impossible, improbable, uncertain, incongruous, or involve scene shifts in dreams. Approximately 75 percent of REM dream reports contain at least one occurrence of some form of bizarreness, but only 10 to 20 percent contain three or more forms or one distinctly impossible event. Bizarreness is less prevalent in non-REM dreams (60 percent) and sleep-onset reports (a third). Object transformations in dreams remain within object categories—suggesting associative constraints operate during dreaming rather than random chaos.

## Key facts

- Approximately 75 percent of REM dream reports contain at least one occurrence of some form of bizarreness. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p152]
- Only 10 to 20 percent of REM dreams contain three or more forms of bizarreness or one distinctly impossible event. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p152]
- Bizarreness is less prevalent in non-REM dreams (60 percent) and shows up in only a third of sleep-onset reports. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p152]
- Dream bizarreness is somewhat greater later in the night and during REM sleep compared to earlier in the night and non-REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p152]
- Object transformations in dreams remain within their class—objects turn into other objects, characters into other characters—but never across categories. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p153]
- The presence of bizarreness in a dream facilitates its encoding, making these dreams more likely to be recalled than mundane dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p152]
