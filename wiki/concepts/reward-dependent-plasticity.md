# Reward-dependent plasticity

## Summary

Reward-dependent plasticity is a synaptic learning rule in which the direction of synaptic change (potentiation or depression) depends on whether a choice was rewarded. In the attractor network model, input synapses onto decision neurons undergo stochastic Hebbian plasticity: synapses potentiate from Down to Up state only when presynaptic and postsynaptic neurons co-activate AND the choice is rewarded, and depress otherwise. This mechanism instantiates reinforcement learning at the synaptic level, allowing synaptic strengths to compute and track returns (reward per choice) rather than income (reward per unit time). Candidate biological loci include cortico-striatal connections and synapses within orbitofrontal cortex.

## Key facts

- In the reward-dependent Hebbian plasticity model, synapses have two states (Down and Up); potentiation corresponds to probabilistic transition from Down to Up, and depression to Up to Down, both depending on coactivation of pre- and post-synaptic neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p495]
- Synapses for inputs to decision neurons are potentiated only if the choice is rewarded, and depressed otherwise, implementing reinforcement learning at the synaptic level. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p495]
- The hypothesis is that dopamine signals can reverse the sign of modification at certain classes of synapses, serving as the reward signal that gates plasticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p495]
- Through reward-dependent plasticity, synaptic strengths cA and cB become proportional to returns RA and RB (reward per choice) for alternatives A and B. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p496]
- Candidate biological loci for this synaptic plasticity include cortico-striatal connections in the basal ganglia and synaptic pathways within the orbitofrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p497]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The biophysical basis of reward-dependent plasticity — whether dopamine directly gates synaptic change or modulates plasticity through secondary mechanisms — remains to be fully elucidated.
