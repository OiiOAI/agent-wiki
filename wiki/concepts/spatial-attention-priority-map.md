# Spatial Attention and the Priority Map

## Summary

Spatial attention allows the brain to select objects in the world for further analysis. The lateral intraparietal area acts as a priority map, summing signals including saccade planning, abrupt stimulus onset, and cognitive aspects of searched-for features. The locus of attention corresponds to the peak of the priority map. Without attention, spatial perception is severely limited, as demonstrated by change blindness.

## Key facts

- The lateral intraparietal area acts as a summing junction for saccade planning, abrupt stimulus onset, and cognitive aspects of searched-for features. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p638]
- The locus of attention can be identified only by examining the entire priority map and choosing its peak. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p638]
- Attention shortens reaction time and makes visual perception more sensitive, including the ability to detect objects at lower contrast. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p636]
- The activity of neurons in the parietal cortex predicts a monkey's locus of spatial attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p643]
- Change blindness occurs when large changes in the visual scene occur outside the focus of attention and are missed until attention is directed to them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p635]
