# Memory Consolidation

## Summary

Memory consolidation is the process by which newly acquired memories become stable and resistant to disruption. Müller and Pilzecker discovered that memory is sensitive to disruption in the first hour after learning but becomes stable after two or more hours. The process requires gene activation and new protein synthesis to convert short-term functional changes into long-term structural changes.

## Key facts

- In 1900, Müller and Pilzecker found that learning a second list immediately after the first interfered with recall 24 hours later, but a two-hour gap did not. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p222]
- This suggested that in the hour after training, memory is still sensitive to disruption and requires time to become fixed or consolidated. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p222]
- Retrograde amnesia from head injuries and concussions occurs because the blow happens before memory traces can be consolidated. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p223]
- Duncan showed in 1949 that electrical stimuli causing convulsions during or immediately after training disrupted memory, but seizures hours later had little effect. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p223]
- Memory consolidation involves signals from synapses traveling to the nucleus to activate regulatory genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p273]
- Single-cell recordings show that cells that fire together during waking experience also fire together during subsequent REM and NREM sleep, reflecting episodic memory trace reactivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p362]
- Sleep deprivation is detrimental to cognitive functioning, and there are clear improvements in memory and problem-solving ability following sleep compared with wakefulness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p361]
- Dreaming about newly learned material enhances subsequent recall, and pre-sleep stories are better recalled in the morning if subjects dreamt about story constituents during the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p362]
- Off-line processing during wakefulness resembles in its functions and effects the off-line processing during sleep, including neural replay of recent experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p365]
- A 12-hour delay with wakefulness shows improved relational memory similar to delays including sleep, demonstrating that off-line processing during wakefulness is especially beneficial for associative memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p367]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mind wandering may be the subjective experience of ongoing off-line processing, including reactivation and recombination of recent experiences.
- Inference: The functions of sleep provide clues about possible functions of off-line processing during wakefulness.
