# Wild Psychoanalysis

## Summary

'Wild Psychoanalysis' (1910) is a paper by Freud criticizing improper psychoanalytic technique. The term refers to amateurish or reckless application of psychoanalytic theories by practitioners who lack proper training and understanding. Freud illustrates his critique with a case of a physician who told a female patient that her anxiety was caused by lack of sexual satisfaction and advised her to 'return to her husband, take a lover, or obtain satisfaction from herself.' The paper outlines technical rules: patients must reach the neighborhood of their repressed material through preparation, and sufficient transference must be formed before interpretive interventions.

## Key facts

- Freud coined the term 'wild psychoanalysis' for amateurish application of psychoanalytic theories without proper training or technique. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3654]
- Freud criticized physicians who reduce sexuality to its 'popular sense'—the need for coitus—without understanding psychoanalysis' extended concept of psychosexuality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3659]
- The paper states that informing patients of their unconscious 'regularly results in an intensification of the conflict in him and an exacerbation of his troubles.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3662]
- Freud established that two conditions must be fulfilled before giving interpretive information: the patient must have reached the neighborhood of what he has repressed, and must have formed sufficient attachment (transference) to the physician. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3662]
- The paper discusses 'actual neuroses' (anxiety neurosis, neurasthenia) as conditions with purely physical causation, which may require different treatment approaches than psychoneuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3660]
- In spring 1910, Freud founded the International Psychoanalytical Association to repudiate responsibility for what is done by non-members who call their procedures 'psychoanalysis.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3663]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's distinction between 'actual neuroses' (somatic causation) and psychoneuroses reflects his evolving view that not all nervous disorders are purely psychological in origin.
