# Comparative Evaluation

## Summary

Comparative evaluation is a decision strategy where two or more options are considered simultaneously to contrast their relative advantages and disadvantages. The initial hypothesis in Klein's firefighter research was that decision makers would use this strategy (the two-option hypothesis), but the study found it was rarely used even for difficult decisions—only 18 of 156 decision points involved comparative evaluation.

## Key facts

- Comparative evaluation was hypothesized as the two-option strategy where decision makers consider a favorite option and a comparison option to show why the favorite was better. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p29-30]
- Soelberg (1967) found that students trained in rational decision methods did not use them for important real decisions like job selection. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p29-31]
- In the firefighter study, only 18 of 156 decision points involved comparative evaluation. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p46]
- Fireground commanders insisted they never made decisions in the sense of comparing options, because there was no time to list all options, let alone evaluate them. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p31]
- The study used conservative coding: any evidence of comparing options was counted as comparative evaluation, even for an instant. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p49]
