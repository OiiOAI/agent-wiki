# Unmanned and Remotely Operated Vehicles

## Summary

Unmanned vehicles are systems where the operator is not onboard but controls the vehicle through a remote control station. They include unmanned air vehicles (UAVs), unmanned ground vehicles (UGVs), and unmanned underwater/surface vehicles (UUVs/USVs). These systems present significant challenges for SA because operators lack direct sensory feedback. Research indicates UAV accident rates are 10-100 times higher than manned aircraft, with human error accounting for 30-70% of accidents depending on the system. Key challenges include poor sensory data, time lags, localization difficulties, data overload, and supporting multi-person team operations.

## Key facts

- Unmanned vehicles are classified into four classes of control: exocentric teleoperation (direct line of sight), egocentric teleoperation (camera views), egocentric semi-automated/automated control, and exocentric semi-automated/automated control. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p240]
- UAVs have accident rates 10-100 times higher than manned aircraft according to U.S. Department of Defense reports. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p241]
- Human error accounts for approximately 30% of unmanned aircraft accidents in Army and Navy systems, rising to 60-70% for Air Force systems, largely related to the Predator UAV. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p242]
- Human operator to robot ratios of 3 to 1 are reportedly common for UGV operations in complex tasks. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p243]
- SA requirements for unmanned vehicles include vehicle status, location, heading, environmental information, sensor status, search area coverage, and projected states. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p244]
- Key challenges for SA in remote operations include poor sensory data, intermittent data, time lags, difficulties in vehicle localization, demanding visual tasks, low-level data overload, multitasking requirements, lack of multi-person support, and increased autonomy management. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p246]
