# Self-Transcendence of the Organism

## Summary

Self-transcendence, as developed by Hans Jonas drawing on Heidegger's concept of transcendence, refers to life's inherent orientation beyond its present condition. Jonas defines it as 'entertaining a horizon beyond its point-identity.' Because an organism must change its matter to maintain its identity, it must project beyond itself—opening into both the temporal horizon of its life cycle and the spatial horizon of the outer world. This self-transcendence is manifested through metabolism, which propels the organism forward in time and outward in space. Jonas links this to biological time and space: 'The internal direction toward the next impending phase constitutes biological time; the external direction toward the co-present not-itself constitutes biological space.'

## Key facts

- Jonas defines self-transcendence as 'entertaining a horizon, or horizons, beyond its point-identity,' achievable only by moving beyond the given condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p154]
- Jonas argues that metabolism propels life outward and forward because the organism must project beyond itself in both space and time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p155]
- According to Jonas, 'the internal direction toward the next impending phase of a being constitutes biological time; the external direction toward the co-present not-itself constitutes biological space.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p155]
- Self-transcendence denotes the fact that being human always points, and is directed, to something, or someone, other than oneself—be it a meaning to fulfill or another human being to encounter. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p83]
- The true meaning of life is to be discovered in the world rather than within man or his own psyche, as though it were a closed system. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p83]
- The more one forgets himself by giving himself to a cause to serve or another person to love, the more human he is and the more he actualizes himself. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p83]
- Self-actualization is possible only as a side-effect of self-transcendence; the more one would strive for self-actualization, the more he would miss it. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p83]
