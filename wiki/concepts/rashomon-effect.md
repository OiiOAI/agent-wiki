# Rashomon Effect

## Summary

The Rashomon Effect describes situations where different witnesses to an event provide significantly different accounts of what happened. Named after Akira Kurosawa's 1950 film 'Rashomon,' it illustrates how incompleteness becomes a tool for bias—each account is filtered through the observer's perspective, resulting in drastically different narratives of the same event. This effect underscores the importance of gathering multiple perspectives and committing to comprehensive data sharing within decision groups.

## Key facts

- The Rashomon Effect is named for the 1950 cinematic classic directed by Akira Kurosawa. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- In the film, four people give drastically different accounts of the same observed scene. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
- The central element of the film demonstrates how incompleteness is a tool for bias. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1325-L1477]
