# Synaptic Competition

## Summary

Synaptic competition is a Darwinian-like developmental process whereby neurons overproduce axonal connections that sample many targets, followed by selective elimination based on correlated firing patterns. Axons that fire synchronously with their target neurons receive growth factor support and persist, while out-of-sync axons lose support and are eliminated. This process creates functionally adaptive neural circuitry with minimal prespecification of details.

## Key facts

- During development, axons initially overproduce and sample many potential targets, but only a fraction of these connections are retained into adulthood through competitive elimination. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p217]
- Donald Hebb's mechanism proposes that axons firing synchronously with recipient cells receive growth factors and strengthen, while asynchronous axons lose support. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p217]
- In early cortical development, axons from every region of cerebral cortex grow to all potential targets, with selective loss later producing regional specialization. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p219]
- Fetal cortex transplanted from one region to another connects as though nothing had happened, demonstrating initial nonspecificity of connection patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p219]
