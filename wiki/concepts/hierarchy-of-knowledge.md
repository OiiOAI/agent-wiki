# Hierarchy of Knowledge

## Summary

The book discusses hierarchy as the fundamental structure of Western knowledge, including kingdoms, empires, churches, armies, businesses, tables of contents, mechanical assemblies, and scientific knowledge. The motorcycle is presented as a system—a hierarchy of concepts, with boxes containing smaller boxes. Philosophy is seen as the highest echelon of this entire hierarchy, broader and more general than any branch of science.

## Key facts

- The structure of concepts is formally called a hierarchy, which since ancient times has been a basic structure for all Western knowledge. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p83]
- Philosophy is seen as the highest echelon of the entire hierarchy of knowledge, a revelation for Phædrus who discovered that science is only a branch of philosophy. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p104]
- The overall name of interrelated structures—hierarchy of containment, causation, and others—is 'system.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p83]
