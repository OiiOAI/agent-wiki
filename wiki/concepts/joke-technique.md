# Joke Technique

## Summary

Freud's systematic analysis of the technical methods used in jokes, encompassing condensation with substitute formation, multiple use of the same material, double meaning, displacement, absurdity, unification, and representation by the opposite. These techniques are dominated by a tendency to compression and economy.

## Key facts

- Joke technique is divided by Freud into three main groups: condensation (with composite words or modification), multiple use of the same material, and double meaning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2690]
- Condensation with substitute formation creates composite words like 'famillionär' (from 'familiär' and 'Millionär') which are the vehicles of the joke's laughter-compelling effect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2669]
- Displacement technique involves diversion of the train of thought and shifting of psychical emphasis onto a topic other than the opening one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2698]
- All joke techniques are dominated by a tendency to compression or economy, which Freud summarizes in Hamlet's words: 'Thrift, thrift, Horatio!'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2691]

## Inferences

- Inference: The joke-work (Witzarbeit) identified by Freud shows striking parallels to the dream-work described in The Interpretation of Dreams, particularly in processes of condensation and substitute formation.
- Inference: The variety of joke techniques suggests that brevity alone does not constitute a joke; there must be a peculiar kind of abbreviation on which the joking character depends.
