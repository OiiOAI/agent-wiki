# Choice set size effects

## Summary

Choice set size effects describe the phenomenon where decision performance degrades as the number of options increases. This may be explained by neural noise constraints in decision circuits: as the choice set grows larger, Poisson-distributed noise in LIP neurons may swamp the signal, leading to inefficiencies. Processes like choice set editing may be mechanisms used by frontal areas to winnow the choice set before processing in parietal areas.

## Key facts

- As the choice set becomes larger, noise may swamp the signal in LIP, leading to profound inefficiencies when selecting amongst many movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Choice set size effects in human choosers may be explained by the finite dynamic range and noise of parietal neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Processes like choice set editing may be tools used by frontal areas to winnow the choice set operated on in parietal areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]

## Inferences

- Inference: Choice set size effects may be a neural-level explanation for well-documented behavioral phenomena like the paradox of choice, where excessive options reduce satisfaction.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific neural mechanisms of choice set editing remain to be characterized.
