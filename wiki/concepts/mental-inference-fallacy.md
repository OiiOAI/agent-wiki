# Mental Inference Fallacy

## Summary

The mental inference fallacy is the scientific mistake of labeling animal behavior using human mental state words (like 'fear' or 'anger') and then inferring that the animal experiences those emotions. When scientists observe a rat freeze in response to a tone previously paired with shock, they call it 'fear learning' and attribute fear to the rat. However, freezing is a behavior, not an emotion—the feeling of fear is a human construction requiring emotion concepts that animals lack. The fallacy occurs when scientists blur the distinction between movement, behavior, and experience.

## Key facts

- The mental inference fallacy occurs when scientists label a behavior like freezing using a mental state word like 'fear' and attribute that mental state to the animal. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p296]
- Scientists who study 'fear learning' are actually studying classical conditioning—pairing a tone with an electric shock to make the tone trigger a freezing response. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p295]
- The rat's brain and body respond as if expecting the shock, but scientists cannot know if the rat experiences fear or simply pain or vigilance. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p294]
- Neuroscientist Joseph E. LeDoux, who popularized 'fear learning,' now argues against using the term 'fear' altogether when referring to a rat, calling it a survival circuit that controls freezing behavior. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p298]
- Baby rats separated from their mother make a high-pitched noise that scientists inferred was distress, but the sound is actually caused by the babies trying to regulate their body temperature. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p298]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mental inference fallacy has created a decades-long misunderstanding in neuroscience, where an entire 'fear' research industry was built on a mislabeled behavior.
- Inference: This fallacy explains why there are so many news stories about animal emotions—the public is reading scientists' mental inferences as scientific facts.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which scientists can ever truly know what animals subjectively experience remains uncertain.
