# Goodness as Rationality

## Summary

Goodness as rationality is Rawls' descriptive theory of value with three stages: (1) A is a good X if it has the properties rational to want in an X; (2) A is a good X for K if it has properties rational for K to want given his circumstances and plan; (3) A is a good X for K when K's plan of life is itself rational. This theory explains why criteria of evaluation vary across kinds of things.

## Key facts

- Stage 1: A is a good X if and only if A has the properties which it is rational to want in an X, given what X's are used for. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p357]
- Stage 2: A is a good X for K if and only if A has the properties which it is rational for K to want in an X, given K's circumstances, abilities, and plan of life. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p357]
- Stage 3 adds that K's plan of life must itself be rational. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p357]
- The concept of rationality by itself is not an adequate basis for the concept of right; in contract theory the latter is derived separately. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p360]
- Goodness as rationality is a descriptive theory maintaining that 'good' has a constant sense analogous to a function sign. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p362]

## Inferences

- Inference: This theory is 'descriptive' because it explains the constant meaning of 'good' rather than assigning it a special prescriptive or emotive meaning.
