# Infantile Material in Dreams

## Summary

Infantile material refers to memories and experiences from early childhood that may appear in dreams, often unrecognized by waking consciousness. Freud identifies this as a third characteristic of dream content, alongside the preference for recent impressions. Such material gains access to dreams either directly (as in children's dreams) or disguised through displacement in adult dreams.

## Key facts

- Freud records Maury's case of a man who revisited his childhood home after 20 years and dreamed of an unknown man and place, later confirmed as genuine childhood memories. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1453]
- The colleague who dreamed of a yellow lion had forgotten it was his childhood toy, demonstrating how infantile material persists unconsciously. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1454]
- Analysis often reveals that what seemed indifferent in childhood has become significant through later displacement processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1447]
- Freud states that nothing that has really remained indifferent can be reproduced in a dream; apparently trivial childhood elements have typically taken on significance through displacement. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1447]

## Inferences

- Inference: The appearance of infantile material in dreams supports the importance of childhood experiences in shaping adult psychology, a central tenet of Freudian theory.
