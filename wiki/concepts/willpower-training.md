# Willpower Training

## Summary

Willpower can be strengthened through regular exercises that train self-control, similar to building a muscle. Research by Mark Muraven, Dianne Tice, and Roy Baumeister found that two weeks of posture exercises or food journaling improved self-control in unrelated tasks. The most effective exercises involve changing habitual behaviors.

## Key facts

- In the first willpower-strengthening experiment, posture exercises (standing and sitting up straight) produced the best results, with improvement most pronounced among students who followed the advice most diligently. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p123]
- Emotion regulation practice (trying to feel positive emotions) did not strengthen willpower, because emotional control relies on tricks rather than genuine willpower. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p123]
- Training increased stamina rather than power; people's initial handgrip strength didn't improve much, but their ability to resist depletion during subsequent tasks did improve. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p124]
- Exercising self-control in one area improved all areas of life—students who did study discipline exercised more and spent less impulsively, and those in fitness programs studied more diligently. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p127]

## Inferences
