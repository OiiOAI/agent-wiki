# Dissociative Identity Disorder

## Summary

A psychiatric condition in which individuals develop multiple master control sites for personality and identity, potentially connected with different life histories and anticipated futures. Damasio uses this condition to argue for the separability of autobiographical self from core self.

## Key facts

- Multiple personality disorder patients may have more than one master control site connected with different convergence zones and dispositions for identity and personality. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
- Damasio suspects multiple master control sites are located in the temporal and frontal cortices and that switching between them enables personality switches. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
- Despite having multiple autobiographical selves, such patients continue to have only one mechanism of core consciousness and only one core self, as all autobiographical selves must use the same central resource. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
- Although multiple personalities may be linked to certain types of biological propensity, they are highly dependent on cultural factors for development and shaping. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p294]
