# Calibrated Uncertainty

## Summary

Calibrated uncertainty is the practice of expressing one's confidence level in a belief or prediction using a numerical probability, rather than vague language. This approach makes implicit knowledge explicit and invites productive debate about the basis for different viewpoints.

## Key facts

- When someone expresses uncertainty numerically (e.g., 'seventy-three percent'), it functions as a communication signal that can be immediately understood by others familiar with this practice. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1944-L1947]
- Expressing uncertainty as a numerical probability invites discussion and debate about the topic, turning a declaration into an opening for dialogue. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1944-L1947]
- Annie Duke's lunch order estimate of 'seventy-three percent' was immediately understood by Stuart Firestein, who then offered his own calibrated estimate (forties) for the same question. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1944-L1947]
