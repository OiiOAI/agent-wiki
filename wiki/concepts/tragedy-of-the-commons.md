# Tragedy of the Commons

## Summary

The tragedy of the commons, described by Garrett Hardin in 1968, occurs when there is escalation or growth in a commonly shared, erodible resource. Each individual benefits from using the resource but shares the costs of its abuse with everyone else. There is weak feedback from the resource's condition to the decisions of users. The solution requires restoring the missing feedback link through education, privatization (so each user feels direct consequences), or regulation ('mutual coercion, mutually agreed upon').

## Key facts

- Hardin's classic example: each rational herdsman adds more cattle to the common pasture because the positive utility of one more animal is +1 while the negative utility shared among all is only a fraction of -1. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p145]
- The tragedy arises from missing or too long delayed feedback from the resource to the growth of users. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p147]
- Examples include overfishing, overexploitation of national parks, and burning fossil fuels causing climate change. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p147]
- Three solutions: educate/exhort users, privatize the commons, or regulate access through mutual coercion mutually agreed upon. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p148]
- Regulation must be enforced by policing and penalties and requires regulators who are informed, not corrupt, and have the good of the whole community at heart. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p149]
