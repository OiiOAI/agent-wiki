# Outside Money Is Plan Z

## Summary

The concept treats outside funding as a last resort to be avoided, presenting it as a 'noose' that gives up control, creates pressure for quick returns over quality, becomes addictive, usually results in bad deals for founders, shifts focus from customers to investors, and is extremely distracting. The authors argue that in a service economy, most businesses don't need expensive infrastructure and can start with minimal capital. They recommend taking on as little outside cash as possible and looking for alternatives before seeking investment.

## Key facts

- The authors argue that outside funding gives up control because founders must answer to investors who may disagree with future decisions. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p39]
- Outside investors wanting their money back in three to five years causes long-term sustainability to be sacrificed for cash-out timing. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p39]
- Raising money is described as incredibly distracting, taking months of pitch meetings and legal maneuvering when focus should be on building something great. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p39]
- The authors note that many business owners who have taken outside funding later regret it, experiencing an investment hangover where they feel trapped by someone else calling the shots. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p40]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bootstrapping forces market validation early because revenue must cover costs from the beginning, unlike venture-funded companies that can postpone profitability.
