# MAP Kinase and Memory

## Summary

MAP kinase (mitogen-activated protein kinase) is a kinase recruited by protein kinase A that migrates to the nucleus during repeated stimulation, where it helps activate genes for long-term memory. Together with PKA, MAP kinase inactivates CREB-2 and activates CREB-1, allowing gene expression required for synaptic growth.

## Key facts

- Protein kinase A recruits another kinase called MAP kinase, which is associated with synaptic growth and migrates to the nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p274]
- Once in the nucleus, protein kinase A activates CREB-1 and MAP kinase inactivates CREB-2. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- Thus long-term facilitation requires not only switching on of some genes, but also switching off of others through MAP kinase action on CREB-2. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
