# Screen Memories

## Summary

A concept introduced by Freud in 1899 referring to memories whose value lies not in their own content but in the relation existing between that content and some other material that has been suppressed. Screen memories owe their retention to a connection with repressed thoughts, and they are characterized by being extremely well remembered while their content is completely indifferent to conscious understanding. They are formed when a repressed wish seeks to transform itself into a childhood scene capable of becoming conscious.

- Screen memories are characterized by being extremely well remembered while their content is completely indifferent. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1198]

- Two psychical forces are concerned in bringing about screen memories: one takes the importance of the experience as a motive for seeking to remember it, while the other tries to prevent any such preference from being shown, resulting in a compromise. - Screen memories stand out from the void of childhood memories and cannot justify their survival by their apparent importance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5004]
- Through the processes of condensation and displacement, what is important is replaced in memory by something that appears unimportant. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5004]
- Freud's own dream provided an example: he saw a one-eyed doctor in a dream who corresponded to the doctor at his birthplace, which he had forgotten by age three.
