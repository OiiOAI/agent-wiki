# Neurons and Nervous Systems

## Summary

Neurons are specialized cells that enable rapid communication across long distances through electrical signals and neurotransmitter release at synapses. Nervous systems evolved to solve the problem of coordinating behavior in multicellular animals, connecting sensory receptors with motor effectors. They first appeared in Cnidaria alongside muscles, with sponges lacking true neurons despite their multicellularity.

## Key facts

- Neurons communicate rapidly across long distances through electrical action potentials and neurotransmitter release at synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p132]
- Neurons have a cell body, dendrites for receiving input, and an axon for sending output over long distances. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p132]
- A synapse consists of presynaptic terminal (sender), postsynaptic site (receiver), and the synaptic cleft between them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p133]
- Nervous systems are fundamentally sensory-motor integration devices that help organisms survive by processing sensory information and organizing responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p133]
- Sponges lack true neurons despite being multicellular animals; nervous systems first appeared in Cnidaria. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p132]
