# Subjective Value

## Summary

Subjective value is the value that an individual places on a stimulus, computed before contact with the stimulus, which can facilitate approach towards or avoidance of the stimulus and can be dynamically assigned based on experience. The striatum plays a critical role in representing subjective value, with ventral regions processing anticipated value and dorsal regions processing outcome value.

## Key facts

- Subjective value is computed before contact with the stimulus and can facilitate approach towards or avoidance of the stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p389]
- Subjective value can be dynamically assigned and updated based on experience with the stimulus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p389]
- Ventral striatal regions represent anticipated value while dorsal striatal regions represent the value of outcomes in the service of choosing future actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p389]
- NAcc activation correlates specifically and proportionally with anticipated gain magnitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p398]
- SV is defined as a real number (0 to 1000) in units of action potentials per second, linearly proportional to mean firing rates of specific neuronal populations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p509]
- SV predicts choice stochastically as the sum of true subjective value and a noise term, making SV theory most closely allied with random utility models from economics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p509]
- When expected utilities predict choice behavior, SV is linearly proportional to those expected utilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p509]
- SV has a unique reference-dependent anchoring point called the baseline firing rate; all subjective values are encoded cardinally relative to this baseline. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p509]
- SV is distinct from welfare or well-being: maximizing SV does not necessarily maximize a chooser's sense of well-being, since SV does not obey the independence axiom globally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p510]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between ventral and dorsal striatum in processing anticipated versus outcome value suggests a temporal flow of information through the striatum during decision making.
- Inference: The finding that NAcc activation correlates with subjective value during anticipation suggests this region is critical for pre-decisional valuation processes.
- Inference: The empirical finding that SV firing rates are linearly related to (subjective) measures of value across diverse reward types (risky gains, delayed gains, certain gains) suggests the valuation system is surprisingly unitary and linear in its encoding properties.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The neural locus of SV encoding is not uniquely identified; candidate areas include the ventral striatum and medial prefrontal cortex, but definitive localization remains uncertain.
