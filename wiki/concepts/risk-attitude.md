# Risk Attitude

## Summary

Risk attitude describes an individual's tendency to avoid or seek risk. Expected Utility theory models this with the curvature of the utility function, while Prospect Theory attributes it to the combined effects of value function shape and probability weighting. Risk attitude is often found to be domain-specific rather than a stable personality trait.

## Key facts

- Risk attitude modeled by EU and traditional risk-return approaches uses a single parameter identified empirically from a person's choices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p127]
- The same person often shows different degrees of risk-taking across financial, career, health, ethical, recreational, and social domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p127]
- Risk-taking for monetary gambling decisions predicted real-world investment decisions far worse than assessed risk-taking for investment decisions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p131]
- Entrepreneurs do not have more positive attitudes towards risk but instead show overly optimistic perceptions of risks involved. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p131]
