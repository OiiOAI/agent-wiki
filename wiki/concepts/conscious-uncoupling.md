# Conscious Uncoupling

## Summary

A therapeutic approach to ending marriages developed and popularized by couples therapist Esther Perel and later coined by Katherine Woodward Thomas. It emphasizes ending relationships with dignity, rituals of closure, and the possibility of maintaining friendship and co-parenting relationships after divorce. Perel uses separation ceremonies, including goodbye letters, to help couples honor their shared history while acknowledging the relationship has ended.

## Key facts

- Conscious uncoupling involves rituals to mark the end of a marriage, honoring what was while accepting the relationship's conclusion. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p286]
- Perel invites couples to write goodbye letters capturing what they'll miss, what they cherish, what they take responsibility for, and what they wish for each other. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p286]
- Perel cites Katherine Woodward Thomas as the originator of the 'conscious uncoupling' terminology. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p286]
- In the case study of Clive and Jade, Perel describes how the separation ceremony helped Jade accept that her husband loved another woman and the marriage was over. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p288]
