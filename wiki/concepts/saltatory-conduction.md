# Saltatory Conduction

## Summary

Saltatory conduction is the rapid propagation of action potentials along myelinated axons, where the signal appears to 'jump' from one node of Ranvier to the next. Myelin insulation increases membrane resistance and decreases capacitance, allowing local currents to spread farther and faster between nodes, where voltage-gated Na+ channels regenerate the signal.

## Key facts

- Myelination decreases membrane capacitance (Cm), allowing faster passive spread of depolarization along the axon internode. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p255]
- Nodes of Ranvier are bare axonal membrane patches (~1 μm long) occurring every 1-2 mm, rich in voltage-gated Na+ channels that regenerate the action potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p255]
- In demyelinated regions, local-circuit currents must discharge larger membrane capacitance and cannot spread as far due to low membrane resistance, potentially blocking conduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p255]
- In myelinated axons, action potentials propagate by jumping from node to node in a process called saltatory conduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p197]
- Myelination decreases membrane capacitance, allowing faster conduction than unmyelinated axons of the same diameter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p197]
- Nodes of Ranvier are bare patches of axon membrane approximately 1 µm in length, rich in voltage-gated Na+ channels, spaced every 1–2 mm along myelinated axons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p197]
- In demyelinating diseases, action potential conduction can be slowed or blocked because local-circuit currents cannot adequately depolarize the demyelinated membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p198]
