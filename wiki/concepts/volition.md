# Volition

## Summary

Volition refers to the experience of being in control of one's actions and being the author of one's thoughts—the feeling of free will. Seth proposes that experiences of volition are distinctive forms of self-related perception constructed according to Bayesian best guessing principles. These experiences play important roles in guiding behavior, are associated with specific brain networks involving 'what,' 'when,' and 'whether' processes, and are useful for flagging instances of voluntary behavior to adjust future actions.

## Key facts

- Volition involves a feeling of 'radical, absolute, buck-stopping up-to-me-ness in choice and action,' in Galen Strawson's words. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p201]
- Experiences of volition are not transparent windows onto how causality operates in the physical world—they are constructed perceptions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p207]
- Voluntary actions are voluntary not because they descend from an immaterial soul or ascend from a quantum soup, but because they express what a person wants to do, even though they cannot choose these wants. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p206]
- Schopenhauer wrote, 'Man can do what he wills, but he cannot will what he wills.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p206]
- Experiences of volition are useful for guiding future behaviour by flagging instances of voluntary behaviour so attention can be paid to consequences and future behaviour adjusted. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p209]
- The feeling of 'I could have done differently' is useful because in a future similar situation one might indeed do differently, not because one actually could have done differently in the past. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p209]
- The competence to control many degrees of freedom is implemented by a brain network implementing three processes: an early 'what' process, a subsequent 'when' process, and a late 'whether' process for inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p208]
- Spooky free will—an intervention in the physical flow of events—is neither real nor necessary; a deterministic universe can chug along just fine for free will as experienced. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p202]

## Inferences

- Inference: Experiences of volition are not illusory in the sense of being wrong—they are perceptual best guesses as real as visual experiences. The phenomenology of alternative possibilities is useful for future behavior adjustment.
- Inference: Experiences of volition can be entirely absent during states of flow when deeply immersed in practiced activities—voluntary actions 'just happen.'
