# Choice of Neurosis

## Summary

Freud addresses the question of what determines whether infantile sexual scenes will give rise to hysteria, obsessional neurosis, or paranoia. While obsessions can be shown to be disguised self-reproaches about sexual aggression in childhood (more common in men), the full determinants of neurosis choice remain uncertain in Freud's 1896 analysis.

## Key facts

- What determines whether the infantile sexual scenes which have remained unconscious will later give rise to hysterical or obsessional neurosis or even to paranoia remains an open question. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1121]
- Obsessions can be regularly shown to be disguised and transformed self-reproaches about acts of sexual aggression in childhood, more often met with in men than women. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1121]
- Freud initially considered that the character of the infantile scenes—whether experienced with pleasure or only passively—might determine the choice of later neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1121]
- The general proposition is that the grounds for determining the choice of neurosis are entirely of a dispositional nature and independent of experiences which operate pathogenically. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4033]
- Dispositions are inhibitions in development; wherever a portion of development clings to a previous stage, a 'point of fixation' results. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4033]
- The disposition to hysteria and obsessional neurosis lies in later phases of libidinal development compared to paraphrenia. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4034]
- Hysterical forms of illness can be observed in earliest childhood; obsessional neurosis usually shows first symptoms between ages 6-8. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4034]

## Inferences

- Inference: The observation that the character of the experience (pleasurable vs. passive) might influence neurosis choice hints at the role of affect and gratification in determining the defensive strategy employed.
- Inference: The differentiation between neuroses depends on where in the libidinal developmental sequence the fixation point occurs.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the age at which childhood actions occur or other factors determine the choice between possible forms of neuropsychoses of defence.
