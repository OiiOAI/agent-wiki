# Bipedalism

## Summary

The evolution of walking on two legs in hominids, which freed the hands for tool use, changed breathing patterns enabling speech, and led to numerous anatomical changes including pelvic restructuring, curved spine, and foot modifications.

## Key facts

- Bipedalism allowed humans to develop free hands capable of carrying objects, with thumbs that can arc across to the little finger (ulnar opposition), enabling fine motor coordination. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p53]
- Bipedalism broke the link between breathing patterns and stride, allowing flexibility for regulating breathing and ultimately speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p52]
- The birth canal became narrower as brains enlarged, making human babies born one year prematurely compared to other apes, requiring longer periods of care. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p54]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bipedalism may have been enabled by climate change forcing ancestors out of tropical forests into open woodland, creating selection pressure for efficient ground locomotion
