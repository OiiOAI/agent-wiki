# Confirmatory Thought

## Summary

Confirmatory thought involves a one-sided attempt to rationalize a particular point of view, promoting and celebrating one's own beliefs while distorting how a group processes information. It amplifies bias because its main purpose is justification rather than accuracy.

## Key facts

- Confirmatory thought involves a one-sided attempt to rationalize a particular point of view, whereas exploratory thought involves even-handed consideration of alternative points of view. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1215-L1220]
- Without an explicit charter for exploratory thought, groups naturally drift toward confirmatory thought, resulting in echo chambers. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1220-L1225]
- Even research communities of highly intelligent and well-meaning individuals can fall prey to confirmation bias, as IQ is positively correlated with the number of reasons people find to support their own side in an argument. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1420-L1425]
- Confirmation bias led reviewers to work extra hard to find flaws with papers whose conclusions they dislike, and to be more permissive about methodological issues when they endorsed the conclusions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1435-L1440]
