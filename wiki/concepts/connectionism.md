# Connectionism

## Summary

Connectionism arose in the early 1980s, revising ideas from cybernetics, and offered the neural network as its central metaphor for the mind. Connectionist models take the form of artificial neural networks composed of layers of neuron-like units linked by weighted connections. Connectionism emphasized perceptual pattern recognition as the paradigm of intelligence rather than deductive reasoning. While more dynamic than cognitivism regarding the environment, connectionism still inherited the idea that cognition is solving predefined problems and that the mind is essentially the skull-bound cognitive unconscious. It provided little new resources for addressing the explanatory gap.

- Connectionism offered a more dynamic conception of the relation between cognitive processes and the environment than cognitivism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p20]
- Connectionism did not involve sensory and motor coupling with the environment but operated on artificial inputs and outputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p21]
- Connectionism enlarged the scope of the computational mind but provided little new resources for addressing the explanatory gap. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p21]
