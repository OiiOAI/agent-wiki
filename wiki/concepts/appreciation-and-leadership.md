# Appreciation and Leadership

## Summary

Appreciation and Leadership represents Boyd's reconceptualization of command and control, replacing traditional top-down directive terminology with concepts emphasizing cognitive processes and inspirational guidance. Appreciation encompasses recognition of worth, clear perception, understanding, and monitoring without interference. Leadership implies inspiring cooperation toward uncommon goals. This shift reflects Boyd's emphasis on implicit bonds, trust, and shared orientation over explicit hierarchical control.

## Key facts

- Boyd proposed appreciation as part of leadership to provide assessment of what's being done in a clear unambiguous way, without interacting or interfering with the system but discerning its character. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p253]
- Leadership must give direction in terms of what is to be done, interacting with system to shape its character to realize objectives. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p253]
- Boyd concluded that direction and shaping should be evident while assessment and discernment should be invisible and should not interfere with operations. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p253]
- Boyd contrasted appreciation with 'control'—which comes from 'contrarotulus,' a counter roll or checklist—and leadership with 'command' meaning to direct, order, or compel. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p253]
- Boyd stated that C&C represents a top-down mentality applied in a rigid or mechanical way that ignored and stifled the implicit nature of human beings to deal with uncertainty, change, and stress. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p253]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's reconceptualization reflects his broader theoretical framework viewing organizations as organic systems requiring adaptation rather than mechanical control, drawing on Polanyi's tacit knowledge and systems theory.
