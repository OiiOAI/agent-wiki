# Perceptual Learning and Cortical Reorganization

## Summary

Perceptual learning involves improved performance in discriminating or identifying sensory inputs through extensive practice. Neuroimaging studies show that perceptual learning involves a shift from cognitive (parietal cortex-dependent) to autonomous (inferior temporal cortex-dependent) processing stages. Training can expand or retract representations in the sensory cortex: monkeys trained to use specific fingers show expanded cortical representations of those fingers, and string instrument players have enlarged representations of their plucking hand in the motor cortex.

## Key facts

- Mirror-reading practice produces a shift in activated brain regions: before training, parietal cortex activity predominates; after extensive practice, activity decreases in parietal cortex but increases in the left inferior temporal cortex, reflecting direct reading of reversed letters without mental rotation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1506]
- Adult monkeys trained to use their middle three fingers show substantial expansion of the area of somatosensory cortex (area 3b) devoted to those fingers after thousands of trials, demonstrating experience-dependent plasticity of cortical maps. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1535]
- String instrument players show a larger cortical representation of their left (fingering) hand in the motor cortex compared to nonmusicians, with the enlargement most pronounced in musicians who began training before age 13. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1536]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinctive modification of brain architecture by each individual's unique experiences, combined with a unique genetic makeup, constitutes a biological basis for individuality.
