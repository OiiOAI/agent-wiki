# Brain Laterality and Emotion

## Summary

Research indicates that positive emotional feelings are associated with EEG arousal of the left frontal hemisphere, while negative and depressive moods are associated with right frontal arousal. The right hemisphere is more specialized for deep emotional communication, while the left hemisphere focuses on social niceties and linguistic competence.

## Key facts

- Positive emotional feelings are associated with EEG arousal of the left frontal areas, while negative and depressive moods are associated with right frontal arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- These hemispheric patterns are evident in infants, with extroverted babies showing more left arousal and inhibited ones showing more right arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- Damage to the left cerebral hemisphere, especially left frontal areas, appears to reduce positive affective tone, while similar damage to the right does not. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- Following strokes to the right hemisphere (the sadder side of the brain), patients may deny that anything bad has happened, while left damage can promote catastrophic despair. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- Rapid transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) of left frontal cortex can evoke feelings of sadness, while right stimulation enhances happiness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- The right hemisphere is more of a specialist for deep emotional communication, while the left is more focused on social niceties. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- Right hemisphere damage disrupts one's ability to express and understand emotional inflections. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]

## Inferences

- Inference: Differences between hemispheres in emotional function may arise largely from learning rather than from deep intrinsic differences in emotional competence.
- Inference: The left hemisphere may appear more happy only as a secondary consequence of its linguistic functions, which reinforce the appearance of a positive social front.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The reasons why the two hemispheres have different emotional specializations remain unclear with no clear evidence supporting any existing theory.
