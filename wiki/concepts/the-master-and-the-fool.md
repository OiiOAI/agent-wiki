# The Master and the Fool

## Summary

The Master and the Fool explores the relationship between mastery and the willingness to be foolish. Leonard argues that significant learning requires emptiness—letting go of the full cup so new things can enter. The 'fool' represents the medieval court jester or the zero in the tarot deck, signifying the fertile void from which all creation springs.

## Key facts

- To be a learner, one must be willing to be a fool—not a stupid person, but one with the spirit of the medieval fool, the carefree figure in the tarot deck bearing the number zero. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L1005-L1008]
- The Zen story of the cup illustrates that if one's cup is already full, there is no space for anything new. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L1010-L1014]
- Abraham Maslow discovered a 'second naivete'—a childlike quality—in people who have met an unusually high degree of their potential. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L1038-L1040]
- Ashleigh Montagu used the term 'neoteny' to describe geniuses such as Mozart and Einstein, referring to newborn-like qualities. [raw/books/productivity/Mastery - The Keys To Success And Long - Term Fulfillment - George Leonard.epub#L1040-L1042]
