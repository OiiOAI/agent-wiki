# Quality

## Summary

The book proposes Quality as the central concept that reconciles classical and romantic understanding. Quality is described as being prior to both subject and object—it is what we experience before we make the intellectual divisions between ourselves and what we perceive. Phædrus saw Quality as underlying all rational thought and sought to understand its relationship to the ghost of rationality.

## Key facts

- Quality is not a result of subjective or objective analysis but precedes both. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p69]
- The narrator seeks to direct attention to the endless landscape from which the sand is taken, suggesting Quality exists in the pre-conceptual landscape of experience. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p69]
- Quality cannot be defined because 'definitions are a product of rigid, formal thinking' yet it is recognized by 'a nonthinking process.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p175]
- Phædrus demonstrated Quality's existence by showing that a world without it would be unlivable: fine arts, poetry, comedy, sports, and marketplaces would all disappear or become meaningless. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p183]
- Quality is described as a 'cleavage term' that splits the world into hip and square, classic and romantic, technological and humanistic, 'and the split is clean.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p185]
- The narrator states: 'A real understanding of Quality doesn't just serve the System, or even beat it or even escape it. A real understanding of Quality captures the System, tames it, and puts it to work for one's own personal use.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p189]
- Phædrus concluded that Quality is neither subjective nor objective but a third entity independent of both. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p201]
- Quality is not a thing but an event—the event at which the subject becomes aware of the object. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p203]
- Quality is the cause of subjects and objects, which are mistakenly presumed to be the cause of Quality. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p203]
- Phædrus discovered that Quality as he understood it corresponded to the Tao of the Tao Te Ching. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p215]
- Quality is shapeless, formless, and indescribable; names and forms given to Quality depend partly on accumulated a priori analogues. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p212]
- Phædrus wrote that Quality is the continuing stimulus which causes humans to invent analogues such as earth, heavens, trees, gods, music, art, science—'All of it. Every last bit of it.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p213]
- Quality is described as preceding both subject and object, event and observer. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p330]
- Socrates described Quality as the soul, self-moving, the source of all things. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p330]
- The One in India must be the same as the One in Greece; if not, you have two. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p330]
- The One cannot be defined in terms of things, since no matter what thing defines it, it describes less than the One itself. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p330]
- Quality can only be described allegorically, through analogy and figures of imagination and speech. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p330]
- Phaedrus concluded that Quality, not dialectic, is the generator of everything we know. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p334]
- Phaedrus realized that any attempt to develop an organized reason around an undefined Quality defeats its own purpose. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p337]

## Inferences

- Inference: Quality appears to be the bridge between analytic (classical) and intuitive (romantic) modes of understanding, functioning pre-intellectually.
- Inference: The crystallization metaphor suggests Quality generates thought patterns rather than being generated by them.
- Inference: Quality represents a Copernican inversion of traditional metaphysical assumptions: rather than subjects and objects generating Quality, Quality generates subjects and objects.
- Inference: Phædrus's equation of Quality with the Tao suggests that Quality is not merely a philosophical concept but the central generating force of all religions and knowledge systems.
- Inference: The Afterword's discussion of Chris's 'pattern' continuing as Nell suggests that Quality may be understood as a larger pattern that transcends individual physical forms—a view consistent with Quality being prior to and underlying material reality.
- Inference: Pirsig's view of Quality as prior to subject-object duality anticipates later developments in process philosophy and relational metaphysics.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The narrator acknowledges his reconstruction of Phædrus's ideas from fragments may contain errors. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p160]
- Uncertain: Phædrus himself felt that this conclusion represented a shift from a metaphysical trinity toward absolute monism, which may have contributed to his eventual breakdown.
