# Flexion-Withdrawal Reflex

## Summary

The flexion-withdrawal reflex is a protective spinal reflex in which a limb is quickly withdrawn from a noxious stimulus. It involves coordinated contraction of flexor muscles and inhibition of extensors, with a crossed-extension reflex in the opposite limb providing postural support.

## Key facts

- Flexion-withdrawal is a protective reflex in which a discrete painful stimulus causes all flexor muscles in the limb to contract coordinately, mediated by divergent polysynaptic pathways in the spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p843]
- The reflex produces excitation of ipsilateral flexor motor neurons and inhibition of extensor motor neurons through Ia inhibitory interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p843]
- A crossed-extension reflex excites contralateral extensor motor neurons and inhibits contralateral flexor motor neurons to provide postural support during withdrawal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p843]
- The flexion-withdrawal reflex persists after complete transection of the spinal cord, demonstrating it is a spinal reflex independent of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p843]
