# Strange Situation Test

## Summary

The strange situation test is the most important study in attachment theory, developed by Mary Ainsworth. It observes how children (typically 9-18 months) behave when separated from and reunited with their mothers. The test reveals different attachment styles based on how children respond to the mother's departure and return.

## Key facts

- The strange situation test is the most important study in attachment theory, developed by Mary Ainsworth. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p33]
- Mary Ainsworth was fascinated by how children's exploratory drive could be aroused or stifled by their mother's presence or departure. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p33]
- In the test, the anxious baby becomes extremely distressed when mother leaves and reacts ambivalently upon return, taking longer to calm down. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p46]
- The avoidant baby acts as though nothing happened when mother leaves and remains unmoved upon her return, though internally their heart rates and cortisol levels are elevated. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p46]
- The secure baby is visibly distressed when mother leaves but is very happy to see her return and quickly resumes play activity. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p46]
