# Feedback Loops

## Summary

Feedback loops are circular arrangements of causally connected elements where an initial cause propagates around links, with each element affecting the next until the last 'feeds back' into the first. Negative or balancing feedback dampens the effects of change and leads to homeostasis. Positive or reinforcing feedback amplifies change in the same direction. In cybernetic systems, feedback enables self-regulation; in complex systems, positive feedback can be a source of new order during far-from-equilibrium conditions. The OODA loop is a type of feedback loop incorporating observation, orientation, decision, and action.

## Key facts

- A feedback loop is 'a circular arrangement of causally connected elements, in which an initial cause propagates around the links of the loop, so that each element has an effect on the next, until the last 'feeds back' into the first element of the cycle.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p114]
- Negative or 'balancing' feedback dampens effects of change and leads to less of the action creating it; it is the essential mechanism of homeostasis allowing living organisms to maintain dynamic balance. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p114]
- Positive or 'reinforcing' feedback amplifies change in the same direction, and while previously regarded as destructive in cybernetics, appears as a source of new order and complexity in far-from-equilibrium systems. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p114]
- Self-regulation by feedback is defined as 'a method of controlling a system by reinserting into it the results of past performance,' controlling a machine based on actual rather than expected performance. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p114]
- Boyd's OODA loop parallels cybernetic feedback loop diagrams, with observation feeding back to shape future orientation, decisions, and actions in a continuous cycle. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p103]
- In experiential learning models, feedback from actions and their results informs decisions and creates new experiences, forming a continuous learning cycle. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p121]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's emphasis on rapid OODA looping reflects understanding that faster negative feedback cycles allow systems to maintain stability and adapt more quickly than adversaries.
- Inference: The strategic objective of creating 'mismatches' between adversary observations and responses can be understood as disrupting feedback loops to prevent effective self-regulation.
