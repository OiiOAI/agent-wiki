# Tabula Rasa

## Summary

Tabula rasa (blank slate) is the philosophical view that the brain is born empty and knowledge is written onto it by experience. This view is an almost inevitable assumption of the outside-in framework because, under the empiricist view, the goal of the brain is to learn and represent truth from sensory inputs. Buzsáki argues against this view, proposing instead that the brain starts with preexisting connectivity and dynamics (preconfigured brain).

## Key facts

- The tabula rasa view is an almost inevitable assumption of the outside-in framework because the goal of the brain is to learn and represent the truth with veridical details. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p23]
- Tabula rasa thinking implies that anything can be written on a blank brain, similar to how free will implies humans can do anything. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p23]
- Many experiments and modeling studies are still performed according to this associationist philosophy-based framework or its modern version called 'connectionism'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p23]
- Early behavioral observations argued against the brain as a blank slate, demonstrating that animals do not associate everything equally and cannot be trained to do all tricks expected. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p23]
- Behaviors related to the animal's ecological niche can be trained easily because the brain is predisposed or 'prepared' to do things that have survival and reproductive advantage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p23]

## Inferences

- Inference: The tabula rasa view has influenced cognitive and social sciences even though most neuroscientists would not openly subscribe to it.
