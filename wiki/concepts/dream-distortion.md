# Dream Distortion

## Summary

Dream distortion (Traumentstellung) refers to the process by which the censorship function of the psyche modifies unacceptable dream thoughts, transforming them into the bizarre, incomprehensible, or distressing imagery of the manifest dream. Freud argues that distortion is evidence of an active censorship operating between two psychical agencies, analogous to political censorship or social politeness.

## Key facts

- Freud names the phenomenon whereby dreams do not say what they mean straight out 'the phenomenon of distortion in dreams.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1407]
- The two psychical agencies theory holds that one agency constructs the dream wish while a second censorship agency modifies it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1413]
- The analogy drawn between dream censorship and political censorship explains how objectionable material is softened, allusive, or disguised in dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1412]
- The intensity of distortion varies with the strength of the censorship, creating ranging degrees of disguise from subtle to impenetrable. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1413]
- The emotional feeling in a dream (such as affection for R.) may be opposite to the latent hostile impulse it conceals, constituting 'hypocritical' distortion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1411]

## Inferences

- Inference: The social parallel to dream distortion—where the politically weaker party must soften and distort expression before authorities—suggests an adaptive function for distortion in preserving psychological equilibrium.
