# Ideal Decision-Making Model

## Summary

A three-stage model for decision-making in knowledge-based organizations: (1) free discussion where all viewpoints are openly debated, (2) reaching a clear decision, and (3) securing full support from all participants. This model contrasts with conventional approaches where people wait to hear superiors' views before contributing.

## Key facts

- The first stage of ideal decision-making is free discussion, in which all points of view and all aspects of an issue are openly welcomed and debated. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1013-L1015]
- In free discussion, the greater the disagreement, the more important the word 'free' becomes; participants tend to wait to see what view will prevail before committing themselves. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1015-L1017]
- The second stage is reaching a clear decision, where the greater the disagreement, the more important it is to frame the terms of the decision with utter clarity. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1025-L1027]
- The third stage requires everyone involved to give the decision reached by the group full support, which does not necessarily mean agreement. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1030-L1032]
- Decisions should ideally be worked out at the lowest competent level where people are closest to the situation and know the most about it. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1045-L1047]
