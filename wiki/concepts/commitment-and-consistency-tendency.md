# Commitment and Consistency Tendency

## Summary

The psychological principle that once people commit to something (especially publicly), they feel compelled to remain consistent with that commitment, even when it's irrational. This can create habitual behavior patterns and make it difficult for people to admit mistakes.

## Key facts

- The consistency principle is a big part of human psychology—once people start stealing, commitment and consistency combines with operant conditioning to make stealing habitual. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p236]
- The more you pour effort and money into something, the more consistency principle makes you think 'Now it has to work; if I put in just a little more, it'll work'—a common way people go broke. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p247]
- If you've reached for an idea yourself, the commitment and consistency tendency means it's pounded in better—you hold it better than information simply presented. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p263]
- Munger's father taught him indirectly using this principle—telling a story requiring mental reach so Charlie had to reach for it himself, making the lesson stick for decades. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p262]
- Munger's father told him the Grant McFadyen story in a way that required a slight mental reach, and because Munger had to reach for it, he held it better—this is the commitment and consistency tendency. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p275]
- Good literature makes the reader reach a little for understanding; if you've reached for it, the idea's pounded in better. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p275]
- Munger's father used indirection on purpose, and look at how powerfully it worked—like Captain Cook's wise use of psychology. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p276]
