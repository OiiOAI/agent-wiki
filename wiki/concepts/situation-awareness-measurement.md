# Situation Awareness Measurement

## Summary

SA measurement provides direct insight into how operators form coherent operational pictures from available information. It serves three primary purposes: design evaluation (determining how new technologies affect operator SA), training evaluation (assessing impact of training approaches on SA), and investigation of the SA construct itself. Key implications for measurement include the distinction between processes versus states (different individuals may use different processes to arrive at the same SA), the disconnect between SA and decision making/performance (SA is a probabilistic precursor, not a guarantee), the importance of not artificially influencing attention distribution during measurement, and the need to account for memory limitations and automaticity effects.

## Key facts

- Direct measurement of SA provides great insight into how operators piece together the vast array of available information to form a coherent operational picture. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p42]
- SA measurement provides a useful index for evaluating system design and training techniques and for better understanding human cognition. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p42]
- As an intervening variable between stimulus and response, SA measurement provides far greater diagnosticity and sensitivity than typically available from performance measures alone. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p42]
- High-level performance measures collected during limited simulation testing are often not sufficiently granular or diagnostic of differences in system designs. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p43]
- SA as a state of knowledge is different than the processes used to achieve that knowledge; different individuals may use different processes to arrive at the same state. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p44]
- Good SA should increase the probability of good decisions and good performance, but does not guarantee it; conversely, poor SA increases probability of poor performance but may not create a serious error. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p44]
- For a large range of the spectrum, SA and workload can vary independently, diverging based on numerous factors; only when workload demands exceed maximum human capacity is SA necessarily at risk. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p45]
- There is no set threshold of SA that can guarantee a given level of performance; it is better to think of SA in relative terms where increased SA increases probability of good performance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p46]
- There is no such thing as too much SA—more SA is always better as it increases likelihood of effective decision making and performance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p46]
- Three primary categories of SA measures exist: performance-based, knowledge-based, and verbalization. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p209]
- Performance-based measures examine external data points; knowledge-based measures attempt to directly ascertain internal awareness; verbalization measures capture thought processes. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p209]
- Performance-based measures have the advantage of being comparatively objective as physical variables are typically recorded directly. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p211]
- Combining different SA measures can identify whether the primary block to satisfactory performance stems from poor situation awareness or poor decision making. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p213]
- Different SA measures examine different data at different times during experiments, so their timing does not conflict and their measurements can be kept independent. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p214]
