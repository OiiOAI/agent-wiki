# Social Play

## Summary

Play in young organisms that trains individuals in social competence. It involves fragments of real behaviors, role experimentation, motor skill development, and demonstrates that moderate transient stress ('stimulation') is beneficial. Play is intrinsically pleasurable, activating dopaminergic pathways, and is vital despite the costs of energy expenditure and predator vulnerability.

## Key facts

- Play is intrinsically pleasurable—dopaminergic pathways activate during play; juvenile rats emit the same vocalizations when playing as when rewarded with food. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p213]
- A child deprived of or disinterested in play rarely has a socially fulfilling adult life. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p213]
- Stuart Brown of the National Institute for Play stated that the opposite of play is not work—it's depression. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p213]
- Rough-and-tumble play (fragments of aggression) is typically done more by males and is boosted by prenatal testosterone. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p213]
