# Mental Models

## Summary

Mental models are internal cognitive representations of systems and operational domains that significantly facilitate SA development. They provide stored knowledge about system purpose, form, functioning, and predictions of future states. For SA specifically, mental models help direct attention to relevant environmental cues, provide means for integrating information for Level 2 comprehension, and enable projection of future states (Level 3 SA). Experts develop robust mental models over time through experience, while novices suffer from insufficiently developed models that limit their ability to interpret information and project outcomes.

## Key facts

- Mental models are defined as mechanisms whereby humans generate descriptions of system purpose and form, explanations of system functioning and observed system states, and predictions of future states. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p237]
- Mental models provide three key advantages for SA: directing attention to relevant cues, integrating information for comprehension (Level 2), and projecting future states (Level 3). [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p237]
- Novices lack knowledge of underlying relationships and do not realize what information to seek following receipt of other information, leading to sporadic and nonoptimal scan patterns. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p236]
- Experienced pilots with high SA were found to have good aircraft handling skills and good task prioritization, with 91.7% of variance in SA scores explained by aircraft handling and ATC communication.
