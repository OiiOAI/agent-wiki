# Ritual and Symbol Learning

## Summary

Ritual is proposed as the essential social context that enabled early hominids with ape-like brains to acquire symbolic relationships. The repetitive, highly redundant nature of ritual performances allows attention to shift from concrete token-object associations to abstract token-token relationships. This process parallels experimental findings with chimpanzees Sherman and Austin, where repeated errorless trials enabled the transition from explicit sign-object associations to implicit sign-sign associations.

## Key facts

- Solving the symbol-learning problem requires maintaining attention on many related indexical associations simultaneously, shifting attention to token-token relationships, and suppressing attention on immediate token-object associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p434]
- Ritual performance provides the high redundancy important for bringing the logic of token-token relationships to the fore. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p435]
- Repetition can render individual details of a performance automatic and minimally conscious while emotional intensity from group participation focuses attention on other aspects of the objects and actions involved. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p435]
- Marriage and puberty rituals serve the function of demonstrating and establishing social symbolic relationships in most human societies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p439]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Yanomamö peacemaking Feast demonstrates how symbolic relationships are established through ritual by reconstituting underlying indexical relationships, analogous to the symbol-discovery process used in chimpanzee experiments.
