# Blood-Brain Barrier

## Summary

The blood-brain barrier is a specialized interface formed by endothelial cells of brain capillaries, characterized by tight junctions, that regulates the exchange of substances between the blood and the brain's interstitial fluid. It protects the brain from circulating toxins while permitting essential nutrients to cross via specific transport mechanisms.

## Key facts

- The endothelial cells of brain barrier capillaries are interconnected by complex arrays of tight junctions that block diffusion across vessel walls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1618]
- Tight junctions of the blood–brain barrier are composed of three principal transmembranous proteins—claudins, occludins, and junctional adhesion molecules—as well as numerous cytoplasmic accessory proteins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1619]
- Barrier vessels have high transendothelial resistance (2,000 ohm-cm2), excluding particles as small as K+ ions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1618]
- The blood-brain barrier is permeable in three ways: (1) by diffusion of lipid soluble substances, (2) by facilitative and energy-dependent receptor-mediated transport of specific water-soluble substances, and (3) by ion channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1619]
- Astroglial foot processes almost completely surround brain capillaries and are believed to influence barrier-specific endothelial differentiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1619]
- The permeability coefficient of the blood–brain barrier for many substances is directly proportional to the lipid-solubility of the substance as measured by the oil–water partition coefficient. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1619]

## Inferences

- Inference: The absence of fenestrations and reduced pinocytotic vesicles in brain endothelial cells, compared to systemic capillaries, contributes to the barrier's restrictive permeability.
- Inference: The complex tight junction arrays in barrier vessels generate resistance 200-400 times greater than non-barrier vessels, explaining selective permeability.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific brain parenchymal signals that induce endothelial barrier formation are not known.
