# Prefrontal Cortex

## Summary

The region of the cortex at the front of the brain that evolved most recently and serves as the CEO of most brain functions, overseeing planning, sequencing, rehearsing, evaluating, understanding, and working memory.

## Key facts

- The prefrontal cortex is the region of the cortex located at the very front of the brain. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p225]
- As the last portion of gray matter to have evolved, the prefrontal cortex oversees the qualities that make us most human. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p225]
- It is the CEO of most brain functions, including planning, sequencing, rehearsing, evaluating, and understanding. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p225]
- The prefrontal cortex is the home of working memory, the brain's RAM, which is crucial to decision making. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p225]
- The prefrontal cortex is distinguished from the rest of the frontal cortex by having an additional layer of neurons (internal granular layer IV). [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p28]
- The prefrontal white matter is disproportionately larger in humans than other primates, suggesting higher connectivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p29]
- Nonprimate mammals have two major regions of prefrontal cortex (orbital and anterior cingulate), while primates have three - the added lateral granular prefrontal cortex is concerned with rational decision-making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p29]
- The prefrontal cortex is traditionally viewed as the headquarters of cognition, receiving inputs from all unimodal and multimodal areas and serving as a super convergence zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p249]
- A gradient of processing exists within lateral prefrontal areas from posterior (more stimulus-specific) to anterior (more abstract conceptual representations). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p252]
- The frontal pole receives inputs only from multimodal convergence zones and creates the most abstract conceptual representations, enabling maintenance of long-term goals for future planning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p252]
- The human prefrontal cortex has novel features not present even in apes, including unique spatial arrangements of cells and unique patterns of connectivity within and between cell layers. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p256]
- Todd Preuss described the human prefrontal cortex as 'rewired and running hot' due to greater interconnectivity between neurons and novel gene expression patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p257]
- Etienne Koechlin described the frontal pole as a functional 'add-on' enabling cognitive branching—the ability to engage simultaneously in multiple options for reasoning and multitasking. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p256]

## Inferences

- Inference: The unique features of human prefrontal cortex, particularly the frontal pole, likely contribute to human capacities for abstract reasoning and multitasking that distinguish us from other primates.
- Inference: Novel connectivity patterns between prefrontal and parietal/temporal areas in humans may underlie enhanced cognitive integration compared to apes.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact contribution of prefrontal rewiring versus cultural evolution to human cognitive uniqueness remains debated.
