# Three Stages of SA Acquisition

## Summary

SA acquisition consists of three sequential cognitive stages: Level 1 (perception and registration of environmental elements within time and space), Level 2 (comprehension through integration with existing knowledge to form mental models), and Level 3 (projection of future element status). All stages rely on working memory resources and can be affected by age-related cognitive changes.

## Key facts

- Stage 1 SA involves perception of elements in the environment within a volume of time and space, where attentional processes select relevant information while disregarding nonessential data. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p297]
- Stage 2 SA is the comprehension of elements' meaning, achieved by integrating information within working memory to form a coherent mental model of the current situation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p302]
- Stage 3 SA enables projection of future actions of elements based on understanding of current status and dynamics, which feeds into decision making. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p304]
- Schemas activate during Stage 2 to help organize and update mental models by giving meaning to individual elements and their interrelationships. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p302]

## Inferences

- Inference: Each stage builds upon the previous one, meaning age-related deficits in earlier stages (e.g., perception) may compound and propagate through subsequent stages, affecting overall SA quality.
