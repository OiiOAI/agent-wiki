# Cell Assemblies

## Summary

Cell assemblies are sets of interconnected neurons that support each other through excitatory synapses and form stable patterns of activity representing thoughts. Donald Hebb proposed this concept in 1949, suggesting that frequently repeated stimulation leads to the development of cell assemblies capable of remaining active after external stimuli disappear. The theory predicts that conscious thoughts are encoded by the stable activation of distributed workspace neurons, with millions of neurons coding different facets of a single mental representation in partial synchrony.

## Key facts

- Donald Hebb proposed that frequently repeated stimulation leads to development of cell assemblies—diffuse structures of interconnected neurons capable of acting briefly as closed systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p220]
- Neurons within an assembly support each other through excitatory pulses, forming a delimited hill of activity in neural space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p220]
- Local cell assemblies combine to form a combinatorial code capable of representing billions of states—for example, a fire extinguisher is encoded by patches responding to handle, body, and hose. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p221]
- Recurrent neural networks with cell assemblies converge to consensus interpretations by mutually supporting neurons that receive greatest activation while suppressing alternatives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p222]
- Crick and Koch describe conscious representations as winning neural coalitions—stable groups that form through mutual support and inhibition of alternatives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p222]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cell assemblies provide the neural substrate for the near-infinite repertoire of conscious thoughts because their combinatorial activation patterns can be exquisitely differentiated
- Inference: The concept explains why conscious states have sharp boundaries—either a pattern is part of the active assembly or it is not
