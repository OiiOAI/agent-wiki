# Wolf Dream

## Summary

The pivotal anxiety dream experienced by the Wolf Man at age 4, immediately before his fourth birthday (Christmas). In the dream, white wolves sat motionless on a walnut tree outside his window. Freud interpreted this dream as revealing the patient's primal scene observation and his fear of castration. The dream incorporated elements from fairy tales ('Little Red Riding Hood' and 'The Wolf and the Seven Little Goats') and his grandfather's story of a tailor and a wolf. The dream marked the transition from a period of naughtiness to one of animal phobia.

## Key facts

- The dream occurred at night during winter, shortly before the patient's fourth birthday and Christmas. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5289]
- The dream featured six or seven white wolves sitting motionless on a walnut tree, looking at the dreamer with strained attention. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5289]
- The dream was the patient's first anxiety dream, and he remained afraid of seeing terrible things in his dreams until ages 11-12. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5289]
- The white wolves were connected to white sheep flocks near the estate, the white underclothes of his parents during the primal scene, and the white goat in fairy tales. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5301]
- The dream synthesized material from the grandfather's story of the tailor and wolf, the fairy tales of Little Red Riding Hood and The Seven Little Goats, and the primal scene. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5301]

## Inferences

- Inference: The motionless wolves represented a reversal of the violent movement observed in the primal scene, transformed through dream work.
- Inference: The dream represented the transformation of a wish for sexual satisfaction from his father into terror of castration.
