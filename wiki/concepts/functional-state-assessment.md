# Operator Functional State Assessment

## Summary

Functional state assessment uses psychophysiological measures to determine whether an operator is in a state conducive to maintaining situation awareness. This includes detecting sleep and fatigue at one extreme, and cognitive overload at the other. Methods include EEG analysis for detecting sleep stages and alertness levels, heart rate variability for sleep deprivation effects, eyeblink analysis for drowsiness, and respiratory measures. Continuous monitoring enables determining periods when proper SA is not possible.

## Key facts

- For SA to be maintained, operators must possess the functional state necessary to intake and process sensory information, access memory, decide if intervention is needed, and perform required actions. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- Sleep, high fatigue, inattention, and cognitive overload interfere with an operator's ability to maintain proper SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- Sleep inertia, the state between awakening and full functional alertness, may not be conducive to maintaining proper SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- Sleep stages are defined entirely upon electrophysiological criteria. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- Heart rate, respiration, oral temperature, and saccadic eye movements all decrease as a function of sleep loss. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- One night's sleep loss was associated with significant increases in heart rate variability. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- Drowsiness is characterized by dominant EEG frequency slowing, slowed heart rate, increased eyeblinks with longer eye closure duration, and decreased EEG alpha abundance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- Cognitive overload is associated with increasing frontal EEG theta band activity, decreasing alpha band activity, decreasing eyeblink rate, and increasing respiration rate. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]
- By continuously monitoring functional state, it is possible to determine operator status and take action to ensure the operator's condition supports optimal SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p179]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The cognitive overload state is noted as not as well defined psychophysiologically compared to sleep and fatigue.
