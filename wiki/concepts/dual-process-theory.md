# Dual Process Theory

## Summary

Dual process theories posit two human reasoning systems: a fast, automatic, heuristic system based on intuitive heuristics and beliefs; and a slower, controlled, analytic system requiring deliberate reasoning. When these systems cue different responses, the analytic system must override the heuristic response. Failures of analytic override explain common reasoning errors.

## Key facts

- Dual process theories explain rational thinking failures by positing heuristic (fast, intuitive) and analytic (slow, controlled) reasoning systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p172]
- Heuristic thinking activates the left lateral temporal lobe (BA 21/22), while analytic thinking activates bilateral superior parietal lobe (BA 7). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p209]
- The right lateral prefrontal cortex (BA 46) mediates successful resolution of belief-logic conflict during reasoning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p208]
- On incongruent base-rate problems, only 45% of participants solved correctly compared to 93% on congruent problems, demonstrating the cost of conflict. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p204]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neural pathways for deductive reasoning and decision making overlap substantially, suggesting they share underlying cognitive architecture.
