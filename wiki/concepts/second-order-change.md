# Second-Order Change

## Summary

Second-order change is change that changes the system itself. While first-order change is change within a system, second-order change involves stepping outside the system and changing the premises or rules governing it. The authors describe this as 'change of change'—a metamorphosis that is the nature of shifts from one logical level to the next higher one. Examples include waking up from a nightmare (where waking is not part of the dream), or solving the nine-dot problem by extending lines beyond the assumed square. Second-order change typically appears illogical, unexpected, and paradoxical from the first-order change perspective.

## Key facts

- Second-order change is change of change—the phenomenon whose existence Aristotle denied categorically. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Second-order change corresponds to Ashby's cybernetic concept of change from transformation to transformation (change of the machine's way of behaving). [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Second-order change involves going from member to class, entailing a shift, jump, discontinuity, or transformation that provides a way out of a system. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- The authors identify four principles of second-order change: (a) it is applied to the attempted solution rather than the problem directly; (b) it appears weird and uncommonsensical; (c) it deals with what is happening in the here and now, not why; (d) it lifts the solution out of paradox by placing it in a different frame. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Second-order change is introduced into the system from the outside and is therefore not understandable in terms of first-order change vicissitudes. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- Second-order change is characterized by the formula 'not a but also not not-a', which contradicts common sense by escaping the false dilemma between a and not-a. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L88-92]
- The Zen master Tai-Hui demonstrated second-order change when he asked monks to name a stick 'beyond affirmation and negation'—a koan designed to force the mind into satori, the quantum jump to a higher logical level. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L88-92]
- Second-order change is achievable even when concrete facts of a situation are immutable; it operates on the level of metareality where meaning and class membership can be altered. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L92-95]
- The insomniac can be helped by paradoxical injunction to lie in bed and not close eyes until fast asleep—the intervention targets the metalevel where counterproductive attempts create the 'Be Spontaneous!' paradox. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L82-86]
