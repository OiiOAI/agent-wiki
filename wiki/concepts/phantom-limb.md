# Phantom Limb

## Summary

Phantom limb is a phenomenon where people who have lost a limb still perceive it as present and attached to their body. It demonstrates that our perception comes from the brain's model of the body rather than direct sensory input, and that these models can be wrong—perceiving things that don't physically exist.

## Key facts

- People who lose a limb often perceive that the missing limb is still there because the brain's model includes the missing limb and its location. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p166]
- Phantom limbs can 'move' into different positions and feel sensations like itching or pain located at particular locations on the nonexistent limb. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p166]
- Phantom limb demonstrates that our brain's model can be wrong—perceiving things that don't exist—showing that we live in a simulation of the world created by our brain, not direct perception of reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p167]

## Inferences

- Inference: Phantom limb proves that perception is constructive—the brain generates our experience of the body based on internal models, not direct sensory data.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The neural mechanisms that lead to phantom limb sensation and why some amputees experience more pain than others are not fully understood.
