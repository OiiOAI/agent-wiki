# LUCA (Last Universal Common Ancestor)

## Summary

LUCA is the hypothesized last common ancestor of all life on Earth, representing the point from which Bacteria and Archaea diverged approximately 3.5 billion years ago. As the mother of all life, LUCA passed survival-relevant genes to every subsequent organism, including genes underlying fundamental cellular processes found in all living creatures.

## Key facts

- LUCA arose between 4 billion and 3.8 billion years ago, about half a billion years after Earth formed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p30]
- LUCA was likely not the first instance of life; RNA, DNA, and proteins formed protocells before LUCA emerged. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p30]
- LUCA's first children were Bacteria and Archaea, which form an essential link in the evolution of all subsequent life including animals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p30]
- LUCA passed survival-relevant genes to every cell that has lived since, including all cells in the human body. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p30]
- LUCA had a cell membrane, inner negative charge, metabolic cycles for energy generation, and replicated using DNA instructions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p68]
