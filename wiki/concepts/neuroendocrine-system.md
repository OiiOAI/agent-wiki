# Neuroendocrine System

## Summary

The neuroendocrine system links the brain to physiological responses through hormone secretion. The hypothalamus controls the pituitary gland, with the posterior pituitary storing and releasing hormones (vasopressin, oxytocin) and the anterior pituitary secreting hormones in response to hypothalamic releasing/inhibiting factors.

## Key facts

- The posterior pituitary is an extension of the brain containing hormone-secreting axon terminals of hypothalamic neurons that release vasopressin and oxytocin directly into systemic circulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1060]
- Vasopressin controls blood pressure and water reabsorption by the kidney; oxytocin controls uterine smooth muscle and milk release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1060]
- Vasopressin and oxytocin are nine-amino acid peptide hormones synthesized as larger prohormones and cleaved within Golgi transport vesicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1060]
- Parvocellular neuroendocrine neurons in the hypothalamus release factors into the hypophysial portal system to control anterior pituitary hormone secretion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1065]
- The median eminence is considered outside the blood-brain barrier due to fenestrated capillaries that allow diffusion of hypophysiotropic factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1066]
