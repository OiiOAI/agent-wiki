# Paranoia

## Summary

Paranoia is a narcissistic neurosis characterized by delusional beliefs (persecution, grandeur, jealousy, erotomania). Freud theorized that persecutory paranoia represents a defense against homosexual impulses that have become too powerful. The megalomania in paranoia results from magnification of the ego due to the drawing in of libidinal object cathexes—a secondary narcissism returning to the original early infantile narcissism. Paranoiacs cannot be treated psychoanalytically because they lack the capacity for transference.

## Key facts

- In the large majority of paranoia cases, the persecutor is of the same sex as the patient, and often the person whom the patient loved most has been turned into his persecutor. - Persecution mania is the form of the disease in which a person defends himself against a homosexual impulse which has become too powerful. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5179]
- In paranoia, megalomania is the direct result of a magnification of the ego due to the drawing in of the libidinal object cathexes—a secondary narcissism which is a return of the original early infantile one. - Homosexual object choice originally lies closer to narcissism than the heterosexual kind, so that when repelling an undesirably strong homosexual impulse, the path back to narcissism is made particularly easy.
