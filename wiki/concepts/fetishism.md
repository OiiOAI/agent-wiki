# Fetishism

## Summary

A 1927 paper by Freud on fetishism, in which he argues that the fetish is a substitute for the woman's (mother's) penis that the little boy once believed in and, because of the threat of castration, refuses to give up. The paper introduces the concept of 'disavowal' (Verleugnung) and argues that this process necessarily implies a split in the subject's ego.

## Key facts

- The fetish is a substitute for the woman's (the mother's) penis that the little boy once believed in and does not want to give up. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6679]
- The boy refuses to take cognizance of the fact of his having perceived that a woman does not possess a penis, and the result is a compromise between the weight of the unwelcome perception and the force of his counterwish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6680]
- The fetish achieves triumph over the threat of castration, serves as protection against it, saves the fetishist from becoming a homosexual, and provides an easily accessible sexual satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6681]
- The analysis of fetishism demonstrates the existence of the castration complex and the terror at the sight of the female genital. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6682]
- The analysis of fetishism led Freud to revise his characterization of neurosis and psychosis, recognizing that disavowal of reality does not necessarily lead to psychosis because the ego can develop a split attitude. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6683]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ego split in fetishism means that one current in the mental life disavows reality while another takes full account of it, leading to coexistence of contradictory beliefs.
- Inference: The choice of specific fetishes (foot, fur, underclothing) corresponds to the last impression before the traumatic observation of female genitalia.
