# Characteristic adaptations

## Summary

Characteristic adaptations are progressively emergent habits and patterns of behavior that develop through the interplay between individuals and their environments. Introduced by psychologists Dan McAdams and Jennifer Pals, this concept describes how underlying psychological traits are expressed in actual behavior through our choices, experiences, and the environments we select and create. Unlike innate personality traits, characteristic adaptations represent what we actively develop—including habits of thought, heuristics, policies, projects, and commitments—that inform and guide our behavioral options.

## Key facts

- Characteristic adaptations emerge through the interplay between individuals and their environments, reflecting how innate predispositions are expressed in actual behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p256]
- We are active players in developing our characteristic adaptations—our experiences are not just things that happen to us but things we engage in and choose. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p256]
- As we mature, we select and create our own environments according to our interests and inclinations, leading to a coevolution of individuals with their environments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of characteristic adaptations bridges the gap between deterministic views of human behavior and the recognition that we actively shape who we become through our choices and engagements with the world.
