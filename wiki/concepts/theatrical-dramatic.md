# Theatrical Play vs. Dramatic Play

## Summary

Theatrical play characterizes finite games, where outcomes are predetermined and scripts exist even if unwritten. Dramatic play characterizes infinite games, where no conclusion is anticipated and all scripts are rendered useless. Seriousness relates to roles and abstractions, while playfulness engages others at the level of choice where no outcome is imposed. To be serious is to press for a specified conclusion; to be playful is to allow for possibility whatever the cost. The theatrical is backward-looking; the dramatic is forward-looking.

## Key facts

- Finite play is referred to as theatrical because it is intended for conclusion and its roles are scripted and performed for an audience. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p30]
- Infinite play is referred to as dramatic because infinite players avoid any outcome whatsoever, keeping the future open, making all scripts useless. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p30]
- Dramatically, one chooses to be a mother; theatrically, one takes on the role of mother. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p30]
- To be serious is to press for a specified conclusion; to be playful is to allow for possibility whatever the cost to oneself. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p29]
- Seriousness is always related to roles, or abstractions; we are playful when we engage others at the level of choice. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p28-29]
- Surprise causes finite play to end; it is the reason for infinite play to continue. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p32]
