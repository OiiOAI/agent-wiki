# Seeking the Wrong Goal

## Summary

Seeking the wrong goal occurs when a system's purpose or goal is defined badly, not measuring what it's supposed to measure. The system will obediently produce exactly what is asked, not necessarily what is wanted. Examples include defining national security as military spending, defining education as money spent per student, and using GNP as a measure of welfare. The GNP counts goods and bads equally, measures effort not achievement, and does not reflect distributional equity.

## Key facts

- If national security is defined as military spending, the system produces military spending, not necessarily security. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p169]
- If education is measured by performance on standardized tests, the system produces test performance, not necessarily education. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p169]
- India's family planning program defined goals as number of IUDs implanted, so doctors implanted loops without patient approval. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p169]
- GNP counts goods and bads, only marketed services, ignores distribution, and measures gross production not efficiency. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p170]
- The solution: specify indicators and goals that reflect the real welfare of the system, and don't confuse effort with result. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p172]
- System behavior is particularly sensitive to the goals of feedback loops. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]
- If goals are defined inaccurately or incompletely, the system may work to produce a result that is not really intended or wanted. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]
- The remedy is to specify indicators and goals that reflect the real welfare of the system, being careful not to confuse effort with result, or you will end up with a system producing effort rather than result. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p226]
