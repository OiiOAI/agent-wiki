# Ackerman Bargaining

## Summary

The Ackerman Bargaining model is a systematic offer-counteroffer method for bare-knuckle bargaining, designed to overcome the predictable 'meet in the middle' dynamic. Named after Mike Ackerman, an ex-CIA figure who founded a kidnap-for-ransom consulting company, the system uses extreme anchoring at 65% of target price, followed by decreasing concessions (85%, 95%, 100%), nonround numbers for credibility, and reciprocity principles without requiring conscious psychological thinking.

## Key facts

- The Ackerman model has six steps: set target price, open at 65% of target, calculate three raises (85%, 95%, 100%), use empathy and 'No' responses to get counters before raising, use precise nonround numbers, and add a nonmonetary item at the final number. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p180]
- Mike Ackerman was an ex-CIA figure who founded a kidnap-for-ransom consulting company; the system was run by Howard Raiffa at Harvard who confirmed it would work in any situation. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p180]
- The first offer at 65% of target sets an extreme anchor that may bring the counterpart to their price limit and induces fight-or-flight reactions limiting cognitive abilities. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p181]
- Diminishing concession sizes (decreasing by half each time) convince counterparts they are squeezing you to the breaking point, which increases their self-esteem about the negotiation. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p181]
- Nonround numbers like $37,893 rather than $38,000 give numbers credibility and weight due to human nature preferences for specific figures. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p182]
