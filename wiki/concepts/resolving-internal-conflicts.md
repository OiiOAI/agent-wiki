# Resolving Internal Conflicts with NVC

## Summary

NVC can be applied to resolve internal conflicts that often result in depression. By translating judgmental inner messages into the NVC format of observations, feelings, needs, and requests, people can free themselves from alienating internal dialogue. The ability to hear one's own feelings and needs and empathize with them can free individuals from depression.

## Key facts

- Ernest Becker attributes depression to cognitively arrested alternatives—when a judgmental internal dialogue alienates people from their needs and prevents them from acting to meet those needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p264]
- Depression is indicative of a state of alienation from one's own needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p264]
- To resolve internal conflicts, one translates inner judgmental messages into NVC form: 'When a, I feel b, because I am needing c. Therefore I now would like d.'. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p264]
- The ability to hear one's own feelings and needs and empathize with them can free us from depression. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p265]
- NVC helps create a more peaceful state of mind by encouraging focus on what is truly wanted rather than on what is wrong with others or oneself. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: NVC replaces clinical diagnosis with questions about feelings, needs, and requested actions, which creates vulnerability but leads to more authentic relationships.
