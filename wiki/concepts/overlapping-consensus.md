# Overlapping Consensus

## Summary

Overlapping consensus refers to a condition where citizens hold considerably different conceptions of justice that nevertheless lead to similar political judgments. This is sufficient for civil disobedience to be reasonable; strict consensus is not necessary. Both sides must believe their views support the same judgment even if positions were interchanged.

## Key facts

- Considerable differences in citizens' conceptions of justice are acceptable provided these conceptions lead to similar political judgments. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p349]
- Overlapping consensus suffices for civil disobedience to be a reasonable and prudent form of political dissent. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p349]
- The condition of reciprocity requires that both sides believe their conceptions support the same judgment in the situation, even were their positions interchanged. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p349]
- When requisite agreement in judgment breaks down and society splits into distinct parts holding diverse opinions on fundamental questions, the basis for civil disobedience no longer obtains. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p349]
