# In-Tray Processing

## Summary

In-tray processing is the systematic method for working through collected items to identify what each one means and what action is required. The key rules are: process the top item first, handle one item at a time, and never put anything back into the in-tray.

## Key facts

- The three basic rules for processing are: process the top item first, process one item at a time, and never put anything back into in-tray. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p170]
- Processing means deciding what an item is and what action is required, not spending time on it. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p170]
- For each actionable item, the next physical visible activity must be identified to move toward closure. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p178]
- Items requiring no action fall into three categories: trash, items to incubate, and reference material. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p174]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discipline of never returning items to the in-tray forces decisions to be made rather than deferred indefinitely.
