# Tragedy of Free-for-Alls

## Summary

Gazzaniga argues that Hardin's 'tragedy of the commons' was misnamed because most actual grazing commons were not free-for-alls but carefully regulated community property where each member owned specific rights. This regulated system prevented the cheating problem that only truly occurs in unregulated free-for-all situations. Misunderstanding this led to disastrous nationalization of communal lands in the 1970s.

## Key facts

- Hardin's 'tragedy of the commons' should be renamed 'tragedy of free-for-alls' since most grazing commons were carefully regulated community property, not unowned resources. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p163]
- In regulated commons, each member owns rights to specific areas (fishing, grazing), making it in owners' interest to maintain those areas long-term, eliminating cheating incentives. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p163]
- Nationalization of communal lands in Africa in the 1960s-70s created government-owned wildlife that was no longer a food/revenue source, eliminating motivation to protect it and incentivizing removal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p164]
- When Zimbabwe returned wildlife ownership to communities, attitudes changed and private land devoted to wildlife doubled—the animals became valuable and worth maintaining. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p164]
- Elinor Ostrom showed in laboratory settings that groups allowed to communicate and develop their own fining methods can manage communal resources almost perfectly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p164]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Zimbabwe success story suggests that clear property rights and local ownership, not just communal management per se, determine sustainable resource outcomes.
