# Optimization Trap

## Summary

The optimization trap occurs when logical systems in biological environments acquire more and more data, refining algorithms and going faster — until the environment changes, making the data a liability. This affects both human bureaucracies and AI, which race to doom rather than marching to it. The solution is developing the planner rather than the plan, maintaining adaptability when conditions shift.

## Key facts

- The more data an AI is given, the better it performs but also the more likely it is to haywire unpredictably, making bad decisions at "calamitous velocity". [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p139]
- Hyperspecialized organisms flourish briefly then go extinct, while generalist species trot stolidly along — maximum adequacy is the prime characteristic of life's long-term winners. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p139]
- The human hand is not ideal at any task but acceptable at countless tasks, winning more challenges than any optimized rival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p139]
- Commonsense lets us know when we don't know, driving decision-making about when to switch plans and which new plan to select. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p140]
