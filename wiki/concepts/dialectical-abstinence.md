# Dialectical Abstinence

## Summary

Dialectical abstinence is a DBT approach to addiction that synthesizes absolute commitment to abstinence with harm reduction planning for potential slips. The goal is complete abstinence from addictive behavior, but if a slip occurs, the goal shifts to minimizing harm and returning to abstinence as quickly as possible.

## Key facts

- Dialectical abstinence synthesizes: (1) commitment to complete abstinence, and (2) harm reduction if slips occur. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p381]
- Pros of abstinence: people who commit stay off longer. Cons: takes longer to get back on wagon after falling off. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p381]
- Pros of harm reduction: people get back on wagon faster after slips. Cons: people who commit to harm reduction alone relapse quicker. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p381]
- Like Olympic athletes who must believe they can win while having lost before, dialectical abstinence involves expecting the best while planning for trouble spots. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p381]
- Dialectical abstinence combines the absolute commitment to stop addictive behaviors with the dialectical understanding that if a slip occurs, the individual recommits immediately rather than viewing the slip as total failure. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p404]
- The plan for abstinence includes: planning alternative activities, spending time with reinforcing non-addicted people, reminding oneself of reasons to stay abstinent, burning bridges with temptation sources, avoiding cues, using skills, finding alternative ways to rebel, and making public announcements. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p404]
- The harm reduction plan specifies that if a slip occurs, the individual calls their therapist or sponsor, gets rid of temptations, reviews skills and handouts, performs opposite action to fight guilt and shame, conducts a chain analysis, and uses cheerleading statements. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p405]
- Extreme thinking patterns addressed include 'I have not quit yet; therefore I am doomed' and 'Now that I've relapsed, I might as well keep going,' which are countered with middle ground thinking that relapsing once does not doom someone to never stopping. [raw/books/psychology/DBT® Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets, Second Edition - Marsha M_ Linehan.pdf#p406]

## Inferences

- Inference: Dialectical abstinence represents a synthesis of acceptance-based and change-based approaches within DBT's broader dialectical framework.
