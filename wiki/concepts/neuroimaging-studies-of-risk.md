# Neuroimaging Studies of Risk

## Summary

Neuroimaging studies have identified brain regions involved in risk processing, including ventral striatum, ventromedial prefrontal cortex, amygdala, and anterior cingulate cortex. Research distinguishes between decisions under risk (known probabilities) and decisions under uncertainty (ambiguous probabilities), finding different neural correlates for each.

## Key facts

- Comparisons of brain activation for ambiguous versus risky outcomes suggest these two choice situations differ in important ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p133]
- Neuroimaging studies suggest strong path dependence in the brain's reaction to economic quantities like risk and variance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p133]
- Neural responses to expected value and variance manipulations show different activation patterns depending on whether probabilities or magnitudes are varied. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p133]
- Studies examining brain activation for gains versus losses find different patterns depending on whether decisions are resolved or not, or whether people make decisions or contemplate options. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p133]
