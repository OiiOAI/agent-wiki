# G Protein-Gated Inward-Rectifier Potassium Channel

## Summary

GIRK channels are inward-rectifier potassium channels that are directly opened by G protein βγ-subunits released upon activation of Gi or Go protein-coupled receptors. They mediate slow inhibitory postsynaptic potentials and regulate neuronal firing rates. The βγ-subunits bind directly to the channel's cytoplasmic surface, causing conformational changes that promote opening.

## Key facts

- GIRK channels are opened directly by the G protein βγ complex dissociated from Gi or Go proteins upon activation of metabotropic receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p361]
- The mechanism by which βγ-subunits activate GIRK channels involves direct binding to cytoplasmic domains of the channel, leading to conformational changes that promote channel opening. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p361]
- GIRK channels pass current more readily in the inward than outward direction, though in physiological situations K+ current is always outward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p361]
- Activation of GIRK channels hyperpolarizes the membrane in the direction of EK (−80 mV), opposing depolarizing pacemaker currents in spontaneously active neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p361]
- ACh released from vagus nerve fibers opens GIRK channels in cardiac muscle cells, hyperpolarizing the cells and slowing the heart rate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p361]
