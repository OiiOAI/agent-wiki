# Hawk-Dove Model

## Summary

The Hawk-Dove model is a game-theoretic framework introduced by J. Maynard Smith and G.R. Price for analyzing animal contests. Hawks always escalate to injury or death; doves merely display conventionally. The ESS equilibrium is a stable polymorphism or mixed strategy with approximately 7 hawks to 5 doves, demonstrating that neither pure strategy is stable.

## Key facts

- In the hawk-dove model, hawks always fight unrestrainedly until seriously injured, while doves merely threaten conventionally and retreat if attacked. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p100]
- With arbitrary point values (50 for win, -100 for injury, -10 for time wasted), a pure dove population yields average pay-off of +15 per contest, which can be invaded by a hawk mutant achieving +50. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p100]
- In a pure hawk population, average pay-off is -25, allowing doves (pay-off 0) to invade—the ESS requires a mixed population. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p101]
- The stable equilibrium ratio under the arbitrary scoring is 7 doves to 5 hawks, where average pay-offs for both types are equal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p102]
- The ESS is not the optimum for individuals: an all-dove population (pay-off 15) is better than the ESS for everyone, but cannot resist invasion by hawks—a conspiracy is unstable against treachery. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p102]
- More complex strategies include Retaliator (dove initially, hawk if attacked), Bully (hawk until hit back, then flee), and Prober-retaliator (mostly retaliator with occasional experimental escalation). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p104]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hawk-dove model shows that 'common sense' about what should evolve can be misleading—ESS analysis reveals why purely selfish or purely cooperative populations are both unstable.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific 7:5 ratio depends on the arbitrary point values assigned; real ESS ratios in nature would depend on actual cost-benefit calculations of injury, time, and resource value.
