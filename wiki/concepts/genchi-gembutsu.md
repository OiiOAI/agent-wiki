# Genchi Gembutsu

## Summary

Genchi Gembutsu is a Japanese term from the Toyota Production System meaning 'go and see for yourself.' It is one of the most important phrases in lean manufacturing vocabulary, instructing managers to base strategic decisions on firsthand understanding of customers and conditions rather than reports or assumptions. While established companies can use this to discover what customers want, startups use it to discover which assumptions require the most urgent testing.

## Key facts

- Genchi gembutsu means 'go and see for yourself' so that business decisions can be based on deep firsthand knowledge. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p82]
- You cannot be sure you really understand any part of any business problem unless you go and see for yourself firsthand; it is unacceptable to take anything for granted or rely on reports of others. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p82]
- The Toyota example of genchi gembutsu involved chief engineer Yuji Yokoya driving 53,000 miles across North America to observe customers before developing the 2004 Sienna minivan. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p83]
