# Stress Response

## Summary

The body's physiological reaction to perceived threats, involving the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) system. The stress response activates the heart, lungs, circulation, and immune system to prepare the body for immediate survival action.

## Key facts

- The HPA system (stress axis) is thought to have evolved in the earliest vertebrates and has been maintained by evolution in all subsequent species. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p77]
- The HPA system manufactures and releases glucocorticoids including cortisol, the main stress hormone. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p77]
- During stress response, blood flow increases as much as 400 percent, preparing muscles, brain, and lungs for action. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p77]
- The stress response suppresses immune function to redistribute immune molecules to skin, bone marrow, and lymph nodes. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p77]
- The stress response has only one job - to save your life in an immediate crisis. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Geek_ Secular Meditation for S - Michael Taft.pdf#p78]

## Inferences

- Inference: Chronic activation of the stress response (chronic stress) causes the beneficial survival mechanisms to become harmful when sustained over time.
