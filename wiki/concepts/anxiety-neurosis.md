# Anxiety Neurosis

## Summary

Anxiety neurosis is a neurosis characterized by chronic anxiety symptoms and anxiety attacks, arising from the damming up of sexual tension. Freud theorized that physical sexual tension that cannot be transformed into psychical affect is instead converted into anxiety.

## Key facts

- Anxiety neurosis appears in two forms: as a chronic state and as an attack of anxiety, which may be combined. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p210]
- Chronic symptoms include hypochondria, agoraphobia, claustrophobia, giddiness on heights, and folie du doute. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p210]
- Anxiety neurosis is acquired especially by men and women in marriage through coitus interruptus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p211]
- Where physical sexual tension accumulates but cannot be turned into affect by psychical working over, the sexual tension is transformed into anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p218]
- Anxiety neurosis affects women who are anaesthetic in coitus just as much as sensitive ones, indicating the source of anxiety lies in the physical rather than psychical sphere. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p218]
- Anxiety neurosis is a neurosis of damming up, like hysteria; hence their similarity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p218]
- Freud separated anxiety neurosis from neurasthenia, arguing that its symptoms are more closely related to each other than to typical neurasthenic symptoms and that both aetiology and mechanism differ fundamentally. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p976]
- The clinical picture of anxiety neurosis includes: general irritability, anxious expectation, anxiety attacks, pavor nocturnus, vertigo, phobias (agoraphobia and others), gastrointestinal disturbances, and paraesthesias. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p977]
- Freud identified sexual aetiology in women including: first sexual experiences, newly married women with anaesthesia, husbands' potency problems, coitus interruptus/reservatus, widowhood/abstinence, and climacteric. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p984]
- Freud identified sexual aetiology in men including: intentional abstinence, unconsummated excitation, coitus interruptus, and male climacteric. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p985]
- Freud observed that anxiety attacks in married women occur regularly two days after a coitus deficient in satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p987]
- Anxiety neurosis symptoms include irritability, anxious expectation, phobias, anxiety attacks, vertigo, tremors, sweating, tachycardia, dyspnoea, and chronic diarrhoea. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p151]
- The specific aetiology of anxiety neurosis consists of sexual disturbances: enforced abstinence, unconsummated genital excitation, coitus interruptus, and sexual efforts exceeding psychical capacity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p151]
- The mechanism of anxiety neurosis involves somatic sexual excitation being deflected from the psychical sphere and expended subcortically in inadequate reactions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p98]
- Anxiety neurosis symptoms include free anxiousness, unrest, expectant anxiety, complete, rudimentary or supplementary anxiety attacks, locomotor vertigo, agoraphobia, insomnia, and increased sensitivity to pain. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1161]
- Anxiety neurosis regularly discloses sexual influences which have in common the factor of reservation or incomplete satisfaction such as coitus interruptus, abstinence together with a lively libido, and unconsummated excitation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1162]
- Freud formulated that anxiety is always libido which has been deflected from its normal employment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1162]

## Inferences

- Inference: The symptoms of anxiety neurosis represent the disjointed pieces of a major anxiety attack, including dyspnoea and palpitations that ordinarily belong to coitus.
- Inference: anxiety neurosis there is a deficit in sexual affect, in psychical libido.
- Inference: Freud's concept of anxiety neurosis was groundbreaking because it established that specific sexual factors (rather than heredity alone) determine the form of the neurosis, while quantity factors determine whether illness occurs at all.
- Inference: The distinction between neurasthenia and anxiety neurosis on both symptomatic and aetiological grounds represents a nosological innovation in neurotic classification.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact age at which 'sexual maturity' (as distinct from puberty) begins remains unclear from this text; Freud mentions the period from eight to ten years as significant for deferred trauma effects.
