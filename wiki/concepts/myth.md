# Myth

## Summary

Carse's philosophy distinguishes myth from explanation: while explanation absorbs the unspeakable into the speakable and establishes islands of order, myth reintroduces the silence that makes original discourse possible. A story attains the status of myth when it is retold, persistently retold, solely for its own sake. Myths are not stories that have meanings but stories that give meanings—they reach through experience to touch the genius in each of us. Myths cannot be observed; once heard, one enters their dimensionality. Myth provokes explanation but accepts none of it. The resonance of myth collapses the distinction between story told and the story of their telling and listening. Myths of irrepressible resonance have lost all trace of an author.
