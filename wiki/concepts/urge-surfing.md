# Urge Surfing

## Summary

Urge surfing is a technique for handling urges by treating them like ocean waves—they start small, build, reach a peak (crest), then subside. In urge surfing, one neither gives in to nor resists urges; instead, one opens up and makes room for them. If an ocean wave encounters resistance (rocks or beach), it crashes and becomes loud, messy, and potentially destructive. Without resistance, it harmlessly rises and falls. The technique involves TAME: Take note (notice and name the urge and where it's felt in the body), Allow (give permission for the urge to be there), Make room (open up and let it rise and peak and fall), and Expand awareness (broaden focus while watching the urge-wave rise, crest, and subside). Most urges last about three minutes from start to end of one wave, though multiple waves may come in challenging situations. Scoring urges on a 0-10 scale helps track whether they're rising, peaking, or falling.

## Key facts

- Urge surfing means treating urges like waves and surfing them until they dissipate, neither giving in to nor resisting them. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p180]
- The term urge surfing was coined by psychologists Alan Marlatt and Judith Gordon in the 1980s as part of groundbreaking work with drug addiction. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p180]
- Most urges from start to end of one wave last about three minutes, though they can sometimes go longer. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p181]
- Urges that seem to go on for ages do so because people are resisting them; when room is made for them, waves usually rise and fall quickly. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p181]
- Scoring an urge on a scale of 0 to 10 enables tracking whether it's rising, peaking, or falling. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p182]
