# Signal Detection Theory

## Summary

Signal detection theory provides a mathematical framework for quantifying an observer's ability to detect and discriminate stimuli. It distinguishes sensitivity (d-prime, the ability to discriminate signal from noise) from response bias (the tendency to say yes or no regardless of sensitivity) by analyzing hits, false alarms, misses, and correct rejections.

## Key facts

- In signal detection theory, sensitivity (d-prime) measures the discriminability between signal and noise distributions, while response bias reflects the decision criterion independent of sensitivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p389]
- ROC (receiver operating characteristic) plots graph the probability of hits against probability of false alarms across different decision criteria; the area under the ROC curve (AUC) provides a single measure of discriminability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]
- Signal detection methods have been applied to correlate neural firing rates with sensory processing, showing that neurometric functions correspond closely to psychometric functions for the same stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]
- The discrimination index d' equates the physical distance between stimulus amplitudes to a psychological scale value of inferred loudness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p505]
- Signal detection theory accounts for false-positive responses when subjects report detecting stimuli on catch trials where no stimulus was presented. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p505]
- The neurometric function plotting neural discriminability corresponds closely to psychometric functions obtained in behavioral tests, providing a physiological basis for behavioral responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p508]
