# Disciplined Serendipity

## Summary

Disciplined serendipity is a learning tactic involving deliberate exposure to unexpected discoveries while maintaining time constraints. The concept involves setting aside a portion of research time (recommended 15-20 percent) for browsing unrelated materials, visiting bookstores, and exploring topics that may seem to have little immediate value. The goal is to build a rich database of diverse knowledge that can later help solve problems through unexpected connections.

## Key facts

- Disciplined serendipity involves browsing unrelated materials and bookshelves while maintaining strict time limits on these explorations. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p88]
- The author recommends limiting serendipity time to 15-20 percent of total research time to prevent sabotaging other research goals. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p89]
- John Underkoffler described discovering new information as rekindling 'the curiosity candle' and called such moments the fun part of research. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p89]
- Curious people create 'anti-reading lists' including publications offering perspectives opposite to their own to break out of echo chambers. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p89]
