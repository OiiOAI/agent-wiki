# Chain Connection

## Summary

A technical relation between expectations in different social positions. If an advantage raises the expectations of the lowest position, it also raises the expectations of all positions in between. Chain connection is a simplifying assumption that helps show how the difference principle can have the same practical consequences as the principles of average utility and efficiency under certain conditions. Rawls acknowledges chain connection may not always hold but argues it is often probable given the wide diffusion of benefits from just institutions.

## Key facts

- Chain connection means that if an advantage has the effect of raising the expectations of the lowest position, it raises the expectations of all positions in between. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p83]
- Chain connection says nothing about the case where the least advantaged do not gain, so it does not mean that all effects move together. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p83]
- When chain connection holds, the representative man who is better off gains by the advantages offered him, and the man who is worse off gains from the contributions which these inequalities make. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p83]
- When contributions of the more favored spread generally throughout society, if the least advantaged benefit so do others in between; a wide diffusion of benefits is favored by institutions set up to advance common interests and by open offices. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p84]
- The difference principle is not contingent on chain connection being satisfied, but when it does hold, the difference principle has the same practical consequences as the principles of average utility and efficiency. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p84]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rawls suggests that within the region of positive contributions (where advantages of favored positions raise the prospects of the least fortunate), any movement toward the perfectly just arrangement both increases average well-being and improves everyone's expectation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Rawls does not examine how likely chain connection is to hold in practice; he acknowledges it is a simplifying assumption.
