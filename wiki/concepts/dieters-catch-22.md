# The Dieter's Catch-22

## Summary

The dieter's catch-22 describes a physiological paradox: to not eat requires willpower, but to have willpower requires eating. Self-control depletes glucose in the bloodstream, and the body 'knows' that sweet foods provide the fastest glucose restoration. Dieting causes people to lose touch with internal hunger and fullness signals because they eat by external rules rather than bodily cues. Research by Kathleen Vohs and Todd Heatherton showed that dieters who suppressed emotions during a sad movie ate over 50% more ice cream than dieters allowed to cry freely, demonstrating ego depletion. The depleted state intensifies all desires, including cravings for sweet foods.

## Key facts

- Dieters who suppressed emotions during a tearjerker ate considerably more ice cream than dieters who cried freely, demonstrating ego depletion. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p205]
- College students who performed self-control tasks had higher desires for sweet foods afterward and ate more sweet (but not salty) snacks. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p207]
- The depleted state makes all desires and cravings more intense than usual, turning up the volume on feelings throughout the day. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p207]
