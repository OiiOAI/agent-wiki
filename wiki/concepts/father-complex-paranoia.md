# Father Complex in Paranoia

## Summary

Freud's concept that paranoia centers on a father complex, where unconscious homosexual desires directed toward the father are repressed and transformed through projection. The persecutor (initially the doctor) represents a displacement of the father figure, and the delusional system represents an attempt to reconcile the forbidden wishes with the ego.

## Key facts

- Freud traced Schreber's struggle with Flechsig to an underlying conflict with his father, whom he loved and whose early death separated him when he was 19. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3759]
- The feminine wishful phantasy in Schreber had its roots in a longing for his father and brother intensified to an erotic pitch, which passed via transference to his doctor Flechsig. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3764]
- Schreber's father being a physician explained the peculiarities of his God, who 'understands nothing about living men and only knows how to deal with corpses.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3763]
- The sun, as a symbol of the father, held special significance in Schreber's delusions—he boasted of being able to gaze at it undazzled, a mythological privilege expressing his filial relation to the sun as father-symbol. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3787]
- Freud concluded that Schreber's relation to God was modeled on his infantile attitude toward his father, 'composed of the same mixture of reverent submission and mutinous insubordination.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3763]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's discovery of the father complex in Schreber connected paranoia to the broader Freudian theory of the Oedipus complex and infantile sexuality, establishing the universal significance of father relations in paranoid disorders.
