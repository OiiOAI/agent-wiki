# Identification in Hysteria

## Summary

Identification in hysteria is the mechanism by which hysterical patients incorporate into their symptoms not only their own experiences but those of others, expressing shared sexual or emotional elements. Freud presents this as an unconscious process of assimilation based on common aetiological factors, not mere imitation. The concept is introduced in relation to dream interpretation where the dreamer may identify with another person.

## Key facts

- Freud defines identification as 'not simple imitation but assimilation on the basis of a similar aetiological pretension; it expresses a resemblance and is derived from a common element which remains in the unconscious.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1419]
- In the dream of the abandoned supper party, the patient identified with her rival friend, creating a symptom of renounced wishes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1419]
- Freud states that identification is most frequently used in hysteria to express a common sexual element, and two lovers are 'one.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1419]

## Inferences

- Inference: The process of identification in hysteria parallels the process of identification in dreams, where the dreamer may take another's place to express wishes that cannot be directly acknowledged.
