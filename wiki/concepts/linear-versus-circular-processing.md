# Linear versus Circular Processing

## Summary

The left hemisphere processes information linearly and sequentially, preferring straight lines, whereas the right hemisphere processes holistically and circularly, accommodating context and the coming together of opposites. These processing styles are reflected in cultural forms: Western architecture and thought are characterised by rectilinearity, while the right hemisphere-oriented cultures value roundness and cyclical patterns.

## Key facts

- The left hemisphere is the hemisphere of linear processing, with a sequential cognitive style, propensity to linear analysis, and a world of getting and utility. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p610]
- Rectilinearity is absent from the natural world; everything that exists follows curved shapes, and even space is curved. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p611]
- The right hemisphere's cognitive processing is not sequential but involves something coming into focus as a whole, understood within its context. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p612]
- The movement of the right hemisphere is described as the musical, whole-bodied, socially generative movement of dance, which is never in a straight line towards something, but always returns to its origins. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p612]

## Inferences

- Inference: The left hemisphere's preference for linear, rectangular forms has shaped the modern urban environment, creating feedback loops that reinforce left-hemisphere-dominant processing.
