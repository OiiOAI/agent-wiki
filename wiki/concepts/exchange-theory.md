# Exchange Theory

## Summary

Exchange theory emerged in the 1950s as an important micro-level sociological perspective. The major figure was George Homans, who developed his theory based on B. F. Skinner's psychological behaviorism. Homans's basic view was that sociology should focus on individual behavior and interaction, on reinforcement patterns, and on the history of rewards and costs that lead people to act as they do. People continue to do what has been rewarding and cease doing what has been costly. Peter Blau's "Exchange and Power in Social Life" (1964) extended the theory by integrating elementary forms of social behavior with exchange at structural and cultural levels. Richard Emerson later developed a more integrated micro-macro approach to exchange theory.
