# Winston's Star

## Summary

Winston's Star is a framework for ensuring spoken and written communications are memorable, consisting of five elements whose labels start with S: Slogan, Symbol, Salient idea, Surprise, and Story. These five elements combine to make work more memorable and lasting.

## Key facts

- Winston's Star consists of five elements: Slogan, Symbol, Salient idea, Surprise, and Story. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p48]
- A slogan serves as a handle—a short, memorable word or phrase that audiences remember. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p41]
- Symbols such as pictures, drawings, maps, graphs, and graphics serve as symbolic handles on work. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p42]
- Presentations should include no more than three salient ideas because most people can remember only a few. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p44]
- Surprises should be noted when work contains them because surprises are what people tell others about. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p45]
- Humans are storytelling animals whose story competences distinguish them from other species. [raw/books/strategy/Make it Clear - Patrick Henry Winston.pdf#p45]
