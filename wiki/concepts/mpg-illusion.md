# MPG Illusion

## Summary

The MPG Illusion is a framing effect identified by Larrick and Soll (2008) where expressing fuel efficiency as miles-per-gallon (MPG) misleads consumers and policymakers about which vehicle upgrades save the most fuel. Switching from 30 to 40 MPG saves 83 gallons over 10,000 miles, while switching from 12 to 14 MPG saves 119 gallons—yet most people intuitively judge the latter improvement as smaller.

## Key facts

- Switching from a 12 MPG car to a 14 MPG car saves 119 gallons over 10,000 miles, while switching from 30 MPG to 40 MPG saves only 83 gallons—yet most people believe the latter improvement is more significant. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p480]
- The correct metric for fuel efficiency decisions is gallons-per-mile (or liters per 100 km, used in most other countries), which would lead to better choices for individuals and policymakers. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p480]
- The MPG Illusion influenced policy: starting in 2013, US fuel economy stickers for new cars included gallons-per-mile information alongside MPG, as mandated under the Obama administration by Cass Sunstein. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p480]
