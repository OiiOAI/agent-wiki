# Unhooking (Defusion)

## Summary

Unhooking, or defusion, is the process of stepping back from thoughts rather than being dominated by them. In ACT, the technical term for this is 'defusion' while everyday language uses terms like being lost, trapped, or consumed by thoughts. When unhooked, thoughts are seen as words passing through the head rather than commands to obey or truths to believe. Various techniques exist including naming the thought, saying 'I'm having the thought that,' musical thoughts, and thanking the mind.

## Key facts

- The technical term for OBEY mode in ACT is 'fusion,' and the opposite process is called 'defusion' (commonly called unhooking). [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p35]
- When fused (hooked), thoughts seem like commands to obey or absolute truths to believe. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p90]
- When defused (unhooked), thoughts are recognized as sounds, words, stories, or bits of language that may or may not be important, wise, or true. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p92]
- The technique 'I'm having the thought that...' provides distance from difficult thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p91]
- Musical thoughts technique involves singing negative thoughts to a tune to see their 'true nature' as strings of words. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p94]
