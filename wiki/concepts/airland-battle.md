# AirLand Battle

## Summary

AirLand Battle was the US military doctrine developed in the 1970s-1980s for defending NATO Europe against Warsaw Pact invasion. The doctrine, led by General William DePuy and refined by Lieutenant General Donn Starry, emphasized aggressive counters deep into enemy territory using superior NATO communications, sensing, and operational flexibility.

## Key facts

- The 1973 Arab-Israeli War demonstrated surprisingly effective portable missiles and rockets that destroyed heavily armored tanks and low-flying aircraft. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p144]
- The Warsaw Pact war plan envisioned a 'double echelon' attack with the first wave degrading defenses and identifying weak points, then the second wave striking through. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p145]
- DePuy developed 'Active Defense' emphasizing tactical movement and exploiting the mobility of tanks, mechanized infantry, and close air support. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p147]
- AirLand Battle expanded to tight coordination between army and air force, using long-range strikes to confuse and disorient enemies and shape their movements. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p148]
