# Strategic Misrepresentation

## Summary

Strategic misrepresentation is the tendency to exaggerate claims when stakes are high, as in job interviews or project bids. It is most common in mega-projects where accountability is diffuse, many businesses are involved, or completion is years away. Oxford professor Bent Flyvbjerg calls it "reverse Darwinism"—whoever produces the most hot air wins.

## Key facts

- Strategic misrepresentation is most vulnerable in mega-projects where A) accountability is diffuse, B) many businesses are involved, or C) the end date is years away. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p196]
- Bent Flyvbjerg found cost and schedule overruns are frequent because the best offer overall doesn't win—only the one that looks best on paper. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p196]
