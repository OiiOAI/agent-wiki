# Paranoia as Neurotic Mechanism

## Summary

Freud presents a detailed analysis of paranoia as a neurotic mechanism involving projection and displacement. In cases of jealous paranoia, the patient hypercathects interpretations of the partner's unconscious while keeping their own unconscious impulses repressed. The behavior of both jealous and persecutory paranoics involves being guided by their knowledge of the unconscious and displacing attention from their own to others' unconscious minds. Dreams of paranoics may either be free from delusion or contain persecutory content depending on the state of their waking delusional ideas.

## Key facts

- Freud describes how the jealous husband perceived his wife's unfaithfulness instead of his own, succeeding in keeping his own unconscious by becoming conscious of hers and magnifying it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5819]
- The abnormality in paranoia is described as consisting essentially in the hypercathexis of interpretations of someone else's unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5800]
- Freud notes that the qualitative factor—the presence of certain neurotic formations—has less practical significance than the quantitative factor, the degree of cathexis these structures attract to themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5802]
- Dreams are distinguished from waking thought by including material belonging to the region of the repressed which must not emerge in waking thought, but preconscious thoughts in dreams may bear the character of any neurosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5821]
