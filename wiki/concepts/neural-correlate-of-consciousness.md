# Neural Correlate of Consciousness

## Summary

The neural correlate of consciousness refers to the neural activity that occurs while a person engages in an activity requiring conscious attention. Crick and Koch coined this term to describe what they sought to identify: specific neurons whose activity correlates with conscious perception. Kandel discusses how this represents the 'easy problem' of consciousness—finding correlations between brain activity and conscious experience—while the 'hard problem' remains explaining how subjective experience arises from these neural processes.

## Key facts

- Crick and Koch defined the neural correlate of consciousness as the material concomitant of conscious perception—neural activity that occurs during conscious mental processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p389]
- Finding neural correlates of consciousness would pinpoint a material concomitant of conscious perception, but for Nagel and Searle this is only the 'easy problem' of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p389]
- The hard problem of consciousness is explaining how subjective experience arises from neural activity, even in the simplest case. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p389]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether identifying neural correlates will lead to solving the hard problem of consciousness remains uncertain.
