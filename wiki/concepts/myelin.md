# Myelin

## Summary

Myelin is the insulating material produced by oligodendrocytes in the CNS and Schwann cells in the PNS that allows rapid conduction of electrical signals along axons. It consists of bimolecular layers of lipid interspersed between protein layers, with a composition of approximately 70% lipid and 30% protein.

## Key facts

- Myelin consists of bimolecular layers of lipid interspersed between protein layers, approximately 70% lipid and 30% protein with high concentrations of cholesterol and phospholipid. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p139]
- CNS myelin is produced by oligodendrocytes, which can form myelin sheaths for segments of up to 30 axons; PNS myelin is produced by Schwann cells, one sheath per axon segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p139]
- The major proteins in CNS myelin are myelin basic protein and proteolipid protein, while PNS myelin contains P0 protein and PMP22. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p139]
- Nodes of Ranvier are unmyelinated gaps where the axon's membrane is exposed to extracellular space for approximately 1 micrometer, enabling saltatory conduction at speeds up to 100 m/s in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p139]
