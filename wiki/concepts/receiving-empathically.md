# Receiving Empathically

## Summary

Receiving empathically is the second component of Nonviolent Communication, involving applying the four NVC components (observations, feelings, needs, requests) to hearing what others express. It requires emptying our mind and listening with our whole being, focusing full attention on the other person without giving advice, reassurance, or explanations.

## Key facts

- Empathy requires us to shed all preconceived ideas and judgments about others. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p135]
- Instead of offering empathy, people tend to give advice or reassurance and explain their own position or feeling. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p135]
- Intellectual understanding blocks empathy; when thinking about words and listening to how they connect to theories, we are looking at people but not with them. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p136]
- We should listen to what people are needing rather than what they are thinking. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p139]
- When asking for information, first express our own feelings and needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p140]
- Reflect back messages that are emotionally charged. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p142]
- Paraphrase only when it contributes to greater compassion and understanding. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p142]
- When we stay with empathy, we allow speakers to touch deeper levels of themselves. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p146]
- We know a speaker has received adequate empathy when we sense a release of tension or the flow of words comes to a halt. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p147]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept emphasizes that presence—not analysis or problem-solving—is the key ingredient of empathy, distinguishing it from mental understanding or sympathy.
