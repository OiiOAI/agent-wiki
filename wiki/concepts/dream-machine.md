# The Dream Machine

## Summary

The Dream Machine is a structured approach to optimising chances of success when you have a specific dream. It distinguishes between dreams (something you want to happen but is not fully under your control) and goals (something you can set and achieve because you have full control). The model incorporates seven distinct cogs: Cog 1 identifies the dream, Cog 2 identifies Foundation Stones, Cog 3 applies the Commitment Screen, Cog 4 creates the plan in stages, Cog 5 oils the wheels with rewards and motivation, Cog 6 audits progress, and Cog 7 deals with outcomes including success, partial success, and failure.

## Key facts

- A dream is something you want to happen but is not fully under your control, with outside influences, so you cannot guarantee it will happen; goals are something you can set and achieve because you have full control of them. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p181]
- Whenever the brain recognises it does not have full control of a situation it sends blood supply to the Chimp, resulting in unease; if it recognises full control then the Chimp relaxes and blood goes to the Human, resulting in calmness and opportunity. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p182]
- Foundation Stones are components you can work on to achieve your dream; each can be given a measurable goal divided into target goals (where you feel you can make gains) and maintenance goals (ones you are happy with but keeping an eye on). [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p184]
- It is best to select just one or two target goals to work on over a fixed period of time; if you spread efforts on too many things you increase your chances of failing. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p184]
- When you reach a camp (goal), make sure you celebrate; monitor success with a chart or visible record; look back to see how far you have come rather than looking up the mountain which may dishearten you. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p187]
- For studying, form a small step to an easy camp: instead of committing to three hours, say you will study for 15 minutes each day and then stop—on a daily basis 15 minutes will cover significant ground and nearly every Chimp will agree to this. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p189]
- The audit cog involves recruiting someone you can be accountable to, checking goals are realistic, ensuring Human method of thinking is employed, making sure problem-solving is by the Human, not repainting the picture, and turning the light on and going to court. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p190]
- Failure or setbacks should be seen as challenges and opportunities to develop yourself and skills; the Chimp sees failure as catastrophic and evidence of worthlessness, while Humans see failure as relative, accept we sometimes do not win, and see it as a learning curve. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p194]
- The Chimp grief reaction to failure typically goes through stages: Denial, Yearning, Bargaining, Anger, Disorganisation, and Reorganisation, taking about three months for the acute phase and up to a year to function again. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p195]
