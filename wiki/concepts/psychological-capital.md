# Psychological Capital (PsyCap)

## Summary

Psychological Capital (PsyCap) is a framework comprising four components: self-efficacy, optimism, hope, and resilience. Research shows that together these predict outcomes greater than the sum of their parts. GTD directly builds all four components by enabling complete inventory of commitments, front-end decision making, and systematic execution that builds confidence through wins.

## Key facts

- Psychological Capital consists of four components: self-efficacy (confidence to take on challenging tasks), optimism (positive attributions about success), hope (perseverance toward goals), and resilience (bouncing back from adversity). [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p342]
- When self-efficacy, optimism, hope, and resilience are considered together as PsyCap, they predict much more than the sum of their individual effects. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p342]
- GTD builds self-efficacy by enabling complete commitment inventory and systematic next-action decisions that produce consistent wins. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p342]
- GTD builds hope through goal-setting ('What does done look like?') and pathway identification ('What's the next action?'). [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p342]
- PsyCap is described as a state rather than a trait—it changes minute to minute based on circumstances, and can be improved through deliberate practices. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p342]

## Inferences

- Inference: The organizational benefits of GTD culture documented by Allen may be explained by collective PsyCap gains—when teams share reliable systems, trust and psychological safety increase.
