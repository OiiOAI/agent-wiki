# Income Inequality and Social Capital

## Summary

Cultures with more income inequality have less social capital (trust, reciprocity, cooperation). This occurs because trust requires reciprocity, which requires equality, whereas hierarchy is about domination. Inequality also produces worse health outcomes through both psychosocial pathways (stress from lack of control) and neomaterialist pathways (secession of wealthy from public goods). Violence increases with inequality as the poor prey on the poor.

## Key facts

- The more income inequality, the less likely people are to help someone in an experimental setting and the less generous and cooperative they are in economic games. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p303]
- Subjective SES (feeling poor compared to others) is at least as good a predictor of health as objective SES, showing it's feeling poor that matters, not just being poor. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p303]
- Extent of income inequality is a major predictor of rates of violent crime across American states and across industrialized nations. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p305]
- When inequality fuels violence, it is mostly the poor preying on the poor rather than class warfare against the wealthy. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p305]
