# Storage Strength vs. Retrieval Strength

## Summary

Robert and Elizabeth Ligon Bjork distinguished between storage strength (the ability to store memories) and retrieval strength (the ability to recall them). Storage strength only increases over a lifetime, but learning is really about building connections that allow information to be retrieved when needed. This shifts focus from cramming facts to creating effective retrieval cues.

## Key facts

- Storage strength, the ability to store memories, only becomes greater over one's lifetime. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p95]
- Learning is not so much about saving information, but about building connections and bridges between pieces of information to circumvent the inhibition mechanism in the right moment. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p96]
- Every piece of information can become the trigger for another piece of information, but there are no natural cues. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p96]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The claim that storage strength only increases over one's lifetime is a theory from the Bjorks, not definitively proven.
