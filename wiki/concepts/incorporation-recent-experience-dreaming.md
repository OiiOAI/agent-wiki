# Incorporation of recent experience into dreaming

## Summary

The incorporation of recent experience into dreaming refers to the phenomenon where fragments of waking experiences appear in subsequent dream content. Rather than veridical replay, recent memories appear as isolated fragments intermingled with remote and semantic material, creating novel scenarios.

## Key facts

- Recent memories constitute a significant component of sleep mentation, with fragments of recent experience often seen in dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p5]
- Dreams rarely consist of an exact replay of a life event; instead, isolated elements of waking episodes are intermingled with fragments of other recent memories, as well as remote and semantic memory material. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p6]
- Waking experiences tend to be incorporated into dream mentation either immediately after the experience or about a week later (the dream lag effect). [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p6]
- Very recent events are more often incorporated into mentation early in the sleep phase, with remote experiences appearing only later in the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p6]
- The laboratory environment itself has a powerful influence on dream content, sometimes overshadowing experimentally introduced stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p5]
- Intensive, engaging learning experiences can dramatically influence dream content, at least early in the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p5]

## Inferences

- Inference: The fragmentary form in which waking experience appears in dreams may reflect a critical feature of memory consolidation, where new memories are interleaved with existing memory traces.
