# Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

## Summary

A theory of motivation defining needs in a hierarchy: physiological, safety/security, social/affiliation, esteem/recognition, and self-actualization. When a lower need is satisfied, a higher one takes over. The first three needs motivate people to show up for work; esteem and self-actualization make people perform once there.

## Key facts

- Maslow defined needs in a hierarchy: physiological, safety/security, social/affiliation, esteem/recognition, and self-actualization; when a lower need is satisfied, a higher one takes over. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1495-L1497]
- Physiological, safety/security, and social needs can motivate people to show up for work, but esteem and self-actualization make people perform once there. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1499-L1501]
- Self-actualization stems from 'what I can be, I must be' and is characterized by the need to achieve one's personal best; unlike other needs, it has no limit. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1510-L1512]
- Two inner forces drive self-actualization: competence-driven (job or task mastery) and achievement-driven (testing oneself against challenges). [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1512-L1514]
- Money motivates within physiological and safety modes until needs are satisfied, but as a measure of achievement it motivates without limit. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1520-L1522]
