# Walter Hess

## Summary

Walter Hess (1881-1973) was a Swiss physiologist who won the 1949 Nobel Prize for discovering the functional organization of the interbrain as coordinator of internal organ activities. His electrical stimulation studies revealed distinct emotional circuits.

## Key facts

- Walter Hess received the 1949 Nobel Prize for his discovery of the functional organization of the interbrain as a coordinator of the activities of the internal organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
- Hess demonstrated that electrical stimulation of specific hypothalamic and midbrain zones could elicit coordinated emotional behavior patterns including anger, fear, curiosity, hunger, and nausea. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
- Hess's work revealed that animals craved stimulation of certain brain sites (like the medial forebrain bundle) but despised stimulation of other nearby sites including anterior hypothalamus and periventricular gray. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p49]
