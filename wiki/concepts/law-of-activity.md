# Law of Activity

## Summary

The Law of Activity states that humans need initiative—the God-given ability to propel themselves into life. Boundaries are formed when children push against the world naturally and the outside world sets limits. Passivity is presented as an ally of evil that never pays off.

## Key facts

- The Law of Activity states that many boundary problems occur because people lack initiative—the God-given ability to propel themselves into life. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p103]
- The best boundaries are formed when a child pushes against the world naturally and the outside world sets limits without destroying the child's spirit. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p103]
- The authors contrast active and passive people using the parable of the talents: those who succeeded were active and assertive; the one who lost out was passive and inactive. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p103]
- The authors state: 'God will match our effort, but he will never do our work for us. That would be an invasion of our boundaries.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p103]
- Hebrew 10:38-39 is cited: 'But if he shrinks back, I will not be pleased with him'—indicating passive shrinking back is intolerable to God. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p103]
- The authors use the analogy of a baby bird needing to peck its own way out of the egg to strengthen itself for the outside world. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p104]

## Inferences

- Inference: The authors suggest that passivity enables evil by not confronting it.
