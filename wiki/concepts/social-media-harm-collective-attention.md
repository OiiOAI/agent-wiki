# Social Media Harm to Collective Attention

## Summary

The argument that social media damages not only individual attention but also society's collective ability to identify problems, distinguish truth from falsehood, and coordinate solutions. The combination of algorithmically amplified misinformation (traveling six times faster than truth on Twitter), political polarization, and radicalization undermines democratic sense-making at a time when complex challenges like climate change require sustained collective focus.

## Key facts

- An MIT study found fake news travels six times faster on Twitter than real news, and during the 2016 U.S. election, falsehoods on Facebook outperformed all top mainstream news sites combined. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p146]
- YouTube's algorithm was found to have recommended Alex Jones/Infowars content 15 billion times, and can lead from factual content about the Holocaust to Holocaust denial within five video recommendations. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p148]
- A study found that a majority of white nationalists named YouTube as the website that most influenced their radicalization. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p148]
- Tristan Harris argued these effects represent 'a system that is systematically, as you turn the crank every day, pumping out more radicalization' — creating 'bad apples' through a 'bad-apple factory.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p148]

## Inferences

- Inference: societies cannot collectively attend to and solve existential threats like climate change due to algorithmic information ecosystem degradation, the attention crisis becomes an existential threat in itself.
