# Toleration of the Intolerant

## Summary

Rawls addresses whether justice requires tolerating the intolerant, concluding that an intolerant sect has no title to complain if denied equal liberty (since it would not grant liberty to others), but that the tolerant should curb the intolerant only when necessary for their own security and that of the institutions of liberty. The key principle is to establish a just constitution with liberties of equal citizenship; the just should be guided by principles of justice rather than by the fact that the unjust cannot complain.

## Key facts

- An intolerant sect has no title to complain when denied an equal liberty, since it would not acknowledge principles that grant liberty to others. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p202]
- The tolerant should curb the intolerant only when they sincerely and with reason believe their own security and that of institutions of liberty are in danger. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p204]
- Even when the freedom of the intolerant is limited to safeguard a just constitution, this is not done in the name of maximizing liberty but only for the sake of equal liberty under a just constitution the principles of which the intolerant themselves would acknowledge in the original position.
