# Recognition-Primed Decision Model

## Summary

The Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) model, formulated by Klein, Calderwood, and Clinton-Cirocco (1986), describes how people make decisions in naturalistic settings without comparing options. The model posits that expertise enables decision makers to recognize situations as familiar and know typical responses. SA is central to all three variations of the RPD model: recognizing situations, analyzing interpretations, and mentally simulating courses of action.

## Key facts

- The RPD model explains how people make decisions in naturalistic settings without having to compare options, using expertise to evaluate situations and recognize typical courses of action. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p71]
- The first variation of the RPD model is the most simplistic: decision makers use experience to recognize a situation as familiar and know the typical reaction. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p72]
- The second variation covers cases where there exist alternative interpretations of the situation's nature, requiring analysis of evidence or construction of a story to determine which explanation is consistent with the evidence. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p72]
- The third variation describes how decision makers evaluate a course of action by mentally simulating it to see if it will work or whether it needs modification. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p72]
- The RPD model identifies four key aspects of SA that recognition carries: the most critical cues, the relevant goals, the events to expect next, and the actions commonly taken. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p73]
- Research with fireground commanders found they rarely compared options; instead, they used experience to recognize typical courses of action, demonstrating that 'recognition of the situation is to know what to do.'. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p75]
- The RPD model is consistent with Endsley's SA model: Level 1 SA corresponds to events in the environment, Level 2 SA maps onto recognition of the situation, and Level 3 SA corresponds to expectancies generated once a situation is recognized as typical. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p74]
- Mental simulation processes used to evaluate courses of action are also used to explain situations, with decision makers building stories encompassing events and using story plausibility to gauge explanation adequacy. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p78]
- The RPD model was first published by Klein and colleagues starting in 1985. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p15]
- The RPD model has three variations: (1) basic recognition and action, (2) diagnosis when situations are ambiguous, and (3) mental simulation to evaluate a course of action. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p47-49]
- In the original firefighter study, 80% of 156 decision points were classified as recognitional decisions using singular evaluation. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p46]
- Only 18 of 156 decision points involved comparative evaluation, and 11 involved novel option generation. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p46]
- The RPD model posits that decision makers evaluate single options by imagining how the course of action will play out, rather than comparing multiple options against each other. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p42]
- Decision makers look for the first workable option (satisficing) rather than the best option (optimizing). [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p52]
- Experienced decision makers use expectancies based on experience to detect anomalies when situations are misread. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p59-60]
- The RPD model appears to describe the decision strategy used most frequently by people with experience, explaining how they can use experience to make difficult decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p134]
- Studies across multiple domains show that 46% to 96% of challenging decisions were coded as using recognitional strategies, with comparative evaluation of options being infrequent. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p131]
- The RPD model is more likely under conditions of greater time pressure, higher experience level, dynamic conditions, and ill-defined goals. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p127]
- People are more likely to use comparative evaluation when they need to justify choices, when conflict resolution is needed, when optimizing, and when the situation is computationally complex. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p128]
- Mosier (1991) found that commercial airline crews made recognitional decisions based on few critical items of information, spending additional time verifying correctness rather than comparing options. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p132]
- Flin, Slaven, and Stewart (1996) found that 90% of strategies used by offshore oil installation managers were consistent with the RPD model. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p133]
- In the RPD model, proficient decision makers detect patterns and typicality, recognizing situations in a glance as cases they have seen before. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- Experience provides the ability to recognize that a situation is a typical case. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- When patterns are broken or expectancies violated, the RPD model describes this as triggering diagnostic efforts including seizing unexpected opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- The RPD model explains situation awareness as emerging from situation recognition, encompassing relevant cues, plausible goals, actions, and expectancies. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p191]
- The RPD model explains how people can make good decisions without generating and comparing a set of options, using pattern matching and mental simulation instead. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p46]
- Firefighters used the RPD process for more than 80 percent of their toughest incidents according to Klein's data analysis. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p48]
- Army officers used intuition in 96 percent of their decisions during planning, and naval commanders relied on intuition in 95 percent of their decisions. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p48]
- The RPD model posits that when faced with a familiar problem, the first solution recognized is likely to work because decision makers don't need the optimal option but rather a quickly identified acceptable option. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p44]

## Inferences

- Inference: The three variations of the RPD model represent a progression from straightforward recognition to situations requiring diagnosis and mental simulation, suggesting different cognitive demands.
- Inference: The finding that fireground commanders do not generate and compare alternatives challenges rational choice theories of decision making.
- Inference: The RPD model's dominance in naturalistic settings suggests that formal decision training may be less useful than experience-based pattern recognition training.
- Inference: The RPD model demonstrates that people can make effective decisions without using a rational choice strategy, providing a counterexample to rational choice theory.
- Inference: The RPD model challenges the classical decision-making model that requires comparing all options, showing that experienced experts rely primarily on pattern matching rather than analytical comparison.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact percentage of decisions made using RPD vs. analytical methods may vary by domain and expertise level, though the research consistently shows high reliance on intuitive processes.
