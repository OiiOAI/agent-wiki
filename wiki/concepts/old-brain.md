# The Old Brain

## Summary

The old brain comprises approximately 30% of the human brain and evolved much earlier than the neocortex. It creates primitive desires and actions including territoriality, mating competition, and both cooperative and aggressive behaviors. While these were successful adaptations for gene propagation, combined with modern technologies they now represent an existential threat.

## Key facts

- The old brain makes up 30% of the human brain and controls bodily functions, basic behaviors, and emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p208]
- The old brain still operates under rules laid down by hundreds of millions of years of survival including fighting for territory, mating rights, and various forms of antisocial behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p177]
- The old brain does not represent an existential risk on its own because past behaviors, even when harmful to individuals, did not threaten all of humanity. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p177]
- The old brain represents an existential threat because neocortex-created technologies can alter and destroy the entire planet while behavior remains dominated by short-sighted old brain impulses. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p177]
- From a gene's perspective, it is acceptable if a human family has more children than it can feed, because some children will survive even if others starve. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p178]
