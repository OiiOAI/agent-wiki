# Our Attitude Towards Death

## Summary

Freud's analysis of the human attitude towards death, arguing that civilization promotes a contradictory stance: publicly acknowledging death's necessity while psychologically denying it. The unconscious treats death as unreal and inapplicable to the self, while primal man and the unconscious show contradictory attitudes—readiness to kill strangers alongside inability to conceive of one's own death. War forces recognition of death's reality.

## Key facts

- Civilized people tend to put death on one side, eliminate it from life, and even have a saying 'to think of something as though it were death'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4682]
- The psychoanalytic school asserts that at bottom no one believes in his own death; in the unconscious everyone is convinced of his own immortality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4682]
- Primaeval man held contradictory attitudes: he took death seriously but also denied it; he had no objection to someone else's death but could not imagine his own. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4685]
- The first and most important prohibition, acquired in relation to dead loved ones, was 'Thou shalt not kill,' representing reaction against hidden hatred behind grief. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4688]
- The unconscious knows nothing negative, no negation; for that reason it does not know its own death. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4690]
- In our unconscious impulses we daily and hourly get rid of anyone who stands in our way; 'Devil take him!' is in our unconscious a serious and powerful death wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4690]
