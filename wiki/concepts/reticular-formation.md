# Reticular Formation

## Summary

The reticular formation is a collection of identifiable nuclei in the upper brain stem that collectively participate in wakefulness, sleep-wakefulness cycles, attention, homeostatic regulation, emotion and feeling, and consciousness. The critical region for consciousness extends from the upper pons upward, at and above the level of trigeminal nerve entry, where signals from the entire body converge. Damage to this posterior sector causes coma; damage to the adjacent anterior sector causes locked-in syndrome without impairing consciousness.

## Key facts

- The reticular formation contains both classical nuclei (cuneiform nucleus, nucleus pontis oralis) and nonclassical nuclei including monoamine nuclei (locus coeruleus, ventral tegmental area, substantia nigra, raphe nuclei) and acetylcholine nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p239]
- Damage to the posterior sector of the upper brain stem causes coma and persistent vegetative state; the anterior sector causes locked-in syndrome without impairing consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p243]
- The critical region for consciousness is at and above the level of trigeminal nerve entry, where signals from the entire body become available to brainstem nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p254]
- Reticular nuclei are anatomically and functionally interwoven with structures that regulate body state and map the body, suggesting the proto-self may include classical reticular nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p240]
- The reticular formation generates a continuous barrage of signals to thalamus and cerebral cortex, establishing geometries of cortical coherence necessary for conscious states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p250]
- The brainstem reticular formation is essential for maintaining wakefulness and attention, projecting to the thalamus and cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p358]
- Acetylcholine nuclei in the brainstem (pedunculopontine and laterodorsal tegmental nuclei) are major components of the ascending activating system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p358]
- Monoamine systems (including noradrenergic, dopaminergic, and serotonergic nuclei) also project widely from the brainstem to forebrain structures and regulate arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p358]
- The parabrachial nucleus, located in the pons, is now known to have extensive connections with forebrain structures and may be important for the neural correlates of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p361]
- Alzheimer's disease produces severe pathological changes in the parabrachial nucleus, which may contribute to autonomic dysfunction and disproportionate respiratory and gastroenteric disease in these patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p361]

## Inferences

- Inference: The overlap of functions in the reticular formation—homeostasis, emotion, attention, consciousness—suggests these are anatomically and functionally intertwined rather than separate systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether classical reticular nuclei should be included as components of the proto-self requires future research.
