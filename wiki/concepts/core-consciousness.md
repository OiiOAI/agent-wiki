# Core Consciousness

## Summary

Core consciousness is the simplest form of consciousness, providing a sense of self limited to the here and now. It is a simple, biological phenomenon with one level of organization, stable across the lifetime of the organism, not exclusively human, and not dependent on conventional memory, working memory, reasoning, or language.

## Key facts

- Core consciousness provides the organism with a sense of self about one moment (now) and one place (here). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Core consciousness does not illuminate the future; only the instant just before is vaguely glimpsed. There is no elsewhere, no before, no after. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Core consciousness is not dependent on conventional memory, working memory, reasoning, or language. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- Impairments that begin at the level of core consciousness demolish the entire edifice of consciousness: extended consciousness collapses as well. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p16]
- The sense of self emerging in core consciousness is called the core self, a transient entity ceaselessly re-created for each object with which the brain interacts. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p18]
- When consciousness is suspended from core consciousness on up, emotion is usually suspended as well, suggesting their underpinnings are connected. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p38]
- Core consciousness provides the knowledge that an individual organism is involved in relating to an object and that the object causes a change in the organism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p77]
- Core consciousness is generated in pulse-like fashion for each content of which we are conscious, producing a transient core self renewed continuously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p126]
- Core consciousness is not the same as wakefulness, attention, working memory, conventional memory, language, or reasoning—all of which can be separated from it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p121]
- When core consciousness fails, extended consciousness fails as well, but the reverse is not true. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p121]
- Emotion and core consciousness are associated: patients with impaired core consciousness lack emotional expressions (background, primary, and secondary emotions). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p99]
- Disturbances of core consciousness target the entire realm of mental activity and all sensory modalities—no island of preserved consciousness remains. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p123]
- Core consciousness is created in pulses, each pulse triggered by each object that we interact with or recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p147]
- Core consciousness produces a nonverbal narrative—an imaged account of the organism-object relationship—which constitutes the feeling of knowing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p182]
- The feeling of knowing enhances the images of the object and improves wakefulness and focused attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p183]
- Core consciousness does not depend on language; languaged animals and human babies would be conscious, contrary to language-dependency hypotheses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p155]
- Core consciousness is implemented by proto-self structures (brainstem nuclei, hypothalamus, somatosensory cortices) and second-order mapping structures (cingulate cortex, thalamus, superior colliculi). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p192]
- Core consciousness is defined by the sense of self in the act of knowing a particular object at a particular moment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p272]
- Core consciousness emerges from second-order neural patterns that describe the relationship between the organism (proto-self) and an object. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p272]
- Damage to brain regions supporting either the proto-self or second-order maps disrupts core consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p271]
- Core consciousness depends most critically on phylogenetically old brain structures beginning in the brain stem and ending with somatosensory and cingulate cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p272]
- Bilateral damage to the hippocampus does not impair core consciousness, nor does bilateral damage to visual or auditory cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p270]

## Inferences

- Inference: Core consciousness represents the foundational biological substrate from which extended consciousness evolved.
- Inference: Core consciousness can be distinguished from the inferences we draw about its contents—it is the unvarnished sense of the organism in the act of knowing, prior to verbal or logical interpretation.
- Inference: Core consciousness evolved earlier than extended consciousness both phylogenetically and ontogenetically.
- Inference: The core self is transient and must be reborn continuously with each consciousness pulse.
- Inference: Core consciousness is distinct from extended consciousness in that it does not require autobiographical memory, working memory, or language—the facilities that allow extended consciousness to operate across time.
- Inference: The counterintuitive finding that evolutionarily older, deeper brain structures are more critical for consciousness than the neocortex suggests consciousness may have originated in ancient regulatory systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms of how second-order structures coordinate to create the integrated neural pattern are not fully specified.
- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which second-order patterns generate the subjective sense of self remain to be fully elucidated.
