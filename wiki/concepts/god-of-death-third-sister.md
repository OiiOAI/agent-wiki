# The Goddess of Death (Third Sister)

## Summary

The third figure in the choice-of-three-women theme, originally representing the Goddess of Death (Atropos/Moira/Parca), later transformed through wish-fulfilment into the most desirable woman (Goddess of Love).

## Key facts

- The third sister originally represents the Goddess of Death (Atropos, the inexorable one of the three Fates). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4014]
- Cordelia's dumbness, Cinderella's hiding, and lead's plainness all point to the 'dumbness' that in dreams represents death. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4012]
- In the Judgement of Paris, the third goddess (Aphrodite) who wins 'said nothing'—suggesting the original death aspect beneath the love aspect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4012]
- Man overcomes death intellectually by transforming the myth: choice replaces necessity, and the figure of terror becomes the fairest and most desirable woman. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4017]
