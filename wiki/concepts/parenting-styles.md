# Parenting Styles

## Summary

Diana Baumrind's influential typology of parenting styles, expanded by Maccoby and Martin into a two-by-two matrix based on demand level and responsiveness. The four styles are authoritative (high demand, high responsiveness), authoritarian (high demand, low responsiveness), permissive (low demand, high responsiveness), and neglectful (low demand, low responsiveness). Different cultures value different styles.

## Key facts

- Authoritative parenting produces adults who are happy, emotionally and socially mature, fulfilled, independent, and self-reliant. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p211]
- Authoritarian parenting tends to produce adults who may be narrowly successful, obedient, conformist, and not particularly happy, with often poor social skills. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p211]
- Permissive parenting produces adults who are self-indulgent with poor impulse control, low frustration tolerance, and poor social skills. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p211]
- Baumrind's typology was influenced by Theodor Adorno's work on the authoritarian personality and the roots of fascism. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p210]
- Maccoby and Martin expanded Baumrind's trio to four styles by adding neglectful parenting, creating a two-by-two matrix. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p211]

## Inferences

- Inference: Each parenting style usually produces adults with that same approach, suggesting intergenerational transmission of parenting behaviors.
- Inference: Different cultures value different styles, supporting the idea that parenting serves cultural reproduction functions.
