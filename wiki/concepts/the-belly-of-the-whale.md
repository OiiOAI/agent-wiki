# The Belly of the Whale

## Summary

The idea that the passage of the magical threshold is a transit into a sphere of rebirth is symbolized by the worldwide womb image of the belly of the whale. The hero, instead of conquering the power of the threshold, is swallowed into the unknown and appears to have died. This passage represents self-annihilation and rebirth—the hero going inward to be born again.

## Key facts

- The idea that the passage of the magical threshold is a transit into a sphere of rebirth is symbolized in the worldwide womb image of the belly of the whale. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p74]
- The hero, instead of conquering or conciliating the power of the threshold, is swallowed into the unknown, and would appear to have died. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p74]
- The temple interior, the belly of the whale, and the heavenly land beyond, above, and below the confines of the world, are one and the same. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p75]
- The passage of the threshold is a form of self-annihilation; the devotee at the moment of entry into a temple undergoes a metamorphosis, shedding his secular character as a snake its slough. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p75]
- This motif appears in diverse cultures: Eskimo Raven swallowed by whale-cow, Zulu children swallowed by an elephant, Herakles swallowed by a sea monster and cutting his way out. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p74-75]
