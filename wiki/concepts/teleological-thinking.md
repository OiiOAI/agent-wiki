# Teleological Thinking

## Summary

Teleological thinking explains facts of nature as resulting from intelligent design or purpose, such as 'giraffes have long necks so they can eat leaves from tall trees.' This mode of thinking is fully developed between ages four and five and may be a default reasoning style.

## Key facts

- Both adults and children resort to teleological explanations for biological processes, such as that lungs are for breathing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p270]
- Children extend teleological reasoning to natural objects, saying clouds exist to rain and mountains exist so people can hike. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p270]
- Three proposals exist for the origins of teleological thinking: it is innate, derives from understanding man-made objects are designed, or derives from rational action understanding in babies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p270]
