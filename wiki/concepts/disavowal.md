# Disavowal

## Summary

Disavowal (Verleugnung) is the psychological process, distinct from repression, whereby the subject refuses to acknowledge an unwelcome reality while still maintaining the contradictory belief. In fetishism, the boy disavows the perception that women lack a penis; in certain psychoses, patients may disavow the death of a loved one. Freud argues that disavowal necessarily implies a split in the ego, with contradictory attitudes coexisting.

## Key facts

- Disavowal differs from repression because the perception persists, and an energetic action is undertaken to maintain the disavowal rather than the perception being entirely wiped out. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6680]
- Disavowal of a piece of reality by the ego does not necessarily lead to psychosis because a split attitude develops: one current does not recognize the fact while another takes full account of it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6683]
- In fetishism, the boy retains the belief that women have a phallus and also gives it up; a compromise is reached under the dominance of primary processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6680]
- Disavowal differs from repression in that it involves rejecting reality rather than simply forgetting; the child says 'No' to reality in some form while the repressed material remains separate in the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7425]
- In the trauma of castration, the child decides to disavow reality and make himself believe there is no reason for fear, so he may retain the drive satisfaction. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7425]
- Disavowal produces two contrary reactions that both persist as the center point of a splitting of the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7425]
- The fetishist disavows his perception of the female genitals lacking a penis but holds fast to the contrary conviction, while simultaneously recognizing the fact and drawing correct conclusions from it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7358]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ego split produced by disavowal is not peculiar to fetishism but occurs in many other situations where the ego must defend itself against intolerable reality.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact relationship between disavowal and repression as defence mechanisms remains to be fully clarified.
