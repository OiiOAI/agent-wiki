# Decreasing Processing Resources in Aging

## Summary

Aging is associated with reductions in processing resources within the information processing framework. This manifests as reduced working memory capacity and efficiency in performing complex operations, initiating mental operations, and dividing attention. Since most SA processes occur in working memory, such age-related changes are of major concern for SA acquisition.

## Key facts

- Aging is associated with reductions in processing resources, conceptualized as the ability to efficiently process information within working memory. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p293]
- Processing resource deficits are most evident when initiating mental operations such as encoding, consciously examining memory contents, or performing complex operations including division of attention. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p293]
- Most of the active processes for SA are thought to occur in working memory, making age-related processing resource reductions a major concern for SA acquisition. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p293]
- Age differences in performance tend to increase along with task difficulty, such as divided attention tasks with meaningless stimuli. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p298]

## Inferences

- Inference: When tasks do not tap into working memory resources, cognitive functioning remains fairly stable with age, suggesting targeted environmental supports could mitigate some SA deficits.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact nature of processing resources remains debated, with some suggesting direct relationship to working memory capacity and others linking it to mental energy.
