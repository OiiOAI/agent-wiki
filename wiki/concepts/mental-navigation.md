# Mental Navigation

## Summary

Mental navigation is Buzsáki's framework for understanding episodic memory, semantic memory, planning, and imagination as forms of navigation through 'cognitive space' rather than physical space. The brain mechanisms evolved for physical navigation by dead reckoning are essentially the same as those used for navigation in cognitive space. Episodic memory is 'mental travel' in time and space referenced to the self, while semantic memory is explicit representation without temporal or contextual embedding. Both emerged from mechanisms initially introduced for spatial navigation.

## Key facts

- Mental time travel allows us to be aware of the past and future—going backward is episodic recall, while traveling into the imagined future is planning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p124]
- The hippocampus is 'blind' to modality and nature of inputs—it processes messages from the neocortex in the same way regardless of origin and returns its judgment to the source. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p122]
- Hippocampal damage impairs mental time travel—patients cannot mentally travel either to the past or future and use intact logic to invent scenarios (confabulation). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p127]
- Greek orators memorized speeches by imagining walking through rooms of their house with each room containing a particular topic; jazz musicians imagine navigating a landscape of notes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p125]
- Semantic memory represents objects, facts, and events independently of temporal context, similar to how place cells and grid cells explicitly define position coordinates in a map. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p125]

## Inferences

- Inference: Grid cell mechanisms could support periodic encoding in multiple cortical areas for abstract categorization and general knowledge organization beyond spatial navigation.
