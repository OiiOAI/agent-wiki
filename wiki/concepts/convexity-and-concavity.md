# Convexity and Concavity

## Summary

Convexity and concavity are mathematical properties that map directly to antifragility and fragility respectively. A convex response (curving outward like a smile) means that additional inputs bring disproportionately larger benefits — antifragility. A concave response (curving inward like a frown) means additional inputs bring disproportionately larger harms — fragility. This framework refines Seneca's asymmetry into a quantitative tool: for the fragile, shocks bring higher harm as intensity increases; for the antifragile, shocks bring higher benefits as intensity increases.

## Key facts

- Convexity and concavity are the two kinds of nonlinearity, with convex resembling a smile and concave resembling a frown. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p341]
- For the fragile, shocks bring higher harm as their intensity increases (up to a certain level). [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p337]
- For the antifragile, shocks bring more benefits as their intensity increases (up to a point). [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p340]
- The cumulative effect of small shocks is smaller than the single effect of an equivalent single large shock for fragile systems. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p340]
- More is different: doubling exposure to something that causes more than double the harm indicates fragility (negative convexity effect). [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p349]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mathematical reason fragility is nonlinear rather than linear is that if a human experienced linear harm from small impacts (like walking), they would already be dead from cumulative harm. This rules out linear fragility in living systems and leaves extreme rare events as the primary fragility driver.
