# Gambling Task

## Summary

A laboratory task designed to assess decision-making performance in a lifelike setting. Subjects choose cards from four decks (A, B, C, D) to win money. Decks A and B offer high rewards ($100) but also high penalties, while decks C and D offer lower rewards ($50) but lower penalties. Normal subjects gradually learn to prefer decks C and D, while patients with ventromedial prefrontal damage persistently choose the disadvantageous decks A and B.

## Key facts

- In the gambling task, decks A and B pay $100 per card but carry high penalties up to $1,250, while decks C and D pay $50 but have much smaller penalties under $100 on average. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p233]
- Normal subjects develop a hunch that decks A and B are more dangerous and switch preference to decks C and D within the first thirty moves, even though they cannot perform precise calculations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p234]
- Ventromedial prefrontal patients systematically turned more cards in decks A and B, went bankrupt, and persisted in disadvantageous choices despite knowing which decks were bad. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p215]
- Skin conductance responses in normal subjects occurred before selecting cards from bad decks, indicating anticipatory learning of negative outcomes, while frontal patients showed no anticipatory responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p220]

## Inferences

- Inference: Normal subjects appear to develop nonconscious predictions of deck goodness/badness that guide their choices before conscious awareness of the pattern.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the gambling task specifically measures somatic marker dysfunction or broader executive function deficits is still debated.
