# Sexual Symbolism in Dreams

## Summary

Sexual symbolism in dreams involves the use of specific objects, actions, and scenes to represent sexual content. Freud discusses symbols including narrow spaces and closed doors representing sexual intercourse, water representing amniotic fluid or birth, and various other genital symbols. He argues against universal bisexual interpretation of all dreams while acknowledging that many dreams admit homosexual 'over-interpretation.' Numerous dreams satisfy needs other than erotic ones, including dreams of hunger, thirst, and convenience.

## Key facts

- Penetrating into narrow spaces and opening closed doors are among the commonest sexual symbols, often representing attempts at coitus. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p357]
- Freud maintains that not all dreams are to be interpreted bisexually, contrary to Stekel and Adler, and that many dreams satisfy needs other than erotic ones. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p356]
- The assertion that all dreams require a sexual interpretation occurs nowhere in Freud's Interpretation of Dreams. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p356]
- Strikingly innocent dreams may embody crudely erotic wishes, and many seemingly indifferent dreams lead back on analysis to unmistakably sexual wishful impulses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p356]
- The symbols water, ship, getting wet, swimming, and micturition have layered meanings: urine, semen, amniotic fluid, copulation, and pregnancy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p361]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's cautious approach to universal sexual interpretation distinguishes his methodology from some contemporaries who saw sexuality in all dream content.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise boundaries between universal sexual symbols and individually variable symbols remain unclear in the text.
