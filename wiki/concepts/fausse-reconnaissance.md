# Fausse Reconnaissance (Déjà Racontré)

## Summary

Fausse reconnaissance (or 'déjà raconté') is a phenomenon during psychoanalytic treatment where a patient maintains with subjective certainty that they have already recounted a particular memory, when in fact they have not. Freud explains this as the patient having felt an urge to communicate the information and intended to do so, but failed to carry it out; the memory of the former intention now serves as a substitute for actually fulfilling it. This phenomenon parallels the déjà vu experience but manifests in the domain of memory and communication rather than perception.

## Key facts

- Fausse reconnaissance occurs when a patient believes they have already reported something when they have only intended to do so but failed to carry it out. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2233]
- The mechanism involves an impulse toward the parapraxis being sufficient to serve as a substitute for its actual execution. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2233]
