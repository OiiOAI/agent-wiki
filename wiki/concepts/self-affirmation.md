# Self-Affirmation

## Summary

Self-affirmation is a psychological intervention involving the recall of positive personal characteristics or values that has been shown to have powerful performance-enhancing consequences. The practice works by pushing back against negative performance effects brought about by stereotype threat and other forms of self-doubt.

## Key facts

- Self-affirmation practice nearly abolished an otherwise striking gender gap between male and female participants in math and spatial reasoning tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p185]
- Self-affirmation training given to UK students from socioeconomically deprived backgrounds reduced the difference in exam results compared with more affluent children by 62 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p185]
- Black students in the US who performed self-affirmation exercises for around fifteen minutes at repeated intervals reduced racial differences in exam performance by 40 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p185]
- Self-affirmation pushes back against the negative performance effects brought about by various forms of stereotype threat—the tendency to perform badly at tasks where one predicts one will do badly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p185]
