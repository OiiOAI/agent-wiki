# Ideomotor Theory

## Summary

Ideomotor theory, developed by Hermann Lotze and William James, proposes that actions come about because we mentally represent the completed effects of the action—the idea (or mental image) of the completed motor action is what brings the actual movements about. This reverses the commonsense notion of causality, with the representation of the effect causing the action itself. Predictive processing provides a mechanistic account of how this works through prediction-error correction.

## Key facts

- Ideomotor theory proposes that actions come about because we mentally represent the completed effects of the action—the idea of the completed action is what brings the actual action about. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p75]
- The core idea reverses a commonsense notion of causality—instead of the action causing the effect, it is the representation of the effect (the completed action) that causes the action itself to unfold. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p75]
- Predictive processing shows how the 'idea' of a successful action can be the very thing that brings that action about, through cascades of prediction-error correction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p77]
- Predictive processing provides a deep unity of perception and action—action is the brain's way of making its own proprioceptive predictions come true. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p79]
