# Nociplastic pain

## Summary

Nociplastic pain is a third category of pain, added in 2016, defined as pain that arises from abnormal processing of pain signals without clear evidence of tissue damage or discrete pathology involving the somatosensory nerve network system. It represents real pain experiences rooted in anomalous processing rather than structural damage.

## Key facts

- In 2016, a third category was added: 'nociplastic' pain, defined as 'pain that arises from the abnormal processing of pain signals without any clear evidence of tissue damage or discrete pathology involving the somatosensory [nerve network] system.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p187]
- Nociplastic pain is thought to arise when otherwise innocent sensory signals are amplified, or when pain-inhibitory systems are dampened. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p187]
- These effects (amplification and dampening) can occur at many different levels in predictive processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p187]
- Chronic pain seldom falls solely into one of the three categories (nociceptive, neuropathic, or nociplastic); there is a continuum of cases involving complex mixtures of all three. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p187]
- Many physicians stress that nociplastic pain is curable, as this sets up a new high-level expectation that can itself play a powerful role in bringing about positive change. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p189]

## Inferences

- Inference: Nociplastic pain likely involves misplaced predictions and misguided levels of confidence in those predictions, rather than actual damage to bodily systems or nerve networks.
- Inference: Central sensitization, where the pain system maintains hypersensitivity long after it can play a damage-limiting role, represents a common mechanism underlying nociplastic pain.
