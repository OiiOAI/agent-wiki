# Falling in Love and Brain Plasticity

## Summary

Falling in love triggers powerful neuroplastic change. The emotion lowers the threshold at which pleasure centers fire, creating 'globalization' - a state where everything gives pleasure. This dopamine surge consolidates plastic change, allowing new associations to be wired into the brain. Love can make previously unattractive features attractive (Stendhal's 'Beauty Dethroned by Love'). However, love's highs, crashes, cravings, and withdrawal are subjective signs of plastic changes occurring in brain structure.

## Key facts

- When people fall in love, their pleasure centers begin firing more easily, creating an enthusiastic state where everything is experienced as pleasurable. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p122]
- Stendhal described 'Beauty Dethroned by Love,' where a young man Alberic was far more drawn to his less beautiful mistress because she promised him more happiness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p121]
- Falling in love makes even a pockmark attractive because the person has experienced so many emotions in its presence that ugliness becomes beauty through association. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p121]
- Love's 'addictive symptoms' - the highs, crashes, cravings, withdrawal, and fixes - are subjective signs of plastic changes occurring in the structure of our brains. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p124]
- A tolerance can develop in happy lovers as they get used to each other, and dopamine likes novelty; lovers can stimulate dopamine by injecting novelty into their relationship. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p124]
- Recent fMRI scans of lovers looking at photos of their sweethearts show that a part of the brain with great concentrations of dopamine is activated; their brains looked like those of people on cocaine. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p124]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neurochemical similarity between romantic love and cocaine intoxication explains why both can lead to impaired judgment and irrational behavior.
- Inference: Love's ability to create 'globalization' of pleasure explains why people in love often describe their partner in glowing terms and dismiss their flaws.
