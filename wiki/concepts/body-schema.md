# Body Schema

## Summary

A body schema is an internal neural representation of the body's position and configuration in space. Superior parietal cortex neurons integrate information about individual joint positions and limb segment positions relative to the body, creating a body schema critical for selecting behavioral goals and controlling ongoing movement. The body schema can be dynamically reconfigured based on experience, such as when learning to use tools.

## Key facts

- PE and MIP neurons integrate information on joint positions and limb segment positions relative to the body, creating a body schema for movement control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p871]
- VIP neurons develop expanded visual receptive fields to incorporate tools after training, as if the distal end of the tool becomes a functional extension of the hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p875]

## Inferences

- Inference: The body schema appears to be plastic and can be rapidly reconfigured to incorporate novel tools as functional extensions of the body, suggesting flexible multimodal integration in parietal cortex.
