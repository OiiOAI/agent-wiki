# Parallel Processing

## Summary

Parallel processing is the simultaneous analysis of different stimulus features (such as location, form, color, and motion in vision) by distinct anatomical pathways in the brain. This computational strategy enables rapid and efficient processing of complex sensory information.

## Key facts

- Sensory information is processed in parallel pathways in the cerebral cortex, with different pathways analyzing different stimulus features simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p393]
- Feedback pathways from the brain regulate sensory coding mechanisms, allowing top-down influences to modify sensory processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p393]
- Parallel processing occurs when different neurons in a pathway perform similar computations simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p88]
- Parallel processing makes evolutionary sense as a strategy for building a more powerful brain because it increases both speed and reliability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p88]
- The importance of abundant, highly specific parallel connections has been recognized by scientists constructing theoretical models of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p88]
- Neural network models capture the highly recurrent architecture of actual neural circuits and the ability of the brain to function without specific sensory input during thinking, sleep, and generation of endogenous rhythms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p88]
