# Brain ignition

## Summary

Brain ignition refers to the sudden, self-amplifying cascade of neural activity that occurs when information crosses the threshold for conscious perception. This avalanche of activity spreads from local sensory areas to global prefrontal-parietal networks, creating a coordinated brain-wide state. The phenomenon resembles a phase transition—conscious perception switches discontinuously from absent to present.

## Key facts

- Conscious ignition occurs when recurrent processing exceeds a threshold and becomes self-reinforcing, causing neurons that are strongly interconnected to burst into a self-sustained reverberating state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p165]
- Conscious perception resembles a phase transition—perception follows an all-or-none pattern with a clear threshold (~50ms delay in masking experiments) separating seen from unseen trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p166]
- When the threshold for conscious perception is crossed, activation in key regions is multiplied by 12 and an entire set of additional regions in parietal and frontal lobes appears that were absent during unconscious processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p151]
- The ignition is bilateral and symmetrical—even when a stimulus is initially presented to one hemisphere, conscious perception invades both hemispheres. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p169]
- Unconscious activity does not lead to ignition—intracranial recordings show that unconscious stimuli create only transient local activity in ventral visual cortex that dies out before 300ms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p177]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ignition metaphor captures how small differences (e.g., a few milliseconds of additional stimulus exposure) can trigger massive downstream effects, analogous to how a small perturbation can trigger an avalanche.
