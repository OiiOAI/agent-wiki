# Validated Learning

## Summary

Validated Learning is the rigorous method for demonstrating progress when embedded in the extreme uncertainty in which startups grow. It is the process of demonstrating empirically that a team has discovered valuable truths about a startup's present and future business prospects. Unlike post-hoc rationalization, validated learning is backed up by positive improvements in the startup's core metrics collected from real customers. It serves as the principal antidote to 'achieving failure'—successfully executing a plan that leads nowhere.

## Key facts

- Validated learning is not after-the-fact rationalization or a good story designed to hide failure; it is a rigorous method for demonstrating progress when embedded in extreme uncertainty. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p41]
- Startups exist not just to make stuff, make money, or even serve customers—they exist to learn how to build a sustainable business. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p18]
- Validated learning is more concrete, more accurate, and faster than market forecasting or classical business planning. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p41]
- The essential unit of progress for startups is learning; effort not absolutely necessary for learning what customers want can be eliminated. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p51]
- At IMVU, positive changes in metrics became the quantitative validation that learning was real, allowing stakeholders to see genuine progress. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p53]
