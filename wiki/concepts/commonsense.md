# Commonsense

## Summary

Commonsense is the primal power that distinguishes humans from AI—the ability to know when you don't know. It tracks environmental volatility, detecting unknown unknowns by noticing when the brain cannot grab a firm 'why' for what it sees. Effective commonsense requires tuning anxiety: processing past anxieties (updating standard operating procedures) while focusing on near-future anxieties (Now + 1, fixing gaze one action ahead). Warren Buffett mastered past anxiety processing; James Simons mastered near-future anxiety detection. Both became extraordinarily successful by knowing when they didn't know.

## Key facts

- Commonsense is the ability that distinguishes humans from AI, which can ace complex calculations yet fumble decisions obvious to children. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p73]
- Children act smarter than AI because they can perform a simple mental operation: they can know when they don't know. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p73]
- AI doesn't know that it doesn't know, so it fabricates guilelessly when queried about something outside its training data. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p74]
- Human decision-making is low information all the way down—confidence in a strategy comes not from evidence that it will work but from the absence of strong indicators that it can't. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p75]
- Anxiety keeps us appropriately vigilant, looking for potential problems and preparing solutions—but if too strong, it sabotages commonsense by abandoning solid plans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p81]
- The Now + 1 technique fixes gaze one action ahead, allowing the brain to gauge the density of unknown unknowns in your path without becoming overwhelmed by distant possibilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p84]
- Benjamin Graham developed value investing by rejecting algorithmic prediction, instead applying Franklin's commonsense: focus on getting the cheapest price today. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p86]
- James Simons' Renaissance Technologies succeeded by using Now + 1 to detect volatility spikes and react fast, as Simons expressed: 'When you smell smoke, you get the hell out!'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p90]
