# cAMP Pathway

## Summary

The cyclic AMP (cAMP) pathway is the best-characterized second-messenger cascade, initiated when transmitters bind to receptors linked to Gs proteins that activate adenylyl cyclase to produce cAMP from ATP.

## Key facts

- The cAMP pathway was the first second-messenger cascade to be discovered and serves as the prototype for understanding other pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p290]
- A G protein-coupled receptor binding a ligand can sequentially activate multiple G protein macromolecules, and each activated adenylyl cyclase produces many cAMP molecules, providing signal amplification. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p291]
- cAMP-dependent protein kinase (PKA) is a heterotetramer consisting of a dimer of two regulatory (R) subunits and two catalytic (C) subunits; cAMP binding causes R and C subunits to dissociate, freeing C subunits to phosphorylate substrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p291]
