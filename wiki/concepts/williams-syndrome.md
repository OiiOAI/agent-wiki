# Williams Syndrome

## Summary

Williams syndrome is a genetic disorder whose symptoms appear to be the opposite of autism—children with this syndrome have a characteristically elfish facial appearance and a sweet, overly friendly social disposition. They are extremely socially outgoing and can chatter smoothly, but there is comparatively modest propositional content in their speech. Nothing substantive is known about the neural basis of this syndrome.

## Key facts

- Williams syndrome is a genetic disorder whose symptoms appear to be the mirror image of autism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Children with Williams syndrome have characteristically elfish facial features and a sweet social disposition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Individuals with Williams syndrome are extremely friendly and socially outgoing, chattering smoothly, but with modest propositional content in speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Nothing substantive is known about the neural basis of Williams syndrome at the brain level. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]

## Inferences

- Inference: Children with Williams syndrome may have highly responsive social interaction systems that are poorly connected to cognitive analyzers
- Inference: The contrast between Williams syndrome and autism suggests social abilities and cognitive analysis may be neurologically separable

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The genetic and neural mechanisms underlying Williams syndrome are not yet characterized
- Uncertain: The relationship between hypersociability and cognitive limitations in Williams syndrome is not understood
