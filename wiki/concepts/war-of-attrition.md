# War of Attrition

## Summary

The war of attrition is a game-theoretic model for conventional contests in well-armored species where injury is unlikely. Disputes are settled by conventional display until one rival gives up. The ESS is that each individual persists for an unpredictable (but statistically average) duration, with poker faces evolved to prevent revealing give-up intentions.

## Key facts

- In the war of attrition, contests are won by whoever persists longest, with both contestants paying the time cost but only the winner gaining the resource. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p106]
- A fixed bidding strategy is evolutionarily unstable because a mutant willing to persist slightly longer would always win, but if all bid the true value, both would give up simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p106]
- The ESS for war of attrition is that each individual persists for an unpredictable time, averaging the true value of the resource, with opponents unable to anticipate when give-up will occur. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p107]
- Natural selection penalizes any signal (like whisker-flickering) that reveals intention to give up, leading to evolution of the 'poker face'—surrender is sudden and unpredictable. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p107]
- Lying is no more evolutionarily stable than truth-telling in war of attrition; if hackle-raising reliably signaled persistence, evolution of bluffers would be followed by evolution of callers' bluffs, restoring equilibrium. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p108]

## Inferences

- Inference: The poker face principle extends beyond animals to human behavior, explaining the evolution of inscrutability in competitive situations where revealing intentions is costly.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the war of attrition model applies to specific well-armored species in nature, as the model assumes injury is effectively impossible.
