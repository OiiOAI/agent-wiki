# Affordances in Grasping

## Summary

Affordances are the pragmatic opportunities that objects afford to an observer, not their visual properties but how the object can be interacted with. The concept, introduced by psychologist James J. Gibson, describes how the sight of an object triggers automatic selection of properties that allow interaction. Neurons in the anterior intraparietal area encode object affordances and project to premotor area F5 for grasp planning.

## Key facts

- Affordances are the pragmatic opportunities that an object affords an observer, not its visual aspects (shape, mass, color) but how the object can be interacted with. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p473]
- The sight of an object automatically triggers the motor act appropriate for grasping it, with visual-dominant and visual-and-motor neurons in the anterior intraparietal cortex encoding object affordances. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p473]
- An F5 neuron can transform a given affordance into an appropriate potential motor act because of the congruence between its response to the affordance and the motor act it controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p473]
- A cup has multiple affordances: grasping by the handle (for drinking), by the body (for moving), or by the rim. Nonvisual factors including object meaning and individual intent determine which affordance is selected. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p473]
- The ventral visual stream (inferotemporal cortex) provides semantic properties of objects to the anterior intraparietal cortex via reciprocal connections, supporting selection of affordances consistent with standard object use. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p473]

## Inferences

- Inference: The affordance concept suggests that perception and action are not strictly sequential but are tightly coupled: object recognition automatically activates motor programs appropriate for interaction.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The relative contributions of bottom-up sensory processing versus top-down cognitive control in affordance selection remain to be fully characterized.
