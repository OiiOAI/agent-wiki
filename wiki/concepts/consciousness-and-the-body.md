# Consciousness and the Body

## Summary

This chunk argues that consciousness is fundamentally body-related. The brain structures most closely tied to consciousness—the brainstem, hypothalamus, somatosensory cortices, and cingulate—all process body signals. Consciousness begins as a feeling, specifically a feeling of what happens in the organism. The body is both the stage for emotions and the substrate for the sense of self. Even spinal cord transection does not abolish feelings because the vagus nerve, cranial nerves, and chemical signaling via blood provide alternative routes for body-to-brain communication.

## Key facts

- Consciousness feels like a feeling, not a clear image in any externally directed sensory modality—it has the nonverbal signature of body states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p312]
- The structures most closely related to consciousness—proto-self and second-order mapping structures—all process body signals of one sort or another. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p313]
- Spinal cord transection does not abolish emotional feelings because the vagus nerve, cranial nerves, and blood-borne chemical signals provide alternative routes for body-to-brain communication. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p289]
- Higher spinal cord lesions correlate with more impaired feelings because less body input reaches the brain, supporting the body's role in feeling. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Feeling of What Happens_ Body and Emot - Antonio R. Damasio.pdf#p290]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that locked-in syndrome patients do not experience the terror one would expect, despite full consciousness, supports the body loop theory: without the ability to enact emotional responses in the body, the emotional color of experiences is blunted.
