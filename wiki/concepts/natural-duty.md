# Natural Duty

## Summary

Natural duties are moral requirements that apply to individuals regardless of their voluntary acts. The most important natural duty is that to support and to further just institutions. Natural duties differ from obligations (which arise from voluntary acceptance) and provide the fundamental basis for political ties in a constitutional regime.

## Key facts

- The most important natural duty is that to support and to further just institutions. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p303]
- Natural duty binds us irrespective of our voluntary acts. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p303]
- The natural duty of justice is the fundamental requirement for individuals from the standpoint of the theory of justice. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p306]
- The duty of mutual respect requires showing respect to others as moral beings with a sense of justice and a conception of the good. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p306]
- The duty of mutual aid is supported by the great value of knowing that we live in a society where people will come to our assistance in difficult circumstances. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p307]
