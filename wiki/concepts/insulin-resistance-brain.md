# Insulin Resistance and Brain Health

## Summary

Chronically elevated insulin, common in modern high-carbohydrate diets, impairs brain function by interfering with amyloid clearance, promoting glycation, and blocking ketone production. Insulin resistance affects approximately half of the US population and is a major risk factor for cognitive decline and dementia.

## Key facts

- Approximately one in two people in the United States have blood sugar control issues including prediabetes or type 2 diabetes. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p124]
- Insulin resistance can evade routine clinical markers for years or decades before detection while impairing memory. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p124]
- Chronically elevated insulin blocks lipolysis, preventing stored fat from being released for fuel. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p126]
- Insulin-degrading enzyme (IDE) has a stronger preference for degrading insulin than amyloid beta, and the presence of insulin inhibits IDE's breakdown of amyloid. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p131]
- A very-low-carbohydrate diet can halve total insulin secretion and increase insulin sensitivity after just one day. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p128]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text infers that reducing insulin through carbohydrate restriction may be key to slowing brain aging, as chronically elevated insulin speeds up underlying aging processes.
