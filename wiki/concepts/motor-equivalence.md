# Motor Equivalence

## Summary

Motor equivalence refers to the ability of the motor system to achieve the same behavioral goal using different effectors or muscle configurations. For example, writing can be performed with the hand, foot, or even between the teeth with similar results. This phenomenon suggests that purposeful movements are represented abstractly in the brain rather than as specific joint motions or muscle contractions.

## Key facts

- Motor equivalence is the ability of different motor systems to achieve the same behavior, as demonstrated by writing with different body parts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- Writing by the same person using the dominant hand, nondominant hand, hand with immobilized wrist, pen between teeth, or pen attached to the foot produces similarly recognizable handwriting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- Motor equivalence suggests that purposeful movements are represented abstractly in the brain rather than as sets of specific joint motions or muscle contractions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
- Such abstract representations provide flexibility of action that would not be practical with preset motor programs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p727]
