# Reciprocal Altruism in Hominid Evolution

## Summary

Reciprocal altruism refers to relationships where individuals sacrifice reproductive opportunities for others with expectation of similar reciprocity in the future. In hominid evolution, maintaining reciprocally exclusive reproductive and food-sharing relationships required symbolic marking because indexical communication cannot represent promises about future behaviors. This created the essential conditions for marriage as a symbolic institution.

## Key facts

- Critical conditions for reciprocal altruism include: ability to recognize individuals and remember past behaviors, detect cheaters, exert control over cheaters, and sufficient advantage from cooperation outweighing costs of detecting and punishing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p430]
- Sexual or mating displays are incapable of referring to what might be or should be; this information can only be given expression symbolically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p431]
- Establishing exclusive sexual bonds in a social setting requires promises from potential cheaters and support from other group members to prevent violations of conduct patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p431]

## Inferences

- Inference: Marriage as a symbolic institution solved the verification and guarantee problem by making reproductive relationships public and enforceable through the social group.
