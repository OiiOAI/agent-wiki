# Obsessions and Phobias

## Summary

Freud's 1895 paper distinguished obsessions from phobias based on their mechanisms. In true obsessions, the emotional state (which may be doubt, remorse, anger, or anxiety) persists while the associated idea varies and is replaced by a substitute. In phobias, the emotional state is always anxiety. Freud proposed that obsessions involve 'false connection' between an affect and a substitute idea, while phobias in anxiety neurosis lack a psychical mechanism and are linked to accumulated unconsummated sexual excitation.

## Key facts

- Freud distinguished true obsessions from phobias: in obsessions the emotional state varies (doubt, remorse, anger, anxiety), while in phobias it is always anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p962]
- In obsessions, the emotional state is always justified but persists indefinitely while the associated idea is a substitute for the original incompatible one. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p963]
- The mechanism of substitution involves defence (Abwehr) of the ego against the incompatible idea. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p967]
- Phobias were classified as part of anxiety neurosis and distinguished from obsessional neurosis by lacking a repressed idea from which anxiety was separated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p969]
- Freud classified phobias into common phobias (fear of snakes, thunderstorms, darkness) and contingent phobias (agoraphobia, fears related to locomotion). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p968]
