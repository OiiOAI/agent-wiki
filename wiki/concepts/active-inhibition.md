# Active Inhibition

## Summary

Active inhibition is the mental mechanism that creates a barrier between conscious awareness and long-term memory, filtering out irrelevant information to prevent the brain from being overwhelmed. Without this filter, we would be flooded by associations. This mechanism explains why forgetting is necessary for effective thinking.

## Key facts

- Forgetting would not be the loss of a memory, but the erection of a mental barrier between the conscious mind and long-term memory. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p94]
- Without a thorough filter, brains would constantly be flooded by memories, making it impossible to focus. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p94]
- Transferring ideas to external memory allows us to forget them, which paradoxically facilitates long-term learning. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p93]

## Inferences

- Inference: The case of Solomon Shereshevsky, who could remember everything but couldn't think abstractly, demonstrates that systematic forgetting is actually an ability we all possess and need.
