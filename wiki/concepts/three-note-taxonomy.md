# Three-note taxonomy

## Summary

Ahrens distinguishes three clearly separated types of notes that must be kept apart to build a critical mass in the slip-box. Fleeting notes are quick reminders (captured on napkins, phones, or notebooks) that are discarded or processed within a day. Permanent notes contain ideas written in full sentences, stored permanently in the slip-box, self-explanatory even without their original context. Project notes are relevant only to a specific writing project and are stored in a project-specific folder, discarded after the project ends. Confusing these categories is identified as a primary reason for low academic productivity.

## Key facts

- Fleeting notes can be written in any way, are only reminders, and should be discarded within a day or two. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p43]
- Permanent notes are never thrown away, stored in the same format in the same place, and written as if for publication. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p43]
- Project notes are kept in a project-specific folder and can be discarded or archived after the project is finished. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p43]
- Confusing these three categories is identified as 'one of the major reasons for not getting much writing or publishing done.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p43]
- A common mistake is treating all notes as permanent (filling journals indiscriminately), which dilutes good ideas and prevents critical mass from forming. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p43-44]
- Another common mistake is collecting notes only within specific projects, which cuts off promising lines of thought after each project ends. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p44]
- The threshold to write an idea down should be as low as possible, but ideas must be elaborated into permanent notes within a day or two. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p45]
- Underlining sentences or writing comments in book margins counts only as fleeting notes and will become useless unless processed into proper notes. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p44]
