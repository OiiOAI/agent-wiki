# An Evidential Dream

## Summary

A 1913 paper presenting the analysis of a nurse's dream that functioned as an 'evidential' admission of having fallen asleep on duty. The dream involved losing a child and searching for it, crossing water, meeting various figures. The patient interpreted it as the nurse's unconscious admission of guilt. Freud uses this to argue that while day residues (thoughts from waking life) may be warnings, admissions, or reflections, they are not the dream itself—the essential factor is an unconscious wish (typically infantile and repressed) that enables the day residues to press through to consciousness. Dreams remain wish fulfillments even when their manifest content includes admissions or warnings.

## Key facts

- The dream showed the nurse lost sight of the patient (child) she was supposed to watch, representing admission that she had fallen asleep and not told the truth afterward. - Day's residues are affectively cathected thought processes from the dream day that have resisted the general lowering through sleep. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3984]
- Day's residues are only the psychical material for the dream-work, lacking the main essential of a dream, which is an unconscious wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3984]
- The essential factor in dream construction is an unconscious wish, typically infantile and repressed, which enables day residues to press through to consciousness during sleep. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3984]
- A dream is in every case a fulfillment of unconscious wish whatever else it may contain—warning, reflection, admission, or other parts of preconscious waking life. ## Inferences

- Inference: This paper demonstrates that dream interpretation during analysis depends not only on associations but also on circumstances of narration, dreamer's behavior, and remarks made at the same session.
- Inference: The classification of dreams as wish, admission, warning, reflection or adaptation confuses the day residues with the dream-work proper.
