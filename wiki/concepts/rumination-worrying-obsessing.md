# Ruminating, Worrying, and Obsessing

## Summary

Ruminating, worrying, and obsessing are forms of ineffective problem-solving that consume time, energy, and attention without delivering solutions. They are triggered by difficult situations, thoughts, or feelings, and provide four major payoffs: temporary escape from unpleasant feelings, eventual answers to problems, a feeling of working hard, and avoidance of the discomfort of taking action.

## Key facts

- Ruminating focuses on past problems, worrying on future problems, and obsessing may involve past, present, future, or alternative realities. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p202]
- These cognitive processes are 'problem-solving in overdrive' where the mind goes over problems full speed without making progress. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p202]
- The four major payoffs are: escape from unpleasant feelings, getting answers to problems, feeling like we are working hard, and avoiding the discomfort of taking action. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p203]
- Psychologists call these payoffs 'reinforcing consequences' because they strengthen the behavior over time. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p203]
