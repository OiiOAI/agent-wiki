# Justice as Fairness

## Summary

Justice as fairness is Rawls's contractarian theory of justice. The central idea is that principles of justice are those that free and rational persons would accept in an initial position of equality as defining the fundamental terms of their association. The original position corresponds to the state of nature in traditional contract theory but is understood as a purely hypothetical situation characterized to lead to a certain conception of justice. Justice as fairness is distinguished from utilitarianism by taking seriously the plurality and distinctness of persons and by requiring that the basic liberties be taken as settled and not subject to political bargaining.

## Key facts

- Justice as fairness is a conception of justice that generalizes and carries to a higher level of abstraction the familiar theory of the social contract as found in Locke, Rousseau, and Kant. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The guiding idea is that principles of justice for the basic structure of society are the object of the original agreement. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The original position of equality corresponds to the state of nature in traditional theory, but is understood as purely hypothetical. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p22]
- The parties in the initial situation are rational and mutually disinterested, not egoists but not taking an interest in one another's interests. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p24]
- Justice as fairness is a deontological theory: it does not specify the good independently from the right, nor does it interpret the right as maximizing the good. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p37]
- In justice as fairness the concept of right is prior to that of the good, meaning just social institutions define the scope within which individuals must develop their aims. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p38]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rawls distinguishes justice as fairness from traditional contract theory by not requiring actual entry into society but rather hypothetical agreement on moral principles.
- Inference: The term 'fairness' in the name conveys that principles of justice are agreed to in an initial situation that is fair between individuals as moral persons.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which the conclusions of justice as fairness must be revised once relations to animals and nature are considered is left undecided.
