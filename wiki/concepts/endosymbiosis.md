# Endosymbiosis

## Summary

Endosymbiosis is the evolutionary process by which an ancestral archaeal cell engulfed a bacterium, creating the first eukaryotic cells. The engulfed bacterium became mitochondria, providing the host cell with a surplus of ATP energy. This revolutionary event enabled eukaryotic cells to grow larger and more complex, eventually leading to multicellular life and the evolution of neurons.

## Key facts

- Eukaryotic cells have a nucleus with enclosed DNA and membrane-bound organelles including mitochondria. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p70]
- Mitochondria have their own private set of genes and are replicated independently from nuclear genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p70]
- The endosymbiosis hypothesis was proposed in 1905 and greatly expanded by Lynn Margulis in the late 1960s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p71]
- The host cell for endosymbiosis was likely an archaeon, distinct from bacteria but sharing some biochemical similarities with eukaryotes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p71]
- The energy surplus from mitochondria allowed eukaryotic cells to escape being 'lean and mean' and maintain more genes, enabling greater cellular complexity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p73]
- The endosymbiotic theory proposes that an archaeal cell engulfed a bacterial cell, which became the mitochondria of eukaryotes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p79]
- Lynn Margulis was the most vocal proponent of endosymbiotic theory and succeeded in changing understanding of eukaryotic origins. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p81]
- The engulfed bacterium avoided digestion and established a mutually beneficial (symbiotic) relationship with the archaeal host cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p79]
- The bacterial cell lost many of its own genes but retained functions related to energy production, becoming a specialized organelle. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p80]
- Endosymbiosis represents convergence (merging of existing life-forms) rather than simple divergence (accumulation of small changes). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p80]
