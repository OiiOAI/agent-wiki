# Transformations of the Hero

## Summary

The stage where cosmogonic cycle proceeds not by the now-invisible gods but by heroes more or less human in character through whom world destiny is realized. Culture heroes appear on the dawn stage of every legendary past—the serpent kings and minotaurs who carried world-creating power and accomplished the titan-work of establishing civilization's foundations.

## Key facts

- With the progress of the cycle, a period came when work required was no longer proto- or superhuman but specifically human: control of passions, exploration of arts, elaboration of institutions. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p248]
- Fu Hsi governed China with serpent body, human arms, ox head; taught fishing, hunting, and deduced the Eight Diagrams from a supernatural tablet. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p249]
- Shen Nung was miraculously conceived through a dragon's influence; he discovered seventy poisonous plants and their antidotes, invented the plough, and composed a pharmacopoeia still in use. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p249]
- Huang Ti's distinguishing endowment was his power to dream; in sleep he could visit remote regions and consort with immortals. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p250]

## Inferences

- Inference: The transformation from primordial to human heroes mirrors the progressive condensation of consciousness from cosmic to ordinary states
