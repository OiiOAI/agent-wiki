# Synaptic Vesicle

## Summary

Synaptic vesicles are membrane-bound sacs in presynaptic terminals that contain neurotransmitters. Bernard Katz predicted their existence based on his finding that transmitters are released in discrete quanta, and electron microscopy by Sanford Palay and George Palade confirmed this prediction in 1955. Each vesicle contains approximately five thousand molecules of neurotransmitter. When an action potential opens voltage-gated calcium channels, calcium influx triggers vesicle fusion with the presynaptic membrane, releasing neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft.

## Key facts

- Katz called the discrete packets of neurotransmitter 'quanta' and proposed each is packaged in a membrane-bound sac called the synaptic vesicle. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
- Electron microscopy images taken by Palay and Palade in 1955 confirmed the existence of synaptic vesicles, showing they cluster near the presynaptic membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
- Calcium influx causes synaptic vesicles to fuse with the surface membrane of the presynaptic terminal, opening a pore through which neurotransmitters are released. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
