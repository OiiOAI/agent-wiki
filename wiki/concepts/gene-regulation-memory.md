# Gene Regulation in Memory

## Summary

Gene regulation in memory refers to the activation of specific genes required for long-term memory formation. Following learning, signals from synapses travel to the nucleus where regulatory genes are activated by transcription factors like CREB, leading to synthesis of proteins needed for synaptic growth and long-term memory storage.

## Key facts

- Jacob and Monod discovered that genes can be regulated—switched on and off—by other genes through regulatory proteins that bind to promoters. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p268]
- The discovery that gene function can be regulated in response to environmental signals led Kandel to ask how sensory signals from learning activate regulatory genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p270]
- Goelet and Kandel proposed that repeated learning trials send signals to the nucleus telling it to activate regulatory genes encoding proteins that direct formation of new synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p273]
- CREB proteins act as the key regulatory switch that converts short-term to long-term memory by activating effector genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p275]
- This showed that genes are not simply determinants of behavior but are also responsive to environmental stimulation such as learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p287]
