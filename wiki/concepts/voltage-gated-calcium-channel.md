# Voltage-Gated Calcium Channel

## Summary

Voltage-gated calcium channels are multimeric membrane proteins that mediate Ca2+ influx in response to membrane depolarization. In neurons, P/Q-type, N-type, and R-type channels at presynaptic active zones trigger transmitter release, while L-type and T-type channels regulate other functions including gene expression and pacemaker activity. Different channel types have distinct biophysical and pharmacological properties.

## Key facts

- Neurons contain five broad classes of voltage-gated Ca2+ channels: L-type, P/Q-type, N-type, R-type, and T-type, encoded by distinct but closely related genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p376]
- P/Q-type (CaV2.1), N-type (CaV2.2), and R-type (CaV2.3) channels are encoded by the CaV2 gene family and require strong depolarization for activation (high voltage activated). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p376]
- T-type channels (CaV3 family) open in response to small depolarizations around the threshold for action potential generation and are called low voltage activated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p377]
- Fast release of conventional transmitters during synaptic transmission is mediated mainly by P/Q-type and N-type Ca2+ channels concentrated at the active zone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p377]
- Ca2+ channels are blocked by specific toxins: ω-agatoxin (P/Q-type), ω-conotoxin (N-type), and SNX-482 (R-type). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p377]
