# Somatosensory Cortex Organization

## Summary

The somatosensory cortex is organized into functionally specialized columns where information from different types of somatosensory receptors is kept anatomically separate until late stages of processing. The cortex maintains a somatotopic representation of the body surface, with adjacent body regions represented in adjacent cortical areas. Receptive fields of cortical neurons integrate information from neighboring receptors, and neurons become increasingly specialized for complex features at higher processing stages.

## Key facts

- The somatosensory cortex is organized into functionally specialized columns where processing of information from different receptor types is anatomically segregated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p447]
- The arrangement of receptors in the sense organ allows further specialization of function within each sensory system, with topography maintained throughout early somatosensory pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p447]
- Neurons in the cerebral cortex are specialized to integrate and detect specific features of stimuli beyond merely their location, responding preferentially to stimulus properties such as orientation of edges, direction of motion, or tonal sequences.
