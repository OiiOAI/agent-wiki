# Prediction Error Minimisation

## Summary

Prediction error minimisation (PEM) is the process by which the brain maintains accurate perceptual predictions. By adjusting top-down predictions to suppress bottom-up prediction errors, the brain's perceptual best guesses stay connected to their real-world causes. This framework, building on Helmholtz's concept of unconscious inference, treats perception as active hypothesis-testing rather than passive sensory registration.

## Key facts

- Prediction error minimisation involves adjusting top-down predictions to suppress bottom-up prediction errors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p85]
- This process allows the brain's perceptual best guesses to maintain their grip on causes in the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p85]
- The idea builds on Helmholtz's concept of perception as 'unconscious inference' from the 19th century. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p84]
