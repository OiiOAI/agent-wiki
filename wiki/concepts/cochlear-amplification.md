# Cochlear Amplification

## Summary

The cochlea actively amplifies sound energy through mechanisms that enhance sensitivity and frequency selectivity. The basilar membrane displays compressive nonlinearity, accommodating the millionfold variation in sound pressure into only two to three orders of magnitude of vibration amplitude. At low sound levels, amplification raises sensitivity by more than 40 to 50 dB, but amplification diminishes as stimulus strength increases. The cochlea can emit sounds (otoacoustic emissions), demonstrating its active mechanical nature.

## Key facts

- The sensitivity of the cochlea is too great and auditory frequency selectivity too sharp to result solely from passive mechanical properties. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p663]
- The basilar membrane displays compressive nonlinearity that accommodates the millionfold variation of sound pressure into only two to three orders of magnitude of vibration amplitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p663]
- Amplification lowers the threshold of hearing by more than 40 to 50 dB SPL at the characteristic frequency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p663]
- When a normal human ear is stimulated with a click, it emits measurable pulses of sound within a few milliseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p663]
- Spontaneous otoacoustic emissions occur in most normal human ears. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p664]
- The sensitivity of the basilar membrane at low intensities is more than 100-fold greater than predicted by passive cochlear models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p722]
- Evoked otoacoustic emissions are sound pulses emitted by the cochlea following acoustic stimulation, demonstrating active mechanical energy release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p723]
- Spontaneous otoacoustic emissions occur in at least 70% of normal human ears, continuously emitting pure tones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p723]
- Outer hair cells undergo electromotility, shortening when depolarized and elongating when hyperpolarized, at frequencies exceeding 80 kHz. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p724]
- The protein prestin in the outer hair cell membrane undergoes conformational changes in response to membrane potential, providing the molecular basis for electromotility. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p725]
- Hair bundles can make spontaneous movements and perform mechanical work against external probes, contributing to the active process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p725]
- The cochlear active process exhibits behavior consistent with a Hopf bifurcation, explaining amplification, frequency selectivity, nonlinear sensitivity, and potential for spontaneous instability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p725]

## Inferences

- Inference: Active hair-bundle motility may function as a tuner and preamplifier while electromotility serves as a power amplifier, with the former operating at lower frequencies and being superseded by the latter at the highest audible frequencies.
