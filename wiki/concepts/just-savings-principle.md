# Just Savings Principle

## Summary

The just savings principle addresses justice between generations, specifying how much each generation should save for future generations. From the original position, parties do not know which generation they belong to and must choose a principle assigning appropriate rates of accumulation to each level of advance. The rate changes depending on the state of society—when people are poor, a lower rate is required; in a wealthier society, greater savings may be expected. Once just institutions are firmly established, net accumulation falls to zero. This principle constrains the difference principle across generations.

## Key facts

- The parties in the original position do not know to which generation they belong or the stage of civilization of their society—the veil of ignorance is complete in these respects. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p263]
- The parties must choose a just savings principle that assigns an appropriate rate of accumulation to each level of advance, with the rate changing depending upon the state of society. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p263]
- Once just institutions are firmly established, the net accumulation required falls to zero—at this point a society meets its duty of justice by maintaining just institutions and preserving their material base. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p263]
- Capital includes not only factories and machines but also knowledge and culture, techniques and skills that make possible just institutions and the fair value of liberty. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p264]
- The just savings principle is defined from the standpoint of the least advantaged in each generation, constraining the application of the difference principle between generations. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p267]
