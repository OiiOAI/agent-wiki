# Membrane Potential

## Summary

Membrane potential is the electrical potential difference across the cell membrane, created by the separation of charge. The resting membrane potential in neurons is primarily maintained by the selective permeability of the membrane to potassium ions through leak channels and by the sodium-potassium pump.

## Key facts

- The resting membrane potential results from the separation of charge across the cell membrane, maintained by ion channels and ion pumps. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
- Resting channels in glial cells are permeable only to potassium, while resting nerve cells have open channels permeable to several ion species including sodium, potassium, and chloride. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
- The electrochemical gradients of sodium, potassium, and calcium are established by active transport of the ions via the sodium-potassium pump and other transporters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
