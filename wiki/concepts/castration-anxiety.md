# Castration Anxiety

## Summary

Castration anxiety (Kastrationsangst) is the fear that one's penis will be taken away, which Freud considered to be always present in nearly all human beings. It arises when the child discovers anatomical differences between the sexes and concludes that women have been castrated.

## Key facts

- Castration anxiety arises when the boy observes that the penis, which he had attributed to all people, is missing in some, leading him to conclude that women have been castrated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7026]
- Freud stated that castration anxiety is always present in nearly all human beings and is something that scarcely anyone is spared. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7026]
- The first consequence of castration anxiety is an enormous intensification of the conflicts inherent in the Oedipus complex. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7026]
- Castration anxiety leads the boy to wish his father dead, and because the father does not die, the wish to kill him is stirred. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7026]
- The boy's passive attitude inclines him to want to accept castration—to become female, and in this way escape castration anxiety once and for all. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7027]

## Inferences

- Inference: Castration anxiety serves as a primary mechanism driving the resolution of the Oedipus complex and the subsequent formation of the superego.
