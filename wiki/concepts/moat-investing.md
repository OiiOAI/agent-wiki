# Moat

## Summary

Moat is Munger and Buffett's term for a company's competitive advantage—the virtual barrier that protects a business from incursions by competitors. Superior companies have deep moats that are continuously widened to provide enduring protection against competitive destruction forces. Buffett emphasizes widening the moat every year as the primary criterion for evaluating a great business.

## Key facts

- Buffett states: 'We think in terms of that moat and the ability to keep its width and its impossibility of being crossed as the primary criterion of a great business.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p65]
- Buffett tells managers to widen the moat every year, even if profits don't increase immediately. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p65]
- Munger and Buffett focus intensely on 'competitive destruction' forces that siege most companies over multiple generations. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p67]
- Munger assesses competitive advantage across products, markets, trademarks, employees, distribution channels, societal trends, and the durability of that advantage. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p67]

## Inferences

- Inference: The moat concept emphasizes that sustainable competitive advantages, rather than temporary profitability, should be the primary focus of long-term investment analysis.
