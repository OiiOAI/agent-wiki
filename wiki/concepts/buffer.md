# Buffer

## Summary

A buffer is extra space—time, resources, or capacity—built in to absorb unexpected events and reduce friction in execution. Essentialists plan for the unpredictable by adding 50 percent to time estimates, practicing extreme preparation, and conducting scenario planning rather than assuming best-case scenarios.

- Norway invested much of its oil windfall in an endowment that grew to $720 billion, the world's largest sovereign wealth fund. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p166]
- The planning fallacy refers to people's tendency to underestimate how long a task will take, even when they have done the task before. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p168]
- In one study, students who estimated how long it would take to complete their senior thesis averaged 27.4 days for best-case and 48.6 days for worst-case, but actual average time was 55.5 days. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p168]
- Roald Amundsen prepared for his South Pole expedition by storing three tons of food for seventeen men, planting twenty markers for return, and bringing four thermometers, while Robert Falcon Scott did the bare minimum.
