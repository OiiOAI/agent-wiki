# Letting Go

## Summary

Letting go, in the context of emotional agility, means holding thoughts and emotions lightly—remaining receptive and open without being hooked by old stories or prejudging new experiences. It involves distancing oneself from interpretations that have been woven into situations and choosing to assume generous intentions rather than harboring injured pride or resentment. The practice allows individuals to end psychological tug-of-wars and reclaim emotional energy for what truly matters.

## Key facts

- Letting go involves maintaining a receptive, open, broader view while holding thoughts and emotions lightly. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p91]
- When hooked by small annoyances, we stop seeing people as fully rounded beings existing beyond our perception of them. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p91]
- Letting go can mean accepting that something is simply part of a person one loves, without a sense of injured pride or resentment. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p92]
- When we let go of one thing, we are left with everything else; clinging to emotional driftwood prevents feeling part of a larger dynamic system. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p93]

## Inferences

- Inference: Letting go is distinct from resignation—it is an active choice to reinterpret situations with generosity rather than passivity.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text does not specify whether letting go requires complete emotional resolution or can be a temporary state.
