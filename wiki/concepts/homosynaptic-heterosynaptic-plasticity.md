# Homosynaptic vs. Heterosynaptic Plasticity

## Summary

Homosynaptic plasticity refers to changes in synaptic strength that occur at synapses directly activated by the learning stimulus, while heterosynaptic plasticity involves changes at synapses not directly activated, mediated by modulatory circuits. This distinction explains how different forms of learning, from habituation to classical conditioning, can modify neural circuits.

## Key facts

- Homosynaptic changes occur in habituation when the sensory and motor neurons firing repeatedly in response to repeated sensory stimuli undergo direct modification. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p216]
- Heterosynaptic changes occur in sensitization when modulatory neurons activated by the tail shock modify synapses between sensory and motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p216]
- Classical conditioning recruits both homosynaptic and heterosynaptic changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p217]
- The modulatory circuit is Lockean in nature, serving as a 'teacher' that fine-tunes behavior in response to learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p235]
