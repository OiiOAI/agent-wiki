# Homeostasis

## Summary

Homeostasis is the process by which the body maintains physiological parameters within a narrow range essential for survival. It involves negative feedback loops that detect deviations from normal values and activate corrective responses through behavioral, autonomic, and neuroendocrine systems.

## Key facts

- Claude Bernard first drew attention to the stability of the body's internal environment, stating that 'the internal environment is a necessary condition for a free life.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1058]
- Walter B. Cannon introduced the concept of homeostasis to describe mechanisms that maintain constancy of composition of bodily fluids, body temperature, blood pressure, and other physiological variables. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1058]
- Homeostatic mechanisms greatly extend the range of habitats, activities, and traumas that can be survived. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1058]
- Modern understanding favors a distributed settling point model where apparent set points emerge from multiple feedback-informed afferent/efferent loops rather than single set points. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1059]
