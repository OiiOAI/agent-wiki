# Homeostasis

## Summary

Homeostasis is the process by which the body maintains physiological parameters within a narrow range essential for survival. It involves negative feedback loops that detect deviations from normal values and activate corrective responses through behavioral, autonomic, and neuroendocrine systems.

## Key facts

- Claude Bernard first drew attention to the stability of the body's internal environment, stating that 'the internal environment is a necessary condition for a free life.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1058]
- Walter B. Cannon introduced the concept of homeostasis to describe mechanisms that maintain constancy of composition of bodily fluids, body temperature, blood pressure, and other physiological variables. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1058]
- Homeostatic mechanisms greatly extend the range of habitats, activities, and traumas that can be survived. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1058]
- Modern understanding favors a distributed settling point model where apparent set points emerge from multiple feedback-informed afferent/efferent loops rather than single set points. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1059]
- Claude Bernard introduced the concept that the internal environment (le milieu intérieur) is a necessary condition for a free life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1108]
- Walter Cannon introduced the concept of homeostasis in the 1930s to describe mechanisms that maintain constancy of composition of bodily fluids, body temperature, blood pressure, and other physiological variables. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1108]
- During exercise, healthy people can increase cardiac output four- to five-fold while maintaining blood pressure within a much narrower range through homeostatic compensatory mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1108]
- The hypothalamus contributes to homeostasis by acting on three major systems: the autonomic motor system, the endocrine system, and a neural system concerned with motivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1107]
- Homeostasis is the active maintenance of a relatively constant internal environment; constancy of the internal environment is the basis of the freedom of action organisms enjoy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1147]
- Regulation is achieved through interlinked control systems with both physiological and behavioral outputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1148]
- Key features of homeostatic control systems include motivational states such as hunger and thirst that arise as responses to internal stimuli and external incentive stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1148]
