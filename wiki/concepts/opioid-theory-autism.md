# Opioid Theory of Autism

## Summary

The opioid theory of autism proposes that dysregulation of brain opioid systems may contribute to the social and communicative deficits observed in autism spectrum disorders. This theory is supported by evidence that opioid receptor antagonists like naltrexone have shown therapeutic effects in some children with autism.

## Key facts

- A neurochemical theory of autism suggests involvement of brain opioids in disrupted social intent and language development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p212]
- Low-dose naltrexone has shown effects on plasma chemistries and clinical symptoms in autism in double-blind placebo-controlled studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p212]
- The opioid excess hypothesis of autism was investigated through double-blind studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p213]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact nature of opioid dysregulation in autism remains to be fully established, and naltrexone effects are variable across individuals.
