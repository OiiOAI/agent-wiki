# Dementia Praecox

## Summary

Dementia praecox (now called schizophrenia) is a narcissistic neurosis first described by Karl Abraham as characterized by a lack of libidinal cathexis of objects. The libido withdrawn from objects returns to the ego, forming the basis of megalomania. Freud compares the process to repression but views it as repression's counterpart. The clinical picture includes both symptoms from the forcing away of libido from objects and restitution attempts where the libido seeks objects again, snatching only 'shadows'—word presentations rather than genuine object relations. This disorder is inaccessible to psychoanalytic treatment because patients lack the capacity for transference.

## Key facts

- Karl Abraham (1908) pronounced the main characteristic of dementia praecox to be that the libidinal cathexis of objects is lacking. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
- In dementia praecox, the libido turned away from objects is turned back onto the ego and this reflexive turning back is the source of the megalomania. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
- Megalomania in dementia praecox is in every way comparable to the familiar sexual overvaluation of the object in normal erotic life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
- In dementia praecox, it seems as though the libido, in its efforts to reach objects, snatches hold of something but only 'their shadows'—the word presentations belonging to them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5177]
- The weak spot in the libidinal development of dementia praecox patients lies in a fixation at the stage of primitive narcissism to which dementia praecox returns in its final outcome.
