# Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT)

## Summary

Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) is an evidence-based psychotherapy developed by Marsha Linehan in the 1990s, originally designed to help individuals with serious emotional regulation issues and suicidal behavior. DBT consists of four pillars—mindfulness, emotional regulation, distress tolerance, and interpersonal effectiveness—united by the overarching theme of mindfulness.

## Key facts

- DBT was developed in the 1990s by Marsha Linehan based on the principles of cognitive behavioral therapy. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- DBT was developed to help individuals with serious issues including inability to regulate emotions and propensity to harm themselves or attempt suicide. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- Clinical trials have found DBT to be effective in helping suicidal and self-harming patients stop their dangerous behavior. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- DBT consists of four pillars joined by one overarching theme: mindfulness. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- The four pillars of DBT are: emotional regulation, distress tolerance, interpersonal effectiveness, and self-management. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- DBT is skills-based rather than just theoretical, requiring daily practice through workbooks and exercises with a therapist. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]
- DBT practice aims to break the chain reaction of negative stimulus → negative emotion → negative thought → negative action. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p408]

## Inferences

- Inference: DBT techniques are adaptable and flexible enough to help people with less dramatic emotional health issues, not just those with borderline personality disorder
