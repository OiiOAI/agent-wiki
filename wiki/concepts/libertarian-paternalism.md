# Libertarian Paternalism

## Summary

Libertarian paternalism, introduced by Thaler and Sunstein in their book Nudge, holds that institutions may 'nudge' people toward better choices without restricting their freedom, by designing choice architecture (defaults, framing, options) to align short-term decisions with long-term interests. Examples include auto-enrollment in retirement savings and the 2013 fuel economy sticker showing gallons-per-mile information.

## Key facts

- Libertarian paternalism allows institutions to steer people toward better decisions without limiting freedom, as long as opting out is easy—the key is that the default matters because deviating from normal requires deliberate action. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p535]
- Save More Tomorrow (designed by Thaler and Benartzi, 2003) automatically increases employee retirement contributions with each pay raise, avoiding loss aversion by framing changes as deferred gains rather than immediate losses. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p536]
- Under President Obama, Cass Sunstein as OIRA administrator mandated fuel economy labels that included gallons-per-mile information alongside MPG, because the MPG framing leads to the 'MPG Illusion' and poor purchase decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p480]
- Libertarian paternalism has attracted support across the political spectrum, from conservative groups to the Obama administration, and the UK's Behavioural Insights Team (the 'Nudge Unit') applies behavioral science to government policy. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p537]
- Extreme belief in human rationality provides intellectual support for libertarian policy: if people are rational, paternalism is unnecessary and immoral. Behavioral economics challenges this by showing that System 1 errors can be systematically exploited. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p534]

## Inferences

- Inference: Libertarian paternalism represents a middle ground between the Chicago School view (humans are rational, government should not interfere) and more directive approaches, accepting that humans make systematic errors while maintaining respect for freedom.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the 'Nudge' approach can address major policy challenges or only marginal ones, and whether its appeal across political ideologies will survive contentious applications.
