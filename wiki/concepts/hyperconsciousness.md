# Hyperconsciousness

## Summary

Hyperconsciousness is a core phenomenological disturbance identified by Louis Sass in schizophrenia and modernist culture, wherein elements of self and experience that normally remain intuitive and unconscious become the objects of detached, alienating attention. This creates multiple levels of awareness—of awareness itself—resulting in paralysis where even everyday 'automatic' actions become problematic.

## Key facts

- Hyperconsciousness involves elements of experience that normally remain intuitive becoming objects of detached, alienating attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p540]
- Multiple levels of consciousness multiply, so that there is an awareness of one's own awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p540]
- Patients report: 'I am not sure of my own movements anymore', 'People just do things, but I have to watch first to see how you do things'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p541]
- Chris Frith identified the core abnormality in schizophrenia as 'an awareness of automatic processes which are normally carried out below the level of consciousness'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p541]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hyperconsciousness parallels the self-conscious, staring mode of attention characteristic of left-hemisphere processing
- Inference: This state is mimicked by modernist art, which Susan Sontag noted 'engenders a stare' rather than inviting engagement
