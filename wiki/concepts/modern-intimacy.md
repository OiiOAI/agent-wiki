# Modern Intimacy

## Summary

Modern intimacy has been redefined as primarily verbal and discursive, emphasizing self-disclosure and emotional communication. This narrow conception, Perel argues, disadvantages those who express connection nonverbally (often men) and denies the expressive capacity of the body, particularly for women's sexuality.

## Key facts

- Modern intimacy has been narrowed to a discursive process involving self-disclosure, verbal sharing of feelings, and being known—a significant departure from earlier forms of connection. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p49]
- The hegemony of spoken intimacy creates a female bias that leaves many men at a disadvantage, since masculine identity is not built around expressing feelings verbally. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p49]
- When disclosure becomes obligatory and personal boundaries are no longer respected, fusion replaces intimacy and possession co-opts love—'the kiss of death for sex.'. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p52]
- Perel proposes 'bilingual intimacy'—honoring both verbal and nonverbal languages of connection, including physical touch, shared activities, and gestures of care. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p56]

## Inferences

- Inference: The rise of verbal intimacy as the primary mode of connection correlates with women's economic independence, which changed expectations for marriage from pragmatic partnership to emotional fulfillment.
