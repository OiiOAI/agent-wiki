# Individual Selection

## Summary

Individual selection is one of three foundational concepts for understanding the evolution of behavior, alongside kin selection and reciprocal altruism. It posits that organisms behave to maximize the number of copies of their genes passed into the next generation through their own reproduction. This concept replaced the earlier, incorrect 'group selection' idea that animals behave 'for the good of the species.'

## Key facts

- Individual selection rests on the principle that evolution is about reproduction, passing on copies of genes, rather than merely survival of the fittest. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p341]
- Individual selection explains competitive infanticide in species where new males takeover groups and kill infants to bring females back into estrus faster. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p347]
- Competitive infanticide has been documented in 119 species including lions, hippos, and chimps. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p349]
- Mountain gorillas practice competitive infanticide, which is logical for an individual maximizing copies of his genes but pushes the species toward extinction. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p349]
