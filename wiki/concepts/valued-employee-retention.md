# Valued Employee Retention

## Summary

How managers should respond when a valued subordinate decides to quit. The initial reaction is crucial—managers must immediately stop what they are doing, let the employee talk without arguing, ask questions to uncover real issues, and then pursue every avenue to retain the employee even if it means transferring to another department.

## Key facts

- When a valued employee announces they are quitting, the manager's initial reaction is absolutely crucial; if they try to postpone the conversation, they confirm the employee's feeling that they are not important. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1939-L1942]
- The manager should let the employee talk without arguing, because after prepared points are delivered, the real issues may come out. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1947-L1950]
- After the initial conversation, the manager should go to their supervisor for help and make it the supervisor's problem to participate in the solution. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1957-L1960]
- The manager must vigorously pursue every avenue to keep a valued employee with the company, even if it means transferring them to another department. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1963-L1965]
- Other superior performers will track what happens to a valued employee, so their morale and commitment to the company hinge on the outcome. [raw/books/business/High_Output_Management.epub#L1972-L1974]
