# Mechanoelectrical Transduction

## Summary

Mechanoelectrical transduction is the process by which hair cells convert mechanical forces into electrical signals. Hair bundles detect mechanical deflection through tip links that mechanically gate ion channels. Deflection toward the tall edge of the bundle opens transduction channels, allowing cation influx and depolarization. This process is extraordinarily sensitive, fast, and frequency-tuned, with adaptation mechanisms that reset the operating point.

## Key facts

- Deflection of a hair bundle toward its tall edge opens transduction channels and depolarizes the cell; deflection toward the short edge closes channels and hyperpolarizes the cell. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p715]
- Each hair cell possesses only a few hundred transduction channels, approximately two per stereocilium. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p716]
- The transduction channels are relatively nonselective cation channels with a conductance of approximately 100 pS and a pore diameter of about 1.3 nm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p716]
- Tip links, composed of cadherin-23 and protocadherin-15, connect adjacent stereocilia and serve as gating springs that mechanically open and close transduction channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p717]
- Transduction channels are located near the tips of stereocilia, where Ca2+ influx is detected by fluorescent indicators during bundle deflection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p717]
- Hair cell transduction operates with a time constant of approximately 80 microseconds at room temperature, allowing detection of high-frequency sounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p718]
- Adaptation in hair cells shifts the sensitivity range in response to sustained stimulation, maintaining sensitivity to transient inputs while rejecting static stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p719]
- Myosin-1c molecules in hair bundles maintain tension on tip links and mediate adaptation by repositioning the upper insertion of the link. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p720]
