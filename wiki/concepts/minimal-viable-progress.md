# Minimal Viable Progress

## Summary

Minimal viable progress is the practice of asking what the smallest amount of progress would be that is useful and valuable toward an essential task. Borrowed from Silicon Valley's concept of minimal viable products, this approach emphasizes starting early, making small advances, and building momentum through celebrating incremental wins rather than attempting grand gestures that lead to abandonment.

## Key facts

- Research by Teresa Amabile and Steven Kramer found that 'everyday progress—even a small win' can make a significant difference in how people feel and perform. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p181]
- Frederick Herzberg identified achievement and recognition for achievement as the two primary internal motivators for people. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p180]
- John Lasseter of Pixar said: 'We don't actually finish our films, we release them'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p185]
- Henry B. Eyring wrote: 'My experience has taught me this about how people and organizations improve: the best place to look is for small changes we could make in the things we do often'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p182]

## Inferences

- Inference: Small wins create psychological momentum—the satisfaction of completing one small task increases motivation for the next, leading to cumulative progress that appears effortless.
