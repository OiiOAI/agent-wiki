# Brain Lateralization

## Summary

The phenomenon of functional specialization between the two brain hemispheres, which is more pronounced in humans than in any other species. The corpus callosum enables integration between hemispheres while allowing each to develop specialized functions, potentially as an adaptive response to increased cortical space demands.

## Key facts

- Nowhere else in the animal kingdom is there such rampant specialization of function as in the human brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p38]
- The left hemisphere is the 'smart half' of the brain - it speaks, thinks, and generates hypotheses, while the right brain has superior visual perception skills. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p21]
- The corpus callosum may have been the 'great enabler' for establishing the human condition by allowing one hemisphere to develop new functions while the other maintained existing ones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p38]
- Humans show a marked decrease in the rate of growth of the corpus callosum compared with intrahemispheric white matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p39]

## Inferences

- Inference: As the brain becomes more lateralized, there is likely an increase in local intrahemispheric circuitry and a reduction in interhemispheric circuitry
