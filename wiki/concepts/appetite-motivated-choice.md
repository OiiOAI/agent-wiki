# Appetite as Motivated Choice

## Summary

Appetite and food intake represent motivated choices influenced by both homeostatic signals from the body and environmental cues. The brain regulates energy intake through hypothalamic integration of peripheral signals including insulin, leptin, ghrelin, GLP-1, and peptide YY. These hormones modulate activity in an appetitive network (hippocampus, amygdala, insula, striatum, orbitofrontal cortex) that controls food-seeking behavior. Obesity is associated with reduced self-control and increased impulsivity, reflecting dysfunction in lateral prefrontal cortex mechanisms that normally constrain food approach behaviors.

## Key facts

- The brain controls energy homeostasis by adapting behavior to guarantee fuel supply for tissue function and long-term energy storage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p377]
- Peripheral signals conveying energy balance include circulating glucose and lipids, vagal nerve information, and peptide hormones (insulin, leptin, ghrelin, GLP-1, PYY). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p377]
- The hypothalamus is the main portal for energy balance information via neurons in the arcuate nucleus that integrate signals from leptin, insulin, ghrelin, and gut satiety signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p378]
- The appetitive network consists of hippocampus, amygdala, insula, striatum, and orbitofrontal cortex, receiving dopaminergic input and implicated in emotion and reward processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p378]
- Increased fMRI activation in appetitive network structures while viewing food cues correlates with preference for highly palatable foods and increased caloric intake. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p378]
- Obese individuals show increased activation in the hippocampus in response to visual and olfactory food cues compared to lean controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p399]
- The amygdala response to food cues reflects incentive value and is modulated by outcome devaluation, hunger, and stress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p399]
- Relative insensitivity of the amygdala to satiation in response to food is predictive of weight gain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p399]
- The orbitofrontal cortex tracks current reward value of food cues and response is modulated by hunger; activity is higher for high-calorie foods and predicts weight gain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p400]
- Striatal activity in response to food cues reflects incentive salience and predicts ad lib food consumption and weight gain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p400]
- Obesity is marked by inability to downregulate OFC response to high-calorie food cues despite satiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p400]
- The ventromedial prefrontal cortex consistently tracks subjective value of stimuli including foods and connects with appetitive network structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p401]
- Self-control in dietary behavior is associated with lateral prefrontal cortex activity that can modulate VMPFC value signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p401]
- Body mass index correlates positively with impulsivity and negatively with executive function and self-control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p402]
- Fasting plasma insulin levels correlate with increased BOLD response to high-calorie food images in hippocampus and taste cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p402]
- Leptin administration reduces ventral striatal activation to food cues in patients with leptin deficiency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p403]
- Ghrelin administration increases BOLD responses to food cues in appetitive regions including amygdala, OFC, and dopaminergic midbrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p403]
- GLP-1 agonist administration attenuates BOLD signal response to food pictures across the appetitive network, especially in the insula. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p404]

## Inferences

- Inference: The convergence of homeostatic signals and cognitive control mechanisms suggests that obesity reflects a failure of top-down regulation over bottom-up food motivation.
- Inference: The modulation of appetitive brain regions by hormones like insulin, leptin, and ghrelin indicates that metabolic state directly influences decision-making about food.
