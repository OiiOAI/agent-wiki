# Extinction

## Summary

Extinction is a form of learning in which the conditioned stimulus (CS) is presented repeatedly without the unconditioned stimulus (US), resulting in weakened conditioned responses. In threat conditioning, extinction creates a new 'CS–no US' association that competes with and suppresses the original CS-US memory. Extinction depends on interactions between the medial prefrontal cortex (particularly the infralimbic region) and the amygdala. Unlike memory erasure, extinction does not eliminate the original memory but instead inhibits its expression.

## Key facts

- Extinction is strongly context-dependent; extinction learned in one context does not generalize well to other contexts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p401]
- Effects of extinction can be reversed through spontaneous recovery, renewal, reinstatement, or stress-induced reversal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p401]
- Extinction requires protein synthesis in both the infralimbic cortex and the amygdala for persistent long-term memory effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p400]
