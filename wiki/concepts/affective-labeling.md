# Affective Labeling

## Summary

Affective labeling is the research term for the practice of using words to describe emotional experiences. Matthew Lieberman and colleagues at UCLA conducted experiments demonstrating that affective labeling moderates distressing emotional experiences. In studies with spider-phobic participants, those who used emotion words during exposure were able to approach the spider more closely, and greater use of words like 'anxiety' and 'fear' was associated with reductions in those emotions. Brain imaging showed that affective labeling leads to lower amygdala activation and higher activation in brain regions supporting emotion regulation. Lieberman called this 'incidental emotion regulation' because subjects were not aware it was reducing unpleasantness.
