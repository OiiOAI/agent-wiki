# Shallow Work Budget

## Summary

A shallow work budget is a strategy where you ask what percentage of your time should be spent on shallow work and settle on a specific answer, then try to stick to this budget. For most non-entry-level knowledge workers, the answer typically falls in the 30 to 50 percent range.

## Key facts

- For most people in most non-entry-level knowledge work jobs, the answer to what percentage of time should be spent on shallow work falls in the 30 to 50 percent range. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p137]
- Fifty percent is a natural upper limit due to psychological distaste for spending majority of time on unskilled tasks, while 30 percent is the lower limit to avoid becoming a knowledge work hermit who never responds to emails. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p137]
- Obeying the shallow work budget requires saying no to projects infused with shallowness and more aggressively reducing shallowness in existing projects. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p137]
- A conversation with your boss about shallow work budget establishes implicit workplace support for turning down obligations and restructuring projects. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p138]
- If your boss replies that you should spend as much shallow work as needed to promptly do whatever is needed at the moment, this indicates the job doesn't support deep work. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p139]

## Inferences

- Inference: A job that doesn't support deep work cannot help you succeed in the current information economy.
