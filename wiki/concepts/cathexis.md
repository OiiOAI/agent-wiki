# Cathexis (Besetzung)

## Summary

A term denoting the investment of psychic energy in an idea or group of ideas. Freud used it to explain how an idea whose affect is unresolved involves a certain amount of associative inaccessibility and incompatibility with new cathexes. This appears to be the first published appearance of the term 'cathexis' in the special sense in which Freud employed it as a fundamental concept in his psychological theory.

## Key facts

- Freud hypothesized that the cause of hysterical paralyses lay in the inaccessibility to fresh associations of a group of ideas connected with a body part, which depended on the idea of that part being involved in a trauma-loaded recollection with unresolved affect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p619]
- He stated that a cathexis of an idea whose affect is unresolved always involves a certain amount of associative inaccessibility and incompatibility with new cathexes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p619]
- Freud noted that this appears to be the first published appearance of the term 'cathexis' in the special sense denoting one of the most fundamental concepts in his psychological theory.
