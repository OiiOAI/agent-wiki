# Childhood Adversity and Adult Outcomes

## Summary

Childhood adversity (abuse, neglect, poverty, witnessing violence) converges in producing similar adult problems: depression, anxiety, substance abuse, impaired cognitive capabilities (particularly frontal cortical function), impaired impulse control and emotion regulation, antisocial behavior including violence, and relationships that replicate childhood adversities. The biological mechanisms involve elevated glucocorticoids damaging the hippocampus and frontal cortex while enlarging and sensitizing the amygdala, plus damage to the dopamine reward system.

## Key facts

- Childhood adversity increases odds of adult depression, anxiety, substance abuse, impaired cognition, impaired impulse control, antisocial behavior, and replicating abusive relationships. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p201]
- Early life stress permanently blunts the ability of the brain to regulate glucocorticoid secretion. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p202]
- By age five, lower socioeconomic status predicts higher basal glucocorticoid levels, thinner frontal cortex, lower frontal metabolism, and poorer frontal function. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p202]
- Childhood adversity accelerates amygdaloid maturation in a way where the amygdala develops the ability to inhibit the frontal cortex rather than vice versa. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p203]
- Childhood adversity increases adult vulnerability to drug addiction through effects on developing dopamine systems, elevated glucocorticoids increasing drug craving, and poorly developed frontal cortex. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p203]
- The more categories of adversities a child suffers, the dimmer their chances of a happy functional adulthood. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p207]
- Witnessing gun violence doubles a child's likelihood of serious violence within the succeeding two years. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p205]

## Inferences

- Inference: The convergence of diverse types of childhood adversity onto similar biological pathways (glucocorticoid dysregulation, amygdala enlargement, frontal atrophy, dopamine depletion) suggests a final common pathway where different stressors trigger similar physiological cascades that alter brain development
- Inference: The fact that not all children exposed to adversity become dysfunctional adults indicates that protective factors (stable family environment, supportive relationships, community resources) can buffer against the biological effects of adversity

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which childhood adversity 'gets under the skin' beyond stress physiology remain an area of active research
- Uncertain: How much brain changes from childhood adversity are due to changes in neuron number versus dendritic processes remains unclear
