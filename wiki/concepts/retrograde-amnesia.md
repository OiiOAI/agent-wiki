# Retrograde Amnesia

## Summary

Retrograde amnesia is the loss of memories formed before the onset of brain damage. Studies of Henry Molaison revealed that his retrograde amnesia was temporally limited but more extensive than initially believed, affecting memories from the years immediately before his operation while sparing remote semantic knowledge but destroying episodic autobiographical memories.

## Key facts

- Henry's retrograde amnesia was 'temporally limited' but affected memories from approximately the last 11 years before his operation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p218]
- Henry's autobiographical memories came entirely from the period more than 41 years prior to testing, when he was 16 years old. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p218]
- The shorthand for the pattern of memory loss is 'last in, first out'—recent memories are lost while distant memories are preserved. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p218]
- At his 35th high school reunion, Henry did not recognize any classmates by sight or name, suggesting significant erasure of high-school memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p219]

## Inferences

- Inference: Retrograde amnesia does not uniformly affect all preoperative memories; semantic and episodic memories are differentially affected based on their dependency on hippocampal structures.
