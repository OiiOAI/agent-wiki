# Four Types of Neuroplasticity

## Summary

Jordan Grafman identified four distinct kinds of neuroplasticity that explain how the brain reorganizes itself: map expansion occurs at boundaries between brain areas through daily activity; sensory reassignment happens when one sense is blocked and another takes over its cortical territory; compensatory masquerade involves using alternative strategies to accomplish tasks; and mirror region takeover occurs when a region in the opposite hemisphere adapts to take over functions of damaged tissue.

## Key facts

- The first type is 'map expansion,' which occurs largely at the boundaries between brain areas as a result of daily activity, with boundary neurons being recruited by competing adjacent areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p278]
- The second type is 'sensory reassignment,' which occurs when one sense is blocked; for example, when the visual cortex is deprived of normal inputs, it can receive new inputs from another sense such as touch. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p279]
- The third type is 'compensatory masquerade,' which takes advantage of the fact that there's more than one way for the brain to approach a task, such as using visual landmarks instead of spatial sense for navigation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p279]
- The fourth type is 'mirror region takeover,' when part of one hemisphere fails, the mirror region in the opposite hemisphere adapts and takes over its mental function. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p279]
- The rules for storing information reflect the use-it-or-lose-it principle: the more frequently we use something, the more easily we retrieve it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p278]
- Grafman believes that boundary areas can expand quickly, within minutes, to respond to moment-by-moment needs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p278]
