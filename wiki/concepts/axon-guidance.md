# Axon Guidance

## Summary

Axon guidance is the process by which developing axons navigate to their synaptic targets using molecular cues detected by the growth cone. The growth cone is a sensory structure at the axon tip that transduces chemical signals into cytoskeletal changes, directing axon extension, turning, and stopping. Key guidance molecule families include slits, semaphorins, ephrins, and netrins.

## Key facts

- The growth cone, discovered by Santiago Ramón y Cajal, is a sensory structure that receives directional cues from the environment and a motor structure whose activity drives axon elongation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1162]
- Growth cones have three main compartments: central core rich in microtubules and organelles, lamellipodia between filopodia, and filopodia that sense environmental signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1163]
- Guidance cues include cell-surface adhesion molecules (cadherins, immunoglobulin superfamily), extracellular matrix proteins (laminins), and secreted factors (semaphorins, slits, ephrins, netrins). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1166]
- Sperry's chemospecificity hypothesis proposed that retinal axons recognize specific targets through molecular matching, confirmed by the discovery that ephrins and Eph kinases guide retinotopic map formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1167]
