# Defense Mechanisms

## Summary

Defense mechanisms are patterned responses men use to avoid fear and anxiety around women. The three main patterns are: Blame Game (blaming external factors for one's fear), Apathy and Avoidance (convincing oneself not to care), and Intellectualizing (using learning as avoidance). Breaking these patterns requires self-awareness, accountability to others, and consistent action despite resistance.

## Key facts

- The three main defense mechanisms are Blame Game, Apathy and Avoidance, and Intellectualizing. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p160]
- Anxiety represents a high level of neediness and investment in others' opinions. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p160]
- The Blame Game leads men to develop bitter beliefs like 'all women care about is money' or 'women in bars are shallow.'. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p161]
- Intellectualizing increases anxiety by building up higher expectations through excessive study. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p163]
- Sharing fears with someone who can empathize and keeping a friend accountable makes overcoming fears 10 times easier. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p163]
- Defense Mechanisms are defined as psychological reactions to anxiety that cause one to avoid taking action. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p266]
- Examples include blame, anger, projection, rationalizations, apathy, etc. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p266]
