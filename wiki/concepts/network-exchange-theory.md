# Network Exchange Theory

## Summary

Network exchange theory (NET), developed by Markovsky, Willer, and colleagues, combines social exchange theory and network analysis to study the distribution of power in networks of exchange. It distinguishes between strong power networks (where some actors cannot be excluded from exchanges, giving them high power) and weak power networks (where all positions are vulnerable to exclusion). The theory uses a graph-theoretical power index (GPI) and resistance model to predict relative power. High power actors in strong power networks obtain virtually all available resources.

## Key facts

- Network exchange theory (NET) was developed by Markovsky, Willer, and their colleagues using a graph-theoretical power index (GPI) based on counting alternative network paths and a resistance model based on actors' expectations about outcomes. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p335]
- The fundamental idea behind network exchange theory is that any social exchange occurs in the context of larger networks of social exchanges. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p334]
- A basic premise of network exchange theory is that the more opportunities an actor has for exchange, the greater the actor's power is, and these opportunities are directly related to the structure of the network. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p334]
- Strong power networks include actors who must be excluded (low power) and actors who cannot be excluded (high power), and high power actors in strong power networks obtain virtually all available resources. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p335]
- In weak power structures, all positions are vulnerable to exclusions, and positions less likely to be excluded benefit proportionally more than positions more likely to be excluded. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p336]
- Yamagishi, Gillmore, and Cook argued that power 'is fundamentally a social structural phenomenon' and cannot be studied meaningfully in the dyad. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p334]

## Inferences

- Inference: Network exchange theory expands agency by allowing low power positions to strategically manipulate network linkages to enhance their bargaining positions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Willer (1999) notes that no one has studied weak power networks in the field, so their empirical significance and even empirical existence are not known.
