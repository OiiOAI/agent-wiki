# Somatotopic Organization

## Summary

Somatotopic organization refers to the systematic mapping of the body surface within neural structures. Neurons that process information from adjacent body regions are positioned adjacent to each other in the brain, creating an orderly topographic representation. This arrangement is maintained throughout the somatosensory pathway from dorsal root ganglia through the spinal cord, brain stem, thalamus, to the cerebral cortex.

## Key facts

- The central axons of dorsal root ganglion cells terminate in the spinal cord forming a neural map of the body surface. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p128]
- Axons entering the cord in the sacral region ascend near the midline in the dorsal column, while those entering at higher levels ascend progressively more laterally, dividing into the gracile fascicle (lower body) and cuneate fascicle (upper body). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p128]
- Wilder Penfield found that sensations from the lower limbs are mediated by neurons near the midline of the brain, while sensations from the upper body, hands, fingers, face, lips, and tongue are mediated by neurons located laterally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p131]
- The amount of cortical surface area devoted to each body part is proportional to the fineness of sensory discrimination, not to the mass of the body part, explaining the large representation of fingers, lips, and tongue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p131]
- The primary somatosensory cortex contains four complete maps of the body surface in Brodmann areas 3a, 3b, 1, and 2. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p135]
