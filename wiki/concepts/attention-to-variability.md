# Attention to Variability

## Summary

Attention to variability is the practice of noticing subtle changes in symptoms, sensations, and health indicators over time. Rather than treating diagnoses as static, this approach recognizes that symptoms fluctuate constantly, and by noticing when symptoms are better or worse and exploring why, individuals can regain control over their health and discover actionable insights.

## Key facts

- Cholesterol levels, blood pressure, vision, and pain fluctuate throughout the day and week, but patients typically treat these numbers as fixed baselines. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p139]
- Participants who recorded heart rate every three hours and noted changes from previous measurements were better able to raise or lower their heart rate on command. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p144]
- Pregnant women who attended to variability of sensations had easier pregnancies and gave birth to babies with significantly better Apgar scores. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p144]
- Adults aged 65-80 concerned about memory who noticed fluctuations in memory ability over a week reported significantly fewer memory lapses and more feelings of control. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p147]
- Chronic pain patients who tracked variability in pain intensity reported significant decreases in pain interfering with daily life. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p148]
- ALS patients with higher mindfulness scores showed slower loss of function in correlational studies. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p149]

## Inferences

- Inference: Attention to variability works through four mechanisms: (1) recognizing symptoms aren't constant reduces distress, (2) the noticing itself is mindful which benefits health, (3) searching for causes increases likelihood of finding solutions, and (4) perceived control increases.
