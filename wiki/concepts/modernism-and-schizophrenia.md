# Modernism and Schizophrenia

## Summary

Modernism and Schizophrenia is a framework developed through the work of Louis Sass identifying remarkable parallels between the phenomenology of schizophrenia and the cultural characteristics of modernism. Both exhibit hyperconsciousness, loss of ipseity, fragmentation, alienation from the body, disruption of context, and what Sass calls the 'unworlding' of the world.

## Key facts

- Louis Sass identifies the same phenomena that characterize schizophrenia in culture at large. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p542]
- Kafka spoke of introspection that 'will suffer no idea to sink tranquilly to rest but must pursue each one into consciousness, only itself to become an idea, in turn to be pursued by renewed introspection'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p542]
- Susan Sontag noted that modernist art 'engenders a stare'—a disintegrating stare characteristic of schizophrenia. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p543]
- Both schizophrenia and modernism exhibit loss of narrative cohesion, dissolution of character, neglect of conventional space-time structure, and loss of comprehensible causal relations. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p544]
