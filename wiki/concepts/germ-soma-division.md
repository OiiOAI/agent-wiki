# Germ-Soma Division

## Summary

The germ-soma division refers to the separation of reproductive cells (germ cells) from body cells (somatic cells), a key feature of complex multicellular organisms. This separation, which first appeared in Cnidaria, ensures that genetic information is passed intact through generations while somatic cells handle the business of survival and can acquire mutations without affecting offspring.

## Key facts

- Germ cells produce gametes (sperm or eggs) and are physically segregated in reproductive organs; somatic cells make up the rest of the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p93]
- Germline sequestration prevents somatic mutations from being passed to offspring, protecting genetic integrity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p130]
- August Weismann proposed that only germ cells pass information between generations; somatic cells cannot affect offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p105]
- In Cnidaria, germ cells are segregated, protecting the individual from passing on somatic mutations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p130]
- Germ-soma separation enables somatic cells to sacrifice their individual interests for the survival of the organism as a whole. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p107]
