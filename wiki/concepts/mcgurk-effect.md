# McGurk Effect

## Summary

The McGurk effect is an auditory illusion where subjects are shown a video clip where the sound 'ba-ba' is played but the person's lips are moving as if saying 'ga-ga.' Neurotypical subjects tend to merge these sources of information and hear 'da-da'—a perceptual illusion that reflects the brain's best guess about the world given the conflicting sensory evidence. The effect is diminished in individuals with autism spectrum condition.

## Key facts

- In the McGurk effect, subjects shown a video where 'ba-ba' is played but lips move as for 'ga-ga' tend to hear 'da-da,' merging the two sources of information. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p67]
- The 'da-da' perception is a kind of illusion—the brain's best guess at what the world might be throwing at it, given the sensory evidence and what it knows about the relation between speech sounds and lip movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p67]
- The McGurk effect is diminished and sometimes entirely absent in those with autism spectrum condition, suggesting they take incoming sounds at closer to face value. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p67]
