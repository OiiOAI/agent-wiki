# Cooperative Learning

## Summary

Cooperative learning is an instructional strategy that organizes classroom activities to encourage positive interdependence among students working in small groups. The approach, exemplified by the work of Elliot Aronson and others, has been shown to reduce intergroup hostility and improve academic outcomes across diverse student populations.

## Key facts

- The jigsaw classroom technique, developed by Elliot Aronson, successfully reduces hostility between ethnic and racial groups in schools by requiring cooperative interdependence among students. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
- The 'Robbers' Cave Experiment' (Sherif et al., 1961) demonstrated how competition between groups creates hostility and how superordinate goals can reduce intergroup conflict. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
- Carlos, a Latino student, showed dramatic improvement in academic performance and self-esteem after his class adopted the jigsaw cooperative learning method. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
- Research supporting cooperative learning includes Johnson and Johnson (1983), DeVries and Slavin (1978), Cook (1990), and Aronson, Bridgeman, and Geffner (1978). [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p259]
