# Similarity Principle

## Summary

Research confirms that people trust others more when they view them as similar or familiar. This primal instinct of belonging means that even shallow similarities (club memberships, college alumni status) increase rapport. In negotiation, triggering this sense that 'we see the world the same way' provides immediate influence. The chapter recounts how Voss identified a CEO's Christian worldview and used the concept of 'stewardship' to establish shared identity and close the deal.

## Key facts

- People trust those who are in their in-group; belonging is a primal instinct and triggering it gives immediate influence. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p200]
- Similarities as shallow as club memberships or college alumni status increase rapport and can be vital for effective deal making and leverage building. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p200]
- Voss used the concept of 'stewardship' (a Christian term for duty to manage resources with honesty) to establish shared identity with a Christian CEO, leading to an immediate hiring decision. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p201]

## Inferences

- Inference: The effect works because of the understanding implied by the moment of similarity, not similarity alone.
