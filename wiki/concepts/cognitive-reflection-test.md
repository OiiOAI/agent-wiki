# Cognitive Reflection Test

## Summary

The Cognitive Reflection Test (CRT), developed by Shane Frederick, consists of three items designed to measure the ability to suppress intuitive but incorrect responses in favor of deliberate correct answers. The test has become one of the most replicated findings in behavioral science, showing that a substantial proportion of people—including elite university students—fail to suppress their System 1 intuitions.

## Key facts

- The bat-and-ball problem (sticks cost $1.10 more than ball, ball costs 10 cents) triggers intuitive wrong answer of 10 cents; correct answer is 5 cents. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p60]
- Over 50% of students at Harvard, MIT, and Princeton gave the intuitive wrong answer to the bat-and-ball problem. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p61]
- Lower CRT scores correlate with impulsive choices, preference for immediate rewards, and hyperbolic discounting. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p65]
- Making the test font difficult to read actually improved performance by triggering greater System 2 effort. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p88]

## Inferences

- Inference: The CRT reveals that intelligent people often fail to engage System 2 when answers feel intuitively satisfying.
