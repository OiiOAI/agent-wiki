# Nutrients for trauma and PTSD

## Summary

Research following natural disasters and mass trauma events has demonstrated that broad-spectrum multinutrient supplementation can substantially reduce PTSD and stress symptoms. After the Christchurch mosque shootings, 77 percent of monitored individuals met criteria for probable PTSD before treatment; after an average of five weeks of multinutrient treatment, this dropped to 23 percent, representing 70 percent remission. Similar results were found after the Christchurch earthquakes.

## Key facts

- After the September 2010 Christchurch earthquake, those who were taking nutrients at the time recovered more quickly from heightened anxiety and stress than those who weren't, with significantly less stress and anxiety two weeks after the earthquake. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p163]
- Following the February 2011 Christchurch earthquake, mean rates of probable PTSD in three nutrient treatment groups dropped from 65 percent to 19 percent, with no change in the untreated group which stayed at about 50 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p167]
- After the Christchurch mosque shootings, before starting treatment 77 percent of twenty-six monitored individuals met or exceeded the cutoff score defining probable PTSD. After an average of five weeks of multinutrient treatment, this rate dropped to 23 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p172]
- The higher dose of EMP (eight capsules per day) had some further advantage in that more people reported greater improvement in mood, anxiety, and energy, with twice as many reporting being 'much' to 'very much' improved. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p166]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Studies have focused on beneficial effects of nutrients for acute trauma. It is not yet known if nutrients can help with PTSD months or years after the trauma occurred.
