# Telepathy

## Summary

Telepathy refers to the alleged phenomenon whereby an event occurring to one person is simultaneously perceived by another person at a distance, without conventional sensory communication. Freud approached this topic cautiously, noting that psychoanalysis could reveal 'telepathic' events that would otherwise remain undiscovered, though he maintained agnosticism about its objective reality.

## Key facts

- Telepathy is defined as the alleged fact that an event occurring at a particular time comes simultaneously to the consciousness of a distant person. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6875]
- Freud reported a dream case where interpretation revealed what appeared to be telepathic content from his patient's daughter giving birth to twins. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6876]
- Freud maintained agnosticism about the objective reality of telepathy while acknowledging its theoretical implications for psychoanalysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6878]
- Telepathy is not bound to dreams but may occur during waking states, though sleep may be particularly suited to receiving telepathic messages. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6875]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud theorized that telepathy might involve a physical process intermediate between two mental acts, analogous to telephone communication, with psychoanalysis having 'paved the way' for such assumptions through its concept of the unconscious.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud explicitly states he has committed to no conviction about telepathy's objective reality and acknowledges the possibility that 'telepathic' dreams may have rational explanations through coincidence or unconscious inference.
