# Receptive Field

## Summary

A receptive field is the region of sensory space (in the external environment or on the body surface) within which a stimulus can influence the firing of a particular sensory neuron. Receptive fields define the spatial and functional properties of sensory neurons and are refined by neural circuits in the CNS.

## Key facts

- The receptive field of a sensory neuron defines the spatial region within which a stimulus can influence that neuron's activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p393]
- Receptive fields provide spatial information about stimulus location, with central and surrounding regions often having antagonistic effects on neuronal firing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p393]
- The region from which a sensation is perceived to arise is called the perceptive field and usually coincides with the receptive field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p445]
- The dimensions of receptive fields play an important role in the ability of a sensory system to encode detailed spatial information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p445]
- The size of the stimulus determines the total number of receptors activated, providing a neural image of the size and contours of the stimulus through spatial distribution of active receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p445]
- Charles Sherrington coined the term 'receptive field' in 1906 for the scratch withdrawal reflex, defining it as the collection of skin points from which the reflex could be elicited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p554]
- H. Keffer Hartline first applied the concept to vision in the horseshoe crab retina, defining the receptive field as 'the region of the retina which must be illuminated in order to obtain a response in any given fiber.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p554]
- Receptive field size varies with eccentricity from the fovea; foveal ganglion cell receptive fields subtend ~0.1° while peripheral fields can be orders of magnitude larger. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- Retinal ganglion cells and LGN neurons have circular center-surround receptive fields with mutually antagonistic center and surround regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p556]
- ON-center cells fire when light turns on in the center; OFF-center cells fire when light turns off in the center; surrounds have opposite response polarity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p556]
- Cortical simple cells have segregated ON and OFF subregions within their elongated receptive fields, responding to light onset in ON regions and light offset in OFF regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p548]
- Complex cells respond to oriented lines or edges regardless of exact position within the receptive field, lacking discrete ON/OFF subregions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p548]

## Inferences

- Inference: Receptive field complexity increases along the visual pathway, from simple light detection to orientation selectivity, reflecting hierarchical processing.
