# Synaptic Capture (Synaptic Tagging)

## Summary

Synaptic capture (or synaptic tagging) is a process by which synapses that receive weak stimulation can undergo long-term changes if they occur within a time window when new proteins are being synthesized at other activated synapses. This explains how gene products from the cell body can selectively strengthen only those synapses that were active during learning.

## Key facts

- When five pulses of serotonin are applied to one synapse and one pulse to another synapse, the second synapse undergoes long-term facilitation by capturing products from the first synapse—this is called synaptic capture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1374]
- Local phosphorylation by PKA is required for synaptic capture; blocking PKA at the capturing synapse prevents it from using newly synthesized gene products. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1375]
- Synaptic capture explains how gene products from the cell body can selectively strengthen only those synapses that were active during learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1374]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'synaptic mark' that allows capture appears to involve PKA activation, which is necessary for marking synapses that can use the newly synthesized proteins.
- Inference: This mechanism is conserved from Aplysia to mammals, as Frey and Morris independently demonstrated similar capture in the rodent hippocampus.
