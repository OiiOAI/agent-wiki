# Acting Out

## Summary

Acting out is the behavioral expression of unconscious memories, impulses, and fantasies rather than their verbal recollection in treatment. Freud introduced this concept through the Dora case, explaining that she 'acted out' an essential part of her recollections and phantasies instead of reproducing them in the treatment, which led to her premature termination.

## Key facts

- Freud stated that Dora 'acted out' (agierte) an essential part of her recollections and phantasies instead of reproducing them in the treatment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2395]
- Acting out occurs when the patient cannot tolerate the tension of remembering and instead behaves in ways that reproduce the repressed material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2395]
- Dora's desertion of Freud was an expression of her unconscious wish to take revenge on Herr K., transferring her feelings to the analyst. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2387]
