# Collective Effervescence

## Summary

Emile Durkheim's concept describing the heightened emotional energy and sense of transcendence that emerges when people engage together in synchronized rituals. Durkheim observed that collective rituals generate powerful shared emotions that 'launch them to an extraordinary height of exaltation.' These experiences temporarily shift individuals from the profane realm (concerned with personal interests) to the sacred realm (where collective interests predominate and the self dissolves). Collective effervescence is proposed as a mechanism underlying the hive switch and the binding power of religion.

## Key facts

- Durkheim described collective effervescence: 'Once the individuals are gathered together, a sort of electricity is generated from their closeness and quickly launches them to an extraordinary height of exaltation.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p248]
- In states of collective effervescence, 'the vital energies become hyperexcited, the passions more intense, the sensations more powerful.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p248]
- Barbara Ehrenreich argues that ecstatic dancing is a universal 'biotechnology' for binding groups together, fostering love, trust, and equality. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p245]

## Inferences

- Inference: Collective effervescence may explain the emotional power of religious services, sports events, and political rallies—all events that generate similar psychological experiences despite different ostensible purposes.
