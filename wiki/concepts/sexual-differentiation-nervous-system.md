# Sexual Differentiation of the Nervous System

## Summary

Sexual differentiation of the nervous system involves genetic and hormonal mechanisms that produce sex differences in brain structure and behavior. The SRY gene on the Y chromosome initiates male development by promoting testis formation, which secretes hormones (MIS, testosterone) that override a female default program. Organizational effects during development (irreversible) differ from activational effects in adulthood (reversible), with testosterone acting via aromatization to estrogen for many brain masculinizing effects.

## Key facts

- The SRY gene encodes a transcription factor that regulates SOX9 expression, which is required for testicular differentiation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1262]
- In males, Müllerian inhibiting substance (MIS) causes regression of the Müllerian duct, while testosterone induces Wolffian duct differentiation into male internal genitalia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1263]
- Organizational effects of sex hormones occur during development and are irreversible, while activational effects occur in adulthood and are reversible. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1262]
- Testosterone is aromatized to estrogen in the brain, and this estrogen mediates many of testosterone's masculinizing effects on the nervous system.
