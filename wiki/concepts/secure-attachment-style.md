# Secure Attachment Style

## Summary

Secure attachment is the most common attachment style, found in over 50 percent of the population. People with a secure attachment style feel comfortable with intimacy and are usually warm and loving. They enjoy being intimate without being overly worried about their relationships, communicate needs effectively, and respond well to their partner's emotional cues.

## Key facts

- Secure people feel comfortable with intimacy and are usually warm and loving. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p16]
- People with secure attachment effectively communicate their needs and feelings to their partner. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p43]
- Secure individuals are strong at reading their partner's emotional cues and responding to them. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p43]
- Secure people take things in stride when it comes to romance and don't get easily upset over relationship matters. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p43]
- The secure baby is visibly distressed when mommy leaves the room but is quick to calm down and resume play upon reunion. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p46]
- Secure individuals have more unconscious access to themes such as love, hugs, and closeness and less access to danger, loss, and separation in studies measuring unconscious reactions. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p111]
- Secures don't react to threatening relationship words even when distracted, unlike avoidants who only stop reacting when distracted, showing secures aren't concerned about threatening thoughts either consciously or subconsciously. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p111]
- When secures were asked to stop thinking about separation and abandonment topics, their skin conductance abruptly went back to normal, suggesting emotional regulation comes effortlessly for them. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p111]
- A meta-analysis by Leslie Atkinson based on forty-one studies with over two thousand parent-child pairs found a weak but significant link between maternal sensitivity and secure child attachment. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p113]
- Genetic association research found a specific pattern of the dopamine receptor DRD2 allele associated with anxious attachment and a variant of the serotonin 5-HT1A receptor linked to avoidance. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p114]
- About 70 to 75 percent of adults remain consistently in the same attachment category at different points in their lives, while 25 to 30 percent report a change in attachment style. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p115]
- Secure people are characterized as great conflict busters, mentally flexible, effective communicators, not game players, comfortable with closeness, quick to forgive, and treat their partners like royalty. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p112]

## Inferences

- Inference: The secure buffering effect described in the research suggests that secure individuals can raise their insecure partners' relationship satisfaction and functioning to their own high level through their natural sensitivity and encouragement.
- Inference: Secure attachment style appears to develop from a mosaic of factors: early connection with parents, genetic predispositions, and romantic experiences as adults, rather than from any single source.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether attachment security in infancy reliably predicts attachment security in adulthood is still debated, with three studies failing to find correlation and two finding significant connection.
