# The Superego (Das Über-Ich)

## Summary

The superego (das Über-Ich) is a differentiation within the ego that functions as an ego ideal or critical agency. It originates from the dissolution of the Oedipus complex, when the child's object cathexes are replaced by identifications with parental figures. The superego represents the internalized moral standards and prohibitions of society, particularly those derived from the father. It is characterized by conscience and manifests as an unconscious sense of guilt.

## Key facts

- The superego is the heir to the Oedipus complex and represents the most powerful impulses and most important libidinal vicissitudes of the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5921]
- The superego is the expression of the most powerful impulses and most important libidinal vicissitudes of the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5921]
- The superego retains the character of the father; the more powerful the Oedipus complex and the more rapidly it succumbed to repression, the stricter will be the domination of the superego over the ego later on. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5920]
- The superego is accessible to consciousness through preconscious verbal residues but its cathectic energy comes from sources in the id. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5936]
- The superego reaches deep down into the id and is farther from consciousness than the ego is. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5934]
- The superego can be supermoral and then become as cruel as only the id can be. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5938]

## Inferences

- Inference: The superego owes its special position to two factors: it was the first identification made while the ego was still feeble, and it is the heir to the Oedipus complex which introduced the most momentous objects into the ego.
