# Gustatory Pleasure

## Summary

Gustatory pleasure refers to the hedonic experiences associated with taste, particularly sweetness. Brain opioid systems appear to mediate these pleasures, with sweet tastes promoting opioid release and creating positive affective experiences that guide food selection.

## Key facts

- Sweet taste promotes opioid release in the brain, and young animals become analgesic after sugar water administration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]
- Opiate receptor antagonists such as naltrexone diminish the pleasurable aspects of sweet taste and accelerate reduction of saccharin intake. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]
- Animals given morphine-paired flavors develop strong preferences that can be extinguished and counterconditioned. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]
- Pleasure is defined as indicating something is biologically useful—stimuli that restore homeostasis are experienced as pleasurable. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]
- Brain opioids are important in governing gustatory choices, suggesting humans may be addicted to foods that naturally activate opioid systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]
- Rats exhibit lip-smacking responses to sweetness and lateral head shaking to bitterness, with comparable responses in human infants. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]

## Inferences

- Inference: The opioid-mediated pleasure of sweet taste may explain both normal food preferences and potential food addictions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether distinct pleasure circuits exist for different consummatory behaviors or a single opioid system provides hedonic value for multiple stimuli.
