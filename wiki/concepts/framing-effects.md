# Framing Effects

## Summary

Framing effects occur when decisions are systematically influenced by how options are described, even when the underlying outcomes are identical. Prospect Theory explains this through the reference-dependent value function and the editing phase where prospects are mentally transformed before evaluation.

## Key facts

- Prospect Theory explicitly acknowledges that choices are influenced by how prospects are cognitively represented in terms of losses and gains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p144]
- Decisions concerning medical treatments differ depending on whether outcomes are described in terms of survival versus mortality rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p144]
- People are more likely to favor a .25 chance of $32 over a .20 chance of $40 when presented as a two-stage game making the $32 outcome feel certain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p144]
- Prospect Theory assumes values of prospects are evaluated relative to subjective reference points where positive changes are coded as gains and negative changes as losses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p77]
- The theory posits that subjective value is a concave function in the gains domain and a steeper convex function in the losses domain, producing risk-aversion for gains and risk-seeking for losses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p77]
- The principle of descriptive invariance states that alternative formulations of the same events should be treated identically if decisions are to be regarded as rational. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p77]
- The principle of descriptive invariance is a normative standard that prospect theory does not challenge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p77]
- Asymmetric ambiguity between options in framing studies may explain framing effects, with the certain option containing missing information compared to the risky option. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p79-81]
- When ambiguity is controlled by including missing information for both options, framing effects can be eliminated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p81]

## Inferences

- Inference: Framing effects may be partially explained by conversational implicatures where missing information in descriptions invites the interpretation 'at least N' for quantities.
- Inference: The conversational account proposes that the effect of frames depends on pragmatic inferences drawn in light of contextual information and conversational norms.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: It remains unclear whether subjects who exhibit framing effects interpret the values expressed in sure options as precise values or as lower bounds, which would affect whether such preferences constitute genuine irrationality.
