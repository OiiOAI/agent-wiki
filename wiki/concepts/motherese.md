# Motherese

## Summary

Motherese (or parentese) is a special speaking style adults use when addressing infants and young children, characterized by higher pitch, slower tempo, exaggerated intonation contours, and more clearly pronounced phonetic units. This speech style appears to assist infants' discrimination of phonetic units and may enhance language learning in the young.

## Key facts

- Motherese has a higher pitch, slower tempo, and exaggerated intonation contours compared to adult-directed speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1412]
- Adults speaking to infants exaggerate features of speech that are critical to their native language; Chinese mothers, for example, exaggerate the four tones in Mandarin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1412]
- Infants prefer listening to infant-directed rather than adult-directed speech when given a choice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1412]
