# Magical Thinking

## Summary

Magical thinking refers to the blurring of boundaries between imagination and reality, a phenomenon Luria observed in S. throughout his life. While typically a passing phase in childhood, this tendency persisted in S., leading him to believe that imagining events could influence their occurrence and that his vivid mental images were equivalent to reality.

## Key facts

- S. stated that to him there was no great difference between things he imagined and what existed in reality, and that if he imagined something was going to happen, it often did. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p147]
- S. recounted an instance where he imagined receiving too much change from a cashier and she actually gave him 20 rubles instead of 10. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p147]
- S. believed he could cure himself by imagining illness passing, and claimed to have cured his son's stomach pains by visualizing the offending food dissolving. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p147]
- S. experienced a childhood habit where he would see clock hands fixed at a particular time and not realize time had passed, leading to chronic lateness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p152]
- S. described using 'magical' techniques in school, trying to fix his teacher with a look to make him look away from S., demonstrating the persistence of childhood magical thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p146]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: While S. intellectually recognized that his magical beliefs might be coincidence, 'in some remote part of his awareness he continued to feel: perhaps it really is true.'
