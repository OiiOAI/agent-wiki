# Decisions from Experience vs Description

## Summary

A distinction in the decision-making literature between choices made when outcome probabilities are learned through direct sampling and feedback (decisions from experience) versus when they are explicitly described (decisions from description). Research shows systematic differences between these two modes of learning about uncertainty.

## Key facts

- Decisions from experience involve learning from repeated sampling of available choice alternatives, while decisions from description rely on externally provided probability distributions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p132]
- Experiential processes involve strong feelings and memory, often dominant in processing, while analytic processes allow for planning and self-regulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p132]
- Rare events are underweighted more in decisions from experience than in decisions from description, a phenomenon known as the 'description-experience gap'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p132]

## Inferences

- Inference: The difference between experiential and analytic processing systems may explain why movement planning under risk succeeds while cognitive decision-making under risk often fails.
