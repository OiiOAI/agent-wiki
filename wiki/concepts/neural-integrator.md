# Neural Integrator

## Summary

The neural integrator is a brain circuit that mathematically integrates velocity signals to produce position signals, enabling the eyes to be held at a new position against elastic restorative forces of the orbit. For horizontal eye movements, integration is performed by the medial vestibular nucleus and nucleus prepositus hypoglossi.

## Key facts

- To move the eye quickly to a new position and keep it there, two forces must be overcome: the elastic force of the orbit that restores the eye to central position and velocity-dependent viscous force that opposes rapid movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p952]
- Neural integration of the velocity signal for horizontal saccades is performed by the medial vestibular nucleus and nucleus prepositus hypoglossi, which convert velocity commands into position commands. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p956]
- Animals with lesions of the medial vestibular nucleus or nucleus prepositus hypoglossi make normal horizontal saccades but the eyes drift back to a middle position after each saccade. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p956]
- Integration of the horizontal saccadic burst requires coordination of the bilateral nuclei propositi hypoglossi and medial vestibular nuclei through commissural connections; a midline lesion of these connections causes failure of the neural integrator. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p956]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neural integrator represents the brain's solution to the mathematical problem of converting velocity commands into sustained position commands, analogous to mathematical integration.
