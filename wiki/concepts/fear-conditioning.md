# Fear conditioning

## Summary

Fear conditioning is a form of Pavlovian learning where an association is formed between an emotionally neutral conditioned stimulus (CS) and an aversive unconditioned stimulus (US). After repeated pairings, presentation of the CS alone elicits defensive freezing and associated autonomic and endocrine responses. The amygdala, particularly the lateral and central nuclei, is essential for this form of learning.

## Key facts

- In Pavlovian fear conditioning an association is learned between the US (e.g., shock) and the CS that predicts the US. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1135]
- The lateral nucleus is thought to be a site of synaptic change during fear conditioning where CS and US signals converge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1136]
- Sensory inputs reach the lateral nucleus from the thalamus both directly and indirectly via sensory areas of cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1136]
- The amygdala detects danger and orchestrates the expression of behavioral and physiological responses via connections to hypothalamus and brain stem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1136]
- Damage to the amygdala prevents fear conditioning from occurring; animals fail to learn the CS-US association. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1135]
