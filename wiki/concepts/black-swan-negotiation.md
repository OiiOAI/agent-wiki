# Black Swan Negotiation

## Summary

The Black Swan concept refers to the three to five pieces of information in every negotiation that, if uncovered, would change everything. Named after Voss's consultancy The Black Swan Group, this concept emphasizes that rare, high-impact information can create breakthroughs and achieve truly amazing deals.

## Key facts

- In every negotiation there are between three and five pieces of information that, if uncovered, would change everything—called Black Swans. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p23]
- Voss named his company The Black Swan Group after this concept. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p23]
- The book promises to teach how to recognize markers that show the Black Swan's hidden nest and tools for employing Black Swans to gain leverage. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p23]
- Black Swans are events or pieces of knowledge outside our regular expectations that cannot be predicted, derived from Taleb's concept after the seventeenth-century discovery that black swans existed despite prior certainty only white swans existed. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p189]
- Voss hypothesizes that in every negotiation each side is in possession of at least three Black Swans—pieces of information that if discovered by the other side would change everything. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p191]
- Conventional questioning and research techniques are designed to confirm known knowns and reduce uncertainty, but they don't dig into the unknown where Black Swans reside. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p192]
- The Griffin hostage crisis (1981) demonstrated a Black Swan: no hostage-taker had ever killed a hostage on deadline, until William Griffin did exactly that—showing that assumptions based on past experience can be dangerously wrong. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p188]

## Inferences

- Inference: Black Swans are often innocuous-seeming information that the counterpart may not understand the value of revealing.
