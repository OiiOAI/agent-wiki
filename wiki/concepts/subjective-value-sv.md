# Subjective value (SV)

## Summary

Subjective value (SV) is the learned value of objects or goods estimated from repeated experience, proposed to be encoded in the mean activity of subpopulations in the medial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum. For primary rewards, SV follows a compressive function of experienced reward, consistent with Weber-type sensory encoding mechanisms. SV is hypothesized to be a stochastic quantity drawn from a spiking distribution, closely allied to reference-dependent random utility models rather than traditional von Neumann-Morgenstern utility.

## Key facts

- Subjective value (SV) is learned from repeated experience and represents the value of objects or goods at a given time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p496]
- The ventral striatum and medial prefrontal cortex show activity correlated with SV under essentially all reward and choice conditions studied. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p496]
- Mean activity in subpopulations of the medial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum encodes SV when options are under consideration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p497]
- SV for primary rewards follows a compressive function of experienced reward, consistent with sensory encoding mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p496]
- Instantaneous SV is necessarily drawn from an underlying distribution due to limited neuronal dynamic range and stochasticity in firing rate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p497]
- Baseline spike rate in ventral striatum and medial prefrontal cortex represents the reference point for SV's reference-dependent encoding. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p497]

## Inferences

- Inference: The compressive encoding of SV for primary rewards suggests that risk aversion over primary rewards arises from Weber-type encoding mechanisms in sensory apparatus.
- Inference: SV's stochastic nature drawn from neural firing distributions implies SV is best modeled as random utility rather than deterministic utility.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The definitive study ruling out that ventral striatum encodes only reward-prediction errors and not SV per se has not yet been conducted.
- Uncertain: The anatomical pathways by which medial prefrontal cortex and ventral striatum influence posterior parietal cortex activity during choice remain undetermined.
