# Advantages of Scale

## Summary

Economic advantages that emerge when businesses grow larger. These include geometric advantages (volume increases faster than surface area), purchasing power, laboratory effects for experimentation, specialization of labor, TV advertising access, informational advantages (brand recognition), social proof effects, and winner-take-all dynamics. However, scale also introduces bureaucracy and territoriality that can destroy advantages.

## Key facts

- Advantages of scale come from simple geometry—as volume increases, capacity grows faster than surface area and material costs. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p174]
- TV advertising created enormous advantages for large companies like Procter & Gamble because the cost was fixed and only affordable at scale. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p175]
- Wrigley has an informational advantage because widespread distribution makes it known and trusted compared to unknown competitors. [raw/books/ strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p175]
- Social proof—where people are influenced by what others do and approve—gives huge advantages to scale, especially with wide distribution like Coca-Cola. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p176]
- In daily newspapers, almost no city in the U.S. aside from very big ones has more than one daily newspaper due to winner-take-all scale effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p177]
- The great defect of scale is bureaucracy, which brings territoriality, perverse incentives, and slow decision-making. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p179]
- Sears, Roebuck lost to Wal-Mart partly because it had layers of people it didn't need, was very bureaucratic, and slow to think. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p184]

## Inferences

- Inference: The airline industry demonstrates that scale advantages in commodities are insufficient when competition destroys profits—scale helps incumbents but doesn't guarantee returns.
