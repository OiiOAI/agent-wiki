# Departure of the Hero

## Summary

The last act in the biography of the hero is the death or departure, where the whole sense of the life is epitomized. The hero would be no hero if death held for him any terror; the first condition is reconciliation with the grave. After death, the hero who represented the dual perspective remains a synthesizing image—sleeping only, to arise in the hour of destiny.

## Key facts

- Abraham perceived Death coming in great glory and beauty, but Death said he takes such form only for the righteous. When Abraham refused to go with him and demanded to see Death's corruption (two heads, one with the face of a serpent, another like a sword), the looks of Death were not able to cause Abraham's soul to depart—so God removed it as in a dream. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p279]
- Charlemagne sleeps only and will arise in the hour of his country's need; during the later Middle Ages he once arose from the dead to participate in a crusade. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p254]
- Quetzalcoatl departed from his city burning his dwellings behind him, burying his treasures, transforming his chocolate trees into mesquite, commanding the multi-colored birds to fly before him—until at the sea he departed on a raft of serpents to his original home Tlapállan. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p280]
- According to another tradition, Quetzalcoatl at the shore immolated himself upon a funeral pyre, and birds with multicolored feathers arose from his ashes; his soul became the Morning Star. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p281]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hero's death/departure represents not an ending but a transformation—the hero's continued presence in some form (sleeping emperor, Morning Star, guiding presence) ensures his aid remains available to those who need it.
