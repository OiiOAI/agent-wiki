# Origins of language

## Summary

Language did not develop primarily for communication or for thought—both existed before language. Evidence suggests that language evolved from music or 'musilanguage': the sophisticated control of vocal apparatus and respiration needed for singing existed long before it would have been required by language. In both phylogeny and ontogeny, music precedes language. The right hemisphere, which mediates music, is the likely origin of this capacity.

## Key facts

- Homo heidelbergensis had a vocal apparatus comparable to modern humans some 300–400,000 years ago, yet sophisticated symbol manipulation requiring language probably did not arise until as little as 40,000 years ago. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p150]
- Examination of the anterior condylar canal (through which the hypoglossal nerve passes, controlling tongue movement) in early human skeletons reveals sizes almost indistinguishable from modern humans, long before language arose; this suggests sophisticated vocal control was used for non-verbal purposes, likely music. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p151]
- Intonation, phrasing and rhythm develop first in children; syntax and vocabulary come only later; newborns are already sensitive to the rhythms of language and prefer 'infant-directed speech' which emphasises prosody. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p152]
- The 'syntax' of music is simpler, less highly evolved, than that of language, suggesting an earlier origin. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p152]
- Music is likely the communication of emotion, the most fundamental form of communication, which in phylogeny, as well as ontogeny, came and comes first. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p153]
- Poetry evolved before prose; early poetry was sung, so the evolution of literary skill progresses from right-hemisphere music (words that are sung), to right-hemisphere language (the metaphorical language of poetry), to left-hemisphere language (the referential language of prose). [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p155]

## Inferences

- Inference: language evolved from music, the right hemisphere's primacy in musical processing suggests it was the original source of human symbolic communication.
