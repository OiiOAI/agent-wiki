# System Traps

## Summary

System traps (or archetypes) are system structures that produce commonly encountered patterns of problematic behavior. Meadows identifies eight major traps: policy resistance (fixes that fail), tragedy of the commons, drift to low performance, escalation, success to the successful (competitive exclusion), shifting the burden to the intervenor (addiction), rule beating, and seeking the wrong goal. Each trap has identifiable structures and potential ways out through system redesign.

## Key facts

- Eight common system archetypes produce problematic behavior: addiction, drift to low performance, escalation, and others. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p140]
- System traps are caused by system structure, not by particular actors or events, so blaming individuals rarely helps. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p140]
- Standard responses (blaming, disciplining, tinkering at the margins) will not fix structural problems. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p140]
- System traps can be escaped by recognizing them in advance and altering the structure through reformulating goals, weakening or strengthening feedback loops, or adding new ones. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p140]
