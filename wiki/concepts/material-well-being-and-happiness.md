# Material Well-Being and Happiness

## Summary

Research demonstrates that increases in material prosperity have little or no correlation with human happiness beyond meeting basic needs. This finding contradicts the Enlightenment assumption that material progress leads to greater well-being, and suggests the left hemisphere's utilitarian approach to maximizing happiness has failed on its own terms.

## Key facts

- Over the last twenty-five years in the US, levels of satisfaction with life have declined despite an enormous increase in prosperity. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p595]
- In 1955, 44 per cent of all US workers enjoyed their working hours more than anything else they did; by 1999 only 16 per cent did. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p595]
- According to Gallup poll data, throughout the 1950s the British were happier than they are today, despite now being three times richer in real terms. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p595]
- Japan experienced a 500 per cent increase in per capita income from 1958 to the late 1990s, yet levels of happiness among the Japanese did not change at all. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p595]
- Geoffrey Miller found that age, sex, race, income, geographic location, nationality, and education level explain less than 2% of the variance in happiness. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p596]
- Happiness reaches a plateau at a remarkably low average national income, variably estimated at $10,000-$20,000 per annum. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p596]

## Inferences

- Inference: The left hemisphere's justification for environmental destruction and cultural erosion in the name of human happiness is unsupported by the evidence, since material well-being does not correlate with happiness beyond basic needs.
