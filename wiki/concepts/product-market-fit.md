# Product/Market Fit

## Summary

Product/market fit describes when a startup finally finds a widespread set of customers that resonate with its product. Marc Andreessen defined it as when 'the market pulls product out of the startup.' In the engine of growth framework, each engine has specific metrics indicating proximity to product/market fit: a viral coefficient of 0.9+ for viral engines, or measurable compounding growth rates for sticky engines. If you are asking whether you have achieved it, you have not.

## Key facts

- Marc Andreessen coined product/market fit to describe when a startup finds customers that resonate with its product: 'In a great market—a market with lots of real potential customers—the market pulls product out of the startup.'. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p204]
- Eric Ries states: 'If you are asking, you are not there yet' regarding whether a startup has achieved product/market fit. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p204]
- A startup with a viral coefficient of 0.9 or more is on the verge of success; metrics for each engine of growth work with innovation accounting to give direction to product development efforts. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p205]
- Two companies both with compounding growth showed company A improving from 0.1% to 5.0% over six months while company B remained stuck at 9.7-10%, indicating company A was making real progress even though B grew faster in absolute terms. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p206]

## Inferences

- Inference: Every engine of growth eventually runs out of gas tied to a given set of customers and their habits, requiring startups to eventually pivot or face stagnation.
