# Forgetting of Impressions and Intentions

## Summary

Freud distinguishes forgetting of impressions (knowledge) from forgetting of intentions (omissions). He argues that forgetting is often motivated by unpleasure, with examples showing forgotten grievances against family members and estrangements.

## Key facts

- The forgetting of distressing impressions and the occurrence of distressing thoughts are opposed by a resistance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2128]
- Forgetting serves to keep distressing material out of consciousness through repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2127]
- A person may forget their own past assertions when reminded of distressing facts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2128]
- Mislaying objects follows the same motivational pattern as forgetting—objects are mislaid through unconscious intention. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2124]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud connects forgetting to the same motivational basis as other parapraxes—the avoidance of unpleasure.
