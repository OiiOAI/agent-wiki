# Exercise and Addiction Recovery

## Summary

Exercise counteracts addiction by filling the void left when substances are removed, providing a healthy alternative reward. Exercise directly counteracts anxiety and depression (which undermine treatment), decreases symptoms in recovering alcoholics and smokers, reduces physical withdrawal symptoms, and reverses brain damage from substances like alcohol. Exercise also boosts self-efficacy and self-regulation, spreading self-control from exercise to other life areas. Australian research showed that exercise improved not only fitness but also reduced smoking, drinking, caffeine consumption, junk food intake, impulse spending, and procrastination.

## Key facts

- Exercise counteracts anxiety and depression directly, which can undermine addiction treatment; people are more impulsive when they feel lousy. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p153]
- Both strength training and aerobic exercise decrease symptoms of depression in recovering alcoholics and smokers. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p153]
- Exercise and abstinence from alcohol both stop and reverse brain damage by increasing neurogenesis and regrowing the hippocampus in adult rats. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p154]
- Australian research showed a two-month exercise program improved not only fitness but also reduced smoking, drinking, caffeine and alcohol consumption, junk food intake, impulse spending, and procrastination. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p154]
- The recommended minimum for rooting out addiction is 30 minutes of vigorous aerobic exercise five days a week. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p155]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept that exercise rebuilds the alcoholic brain by increasing neurogenesis suggests neuroprotective effects beyond just mood improvement.
