# The Methylation Cycle and Epigenetics

## Summary

The methylation cycle is a biochemical process in which methyl groups (one carbon and three hydrogen atoms) are transferred to DNA, hormones, neurotransmitters, and cell membranes, enabling DNA repair, hormone regulation, and cellular healing. This process is dependent on nutrient cofactors including zinc, folate, vitamin B2, vitamin B6, and vitamin B12. Epigenetics refers to the modification of gene expression without changing the underlying DNA sequence; methylation is a key epigenetic mechanism that can silence or activate genes. The agouti mouse experiments demonstrated that maternal diet during pregnancy (specifically micronutrients like B12 and folate) determines whether offspring are yellow and prone to disease (unmethylated agouti gene) or brown and healthier (methylated agouti gene), proving nutrition directly alters genetic expression. Julia's team conducted the first study showing that multinutrient supplementation modifies DNA methylation in children with ADHD.

## Key facts

- The agouti mouse model demonstrated that maternal diet during pregnancy determines offspring coat color and health: adequate micronutrients (B12, folate) lead to brown, healthier mice (agouti gene silenced by methylation), while low-nutrient diets yield yellow, disease-prone mice. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p60]
- The Framingham Heart Study (2019) reported that a healthier diet was associated with the expression of genes involved in metabolic function in approximately 5,000 participants, suggesting diet can modify gene expression in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p60]
- Julia's team conducted the first study showing that multinutrient supplementation modifies DNA methylation in children with ADHD, at over 850,000 sites in the human genome, though the magnitude of change was modest. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p61]
- Telomere length—a biomarker of aging—is shortened by chronic stress, poor diet, oxidative stress, and inflammation. Ultra-processed food consumption was associated with shorter telomeres in 886 older adults in the SUN project in Spain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p74]
- Linus Pauling stated in 1968: 'What is probably inherited in mental illness are genes that regulate brain metabolism of essential nutrients,' anticipating the epigenetic and cofactor-dependent understanding of mental disorders. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p30]
