# Concentration Ratio

## Summary

A concentration ratio measures the degree of market dominance by the largest firms in an economy or sector. It is calculated by taking the total value-added, employment, or market capitalization of the top X firms (typically 3-10 for individual sectors) and dividing by the total for that sector or economy.

## Key facts

- Concentration ratios are commonly used as a measure of monopoly or oligopoly in particular economic sectors. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p369]
- Small European countries including Switzerland, Sweden, and Holland have higher ten-firm concentration ratios than the United States, Japan, or Germany. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p370]
- Beyond a certain minimum population and level of economic development, the correlation between an economy's absolute size and its ability to produce large companies appears to be weak. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p370]
