# Nirvana

## Summary

Buddhist concept meaning 'the Extinguishing of the Threefold Fire of Desire, Hostility, and Delusion.' The Bodhisattva who could enter Nirvana pauses at the threshold, choosing to remain in the world to help all beings. This represents realization that the distinction between eternity and time is only apparent—dissolved in perfect knowledge.

## Key facts

- Nirvana means 'the Extinguishing of the Threefold Fire of Desire, Hostility, and Delusion,' corresponding to Freudian eros and thanatos. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p130]
- The Buddha's temptation under the Bo Tree involved Kama-Mara, literally 'Desire—Hostility' or 'Love and Death,' the magician of Delusion. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p130]
- The Bodhisattva's renunciation of immediate Nirvana demonstrates that time and eternity are two aspects of the same experience-whole: 'The jewel is in the lotus' (om mani padme hum). [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p122]
