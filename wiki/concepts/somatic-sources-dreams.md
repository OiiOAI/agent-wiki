# Somatic Sources of Dreams

## Summary

Somatic sources of dreams include objective sensory stimuli from external objects, internal states of excitation of the sense organs, and somatic stimuli from the interior of the body. Freud argues that while these sources can provide material for dreams, they do not fully explain dream formation, which requires psychical motives including the wish to sleep.

## Key facts

- The significance of objective excitations of the sense organs is established from numerous observations and has been experimentally confirmed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p220]
- External stimuli do not necessarily compel dreaming; the sleeper may disregard them, be aware of them without weaving them into dreams, or wake up to get rid of them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p224]
- The essential nature of the dream is not altered by somatic material being added to its psychical sources; a dream remains the fulfillment of a wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p228]
- Dreams are guardians of sleep and not its disturbers; they serve the purpose of prolonging sleep instead of waking up. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p233]

## Inferences

- Inference: When somatic stimuli are intense enough to force psychical attention, they serve as a fixed point for dream formation, but the dream-work seeks a wish fulfillment to correspond to this nucleus.
