# Invariants

## Summary

Invariants are properties of the environment that persist while other properties change. Gibson argued that perception involves extracting invariants from the continuously changing flow of stimulation. As an observer moves, some aspects of the optic array remain invariant (specifying persistent properties of the environment) while others vary (specifying motion, distance, etc.). The detection of invariants is what allows perception of stable properties like size, shape, and surface color.

## Key facts

- The environment persists in some respects and changes in other respects; there are variants and invariants in any transformation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p39]
- The occlusion edge of a closer surface is only visible over time and is a relational property of environment-organism processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p23]
- Perception of invariants was central to Gibson's 1950 work on texture gradients showing that the projective geometry of the receding ground to the eye is the same as that of objects on the ground as they recede. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p19]
