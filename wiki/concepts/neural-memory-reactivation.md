# Neural memory reactivation during sleep

## Summary

Neural memory reactivation during sleep refers to the phenomenon where patterns of neural activity established during waking experience are reproduced during subsequent sleep. This replay has been observed in rodents as patterns of cell firing during hippocampal sharp-wave ripple events, and in humans through functional imaging studies showing reactivation of brain regions active during encoding.

## Key facts

- In animals, memory reactivation is seen as patterns of cell firings during waking exploration that are reiterated in subsequent NREM sleep on a faster timescale. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p2]
- Neural pattern reactivation after learning predicts subsequent gains in memory performance. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p2]
- The strength of neural-level memory replay decays quickly across time within NREM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p4]
- Memory reactivation in rodents occurs not only in the hippocampus but also in sensory cortices, potentially relating to perceptual imagery during dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p4]
- Patterns of neural activity during replay statistically resemble but are not identical to those established during waking experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/wamsley__stickgold_sleep_medicine_clinics_2011.pdf#p7]

## Inferences

- Inference: The presence of memory-related brain activity during sleep does not necessarily imply conscious experience of that activity, and therefore does not automatically indicate a relationship to dream content.
