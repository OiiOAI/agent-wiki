# Rejection of the Seduction Theory

## Summary

In Letter 69 (September 21, 1897), Freud confided to Fliess that he no longer believed in his theory of the traumatic aetiology of the neuroses—the seduction theory. He had observed that every case blamed the father as a pervert, realised there are no indications of reality in the unconscious, and recognised that sexual phantasy seizes upon the theme of parents. This marked a pivotal turning point in psychoanalytic history.

## Key facts

- Freud stopped believing in his theory that actual sexual seduction in childhood was the universal aetiology of neuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p286]
- Freud was surprised by the unexpected frequency of hysteria with the same determinant of paternal perversion, finding such widespread perversity towards children improbable. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p286]
- Freud gained the certain insight that there are no indications of reality in the unconscious, so that one cannot distinguish between truth and cathected phantasy. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p286]
- Freud reflected that in the most deep-going psychosis the unconscious memory does not break through, suggesting that phantasies may seize upon the theme of parents. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p286]
- Freud became prepared to give up two things: the complete resolution of a neurosis and the certain knowledge of its aetiology in childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p287]

## Inferences

- Inference: This rejection of the seduction theory led Freud to focus on the role of phantasies and ultimately on the discovery of infantile sexuality.
