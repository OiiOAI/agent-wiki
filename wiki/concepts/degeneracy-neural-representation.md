# Degeneracy in Neural Representation

## Summary

Degeneracy refers to the principle that different representations need not be separate in the brain, just separable. When the brain constructs a concept on the fly, each instance is created with its own pattern of neurons. More conceptually similar instances have neural patterns closer to one another, and some instances may overlap using some of the same neurons. This means no single instance of an emotion category looks exactly like its statistical summary, but each instance is closer to its own category summary than to other summaries.

## Key facts

- Degeneracy means that different representations need not be separate in the brain, just separable. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p337]
- When the brain constructs a concept for use in a specific situation, each instance is created with its own pattern of neurons. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p337]
- The more conceptually similar the instances are, the more the neural patterns will be close to one another in the default mode network, and some will overlap using some of the same neurons. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p337]
- No single instance of anger, disgust, fear, happiness, or sadness looks exactly like its associated statistical summary, due to degeneracy. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p341]
- Barrett applied Darwin's principle of population thinking to the construction of emotions, showing diverse neuron combinations for each category. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p341]
