# Fundamental Rule of Psychoanalysis

## Summary

The fundamental rule is the technical requirement imposed on the patient in psychoanalytic treatment: to communicate everything that occurs to him without criticism or selection, telling whatever goes through his mind regardless of whether it seems irrelevant, nonsensical, or unpleasant. The analyst imposes this rule at the beginning of treatment and it is the necessary counterpart to the analyst's evenly suspended attention. Obedience to the rule weakens under the dominance of resistances, and the patient's disregard of it marks a turning point in the analysis. The rule's effect is regularly demonstrated when intimate material about third parties arises—the patient's impulse to discretion conflicts with the requirement to say everything.

## Key facts

- The fundamental rule requires the patient to say everything that comes to mind without criticism or selection, including material that seems irrelevant, nonsensical, or unpleasant.
