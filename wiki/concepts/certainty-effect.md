# Certainty Effect

## Summary

The certainty effect is the tendency to overweight outcomes that are practically certain relative to their objective probability, making near-certain gains seem especially attractive (causing risk aversion in gains) and near-certain losses seem especially painful (causing risk seeking to avoid them). It is the strongest psychological force in the weighting function and explains why people accept settlement offers that are less than the expected value of a trial, and why they reject gambles they would accept if the probabilities were more extreme.

## Key facts

- A 2% chance of losing a reward reduces its utility from 100 to 87.1—a 13% reduction—demonstrating that near-certain negative outcomes receive disproportionately high decision weight, much stronger than the overweighting of small probabilities at the other extreme. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p407]
- The certainty effect is the central mechanism in the Allais Paradox: the difference between 100% and 98% is psychologically overwhelming even when the logical difference is small, leading rational economists to violate the substitution axiom of expected utility theory. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p404]
- The structured settlement industry in the United States exploits the certainty effect: people are willing to accept significantly less than the expected value of a legal award in exchange for certainty, and companies profit by providing that certainty at a discount. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p403]
- The certainty effect interacts with loss aversion: a certain loss feels especially terrible and a near-certain loss (90% chance) feels almost as terrible, while the reduction from 90% to 89% probability of loss has almost no psychological impact, creating a 'certainty cliff' at the top of the probability scale. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p407]

## Inferences

- Inference: The certainty effect explains why 'almost certain' medical outcomes are treated as certain by patients and physicians: the psychological difference between a 95% and 100% survival chance is perceived as categorically different, driving treatment choices in ways that expected utility theory cannot explain.
