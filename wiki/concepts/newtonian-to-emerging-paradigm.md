# Paradigm Shift (Scientific)

## Summary

The shift from the Newtonian paradigm to an emerging paradigm represents a movement away from Cartesian, Newtonian, linear, analytical, objectivistic, reductionist, deterministic, mechanical concepts toward focus on change, diversity, evolution, unpredictability, complexity, uncertainty, non-equilibrium and non-linearity. This scientific paradigm shift influenced Boyd's entire conceptual framework.

## Key facts

- The Newtonian paradigm rests on linear principles where proportionality and superposition apply – the whole equals the sum of its parts. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p104]
- Reductionism breaks problems into constituent pieces, solves each separately, then sums results – leading to principles of replication and demonstrable cause and effect. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p104]
- The Newtonian view was deterministic – Newton's equations were considered fixed laws determining all changes; Laplace hypothesized a 'demon' that could predict any future event from adequate comprehension of the present. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p105]
- The Second Law of Thermodynamics introduced the idea of irreversible processes and an 'arrow of time' – entropy measures increasing disorder; isolated systems proceed toward equilibrium (heat death). [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p106]
- Quantum theory revealed wave-particle duality and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle – the more precisely one measures position, the less precisely momentum can be known. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p107]
- Capra noted in 1982 that the world view emerging from modern physics can be characterized as organic, holistic, and ecological – a systems view where the universe is one indivisible dynamic whole whose parts are essentially interrelated. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p110]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd deliberately incorporated the emerging paradigm's concepts into his military theory, applying insights from quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, and systems theory to strategy and warfare.
