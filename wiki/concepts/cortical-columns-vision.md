# Cortical Columns in Visual Cortex

## Summary

Visual cortex is organized into functional columns—vertical aggregates of neurons sharing similar response properties—that systematically tile the cortical surface. A hypercolumn (~1 mm extent) contains the full complement of orientation preferences and ocular dominance combinations for analyzing one point in visual space.

## Key facts

- Ocular-dominance columns reflect segregation of thalamocortical inputs from alternating layers of the LGN representing left and right eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- Orientation columns cycle through the full 180° range every ~750 μm across the cortical surface, with pinwheel structures at singularities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- One full cycle of orientation columns or a full pair of left- and right-eye dominance columns constitutes a hypercolumn, approximately 750-1000 μm in extent. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p555]
- A cortical computational module ~1 mm square contains all possible combinations of orientation preference and left/right-eye dominance (a hypercolumn) plus blobs and interblobs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p559]
- Cytochrome oxidase blobs in V1 contain clusters of color-selective neurons with poor orientation selectivity, representing surface information rather than edges. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p557]
- In area V2, cytochrome oxidase reveals thick stripes (direction/disparity-selective), thin stripes (color-selective), and pale stripes (orientation-selective). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p557]
- Intrinsic-signal optical imaging visualizes functional columns by detecting activity-associated changes in blood flow and hemoglobin oxidation state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p557]

## Inferences

- Inference: Columnar organization minimizes wiring distance between neurons with similar functional properties, economizing brain volume and maximizing processing speed.
