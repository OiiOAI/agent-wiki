# Emotional Intelligence in the Workplace

## Summary

Emotional intelligence in the workplace influences leadership effectiveness, innovation, customer relations, and employee well-being. Research correlates EI skills with creativity, job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and managerial performance. Supervisor EI significantly impacts employee engagement, burnout, and unethical behavior.

## Key facts

- According to a 2018 Gallup survey of 30,628 employees, over half are 'not engaged' at work while 13 percent have miserable experiences, costing businesses $450 billion annually in lost productivity. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p201]
- Research by Stéphane Côté shows emotional intelligence correlates with creativity, innovation, organizational commitment, job satisfaction, customer service ratings, managerial performance, social support, leadership emergence, and job performance. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p204-205]
- Inspiration, respect, and happiness are about 50 percent higher, and frustration, anger, and stress are 30 to 40 percent lower when there is a supervisor with strong emotion skills. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p206]
- Employees who are afraid to speak up or feel forced to do something unethical are significantly more likely to miss work and have greater intentions of leaving their job. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p206]
- Stanford University research suggests over 120,000 deaths annually may be attributable to workplace stress, accounting for up to $190 billion in health care costs. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p202]
- Only 42 percent of top employers believe new graduates are adequately prepared for the workforce with respect to social and emotional skills. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p191]

## Inferences

- Inference: The research suggests that workplace EI training is often inadequate because organizations have not developed comprehensive programs that build lasting habits, creating a gap between recognition of importance and actual skill development.
