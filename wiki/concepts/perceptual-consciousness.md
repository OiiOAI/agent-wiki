# Perceptual Consciousness

## Summary

Perceptual consciousness refers to the brain mechanisms by which sensory stimuli become consciously experienced. Research on visual consciousness has been particularly advanced, revealing that the brain processes visual information through distinct dorsal and ventral streams, and that conscious awareness of stimuli requires interactions between sensory cortices and prefrontal/parietal cortical networks, while nonconscious processing can occur within sensory areas alone.

## Key facts

- The visual system has two processing streams: the ventral stream for object recognition and the dorsal stream for processing stimulus location and motion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p238]
- Blindsight patients with visual cortex damage can respond to stimuli in their blind field without conscious awareness of seeing them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p238]
- When healthy subjects can consciously report seeing a visual stimulus, areas of the visual cortex are activated along with prefrontal cortex and posterior parietal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p239]
- With masked presentations where subjects deny seeing the visual stimulus, only the visual cortex is active—frontal and parietal areas are not. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p239]
- Conscious awareness of stimuli is impaired when neural activity in the prefrontal or parietal cortex is disrupted. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p240]
- The prefrontal and parietal cortices are convergence zones where diverse information about an experience can be integrated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p245]
- Crick and Koch argued that consciousness emerges by way of loops of long-range connections from the visual cortex to the prefrontal cortex and back. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p246]
- Global workspace theory emphasizes that consciousness emerges as a property of global reentrant broadcasting and amplification across a network. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p247]
- Ned Block distinguishes between phenomenal consciousness (occurring in the visual cortex) and access consciousness (involving prefrontal and parietal areas). [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p248]
- David Rosenthal argues that prefrontal areas are needed to create higher-order representations that are accessed and phenomenally experienced. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p248]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dorsal stream ('where/how' pathway) is implicated in blindsight because it receives visual inputs from the visual thalamus and brain stem, allowing processing without the primary visual cortex.
- Inference: Only information that is returned from the prefrontal cortex to the sensory cortex via reentrant connections can be consciously accessed.
- Inference: Consciousness is a product of circuits and systems, not any single brain area.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: It remains debated whether blindsight represents unaccessed phenomenal consciousness or simply nonconscious behavioral control.
- Uncertain: Whether phenomenal experience requires prefrontal cortex involvement or merely cognitive access to phenomenal states is not definitively resolved.
