# Sensitization

## Summary

Sensitization is a form of non-associative learning where an animal learns to respond more strongly to stimuli after experiencing a noxious or important event. In Aplysia, a strong shock to the tail or head produces sensitization of the gill-withdrawal reflex, strengthening synaptic connections between sensory and motor neurons through the action of serotonin.

## Key facts

- Sensitization is produced by applying a strong shock to either the head or the tail, causing the animal to recognize the strong stimulus as noxious. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p202]
- During sensitization, the synaptic connection strengthens as the sensory neuron releases more neurotransmitter. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p213]
- The shock to the tail activates modulatory interneurons that release serotonin, which increases cyclic AMP in sensory neuron terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p234]
- In long-term sensitization, the number of synaptic terminals more than doubles from 1300 to 2700, and the proportion of active synapses increases from 40% to 60%. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p225]
- Five separate pulses of serotonin, simulating five shocks to the tail, strengthened synaptic connection for days and led to growth of new synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p267]
