# Community Feeling

## Summary

Community feeling (Gemeinschaftsgefühl) is presented as the ultimate goal of interpersonal relations in Adlerian psychology. It refers to a sense of others as comrades, an awareness of 'having one's own refuge' within the community. The philosopher explains that Adler's concept of community extends beyond the household, school, workplace, and local society to include all of humanity, the universe, time from past to future, and even inanimate objects. People search for a sense of belonging—that 'it's okay to be here'—which is acquired through active commitment to the community rather than simply being present.

- Community feeling is having a sense of others as comrades and an awareness of 'having one's own refuge' within the community. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p184]
- When Adler refers to community, he treats it as all-inclusive, covering not only nations and all of humanity but also the entire axis of time from the past to the future, and including plants, animals, and even inanimate objects. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p185]
- A sense of belonging is something one acquires through one's own efforts—it is not something one is endowed with at birth. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p193]
- Society's smallest unit is 'you and I.' When there are two people, society emerges in their presence, and community emerges there too. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p186]

## Inferences

- Inference: Community feeling is contrasted with being self-centered, which the philosopher identifies as seeing others as merely 'people who will do something for me.'

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The philosopher acknowledges that Adler himself called this community 'an unattainable ideal,' suggesting practical limits to the concept's application.
