# Compassion Fatigue

## Summary

A state occurring when the cognitive load of repeated exposure to the pain of dissimilar others whose perspective is challenging to take has exhausted the frontal cortex. This explains why people become less helpful to strangers (but not family members) under cognitive load, and why extended engagement with suffering can paradoxically reduce helping behavior.

## Key facts

- Empathy fatigue can be viewed as the state when the cognitive load of repeated exposure to the pain of dissimilar others has exhausted the frontal cortex. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p547]
- Increasing people's cognitive load makes them less helpful to strangers but not to family members. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p547]
- People are more charitable when contemplating one person in need than a group, as illustrated by Mother Teresa's observation: 'If I look at the mass, I will never act. If I look at the one, I will.'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p547]
