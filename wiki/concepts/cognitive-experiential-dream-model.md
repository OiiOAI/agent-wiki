# Cognitive-Experiential Dream Model

## Summary

Developed by Clara Hill at the University of Maryland, the cognitive-experiential dream model integrates ideas from multiple schools of thought (Gestalt, Jungian, phenomenological, psychoanalytic) and has strong research support for its effectiveness. The model consists of three stages: Exploration (using the DRAW acronym: Description, Reexperiencing, Association, Waking-life triggers), Insight (creating meaning by integrating exploration findings), and Action (considering possible life changes).

## Key facts

- The cognitive-experiential dream model was developed by Clara Hill, a psychotherapy researcher and professor of psychology at the University of Maryland–College Park. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p197]
- The model has three stages: exploration (DRAW: Description, Reexperiencing, Association, Waking-life triggers), insight (creating meaning), and action (considering life changes). [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p197]
- Studies show Hill's model contributes to the client-therapist working alliance, increases client involvement in therapy, and enhances understanding of client dynamics. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p198]
- The model can improve clients' well-being, potentially reducing symptoms of anxiety and depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p198]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cognitive-experiential model's effectiveness likely stems from its integration of multiple therapeutic traditions and its focus on empowering the dreamer to discover personal meaning rather than imposing external interpretations.
