# Circle of Competence

## Summary

Circle of Competence is the investment principle that investors should stay within areas where they have genuine understanding and expertise, distinguishing between true mastery (Max Planck) and superficial familiarity (chauffeur who memorized a lecture). Munger applies a basic overlay to limit his investment field to simple, understandable candidates, placing complex or poorly understood investments into 'too tough to understand' baskets.

## Key facts

- Munger tells the story of Max Planck and his chauffeur: the chauffeur memorized a lecture but could not answer a difficult question, representing the difference between true mastery and mere recitation. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p65]
- Munger uses three baskets for investing: yes, no, and too tough to understand. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p64]
- Pharmaceuticals and technology companies often go directly into the 'too tough to understand' basket. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p64]
- Munger emphasizes staying within a well-defined circle of competence as part of intellectual humility. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p73]
- Munger states: 'If you have competence, you know the edge. It wouldn't be a competence if you didn't know where the boundaries lie.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p106]
- He uses three baskets: in, out, and too tough. If they don't have special insight, they put it in the 'too tough' basket. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p106]
- Munger acknowledges they don't feel they have any great advantage in high-tech and avoid it based on personal inadequacies. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p181]
- He notes it is very hard to enlarge one's circle of competence. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p181]
- Munger uses the analogy that if you play games where others have the aptitudes and you don't, you're going to lose. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p181]

## Inferences

- Inference: Recognizing the limits of one's knowledge prevents costly errors that come from overconfident investment decisions outside genuine areas of expertise.
