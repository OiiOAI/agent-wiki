# Parental Investment

## Summary

Parental Investment (PI) is R.L. Trivers's 1972 concept measuring any investment by a parent in an offspring that increases the offspring's chance of survival at the cost of the parent's ability to invest in other offspring. It is measured in units of decreased life expectancy for other children rather than calories or absolute food quantities. The concept serves as a common currency for analyzing resource allocation decisions in reproduction.

## Key facts

- Parental Investment is defined as 'any investment by the parent in an individual offspring that increases the offspring's chance of surviving (and hence reproductive success) at the cost of the parent's ability to invest in other offspring'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p158]
- When a child consumes parental resources (e.g., milk), the PI that consumption represents is measured not in pints or calories, but in units of increased probability that other children of the same parent will die from not receiving those resources. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p158]
- The optimal parental investment strategy balances bearing (producing new offspring) against caring (investing in existing offspring), with natural selection favoring genes that maximize surviving grandchildren rather than merely the number of offspring produced. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p159]
- Parents should prefer saving older children over younger ones in life-or-death choices, because more has already been invested in the older child and the younger child would require additional costly resources to reach the same developmental stage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p159]
- A mother who is equally related to all her children should have no genetic favorites, though she may strategically invest less in runts (smaller, weaker offspring with lower life expectancy) to maximize total surviving offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p159]
