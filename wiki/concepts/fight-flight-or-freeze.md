# Fight, Flight or Freeze Response

## Summary

The Fight, Flight or Freeze (FFF) response is an instinctive survival mechanism located in the Chimp's Jungle Centre. It triggers immediate intense emotional reactions when danger or perceived danger threatens, and is nature's way of ensuring survival. In modern society, this response is often inappropriate for non-life-threatening situations.

## Key facts

- The Fight, Flight or Freeze response is the most important instinct the Chimp possesses, shared across the entire animal kingdom. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p27]
- Fight means taking on the threat, Flight means running away, and Freeze means avoiding or giving in to the threat. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p27]
- Which FFF response the Chimp chooses depends on how vulnerable it feels at the time. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p27]
- Anxiety occurs when the Chimp perceives threat but no decision is made, acting as nature's way of forcing a decision. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p27]
- The FFF response in modern society is often inappropriate because the Chimp still believes it is in the jungle when facing everyday situations like public speaking. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p28]

## Inferences

- Inference: The intensity of the FFF response is typically disproportionate to actual events because it evolved for genuine life-threatening situations.
