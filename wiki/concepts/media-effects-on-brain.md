# Media Effects on the Brain

## Summary

Electronic media—television, video games, the Internet—change brain structure and function through massed practice and repeated stimulation. McLuhan's insight that 'the medium is the message' is supported by neuroscience showing that different media activate different brain areas. Television triggers the orienting response at rates up to one per second, causing continuous orienting response with no recovery and leading to difficulties with slower activities like reading and complex conversation.

## Key facts

- Merzenich stated: 'Our brains are massively remodeled' by exposure to the Internet, reading, television, video games, modern electronics, and contemporary music. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p311]
- A study of over 2,600 toddlers showed that for every hour of TV watched each day, chances of developing serious attentional difficulties at age seven increased by 10 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p311]
- McLuhan argued that each medium reorganizes our mind and brain irrespective of content, stating 'The medium is the message.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p312]
- Brain scan studies by Michael and Just showed that different brain areas are involved in hearing speech versus reading it, and different comprehension centers process heard versus read words. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p313]
- Video games trigger dopamine release (the reward neurotransmitter) in the brain, meeting all conditions for plastic brain map changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p314]
- Typical music videos, action sequences, and commercials trigger orienting responses at a rate of one per second, putting viewers into continuous orienting response with no recovery. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p314]
- Forty-three percent of U.S. children two years or younger watch television daily, and a quarter have TVs in their bedrooms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p311]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The correlation between TV watching and attentional difficulties may not be causal; parents of children with more attentional difficulties might deal with them by putting them in front of television sets.
