# Public Commitment

## Summary

Public commitment is the principle that when individuals make their beliefs or intentions visible to others, they experience increased pressure to maintain consistency with those commitments. This psychological phenomenon occurs because society values consistency, viewing inconsistent individuals as 'fickle, uncertain, pliant, scatterbrained, or unstable' while consistent individuals are seen as 'rational, assured, trustworthy, and sound.'

## Key facts

- Deutsch and Gerard's experiment demonstrated that public commitment produces the strongest adherence to decisions, with written and signed commitments being more powerful than private written commitments. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p80]
- Jury studies showed that hung juries were significantly more frequent when jurors expressed opinions with a visible show of hands rather than by secret ballot. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p80]
- Weight-reduction clinics require clients to write down weight-loss goals and show them to friends, relatives, and neighbors as a commitment device. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p81]
