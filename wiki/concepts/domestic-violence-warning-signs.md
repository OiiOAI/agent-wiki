# Domestic Violence Warning Signs

## Summary

A comprehensive list of 30 pre-incident indicators associated with spousal violence and murder, compiled by Gavin de Becker based on his experience with thousands of cases. The list covers behavioral patterns, control mechanisms, and warning behaviors that may predict future violence.

## Key facts

- The list of reliable pre-incident indicators includes: intuitive feelings of risk, accelerated relationship pace, intimidation as conflict resolution, verbal abuse, threats as control instruments, symbolic violence, prior battering, substance abuse, history of police encounters, financial control, jealousy, refusal to accept rejection, and projection of extreme emotions. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p118]
- Additional warning signs include: minimizing abuse, disproportionate time spent talking about the partner, attempts to enlist friends/relatives, inappropriate surveillance, blaming others, inflexible/resistant behavior, identification with violent people, mood swings, and references to weapons as instruments of power. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p118]
- Spousal homicide is described as America's most predictable serious crime, with 30 warning signs available for prediction and prevention. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p119]
- Approximately 75 percent of spousal murders happen after the woman leaves, meaning estrangement—not argument—begets the worst violence. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p124]
- Most husbands who kill their wives stalk them first, and killing is usually a decision, not a loss of control. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p124]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book argues that domestic violence is a 'long, slow crime' that can be predicted if warning signs are recognized, contrary to popular belief that it strikes without warning.
- Inference: The book suggests that batterers who are most violent show physiological calm (dropping heart rates) during violent episodes, not fury or loss of control.
