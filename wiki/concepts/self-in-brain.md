# Self

## Summary

The self is proposed as a construct generated by the dialogue between thalamus and cortex—a temporally coherent binding event that weaves fractionalized components of external and internal reality into a single construct. It is the centralization of prediction, binding therefore being the substrate of self. The self is an abstraction that exists only as a calculated entity, similar to concepts like 'Uncle Sam.'

## Key facts

- The self is generated by the dialogue between thalamus and cortex; binding events comprise the substrate of self. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p126]
- The thalamocortical system by its hub-like organization allows radial communication of thalamic nuclei with sensory, motor, and associational areas of cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p126]
- Following damage to intralaminar thalamic nuclei, patients are not aware of inputs from intact specific thalamocortical circuits—inputs are received but completely ignored. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p126]
- Prediction must be centralized to one predictive organ; timing mismatches would occur if multiple seats of prediction made competing judgments. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p127]
- The self is an invention of the brain—a complicated eigenvector that exists only as a calculated entity. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p128]

## Inferences

- Inference: The self emerges as an organizational solution to the problem of coordinating prediction across the nervous system, binding disparate brain regions into unified experience.
