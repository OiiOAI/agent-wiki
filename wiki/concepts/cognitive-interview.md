# The Cognitive Interview

## Summary

The Cognitive Interview is an evidence-based interviewing technique developed in the 1980s by Ronald Fisher and Edward Geiselman to improve witness recall without increasing suggestibility. It includes four original components: complete reporting, mental reinstatement of context, varying temporal order, and perspective-taking. Studies show significant gains in witness recall with minimal increase in inaccurate information.

## Key facts

- The Cognitive Interview was developed in the 1980s by cognitive psychologists Ronald Fisher and Edward Geiselman based on findings from controlled memory studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p161]
- The first component involves asking witnesses to report everything about the incident, as police often ask highly specific questions that do not maximize recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
- The second component requires witnesses to mentally reinstate the context or setting in which the incident occurred, which laboratory studies show enhances memory retrieval. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
- Third, witnesses are asked to recall events in different temporal orders—beginning to end and vice versa—which has yielded improved recall in controlled studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
- Finally, witnesses are asked to take different perspectives on an event, such as viewing it from the perpetrator's or victim's perspective. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
- Virtually all experiments comparing the Cognitive Interview to standard police techniques find significant—and sometimes extremely large—gains in witness recall. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
- While the Cognitive Interview can yield greater reporting of inaccurate information, the amount is typically small and there is no evidence it reduces eyewitness accuracy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
- All police forces in England and Wales receive training in the Cognitive Interview and use it routinely when interrogating witnesses. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p162]
