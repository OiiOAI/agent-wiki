# Longing (Sehnsucht)

## Summary

McGilchrist distinguishes between wanting (an impulsion 'from behind' toward an inert target) and longing (an attraction 'from in front' toward something from which one is already not wholly separate). Wanting is linear, unidirectional, and mechanical; longing is holistic, bidirectional, and creates 'betweenness.' Longing is full of mixed emotions—its targets include home, death, the loved one, the warm South, childhood, and the past. It represents movement of faith in uncertainty and is associated with the right hemisphere's world.

## Key facts

- Wanting is a drive like a hydraulic force, a mechanical pressure; longing is more like a magnetic field, an electric attraction. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p505]
- Wanting has a clear view of its target; longing intuits its 'Other.' Wanting is simple and unmixed; longing is full of mixed emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p505]
- Distance in time and space produces longing, which is neither in the subject nor the object but arises between them, and is intrinsically melancholic. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p505]
- Nostalgia (a term coined in the 18th century) was already the basis of Plato's Symposium fable of the divided creature longing to be reunited with its other half. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p505]

## Inferences

- Inference: The targets of longing—home conflated with death, the eternal feminine, childhood, the past—are all associated with right hemisphere functions: autobiographical memory, emotional depth, and temporal perspective.
