# Fog of War (Business Application)

## Summary

The fog of war refers to the detachment from reality that strikes the less agile competitor. By becoming more agile than competitors, you can cause this fog to grow in their minds, decreasing the quality of their decisions and eventually attacking their abilities to make effective decisions altogether. A similar effect, breakdown in quality of energy, is known in physics as entropy. In competitive situations, the less agile competitor acts like a closed system, and the fog begins to grow. Fog plus menace generates frustration and eventually panic.

## Key facts

- Liddell Hart observed of the French in 1940 that 'their timing was too slow to catch up with the changing situations,' and it caused their ability to make decisions to break down. - Signs your strategy is working include: competitor's new products are consistently late, he starts blaming the customer, personnel turnover is high, he becomes more 'Theory X,' and launches witchhunts for 'the cause of the problem.'. - Ernest Hemingway described in A Farewell to Arms how after defeat, Italian officers screened retreating soldiers and executed captains and above—a result of the fog and frustration.
