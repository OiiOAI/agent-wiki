# Multiple Mental Models

## Summary

Multiple Mental Models is a decision-making framework advocated by Charles T. Munger that draws upon concepts from multiple disciplines. Rather than relying on a single model or academic field, practitioners use mental models from economics, psychology, philosophy, engineering, and other fields to form a comprehensive 'latticework' of knowledge for better decision-making.

## Key facts

- Munger's mental models are drawn from every discipline imaginable and recur repeatedly in his talks. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p6]
- Munger's lessons hang together in a coherent 'latticework' of knowledge, available for recall and use when needed. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p5]
- The Multiple Mental Models approach does not focus on 'business portfolio strategy' or 'beta' or 'Cap M' but centers on fundamental truth, human accomplishment, human foibles, and the arduous path to wisdom. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p6]
- One talk demonstrates how diverse mental models can be applied to accomplish building a two-trillion-dollar business. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p5]
- Munger uses approximately one hundred mental models drawn from diverse disciplines for investment analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p57]
- Key examples of his mental models include: redundancy/backup system from engineering, compound interest from mathematics, breakpoint/tipping-moment/autocatalysis from physics and chemistry, modern Darwinian synthesis from biology, and cognitive misjudgment models from psychology. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p57]
- Munger cites biologist Julian Huxley's idea that 'life is just one damn relatedness after another,' requiring mental models to see relatedness and effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p57]
- Munger learned to develop his own multidisciplinary approach after finding that graduate schools would not provide adequate cross-disciplinary training. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p58]
- The Little Red Hen's approach ('Then I'll do it myself') represents Munger's self-education philosophy when professors would not provide appropriate multidisciplinary approaches. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'latticework' metaphor suggests that knowledge from different disciplines should be interconnected like a lattice structure, providing multiple support points for thinking rather than relying on a single pillar.
- Inference: Munger's use of diverse mental models creates a more robust analytical framework than single-discipline approaches, similar to how ecological systems require understanding of multiple interacting factors.
