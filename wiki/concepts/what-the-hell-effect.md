# The What-the-Hell Effect

## Summary

The what-the-hell effect (formally counterregulatory eating) describes how dieters who exceed their calorie limit for the day regard their diet as blown and then binge, consuming far more than the original lapse. Dieters have a fixed mental target for daily calories, and when exceeded, the day is classified as a failure regardless of what else happens. Once this happens, dieters stop counting and become unaware of how much they are eating. This phenomenon was discovered by Peter Herman and Janet Polivy in experiments where dieters who consumed large milkshakes subsequently ate more crackers and cookies than dieters who had consumed nothing.

## Key facts

- Dieters who exceeded their daily calorie limit ate more cookies and crackers than dieters who had not eaten for hours. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p203]
- Once dieters broke their diet and succumbed to the what-the-hell effect, they stopped counting calories and became even less aware than nondieters of what they ate. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p203]
- Virtue cannot resume until tomorrow after a diet is 'blown,' leading dieters to think 'What the hell, I might as well enjoy myself today.'. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p202]
