# Voltage-Gated Ion Channels

## Summary

Voltage-gated ion channels are transmembrane proteins that open or close in response to changes in membrane potential. They are essential for action potential generation and propagation. Na+, K+, and Ca2+ channels share a common architecture of six transmembrane segments (S1–S6) with a P-region forming the selectivity filter. The S4 segment functions as the voltage sensor. Different channel types exhibit distinct activation/inactivation kinetics and pharmacological properties.

## Key facts

- Voltage-gated Na+ channels open rapidly in response to depolarization but then inactivate; K+ channels open more slowly and do not inactivate rapidly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p206]
- Tetrodotoxin selectively blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels; tetraethylammonium blocks voltage-gated K+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p204]
- The S4 segment contains positively charged arginine or lysine residues and acts as the voltage sensor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p217]
- The P-region between S5 and S6 forms the selectivity filter determining ion specificity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p216]
- Voltage-gated K+ channels consist of four subunits, each containing the six transmembrane segments; Na+ and Ca2+ channels have a single large subunit with four homologous repeats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p216]
