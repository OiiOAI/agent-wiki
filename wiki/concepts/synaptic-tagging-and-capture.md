# Synaptic Tagging and Capture

## Summary

Synaptic tagging and capture is a cellular mechanism explaining how gene products synthesized in the cell body can selectively enhance only those synapses that actively participated in learning. Newly synthesized mRNAs and proteins are distributed throughout the neuron by fast axonal transport, but only synapses that have been 'tagged' by prior synaptic activity can productively incorporate these products to strengthen transmission and grow new synaptic connections. The tag involves PKA-dependent phosphorylation and local protein synthesis.

## Key facts

- In Aplysia sensory neurons, five pulses of serotonin applied to one set of synapses produce nuclear gene expression and synthesis of new proteins that are distributed to all synapses; a single pulse of serotonin at a second synapse enables it to capture these products and undergo long-term facilitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1526]
- Synaptic capture requires PKA activity at the receiving synapse; blocking PKA with Rp-cAMPS at synapses that received only one pulse of serotonin completely blocks their ability to capture long-term facilitation from synapses receiving five pulses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1527]
- The late maintenance phase of long-term facilitation (beyond 24 hours) requires local protein synthesis at the activated synapse, whereas the early phase (24 hours) requires nuclear transcription and PKA but not local translation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1527]

## Inferences

- Inference: The concept of synaptic tagging and capture, developed in Aplysia, was independently proposed for the mammalian hippocampus (Frey and Morris), suggesting it may be a general mechanism for synapse-specific long-term plasticity.
