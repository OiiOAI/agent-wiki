# Illusion (Freudian concept)

## Summary

In Freudian psychology, an illusion is distinguished from a delusion in that it may yet be shown to be realizable, whereas an illusion cannot. Religious ideas are classified by Freud as illusions—not errors or lies, but wish-fulfillments that have become detached from reality. An illusion is characterized by the predominance of wishfulfilment over reality-testing, making it psychologically compelling even without empirical foundation.
