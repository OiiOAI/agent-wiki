# Hierarchical Inference in Discrete Time

## Summary

Hierarchical inference in Active Inference involves models where variables evolve at different timescales—slower for higher levels and faster for lower levels. Higher-level states provide empirical priors for lower levels, which generate observable outcomes. This structure is exemplified in language processing where sentences, words, and phonemes exist at different hierarchical levels.

## Key facts

- Hierarchical models include variables that evolve at different timescales: slower for higher levels and faster for lower levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p147]
- Each state at the higher level persists throughout multiple time steps at the lower level, so the duration of higher-level states transcends that of lower-level states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p147]
- Learning models can be considered hierarchical generative models highlighting a separation of timescales between faster inferential dynamics and slower learning dynamics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p150]
