# Multidisciplinary Thinking

## Summary

Munger's approach of learning the big ideas from all major disciplines and using them together as a mental toolbox. He developed this habit in law school, recognizing that 'legal' approaches that consider one thing without its interacting partner are ridiculous. The big ideas carry about 95% of the freight. This approach makes life more fun, more constructive, and wealthier than genetic gifts alone explain.

## Key facts

- Munger learned in law school that 'a legal mind is a mind that considers it feasible and useful, when two things are all twisted up together and interacting, to try to think about one thing without considering the other,' which he recognized as ridiculous. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p434]
- Munger states: 'The really big ideas carry about 95% of the freight, so it wasn't at all hard for me to pick up about 95% of what I needed from all the disciplines.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p435]
- Alfred North Whitehead said civilization advanced only when man 'invented the method of invention,' and one can progress only by learning the method of learning. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p433]
- Cicero said: 'A man who doesn't know what happened before he's born goes through life like a child,' generalized by Munger to include knowing big ideas from all disciplines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p437]
- Broadscale problems, by definition, cross many academic disciplines, making a unidisciplinary attack on them analogous to playing bridge while ignoring all but trump cards. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p326]
- The best and most practical wisdom is elementary academic wisdom, but it must be applied in a multidisciplinary manner, avoiding extreme balkanization into disciplines and subdisciplines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p301]
- Munger proposes a course called 'Remedial Worldly Wisdom' that would include properly taught psychology and other multidisciplinary content. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p288]
- The five helpful notions for problem-solving are: (1) decide big 'no-brainer' questions first, (2) apply numerical fluency, (3) invert (think in reverse), (4) apply elementary multidisciplinary wisdom, and (5) watch for combinations of factors (lollapalooza effects). [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p300]
- Munger had an 'early and extreme multidisciplinary cast of mind' and couldn't stand reaching for a small idea in his own discipline when big ideas were available in other disciplines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p405]
- He was born with a huge craving for synthesis and would 'rag the problem' repeatedly until he achieved understanding. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p405]
- At Caltech at a young age, Munger absorbed the 'fundamental full attribution ethos of hard science' which he applied to soft sciences as well. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p406]
- Munger states: 'If you skillfully follow the multidisciplinary path, you will never wish to come back. It would be like cutting off your hands.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p451]
- He recommends using tools checklist-style because 'you'll miss a lot if you just hope that the right tool is going to pop up unaided whenever you need it.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p412]
- Cicero said that a man who doesn't know what happened before he's born goes through life like a child; Munger generalizes this to include knowing big ideas from all disciplines. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p465]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger's multidisciplinary approach was not just intellectual curiosity but practical problem-solving—he sought to avoid being 'the perfect damn fool' that specialists become when they can't see beyond their own field.
- Inference: Munger learned the fundamental organizing ethos of hard science through elementary physics and math, which he then applied to softer disciplines throughout his life.
- Inference: The multidisciplinary approach works because reality itself is not organized by academic departments.
- Inference: Munger believes the 'big ideas' carry about 95% of the freight, making it not hard to pick up most of what one needs from all disciplines.
- Inference: His multidisciplinary approach made life more fun, more constructive, more helpful to others, and made him richer than genetic gifts alone would explain.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Munger notes the danger of multidisciplinary thinking: knowing more than experts in other fields can cause offense when you see correct answers they miss, creating difficult social dynamics.
