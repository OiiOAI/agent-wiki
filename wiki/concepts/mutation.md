# Mutation

## Summary

Mutations are errors in DNA copying that can alter gene function. Three classical types include point mutations (single nucleotide changes), deletion mutations (loss of nucleotides), and insertion mutations (addition of extra nucleotides).

## Key facts

- A point mutation involves incorrect copying of a single nucleotide and may or may not change the amino acid sequence depending on codon redundancy. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p731]
- A deletion mutation removes one or more nucleotides, potentially frameshifting the entire message downstream or generating gibberish. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p732]
- An insertion mutation duplicates a nucleotide during copying, potentially changing the reading frame and producing a different protein message. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p732]
- Deletion and insertion mutations often have major consequences, usually deleterious, but sometimes produce novel advantageous proteins. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p733]
