# Fusion Pore

## Summary

A fusion pore is a temporary channel that spans the membranes of a synaptic vesicle and the plasma membrane during exocytosis. It forms initially with a conductance of approximately 200 pS (similar to gap junctions) and can rapidly dilate from approximately 5 to 50 nm in diameter, enabling transmitter release before full vesicle collapse.

## Key facts

- The fusion pore starts with a single-channel conductance of approximately 200 pS, similar to gap-junction channels, and rapidly dilates during exocytosis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p388]
- Transmitter can be released through the fusion pore prior to pore expansion and vesicle collapse, as demonstrated by amperometry showing 'foot' signals preceding full fusion events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p388]
- The reversible opening and closing of a fusion pore represents a very rapid method of membrane retrieval, as used in 'kiss-and-run' and 'kiss-and-stay' pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p389]
- The fusion pore diameter rapidly increases from approximately 2 nm to 7-8 nm within 10 ms as the pore dilates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p390]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The circumstances under which small clear vesicles at fast synapses discharge transmitter through a fusion pore, as opposed to full membrane collapse, are uncertain.
