# Moat

## Summary

The Moat concept describes a company's sustainable competitive advantage—the virtual barrier that protects it from competitors. Munger and Buffett seek businesses with deep, widening moats that can endure competitive destruction forces over long periods. Superior companies continuously widen their moats to provide enduring protection against incursions.

## Key facts

- Munger refers to a company's competitive advantage as its 'moat'—the virtual physical barrier it presents against incursions. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p74]
- Buffett states: 'We think in terms of that moat and the ability to keep its width and its impossibility of being crossed as the primary criterion of a great business'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p73]
- Munger assesses competitive advantage in every respect: products, markets, trademarks, employees, distribution channels, and societal trends. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p74]
- Munger and Buffett are focused on 'competitive destruction' forces that siege most companies over the long term. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p75]
- Munger notes: 'Old moats are getting filled in and new moats are harder to predict, so it's getting harder'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p112]
