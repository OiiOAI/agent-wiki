# Ketamine as Antidepressant

## Summary

Ketamine, an NMDA-type glutamate receptor antagonist originally developed as a dissociative anesthetic, has shown rapid antidepressant effects within hours that persist for up to a week after a single intravenous dose. This finding has opened promising new avenues of research focused on glutamate signaling in depression, though ketamine's potential for abuse and psychotic-like side effects limits its direct clinical utility.

## Key facts

- A single intravenous dose of ketamine, which blocks NMDA-type glutamate receptors, produces antidepressant effects within hours that persist for approximately a week. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1466]
- Ketamine was developed as a dissociative anesthetic that distances a person from the experience of their body and can produce psychotic-like symptoms and euphoria. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1466]
- The discovery of ketamine's rapid antidepressant effects has led to promising new avenues of research focused on signaling initiated by NMDA receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1466]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text suggests that because ketamine can produce psychotic-like symptoms and euphoria (making it an abused street drug), it is not likely to prove directly useful as an antidepressant despite its rapid effects, driving research toward understanding the mechanism.
