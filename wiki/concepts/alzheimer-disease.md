# Alzheimer Disease

## Summary

Alzheimer disease (AD) is the most common cause of dementia in the elderly, characterized by progressive impairment of memory and cognitive faculties. The disease brain exhibits atrophy, extracellular amyloid plaques containing Aβ peptides derived from amyloid precursor protein (APP), and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles containing hyperphosphorylated tau protein. Key genetic risk factors include mutations in APP and presenilin genes (familial AD) and the ApoE4 allele (sporadic AD).

## Key facts

- Alzheimer disease affects approximately one-eighth of people older than 65 years, with the number of affected individuals expected to triple in the next 25 years. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1386]
- AD is characterized by three pathological hallmarks: brain atrophy, extracellular amyloid plaques containing Aβ peptides, and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles containing hyperphosphorylated tau. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1386]
- Amyloid plaques are formed from Aβ peptides (Aβ40 and Aβ42) generated by proteolytic cleavage of amyloid precursor protein (APP) by β- and γ-secretases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1388]
- Neurofibrillary tangles contain aggregates of hyperphosphorylated tau protein, which normally stabilizes microtubules but loses this function when phosphorylated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1391]
- The ApoE4 allele is the most significant genetic risk factor for sporadic late-onset AD, present in 40-50% of AD patients compared to a few percent of the general population. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1393]
- Pittsburgh compound B (PIB) enables visualization of amyloid plaques in living patients using positron emission tomography (PET). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1394]
