# Jigsaw Classroom

## Summary

The jigsaw classroom is a cooperative learning approach developed by Elliot Aronson where students work in teams with each member possessing only one piece of information needed to complete a task. This structure creates mutual interdependence and has been shown to reduce prejudice and increase cross-group friendships in desegregated classrooms.

## Key facts

- In jigsaw classrooms, students are divided into teams where each student teaches one piece of the material, requiring cooperation for all to succeed. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p166]
- Compared to traditional competitive classrooms, jigsaw learning produced significantly more friendship and less prejudice between ethnic groups. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p166]
- Self-esteem, liking for school, and test scores improved for minority students in jigsaw classrooms. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p166]
- White students also benefited, with increased self-esteem and liking for school while maintaining comparable test performance. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p166]

## Inferences

- Inference: The jigsaw approach succeeds because it transforms potential competitors into necessary allies, replacing the competitive classroom dynamics that foster ethnic hostility with cooperative ones that build cross-group relationships.
