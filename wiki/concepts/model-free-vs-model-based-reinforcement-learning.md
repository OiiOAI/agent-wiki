# Model-Free vs Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

## Summary

Model-free reinforcement learning algorithms update value functions entirely according to the difference between expected and actually received reward (reward-prediction error), while model-based algorithms maintain a dynamic model of the environment and can update value functions more quickly when the environment changes. Humans and primates employ a mixture of both learning algorithms during social decision-making tasks.

## Key facts

- In model-free reinforcement learning, value functions are updated entirely according to the difference between reward expected from current value functions and reward actually received, referred to as reward-prediction error. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p220]
- Model-free reinforcement learning algorithms are relatively inflexible; when environment properties change abruptly, entire value functions must be relearned gradually, analogous to habit learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p220]
- Model-based reinforcement learning maintains a dynamic model of the environment, updated through experience, allowing value functions to be updated more quickly when the environment changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p220]
- Human choice behaviors during repeated social interactions display features predicted by both model-free and model-based reinforcement learning, suggesting a mixture of both algorithms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p227]
- Prediction error signals derived from model-free and model-based reinforcement learning algorithms are colocalized in the ventral striatum during repeated games. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p227]
- The reliability of recursive inference about possible strategies of other decision-makers during social decision-making is reflected in the medial frontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p229]

## Inferences

- Inference: The colocalization of model-free and model-based signals in ventral striatum suggests this area updates value functions regardless of the exact learning algorithm used.
- Inference: The finding that model-free and model-based signals are in the same brain region implies that both algorithms may compete or be arbitrated at higher-level regions like lateral prefrontal cortex.
