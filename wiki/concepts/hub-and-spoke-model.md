# Hub-and-Spoke Model of Innovation

## Summary

An innovation architecture combining soundproofed private offices (spokes) connected to shared common areas (hubs), supporting both isolated deep thinking and serendipitous collaborative encounters.

## Key facts

- The hub-and-spoke model combines soundproofed offices connected to large common areas, supporting both deep work and serendipitous encounters. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p78]
- This model was deployed in MIT's Building 20 and Bell Labs, both of which used private offices connected by shared long hallways. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p78]
- Bell Labs' design featured long hallways where traveling without encountering acquaintances and ideas was almost impossible. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p77]
- MIT professors demanded soundproofing gaskets in the Stata Center despite Frank Gehry's open design, prioritizing the ability to close themselves off. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p78]

## Inferences

- Inference: The hub-and-spoke model separates the pursuit of serendipitous encounters from efforts to think deeply, optimizing each separately rather than mixing them into a 'sludge.'
