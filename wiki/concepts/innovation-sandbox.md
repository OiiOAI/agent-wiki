# Innovation Sandbox

## Summary

An innovation sandbox is a mechanism for empowering innovation teams openly within established organizations. It contains the impact of new innovations while not constraining the startup team's methods. Key rules include: any team can create split-test experiments affecting only sandboxed parts; one team must see experiments through end-to-end; experiments have time and customer-count limits; and teams use standard actionable metrics for evaluation.

## Key facts

- The innovation sandbox contains the impact of the new innovation but does not constrain the methods of the startup team. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p242]
- Any team can create a split-test experiment affecting only sandboxed parts of the product, certain customer segments, or certain territories. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p242]
- No experiment can run longer than a specified amount of time (usually a few weeks for simple features, longer for disruptive innovations) and cannot affect more than a specified percentage of the company's mainstream customer base. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p243]
- Every experiment must be evaluated on the basis of a single standard report of five to ten actionable metrics, and every team working inside the sandbox must use the same metrics to evaluate success. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p243]
- The sandbox allows customers to be considered real, with the innovation team attempting to establish long-term relationships rather than treating it as a mere concept test. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p243]

## Inferences

- Inference: The innovation sandbox concept was demonstrated by the SnapTax team within Intuit, which successfully created a startup within the larger company using this 'island of freedom' approach.
