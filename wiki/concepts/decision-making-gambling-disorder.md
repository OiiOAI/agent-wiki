# Decision-Making in Gambling Disorder

## Summary

Gambling disorder represents a behavioral addiction characterized by impaired control over gambling despite negative consequences, driven by an exaggeration of multiple psychological distortions characteristic of human decision-making and underpinned by dysfunction in neural circuitry subserving reinforcement learning and value-based choice.

## Key facts

- Modern commercial gambling is structured with a 'house edge' ensuring consistent profit for casinos; slot machines typically have a return-to-player value between 85-95%. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p339]
- Gambling disorder affects approximately 0.2-5.3% of adults depending on diagnostic criteria, with 78% of adults reporting lifetime gambling participation in North America. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p339]
- Unlike dopamine D2/3 receptor decreases seen in substance addictions, pathological gambling in the general population shows intact striatal D2/3 receptor levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p293]
- Gambling is associated with increased dopamine release in the ventral striatum, similar to other addictions, with enhanced responses to gambling-related cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p294]

## Inferences

- Inference: The intact D2/3 receptor levels in pathological gambling contrast with substance use disorders, suggesting potentially distinct neurobiological mechanisms despite overlapping behavioral presentations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The relationship between individual gambling-related cognitive distortions (illusion of control, gambler's fallacy) and underlying neural dysfunction requires further investigation.
