# The Troop

## Summary

The Troop represents a small band of people who help, nurture, and develop you, and whom you can trust to stand by you and protect you. Both the Human and Chimp need a troop but for different reasons. The Chimp needs a troop for safety and survival (following chimpanzee instinct), while the Human wants a troop based on shared values like integrity, honesty, and selflessness. The problem arises when the Chimp misinterprets the Human's societal values and tries to include everyone in its troop, making it vulnerable to attacks from those who are not truly supportive.

## Key facts

- The Troop Moon represents the people that support you and is critical to stabilise how you work with everybody else outside the troop. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p104]
- The Chimp selects troop members based on what they can offer it, or on what it can offer them for Chimp fulfilment, often based on looks and superficial qualities. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p107]
- The Human selects troop members based on significant qualities found in the Humanity Centre such as integrity, honesty, selflessness, remorse, positive outlook, and sense of humour. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p108]
- The troop drive is the main factor behind beliefs such as 'I must please everybody', 'I need to prove myself all of the time', and 'I mustn't fail anything'. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p106]
- The Human misleads the Chimp by saying society's values mean everyone should be included, but this leaves the Chimp vulnerable because people often act in Chimp mode. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p106]
- The estimate for the number of people who have psychopathic brain patterns is around 1 in 150. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p93]
