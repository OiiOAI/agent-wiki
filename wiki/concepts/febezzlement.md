# Febezzlement

## Summary

Febezzlement is a term coined by Charlie Munger to describe the functional equivalent of embezzlement. It refers to wealth stripped away through layers of unnecessary investment managers and consultants—money that appears to be earned but actually represents a disguised wealth effect from rising stock prices that would not exist without market appreciation.

## Key facts

- Munger coined the term 'febezzlement' to stand for the functional equivalent of embezzlement in investment management practices. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p352]
- Febezzlement occurs when foundations waste three percent of assets per year in unnecessary investment costs while still feeling richer, and the recipients think they are earning income. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p352]
- The term is derived from John Kenneth Galbraith's concept of 'bezzle'—the increase in undisclosed embezzlement in any period. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p351]

## Inferences

- Inference: Febezzlement differs from traditional embezzlement because it is not self-limiting; the process can expand indefinitely by feeding on itself as long as markets rise.
