# Febezzlement

## Summary

Febezzlement is a term coined by Charlie Munger to describe the functional equivalent of embezzlement. It refers to wealth stripped away through layers of unnecessary investment managers and consultants—money that appears to be earned but actually represents a disguised wealth effect from rising stock prices that would not exist without market appreciation.

## Key facts

- Munger coined the term 'febezzlement' to stand for the functional equivalent of embezzlement in investment management practices. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p352]
- Febezzlement occurs when foundations waste three percent of assets per year in unnecessary investment costs while still feeling richer, and the recipients think they are earning income. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p352]
- The term is derived from John Kenneth Galbraith's concept of 'bezzle'—the increase in undisclosed embezzlement in any period. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p351]
- If a foundation wastes three percent of assets per year in unnecessary investment costs while the portfolio is rising, it still feels richer despite the waste, while the people getting the wasted three percent think they are virtuously earning income. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p371]
- Febezzlement creates a disguised wealth effect from rising stock prices—the situation functions like undisclosed embezzlement without being self-limited, and the process can expand by feeding on itself. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p371]
- The typical mutual fund investor gained 7.25 percent per year in a fifteen-year period when the average stock fund gained 12.8 percent per year—a real performance lag of over five percent of assets per year. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p373]
- Galbraith's 'bezzle' is the increase in any period of undisclosed embezzlement, which has powerful stimulating effect on spending because the embezzler spends more while the employer spends as before. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p369]
- Munger coined 'febezzlement' from 'functional equivalent' and 'embezzlement' to describe economic activities that operate like embezzlement. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p443]
- Galbraith's observation was that undisclosed embezzlement stimulates the economy because the victim thinks he's rich and spends, while the thief has new purchasing power. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p442]
- Munger identifies billions wasted in investment management as the functional equivalent of undisclosed embezzlement, where advisers sell detriment for money. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p443]
- Derivative activities contain large febezzlement effects and some ordinary embezzlement effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p452]

## Inferences

- Inference: Febezzlement differs from traditional embezzlement because it is not self-limiting; the process can expand indefinitely by feeding on itself as long as markets rise.
- Inference: Munger quitclaims this idea to any graduate student with independent means who might pursue it as a thesis topic.
