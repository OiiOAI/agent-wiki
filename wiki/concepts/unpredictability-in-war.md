# Unpredictability in war

## Summary

Unpredictability in war is caused by interaction, friction, and chance according to Beyerchen's analysis of Clausewitz. Clausewitz understood that war involves an animate object that reacts, producing not single reactions but dynamic interactions and anticipations that pose fundamental problems for any predictive theory. Such patterns can be theorized only in qualitative and general terms, not in the specific detail needed for prediction.

## Key facts

- The core cause of analytical unpredictability in war is the very nature of interaction itself, according to Beyerchen's analysis of Clausewitz. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p171]
- War is not an exercise of the will directed at inanimate matter; in war, the will is directed at an animate object that reacts, producing dynamic interactions and anticipations. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p171]
- Interactions at every scale within armies and between adversaries amplify micro-causes and produce unexpected macro-effects, showing interaction between the tactical and strategic level. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p172]
- Since interaction is intrinsic to the nature of war, it cannot be eliminated, and the precise knowledge needed to anticipate the effects of interaction is unattainable. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p172]

## Inferences

- Inference: Unpredictability due to chance is unavoidable in war because we can never recover the precise initial conditions even of known developments in past wars, much less developments in current wars distorted by the fog of uncertainty.
