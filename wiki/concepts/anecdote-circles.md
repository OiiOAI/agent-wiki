# Anecdote Circles

## Summary

Anecdote circles are a narrative technique developed by Tony Quinlan and Ron Donaldson for engaging employees to capture narrative fragments. They allow exploration of the current environment with diverse people and views, supporting emergence of ideas without premature assumptions.

## Key facts

- Tony Quinlan and Ron Donaldson used anecdote circles with Pfizer to engage employees and capture narrative fragments. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p145]
- In anecdote circles, someone tells a narrative about their work environment, others contribute theirs, and patterns emerge from shared narratives. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p145]
- At Pfizer, anecdote circles revealed that product knowledge was secondary to sales representatives' attitude and confidence in their product. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p145]
- Anecdote circles encourage people to share descriptive anecdotes from their working lives, ideally with diverse groups and multiple facilitators to ensure a range of voices are heard. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p284]
- Two effective prompts for anecdote circles are: 'Think back to your last week at work, think of one story from your experience when things were made either easier, or more challenging to work here' and 'Which one story from your last week would you share with someone who does not work here?'. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p284]
- Anecdote circles create opportunities for collective interpretation of the meaning of events and experiences in the workplace, with the potential to allow innovative, contextually relevant practices to emerge. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p285]
