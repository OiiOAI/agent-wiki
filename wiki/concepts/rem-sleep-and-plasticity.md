# REM Sleep and Plasticity

## Summary

REM sleep is required for plastic brain development and enhancement of emotional memory retention. Research shows that sleep deprivation reduces neuronal size in the visual cortex, while REM sleep helps the hippocampus convert short-term memories into long-term ones, facilitating structural brain change.

## Key facts

- Scores of studies show that sleep helps consolidate learning and memory and effects plastic change, with 'sleeping on a problem' improving performance the next day. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p244]
- Marcos Frank's team showed that kittens deprived of sleep had less plastic change in their brain maps than kittens that got full sleep, demonstrating that sleep enhances neuroplasticity during critical periods. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p244]
- Gerald Marks found that kittens deprived of REM sleep had smaller neurons in their visual cortex, suggesting REM sleep is necessary for normal neuronal growth. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p244]
- REM sleep is particularly important for enhancing our ability to retain emotional memories and for allowing the hippocampus to turn short-term memories into long-term ones, leading to structural brain change. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p244]
- Infants spend many more hours in REM sleep than adults, and it is during infancy that neuroplastic change occurs most rapidly, with REM sleep required for plastic brain development. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p244]
- Each day in analysis, Mr. L. worked on core conflicts and traumas, and at night dream evidence showed his brain reinforcing the learning and unlearning he had done. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p244]
