# Splitting of Consciousness

## Summary

Splitting of consciousness (Spaltung) refers to the coexistence within the same individual of normal conscious mental life alongside large complexes of unconscious ideas and processes. This fundamental phenomenon explains how hysterical patients can remain unaware of the psychical origins of their symptoms while unconscious ideas continue to operate as contemporary agents, producing symptoms years after the original trauma.

## Key facts

- Large complexes of ideas and involved psychical processes with important consequences remain completely unconscious in many patients and coexist with conscious mental life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p222]
- The patient was in the peculiar situation of knowing and at the same time not knowing regarding her feelings for her brother-in-law—a situation in which a psychical group was cut off. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p147]
- Hysterics suffer mainly from reminiscences; the determining process continues to operate for years as a directly releasing cause, like a foreign body. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p221]

## Inferences

- Inference: The splitting is maintained by the conversion mechanism: when the incompatible idea is repressed and its affect converted to somatic symptoms, the idea loses its emotional intensity and is no longer perceived as significant, reinforcing the amnesia.
