# Placebo Effect

## Summary

The placebo effect refers to symptomatic relief obtained without clinically active ingredients. Placebo-induced changes have been shown to reach far down, altering responses at the level of the spinal cord. The effect depends on the patient's estimate of the power of the intervention—inert substances delivered by syringe are typically more effective than pills. A 2013 UK survey found that 97 percent of family doctors had at some time prescribed a placebo to a patient.

## Key facts

- Placebo-induced changes have been shown to reach far down, altering responses even at the level of the spinal cord, indicating they are not simply superficial effects on high-level reporting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p47]
- The higher the patient's estimate of the power of the intervention, the greater its effect—inert substances delivered by syringe are typically more effective than those delivered by pill. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p47]
- A large 2013 survey in the UK found that 97 percent of family doctors had at some time prescribed a placebo (pure or impure) to a patient. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p48]
- Doctors sometimes prescribe 'impure placebos'—drugs that have clinically active ingredients but ones not relevant to the patient's specific symptoms—which are often very effective because they activate potent expectations of relief. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p48]
- The more arduous the treatment, the more likely it will be effective—sham surgery outperforms injections, which outperform pills. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p121]
- In 1959, patients who had sham internal mammary artery ligation reported the same immediate relief from chest pain as those who had the actual procedure, lasting at least three months. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p121]
- Arthroscopic knee surgery was no more effective than sham surgery in a two-year study of pain and walking ability. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p122]
- Behavioral economist Dan Ariely found that the more people pay for pills, the more effective they tend to be. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p123]
- Yellow pills are most effective for depression, green for anxiety, white for ulcers, and red for energy. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p123]
- Open-label placebos—where patients know they are taking placebos—still reduce symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p127]
- A cancer patient with orange-sized tumors responded to a 'double-strength' medication that was actually a placebo, with tumors disappearing and reappearing based on what he was told about his treatment. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p125]

## Inferences

- Inference: Placebo effects demonstrate that conscious and unconscious expectations of relief are impacting the whole web from which experience is constructed.
- Inference: The placebo effect may be available to all people, not just those with specific genetic predispositions, since mind-body unity suggests any mental change manifests physically.
