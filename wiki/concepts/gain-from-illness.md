# Gain from Illness

## Summary

The gain from illness refers to the advantages the ego obtains from the neurosis. Primary gain is internal — the avoidance of psychic conflict through symptom formation. Secondary gain involves external advantages like sympathy, care, or avoidance of responsibilities. The ego may make a 'bad bargain' by accepting neurosis.

## Key facts

- By escaping into a neurosis the ego obtains a certain internal 'gain from illness' — settling the conflict by constructing a symptom is the most convenient way out and agreeable to the pleasure principle [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5141]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5141]
- External gain from illness occurs when the neurosis brings real advantages: for example, a woman may use illness as a weapon against a brutal husband, finding a helper in her doctor and forcing the husband to allow her freedom from married oppression [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5141]. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5141]
- The ego would like to free itself from the unpleasure of symptoms without giving up the gain from illness, but this is impossible [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5142].
