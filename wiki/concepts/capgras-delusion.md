# Capgras Delusion

## Summary

Capgras delusion is the belief that a loved one has been replaced by an impostor. Research suggests this delusion is triggered when bodily responses to a loved one's presence cease unexpectedly. These missing responses act as evidence that the predictive brain must explain, causing visual and auditory experiences to become subtly reconfigured in ways that seem to confirm the delusional belief.

## Key facts

- Capgras delusion seems to be triggered when bodily responses to a loved one's presence cease unexpectedly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p104]
- Missing bodily responses such as raised heartbeat or increased galvanic skin response when in the loved one's presence are not consciously registered but their absence acts as evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p104]
- The subtly altered visual and auditory experiences that follow put the Capgras patient in the strange position of seeming to have gathered additional perceptual evidence that something important has changed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p105]

## Inferences

- Inference: This plausibly sets the scene for the emergence of the full delusional belief that the loved one has been replaced by a similar-looking but imperfect impostor [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p105]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific neural mechanisms underlying the failure of bodily response to loved ones in Capgras patients require further investigation
