# Age-Appropriate Boundary Training

## Summary

Age-Appropriate Boundary Training is the process of teaching children boundaries at each developmental stage, from infancy through adolescence. The framework presented in Boundaries describes specific tasks and appropriate boundary interventions for each age group, recognizing that children must first develop secure attachment before they can learn separation and limits.

## Key facts

- Birth to Five Months: The primary task is establishing attachment with the primary caregiver; the infant needs security and a sense of belonging rather than limit-setting at this stage. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p195]
- Five to Ten Months (Hatching): Parents should encourage attempts at separateness while remaining anchors the child clings to, allowing the child to explore people and objects beyond the primary attachment figure. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p196]
- Ten to Fifteen Months (Practicing): Children develop the ability to understand and respond to 'no'; parents should rejoice in the baby's no as it represents the beginning of taking responsibility for life. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p197]
- Eighteen to Thirty-six Months: Goals include the ability to be emotionally attached without losing self, saying appropriate nos without fear of losing love, and accepting nos from others without withdrawing emotionally. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p197]
- Three to Five Years: Children enter sex-role development, identifying with the same-sex parent and developing competitive feelings; parents must allow identification while keeping clear lines between parent and child. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p199]
- Six to Eleven Years (Latency): Children prepare for adolescence by learning task orientation through schoolwork and play, connecting with same-sex peers, and learning delay of gratification, goal orientation, and time budgeting. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p199]
- Eleven to Eighteen Years (Adolescence): The 'de-parenting' process begins; teens need to set their own relational, scheduling, values, and money boundaries while suffering real-life consequences for crossing them. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p200]
