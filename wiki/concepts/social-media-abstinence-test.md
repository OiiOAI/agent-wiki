# Social Media Abstinence Test

## Summary

A strategy for evaluating social media services by completely abstaining for 30 days and then asking whether the time would have been notably better with the service and whether people cared about the absence.

## Key facts

- The strategy asks you to ban yourself from all social media services for thirty days without formally deactivating or announcing the absence. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p120]
- After thirty days, ask: (1) Would the last thirty days have been notably better with this service? (2) Did people care that you weren't using it?. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p121]
- If the answer is 'no' to both questions, quit the service permanently; if 'yes,' return to using it. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p121]
- The average Twitter user has only 208 followers, revealing that the sense of importance generated by social media is often a 'simulacrum' rather than genuine impact. [raw/books/productivity/Deep Work_ Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World - Cal Newport.pdf#p122]

## Inferences

- Inference: The abstinence test replaces marketing-driven fear of missing out with reality: most social media services are not as important as they claim to be.
