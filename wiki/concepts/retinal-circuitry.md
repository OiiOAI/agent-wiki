# Retinal Circuitry

## Summary

The retina contains a layered neural circuit that transforms light patterns into neural signals through interactions between five major cell types: photoreceptors (rods and cones), bipolar cells, horizontal cells, amacrine cells, and ganglion cells. This circuit performs center-surround spatial filtering, separates ON and OFF pathways, and implements temporal filtering.

## Key facts

- The retina has five distinct cell types arranged in three cellular layers separated by two synaptic layers: outer nuclear layer (photoreceptors), inner nuclear layer (bipolar, horizontal, amacrine cells), and ganglion cell layer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p570]
- Horizontal cells have broadly arborizing dendrites in the outer plexiform layer and are electrically coupled to each other through gap junctions, measuring average illumination over broad regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p583]
- Horizontal cell feedback to photoreceptor terminals creates antagonistic surrounds, contributing to center-surround receptive field organization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p583]
- ON bipolar cells use metabotropic glutamate receptors (mGluR6) that close cation channels; OFF bipolar cells use ionotropic AMPA/kainate receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p583]
- Amacrine cells form reciprocal inhibitory synapses with bipolar cell terminals and provide lateral inhibition in the inner retina, contributing to temporal filtering and antagonistic surrounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p583]
- Rod signals enter the cone pathway through AII amacrine cells, which provide excitatory input to ON cone bipolar cells and glycinergic inhibition to OFF cone bipolar cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p587]

## Inferences

- Inference: The complex interplay between excitatory and inhibitory circuits allows the retina to extract specific features (edges, motion, color) while discarding redundant information (uniform illumination).
