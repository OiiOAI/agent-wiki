# Consciousness and Decision-Making

## Summary

This chunk proposes that consciousness can be understood through the lens of decision-making. States of knowing and awareness are characterized as provisional commitments or affordances. Consciousness may represent a meta-level decision to report or communicate internal states, bridging the gap between unconscious processing and conscious experience.

## Key facts

- States of knowledge resemble perceptual decisions in having temporal persistence and freedom from immediacy, representing provisional commitments to propositions based on evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1409]
- Conscious awareness may result from a decision to commit to the possibility of reporting, analogous to how the brain decides to execute other motor actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1413]
- Nonconscious processing can perform complex evaluations (like the decision to awaken to a baby's cry) before reaching conscious awareness, suggesting consciousness is not required for sophisticated decision-making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1412]
- The brain may entertain the possibility of reporting as an affordance, similar to how it considers possibilities for looking, reaching, or grasping. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1413]
