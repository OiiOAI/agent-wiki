# Fear Conditioning and the Amygdala

## Summary

Learned (conditioned) fear is an adaptive form of associative learning in which a neutral stimulus becomes associated with a noxious unconditioned stimulus. In mammals, fear conditioning is centered on the amygdala, which receives direct and indirect input pathways for both conditioned (tone) and unconditioned (shock) stimuli. NMDA receptor-dependent LTP in the lateral nucleus of the amygdala underlies fear memory formation, with CREB expression in amygdala neurons determining which cells are recruited for long-term fear memory.

## Key facts

- In Pavlovian fear conditioning, a neutral tone (CS) paired with a foot shock (US) becomes capable of eliciting conditioned fear responses (freezing) through synaptic plasticity in the lateral nucleus of the amygdala. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1530]
- Fear conditioning recruits LTP in the lateral amygdala; the size of electrically induced LTP is reduced in amygdala slices from animals previously trained for fear, suggesting fear learning occludes further LTP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1532]
- Neurons with high basal levels of CREB expression in the amygdala are selectively recruited for long-term fear memory; if these high-CREB neurons are ablated after learning, the fear memory is blocked. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1531]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conservation of cAMP-PKA-CREB pathway involvement in fear conditioning across flies, Aplysia, and mammals suggests this molecular cascade represents an evolutionarily ancient mechanism for associative memory storage.
