# Hall and Van de Castle Dream Scoring System

## Summary

The Hall and Van de Castle (HVC) dream scoring system, developed in the 1960s by Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle, is the most well-known, best-validated, and most extensive dream scoring system ever designed. Based on their analysis of 1,000 dream reports from 100 male and 100 female college students, they published 'The Content Analysis of Dreams' in 1966, describing rules for scoring characters, settings, objects, actions, social interactions, and emotions. The system allows for an immensely detailed and mostly objective coding of dream reports, laying the foundation for fifty years of scientific studies of dream content.

## Key facts

- The Hall and Van de Castle dream scoring system was developed in the 1960s by Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p139]
- Hall and Van de Castle analyzed 1,000 dream reports collected from 100 male and 100 female college students. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p140]
- Their 1966 book 'The Content Analysis of Dreams' described rules for scoring characters, settings, objects, actions, social interactions, and emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p140]
- The HVC system allows for scoring aggressive social interactions based on who initiated the aggression, whether it was reciprocated, and its severity ranging from covert hostility to murder. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p140]
- DreamBank is an online database of over 20,000 dreams, many already scored according to the HVC system. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p141]
- Aggressive social interactions occur in a slightly greater proportion of people's dreams (46 percent) than do friendly interactions (40 percent). [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p161]
- Men's dreams contain twice as many male characters as female characters, while women's dreams contain an equal proportion of male and female characters. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p161]
