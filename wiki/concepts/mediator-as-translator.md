# Mediation

## Summary

In NVC mediation, the mediator serves as a translator who reformulates each party's message so it can be understood by the other. The purpose of interrupting and redirecting is to restore the process of making observations, identifying feelings, connecting feelings with needs, and making doable requests using clear, positive action language.

## Key facts

- In mediation, the mediator's role is that of a translator—translating each party's message so as to be understood by the other. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p243]
- When interrupting during mediation, the mediator checks that the speaker feels accurately translated, and while the mediator translates many messages even if guessing, the speaker remains the final authority on accuracy. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p243]
- The purpose of interrupting in mediation is to restore the process of making observations, identifying and expressing feelings, connecting feelings with needs, and making doable requests. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p244]
- One method for conflicts where parties are unwilling to meet uses audio recording: the mediator works with each party separately while playing the role of the other side. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p244]
