# Existential Risk

## Summary

Existential risk refers to threats that could cause humanity's extinction or permanent civilizational collapse. Hawkins identifies two fundamental systemic risks associated with the human brain: the older parts of the brain creating selfish and shortsighted behaviors, and the neocortex forming false beliefs that lead to actions against long-term interests.

## Key facts

- The Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists has maintained the Doomsday Clock since 1947 to highlight proximity to making Earth uninhabitable. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p175]
- The Doomsday Clock was expanded in 2007 to include climate change as a second potential cause of self-inflicted extinction. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p175]
- The first fundamental risk is the old brain creating selfish and shortsighted behaviors combined with globe-altering technologies created by the neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p176]
- The second fundamental risk is that the neocortex can form false beliefs about fundamental aspects of the world, leading to actions against long-term interests. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p176]
- Hawkins argues the combination of the old brain and viral false beliefs is a greater existential risk than AI. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: Nuclear weapons and climate change represent the first two major human-created existential threats, both emerging within the last 100 years.
