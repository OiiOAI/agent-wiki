# Ocular Dominance Plasticity

## Summary

Ocular dominance plasticity refers to the experience-dependent remodeling of connections in the visual cortex based on visual input from each eye. Hubel and Wiesel's pioneering studies showed that monocular deprivation during a critical period after birth leads to expansion of the open eye's ocular dominance columns at the expense of the deprived eye, with corresponding loss of binocular responsiveness in cortical neurons. This plasticity depends on competitive interactions between inputs from the two eyes driven by correlated activity patterns.

## Key facts

- Hubel and Wiesel demonstrated that monocular deprivation during early postnatal life permanently shifts ocular dominance in the visual cortex, with most cortical neurons losing responsiveness to the deprived eye. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1260]
- The structural basis of ocular dominance plasticity involves expansion of ocular dominance columns representing the open eye and shrinkage of those representing the closed eye. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1261]
- Following monocular deprivation in mice, input from the closed eye weakens rapidly through long-term depression (LTD)-like mechanisms, followed by compensatory strengthening of input from the open eye through homeostatic plasticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1267]
- Enhancing GABAergic signaling advances the critical period for ocular dominance plasticity, while delaying GABA signaling delays it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1268]
