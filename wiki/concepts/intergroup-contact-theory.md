# Intergroup Contact Theory

## Summary

The theory that intergroup tensions are reduced when members of different groups interact under appropriate conditions. While contact can elevate hostilities under certain circumstances (unequal treatment, ambiguous boundaries, symbolic displays), sustained contact in equal-status, cooperative settings with shared goals typically decreases prejudice substantially.

## Key facts

- Intergroup contact worsens things when the two groups are treated unequally or are unequal in number, where the smaller group is surrounded, where intergroup boundaries are ambiguous, and when groups vie to display symbols of their sacred values. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p642]
- A 2006 meta-analysis of approximately 500 studies with over 250,000 subjects from 38 countries found that beneficial effects of intergroup contact were roughly equal for group differences in race, religion, ethnicity, or sexual orientation. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p642]
- Contact between a traditionally dominant group and a subordinate minority usually decreases prejudice more in the former; the latter have higher thresholds. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p642]
