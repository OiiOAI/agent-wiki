# Complex Facilitation

## Summary

Complex facilitation is an approach to guiding groups through complex issues using Cynefin principles. It contrasts with traditional facilitation by avoiding predetermined outcomes, embracing ambiguity, and enabling emergence rather than directing results. Key methods include anecdote circles, hexagonal post-it clustering, and distributed cognition design.

## Key facts

- Complex facilitation is defined as 'the facilitation of complex methods to make sense of complex issues in order to take action.'. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p359]
- Traditional facilitation methods are useful in ordered systems where outcomes are known, but are far less valuable in complex systems. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p359]
- Design principles of complex facilitation include: avoiding pattern entrainment, designing for distributed cognition and multiple perspectives, enabling descriptive self-awareness, and ensuring requisite levels of ambiguity. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p361]
- Vivienne Read was one of the first independent practitioners trained in Cynefin and associated methods in 2003. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p354]
- Hexagonal post-it notes are used in complex facilitation because they cluster in ways that squares do not, helping people break out of thinking in categories. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p361]
- In a 2004 complex facilitation case study in Australia, 350 people reached agreement on 70 specific recommendations for action over two days. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p368]

## Inferences

- Inference: Complex facilitation shifts responsibility for outcomes from the facilitator to the group, distinguishing it fundamentally from traditional facilitation approaches.
