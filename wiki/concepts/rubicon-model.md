# Rubicon Model of Action Phases

## Summary

The Rubicon model divides decision-making into predecisional and postdecisional phases, named after the river that Caesar crossed to begin a civil war. Crossing the Rubicon—actually making the decision—was found to be the most fatiguing part of the process, depleting willpower regardless of whether the decision involved choosing a computer or starting a civil war.

## Key facts

- Psychologists distinguish between automatic processes (like multiplying 4 times 7) and controlled processes (like computing 26 times 30), with the latter requiring willpower. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p89]
- The Rubicon model is named after the river separating Italy from the Roman province of Gaul; Caesar's crossing initiated civil war, representing the point of no return. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p89]
- In Kathleen Vohs's Dell computer experiment, people who actually made decisions gave up sooner on subsequent anagram tests than those who merely evaluated options or implemented others' choices. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p90]
- Making pleasant choices is less depleting only up to a point; when the bridal registry task extended to twelve minutes, both people who enjoyed choosing and those who disliked it were equally depleted. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p91]

## Inferences

- Inference: The actual moment of decision ('casting the die') consumes more mental resources than the deliberation or implementation phases.
