# Critical Theories of Race and Racism

## Summary

Critical theories of race and racism (CTRR) are rooted more in the social sciences, including sociology, than critical race theory (which originated in law). CTRR deals with cutting-edge theoretical issues including the relationship between race and racism and agency-structure, political economy, and globalization. They have a broader, even global focus than critical race theory and adopt a macrostructural and macrocultural approach focusing on power. A general conclusion of CTRR is that "race matters" and continues to matter throughout the structures and institutions of society. CTRR includes ideas that racism is endemic to American life, that race is a social construction, and that different minority groups have been racialized at different times. Bonilla-Silva's analysis of "color blindness" as a smoke screen for continued racial discrimination is an example of CTRR work.
