# Triune Brain

## Summary

Paul MacLean's triune brain concept divides the mammalian brain into three evolutionary strata: the reptilian brain (basal ganglia) elaborating basic motor plans and primitive emotions; the limbic system (old-mammalian brain) generating social emotions; and the neocortex (neomammalian brain) enabling cognitive/rational functions. Although anatomists debate its precise boundaries, the concept provides a useful framework for understanding brain organization.

## Key facts

- The reptilian brain (basal ganglia/extrapyramidal motor system) elaborates basic motor plans including axial movements and primitive behavioral routines such as elimination, shelter-seeking, and social displays. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- The old-mammalian brain (limbic system) elaborates social emotions including maternal acceptance, social bonding, separation distress, and rough-and-tumble play. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- The neomammalian brain (neocortex) is rudimentary in other vertebrates and exhibits the greatest diversification among mammalian species, serving as the storehouse of cognitive skills. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- The neocortex can be influenced by emotions and influences them through various appraisal processes, but it is not a fundamental neural substrate for the generation of affective experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p44]
- The cortex can come to be influenced by emotions and influences them through various appraisal processes, but it cannot generate emotionality without the ancient subcortical functions of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p45]

## Inferences

- Inference: The triune brain concept suggests that emotional feelings arise from subcortical systems that are conserved across mammalian species, implying that basic emotions in humans may share homologous neural substrates with other mammals.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The triune brain concept is described as largely a didactic simplification from a neuroanatomical perspective, and some modern neuroscientists disagree that the limbic system should be considered an anatomically and functionally distinct entity.
