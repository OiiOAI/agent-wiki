# Negative Therapeutic Reaction

## Summary

The negative therapeutic reaction is a pathological response to psychoanalytic treatment in which patients deteriorate rather than improve as therapy progresses. It represents one of the most powerful resistances to recovery, arising from an unconscious sense of guilt that finds satisfaction in illness and refuses to give up the punishment of suffering.

## Key facts

- When one speaks hopefully to such patients or expresses satisfaction with the progress of treatment, they show signs of discontent and their condition invariably becomes worse. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5934]
- The negative therapeutic reaction reveals itself as the most powerful of all obstacles to recovery, more powerful than narcissistic inaccessibility, a negative attitude towards the physician, or clinging to the gain from illness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5934]
- The sense of guilt is finding its satisfaction in the illness and refuses to give up the punishment of suffering. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5934]
- The battle with the obstacle of an unconscious sense of guilt cannot be fought directly; it requires the slow procedure of unmasking its unconscious repressed roots. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5935]
