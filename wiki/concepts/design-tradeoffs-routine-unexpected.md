# Design Trade-offs for Routine and Unexpected Performance

## Summary

This chapter examines the fundamental design trade-off between optimizing systems for routine performance versus supporting situation awareness (SA) during unexpected circumstances. The dilemma for designers is whether to make designs for the routine that occupies most of a pilot's time or for the unusual that occurs only rarely but has potentially dangerous consequences if not well-supported. Four alternative solutions to this trade-off are proposed: the compromise display (midpoint of egocentricity like the tethered display), the dual display (optimal display for each task presented simultaneously), the sequential display (flexible or multifunction displays depicting different renderings at different times), and the automated system approach (design primarily for global SA support and assume local guidance automation).

## Key facts

- The design dilemma is whether to make designs for the routine that occupies most of a pilot's time or for the unusual that occurs only rarely but has potentially dangerous consequences if not well-supported. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p226]
- Four alternative solutions to the trade-off exist: compromise display, dual display, sequential display, and automated system approach. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p231]
- A compromise display at the midpoint of egocentricity 'satisfaces' by providing adequate support for both routine and unexpected tasks, with advantages when display space is limited. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p231]
- The rotating 'tether' display concept achieves compromise by providing an adequate view around and behind ownship while preserving some ego-referenced aspects of the pilot's forward view. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p231]
- The dual display approach designs the optimal display for each task and presents them simultaneously, but has three limitations: not economical use of limited space, added scanning demands, and hampered cognitive linkage between display panels. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p232]
- The sequential display uses flexible or multifunction displays but has drawbacks including working memory limitations, manual navigation burden, and inconsistency of representation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p232]
- The automated system approach designs primarily for support of global SA and assumes local guidance can be automated—local guidance is predictable and easily lends itself to effective automation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: The weighting of optimal design for global SA should exceed the proportion of time such awareness is actually required, because unexpected events carry disproportionate expected cost of failure.
