# Garland Test

## Summary

The Garland test, named after Alex Garland's 2014 film Ex Machina, transforms the Turing test from a test of intelligence into a test of consciousness. Rather than asking whether judges can distinguish machine from human, it asks whether the machine makes conscious persons feel it is conscious. Seth notes this test is 'pristine'—not yet passed—and reveals more about humans than about machines.

## Key facts

- In Ex Machina, Nathan says the challenge is 'to show you that she's a robot—and see if you still feel she has consciousness,' transforming the Turing test from intelligence to consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p241]
- What matters in the Garland test is not whether Ava is a machine, nor whether she has consciousness, but whether Ava makes a conscious person feel that she is conscious. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p241]
- The Garland test reveals this kind of test for what it really is: a test of the human, not of the machine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p241]
- When it comes to consciousness, there's no equivalent to the Ukrainian chatbot—attempts to create simulacra of sentient humans have often produced anxiety and revulsion rather than complex emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p242]
