# Spinothalamic Tract

## Summary

The spinothalamic tract is the principal pathway transmitting noxious, thermal, and visceral information to the thalamus and cerebral cortex. It originates from neurons in spinal cord laminae I, V, and VII—the main targets of small-diameter Aδ and C fibers—and crosses midline to ascend contralaterally.

## Key facts

- More than 50% of spinal projections to the thalamus originate from lamina I neurons, which receive inputs from nociceptors, thermal receptors, visceral afferents, or itch receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p544]
- Lamina V wide-dynamic-range neurons respond to combinations of tactile, visceral, thermal, and noxious stimuli and converge information from different submodalities at the first synapse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p544]
- Most spinothalamic fibers (82%) cross midline and ascend contralaterally; 12% ascend ipsilaterally and 6% bilaterally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p544]
- Due to decussation, unilateral spinal cord injury produces tactile/proprioceptive loss on the same side as the lesion and thermal/pain loss on the opposite side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p544]
