# Circumcision as Symbol

## Summary

Freud interprets circumcision as the symbolic substitute for castration, representing submission to the primal father's will. The practice preserved the external mark of Mosaic religion through the Kadesh compromise and signifies acceptance of painful sacrifice to the father figure.

## Key facts

- Circumcision is described as the symbolic substitute for the castration which the primal father once inflicted upon his sons in the plenitude of his absolute power. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7283]
- Freud states that whoever accepted circumcision showed submission to the father's will, even if it imposed the most painful sacrifice on him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7283]
- Circumcision was preserved through the Kadesh compromise as at least the external mark of the religion of Moses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7230]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud connects circumcision to the 'uncanny' impression it makes on non-Jews, suggesting it recalls the dreaded castration and along with it a portion of primaeval past that is gladly forgotten.
