# Periodic Paralysis

## Summary

Periodic paralyses are inherited muscle diseases characterized by transient episodes of weakness due to abnormal muscle fiber excitability. Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis involves high blood potassium and mutations in voltage-gated sodium channels causing inactivation defects. Hypokalemic periodic paralysis involves low blood potassium and mutations in calcium or sodium channel voltage sensors creating anomalous leak currents.

## Key facts

- Periodic paralyses are rare inherited muscle diseases characterized by transient episodes of weakness with normal muscle function between attacks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis is caused by missense mutations in Na+ channels with inactivation defects, producing myotonia or paralysis depending on severity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Hypokalemic periodic paralysis is caused by missense mutations in voltage-sensor domains of Ca2+ or Na+ channels creating anomalous leak currents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- During an attack in hypokalemic paralysis, the resting potential depolarizes to about -60 mV, inactivating most Na+ channels and rendering muscle inexcitable. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Both forms are inherited as autosomal dominant traits, and consciousness and respiration are spared during attacks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
