# Inside View

## Summary

The inside view, a label coined by Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman and Dan Lovallo, describes the tendency to ignore related pertinent data and believe that 'this case is different.' This bias leads people to see themselves, their group, their project, their company, or their nation as special and distinct from others. It played a key role in the 2008 financial crisis when policy makers believed the U.S. was different from other nations that had experienced credit booms.

## Key facts

- The inside view describes the tendency to ignore related pertinent data and believe that 'this case is different.'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- People tend to see themselves, their group, their project, their company, or their nation as special and different. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- The inside view was coined by Daniel Kahneman and Dan Lovallo. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- In the 2008 crisis, inside-view premises included: other nations' economic histories had little relevance to modern America, the Fed had eliminated major economic swings, and America's deep financial markets could withstand shocks. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p101]
- The inside view focuses on the specific circumstances of the current case—plans, early progress, and expectations—to make predictions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p320]
- The outside view uses statistical information from a reference class of similar past cases to establish base rates and predictions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p321]
- Kahneman discovered through his own curriculum development project that the outside view (40% failure rate, 7-10 year completion time for similar projects) was available but not used until explicitly requested. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p318]
- When the outside view is finally considered, people typically ignore it in favor of the inside view, similar to how Nisbett and Borgida's participants ignored base rate information about helping behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p322]

## Inferences

- Inference: The outside view provides more accurate predictions because it accounts for unknown unknowns and the typical distribution of outcomes in comparable situations, whereas the inside view WYSIATI (what you see is all there is) ignores relevant statistical evidence.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text does not specify precise conditions under which the outside view outperforms the inside view, leaving open the question of when case-specific information legitimately warrants departing from base rates.
