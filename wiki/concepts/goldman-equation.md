# Goldman Equation

## Summary

The Goldman equation quantifies the membrane potential as a function of the concentrations and permeabilities of multiple ion species. It extends the Nernst equation to situations where the membrane is permeable to more than one ion, showing that the contribution of each ion to the membrane potential depends on both its concentration gradient and its membrane permeability.

## Key facts

- The Goldman equation states: Vm = (RT/F) ln [(PK[K+]o + PNa[Na+]o + PCl[Cl−]i) / (PK[K+]i + PNa[Na+]i + PCl[Cl−]o)]. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p247]
- At rest, permeability ratios PK:PNa:PCl = 1.0:0.04:0.45, showing K+ permeability dominates at rest. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p246]
- At the peak of the action potential, permeability ratios shift to PK:PNa:PCl = 1.0:20:0.45, showing Na+ permeability dominates during the rising phase.
