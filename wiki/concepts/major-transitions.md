# Major Transitions in Evolution

## Summary

Rare but pivotal events in evolutionary history when previously independent units began cooperating so successfully that they formed new, higher-level organisms. Biologists Maynard Smith and Szathmáry identify eight clear examples over 4 billion years. Each transition required solving the free rider problem—suppressing competition among lower-level units so that higher-level selection could operate. Examples include: prokaryotes joining into eukaryotes (~2 billion years ago), multicellular organisms emerging, and eusocial insects forming colonies. Human societies represent the most recent major transition, enabling cities, states, and civilizations.

## Key facts

- The eight major transitions identified by Maynard Smith and Szathmáry include: replicators in protocells, chromosomes, eukaryotes, multicellular organisms, eusocial insects, and human societies. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p221]
- When colonial insects emerged (~100 million years ago), they displaced solitary competitors and transformed terrestrial ecosystems. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p222]
- Humans began building 'ever more elaborate nests' with agriculture, leading to cities functioning as superorganisms able to raise walls and armies. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p224]

## Inferences

- Inference: Major transitions explain how cooperation could evolve despite the inherent selfishness of natural selection—once free riding is suppressed at one level, selection can favor cooperative traits at the next level up.
