# One-Text Procedure

## Summary

The One-Text Procedure is a mediation technique in which a third party (or sometimes a participant acting as mediator) develops a draft agreement, solicits criticism, and iteratively improves it. The procedure separates inventing from deciding, reduces the number of decisions required, and prevents parties from becoming locked into positions. Famous applications include the Camp David negotiations between Egypt and Israel in 1978.

## Key facts

- In the one-text procedure, a third party asks about interests rather than positions: not how big a bay window is wanted, but why it is wanted. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p57]
- The one-text procedure simplifies the process of decision-making: each party has only one decision to make—yes or no. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p58]
- The most famous use of the one-text procedure was by the United States at Camp David in September 1978 when mediating between Egypt and Israel. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p58]
- After thirteen days and some twenty-three drafts, the United States had a text it was prepared to recommend. When President Carter did recommend it, Israel and Egypt accepted. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p58]
- You do not have to get anyone's consent to start using the one-text procedure—simply prepare a draft and ask for criticism. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p58]
