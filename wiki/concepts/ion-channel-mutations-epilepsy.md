# Ion Channel Mutations in Epilepsy

## Summary

Mutations in ion channel genes (channelopathies) constitute a major cause of monogenic epilepsies. More than 70 genes have been linked to an epileptic phenotype. Affected proteins include ion channel subunits, proteins involved in synaptic transmission, and molecules involved in Ca2+ signaling. The first autosomal dominant epilepsy gene discovered was a mutation in the alpha-4 subunit of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor causing autosomal dominant nocturnal frontal lobe epilepsy (ADNFLE).

## Key facts

- More than 70 genes have been linked to an epileptic phenotype; approximately half discovered in humans and the others in animals, mostly mice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1187]
- The first autosomal dominant epilepsy gene discovered causes autosomal dominant nocturnal frontal lobe epilepsy (ADNFLE) and involves a mutation in the alpha-4 subunit of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1187]
- When examined in vitro, mutant channel proteins are most commonly associated with either reductions in expression on the plasma membrane surface or altered kinetics of the channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1187]
- The GEFS+ syndrome (generalized epilepsy with febrile seizures plus) can involve mutations in genes for three different Na+ channel subunits and two GABAA receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1188]
- One mutant gene can give rise to different epilepsy phenotypes, whereas different mutant genes can cause the same epilepsy phenotype. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1188]
