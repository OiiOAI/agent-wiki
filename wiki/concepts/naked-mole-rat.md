# Naked Mole Rat

## Summary

The naked mole rat (Heterocephalus glaber) is a eusocial mammal living in large underground colonies in East Africa. Only one female breeds, mated by two or three males; all other individuals of both sexes are nonbreeding workers. Studied by Jennifer Jarvis, Robert Brett, Richard Alexander, and Paul Sherman, they represent the closest mammalian analogue to social insects like ants and termites.

## Key facts

- Naked mole rat colonies typically number 70-80 individuals but can grow to hundreds; the burrow network of one colony can be two to three miles in total length, with three to four tons of soil excavated annually. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p367]
- The queen is mated by only two or three males; if she is removed, previously sterile females come into breeding condition and fight for the position of queen. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p367]
- Worker roles depend on size: smallest 'frequent workers' dig and transport soil and feed young; largest nonbreeders may be soldiers defending against snakes; intermediate-sized individuals behave intermediately. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p367]
- They feed mainly on underground tubers that can outweigh 1,000 mole rats and last for months or years; a single mole rat alone would search so long it would wear its teeth out before finding one. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p371]
- A large social colony with miles of burrows is an efficient tuber-mine, making each individual economically better off as part of the union, exemplifying the 'hollow tree' principle. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p371]
- Dawkins speculates that naked mole rats might produce dispersing reproductives similar to locusts, possibly represented by an already-known hairy rodent species, though this seems unlikely. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p368]

## Inferences

- Inference: The naked mole rat case refutes the notion that haplodiploidy is necessary for eusociality; the 'going concern' argument (maintaining access to a valuable food source) may be sufficient.
- Inference: Like social insect queens, the naked mole rat queen has larger litters than normal for rodent size.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether large nonbreeders truly function as soldiers or 'food vats' remains under investigation.
- Uncertain: Whether naked mole rats produce a distinct dispersing caste under certain conditions is purely speculative.
