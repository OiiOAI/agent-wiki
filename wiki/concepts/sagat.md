# SAGAT

## Summary

SAGAT (Situation Awareness Global Assessment Technique) is the primary objective measure for directly assessing operator Situation Awareness. Developed by Endsley, it freezes simulation activity at randomly selected intervals, blanks operator displays, and administers a battery of queries covering Level 1, 2, and 3 SA. Responses are scored against ground truth data. SAGAT has strong psychometric properties including predictive validity and reliability. It provides diagnostic information about which specific SA requirements are supported or not by a system design, making it valuable for iterative design evaluation.

## Key facts

- SAGAT freezes simulation activity at randomly selected intervals, blanks displays, and queries operators on their perception, comprehension, and projection of the situation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p290]
- SAGAT has demonstrated predictive validity—pilots with good SAGAT scores are far more likely to have good operational outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p291]
- SAGAT queries are developed from GDTA (Goal-Directed Task Analysis) and cover the full range of operator SA requirements relevant to the domain. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p292]
- Each SAGAT query should be administered 30-60 times across subjects and trials for each experimental condition. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p294]
- SAGAT stops should not be predictable, should not occur within the first 3-5 minutes, and should not occur within 1 minute of each other. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p294]
