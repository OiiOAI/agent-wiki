# System 2

## Summary

System 2 is the slow, effortful, and deliberate mode of thinking that requires attention and cognitive resources. It handles complex calculations, logical reasoning, comparisons, and choices that System 1 cannot perform. System 2 monitors System 1's suggestions and can override or correct them when errors are detected. However, System 2 is inherently lazy, often accepting System 1's outputs with minimal verification. It is responsible for self-control, sustained attention, and systematic analysis.

## Key facts

- System 2 activities require attention and are disrupted when attention is diverted. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p33]
- System 2 allocates mental effort to demanding computations, including multiplying 17 x 24 and comparing washing machines. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p33]
- System 2 has limited capacity; attention can be overloaded and must be rationed. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p48]
- System 2 is characterized by laziness—unwilling to invest more effort than necessary. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p28]
- System 2 is responsible for self-control and overriding System 1 impulses. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p38]
- When ego-depleted (busy or fatigued), System 2 fails to suppress System 1 intuitions that are wrong. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p35]
- System 2 can program memory to follow instructions that override habitual responses. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p51]

## Inferences

- Inference: The laziness of System 2 explains why people often accept intuitive answers without checking them.
