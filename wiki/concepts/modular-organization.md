# Modular Organization

## Summary

Modular organization refers to the brain's division into computational modules—discrete brain regions with specialized functions that interact through serial and parallel processing to produce perception, movement, language, and thought.

## Key facts

- Perception, movement, language, thought, and memory are made possible by the interlinkage of serial and parallel processing in discrete brain regions—computational modules. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p21]
- The brain contains many representations of information organized by relevance of features for particular computations and by the variety of uses for neural information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p23]
- Functional modularity allows for redundancy; remaining parts of a network may modify their performance to accommodate breakdown of one pathway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p21]
