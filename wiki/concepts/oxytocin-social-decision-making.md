# Oxytocin and Social Decision-Making

## Summary

Oxytocin (OT) is a neurohormone that plays a crucial role in social behavior and decision-making. OT modulates decision processes through dual modes of release (dendritic volume transmission and axonal transmission), affects sensory and emotional perception of social stimuli, and influences social decisions including trust, cooperation, empathy, and prosocial behavior. OT interacts with dopaminergic, serotonergic, and opioid systems in reward processing, and also affects learning and memory, particularly for social and emotional stimuli.

## Key facts

- Oxytocin has a dual mode of release: dendritic diffusion (volume transmission) and classical axonal transmission via parvocellular and magnocellular neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p367]
- The social brain circuit consists of two main networks: one centered in the amygdala for emotional significance processing, and one centered in the nucleus accumbens for coding reward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p367]
- OT receptors are distributed in primary sensory areas according to species-specific preferred social modalities: olfaction for rodents, auditory for birds, and visual for primates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p368]
- In adult mice, OT orients maternal behavior in response to pup calls by regulating inhibitory/excitatory activity in auditory cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p368]
- Intranasal administration of OT in humans leads to improved recognition of facial emotional expressions and emotional valence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p368]
- fMRI investigations show that OT reduces amygdala response to arousing emotional stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p368]
- OT reduces phobic fear in humans and enhances extinction-based psychotherapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p392]
- OT has weak to moderate effect on trust that appears to be context-dependent. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p369]
- In humans, OT has been associated with increasing prosociality toward in-group but not out-group members. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p390]
- OT modifies pair bonding via dopamine modulation in the nucleus accumbens in monogamous voles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p390]
- OT increases activity in reward areas when subjects see their own partner, mediated by dopamine D1/D2 receptor balance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p390]
- OT has been found to reduce drug seeking and consumption, especially when directly administered in the nucleus accumbens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p391]
- OT improves efficiency of hippocampal neurotransmission by increasing firing rate of fast-spiking interneurons, enhancing signal-to-noise ratio. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p392]
- OT's effects are not necessarily always prosocial; accounts challenge the prosocial specificity of this hormone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p392]
- OT remains a therapeutic target for psychiatric disorders such as autism, depression, and schizophrenia where social behavior and reward processing are impaired. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p392]
