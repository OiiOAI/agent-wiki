# Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)

## Summary

Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT, pronounced 'act') is a science-based psychological approach developed in the mid-1980s by Steven C. Hayes and colleagues. With over 3000 published studies demonstrating its effectiveness, ACT helps people handle difficult thoughts and feelings while taking action guided by their values. It is used to treat depression, anxiety, addiction, trauma, chronic pain, and many other conditions.

## Key facts

- ACT was developed in the United States in the mid-1980s by psychologist Steven C. Hayes and his colleagues Kelly Wilson and Kirk Strosahl. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p11]
- ACT is a science-based approach with over 3000 published studies demonstrating its effectiveness. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p11]
- ACT has spread globally, with hundreds of thousands of practitioners in dozens of countries. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p11]
- ACT has been shown effective for depression, addiction, anxiety disorders, psychosis, chronic pain, and trauma. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p11]
- The technical term for OBEY mode in ACT is 'fusion,' and the technical term for STRUGGLE mode is 'experiential avoidance.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p35]
- ACT practitioners worldwide include psychologists, therapists, counselors, coaches, and doctors. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p11]
