# Psychosurgery

## Summary

Psychosurgery refers to surgical destruction of brain tissue to treat psychiatric disorders. The field gained popularity in the 1930s-1950s following António Egas Moniz's development of prefrontal leucotomy, for which he won the Nobel Prize. Walter Freeman popularized lobotomy in the United States, performing over 3,000 operations. Scoville developed medial temporal lobotomy, targeting the limbic system instead of the frontal lobes. Psychosurgery declined after the 1950s due to devastating side effects including death, seizures, and dementia, replaced by antipsychotic medications.

## Key facts

- António Egas Moniz developed prefrontal leucotomy in 1935 and won the Nobel Prize for his work. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p22]
- Walter Freeman performed over 3,000 lobotomies in the United States. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p26]
- Freeman used an ice pick for transorbital lobotomies, which could be done in ten minutes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p25]
- Scoville performed frontal lobotomies on forty-three psychotic patients during the 1940s before developing medial temporal lobotomy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p28]
- Psychosurgery declined in the late 1950s due to growing concerns about hazardous side effects including death, suicide, seizures, and dementia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p27]
- Rosemary Kennedy was lobotomized by Walter Freeman in 1941 at age 23, resulting in severe disability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p20]
