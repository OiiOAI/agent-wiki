# Auto-erotism

## Summary

Auto-erotism is the term Freud adopted from Havelock Ellis to describe sexual satisfaction obtained from one's own body without the involvement of another person as a sexual object. The concept is exemplified in Freud's analysis of thumbsucking (sensual sucking) in infants, where pleasure is derived from the body's own erotogenic zones. This represents the first stage of sexual development before object choice emerges.

## Key facts

- Auto-erotism refers to sexual activity where the drive is not directed towards other people but obtains satisfaction from the subject's own body. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2454]
- In thumbsucking, the child's lips behave like an erotogenic zone, with stimulation by the warm flow of milk causing pleasurable sensation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2454]
- To begin with, sexual activity attaches itself to one of the functions serving the purpose of self-preservation (such as taking nourishment) and does not become independent until later. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2454]
- Thumbsucking may continue into maturity or even persist all through life, sometimes combined with rubbing sensitive body parts or leading to masturbation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2450]

## Inferences

- Inference: Auto-erotism represents the earliest phase of sexual development, preceding the Oedipal phase where external objects become sexual targets.
- Inference: The concept explains how sexual gratification can develop independently of interpersonal relationships.
