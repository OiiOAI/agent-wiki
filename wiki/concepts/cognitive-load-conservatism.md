# Cognitive Load and Conservatism

## Summary

Research demonstrates that increasing cognitive load—through time pressure, fatigue, hunger, distraction, or alcohol—makes people more politically conservative. This supports the view that conservatism relies more on gut-level, intuitive processing, while liberalism requires more cognitive effort to override initial biases with situational reasoning.

## Key facts

- Increasing cognitive load makes people more conservative; conservatives start gut and stay gut, while liberals go from gut to head when given time to think. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p461]
- People become more conservative when tired, in pain, distracted with a cognitive task, or when blood alcohol levels rise. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p462]
- In a study of 1,100 judicial rulings, prisoners were granted parole at ~60% when judges had recently eaten, but at ~0% rate just before judges ate. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p462]
- Snap judgments produce personal attributions for events (e.g., poverty = laziness) in both conservatives and liberals; only liberals shift toward situational explanations with more thinking time. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p460]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that willpower depletes glucose, and that glucose depletion increases conservatism, connects moral/political psychology to metabolic neuroscience.
