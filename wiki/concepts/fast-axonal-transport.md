# Fast Axonal Transport

## Summary

Fast axonal transport moves membranous organelles toward axon terminals (anterograde direction) and back toward the cell body (retrograde direction) at rates up to 400 mm per day in warm-blooded animals. Anterograde transport uses kinesin motors, while retrograde transport uses dynein motors. Both motors move cargo along microtubule tracks in a stop-and-start saltatory fashion. Fast transport carries synaptic vesicle precursors, large dense-core vesicles, mitochondria, and protein particles carrying RNAs.

## Key facts

- Fast axonal transport moves membranous organelles at up to 400 mm per day in warm-blooded animals, compared to slow axonal transport at 0.2 to 2.5 mm per day. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p143]
- Fast anterograde transport uses plus-end-directed molecular motors called kinesin, while fast retrograde transport uses minus-end-directed motors called dyneins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p144]
- Kinesin is a heterotetramer composed of two heavy chains and two light chains, with the globular head acting as the ATPase motor domain attached to microtubules. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p144]
- Dyneins consist of a multimeric ATPase protein complex with two globular heads on two stalks connected to a basal structure, similar to those found in cilia and flagella. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p145]
- Fast retrograde transport delivers signals that regulate gene expression, including activated growth factor receptors transported back to the nucleus, and is important during nerve regeneration and axon regrowth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p144]
