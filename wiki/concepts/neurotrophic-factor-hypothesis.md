# Neurotrophic Factor Hypothesis

## Summary

The neurotrophic factor hypothesis states that cells at or near the target of a neuron secrete small amounts of an essential nutrient or trophic factor, and uptake of this factor by nerve terminals is needed for the survival of the neuron. This hypothesis was confirmed by the discovery of nerve growth factor (NGF).

## Key facts

- The core of the hypothesis is that target cells secrete low levels of neurotrophic factors that are internalized and transported to the cell body, promoting neuronal survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1149]
- Neurons that fail to receive adequate amounts of neurotrophic factor die through apoptosis, a programmed cell death mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1149]
- The nervous system generates up to twice as many neurons as survive in adulthood, and excess neurons are eliminated by the cell death program controlled by neurotrophic factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1153]
