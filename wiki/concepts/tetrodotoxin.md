# Tetrodotoxin

## Summary

Tetrodotoxin (TTX) is a potent neurotoxin from certain Pacific puffer fish that specifically blocks voltage-gated sodium channels with nanomolar affinity. It is widely used in electrophysiology research to isolate potassium currents and study sodium channel function.

## Key facts

- Tetrodotoxin blocks voltage-gated Na+ channels with very high potency in the nanomolar concentration range. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- Ingestion of only a few milligrams of tetrodotoxin from improperly prepared puffer fish (fugu) can be fatal in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- TTX is used experimentally to block Na+ currents and isolate K+ currents in voltage-clamp studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
