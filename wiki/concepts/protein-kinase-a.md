# Protein Kinase A and Memory

## Summary

Protein kinase A (PKA) is an enzyme activated by cyclic AMP that plays a critical role in short-term memory by phosphorylating target proteins. In Aplysia sensory neurons, PKA is activated by serotonin during sensitization and enhances the release of neurotransmitter glutamate, strengthening synaptic connections.

## Key facts

- Krebs discovered that protein kinase A is a complex molecule made of four units—two regulatory and two catalytic—with cyclic AMP binding to regulatory units causing them to fall off catalytic units. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p240]
- Phosphorylation activates some proteins and inactivates others and can be readily reversed, serving as a simple molecular switch. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p239]
- Injecting the catalytic unit of protein kinase A directly into a sensory neuron strengthened synaptic connections by enhancing glutamate release. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p242]
- Injecting an inhibitor of protein kinase A blocked the ability of serotonin to enhance glutamate release. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p242]
- Repeated pulses of serotonin produce higher concentrations of cyclic AMP, causing protein kinase A to move into the nucleus where it activates genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p274]
