# Inner Speech

## Summary

Inner speech is internal verbal thought that emerged evolutionarily from the internalization of spoken language. Vygotsky theorized that as children acquire language, it branches into inner and outer forms. Darwin argued speech was necessary for complex thought, though this view is now considered too strong—baboons demonstrate complex thought processing despite limited vocal communication.

## Key facts

- Vygotsky proposed that as children acquire spoken language, it internalizes into inner speech, which has distinct patterns from ordinary speech and enables organized thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p111]
- Darwin argued in 1871 that complex thought requires speech, either inner or outer, as a medium for carrying on long trains of thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p112]
- Baboons construct narratives from simple vocalizations, demonstrating complex internal processing without sophisticated vocal communication. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p114]
- Inner speech arises from efference copy mechanisms in speech production, where copies of intended speech create internal auditory images. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p116]
- Inner speech appears to be important for System 2 thinking—slow, deliberate reasoning—providing a medium for experiment and self-control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p117]
