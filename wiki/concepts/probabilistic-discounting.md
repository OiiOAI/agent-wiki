# Probabilistic Discounting

## Summary

Probabilistic discounting measures risky choice, the decision between more certain small rewards and less certain large rewards. Although probabilistic discounting can be subsumed in the same theoretical framework as temporal discounting, mounting evidence indicates that the underlying neural substrates are distinct. Nucleus accumbens and orbitofrontal cortex are implicated in probabilistic discounting, with D1 and D3 receptors playing contrasting roles.

## Key facts

- Risky choice is the decision between more certain small rewards and less certain large rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p92]
- Temporal and probabilistic discounting engage similar processes in theoretical terms but there is mounting evidence that they are distinct with different underlying neural substrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p86]
- Lesions of the nucleus accumbens core induce risk-averse choice in rats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p92]
- D1 receptor blockade reduces risky choice, much like lesions or inactivation, while D3 receptor antagonism has opposite effects to D1 receptor antagonism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p92]
- Reversible inactivation of the medial orbitofrontal cortex with baclofen/muscimol infusions increases risky choice on probabilistic discounting tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p93]
