# Social Support and Self-Control

## Summary

Social support is a critical external factor that predicts success in self-control endeavors such as maintaining sobriety from alcohol. Research conducted by Carlo DiClemente with men in Baltimore therapy found that those who contacted others for help and social support abstained more frequently and had less serious lapses. Social support operates in two ways: reducing stress from loneliness, and—when the social circle consists of people with healthy habits—providing positive peer pressure. This is why groups like Alcoholics Anonymous can be vital for recovering alcoholics.

## Key facts

- Men in therapy for alcohol abuse who received social support abstained more frequently and had less serious lapses than those without support. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p162]
- Loners and lonely people tend to have more mental and physical illness than people in rich social networks. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p162]
- People drink more when encouraged by their friends, making social support for not drinking essential for those struggling with alcohol problems. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p163]
- Resolutions are more likely to be kept if made in the presence of other people, especially a romantic partner. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p163]
