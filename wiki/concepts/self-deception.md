# Self-Deception

## Summary

Self-deception refers to unconscious processes that allow individuals to maintain false beliefs about themselves or their behavior while avoiding the anxiety that truthful self-awareness would produce. Research by Dan Batson demonstrated 'moral hypocrisy' where people act unfairly while believing themselves moral.

## Key facts

- Batson found 80-90% of participants who didn't flip a coin assigned themselves the better task, yet rated themselves as moral for even considering fairness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p115]
- High scorers on moral responsibility tests were more likely to flip a coin to appear moral but no more likely to actually be moral by accepting the coin's result. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p115]
- Participants only stopped cheating when sitting in front of a mirror, suggesting self-awareness forces alignment between stated values and actual behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p115]
- Self-deception helps liars avoid detection by reducing anxiety that would otherwise leak to facial expressions and voice tone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p112]
