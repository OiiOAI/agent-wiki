# Cyclic AMP Pathway

## Summary

The cyclic AMP (cAMP) pathway is a prototypic second-messenger signaling cascade initiated by G protein-coupled receptors. Activation of Gs protein stimulates adenylyl cyclase to produce cAMP from ATP, which activates protein kinase A (PKA). PKA phosphorylates target proteins including ion channels, synaptic vesicle proteins, and transcription factors, modulating neuronal excitability and synaptic transmission.

## Key facts

- The binding of transmitter to receptors linked to the cAMP cascade activates the Gs protein, which stimulates adenylyl cyclase to catalyze conversion of ATP to cAMP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p350]
- A Gs protein typically remains active for a few seconds before its bound GTP is hydrolyzed to GDP, after which it reassociates with the βγ complex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p350]
- The major target of cAMP in most cells is cAMP-dependent protein kinase (protein kinase A or PKA), a heterotetrameric enzyme consisting of two regulatory and two catalytic subunits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p350]
- In the presence of cAMP, each regulatory subunit binds two molecules of cAMP, leading to dissociation of regulatory and catalytic subunits and freeing the catalytic subunits to phosphorylate substrate proteins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p350]
- Protein kinase A targets include voltage-gated and ligand-gated ion channels, synaptic vesicle proteins, enzymes involved in transmitter biosynthesis, and proteins that regulate gene transcription.
