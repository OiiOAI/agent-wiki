# Contingent Self-Esteem

## Summary

Contingent self-esteem is the belief that one's worth must be earned and is conditional on behaving a certain way. It develops when children perceive that their parents' love is conditional on their performance or behavior. This can manifest in the overachieving student who falls apart when they underperform, or the athlete who shuts down when they flub a key play. Children raised with contingent self-esteem tend to lack the resilience to handle failure.

## Key facts

- Contingent self-esteem is the belief that one's worth must be earned. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p219]
- One unintended consequence of overparenting is that kids can grow up thinking their parents' love is conditional on their behaving a certain way. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p219]
- Contingent self-esteem can manifest in the overachieving student who studies hard, earns top grades, becomes class president, but who falls apart when she underperforms on an exam. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p219]

## Inferences

- Inference: Contingent self-esteem contrasts with secure self-worth, which allows children to take risks and fail without their fundamental value being questioned.
