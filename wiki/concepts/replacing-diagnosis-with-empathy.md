# Replacing Diagnosis with Empathy

## Summary

NVC replaces clinical diagnosis with empathic understanding. Rather than categorizing clients with diagnostic labels, practitioners ask what the person is feeling, needing, and what action would help them live more happily. This approach creates authentic, mutual relationships rather than hierarchical professional relationships characterized by emotional distance and diagnosis.

## Key facts

- NVC practitioners replace diagnosis with asking: 'What is this person feeling? What is she or he needing? How am I feeling in response, and what needs of mine are behind my feelings? What action would I request?'. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p270]
- Martin Buber posited that human growth occurs through authentic 'I-Thou' relationships where individuals express themselves vulnerably, which is difficult in psychotherapist-client roles where appointments and fees create distance. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p268]
- When Rosenberg avoided diagnosing people and stayed connected to the life going on in them and in himself, people usually responded positively. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p271]
- By adopting NVC skills and consciousness, professionals can counsel others in genuine, open, mutual encounters rather than relationships characterized by emotional distance, diagnosis, and hierarchy. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p271]

## Inferences

- Inference: Research cited by Rosenberg indicated a lack of agreement among psychiatrists and psychologists regarding diagnostic terms, with diagnoses depending more on the psychiatrist's training school than on patient characteristics.
