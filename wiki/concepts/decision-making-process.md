# Decision-making process model for SA measurement

## Summary

The chapter presents a four-stage model of decision-making tasks that illustrates where situation awareness is created by the operator and can be measured. The model follows the processing of a control signal during monitoring for and reacting to hazardous situations. The serial nature of the model shows how a weakness at any subtask has implications for subsequent subtasks that rely on its outputs. This framework distinguishes between external information (available to the operator and their observable actions) and internal information (internal to the operator).

## Key facts

- The model follows the different stages of processing of a control signal during monitoring for and reacting to hazardous situations. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p205]
- The model assumes a purely serial nature of the task where each subfunction receives an input, processes it, and outputs a new set of information for the next subfunction. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p205]
- A weakness at any subtask has implications for subsequent subtasks that rely on its outputs. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p205]
- The model delineates the information available for measurement externally to the operator and the information internal to the operator. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p206]
- The external information consists of the information made available to the operator and the discernible actions they take; these values can be measured unambiguously. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p206]
- A 'disconnect' has been noted between situation awareness and decision making: in some situations, the causality is neither absolutely necessary nor sufficient. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p207]
