# Prospect Theory

## Summary

Prospect theory, developed by Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky in 1979, is a non-normative behavioral economic theory of decision under risk that incorporates psychological insights. It features reference-dependence (valuation depends on a point of reference), a regressive non-linear transformation of objective probability, and the finding that losses loom larger than equivalent gains.

## Key facts

- Kahneman and Tversky modified expected utility to incorporate psychophysical reference-dependence where valuation of outcomes depends on a point of reference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p4]
- Prospect theory incorporates a regressive non-linear transformation of objective probability rather than using actual probabilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p4]
- The theory demonstrates that the effect of a loss looms larger than the effect of an equivalent gain, a phenomenon empirically established by Kahneman and Tversky. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p15]
- Kahneman and Tversky's experimental demonstrations showed framing effects that attack the implicit axiom of description invariance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p4]
- Prospect theory represents choice as a function of the value of the choices rather than as a function of a person's overall utility. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- Decision makers are more sensitive to a given absolute-sized loss than an identically-sized gain, leading to loss aversion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- Prospect theory leads to the reflection effect: decision makers tend to be risk-seeking when dealing with perceived losses but risk-averse when dealing with perceived gains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- PT's value function is defined not over absolute outcomes but relative to a reference point, leading to concave shape for gains (risk-averse) and convex shape for losses (risk-seeking). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p130]
- Loss aversion in PT (parameter λ) reflects that losses loom larger than equivalent gains, explaining phenomena such as the endowment effect and status quo bias. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p130]
- PT incorporates a psychological transformation of objective probabilities into decision weights, where small probabilities are overweighted and large probabilities underweighted. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p130]
- Prospect Theory value function v(x) is concave for gains, convex for losses, and steeper for losses than gains (loss aversion). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p143]
- The decision weight w(p) in Prospect Theory represents the impact of probability p on the valuation of a prospect, rather than subjective belief. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p143]
- The fourfold pattern of risk attitudes consists of: risk seeking for low-probability gains, risk aversion for high-probability gains, risk aversion for low-probability losses, and risk seeking for high-probability losses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p142]
- The weighting function exhibits diminishing sensitivity to changes in probability near impossibility and certainty. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p143]

## Inferences

- Inference: Prospect theory represents a paradigm shift from normative to descriptive models of decision-making, prioritizing psychological realism over mathematical elegance.
- Inference: The theory's success in generating compelling experimental counterexamples to expected utility theory helped establish behavioral economics as a serious research program.
- Inference: Prospect Theory provides a psychologically grounded alternative to expected utility that captures many empirical anomalies in human choice under risk.
- Inference: Capuchins' exhibition of loss aversion and reference dependence suggests PT's psychological modifications may represent evolutionarily ancient mechanisms rather than uniquely human or culture-specific phenomena.
