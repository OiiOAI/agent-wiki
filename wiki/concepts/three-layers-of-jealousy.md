# Three Layers of Jealousy

## Summary

Freud distinguishes three layers or grades of jealousy: (1) competitive or normal jealousy, rooted in the Oedipus complex and compounded of grief, narcissistic wound, and enmity toward the rival; (2) projected jealousy, derived from the subject's own unfaithfulness or repressed impulses, where the subject projects their own impulses onto the partner; (3) delusional jealousy, which has its origin in repressed impulses toward unfaithfulness with a person of the same sex and represents a form of paranoia.

## Key facts

- Normal jealousy is described as compounded of grief at losing the loved object, the narcissistic wound, feelings of enmity against the rival, and self-criticism holding the subject accountable. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5797]
- Normal jealousy is rooted in the Oedipus or brother-and-sister complex of the first sexual period and may be experienced bisexually. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5797]
- Projected jealousy is derived from the subject's own actual unfaithfulness or from impulses toward it that have succumbed to repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5798]
- Delusional jealousy is described as what is left of a homosexuality that has run its course and takes the formula 'I do not love him, she loves him!'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5799]

## Inferences

- Inference: The formula for delusional jealousy suggests that paranoia defensively transforms homosexual impulses by attributing them to the partner instead.
