# Psychoanalytic Therapy

## Summary

Psychoanalytic therapy operates by replacing the unconscious with the conscious—lifting repressions, removing the preconditions for symptom formation, and transforming the pathogenic conflict into a normal one that can find resolution. The therapeutic process involves discovering repressions and the resistances maintaining them, then communicating this knowledge to the patient. The patient's desire for recovery and intelligence, supported by the analyst's interpretations, enable the resolution of conflicts. The analyst uses transference to create new editions of old conflicts, which can then be resolved under more favorable conditions (a stronger ego, helper at hand). This differs fundamentally from hypnotic suggestion, which merely covers up conflicts without resolving them.

## Key facts

- Psychoanalytic therapy lifts repressions, removes the preconditions for symptom formation, and transforms the pathogenic conflict into a normal one for which a solution must be possible. - The therapeutic task consists in freeing the libido from its present attachments and making it once more serviceable to the ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5205]
- Psychoanalytic treatment has been compared to surgery (aiming to expose and get rid of something) while hypnotic treatment has been compared to cosmetics (covering up and glossing something over). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5202]
- The analyst overcomes resistance by discovering it and showing it to the patient, with the patient's intelligence recognizing what is repressed and finding translations for it. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5190]
- The decisive therapeutic change is the elimination of repression in the renewed conflict so that libido cannot withdraw from the ego by flight into the unconscious. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5206]
- Freud states that psychoanalysis possesses the doctor either entirely or not at all, as it cannot be handled like glasses put on for reading and taken off for walking. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6979]
- Freud argues that psychoanalysis is the most powerful form of psychotherapy but also the most laborious and time-consuming, reserved for severe cases. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6979]
- Freud identifies limitations of psychoanalytic success arising from psychical rigidity and the form of illness with its deeper determinants. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6979]
- Freud notes that the field of analytic therapy lies in transference neuroses—phobias, hysteria, obsessional neurosis—while narcissistic and psychotic conditions are unsuitable to a greater or lesser extent. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6980]
- Freud acknowledges that psychical changes take place slowly, and if they occur rapidly it is a bad sign. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6981]

## Inferences

- Inference: The psychoanalytic treatment aims to make the patient 'a different man' by raising his mental life to a higher level of development, though at bottom he has remained the same

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges uncertainty about whether failures should be blamed on case peculiarities or clumsy therapeutic handling.
