# History of Dream Research

## Summary

Dream research has progressed through several waves. The first wave was primarily clinical, lasting from the late 1800s to the late 1930s. A second wave arose from the 1953 discovery of REM sleep by Aserinsky and Kleitman, leading to laboratory studies. However, the equation of REM sleep with dreaming proved overly simplistic, and dream research entered a decline through the 1980s. A renaissance began in the 1990s with renewed interest in cognitive and phenomenological aspects, evidence that sleep plays a key role in learning and memory, and growing interest in PTSD nightmares and lucid dreaming. By the 21st century, dream science was widely accepted as legitimate.
