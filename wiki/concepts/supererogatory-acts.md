# Supererogatory Acts

## Summary

Supererogatory acts are actions that would be duties were not certain exempting conditions fulfilled that make allowance for reasonable self-interest. They are beneficent acts undertaken at considerable loss or risk to the agent, beyond what natural duty requires.

## Key facts

- A beneficent act advances another's good and is at our liberty to do or not; a benevolent action is performed for the sake of the other's good. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p390]
- A supererogatory act is one that brings much good for another and is undertaken at considerable loss or risk to the agent beyond what natural duty requires. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p390]
- Supererogatory actions are ones that would be duties were certain exempting conditions fulfilled that make allowance for reasonable self-interest. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p390]
- The definition of supererogatory acts depends upon the full theory of the good. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p355]
