# Time-Consciousness

## Summary

Time-consciousness is the stream of awareness through which experiential moments are constituted as a unified flow. According to Husserl's analysis, any present phase of consciousness retains the just-past phase through its threefold structure of primal impression, retention, and protention, creating a retentional continuum stretching back over past experience. This retention is intentional rather than literal—the past is "intentionally contained" in the present, not actually present. Two types of intentionality structure this continuum: lengthwise (or horizontal) intentionality unifies consciousness over time, while transverse intentionality ensures the continuity of experienced temporal objects. The Living Present (stehender Strom) is understood as both standing (not moving through time) and streaming (continuous transformation), described as "standing-streaming." The absolute flow of consciousness is posited as the deepest level of time-consciousness, self-constituting through its own horizontal intentionality and not literally in time.

## Key facts

- Any present phase of consciousness retains the whole just-past phase, specifically its primal impression, retention, and protention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p314]
- The retentional continuum is characterized by decreasing clarity and affective force as it sinks back into the past. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p314]
- Lengthwise intentionality unifies consciousness over time because retention retains the previous phases of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p315]
- Transverse intentionality ensures the continuity of the experienced temporal object through retention of the intentional object belonging to previous phases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p315]
- Husserl distinguishes three levels of temporality: external temporal objects, intentional acts, and absolute consciousness (the absolute flow). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p316]
- The absolute flow is described as self-constituting through its horizontal intentionality, retaining and protending itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p317]
- Husserl denies that the absolute flow is a process unfolding in time—it is the self-constituting flow of phenomenal time itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p317]
- The living present does not flow through time; time "wells up" within it, described as "standing-streaming.". [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p318]

## Inferences

- Inference: Time-consciousness cannot be reduced to an objective clock because concepts applicable to constituted time (phenomenal time) cannot apply univocally to the time-constituting absolute flow.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the three levels of temporality (external objects, intentional acts, absolute consciousness) are sufficient or whether further levels may be required.
