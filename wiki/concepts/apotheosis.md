# Apotheosis

## Summary

The hero's attainment of divine status through transcending the last terrors of ignorance. When consciousness is annihilated, the hero becomes free of all fear, beyond the reach of change. The androgynous character of divine figures symbolizes the transcendence of duality. The Bodhisattva Avalokiteshvara exemplifies this state—enclosing rather than being enclosed by pain and pleasure.

## Key facts

- When the envelopment of consciousness has been annihilated, the hero becomes free of all fear, beyond the reach of change—this is the release potential within us all attainable through herohood. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p122]
- The world is filled by but does not hold the Bodhisattva; rather, it is he who holds the world, the lotus; pain and pleasure do not enclose him, he encloses them. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p122]
- The androgynous character of the divine—masculine Avalokheshvara, feminine Kwan Yin—conducts the mind beyond objective experience into a symbolic realm where duality is left behind. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p122]
