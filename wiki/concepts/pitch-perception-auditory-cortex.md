# Pitch Perception in Auditory Cortex

## Summary

Pitch perception is crucial for speech and music recognition. Specialized pitch-selective neurons in primate auditory cortex extract pitch from harmonic complex sounds even when the fundamental frequency is physically absent (missing fundamental phenomenon). These neurons are localized in a pitch center at the lateral end of Heschl's gyrus in humans and at the low-frequency border of A1 in marmosets. Harmonic template neurons extract harmonic patterns essential for processing vocalizations and music.

## Key facts

- Pitch-selective neurons are tuned to pure tones with a best frequency and respond to harmonic complexes with a fundamental near their best frequency even when harmonics fall outside the neuron's excitatory frequency response area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p721]
- The pitch center in marmoset monkeys is confined to the low-frequency border of A1, rostral auditory cortex (area R), and lateral belt areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p721]
- Human imaging studies have identified a restricted region at the lateral end of Heschl's gyrus that extracts pitch of harmonic complex sounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p721]
- Pitch perception of the missing fundamental is robust despite spectral filtering that loses harmonic components. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p721]
- Marmoset monkeys can discriminate a missing fundamental in harmonic sounds with precision as small as one semitone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p721]
