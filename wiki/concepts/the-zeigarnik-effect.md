# The Zeigarnik Effect

## Summary

The Zeigarnik effect is the psychological phenomenon that uncompleted tasks and unmet goals tend to pop into one's mind. Recent experiments by Masicampo and Baumeister revealed that the effect is eliminated when a specific plan is made specifying what, where, and when the task will be done. The unconscious mind cannot make plans itself, so it nags the conscious mind to do so—once the plan is formed, the nagging stops.

## Key facts

- Uncompleted tasks and unmet goals tend to pop into one's mind, stopping once the task is completed. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p79]
- Making a specific plan (what, where, when) eliminates the Zeigarnik effect and allows the mind to focus on other tasks. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p80]
- Students who made plans for unfulfilled tasks performed better on reading comprehension than those without plans. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p80]
