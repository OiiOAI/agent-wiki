# Willpower

## Summary

Willpower is the ability to force yourself to take action regardless of motivation. Research by Roy Baumeister shows willpower is a limited daily resource that can be strengthened through exercise, similar to a muscle. Unlike motivation, willpower is reliable and can be scheduled. Willpower is central to the Mini Habits system because the small actions require minimal willpower to execute.

## Key facts

- Willpower is described as reliable if you can force yourself to do something, unlike motivation which fluctuates based on feelings. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p46]
- Roy Baumeister's 1999 study found that students who exercised willpower to improve posture for two weeks showed marked improvement on subsequent self-control measures. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p46]
- A two-month aerobic exercise program resulted in improvements in other unrelated self-control activities, showing willpower can be strengthened. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p47]
- Baumeister's 1996 ego depletion study with chocolate cookies and radishes showed that resisting temptation drains willpower for subsequent tasks. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p48]
- Making difficult decisions also drains willpower, sharing the same energy pool as self-control. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p49]
- The five biggest causes of ego depletion are: effort, perceived difficulty, negative affect, subjective fatigue, and blood glucose levels. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p49]
- Using willpower, you can schedule an activity and do it whether motivated or not, allowing for consistency and habit formation. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p47]
- Walter Mischel's Marshmallow Test at Stanford found that children who delayed gratification showed higher academic achievement with SAT scores 210 points higher on average. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p51]
- Stanford professor Baba Shiv's research showed that memorizing seven digits (vs. two) nearly doubled the chance of choosing cake over fruit salad. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p52]
- Researchers estimate workers are interrupted every 11 minutes and spend nearly a third of their day recovering from distractions. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p37]
- Research on Israeli parole judges showed 65% parole approval at start of day, dropping to near zero by end of each work period. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p54]
- Willpower is compared to a cell phone battery that depletes through use and can be recharged with rest and glucose. [raw/books/productivity/The ONE Thing - Gary Keller.pdf#p52]

## Inferences

- Inference: Since willpower is limited but strengthenable, mini habits work by requiring so little willpower that even depleted people can still perform them.
- Inference: The implication is that one's most important work should be done early in the day when willpower is at full strength.
