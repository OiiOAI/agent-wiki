# Waste

## Summary

Carse develops a philosophical concept of waste that extends beyond physical refuse to encompass human waste—persons declared superfluous by society. Waste is unveiling: it reveals that society is a species of culture that can be other than it is. Since nature cannot be used against itself, we do not exhaust nature but rearrange societal patterns in ways that reduce our ability to respond creatively to natural spontaneity. Human waste (waste persons) are those no longer useful as resources to society—apatrides or noncitizens placed out of view in ghettos, slums, reservations, camps, and remote territories. The creation of human waste parallels the creation of natural waste as a consequence of treating nature as resource rather than source.

## Key facts

- Waste is not unnatural; trash and garbage of a civilization are nature in a form society no longer can exploit for its own ends. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p166]
- Society regards its waste as an unfortunate but necessary consequence of activities—what is left when essential goods are available—but waste is what we have made. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p166]
- Waste is unveiling: it shows us that while nature is not whatever we want it to be, society is only what we want it to be. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p167]
- Waste is the antiproperty that becomes the possession of losers—it accumulates in slums, sewage runs downstream, airborne acid drifts hundreds of miles to settle on lands of those powerless to halt its disposal. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p167]
- A society that creates natural waste creates human waste; waste persons are those no longer useful as resources to a society and have become apatrides or noncitizens. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p168]
- Human trash is not an indirect result of proper societal conduct; it is society's direct product. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p168]
- Waste persons do not constitute an alternative or threatening society; they constitute an unveiling culture and are therefore 'purged.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p169]
