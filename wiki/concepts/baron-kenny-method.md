# Baron-Kenny Method

## Summary

The Baron-Kenny method, developed by Reuben Baron and David Kenny in 1986, was the dominant approach to mediation analysis for decades. It uses linear regression to detect and evaluate mediation by comparing coefficients with and without the mediator in the model. Despite its widespread use (73,000+ citations), the method has fundamental limitations: it only works for linear models, cannot detect interactions between treatment and mediator, and confuses statistical procedure with causal meaning.

## Key facts

- The Baron-Kenny method was developed in 1986 and became the dominant approach to mediation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2950-L2960]
- The method ranks 33rd among most cited scientific papers with over 73,000 citations. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2958-L2965]
- The method fails when there are interactions between treatment and mediator. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3080-L3090]
- David Kenny acknowledged the limitations by 2014, recognizing that 'causal mediation analysis' was needed. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2965-L2975]
