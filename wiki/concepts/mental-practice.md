# Mental Practice

## Summary

Mental practice is the technique of mentally rehearsing an action or skill without physical execution. Research by Alvaro Pascual-Leone demonstrated that imagining an act and doing it engage many of the same brain regions, and that mental practice alone can produce measurable physical changes in the brain's motor system. Studies showed that two hours daily of mental piano practice for five days produced similar brain map changes as physical practice, and that mental muscle contractions can increase actual muscle strength by 22%.

## Key facts

- Mental practice alone produced the same physical changes in the motor system as actually playing a piano piece, with imagining players becoming as accurate as actual players on their third day. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p208]
- When mental practice group was given a single two-hour physical practice session after mental training, their performance improved to the level of the physical practice group's performance at five days. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p208]
- In the Guang Yue and Kelly Cole study, people who imagined doing finger muscle exercises increased muscle strength by 22% compared to 30% for those who actually exercised. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p210]
- Brain scans show that when people visualize a simple object, the primary visual cortex lights up just as it would if actually looking at the object. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p210]
- Mental chess champion Anatoly Sharansky survived years in solitary confinement by playing mental chess, holding games from both perspectives in his head. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p209]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mental practice could be particularly valuable for rehabilitation when physical practice is limited, such as for paralyzed patients or during recovery from injury.
