# Dendritic Spines

## Summary

Dendritic spines are thin processes emerging from dendrites that contain the receptive portion of excitatory synapses. They are sites where concentrations of polyribosomes mediate local protein synthesis, supporting synaptic plasticity. Spines come in several morphological types including thin, stubby, and mushroom-shaped spines, with the mushroom type having larger heads. The smooth endoplasmic reticulum is prominent at the base of spines, and spine morphology changes during development and in response to synaptic activity.

## Key facts

- The smooth endoplasmic reticulum is prominent at the base of thin processes called spines, which are the receptive portion of excitatory synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p184]
- Concentrations of polyribosomes in dendritic spines mediate local protein synthesis, thought to be important for sustaining molecular changes at the synapse that underlie long-term memory and learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p184]
- Only a select group of mRNAs are transported into dendrites, including those encoding actin and cytoskeletal-associated proteins, MAP2, and the alpha-subunit of Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase, which are translated in response to presynaptic neuron activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p146]
- Three types of dendritic spine shapes exist in mature pyramidal cells: thin spines with thinne receptive surfaces, stubby spines that are small and rare, and mushroom-shaped spines with larger heads. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p185]
- Close to 95% of all excitatory inputs in the brain terminate on spines, avoiding dendritic shafts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p343]
- The thin necks of spines provide a barrier to diffusion of various signaling molecules from the spine head to the dendritic shaft. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p343]
- A relatively small Ca2+ current through NMDA receptors can lead to a relatively large increase in [Ca2+] that is localized to the head of the individual spine that is synaptically activated. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p343]
- When a backpropagating action potential is paired with presynaptic stimulation, the spine Ca2+ signal is greater than the linear sum of the individual Ca2+ signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p343]
- Spines implement synapse-specific local learning rules important for the ability of neural networks to store meaningful information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p343]
- Dendritic spines are thin processes at the base of which smooth endoplasmic reticulum is prominent, serving as the receptive portion of excitatory synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p125]
- Three types of dendritic spine shapes exist: thin, stubby, and mushroom-shaped, with mushroom spines having larger heads that contain flat cisternae of smooth endoplasmic reticulum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p127]
- Concentrations of polyribosomes in dendritic spines are presumed to serve local protein synthesis for synaptic modifications. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p125]
- Proteins, ribosomes, and mRNA are concentrated at the base of dendritic spines, with ribosomes selectively concentrated at the junction of the spine and main dendritic shaft. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p135]
