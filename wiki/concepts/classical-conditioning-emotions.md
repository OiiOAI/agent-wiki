# Classical Conditioning of Emotions

## Summary

Classical conditioning provides a fundamental mechanism for emotional learning, allowing neutral environmental stimuli to acquire emotional significance by association with unconditioned emotional stimuli. This process allows emotional systems to respond to predictive cues rather than only direct threats or rewards.

## Key facts

- In classical conditioning of fear, environmental cues become conditional stimuli that can arouse the conditioned response of fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p18]
- The conditioned response may simply be arousal of the unconditioned fear processes rather than a separate learned response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p18]
- Classical conditioning is one of the most powerful and effective ways to study emotional learning in the laboratory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p18]
- Conditioned place preference and aversion measures can evaluate whether animals have emotional experiences during various activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p30]

## Inferences

- Inference: Classical conditioning of emotional responses provides evidence that animals may have internally experienced affective states

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural circuits mediating emotional conditioning versus simple response learning remain under investigation
