# Mnemic Symbol

## Summary

A somatic symptom or perception that serves as a symbolic representation of a traumatic event or experience. Freud applied this concept to hysterical pains (as memories of pains), to motor symptoms like tics (as somatic mnemic symbols of the trauma that produced them), and to subjective sensations like olfactory hallucinations. The mnemic symbol persists as a representation of the original trauma and is reproduced whenever associated memories are activated.

## Key facts

- Some of Frau von N.'s pains were 'memories of pains'—mnemic symbols of the times of agitation and sick-nursing which played a large part in her life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p620]
- Her tic (tongue-clacking) became a somatic mnemic symbol of the whole traumatic scene, fixed at the moment when horror at the noise produced against her will made the moment traumatic. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p622]
- Her stammering was the residue of a similar exciting cause—the attempted convulsive inhibition of the organs of speech made into a symbol of the event for her memory. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p623]
- The symptom of wriggling toes in another patient was a mnemic symbol of the whole depressing train of thought about the size of her feet. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p624]
- Miss Lucy R.'s subjective sensation of burnt pudding was a mnemic symbol of the traumatic scene where conflicting affects of affection for the children and the desire to leave them were in conflict. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p642]
- Freud argued that whatever the origin of motor symptoms, they all have in common that they can be shown to have an original or long-standing connection with traumas and stand as symbols for them in the activities of the memory.
