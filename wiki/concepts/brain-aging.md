# Aging of the Brain

## Summary

The brain undergoes structural and functional changes with normal aging, including brain shrinkage, loss of white matter integrity, decreased dendritic arbor density, reduced synapse density, and altered neurotransmitter levels. Cognitive changes vary widely among individuals but typically include declines in working and long-term memory, visuospatial abilities, and verbal fluency, while vocabulary and comprehension are largely preserved.

## Key facts

- Brain weight decreases approximately 0.2% per year from college age onward and about 0.5% per year in the 70s, with enlargement of ventricles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1381]
- With age, myelin becomes fragmented and lost, leading to decline in white matter integrity, and density of dendritic arbors and synapses decreases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1381]
- Levels of enzymes synthesizing neurotransmitters (dopamine, norepinephrine, acetylcholine) decrease with age, causing functional deficits in corresponding synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1382]
- Genetic pathways involving insulin and insulin-like growth factors regulate aging; disruption of these genes increases resistance to oxidative damage and extends lifespan in model organisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1332]
