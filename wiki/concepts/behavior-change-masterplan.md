# Behavior Change Masterplan

## Summary

The Behavior Change Masterplan is Fogg's systematic three-phase approach for disrupting unwanted habits. It builds on the Behavior Model (B=MAP) and provides a step-by-step algorithm for untangling habits, comparing the process to untying knots in a rope—systematic, gradual, and starting with the easiest tangles rather than applying force all at once.

## Key facts

- Phase 1 focuses on creating new positive habits to build Skills of Change and identity shift before tackling specific bad habits. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p227]
- Phase 2 addresses stopping specific behaviors by altering motivation, ability, or prompts—with prompt being the first priority. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p229]
- Phase 3 involves swapping a new habit for an old one, only attempted after phases 1 and 2 haven't fully resolved the habit. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p243]
- For stopping habits, the Ability Chain factors are: time, money, physical effort, mental effort, and routine conflict. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p235]
- Fogg recommends starting with the easiest specific habit to untangle first, not the hardest one. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p231]
- Demotivators (punishment, threats, negative visualization) are discouraged as they often cause more harm than good. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p242]

## Inferences

- Inference: The masterplan sequence (create new habits first, then stop old ones, then swap) suggests Fogg believes building capability and confidence is more effective than directly attacking problems.
- Inference: The three ways to handle prompts (remove, avoid, ignore) are ordered by effectiveness, with removal being simplest and ignoring being most willpower-dependent.
