# Ecological Optics

## Summary

Ecological optics is the study of the structured information available in light for perceiving the environment. It differs from physical optics (radiant energy), geometrical optics (rays and images), and physiological optics (how light stimulates the eye). Gibson coined the term in 1961, arguing that existing optical sciences did not provide a coherent framework for understanding perception because they failed to address the level of environmental description appropriate for animals.

## Key facts

- Ecological optics rests on the distinction between luminous bodies and nonluminous bodies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p72]
- The term 'ecological optics' first appeared in print in an article in Vision Research in 1961. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p73]
- Ecological optics concerns itself with ambient light rather than radiant light, and with information for perception rather than energy for stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p74]
- Illumination is a fact of higher order than radiation, arising from light reverberating between earth and sky and between facing surfaces. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p74]
- Ecological optics is concerned with many-times-reflected light in the medium (illumination), whereas physical optics is concerned with electromagnetic energy (radiation). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p89]
- Ambient light coming to a point in the air is profoundly different from radiant light leaving a point source—the former has structure, the latter does not. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p89]
- If ambient light were unstructured or undifferentiated, it would provide no information about an environment, although it would stimulate photoreceptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p89]
- The environment does not communicate with observers; the world is specified in the structure of light that reaches us, but it is entirely up to us to perceive it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Ecological Approach to Visual Perception_ Classic - James J_ Gibson.pdf#p88]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between ambient light and radiant light is fundamental to ecological optics because ambient light has structure that can specify the environment, while radiant light does not.
- Inference: Ecological optics provides the foundation for understanding how perception can occur without the transmission of images or signals, contradicting traditional information-processing theories of vision.
