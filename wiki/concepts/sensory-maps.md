# Sensory Maps

## Summary

Sensory maps are orderly topographic representations of sensory receptors in the brain. Wade Marshall's pioneering work showed that different sensory systems share a common organizational logic: all sensory information is organized spatially in the form of precise maps. Touch representation preserves neighboring areas of skin in neighboring areas of somatosensory cortex, the retina is mapped in visual cortex, and sound frequencies are mapped in auditory cortex. Parts of the body most sensitive to touch are represented by the largest cortical areas.

## Key facts

- Marshall discovered that the internal representation of touch is organized spatially, with neighboring areas on the body surface preserved in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p122]
- All sensory information is organized topographically in the brain in the form of precise maps of the body's sensory receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p125]
- The parts of the body most sensitive to touch (fingers, mouth) are represented by the largest areas of the somatosensory cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p124]
