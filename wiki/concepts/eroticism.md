# Eroticism

## Summary

Eroticism, as defined by Perel, is an expanded concept beyond mere sexuality—referring to a quality of aliveness, a pathway to freedom, and a state of grace. It requires separateness and thrives in the space between self and other, fueled by mystery, novelty, and controlled aggression rather than by familiarity and merger.

## Key facts

- Perel defines eroticism as a quality of aliveness and a pathway to freedom, broader than the narrow modern definition of sexuality as merely genital acts. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p13]
- Eroticism requires separateness—it thrives in the space between the self and the other, not in fusion or merger. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p10]
- Erotic desire is accompanied by feelings like aggression, jealousy, and discord that would seem to cramp love's style. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p10]
- Desire is fueled by the unknown and is inherently anxiety-producing; engaging with the mystery of one's partner keeps desire alive. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p29]
- The dynamics that cause conflict in relationships—particularly power, control, dependency, and vulnerability—can become desirable when experienced through the body and eroticized. [raw/books/psychology/Mating in Captivity_ Unlocking Erotic Intelligence - Esther Perel.pdf#p11]

## Inferences

- Inference: Perel's expanded definition of eroticism, learned from her survivor parents, connects to her clinical observation that couples lose desire when they confuse love with merging.
