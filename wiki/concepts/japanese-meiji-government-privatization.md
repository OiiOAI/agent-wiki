# Japanese Meiji Government Privatization

## Summary

Following the Meiji Restoration of 1868, the Japanese government founded and operated numerous industries in transportation, mining, engineering, and weapons manufacture. These enterprises, many of which lost money, were quickly sold off—often at bargain-basement prices—and later became the foundation of large private fortunes. This program represented a broad-ranging privatization effort occurring approximately a hundred years before privatization became fashionable in Europe and Latin America.

## Key facts

- The Japanese government founded and operated numerous industries after 1868, particularly in transportation, mining, engineering, and weapons manufacture. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p378]
- Many of these government enterprises lost money and were quickly sold off, often at bargain-basement prices. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p378]
- These sold enterprises later became the basis of some of the large, private fortunes in later decades. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p378]
- The Japanese government thus engaged in a broad-ranging privatization program approximately a hundred years before this became fashionable in Europe and Latin America. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p378]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Meiji government's willingness to divest from unprofitable state enterprises suggests pragmatic economic governance rather than ideological commitment to privatization.
