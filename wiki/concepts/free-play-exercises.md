# Free-Play Exercises

## Summary

Free-play exercises are tactical training scenarios with no predetermined scenario or rules, where participants face unexpected developments and must make decisions under uncertainty. They were introduced to the Marine Corps by General Al Gray at Fort Pickett in 1981 and represent a core element of maneuver warfare training by testing combat leaders' ability to think independently rather than follow scripted plans.

## Key facts

- General Al Gray ordered the first free-play exercise at Fort Pickett, Virginia, in late summer 1981, choosing an unfamiliar base so no one would have terrain advantage. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p378]
- Traditional Marine Corps tactical exercises were choreographed, with battalion commanders receiving orders specifying which objectives to seize, creating no incentive for independent thinking. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p378]
- Free-play exercises simulated unexpected developments such as an 82nd Airborne element parachuting on a flank without warning, testing real decision-making ability. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p378]
- Careerists hated free-play because losing could end promotion chances, while true combat leaders loved it. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p378]
- Free-play exercises included post-exercise critiques by enlisted men and junior officers, including contradicting battalion commanders. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p378]
- Lieutenant Colonel Ray Smith, a graduate of the Fort Pickett exercises, led Marines in Grenada with tactics that exemplified maneuver warfare principles. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p381]
- Brigadier General Mike Myatt, another Fort Pickett graduate, commanded the 1st Marine Division in the Gulf War where Marines bypassed strong points and penetrated deep behind Iraqi lines. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p419]

## Inferences

- Inference: The success of Fort Pickett graduates in Grenada and the Gulf War validated free-play exercises as a method for identifying and developing real combat leaders.
