# Declarative and Nondeclarative Memory

## Summary

The distinction between declarative (explicit) and nondeclarative (implicit) memory was clarified through research with amnesic patients. Learning with awareness (declarative memory) is impaired in amnesia, whereas learning without awareness (nondeclarative memory) is typically preserved. This fundamental distinction underlies much of modern memory research and was demonstrated in Henry through preserved motor-skill learning, classical conditioning, and priming effects.

## Key facts

- Learning with awareness (declarative memory) is impaired in amnesia, whereas learning without awareness (nondeclarative memory) typically is not. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p350]
- Decades of research established that short-term and long-term memory are separate processes that depend on different brain circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p350]
