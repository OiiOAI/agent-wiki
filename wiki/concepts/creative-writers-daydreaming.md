# Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming

## Summary

Freud's paper comparing the creative writer to the daydreaming child, arguing that both create worlds of phantasy invested with large amounts of emotion and separated sharply from reality. The paper identifies daydreams as the key to understanding creative writing: both are driven by unsatisfied wishes (predominantly erotic or ambitious), shaped by three periods of time (current impression, infantile memory, and future fulfillment), and employ condensation and distortion to bypass the ego's censorship.

## Key facts

- The creative writer does the same as the child at play: he creates a world of phantasy which he takes very seriously while separating it sharply from reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3028]
- As people grow up they cease to play and give up the yield of pleasure from playing, but replace it by phantasying—building castles in the air and creating daydreams—because we can never give anything up, only exchange one thing for another. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3029]
- A happy person never phantasies, only an unsatisfied one; the driving forces of phantasies are unsatisfied wishes, and every phantasy is the fulfilment of a wish, a correction of unsatisfying reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3031]
- Phantasies hover between three moments of time—the current impression that provokes a major wish, a memory of an earlier experience (usually infantile) in which the wish was fulfilled, and a future situation representing fulfillment of the wish. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3031]
- Night dreams are phantasies like daydreams, as language recognized by calling daydreams by that name; night dreams include wishes of which we are ashamed that have been repressed and can only express themselves in distorted form. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3033]
- The creative writer transforms his egoistic daydreams by altering and disguising them, and bribes us by purely formal aesthetic yield of pleasure (fore-pleasure), making possible the release of greater pleasure from deeper psychical sources. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3036]
- A strong experience in the present awakens in the creative writer a memory of an earlier experience (usually from childhood) from which a wish proceeds that finds fulfillment in the creative work, which exhibits elements of both the recent occasion and the old memory. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3035]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's observation that myths and legends are 'distorted vestiges of the wishful phantasies of whole nations, the secular dreams of youthful humanity' extends his day-dreaming theory to collective cultural products.
