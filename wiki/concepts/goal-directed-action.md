# Goal-Directed Action

## Summary

Goal-directed actions are behaviors whose performance depends on: (1) the current value of their outcome, and (2) the extant contingency between the action and its outcome. Unlike habits, goal-directed actions remain sensitive to changes in outcome value and can be rapidly updated when contingencies change.

## Key facts

- An action is considered goal-directed if its performance respects the current value of its outcome and the contingency between action and outcome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p71]
- The posterior dorsomedial striatum appears to be the neural locus of goal-directed action, necessary for both learning and expression of action-outcome contingencies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p80]
- Unlike posterior dorsomedial striatum lesions, lesions of prelimbic cortex or basolateral amygdala spare the performance but not the acquisition of goal-directed actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p75]

## Inferences

- Inference: Different brain structures support different components of goal-directed action: learning (PL, MD, BLA), value retrieval (IC, mOFC), and performance (NAc core, pDMS).
- Inference: The pDMS integrates information from these structures to form the neural substrate where action-outcome learning and value information converge.
