# Spinal Shock

## Summary

Spinal shock is a period of areflexia or hyporeflexia that immediately follows complete transection of the spinal cord. During spinal shock, all spinal reflexes below the level of transection are reduced or completely suppressed. Over weeks to months, spinal reflexes gradually return, often greatly exaggerated, as in the case where light touch to the foot may elicit strong flexion withdrawal.

## Key facts

- Spinal shock follows complete transection of the spinal cord, causing all spinal reflexes below the lesion level to be reduced or suppressed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Spinal reflexes gradually return over weeks and months, often greatly exaggerated, with light touch potentially eliciting strong flexion withdrawal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Approximately 11,000 Americans sustain spinal cord injuries annually, with more than 250,000 having permanent disability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Approximately 11,000 Americans sustain spinal cord injuries each year, with more than half producing permanent disability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p860]
- The initial shock is thought to result from the sudden withdrawal of tonic facilitatory influence from the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p860]
- Recovery mechanisms may include sprouting of afferent sensory terminals and denervation supersensitivity owing to increased numbers of postsynaptic receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p860]
- The period of recovery from spinal shock is much shorter in animals than in humans: rarely more than a week in nonhuman primates, only a few hours in cats and dogs, reflecting greater supraspinal influence on spinal circuits in humans.
