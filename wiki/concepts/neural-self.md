# Neural Self

## Summary

The neural self refers to the biological substrate of selfhood and subjectivity, which emerges from the continuous reconstruction of two sets of representations: (1) representations of an individual's autobiography (past experiences and future plans), and (2) primordial representations of the individual's body state. Subjectivity emerges when the brain produces images of an organism in the act of perceiving and responding to an object, creating a third-party perspective on the self.

## Key facts

- The neural self resides with the continuous reactivation of two sets of representations: autobiographical memories (past and future) and primordial representations of body state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p239]
- The self is not a homunculus but rather an endlessly reconstructed biological state based on invariant organism structure and slowly evolving autobiographical data. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p239]
- Subjectivity emerges when the brain produces not just images of an object and images of organism responses, but a third kind of image: an organism in the act of perceiving and responding to an object. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p243]
- Complete anosognosia (denial of deficits) partially demolishes the substrate of the neural self, leaving patients unable to refer their condition to themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p237]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neural self requires not wakefulness alone but a specific combination of autobiographical representations and body state representations that can be perturbed by perception.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise third-party ensemble structures responsible for generating subjective experience have not been definitively identified.
