# East-West Cognitive Differences

## Summary

East Asians and Westerners perceive the world and think about it differently, with East Asians showing a more holistic approach, greater reliance on context, and more even use of both hemispheres. Western strategies appear steeply skewed towards the left hemisphere, while East Asian cultures use strategies of both hemispheres more evenly.

## Key facts

- East Asians are more likely to attend to the broad perceptual and conceptual field, noticing relationships and changes, and grouping objects according to family resemblances based on appreciation of the whole. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p622]
- Westerners are significantly more likely to give one-dimensional, rule-based responses based on individual components of stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p622]
- East Asians use a more dialectical mode of reasoning, more willing to accept contradictory perspectives, whereas Westerners adopt a more either/or approach. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p623]
- Japanese scenes were found to be more ambiguous and contain more elements than American scenes; both Japanese and American participants primed with Japanese scenes attended more to contextual information. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p623]
- Every cognitive difference between Westerners and East Asians takes the form of greater reliance on the left hemisphere by Westerners, with no single difference suggesting greater reliance on the right. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p626]
- Evidence suggests East Asian cultures use strategies of both hemispheres more evenly, while Western strategies are steeply skewed towards the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p627]
