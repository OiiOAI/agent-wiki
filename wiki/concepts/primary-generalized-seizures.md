# Primary Generalized Seizures

## Summary

Primary generalized seizures disrupt normal brain activity in both cerebral hemispheres simultaneously, unlike focal seizures which begin in a localized region. The most studied type is the typical absence seizure (petit mal), characterized by the distinctive 3 Hz spike-wave EEG pattern. These seizures are thought to arise from thalamocortical circuits, potentially combined with a general abnormality in membrane excitability of cortical neurons.

## Key facts

- Primary generalized seizures disrupt normal brain activity in both cerebral hemispheres simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1180]
- The characteristic 3 Hz spike-wave EEG pattern of typical absence seizures was first identified by Hans Berger in 1933. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1180]
- Typical absence seizures begin suddenly, last 10 to 30 seconds, and produce loss of awareness with only minor motor manifestations such as blinking or lip smacking. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1181]
- The thalamocortical connections that participate in the generation of sleep spindles are thought to be essential for the generation of primary generalized seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1182]
- In typical absence seizures, the summated activity of depolarizing bursts produces the spike while the summated inhibition produces the wave of the spike-wave EEG pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1182]
