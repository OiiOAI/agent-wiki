# Polygyny

## Summary

Polygyny is a mating system in which one male mates with multiple females. The chunk discusses its prevalence in human societies despite most actual relationships being monogamous, and its correlation with sexual dimorphism in animals.

## Key facts

- Most human marriage relationships are monogamous even though most societies allow or encourage polygynous marriages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p526]
- Polygyny requires significant accumulation of resources and power by particular males at the expense of many others, leaving the rest with insufficient resources to become polygynous. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p526]
- High degrees of sexual dimorphism are highly correlated with degree of polygyny in a species, favored by sexual selection mediated by threat and fighting behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p526]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation between sexual dimorphism and polygyny suggests that physical competition among males for mates drives evolutionary changes in body size
- Inference: Negative consequences of polygynous systems are not equitably distributed between men and women or between rich and poor
