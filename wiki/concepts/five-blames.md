# Five Blames

## Summary

The Five Blames is the negative counterpart to the Five Whys, occurring when frustrated team members point fingers at each other trying to assign fault rather than understanding systemic causes. It represents a psychological trap where people assume mistakes are due to defects in others' department, knowledge, or character rather than bad processes. The antidote includes ensuring all affected people participate in root cause analysis and adopting the mantra: 'If a mistake happens, shame on us for making it so easy to make that mistake.'

## Key facts

- The Five Blames occurs when instead of asking 'why' repeatedly to understand what went wrong, frustrated teammates start pointing fingers at each other to decide who is at fault. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p217]
- Whoever is left out of the Five Whys discussion ends up being the target for blame, whether junior employee or CEO. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p218]
- The goal of Five Whys is to see the objective truth that chronic problems are caused by bad process, not bad people. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p218]
- To escape the Five Blames, the most senior people should repeat: 'If a mistake happens, shame on us for making it so easy to make that mistake.'. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p219]
