# Reciprocal Innervation

## Summary

Reciprocal innervation is a principle of motor organization, described by Sherrington, whereby activation of one group of muscles is accompanied by inhibition of their antagonist muscles. This is mediated through Ia inhibitory interneurons that receive input from Ia afferents and project to antagonist motor neurons.

## Key facts

- Reciprocal innervation is excitation of one group of muscles accompanied by inhibition of their antagonists, a key principle of motor organization described by Sherrington. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p843]
- Ia inhibitory interneurons receive excitatory input from Ia afferents and make inhibitory connections with alpha motor neurons innervating antagonist muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p848]
- The latency between afferent volley and inhibitory postsynaptic potential in motor neurons following antagonist muscle stretch is approximately 1.6 ms, indicating a disynaptic pathway through Ia inhibitory interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p848]
