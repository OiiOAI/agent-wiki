# The Implicit and the Explicit

## Summary

McGilchrist argues that metaphor (subserved by the right hemisphere) comes before denotation (subserved by the left hemisphere), making the implicit epistemologically and historically prior to the explicit. The roots of explicitness lie in the implicit—one cannot unfold something unless it is already folded, cannot draw away unless there is something to draw from. The left hemisphere's task is to bring things into focus, rendering the implicit explicit, but this process returns us to what we already know. Many things important to human life simply cannot withstand close attention, as their nature is to be indirect or implicit.

## Key facts

- Metaphorical meaning is prior to abstraction and explicitness, both historically and epistemologically. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p255]
- The roots of explicitness lie in the implicit; one cannot unfold (ex-plicare) unless something is already folded. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p256]
- Lichtenberg stated that most expressions are metaphorical and the philosophy of our forefathers lies hidden in them. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p256]
- Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger, Scheler, and later Wittgenstein each tried to take philosophy beyond the explicit. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p256]
- Explicitness forces the sheering away of context and loss of transparency or semi-transparency. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p259]

## Inferences

- Inference: The process of rendering implicit experience explicit is necessary for control and manipulation, but destructive when not reintegrated into the holistic context provided by the right hemisphere
