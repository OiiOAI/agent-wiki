# Design (in Strategy)

## Summary

Design in strategy refers to the engineering of fit among parts, specifying how actions and resources will be combined. A design-type strategy is an adroit configuration of resources and actions that yields an advantage in a challenging situation. Design problems involve sharp trade-offs: given a set bundle of resources, the greater the competitive challenge, the greater the need for clever tight integration of resources and actions.

## Key facts

- Design is the engineering of fit among parts, specifying how actions and resources will be combined. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p41]
- A design-type strategy is an adroit configuration of resources and actions that yields an advantage in a challenging situation. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p55]
- Resources and tight coordination are partial substitutes—if the organization has few resources, the challenge can be met only by clever tight integration. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p55]
- Three aspects of strategy in bold relief are premeditation, anticipation of others' behavior, and the purposeful design of coordinated actions. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p54]
