# Autonomy in Children

## Summary

Autonomy means self-governance—living according to choices one makes. Unlike mere independence, truly autonomous actions are those you fully own and endorse with your deepest self, without coercion from either the outside or one's own unchecked impulses. Research shows that children raised with autonomy develop stronger intrinsic motivation, are more likely to understand the value of truth-telling, and are more generous than children raised under controlling or coercive conditions.

## Key facts

- Autonomy means self-governance, or rule by the self, and an autonomous person lives according to the choices he or she makes. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p228]
- Truly autonomous actions are those you fully own and endorse with your deepest self, without coercion from either the outside or your own unchecked impulses. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p228]
- Children raised in a barter-or-bribe economy don't develop the strong autonomous self that can create distance between real desires and preprogrammed responses. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p230]
- People taught to act in the expectation of extrinsic rewards turn out to be less happy, less successful, and have less satisfying relationships than those who are internally motivated. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p230]
- Children who are given free choice are more generous than those who are coerced. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p232]
- There is a direct connection between how much a parent tries to control his or her teen's behavior and thoughts and how well the teen understands the value of telling the truth. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: Autonomy-supportive parenting practices—explaining rationales, offering genuine choices, and being open to the child's thoughts—foster both ethical development and emotional resilience.
