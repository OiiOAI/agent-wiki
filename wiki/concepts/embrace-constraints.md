# Embrace Constraints

## Summary

The concept of embracing constraints argues that limited resources force creativity and efficiency rather than hindering them. The authors draw parallels to writers who use formal constraints like sonnets or haiku to spark creativity, and cite Southwest Airlines' decision to fly only Boeing 737s as a business example. When building Basecamp, the seven-hour time difference between team members and lack of outside funding forced simplicity. The authors continue to impose self-created constraints like limiting products to one or two people at a time.

## Key facts

- The authors argue that constraints are advantages in disguise that force creativity and eliminate waste. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p53]
- Southwest Airlines flies only Boeing 737s, allowing any pilot, flight attendant, or ground crew member to work any flight and all parts to fit all planes, resulting in lower costs and easier operations. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p53]
- When building Basecamp, 37signals had a seven-hour time difference between principals, a small team, and no outside funding, which forced them to keep the product simple. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p53]
- The authors continue to impose constraints even with more resources available, keeping only one or two people working on a product at a time. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p54]

## Inferences

- Inference: Self-imposed constraints can be more valuable than external ones because they are chosen deliberately to achieve specific outcomes.
