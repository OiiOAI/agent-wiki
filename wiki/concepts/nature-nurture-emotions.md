# Nature vs Nurture in Emotions

## Summary

Panksepp argues that emotional processes emerge from an epigenetic mixture where nature provides intrinsic potentials through genetically dictated brain circuits, and nurture provides opportunities for these potentials to manifest in diverse ways. Best estimates suggest approximately 50% heritability for major personality and temperamental variables.

## Key facts

- Heritability estimates using identical twins reared apart suggest about 50% of observed variation in human personality must be attributed to nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p16]
- Every phenotype is epigenetic, a mixture of nature and nurture; identical seeds planted in different environments yield different plants. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p16]
- Early experiences, especially during development, can permanently change the fine details of brain function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p16]
- Experience is more influential in changing quantitative expressions of neural systems rather than their essential nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p16]

## Inferences

- Inference: No behaviors are ever inherited in a formal sense; only potentials for bodily tissues are inherited, including dispositions to feel, think, and act

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Specific heritability estimates vary depending on cultural context and measurement approaches
