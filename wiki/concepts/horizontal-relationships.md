# Horizontal Relationships

## Summary

Horizontal relationships refer to equal relationships between people based on respect rather than hierarchy. The philosopher argues that Adlerian psychology refutes all forms of vertical relationships and proposes that all interpersonal relationships be horizontal. In horizontal relationships, one neither praises nor rebukes, but instead uses encouragement—expressing gratitude and respect without judging. This contrasts with vertical relationships where praise and criticism establish a hierarchy of ability.

## Key facts

- In Adlerian psychology, one must not praise and one must not rebuke. This is the standpoint of Adlerian psychology in all forms of communication with other people. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p202]
- Praise is described as 'the passing of judgment by a person of ability on a person of no ability' and is a form of manipulation. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p203]
- The feeling of inferiority arises within vertical relationships; if one can build horizontal relationships that are 'equal but not the same' for all people, there will no longer be any room for inferiority complexes to emerge. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p204]
- Encouragement, based on horizontal relationships, assists others without intervening in their tasks. [raw/books/psychology/The courage to be disliked_ the Japanese phenomenon that - Ichiro Kishimi, and Fumitake Koga.pdf#p207]

## Inferences

- Inference: The philosopher distinguishes encouragement from praise because praise implies the recipient lacks ability, whereas encouragement acknowledges capability and focuses on contribution.
