# Creativity and Dreams

## Summary

Dreams may facilitate waking creativity both through explicit problem solving (as in famous examples of Mendeleyev's periodic table, Loewi's neurotransmitters, and Kekulé's benzene ring) and through the creative exploration of associative neural networks that characterizes normal dreaming.

## Key facts

- Dmitri Mendeleyev reportedly saw the periodic table of elements fully formed in a dream in 1869. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p177]
- Otto Loewi dreamed of an experiment demonstrating chemical neurotransmission that won him the Nobel Prize in 1936. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p177]
- August Kekulé discovered benzene's ring structure after seeing a snake seize its own tail in a dream, leading him to hypothesize a ring configuration for the six-carbon molecule. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p178]
- Creative breakthroughs in dreams are rare; most famous problem-solving dreams occurred during the hypnagogic period after extended prior work on the problem. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p196]
- According to NEXTUP, the true creativity in dreaming lies in the creative exploration of associative neural networks, finding weak associations potentially useful for addressing problems. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p184]
