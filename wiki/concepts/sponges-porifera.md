# Sponges (Porifera)

## Summary

Sponges (Porifera) are the simplest animals and are believed to have been the first animals to evolve, appearing approximately 800 million years ago. Despite being multicellular, they lack true tissues and organs, occupying an intermediate position between unicellular protozoa and more complex eumetazoan animals. Their choanocyte cells directly link them evolutionarily to choanoflagellates.

## Key facts

- Sponges are considered the first animals, having appeared around 800 million years ago based on molecular evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p116]
- Sponges lack true tissues, organs, and nervous systems; they are sessile creatures attached to substrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p123]
- Choanocyte cells in sponges have flagella and collars structurally similar to choanoflagellates, linking sponges to their protozoan ancestors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p123]
- Sponges feed by moving water through their inner cavity using choanocyte flagella beating, filtering bacteria as food. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p125]
- Sponges reproduce both asexually through budding and sexually by producing sperm and eggs from choanocytes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p126]
