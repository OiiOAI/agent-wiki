# Visual Agnosia

## Summary

Visual agnosia is a loss in the ability to recognize objects despite intact visual perception. There are two major categories: apperceptive agnosia (inability to construct sensory representations of visual stimuli) and associative agnosia (inability to associate sensory representations with meaning, function, or utility). Apperceptive agnosia results from damage to the posterior inferior temporal cortex, while associative agnosia results from damage to the anterior inferior temporal cortex.

## Key facts

- Visual agnosia is a condition in which patients cannot recognize visual objects despite relatively normal visual perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p614]
- Apperceptive agnosia results from disruption of the first stage of object recognition: integration of visual features into sensory representations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p614]
- Associative agnosia results from disruption of the second stage of object recognition: association of the sensory representation with knowledge of the object's meaning or function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p614]
- Apperceptive agnosia is most common following damage to the posterior inferior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p614]
- Associative agnosia is more common following damage to the anterior inferior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p614]
