# Moral Dumbfounding

## Summary

Moral dumbfounding is the striking phenomenon Haidt discovered in his research where people make strong moral judgments but cannot provide adequate reasons to justify those judgments. When challenged to explain their condemnation of harmless taboo violations (such as consensual incest between adults using contraception), subjects cling to their initial judgments while stammering and grasping for reasons. This finding demonstrates that moral reasoning often follows moral judgment rather than causing it, supporting the social intuitionist model's claim that intuitions come first and reasoning second.

## Key facts

- In his dissertation research, Haidt found that 38 percent of subjects invented victims when confronted with harmless taboo violations, even claiming absurd harms like toilet clogs from flag rags. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p39]
- Even when interviewers corrected invented victim claims, subjects still refused to say the act was OK, saying 'I know it's wrong, but I just can't think of a reason why.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p39]
- In a study with Scott Murphy, Haidt used 'harmless taboo stories' including incest and cadaver-eating scenarios. When challenged, subjects generated far more reasons and discarded far more reasons than in standard moral dilemmas, showing they were flailing to justify gut feelings. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p52-55]
- One subject, after failing to provide reasons, exclaimed: 'I really—um, I mean, there's just no way I could change my mind but I just don't know how to—how to show what I'm feeling, what I feel about it. It's crazy!'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p55]
