# Embodied Cognition

## Summary

Embodied cognition refers to how physical and sensory experiences influence mental states and judgments. Research demonstrates that bodily changes such as holding warm versus cold objects, smiling versus frowning, or changes in temperature affect perceptions, social judgments, and emotional experiences, supporting the broader mind-body unity framework.

## Key facts

- Participants holding hot coffee perceived described people as 'warmer' than those holding iced coffee in Bargh's Yale study. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p112]
- Holding a warm drink led people to feel closer to those they thought about than when holding a cold beverage. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p112]
- People are happier and more satisfied with their lives when weather is warm. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p112]
- Naomi Eisenberger found that brain patterns for social rejection in the anterior cingulate cortex are the same as those for physical pain. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p112]
- Participants holding a pencil with their lips (mimicking a frown) were less amused by cartoons than those holding it with their teeth (mimicking a smile). [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p113]

## Inferences

- Inference: Physical pain could potentially be ameliorated by psychological means since physical and psychological pain patterns occur in the same brain region.
