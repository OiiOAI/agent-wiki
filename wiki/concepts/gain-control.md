# Gain Control

## Summary

Gain control is a fundamental neural mechanism that amplifies or attenuates signals through multiplication or division, rather than simple addition or subtraction. It enables coordinate transformations, normalization of sensory inputs, attention, and velocity-based modulation of place cell firing rates.

## Key facts

- Gain modulation requires two sources: an amplifier and a modulator, which when multiplied generate outputs larger or smaller than expected from simple addition. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p300]
- Gain control in individual neurons occurs through shunting, where increased membrane conductance reduces the efficacy of synaptic currents via Ohm's law. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p301]
- GABAA receptor-mediated inhibition provides negative gain (division) on excitatory inputs by shunting neurons without changing membrane potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p303]
- Short-term synaptic plasticity turns neurons into 'ratio meters' because depressing synapses become less efficient with increasing spike rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p304]
- Subcortical neuromodulators (acetylcholine, norepinephrine, serotonin, dopamine, histamine) affect excitability by affecting conductance, exerting multiplicative gain control. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p305]
- Gain fields in parietal cortex allow multiplication of firing rates induced by receptive field profile with modulation by eye position. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p308]
- Selective attention multiplies or gain-modulates neuronal response by a constant factor without changing tuning characteristics. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p313]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gain control through dynamic synapse changes can adjust the rate of change across neurons in sequences, conceptually replacing the concept of time (Chapter 10 reference).
- Inference: Attention may be viewed as internalized gain control, substituting for velocity in internalized brain functions like memory and imagination.
