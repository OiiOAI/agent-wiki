# Hysterogenic Zone

## Summary

A region of the body that becomes abnormally sensitive to pain, pressure, or tactile stimulation in hysteria, and whose stimulation can evoke hysterical symptoms or attacks. Freud identified that stimulation of such zones in a hysteric produces expressions of pleasure rather than pain, because the stimulation connects with the thoughts and memories associated with that body part. The localization of hysterogenic zones can be explained by their connection with specific traumatic experiences.

## Key facts

- When Fräulein Elisabeth von R.'s hyperalgesic skin and muscles were pressed or pinched, her face assumed an expression of pleasure rather than pain, because stimulation had touched upon a hysterogenic zone connected with the thoughts concealed behind the pain. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p661]
- Her right thigh became the hysterical pain zone because her father used to rest his leg on that spot while she renewed his bandage—a hundred repetitions that established it as the hysterogenic zone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p671]
- Freud found that the right leg became painful when memories concerned nursing her sick father, while the left leg became painful when memories concerned her dead sister and her brothers-in-law, suggesting different zones corresponded to different psychical determinants. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p672]
