# Population Growth

## Summary

Population growth is identified as a driver of climate change alongside per-capita pollution. Earth's population tripled from 3 billion in 1960 to nearly 8 billion today. The old brain's drive to have children conflicts with neocortical understanding that unlimited growth is unsustainable, representing a fundamental challenge to long-term human survival.

## Key facts

- Earth's population was about 3 billion in 1960 and is now approaching 8 billion. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p178]
- Human-caused climate change results from two factors: the number of people on Earth and how much pollution each person creates. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p178]
- The old brain wants to have children while the neocortex understands that unlimited population growth is unsustainable. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p179]
- Intelligence has been used to improve farming and create food distribution technologies that have reduced hunger during population nearly tripling. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p179]
- Either population growth stops or sometime in the future there will be great human suffering on Earth. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hawkins argues that empowering women to control fertility is the 'simple and clever solution' to population growth because it addresses old brain drives through neocortical planning.
