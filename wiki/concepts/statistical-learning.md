# Statistical Learning

## Summary

Statistical learning is the fundamental ability humans are born with to detect patterns and regularities in sensory input. Beginning in infancy, the brain computes probabilities of which sights, sounds, smells, touches, and interoceptive sensations go together. This learning is enhanced by words, which signal intentional communication and invite infants to form purely mental concepts beyond perceptual similarities.

## Key facts

- Humans are born with the ability to learn patterns and compute probabilities from sensory regularities. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p113]
- Babies learn statistical regularities in sound and vision, and can generalize associations across senses. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p114]
- By one year of age, statistical learning reduces infants' ability to hear differences between sounds only to those in their native languages. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p114]
- Ten- to fourteen-month-old infants can estimate probabilities to maximize desired outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p114]
- Sixteen-month-old children can infer subjective preferences of others that differ from their own. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p115]
- Words spoken by adults with intentional communication invite infants to form concepts. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p116]

## Inferences

- Inference: Statistical learning in infancy lays the foundation for the conceptual system, but words are necessary to create purely mental, goal-based concepts that transcend perceptual similarities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise developmental timeline of statistical learning versus word-based concept formation remains uncertain.
