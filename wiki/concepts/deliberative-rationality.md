# Deliberative Rationality

## Summary

Deliberative rationality is the hypothetical process by which a person would choose a rational plan if consequences were accurately foreseen and adequately realized in imagination. A person's good is determined by the plan he would choose with full deliberative rationality. It involves reviewing what it would be like to carry out plans in light of all relevant facts.

## Key facts

- The rational plan for a person is the one which he would choose with deliberative rationality, understood as the outcome of careful reflection. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p371]
- Deliberative rationality assumes no errors of calculation, correct assessment of facts, and adequate knowledge of what one really wants. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p372]
- The objectively rational plan for a person determines his real good; the subjectively rational plan is what he follows with available information. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p372]
- A rational person will not usually continue to deliberate until finding the best plan; often he will be content with a satisfactory plan meeting minimum conditions. [raw/books/philosophy/A Theory of Justice_ Original Edition - Rawls, John.pdf#p372]
