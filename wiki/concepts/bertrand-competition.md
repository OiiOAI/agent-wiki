# Bertrand Competition

## Summary

Bertrand competition describes markets where products are standardized, competitors have excess capacity, prices are transparent to buyers, and the market responds quickly to price cuts. In Bertrand competition, prices are driven down to cash costs, making profitability nearly impossible without significant cost advantages or market power.

## Key facts

- Bertrand competition occurs when the market responds quickly and decisively to price cuts, with plenty of capacity, prices easily communicated, and products standardized. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p175]
- In Bertrand competition, prices are driven all the way down to cash costs. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p175]
- The only way to win in Bertrand competition is to drive out competitors and monopolize supply or have significantly lower real costs than anyone else. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p175]

## Inferences

- Inference: Successful strategies are built on supplying better quality, specializing in market segments, or leveraging customer inertia rather than competing purely on price.
