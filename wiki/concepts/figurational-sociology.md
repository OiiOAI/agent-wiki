# Figurational Sociology

## Summary

Figurational sociology, developed by Norbert Elias, is a framework for understanding social reality through the concept of figuration—interdependent networks of people. The concept makes it possible to think of people as both individuals and as societies simultaneously, overcoming the dichotomy between 'individual' and 'society.' Figurations are social processes involving the interweaving of people, with power central to these constantly fluctuating equilibria. Elias applied this framework to analyze the civilizing process in Western societies.

## Key facts

- Figuration 'makes it possible to resist the socially conditioned pressure to split and polarize our conception of mankind, which has prevented us from thinking of people as individuals at the same time as thinking of them as societies.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p413]
- Figurations are social processes involving the 'interweaving' of people, not structures external to and coercive of relationships, but those interrelationships themselves. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p413]
- Individuals are seen as open and interdependent; figurations are made up of such individuals. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p413]
- Power is central to social figurations, which are 'a fluctuating, tensile equilibrium, a balance of power moving to and fro.'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p413]
- The concept of figuration applies at both micro and macro levels and to every social phenomenon between those poles. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p413]

## Inferences

- Inference: Figurational sociology challenges the tendency in Western thought to see individuals and societies as fundamentally opposed, instead viewing them as different aspects of the same interweaving of people.
