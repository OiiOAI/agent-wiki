# The Do-Nothing Room

## Summary

The Do-Nothing Room was a solution developed through NVC dialogue with troubled students at an alternative school. It provided a space for students who were upset or not ready to study, and for those whose behavior prevented others from learning. When a teacher skilled in NVC was placed there, productive conversations occurred. The success came from students creating it themselves and making its protective purpose clear.

## Key facts

- The Do-Nothing Room was created at an alternative school after dialogue with students who expressed disgust with teachers who stood around doing nothing when problems occurred. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p258]
- Students suggested the room for those who don't feel like doing anything or who are too upset to study, where they could go until they felt ready to participate. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p258]
- The Do-Nothing Room succeeded when students who devised it made its protective purpose clear—to protect the rights of students who wanted to learn. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p259]
- The teacher placed in the Do-Nothing Room was the one who had best mastered NVC, and she had productive talks with the children who came in. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p259]
- The Do-Nothing Room was an immense success in restoring order because it was known to be a product of student brainstorming rather than staff decree. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p259]

## Inferences

- Inference: The success demonstrated that there were other means of resolving conflicts besides withdrawal or punitive force when both parties felt heard.
