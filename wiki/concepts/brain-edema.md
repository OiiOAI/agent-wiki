# Brain Edema

## Summary

Brain edema is an increase in brain volume due to increased water content, occurring as vasogenic edema (extracellular fluid accumulation from increased capillary permeability) or cytotoxic edema (intracellular swelling of neurons and glia from ATP pump failure or osmotic imbalance).

## Key facts

- Vasogenic edema is attributed to increased permeability of brain capillary endothelial cells, which increases the volume of the extracellular fluid; white matter is generally affected more than gray. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1628]
- Cytotoxic edema refers to the intracellular swelling of injured neurons, glia, and endothelial cells, occurring in hypoxia when failure of the ATP-dependent Na+-K+ pump allows Na+ and water to accumulate within cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1628]
- Aquaporins (AQP0-AQP10) are water channels that regulate fluid homeostasis by facilitating water movement through cellular membranes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1628]
- AQP4 protein appears in endothelial cells and perivascular astrocyte processes concurrent with blood-brain barrier development and expression is altered in disorders producing BBB dysfunction and cerebral edema. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1628]
