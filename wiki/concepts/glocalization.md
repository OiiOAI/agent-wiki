# Glocalization

## Summary

Glocalization is Roland Robertson's concept describing the interpenetration of the global and the local, resulting in unique outcomes in different geographic areas. It represents a critique of both pure homogenization and pure heterogenization perspectives on globalization.

## Key facts

- Glocalization involves the interpenetration of the global and the local resulting in unique outcomes in different geographic areas. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p489]
- The world is growing more pluralistic and glocalization theory is exceptionally alert to differences within and between areas of the world. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p489]
- Individuals and local groups have great power to adapt, innovate, and maneuver within a glocalized world. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p489]
- Glocalization tends to be tied to 'something' (full forms with distinctive content) and stands in opposition to grobalization's spread of 'nothing'. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p489]
- Related concepts include hybridization (combining elements from different cultures), creolization (mixing of previously unintelligible languages and cultures), and glocalization itself. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p490]

## Inferences

- Inference: Glocalization represents a middle ground between cultural convergence (homogenization) and cultural differentialism (perpetual heterogenization).
- Inference: The concept captures how global processes are filtered and transformed by local contexts, producing hybrid forms.
