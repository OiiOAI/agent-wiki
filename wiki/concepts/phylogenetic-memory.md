# Phylogenetic Memory

## Summary

Phylogenetic memory refers to the genetic memory accrued and pruned over many lifetimes, representing structural morphologies and basic functional brain wiring present at birth. It includes the opposable thumb, brain architecture, and functional modules like FAPs. This differs from referential memory (individual experience) and provides the structural and functional a prioris of the body and brain.

## Key facts

- Phylogenetic memory determines species form—the whole animal and organ architectures echoed across millennia. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p180]
- The nervous system at birth is not a blank slate (tabula rasa) but is fully wired with incredible amounts of knowledge derived from genetic wiring. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p180]
- A newborn wildebeest can run within five seconds of birth, demonstrating that FAPs like running are phylogenetically prewired, not learned through ontogeny. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p183]
- The calf's ability to recognize danger (lion) and contextually implement fear to release running demonstrates that emotional states and their link to FAPs are also prewired at birth. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p184]
- Phylogenetic memory and dynamic electrochemical memory together provide the structural and functional a prioris of body and brain at birth. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: The existence of prewired, functional FAPs at birth (like running) and their emotional triggering demonstrates that consciousness capabilities are phylogenetic functional a prioris, not solely learned through individual experience.
