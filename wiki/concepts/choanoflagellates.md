# Choanoflagellates

## Summary

Choanoflagellates are single-celled eukaryotic organisms that share a common ancestor with animals, making them the closest living relatives of the animal kingdom. They possess flagella and collar structures that are remarkably similar to choanocyte cells found in sponges, providing crucial evidence for the colonial flagellate hypothesis of animal origins.

## Key facts

- Choanoflagellates are predators that feed on bacteria, using flagella to create water currents and a collar to trap prey. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p111]
- They can control the direction of their movements toward or away from stimuli through electrical signals that cause flagella beating. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p111]
- Choanoflagellates reproduce both sexually and asexually; sexual reproduction is triggered by food limitation or bacterial parasite infection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p111]
- They form clonal colonies through asexual cell division, which helps minimize physiological conflict and defection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p111]
- Animals and choanoflagellates are sister groups that share a common ancestor. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p110]
