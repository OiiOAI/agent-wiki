# Quantal Transmission

## Summary

Quantal transmission describes how neurotransmitter is released in discrete packets called quanta, each representing the contents of a single synaptic vesicle. The postsynaptic response is the sum of these quantal units.

## Key facts

- Transmitter is released in discrete quantal units, and the end-plate potential varies in stepwise increments corresponding to integral multiples of the unit potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p319]
- Spontaneous miniature end-plate potentials represent the response to single quanta of transmitter released randomly from the presynaptic terminal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p320]
- A quantum at the neuromuscular junction contains approximately 5,000 molecules of ACh, producing about 2,000 channel openings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p321]
- The mean postsynaptic response E = n × p × a, where n is the number of releasable quanta, p is the release probability, and a is the quantal size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p321]
