# Pari-Mutuel System Analogy

## Summary

Charlie Munger uses the pari-mutuel betting system at racetracks as an analogy for stock market efficiency. The analogy illustrates that markets are somewhat efficient like racetracks, but not perfectly so, and that patient, disciplined investors can find mispriced opportunities even after transaction costs.

## Key facts

- A pari-mutuel system is described as a market where everyone bets and odds change based on what's bet. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p184]
- The track takes seventeen percent off the top, so investors must beat others by a significant margin just to break even. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p185]
- The one thing all winning bettors have is quite simple: they bet very seldom. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p186]
- Markets are partly efficient and partly inefficient—it's not possible for everyone to beat the market. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p183]
- The stock market has much lower 'house take' than racetracks, making it easier for shrewd investors. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p186]
