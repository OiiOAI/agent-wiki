# Stability Training

## Summary

Stability is the subconscious ability to harness, decelerate, or stop force during movement. It serves as the foundation for cardiovascular fitness and strength, enabling safe and efficient force transmission through muscles and bones rather than joints and spinal hinge points. Stability training focuses on breath, feet, spine, and shoulders.

## Key facts

- Stability enables the creation of maximum force in the safest manner possible, connecting different muscle groups while protecting joints and the spine. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p275]
- Most acute injuries like torn ACL or hamstring tears are rarely sudden but result from underlying chronic weakness or lack of stability. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p273]
- When stability is lacking, force dissipates through joints like knees, elbows, shoulders, and the spine, leading to injury. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p275]
- The DNS framework identifies three breathing phenotypes: Mr. Stay Puft (hyperinflated), Sad Guy (compressed), and Yogini (uncontrolled flexibility). [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p282]
- Intra-abdominal pressure (IAP) created by breathing is the foundational element for stability training, stabilizing the spine like air pressure in a sealed bottle. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p285]
- The four foundational elements of strength are: grip strength, concentric and eccentric loading, pulling motions, and hip-hinging movements. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p266]

## Inferences
