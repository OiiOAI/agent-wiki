# Evil

## Summary

Evil is identified as the termination of infinite play—the coming of infinite play to an unheard silence where drama does not continue in others. Evil is not the attempt to eliminate another's play according to rules, nor is it mere acquisition of power; evil is the forced recognition of a title without competition, achieved by silencing others. Evil originates in the desire to eliminate evil itself and in the belief that history can be tidied up and brought to a sensible conclusion. Evil is the restriction of all play to one or another finite game.

## Key facts

- Evil is the termination of infinite play; it is infinite play coming to an end in unheard silence—the loss of listeners for a voice. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p39-40]
- Evil is not the acquisition of power but the expression of power; it is the forced recognition of a title, for recognition cannot be forced. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p40]
- Evil is never intended as evil; it originates in the desire to eliminate evil. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p41]
- Evil arises in the honored belief that history can be tidied up, brought to a sensible conclusion; it is evil to act as though the past is bringing us to a specifiable end. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p41]
- Evil is not the inclusion of finite games in an infinite game but the restriction of all play to one or another finite game. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p42]
- Because patriotism is the desire to contain all other finite games within itself—to embrace all horizons within a single boundary—it is inherently evil. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p69]
