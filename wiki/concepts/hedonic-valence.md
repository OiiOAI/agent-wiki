# Hedonic Valence

## Summary

Hedonic valence is the subjective property of affect whereby states are experienced as good or bad, pleasant or unpleasant. Solms argues this inherently subjective property is what distinguishes affective consciousness from mere information processing, and is what makes suffering possible.

## Key facts

- According to Solms, hedonic valence is the inherently subjective property that only applies to and for a system and can therefore only be felt by that system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]
- Increasing free energy is an existential crisis for any self-organising system, which must develop internal models conferring the capacity for self-preservative action. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]
- Affect hedonically valences biological needs so that increasing and decreasing deviations from homeostatic settling points are felt as unpleasure and pleasure respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p274]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hedonic valence emerges from the system's 'point of view' - the fact that self-organising systems have a stake in their own persistence.
