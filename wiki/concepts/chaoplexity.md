# Chaoplexity

## Summary

Chaoplexity is the interdisciplinary field combining chaos theory and complexity theory as applied to understanding warfare and strategic behavior. The literature examines how armed forces and social systems operate as Complex Adaptive Systems in non-linear environments where friction, uncertainty, and unpredictability are irremovable features of war.

## Key facts

- Warfare is characterized as a non-linear phenomenon with two or more co-evolving, adapting competitors where actions of every agent influence and shape the environment. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p175]
- As both allied and enemy actions influence the environment, warfare involves the co-evolution of all involved parties, where friction, uncertainty and unpredictability reign and are irremovable from war. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p175]
- Steven Mann lists four factors of the environment of a strategic entity which shape the criticality of a system: initial shape of the system, underlying structure, cohesion among actors, and conflict energy of individual actors. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]
- Mann states that all stability is transient in international affairs; stability is no more than a consequence and should never be a goal. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]

## Inferences

- Inference: The chaoplexity literature suggests that insights from systems theory, chaos theory, and complexity theory are relevant for understanding economic, financial, industrial, transportation, and other social and technical systems.
- Inference: The literature indicates that the international political arena of states forms a complex system and war is a non-linear phenomenon, suggesting Boyd's application of insights from chemical and biological systems to the social arena was warranted.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which military applications of chaos and complexity theory have been validated through empirical military operations rather than theoretical modeling.
