# Attitude Polarization

## Summary

The psychological phenomenon where people with opposing partisan views move further apart rather than closer together after receiving the same body of information. Liberals and conservatives diverge when reading the same research on death penalty deterrence, evaluating presidential debate arguments, or considering affirmative action or gun control.

## Key facts

- When given a single body of information, liberals and conservatives actually move further apart on attitude measures rather than converging. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p102]
- Attitude polarization occurs when partisans read about research on whether the death penalty deters crime. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p102]
- Polarization also occurs when partisans rate the quality of arguments made by candidates in a presidential debate. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p102]
- Attitude polarization extends to evaluations of arguments about affirmative action or gun control. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p102]

## Inferences

- Inference: Attitude polarization helps explain why scientific evidence often fails to resolve political disputes—partisans interpret the same studies through their different moral foundations, reaching opposite conclusions
