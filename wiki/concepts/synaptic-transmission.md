# Synaptic Transmission

## Summary

Synaptic transmission is the process by which neurons communicate via chemical neurotransmitters. When action potentials reach axon terminals, they trigger calcium influx that causes synaptic vesicles to fuse with the plasma membrane and release neurotransmitter into the synaptic cleft. The neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the postsynaptic neuron, generating excitatory or inhibitory synaptic potentials depending on receptor type.

## Key facts

- Neurotransmitters are stored in synaptic vesicles concentrated at active zones in presynaptic terminals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p115]
- The amount of neurotransmitter released is determined by the frequency of action potentials arriving at the terminal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p115]
- Whether a synaptic potential is excitatory or inhibitory depends on the type of receptor in the postsynaptic cell, not the particular neurotransmitter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p115]
- Dale and Loewi discovered that when an action potential reaches axon terminals, it causes release of a neurotransmitter into the synaptic cleft. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p104]
- Katz established that voltage-gated channels generate action potentials for long-range signaling within neurons, while chemical transmitter-gated channels generate synaptic potentials for local signaling between neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p110]
- Neurotransmitters are released in discrete packets called quanta, each containing about five thousand molecules packaged in synaptic vesicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
- Calcium influx into presynaptic terminals sets off a series of molecular steps leading to neurotransmitter release, translating electrical signals into chemical signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
- Most synapses are chemical, but some neurons form electrical synapses with small bridges called gap junctions allowing current to pass directly. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p112]
