# Meeting with the Goddess

## Summary

The hero encounters the Great Mother figure—the Magna Mater—who represents the bliss of infancy regained. This meeting symbolizes the hero's confrontation with the nurturing, life-giving principle and the recovery of the archetypal image of divine perfection. The goddess may appear in various forms as guide, protector, or transformative force.

## Key facts

- The Meeting with the Goddess (Magna Mater) represents the bliss of infancy regained. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p34]
- The Great Mother archetype appears throughout mythology as the womb of redemption, the source from which the hero draws renewed life. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p22-23]
- The archetype of the divine mother is symbolized in dream imagery as the mother figure of the Madonna and Bambino. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p13]
- The mother-imago is retained as the unconscious basis of all images of bliss, truth, beauty, and perfection. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p13]
- The ultimate adventure is represented as a mystical marriage of the triumphant hero-soul with the Queen Goddess of the World, occurring at the nadir, zenith, or uttermost edge of the earth. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p91]
- The Lady of the House of Sleep is the paragon of all beauty, the reply to all desire, the bliss-bestowing goal of every hero's earthly and unearthly quest. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p91]
- Woman represents the totality of what can be known; the hero is the one who comes to know, and as he progresses, the form of the goddess undergoes transfigurations. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p95]
