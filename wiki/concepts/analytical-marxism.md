# Analytical Marxism

## Summary

Analytical Marxism is a neo-Marxist approach that brings mainstream, state-of-the-art methods of analytical philosophy and social science to bear on Marxian substantive issues. Practitioners are inspired by Marxian questions and pursue them with contemporary tools of logic, mathematics, and model building, while maintaining a nondogmatic approach to Marx's theory.

## Key facts

- Analytical Marxism adopts a nondogmatic approach to Marx's theory, viewing it as a form of nineteenth-century social science with great power and a valid core but also with substantial weaknesses. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p209]
- Analytical Marxists reject dialectics as a distinctive Marxian methodology and criticize teleological reasoning that assumes history unfolds as intended. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p209]
- G. A. Cohen reinterpreted Marx as employing functional explanation rather than dialectics, arguing that social phenomena are explained by their consequences for other social phenomena. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p210]
- Erik Olin Wright developed the concept of 'contradictory locations within class relations,' arguing that a given position may be simultaneously in more than one class, such as managers who are both bourgeois (supervising) and proletarian (supervised). [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p213]
- Analytical Marxism is not a unified or internally consistent body of thought; the differences among its major practitioners are enormous. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p212]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cohen's functional reinterpretation of Marx allows analytical Marxists to employ functional explanations without accepting structural functionalism's conservative tenets, enabling revolutionary analysis.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text notes that there is debate over whether analytical Marxism should be called Marxist at all, with Roemer himself uncertain.
