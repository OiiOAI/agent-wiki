# Length Constant

## Summary

The length constant (λ) is the distance along a neuronal process at which a passive voltage change decays to 37% (1/e) of its initial value. It is determined by the ratio of membrane resistance to axial resistance (λ = √(rm/ra)) and measures the efficiency of electrotonic conduction. Larger diameter axons and myelination increase the length constant.

## Key facts

- The length constant λ = √(rm/ra), where rm is membrane resistance per unit length and ra is axial resistance per unit length. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p253]
- Voltage decay with distance follows ΔV(x) = ΔV0 × e^(−x/λ), where x is distance from current injection site. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p253]
- Typical length constants for unmyelinated axons range from 0.5 to 1.0 mm; myelinated axons have longer length constants up to ~1.5 mm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p253]
