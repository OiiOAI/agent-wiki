# Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy

## Summary

Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) is a psychotherapeutic approach that combines cognitive interventions with behavioral methods like exposure. Founded by Aaron Beck, CBT is based on the assumption that dysfunctional cognitions (beliefs/schemas) underlie pathological emotional states and behaviors. Treatment involves identifying and challenging maladaptive beliefs, replacing them with realistic thought patterns, and often incorporating exposure to feared stimuli. CBT consumed behavior therapy from within, reframing exposure as a method to disconfirm catastrophic misinterpretations rather than solely as extinction.

## Key facts

- Aaron Beck argued that cognitive change is a prerequisite for sustainable emotional and behavioral change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p370]
- According to Beck, cognitive therapists modify mental content (maladaptive thoughts or beliefs) whereas behavior therapists try to change overt behaviors themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p371]
- Successful therapy involves changes in both explicit (conscious) and implicit (nonconscious) cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p372]
