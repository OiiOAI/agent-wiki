# Cadence and Control

## Summary

Cadence matters more than velocity if seeking stability and endurance. In crisis, daily or quasi-daily cycles in small teams address urgency and uncertainty. Weekly feedback cycles help align functions. The ability to shift to bi-weekly cycles indicates a certain level of stability. Control should be relaxed during crisis as overall risk perception changes. Post-crisis, balancing the pendulum between crisis-induced freedom and bureaucratic controls requires deliberate management.

## Key facts

- Cadence matters more than velocity if we seek stability and endurance. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p54]
- Daily or quasi-daily cycles in small teams serve the purpose of addressing urgency and uncertainty when changing context generates constant shifts in priorities. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p54]
- The ideal is to increase velocity while reducing cadence of review, which reduces the energy cost of management. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p54]
- Crisis control should be relaxed as overall perception of risk changes; fundamental steps can be accelerated and unnecessary steps removed. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p55]
- The pendulum swing between crisis-induced freedom and reimposition of bureaucratic controls is inevitable unless deliberately addressed. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p55]
