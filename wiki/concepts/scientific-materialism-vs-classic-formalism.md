# Scientific Materialism vs. Classic Formalism

## Summary

Phædrus analyzed the objection that Quality is 'just what you like' into two main sources: scientific materialism (which holds that only what is measurable by scientific instruments is real) and classic formalism (which insists that what isn't understood intellectually isn't understood at all). He found scientific materialism easier to refute using reductio ad absurdum, but classic formalism proved more formidable as it argued that immediate emotional impulses should be subordinated to rational long-term considerations.

## Key facts

- Scientific materialism holds that what is composed of matter or energy and is measurable by instruments of science is real; anything else is unreal or unimportant. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p198]
- Classic formalism insists that what isn't understood intellectually isn't understood at all; Quality is unimportant because it's an emotional understanding unaccompanied by intellectual elements of reason. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p198]
- Phædrus refuted scientific materialism by showing that concepts like the number zero, the law of gravity, and entire bodies of science cannot exist independently of subjective considerations. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p199]
- The classic formalism argument—that Quality was mere romantic surface appeal without long-term classic understanding—was the one that threatened to destroy Phædrus's whole thesis. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p200]

## Inferences

- Inference: The split between romantic Quality (instantaneous impressions, present-focused) and classic Quality (multiple considerations over time) threatened to cleave Quality itself into two kinds, which Phædrus resolved by showing they are two time aspects of Quality.
