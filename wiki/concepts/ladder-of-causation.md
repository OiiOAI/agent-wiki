# Ladder of Causation

## Summary

A three-level hierarchical framework for causal reasoning introduced in 'The Book of Why'. The three levels are: (1) observation, (2) intervention, and (3) counterfactuals. It serves as the central metaphor of the book and distinguishes causal reasoning from traditional statistical analysis.

## Key facts

- The Ladder of Causation has three steps: observation, intervention, and counterfactuals. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L239-L239]
- The Ladder of Causation is described as the central metaphor of 'The Book of Why'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L239-L239]
- The Ladder of Causation illuminates distinctions that data scientists working through a 'model-blind lens' have missed. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L239-L239]
- The Ladder of Causation is covered in Chapters 7-9 of "The Book of Why". [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L249-L251]
- Chapter 7 addresses questions about intervention and the automation of answers to do-type questions. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L249-L251]
- Pearl and his students spent twenty years developing automation for do-type questions. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L249-L251]
- The causal inference engine produces yes/no answers and estimands for causal queries. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L249-L251]
- The first rung, association, involves detecting regularities and answering 'What if I see?' questions. This is what owls do when tracking prey and what deep learning systems do when fitting functions to data. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L350-L365]
- The second rung, intervention, involves predicting effects of deliberate changes and answering 'What if I do?' questions. This cannot be answered from passively collected data alone. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L365-L380]
- The third rung, counterfactuals, involves imagining worlds that do not exist and answering 'What if I had done?' and 'Why?' questions. No experiment can deny treatment to an already treated person. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L380-L400]
- Counterfactuals are at the top of the Ladder because they deal with worlds that cannot be seen, whereas rung one deals with the seen world and rung two with a seeable world. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L400-L410]
- Deep learning systems operate entirely in associational mode, fitting functions to data streams, and cannot answer questions about interventions which by definition break the rules of the training environment. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L365-L375]
- The Ladder of Causation distinguishes three levels of causal reasoning. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3367]
- Technical details of the three levels are found in Chapter 1 of Pearl (2000). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3367]
- Comparisons between the Ladder and human cognitive development were inspired by Harari (2015) and Kind et al. (2014). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3370-L3372]
- The Lion Man figurine is referenced in research on the development of causal understanding. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3372-L3373]
- Research on causal understanding development in babies is cited from Weisberg and Gopnik (2013). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3373-L3374]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mastery of the Ladder of Causation framework is presented as enabling readers to become 'proficient causal reasoners', suggesting it provides capabilities beyond traditional data analysis approaches.
- Inference: The three rungs of the ladder likely correspond to association (correlation), intervention ( causation), and counterfactuals (imagination), following the naming convention in Pearl's earlier work "Causality"
- Inference: The book presents the Ladder of Causation as a theoretical framework that bypasses debates about what causality 'is' by focusing on what causal reasoners can compute or answer.
- Inference: Research has shown that three-year-olds already understand the entire Ladder of Causation, suggesting this capability develops early in human cognition.
- Inference: The Ladder of Causation framework provides a hierarchy where higher rungs require more sophisticated causal reasoning capabilities, paralleling the evolution of human cognitive abilities.
