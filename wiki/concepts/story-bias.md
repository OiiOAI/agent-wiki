# Story Bias

## Summary

Story Bias describes the human tendency to knit complex realities into neat, emotionally satisfying narratives. We shape events into meaningful stories even when no real pattern exists, preferring coherent narratives over abstract data. This leads to false understanding and distorted decision-making.

## Key facts

- Story bias is when we knit a muddle of details into a neat story; we want our lives to form a pattern that can be easily followed, which many call 'meaning.'. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p38]
- Stories are dubious entities: they simplify and distort reality and filter things that don't fit, but apparently we cannot do without them as mythology is older than philosophy. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p38]
- E.M. Forster's example demonstrates that we retain 'The king died and the queen died of grief' better than 'The king died and the queen died' despite the former being longer—emotional connection trumps information theory predictions. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p39]
