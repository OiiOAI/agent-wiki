# X-as-a-Service Mode

## Summary

X-as-a-Service is one of three team interaction modes in Team Topologies where one team provides services to other teams. This mode helps other teams deliver quickly but is only effective when the boundary between teams is suitable.

## Key facts

- X-as-a-Service is one of three team interaction modes in Team Topologies. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2192]
- X-as-a-Service can help other teams deliver quickly. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2194]
- X-as-a-Service only works effectively when the boundary is suitable. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2194]
- X-as-a-Service mode means consuming or providing something with minimal collaboration. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2430-L2440]
- X-as-a-Service works best during later phases of systems development and periods where predictable delivery is needed. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2440-L2450]
- Constraint: A team should expect to use X-as-a-Service with many other teams simultaneously, whether consuming or providing. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2490-L2500]
- The team providing the service must have a strong sense of responsibility toward both consumers and the viability of the thing they are providing. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2460-L2470]
- Typical uses: Stream-aligned teams and complicated-subsystem teams consuming Platform-as-a-Service; consuming a component or library from a complicated-subsystem team. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2500-L2510]

## Inferences

- Inference: Teams should evaluate boundary suitability before adopting X-as-a-Service interactions to ensure effective delivery support.
- Inference: X-as-a-Service mode is essential for scaling organizations as it allows many teams to consume services without requiring heavy collaboration overhead.
