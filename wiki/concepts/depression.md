# Depression

## Summary

Depression is a common mental illness affecting about 5% of the world's population characterized by unpleasant mood, inability to experience pleasure, loss of interest, sleep disturbances, and cognitive slowing. The most effective antidepressants are selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), which increase serotonin availability. Recent research shows antidepressants also stimulate production of new neurons in the hippocampus.

## Key facts

- Depression affects about 5 percent of the world's population at some time in their lives; in the United States, 8 million people are affected at any given time. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p367]
- Women are affected by depression two to three times as often as men. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p367]
- Although MAOIs inhibit serotonin reuptake within hours, they take at least three weeks to alleviate depression symptoms, suggesting the brain must 'learn' how to become happy again. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p368]
- Ronald Duman and Rene Hen found that antidepressant drugs increase the ability of the dentate gyrus of the hippocampus to generate new nerve cells over the same two to three weeks needed for drugs to work. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p369]
