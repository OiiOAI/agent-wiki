# Neural Circuit

## Summary

Neural circuits are interconnected groups of neurons that mediate specific behaviors. Circuits can involve convergence (many inputs to one neuron) and divergence (one neuron to many targets). The knee-jerk reflex illustrates a simple monosynaptic circuit where stretch receptors activate motor neurons to contract the stretched muscle, with feedforward inhibition of antagonist muscles. More complex behaviors require coordination of many circuits across brain regions.

## Key facts

- The knee-jerk reflex involves sensory neurons making direct excitatory connections with extensor motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p109]
- Each sensory neuron in the stretch reflex activates 45-50 motor neurons (divergence), and each motor neuron receives input from approximately 130 sensory neurons (convergence). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p110]
- Feedforward inhibition ensures that flexor and extensor pathways inhibit each other, so only muscles appropriate for movement are recruited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p111]
- Every behavior is mediated by specific sets of interconnected neurons, and every neuron's behavioral function is determined by its connections with other neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p79]
- Divergence occurs when one neuron activates many target cells, common in input stages of the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p80]
- Convergence occurs when many neurons connect to one target cell, common at output stages; a motor neuron receives input from many sensory neurons to ensure activation only when sufficient sensory neurons fire. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p80]
- Feed-forward inhibition occurs when afferent neurons excite both the primary pathway and inhibitory interneurons that suppress opposing pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p81]
- Feedback inhibition is a self-regulating mechanism where neurons act through inhibitory interneurons to reduce their own activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p81]
