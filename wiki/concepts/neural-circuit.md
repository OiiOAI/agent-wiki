# Neural Circuit

## Summary

Neural circuits are interconnected groups of neurons that mediate specific behaviors. Circuits can involve convergence (many inputs to one neuron) and divergence (one neuron to many targets). The knee-jerk reflex illustrates a simple monosynaptic circuit where stretch receptors activate motor neurons to contract the stretched muscle, with feedforward inhibition of antagonist muscles. More complex behaviors require coordination of many circuits across brain regions.

## Key facts

- The knee-jerk reflex involves sensory neurons making direct excitatory connections with extensor motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p109]
- Each sensory neuron in the stretch reflex activates 45-50 motor neurons (divergence), and each motor neuron receives input from approximately 130 sensory neurons (convergence). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p110]
- Feedforward inhibition ensures that flexor and extensor pathways inhibit each other, so only muscles appropriate for movement are recruited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p111]
