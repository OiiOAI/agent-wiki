# The Frankfurt School

## Summary

The Frankfurt School (or critical school) of Marxian theory was founded in Frankfurt, Germany, on February 3, 1923, through the efforts of Felix J. Weil. Associated thinkers include Max Horkheimer, Theodor Adorno, Erich Fromm, Herbert Marcuse, and later Jürgen Habermas. Initially traditional Marxists focused on the economic domain, but around 1930 the school shifted attention to the cultural system, seen as the major force in modern capitalist society. The school combined Marx and Weber to create "Weberian Marxism" and later integrated Freudian theory. In 1934, the Institute of Social Research moved to Columbia University in New York due to Nazi hostility, returning to Germany in 1949. The critical school had to await the late 1960s before being "discovered" by large numbers of American theorists.
