# Horizon

## Summary

The horizon is the defining limit of infinite play, in contrast to boundaries which define finite games. A boundary is a phenomenon of opposition where hostile forces meet; one cannot move beyond a boundary without being resisted. A horizon, by contrast, is a phenomenon of vision—the point beyond which we cannot see, opening onto all that lies beyond itself. One never reaches a horizon; it has no place, encloses no field, and its location is always relative to the view. Infinite players move toward the horizon; finite players move within boundaries. To live horizonally is to be always in passage.

## Key facts

- A boundary is a phenomenon of opposition; it is the meeting place of hostile forces; where nothing opposes there can be no boundary. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p69]
- A horizon is a phenomenon of vision; one cannot look at the horizon; it is simply the point beyond which we cannot see; there is nothing in the horizon itself that limits vision. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p69]
- One never reaches a horizon; it is not a line; it has no place; it encloses no field; to move toward a horizon is simply to have a new horizon. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p70]
- Every move an infinite player makes is toward the horizon; every move made by a finite player is within a boundary. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p70]
- Who lives horizonally is never somewhere, but always in passage. [raw/books/philosophy/Finite and Infinite Games_ A Vision of Life as Play and - Carse, James.pdf#p70]
