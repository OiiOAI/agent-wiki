# Singular Evaluation

## Summary

Singular evaluation is a decision strategy where options are evaluated one at a time on their own merits, with each option rejected or accepted before moving to the next. This contrasts with comparative evaluation, where options are weighed against each other simultaneously. The firefighter studies found that 80% of decisions used singular evaluation rather than comparison.

## Key facts

- Singular evaluation means evaluating each option on its own merits, even if cycling through several possibilities. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p41]
- In the overpass rescue example, the lieutenant evaluated options one at a time, rejected each when problems were identified, and moved to the next most typical rescue technique. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p39-40]
- The lieutenant's deliberations about options took only about one minute in the overpass rescue case. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p40]
- Michael Doherty (1996) asserted that it may be psychologically impossible to make direct comparisons of two complex options in one's head if the cognitive work is done completely intuitively. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p54]
