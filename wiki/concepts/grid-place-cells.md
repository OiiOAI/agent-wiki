# Grid Cells and Place Cells

## Summary

Grid cells and place cells are neurons that create maplike representations in the brain. Place cells (discovered in 1971 by John O'Keefe) fire when an animal is at a specific location. Grid cells (discovered in 2005 by May-Britt and Edvard Moser) fire at multiple locations forming a regular grid pattern. Together they allow navigation and location tracking. Hawkins proposes that equivalent cells exist throughout the neocortex, enabling every cortical column to create reference frames for objects and concepts.

## Key facts

- Place cells were discovered in 1971 by John O'Keefe; they fire when an animal is at a particular location in an environment. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p65]
- Grid cells were discovered in 2005 by May-Britt Moser and Edvard Moser; they fire at multiple equally-spaced locations forming a grid pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p65]
- Place cells and grid cells exist in the hippocampus and entorhinal cortex, which are older parts of the brain involved in navigation. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p64]
- Grid cells create reference frames while place cells associate sensory input with locations in those reference frames. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p65]
- Humans have grid cells and place cells and always maintain a sense of location, even with eyes closed. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p66]
- fMRI research by Doeller, Barry, and Burgess provided evidence that grid cells also exist in parts of the neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p82]
- The neocortex has about 150,000 copies of the navigation circuit, one per cortical column, allowing it to track thousands of locations simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p67]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neocortex likely evolved to use a derivative of the navigation system found in older brain structures, applying the same mechanisms to model objects rather than environments.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The detailed mechanisms by which cortical grid cells differ from those in the entorhinal cortex remain to be fully characterized.
