# Scenius

## Summary

A term coined by musician Brian Eno describing a model of creativity where great ideas are birthed by a group of creative individuals—artists, curators, thinkers, and other tastemakers—who make up an 'ecology of talent.' Scenius acknowledges that good work isn't created in a vacuum and that creativity is always collaborative, resulting from a mind connected to other minds.

## Key facts

- Brian Eno coined the term 'scenius' as an alternative to the 'lone genius' myth. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p16]
- Under the scenius model, great ideas are birthed by a group of creative individuals who form an 'ecology of talent.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p16]
- Kleon notes that many people we think of as lone geniuses were actually part of 'a whole scene of people who were supporting each other, looking at each other's work, copying from each other, stealing ideas, and contributing ideas.'. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p16]
- Being a valuable part of a scenius is about what you have to contribute—the ideas you share, the quality of connections you make, and the conversations you start—rather than how smart or talented you are. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p18]
- The Internet is described as 'a bunch of sceniuses connected together, divorced from physical geography,' with blogs, social media, email groups, and forums serving as virtual scenes. [raw/books/productivity/Show Your Work!_ 10 Ways to Share Your Cre - Austin Kleon.pdf#p20]
