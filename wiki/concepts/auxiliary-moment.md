# Auxiliary Moment

## Summary

A moment in which two previously divided psychical groups temporarily converge, as in somnambulistic consciousness, allowing conversion to occur. The auxiliary moment differs from the actual traumatic moment (when the incompatibility first forces itself upon the ego) by providing the conditions under which the originally repressed material can become active and produce hysterical symptoms. In Miss Lucy R.'s case, the scene with the chief accountant was an auxiliary moment that revived the memory of the earlier traumatic scene with her employer.

## Key facts

- Freud defined the characteristic feature of an auxiliary moment as the temporary convergence of two divided psychical groups, as they do in the extended consciousness occurring in somnambulism. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p648]
- In Miss Lucy R.'s case, the actual traumatic moment was the employer's outburst about the children being kissed, but the hysterical symptoms did not start until the auxiliary moment of the chief accountant scene two months later. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p648]
- The second auxiliary moment (the burnt pudding scene) repeated the mechanism of the first one: a powerful impression temporarily reunited consciousness and conversion again took the path opened on the first occasion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p648]
- In Katharina's case, the scene of discovery both deserved to be called auxiliary and equally deserved to be called traumatic, as it was operative on account of its own content and not merely as something that revived previous traumatic experiences. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p657]

## Inferences

- Inference: The auxiliary moment concept explains why hysterical symptoms often do not appear immediately after the original trauma—they require a subsequent experience that allows the split-off psychical material to re-connect with the ego.
