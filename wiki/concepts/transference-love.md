# Transference Love

## Summary

Transference love is the phenomenon in which a patient falls in love with the analyst during psychoanalytic treatment. Freud argued that this is simultaneously genuine and induced by the analytic situation, intensified by resistance, and characterized by diminished regard for reality. The analyst must neither reject nor accept this love but treat it as unreal, tracing it back to its unconscious origins to extract its analytic content. The patient's relatives often wish to interrupt treatment upon discovering this phenomenon, but Freud insisted that abandoning analysis merely because of transference love would deprive the patient of therapeutic benefit. The situation is unavoidable in analysis and cannot be resolved by transferring to another analyst, as the same phenomenon will recur.

## Key facts

- Transference love is induced by the analytic situation, intensified by resistance, and characterized by a high degree of disregard for reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3887]
- The analyst must withhold any response to transference love while keeping firm hold of it as material to be traced back to unconscious origins. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3885]
- If the patient falls in love with one analyst and breaks off treatment, she will fall in love with the next analyst too—transference love is bound to the analytic situation, not the analyst's person. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3880]

## Inferences

- Inference: The therapeutic management of transference love demonstrates the paradox of psychoanalysis: the very phenomenon that threatens the treatment also provides the means for its success.
