# War Neuroses

## Summary

War neuroses are traumatic neuroses whose occurrence was made possible by a conflict in the ego—specifically between the soldier's old peaceful ego and his new warlike ego. Freud distinguished them from ordinary traumatic neuroses by their relation to internal conflict and from transference neuroses by their aetiology involving military duty. Most war neuroses disappeared when war conditions ceased.

## Key facts

- War neuroses are 'to be regarded as traumatic neuroses whose occurrence has been made possible or has been promoted by a conflict in the ego'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5470]
- The conflict in war neuroses is 'between the soldier's old peaceful ego and his new warlike one,' becoming acute when the peace ego realizes the danger from the 'parasitic double' of the warlike ego. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5470]
- A 'significant fact' is that 'when war conditions ceased to operate, the greater number of the neurotic disturbances brought about by the war simultaneously vanished'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5468]
- Freud notes that the war neuroses are unsuitable material for proving the sexual theory of neuroses, as the work to prove it 'has not yet been taken in hand'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5469]
- The memorandum on electrical treatment states that physicians had recognized war neurotics as suffering from mental conflicts and unconscious wishes—the 'unconscious inclination to withdraw from the demands' of active service. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5474]

## Inferences

- Inference: The disappearance of war neuroses with the end of the war provides empirical support for Freud's view that these disorders had psychical causation rooted in the specific conflict between military duty and the soldier's peaceful self-concept.
