# Central gray area

## Summary

The central gray area (periaqueductal gray) is a cell-dense structure surrounding the fluid-filled central canal connecting the forebrain ventricles with the fourth ventricle and spinal canal. It is the convergence center for vocalization, connecting the oral, vocal, and respiratory muscles. Electrical stimulation of this area can produce the full range of vocal calls in cats, squirrel monkeys, and macaque monkeys.

## Key facts

- Electrical stimulation of the central gray area in cat, squirrel monkey, and macaque monkey brains produces the full range of vocal calls for these species. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p252]
- The corresponding structure has been shown to elicit communicative gestures, postures, and sound production in essentially every vertebrate species studied, including fish, frogs, reptiles, birds, and mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p252]
- The central gray area receives descending inputs from forebrain limbic areas (hippocampus, amygdala, cingulate cortex) and hypothalamus, which are specifically associated with emotional state and autonomic arousal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p249]
- Among the output targets of the central gray area are both respiratory premotor regions of the brain stem reticular region and motor neurons that control the larynx (nucleus ambiguus). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p249]
