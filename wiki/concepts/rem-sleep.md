# REM Sleep

## Summary

REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep is a distinctive sleep stage characterized by EEG activity similar to wakefulness, vivid dreams, rapid eye movements, and profound muscle atonia. It is generated by a network of brainstem neurons centered in the pons, specifically the subceruleus region, which projects to the medulla and spinal cord to activate inhibitory interneurons that hyperpolarize motor neurons. REM sleep typically begins approximately 90 minutes after sleep onset and episodes become longer throughout the night.

## Key facts

- During REM sleep, muscle tone is extremely low due to inhibition of motor neurons by descending pathways from the brainstem, affecting nearly all motor neurons except those supporting respiration and eye movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1130]
- REM sleep is generated by glutamatergic neurons in the subceruleus region of the pons, which project to the medulla and spinal cord to activate GABAergic and glycinergic interneurons that hyperpolarize motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- GABAergic neurons in the ventrolateral periaqueductal gray matter inhibit subceruleus neurons, preventing REM sleep entry; mutual inhibition between these regions forms a flip-flop switch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- Antidepressants that increase brain levels of serotonin or norepinephrine reduce REM sleep because noradrenergic locus ceruleus and serotonergic dorsal raphe neurons inhibit the subceruleus REM-generating region. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1133]
- Over the course of a night, non-REM sleep becomes progressively lighter while REM sleep episodes become longer. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1130]

## Inferences

- Inference: The loss of motor atonia during REM sleep in REM sleep behavior disorder suggests the subceruleus neurons or their descending projections to medullary inhibitory interneurons are damaged.
