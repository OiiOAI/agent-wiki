# Neuropsychoanalysis

## Summary

Neuropsychoanalysis is a field founded by Mark Solms that integrates neuroscience with psychoanalysis. Solms coined the term and established the International Neuropsychoanalysis Society with Jaak Panksepp in 2000. The field takes seriously the subjective reports of patients — a practice derided by much of mainstream neuroscience — and attempts to translate psychoanalytic concepts (such as drive, wish-fulfilment, and the unconscious) into neurobiological terms. Solms argues that psychoanalysis currently provides the best conceptual starting point for tackling subjectivity scientifically, and that the field's opposition to Freud led neuroscience into errors such as conflating REM sleep with dreaming and assuming the cortex generates consciousness.

## Key facts

- Solms coined the term 'neuropsychoanalysis' and founded the International Neuropsychoanalysis Society with Jaak Panksepp in 2000. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p48]
- Eric Kandel published 'A new intellectual framework for psychiatry' (1998) arguing that psychiatry of the twenty-first century should be based on integration of neuroscience with psychoanalysis, and later stated 'Psychoanalysis still represents the most coherent and intellectually satisfying view of the mind'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p48]
- Neuropsychoanalysis takes seriously the subjective reports of patients and attempts to translate psychoanalytic concepts into neurobiological terms, based on the functional mechanisms inferred by Freud. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p47]
- Solms argues that neuropsychoanalysis opens new approaches to common psychiatric disorders such as addiction and major depression, as well as to confabulation and anosognosia. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p47]
- Solms argues that the neuropsychology of his time might as well have been called 'neurobehaviourism', given its exclusion of subjectivity — a stance he contrasts with neuropsychoanalysis, which pivots on lived experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p41]

## Inferences

- Inference: Neuropsychoanalysis proposes that Freud's 'unconscious' corresponds to subcortical brain processes, and that his 'drive' corresponds to homeostatic affects generated by brainstem and hypothalamic systems — suggesting Freud had the right goals but localised functions incorrectly in the cortex.
- Inference: The field's central claim is that subjective reports are scientifically valid data, as demonstrated by Solms's successful predictions from dream content and confabulation analysis in neurological patients.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the neuropsychoanalytic framework will gain broader acceptance within mainstream neuroscience remains to be seen; Solms acknowledges that 'psychoanalysis' carries historical baggage that complicates its scientific reputation.
