# System

## Summary

A system is a set of things—people, cells, molecules, or whatever—interconnected in such a way that they produce their own pattern of behavior over time. Systems consist of three essential components: elements, interconnections, and a function or purpose. The behavior of a system arises from its structure rather than from external forces alone.

## Key facts

- A system is an interconnected set of elements that is coherently organized in a way that achieves something. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p26]
- A system must consist of three kinds of things: elements, interconnections, and a function or purpose. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p26]
- A system is more than the sum of its parts; it may exhibit adaptive, dynamic, goal-seeking, self-preserving, and sometimes evolutionary behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p27]
- The least obvious part of the system, its function or purpose, is often the most crucial determinant of the system's behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p32]
- The system, to a large extent, causes its own behavior; an outside event may unleash behavior but the same event applied to a different system produces a different result. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p18]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because systems cause their own behavior, fixing system problems requires restructuring the system itself rather than just addressing external triggers.
