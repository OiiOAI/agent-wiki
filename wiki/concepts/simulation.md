# Simulation

## Summary

Simulation is the process by which the brain changes the firing of its own sensory neurons in the absence of incoming sensory input. It allows the brain to 'hallucinate' or recreate past experiences, concepts, or imagined events. Barrett argues that simulation is the brain's default mode for all mental activity, including perception, understanding language, feeling empathy, remembering, imagining, and dreaming. In emotion, the brain simulates emotion concepts to make meaning of internal bodily sensations.

## Key facts

- Simulation means that your brain changed the firing of its own sensory neurons in the absence of incoming sensory input. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p40]
- Reading the word 'apple' causes the brain to respond as if an apple were present, combining bits of knowledge from previous experiences to construct a mental instance. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p40]
- Simulation is the default mode for all mental activity and holds a key to understanding how the brain creates emotions. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p41]
- People spend at least half their waking hours simulating rather than paying attention to the world, and this pure simulation strongly drives their feelings. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p88]
