# Reward Processing in Striatum

## Summary

The striatum processes reward-related information essential for decision making. The ventral striatum (nucleus accumbens) responds to reward anticipation and is sensitive to magnitude. Dopamine signals prediction errors—the difference between received and expected rewards. The striatum integrates reward-related prefrontal inputs with cognitive information to guide decisions.

## Key facts

- The striatum is functionally divided into dorsal and ventral regions; the dorsal is subdivided into caudate and putamen in primates, and the ventral contains the nucleus accumbens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p219]
- Dopaminergic neurons signal prediction errors: firing occurs for unpredicted rewards, shifts to predictive stimuli when learned, and depresses when predicted rewards are omitted. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p222]
- Ventral striatum BOLD responses parametrically order according to reward magnitude, with higher responses when anticipating higher rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p224]
- Microdialysis studies show increased dopamine release in nucleus accumbens during presentation of conditioned stimuli associated with cocaine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p223]
- The Monetary Incentive Delay task shows increased ventral striatum activation for reward cues but not loss cues, and less response in adolescents compared to adults. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p224]

## Inferences

- Inference: The striatum is positioned to integrate reward-related information with cognitive information due to convergence of inputs from prefrontal cortex (DLPFC, OFC, ACC) and dopaminergic midbrain structures.
