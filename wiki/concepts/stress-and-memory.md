# Stress and Memory

## Summary

Stress affects memory through hormones released during the stress response. While acute stress can enhance memory for events occurring before or after the stressor, chronic stress can damage the hippocampus, reduce its volume, and contribute to PTSD symptoms. Stress also impairs executive functions and alters what details are remembered.

## Key facts

- The hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and amygdala all have receptors for stress hormones, and stress affects memory in complex ways—sometimes enhancing and sometimes suppressing it depending on many interacting factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p99]
- Cortisol, the most extensively studied stress hormone, spikes during stress and can improve ability to retain memories for what happened right before or after the stressful event. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p99]
- Like noradrenaline, stress hormones promote plasticity by initiating changes that consolidate connections within cell assemblies representing the memory for a stressful event. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p99]
- Stress tips the chemical balance in the brain, downregulating executive functions mediated by the prefrontal cortex while enhancing amygdala sensitivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p100]
- Soldiers in combat, children in abusive homes, or anyone spending sustained time in threatening situations are vulnerable to neurotoxic effects of stress and often show reductions in hippocampus volume. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p100]
- Hippocampal dysfunction in people with PTSD may cause traumatic memories to become overgeneralized, so that tangentially related situations or feelings can trigger intense flashbacks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p100]
