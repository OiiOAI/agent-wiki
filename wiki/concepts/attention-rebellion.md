# The Attention Rebellion

## Summary

The Attention Rebellion is the author's proposed social movement to reclaim human attention from the forces degrading it. The author outlines three initial goals: banning surveillance capitalism, introducing a four-day work week, and rebuilding childhood around free play. The movement draws on lessons from successful activism like the climate movement, and frames attention liberation as a prerequisite for solving other crises including the climate emergency.

- The author proposes three big goals for the Attention Rebellion: ban surveillance capitalism, introduce a four-day week, and rebuild childhood around free play. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p293]
- Ben Stewart, who helped stop new coal plants in Britain, explained that the first step is a 'consciousness-raising breakthrough cultural moment' where people recognize their brains have been frazzled. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p294]
- The author argues we are not medieval peasants begging at the court of King Zuckerberg—we are free citizens who own our own minds and society. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p297]
- Thomas Hylland Eriksen explained that since the Industrial Revolution, economies have been built around economic growth, requiring constant speed increases that degrade attention. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p298]
- Dr. Charles Czeisler said that if we all slept as much as our brains need, 'it would be an earthquake for our economic system, because our economic system has become dependent on sleep-depriving people.'.
