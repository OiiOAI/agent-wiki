# RULER Framework

## Summary

RULER is an evidence-based approach to social and emotional learning developed at the Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence. It consists of five emotion skills: Recognizing (noticing emotions in one's own body and in others' expressions), Understanding (knowing causes and consequences of emotions), Labeling (using precise vocabulary for emotions), Expressing (displaying emotions appropriately for context), and Regulating (managing emotions to achieve goals). The first three skills help identify and decode emotions; the last two enable effective management of emotions.

## Key facts

- The first RULER skill is Recognizing: noticing a change in one's own thoughts, energy, or body or in someone else's facial expression, body language, or voice. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p53]
- The second skill is Understanding: knowing the cause of emotions and seeing how they influence thoughts and decisions. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p53]
- The third skill is Labeling: making connections between an emotional experience and precise terms to describe it. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p53]
- The fourth skill is Expressing: knowing how and when to display emotions depending on setting, people, and context. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p53]
- The fifth skill is Regulating: monitoring, tempering, and modifying emotional reactions in helpful ways to reach personal and professional goals. [raw/books/psychology/Permission to feel _ unlocking the power of emotions to help - Marc Ph D Brackett; OverDrive, Inc.pdf#p53]
- RULER classrooms where the approach was implemented with greatest fidelity had students with more developed emotion skills after ten months compared with classrooms with less fidelity.
