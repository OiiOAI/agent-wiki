# SA Metrics Sensitivity

## Summary

SA metrics sensitivity refers to the ability of a measurement technique to detect changes in situation awareness resulting from interface manipulations or other interventions. This chapter found that performance-based measures and subjective ratings (especially SART) demonstrated adequate sensitivity, while memory probes showed conditional sensitivity depending on question design.

## Key facts

- A measure was defined as sensitive if the 95% confidence interval around the proportion of positive outcomes was completely above the 50% level. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p247]
- Performance-based measures passed the sensitivity test with their 95% CI completely above the 50% level. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p248]
- Subjective ratings passed the sensitivity test with their 95% CI completely above the 50% level. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p248]
- Memory probe measures failed the sensitivity test as their 95% CI was not completely above the 50% level. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p248]
- Wide breadth memory probes (e.g., SAGAT) showed sensitivity, while narrow breadth probes did not. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p249]
