# Affective Realism

## Summary

Affective realism is the phenomenon whereby people experience their feelings as information about the outside world rather than as their own internal experience. Because affect colors perception, people imbue neutral objects with valence—seeing threatening weapons where there are cell phones, or interpreting a friend's irritability as directed at them when it may stem from hunger or fatigue.

## Inferences

- Inference: Affective realism may contribute to tragic outcomes in high-pressure situations where quick judgments are required, such as police shootings and military incidents.
- Inference: Affective realism explains why eyewitness testimony—once considered the gold standard of evidence—is fundamentally unreliable and why legal reforms are necessary to educate jurors about the constructed nature of perception.
- Inference: The concept has implications for Stand Your Ground laws, as affective realism can cause people to perceive threats where none exist, leading to innocent people being shot.
