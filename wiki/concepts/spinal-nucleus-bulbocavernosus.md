# Spinal Nucleus of the Bulbocavernosus (SNB)

## Summary

The SNB is a sexually dimorphic motor nucleus in the lumbar spinal cord that innervates the bulbocavernosus and levator ani muscles. In rats, males have more and larger SNB motor neurons than females due to testosterone-dependent survival during early postnatal development, with female motor neurons undergoing apoptosis.

## Key facts

- The SNB in adult male rats contains many more motor neurons than in females, and male motor neurons are larger with more extensive dendritic arbors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1266]
- In females, many SNB motor neurons and fibers in target muscles die in early postnatal life due to absence of circulating testosterone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1267]
- Testosterone acts on androgen receptor-expressing bulbocavernosus muscles, which then provide trophic support (likely CNTF) to innervating SNB motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1267]
