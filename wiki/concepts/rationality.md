# Rationality

## Summary

Rationality is defined in the book as the ability to counteract emotional effects on thinking—to think instead of react, to open the mind to what is really happening rather than what is being felt. It is presented as a power that must be cultivated rather than a natural state, requiring awareness of how emotions continually distort judgment.

## Key facts

- The book defines rationality as the ability to counteract emotional effects, to think instead of react and open the mind to reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p25]
- Pericles served as the book's ideal exemplar of rationality in leadership. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p32]
- Rational people demonstrate over time the ability to finish projects, realize goals, work effectively with teams, and create lasting results. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p41]
- The book states that rationality is not about transcending emotion but achieving balance where emotional energy serves the thinking self. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p42]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book positions rationality as a skill that can be developed through three steps: recognizing cognitive biases, understanding high-grade irrationality triggered by external factors, and practicing strategies to strengthen rational thinking.
