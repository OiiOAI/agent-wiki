# Paradigm

## Summary

A paradigm is a fundamental image of the subject matter within a science, defining what should be studied, what questions should be asked, how they should be asked, and what rules should be followed in interpreting the answers. It serves to differentiate one scientific community from another and is the broadest unit of consensus within a science.

## Key facts

- Kuhn used the term 'paradigm' in at least twenty-one different ways according to Margaret Masterman. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p564]
- A paradigm subsumes, defines, and interrelates the exemplars, theories, and methods and instruments that exist within it. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p564]
- The paradigm concept can differentiate: (1) one scientific community from another, (2) different historical stages in a science's development, and (3) cognitive groupings within the same science. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p564]
