# Cortical Microcircuits

## Summary

Cortical microcircuits are functional units comprising several neural populations connected to one another, with a stereotyped pattern of connectivity replicated over many cortical regions. The six-layered structure of neocortex can be mapped to the message passing architectures of predictive coding, with ascending inputs to layer IV spiny stellate cells associated with prediction errors for hidden causes.

## Key facts

- The neocortex is divided into six layers (laminae), numbered from superficial to deep, with each layer characterized by specific cell types and patterns of connectivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p95]
- Ascending projections from lower cortical areas or sensory thalamic nuclei tend to target the spiny stellate cells in layer IV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p95]
- Descending projections from higher cortical areas target both superficial and deep layers of lower areas, with origins in deep (layer VI) pyramidal cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p95]
- Deep pyramidal cells (Betz cells) in layer V project to subcortical nuclei and spinal motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p95]
