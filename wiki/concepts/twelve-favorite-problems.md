# Twelve Favorite Problems

## Summary

Twelve Favorite Problems is an exercise inspired by Nobel Prize-winning physicist Richard Feynman, who maintained a list of open questions and tested new information against each one to find connections. The author recommends identifying 12 open-ended questions that represent persistent interests, which then guide what to capture. These questions tend to remain consistent over time, making any related content worth preserving far into the future.

## Key facts

- Richard Feynman maintained twelve favorite problems and tested new results against each one, leading to connections that appeared to others as flashes of brilliance. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p55-56]
- Feynman's approach allowed him to follow his interests wherever they led and make connections across seemingly unrelated subjects. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p56]
- Feynman's famous plate-spinning epiphany during a Cornell cafeteria lunch eventually informed work that led to his Nobel Prize in Physics. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p56-57]
- The author recommends phrasing favorite problems as open-ended questions that invoke wonder and curiosity. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: The twelve-favorite-problems approach counters recency bias by ensuring that past accumulated knowledge remains accessible rather than being overwritten by recent information.
