# Adult Attachment Styles

## Summary

Adult attachment styles are patterns in which people perceive and respond to intimacy in romantic relationships. The three main attachment styles—Secure, Anxious, and Avoidant—parallel those found in children. Secure people feel comfortable with intimacy and are usually warm and loving; anxious people crave intimacy and worry about their partner's ability to love them back; avoidant people equate intimacy with loss of independence.

## Key facts

- Adult attachment designates three main attachment styles: Secure, Anxious, and Avoidant. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p16]
- Attachment styles differ in view of intimacy, conflict handling, attitude toward sex, ability to communicate needs, and expectations from partners. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p17]
- Adult attachment research has been confirmed in hundreds of scientific papers across many countries and cultures. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p17]
- Attachment styles are stable but plastic; on average, one in four people change their attachment style over a four-year period. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p19]
- Attachment styles in adulthood are influenced by parenting style as babies, but also by life experiences. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p17]
