# Hair Cell Adaptation

## Summary

Hair cells adapt to sustained stimulation through a process that continuously resets the hair bundle's range of mechanical sensitivity. During a prolonged step stimulus, the receptor potential initially changes then progressively returns toward the resting value while the sigmoidal relationship between membrane potential and bundle position shifts in the direction of the stimulus. This allows hair cells to maintain high sensitivity to transient stimuli while rejecting static inputs. Adaptation involves myosin motors and calcium feedback at the transduction channel.

## Key facts

- During a prolonged step stimulus, the sigmoidal relationship between the membrane potential and the bundle position shifts by approximately 80% of the deflected position. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p661]
- Adaptation allows hair cells to maintain high sensitivity to transient stimuli while rejecting static inputs a million times as large. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p661]
- Myosin molecules at the upper end of each tip link are thought to maintain tension by ascending the actin core of the stereocilium. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p661]
- Calcium ions entering through transduction channels bind to or near the channel's pore, energetically favoring channel closure and contributing to fast adaptation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p661]
- The time constant of adaptation varies systematically along the tonotopic axis of auditory organs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p661]
