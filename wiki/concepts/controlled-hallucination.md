# Consciousness as Controlled Hallucination

## Summary

Seth's central thesis is that conscious experiences are 'controlled hallucinations'—the brain's best guesses about the causes of sensory signals. Rather than passively reading sensory input, the brain actively predicts from the top down, with sensory signals serving as prediction errors that constrain these predictions. Perceptual experience is determined by the content of top-down predictions, not by sensory signals themselves. Normal perception and hallucination exist on a continuum—the difference is whether perceptions remain yoked to real-world causes.

## Key facts

- The brain is constantly making predictions about the causes of sensory signals, cascading top-down through perceptual hierarchies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p85]
- Sensory signals serve as prediction errors, registering differences between what the brain expects and what it receives at each level of processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p85]
- Perceptual experience is determined by the content of top-down predictions, not by sensory signals themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p86]
- In hallucination, perceptual predictions are not properly updated by prediction errors and lose their grip on real-world causes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p86]
- The brain automatically 'discounts the illuminant'—adjusting for ambient lighting so colour perception picks out invariant surface properties. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p88]
- Perceptual experience is built from brain-based predictions, not passive sensory registration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p95]
- We never experience the world 'as it is' — perceptual content is constructed by the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p95]
- The 'control' in controlled hallucination is as important as the 'hallucination' — perception is constrained by sensory data and evolutionary design for survival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p96]
- Perceptual predictions flow predominantly in a top-down direction while prediction errors flow bottom-up. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
- What we perceive is given by the content of all top-down predictions once sensory prediction errors have been minimized or 'explained away'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]
- Perceptual content is a top-down controlled hallucination, rather than a bottom-up 'readout' of sensory data. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p107]

## Inferences

- Inference: perception is controlled hallucination, then hallucination can be considered uncontrolled perception—they exist on a continuum rather than as opposites.
- Inference: We are all hallucinating all the time; reality is simply when we agree about our hallucinations.
- Inference: The theory implies that all conscious perceptual experience — including experiences of colors, objects, and causality — are constructions rather than direct representations of external reality.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neural mechanisms by which the brain implements Bayesian inference remain to be fully characterized.
