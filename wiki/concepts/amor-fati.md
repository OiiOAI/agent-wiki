# Amor Fati (Love of Fate)

## Summary

Amor fati, meaning 'love of fate,' is a philosophical approach that involves accepting and embracing all aspects of life—including suffering, pain, failure, and ultimately death—not as burdens but as opportunities for learning and strengthening oneself. Coined by Friedrich Nietzsche, this philosophy asserts that what we cannot control, particularly death, should be accepted fully. Events are seen as fateful, occurring for reasons from which we can glean lessons, and pain is welcomed as a teacher rather than avoided.

## Key facts

- Nietzsche defined amor fati as wanting nothing to be other than it is, not in the future, not in the past, not in all eternity. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p583]
- The philosophy teaches accepting illness and physical pain, seeing them as opportunities to retreat, slow down, and appreciate good health. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p584]
- When relationships fail, amor fati involves seeing what was wrong in the dynamic and what was missing, without avoiding further relationships. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p584]
- With practice, this love of fate becomes easier and quicker to apply, lightening the burdens one carries. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p585]
