# Emotional Investment

## Summary

Emotional Investment refers to the degree to which a person sacrifices or alters their own thoughts, feelings, and motivations for someone else. The concept is central to understanding attraction dynamics: a man's attractiveness is determined by whether his level of investment in a woman is lower than her level of investment in him. When investment becomes unbalanced, with one person (typically the needy man) investing more than the other, relationships become dysfunctional. The book contrasts high-investment behaviors (rearranging schedules, buying gifts, agreeing with everything) with low-investment behaviors (maintaining one's own identity and values, being willing to walk away).

## Key facts

- By investment, Manson means the degree to which you sacrifice or alter your own thoughts, feelings, and motivations for someone else. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p24]
- As a man, you should not be willing to sacrifice your thoughts, feelings, and motivations for someone else more than they sacrifice theirs for you. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p24]
- James has a high level of emotional investment in the women he meets — he's not confident, he is needy, and immediately enslaves his identity to what he believes will make women like him. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p27]
- Jeff has a low level of investment — he's content with his life and proud of who he is. He is confident and non-needy. If a woman doesn't appreciate that, he figures he's better off without her. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p27]
- The failure pattern: Guy meets girl with low investment, enters relationship, gradually invests more while letting his personal life slip away, until girl leaves and dates someone less invested. [raw/books/psychology/Models - Mark Manson.pdf#p30]

## Inferences

- Inference: To maintain attraction, a man must continue investing in himself even as relationships develop, maintaining his own identity, social life, and personal growth.
