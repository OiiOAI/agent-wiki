# Two-stage model of free will

## Summary

The two-stage model of free will, proposed by psychologist and philosopher William James in 1884, incorporates indeterminism into cognition while preserving the causal role of the agent. In the first stage, a set of possible actions occurs to the organism, influenced by some degree of randomness. In the second stage, the organism selects from these presented options based on current goals and beliefs, choosing the most congruent action. This model breaks the bonds of determinism by combining genuine randomness with deliberate selection by the agent.

## Key facts

- William James proposed the two-stage model of free will in 1884, incorporating randomness in idea generation but agency in final selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p206]
- In the first stage, possible actions spring to mind with some degree of randomness; in the second stage, the organism selects based on goals and beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p206]
- James stated: 'Our thoughts come to us freely. Our actions go from us willfully.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p206]
- The model parallels evolution by natural selection, where random variations are generated but selection determines outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p207]

## Inferences

- Inference: The two-stage model provides a framework where free will exists not in generating options (which involves randomness) but in selecting among them (which involves agency).
- Inference: Creative thoughts can be seen as facilitated by chance but filtered by choice, aligning with Linus Pauling's observation about generating many ideas and then discarding wrong ones.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural mechanisms implementing the selection stage remain an active area of research.
