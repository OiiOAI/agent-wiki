# Active Zone

## Summary

The active zone is a specialized region of the presynaptic plasma membrane where synaptic vesicles are docked, primed, and positioned in close proximity to voltage-gated calcium channels for rapid, synchronized neurotransmitter release. It contains a conserved macromolecular protein scaffold including RIM, RIM-BP, and Munc13.

## Key facts

- The active zone coordinates docking and priming of synaptic vesicles to enable the speed and tight regulation of release through a conserved set of proteins forming one large macromolecular structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p395]
- RIM binds synaptic vesicle proteins Rab3 and Rab27 to tether vesicles, binds Munc13 to facilitate SNARE complex assembly, and recruits calcium channels via RIM-BP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p395]
- Electron microscopic tomography at the frog neuromuscular junction reveals synaptic vesicles tethered by ribs and beams attached to pegs in the presynaptic membrane, which may correspond to voltage-gated calcium channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p396]
- Phosphorylation of RIM by cAMP-dependent protein kinase is implicated in enhancement of transmitter release associated with long-term synaptic plasticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p395]
