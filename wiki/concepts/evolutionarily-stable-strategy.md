# Evolutionarily Stable Strategy (ESS)

## Summary

An Evolutionarily Stable Strategy (ESS) is a behavioral strategy that, if adopted by most members of a population, cannot be bettered by any alternative strategy. Introduced by J. Maynard Smith and G.R. Price (1973), ESS theory applies game theory to evolution, explaining why animal aggression often appears 'restrained' rather than lethally competitive. An ESS is stable not because it benefits the group, but because it is immune to invasion by mutant strategies.

## Key facts

- ESS was defined by Maynard Smith as a strategy which, if adopted by most members of a population, cannot be bettered by an alternative strategy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p99]
- An ESS is stable not because it is advantageous to individuals, but because it is immune to treachery from within—a conspiracy of all-doves would be better but cannot resist invasion by hawks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p102]
- The hawk strategy (fight unrestrainedly, retreat only when injured) is not evolutionarily stable because in a hawk population, retaliator and dove strategies gain advantage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p104]
- The retaliator strategy (behave like dove initially, but retaliate if attacked) is evolutionarily stable in computer simulations, as no other strategy can invade a population of retaliators. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p104]
- ESS theory shows that dominance hierarchies are best understood as stable outcomes of asymmetric contests with individual recognition, not as group-level adaptations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p113]
- In asymmetric contests, purely arbitrary asymmetries (like resident/intruder) can give rise to ESS even when they confer no inherent advantage, because they allow disputes to be settled without costly fighting. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p109]
- Dawkins calls ESS one of the most important advances in evolutionary theory since Darwin, applicable wherever conflict of interest exists. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p115]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'gloved fist' nature of animal fighting is explained by ESS rather than by group-level altruism: restraint is stable because it prevents escalation that would harm both parties.
- Inference: Territorial behavior may arise as an ESS from the arbitrary asymmetry of who arrives first at a location, not primarily from resource-defense benefits.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether paradoxical strategies (attack when smaller, flee when larger) actually occur in nature, though the Mexican social spider Oecobius civitas shows related behavior.
