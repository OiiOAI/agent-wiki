# Cytoskeleton of Neurons

## Summary

The neuronal cytoskeleton determines cell shape and is responsible for the asymmetric distribution of organelles within the cytoplasm. It consists of three filamentous structures: microtubules, neurofilaments, and microfilaments. These filaments and associated proteins account for approximately a quarter of total cell protein. The cytoskeleton provides tracks along which organelles and proteins are rapidly transported by molecular motors, and its dynamic state permits mature neurons to retract old axons and dendrites and extend new ones.

## Key facts

- The cytoskeleton includes three filamentous structures: microtubules (25 nm diameter), neurofilaments (10 nm), and microfilaments (3-7 nm), which together account for approximately a quarter of total cell protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p139]
- In axons, microtubules display a single orientation with the plus end directed away from the cell body, while in proximal dendrites microtubules can be oriented both ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p139]
- MAP2 is present in dendrites but not in axons, where tau proteins and MAP1b are present instead, contributing to the differential stability of microtubules in these compartments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p139]
- Microfilaments are concentrated at the cell periphery in cortical cytoplasm, forming a network with actin-binding proteins that plays key roles in growth cone motility, generation of specialized microdomains, and formation of synaptic specializations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p140]
- The dynamic state of microtubules and microfilaments permits mature neurons to retract old axons and dendrites and extend new ones, thought to be a major factor in synaptic plasticity and mechanisms of long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p140]
