# Stream-aligned team

## Summary

A stream-aligned team is the primary team type in modern software organizations, aligned to a single, valuable stream of work such as a product, service, user journey, or user persona. The team is empowered to build and deliver customer or user value as quickly, safely, and independently as possible, without requiring hand-offs to other teams. Stream-aligned teams work on the full spectrum of delivery and are closest to the customer, enabling rapid incorporation of feedback and near real-time reaction to system problems.

## Key facts

- Stream-aligned team is the primary team type in a software organization, with the purpose of supporting and enabling other fundamental team topologies to reduce burden on stream-aligned teams. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- A stream-aligned team is aligned to a single, valuable stream of work—could be a single product or service, a single set of features, a single user journey, or a single user persona. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Stream-aligned teams aim to produce a steady flow of feature delivery with minimal (ideally zero) hand-offs of work to other teams. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Stream-aligned teams should possess multiple capabilities including application security, design and architecture, development and coding, infrastructure and operability, metrics and monitoring, product management, testing and quality assurance, and user experience. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Amazon adopted highly independent service teams around 2002 based on a mandate from Jeff Bezos, requiring each team to be fully responsible for developing and operating its own service. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]
- Amazon limits the size of its software teams to those that can be fed by two pizzas to increase accountability and maximize speed of delivery. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L1271-L2183]

## Inferences

- Inference: Stream-aligned teams represent the dominant team topology type, with the ratio of stream-aligned teams to other team types recommended to be between 6:1 and 9:1.
- Inference: The term 'stream aligned' is preferred over 'product' or 'feature' team because it better emphasizes flow and applies to a wider range of situations including non-product contexts.
