# Tragic Optimism

## Summary

Tragic optimism is Frankl's concept about how to remain optimistic in the face of pain, guilt, and death. It maintains that life retains its meaning even in suffering, and that the transitoriness of life makes our choices and responsibilities all the more significant. Frankl contrasts the pessimist, who tears off calendar pages with fear, with the activistic person who files each day away with diary notes and can reflect with pride on the richness of life already lived—including bravely suffered sorrows, which are things of which to be most proud.

## Key facts

- Tragic optimism is how to remain optimistic in the face of pain, guilt, and death, maintaining that life's meaning is unconditional and includes the potential meaning of unavoidable suffering. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p85]
- The pessimist observes with fear and sadness that his wall calendar grows thinner with each passing day, while the person who attacks the problems of life actively removes each successive leaf and files it neatly after jotting down diary notes. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p90]
- Bravely suffered sorrows are things of which one can be most proud, though these are things which cannot inspire envy. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p90]
