# Decision Fatigue

## Summary

Decision fatigue is a form of ego depletion that occurs from making many decisions. As willpower is spent on decisions throughout the day, people's ability to make good choices deteriorates. This explains why judicial decisions become more lenient toward the end of the day, why food shopping leads to poorer choices when tired, and why the temptation to indulge increases after prolonged decision-making.

## Key facts

- The concept is introduced in Chapter 4 as a form of ego depletion occurring from making many decisions. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p85]
- Chapter 3 notes that 'decide' shares the Latin root caedere (to cut/kill) with 'homicide', reflecting how each choice closes off alternatives. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p83]
- Decision fatigue was first identified by Jean Twenge in Baumeister's laboratory while she was planning her wedding and experiencing the depleting effects of choosing from a bridal registry. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p87]
- In experiments, people who made many choices (deciders) gave up significantly sooner on self-control tests than those who simply evaluated products without choosing (nondeciders). [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p88]
- Shoppers in a mall who had already made the most decisions in the stores gave up the quickest on subsequent math problems, demonstrating that 'when you shop till you drop, your willpower drops, too.'. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p89]
- Eliot Spitzer, despite his reputation for political savvy and firm discipline, arranged a hotel tryst with a prostitute, illustrating how decision fatigue affects executives and leaders. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p86]
- Roy Baumeister found that students who had to make many decisions pulled their hands out of ice water much sooner than those who simply wrote about items. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p123]
- IKEA locates its restaurants in the middle of stores because decision fatigue sets in during the trek through maze-like display areas. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p124]
- Israeli parole court study found the percentage of favorable decisions dropped from 65% to nearly zero within a session and returned to 65% after a recess. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p124]

## Inferences

- Inference: Decision fatigue helps explain why ordinarily sensible people get angry at colleagues, splurge on clothes, buy junk food, and cannot resist sales pitches at checkout counters.
- Inference: The occupational hazard of being 'the decider' affects everything from CEO careers to prison sentences.

- Uncertain: The exact neurological mechanisms underlying decision fatigue beyond glucose depletion are not fully specified in this chunk.
