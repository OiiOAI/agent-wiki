# Feeding Regulation

## Summary

Feeding regulation involves the neural control of food intake through short-term satiety signals and long-term energy balance mechanisms. The lateral hypothalamus promotes feeding and foraging, while the ventromedial hypothalamus monitors metabolic status. Multiple neurochemicals including dopamine, opioids, GABA, and neuropeptides modulate feeding behavior.

## Key facts

- Short-term feeding signals include oral factors, gastrointestinal factors, metabolic factors, and various neurochemical influences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p94]
- DA and cholinergic receptor blockade, and facilitation of GABA activity, selectively decrease appetitive approach behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p86]
- Neuropeptide Y (NPY) can dramatically increase feeding and induce obesity with repeated injections. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p99]
- Bombesin, released from the stomach following eating, may promote normal satiety and increase social play behavior in rats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p95]
- Animals adjust food intake precisely when a proportion of daily energy is administered directly into the stomach, regardless of macronutrient source. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p93]
- Conditioned taste aversions are mediated by the area postrema in the brain stem and medial temporal lobes, areas with weaknesses in the blood-brain barrier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p100]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ability of brain opioids to enhance gustatory pleasure may explain why sweet and fatty foods are highly preferred and potentially addictive.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Many purported 'satiety agents' may actually produce malaise or anxiety rather than normal satiety feelings.
