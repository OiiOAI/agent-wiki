# Component Drives

## Summary

Component drives (Partialtriebe) are the constituent elements of the sexual drive derived from different erotogenic zones and somatic sources. Freud proposed that sexuality is composed of multiple drives including scopophilia, exhibitionism, sadism, and masochism, which can operate independently before being integrated into adult genital sexuality. This concept explains the diversity of perversions and their manifestations in neuroses.

## Key facts

- The sexual drive can be traced back to a number of component drives that are not of a primary nature but are susceptible to further analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2440]
- A drive is the psychical representative of an endosomatic, continuously flowing source of stimulation, as contrasted with a stimulus which is set up by single excitations coming from without. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2440]
- The scopophilic and exhibitionistic drive and the active and passive forms of the drive to cruelty play especially prominent roles in the formation of symptoms in psychoneuroses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2439]
- Whenever an active perversion is found in the unconscious, its passive counterpart will regularly also be found in operation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2440]

## Inferences

- Inference: The theory of component drives explains why perversions can manifest in such varied forms—their origins lie in different somatic zones and drive types.
- Inference: Neuroses and perversions represent different outcomes of the same underlying component drives.
