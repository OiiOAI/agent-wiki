# Strategy (Boyd's Definition)

## Summary

Boyd defines strategy as 'a mental tapestry of changing intentions for harmonizing and focusing our efforts as a basis for realizing some aim or purpose in an unfolding and often unforeseen world of many bewildering events and many contending interests.' Unlike plans, strategy is not a fixed roadmap but a dynamic framework that incorporates selecting desired futures, creating mechanisms to harmonize plans and actions, and occasionally shifting to alternate futures.

## Key facts

- Strategy includes selecting the view of the future we want, creating devices to harmonize all the plans and actions designed to achieve that future, and on relatively rare occasions, shifting to an alternate future. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p69]
- Helmuth von Moltke concluded that 'Strategy is a system of ad hoc expedients.'. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p71]
- The Chinese commentary on Sun Tzu summarized strategy as a way to respond to changing conditions and create timely options; the strategy is in the responding and creating, not in the options themselves.
