# Kairos

## Summary

Kairos is the Greek concept of qualitative, opportune time—the experienced moment when one is fully present. Essentialists distinguish between chronos (clock time) and kairos (the right moment), recognizing that meaningful execution occurs only in the present. The practice of focusing on 'what is important now' enables undivided attention and frictionless contribution.

- The ancient Greeks distinguished between chronos (quantitative clock time) and kairos (qualitative, opportune time). [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p198]
- Larry Gelwix coached Highland High School rugby to 418 wins by keeping players focused on the question 'What's important now?'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p197]
- Thich Nhat Hanh taught mindfulness through practices like drinking tea fully present, stating 'In that moment you are real, and the cup of tea is real'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p206]
- Jiro Ono, the world's greatest sushi chef, works entirely in the present moment, making decades of practice appear effortless. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p200]

## Inferences

- Inference: Living in kairos rather than chronos is not about productivity optimization but about experiencing life fully—a shift from doing to being that paradoxically increases both effectiveness and satisfaction.
