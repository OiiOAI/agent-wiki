# Shewhart Cycle

## Summary

The Shewhart Cycle, displayed by Walter A. Shewhart in 1939, represents a fundamental shift from linear specification-production-inspection to a cyclical scientific method. Shewhart showed how specification, production, and inspection correspond to making a hypothesis, carrying out an experiment, and testing the hypothesis.

## Key facts

- Shewhart displayed the new version of the 'Shewhart Cycle' in 1939 in his book 'Statistical Method from the Viewpoint of Quality Control'. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- The cycle contrasts the old view of specification, production, and inspection in a straight line with the new cyclical approach. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Shewhart wrote that the three steps constitute a dynamic scientific process of acquiring knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Shewhart's 1939 book was edited by a 39-year-old W. Edwards Deming. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
