# Resulting

## Summary

'Resulting' is a poker term describing the cognitive trap of equating the quality of a decision with the quality of its outcome. Annie Duke warns that this leads people to change their strategy based on short-term results rather than the underlying decision quality, which is dangerous because outcomes are influenced by luck and hidden information.

## Key facts

- When Duke started playing poker, experienced players warned her about the dangers of resulting, cautioning her to resist changing strategy just because a few hands didn't turn out well in the short run. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L268-L271]
- In consulting exercises, Duke found that everyone identifies their best and worst results rather than their best and worst decisions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L276-L279]
- A CEO identified firing his company president as his worst decision solely because it produced a bad result, despite having gone through a thoughtful analysis process including executive coaching. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L291-L314]
- Duke states that 'resulting' assumes decisions are right or wrong based on outcomes, but decisions should be evaluated by the process and probabilities considered in advance. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L438-L448]
- Resulting evaluates decisions solely by outcomes rather than process quality or information available at the time. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2155-L2175]
- Resulting conflates luck and skill, making accurate learning from experience impossible. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2155-L2175]
- When luck produces good outcomes from bad decisions, resulting rewards poor reasoning. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2155-L2175]
- When luck produces bad outcomes from good decisions, resulting punishes sound reasoning. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2155-L2175]
- Duke argues that 'fielding the outcome'—considering luck's role—is necessary before evaluating decision quality. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2155-L2175]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book's central argument is that resulting leads to poor decision-making because it ignores the role of luck and incomplete information in determining outcomes.
- Inference: Duke's solution is to focus on decision quality (process and probabilities) rather than outcome quality.
- Inference: The Pete Carroll Super Bowl play-calling decision is used as a case study where resulting—rather than examining the decision process—led to unfair criticism.
