# Mental Models of Experts

## Summary

Experts possess mental models of how tasks are performed, teams coordinate, and equipment functions. These models allow experts to know what to expect and to notice when expectancies are violated. Mental models of tasks enable experts to integrate subtasks smoothly without feeling they are performing separate steps. Team coordination models let experts anticipate teammates' needs and actions, as illustrated by a rookie soccer striker who learns to position herself correctly. Equipment mental models allow experts to interpret system outputs, recognize limitations, and develop compensating strategies, exemplified by a navy electronic warfare technician who called his console 'a liar.'

## Key facts

- Experts have mental models of tasks, team coordination, and equipment, which let them know what to expect and notice violations of expectancies. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p192]
- Mental models of tasks enable experts to integrate subtasks smoothly; they do not feel they are performing separate subtasks. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p192]
- A rookie soccer striker may have speed and coordination but often positions herself wrong, trailing plays instead of leading them. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p192]
- A navy electronic warfare technician referred to his console as 'a liar' because he understood it could report spurious airplanes under certain conditions. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p192]
- Experts understand that equipment is reliable but limited in predictable ways, whereas novices tend to either trust equipment blindly or distrust it entirely when misled. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p192]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mental models are built through experience and allow experts to operate fluidly by anticipating and adapting to situations holistically rather than through conscious step-by-step processing.
