# Habituation

## Summary

Habituation is the simplest form of nonassociative implicit learning, characterized by a decrease in response to a repeatedly presented benign stimulus. In Aplysia, habituation of the gill-withdrawal reflex occurs through homosynaptic depression—a reduction in neurotransmitter release from presynaptic sensory neurons onto motor neurons—that persists for minutes in short-term habituation and up to 3 weeks in long-term habituation.

## Key facts

- In Aplysia, habituation of the gill-withdrawal reflex involves decreased glutamate release from sensory neuron presynaptic terminals onto motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1315]
- A single session of 10 stimuli produces short-term habituation lasting minutes, while four sessions over days produces long-term habituation lasting up to 3 weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1316]
- Long-term habituation in Aplysia reduces the percentage of sensory neurons making detectable connections with motor neurons from 90% to 30%. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1316]
