# The Extended Disorder Family

## Summary

A set of sixteen related concepts that function equivalently in their effect on fragile and antifragile systems: uncertainty, variability, imperfect knowledge, chance, chaos, volatility, disorder, entropy, time, the unknown, randomness, turmoil, stressors, errors, dispersion of outcomes, and unknowledge. Members of this 'family' all have equivalent effects on systems, penalizing the fragile and benefiting the antifragile.

## Key facts

- The Extended Disorder Family consists of: uncertainty, variability, imperfect and incomplete knowledge, chance, chaos, volatility, disorder, entropy, time, the unknown, randomness, turmoil, stressors, errors, dispersion of outcomes, and unknowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p30]
- Uncertainty, disorder, and the unknown are completely equivalent in their effect on antifragile systems, which benefit from them, and fragile systems, which are penalized by them. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p30]
- Time is functionally similar to volatility; the more time passes, the more events and disorder occur, bringing the errors or reverse errors that benefit antifragile systems. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p30]
- The fragile breaks with time; antifragile systems benefit from time because it brings errors that can be exploited for improvement. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p30]

## Inferences

- Inference: The equivalence of these factors suggests a unified treatment of uncertainty across domains, simplifying decision-making by focusing on the common property of penalizing fragility.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether all sixteen members of the disorder family are truly equivalent or if there are meaningful distinctions between them in specific contexts.
