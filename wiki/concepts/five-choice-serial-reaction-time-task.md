# Five-Choice Serial Reaction Time Task

## Summary

The five-choice serial reaction time task (5CSRTT) is a behavioral paradigm used to measure waiting impulsivity and attentional performance in rodents. In the task, rats or mice are trained to detect brief visual stimuli to earn food reinforcers by making a nosepoke response in the illuminated location. Individuals vary considerably in their tendency to respond prematurely (before the stimulus appears), and this variation has trait-like characteristics, predicting vulnerability to substance abuse.

## Key facts

- In the 5CSRTT, rats or mice are trained to detect brief visual stimuli to earn food reinforcers by a nosepoke response in the illuminated location. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p86]
- Incorrect or premature nosepoke responses are punished by a time-out from reinforcement, signaled by dimming of the house lights. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p86]
- Individual variation in premature responding on the 5CSRTT is associated with increased propensity for cocaine, nicotine, and sucrose reinforcement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p88]
- High-impulsive rats on the 5CSRTT show reduced D2/3 receptor binding in the ventral striatum before exposure to cocaine, suggesting low D2/3 binding is an endophenotype for stimulant abuse. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p88]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 5CSRTT provides a translational measure of impulsivity that can be applied across species, enabling cross-species comparative studies of neural substrates.
