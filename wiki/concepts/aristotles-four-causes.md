# Aristotle's four causes

## Summary

Aristotle articulated a theory of causality defining four types of explanation for how natural objects and systems behave. The material cause concerns what something is made of. The efficient cause concerns external forces inducing change. The formal cause relates to the organization of components. The final cause refers to the end or intended purpose. Aristotle saw these as complementary perspectives. Francis Bacon later argued science should focus only on material and efficient causes (mechanisms), consigning formal and final causes to metaphysics, an attitude that remains prevalent but focuses exclusively on 'how' questions rather than 'why' questions.

## Key facts

- Aristotle's four causes: material (what it's made of), efficient (external forces), formal (organization), and final (purpose or intended end). [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p165]
- Francis Bacon argued science should focus solely on material and efficient causes (mechanisms), consigning formal and final causes to 'magic'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p165]
- Mechanistic explanations answer 'how' questions but not 'why' questions; the 'why' relates to function and purpose built into structural configuration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p166]
