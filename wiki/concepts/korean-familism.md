# Korean Familism

## Summary

Korean familism is a cultural orientation that prioritizes the family unit above all other social institutions, creating strong bonds of loyalty and mutual obligation within kinship groups. Unlike Japanese Confucianism, which extended family loyalty to the larger social hierarchy, Korean familism remained intensely focused on the nuclear and extended family, with kinship ties serving as the primary basis for economic cooperation and business organization.

## Key facts

- Korean familism is more intense and exclusive than Japanese familism, remaining focused on the family rather than extending to hierarchical social relationships. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p401]
- Korean family ties remain more important than ties to the workplace or other social institutions compared to Japan. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]
- The cultural roots of greater Korean individualism arise out of the nature of Korean familism. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]
- Korean lineage organizations were institutionalized to a greater degree than in China or Japan, with lineage-wide property ownership and management. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p402]
- The surname Kim, for example, is shared among seven or eight large lineages, not all claiming ancestry from the same lineage. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p402]
- Korean workers display significant suspicion and distrust in revealing their opinions or opening up to outsiders about their social relations within the firm. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p403]

## Inferences

- Inference: The intensity of Korean familism made family the natural unit for organizing business, explaining why chaebol leadership remained tightly controlled by founding families rather than professional managers.
- Inference: The cultural roots of Korean individualism arise from Korean familism because intense family bonds create weaker ties to broader social institutions.
