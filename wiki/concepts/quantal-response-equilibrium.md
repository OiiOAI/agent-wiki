# Quantal Response Equilibrium

## Summary

Quantal Response Equilibrium (QRE), developed by McKelvey and Palfrey (1995, 1998), is an equilibrium concept that relaxes the assumption that players make decisions without errors. In QRE, best response functions account for the probability that players will not successfully implement their intended strategy.

## Key facts

- A quantal response equilibrium exists for any finite strategic game under the logistic response function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p51]
- Quantal response equilibrium can be interpreted as a reinforcement learning model. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p51]

## Inferences

- Inference: QRE provides a behavioral foundation for stochastic choice in games by incorporating errors into players' decision processes.
