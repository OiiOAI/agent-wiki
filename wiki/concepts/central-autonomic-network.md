# Central Autonomic Network

## Summary

The central autonomic network is a distributed system of brain regions that coordinates autonomic function with behavior, emotion, and homeostasis. It includes the nucleus of the solitary tract, parabrachial nucleus, ventrolateral medulla, periaqueductal gray, hypothalamus, amygdala, and cortex. The network integrates visceral sensory information and generates coordinated autonomic responses.

## Key facts

- The nucleus of the solitary tract is the main entry point for general visceral sensory information from cranial nerves IX and X, distributing this information to preganglionic parasympathetic neurons and to regions coordinating autonomic and respiratory reflexes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1121]
- The nucleus of the solitary tract projects to the pontine parabrachial nucleus, which is important for behavioral responses to visceral information and taste, and both nuclei project to the hypothalamus, amygdala, and cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1121]
- The periaqueductal gray in the midbrain helps redirect blood flow during the defensive 'fight-or-flight' response, enhancing running capacity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1121]
- The hypothalamus, specifically the descending division of the paraventricular nucleus and cell clusters in the lateral zone, projects heavily to both parasympathetic and sympathetic preganglionic neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1122]
