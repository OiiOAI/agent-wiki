# SRY Gene

## Summary

SRY (Sex-determining Region Y) is the master regulatory gene on the Y chromosome that initiates male sexual differentiation. It encodes a transcription factor containing an HMG box DNA-binding domain that regulates expression of downstream genes, including SOX9, to promote testis formation over ovarian development.

## Key facts

- SRY resides on the nonhomologous region of the short arm of the Y chromosome and is the sex-determining locus in many mammals including primates and most rodents. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1262]
- SRY initiates a cascade by regulating SOX9, another transcription factor required for differentiation of the testes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1262]
- Rare SRY translocations to the X chromosome produce XXSRY males, while YΔSRY deletions produce phenotypically female XY individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1262]
