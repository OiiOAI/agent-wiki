# Phylotypic body plan

## Summary

The phylotypic body plan is the spatial organization an animal shares with all members of its phylum, arising during the phylotypic stage of early embryonic development. For chordates, this is the pharyngula stage when the four distinguishing features (notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, gill slits, and post-anal tail) are present. Body plans have been highly conserved for 530 million years while showing considerable diversification at pre- and post-phylotypic stages, leading to the image of the phylotypic stage as an 'isthmus' between diversified stages.

## Key facts

- The phylotypic body plan is the spatial organization an animal shares with all members of its phylum, arising during early development at the phylotypic stage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p193]
- Body plans can be described not only anatomically but in terms of gene homology (compartments of selector gene expression) and homology of process (spatial organization of compartmentalized developmental processes). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p193]
- Early development converges on the phylotypic stage and then diverges; all members of a phylum look similar at this stage despite differences earlier and later in development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p194]
- The phylotypic processes have been conserved because of selections on the diversifications they allowed; it is the diversifiability of the body plan, not just its anatomy, that has been continuously selected. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p197]
