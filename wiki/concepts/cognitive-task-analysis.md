# Cognitive Task Analysis

## Summary

Cognitive Task Analysis (CTA) is a methodology for understanding how people perform tasks mentally—how they perceive, think, decide, and remember. The book describes Goal-Directed Task Analysis (GDTA) as a form of CTA used to identify SA requirements. CTA involves interviews with subject matter experts (SMEs), review of documentation, and observation to understand the cognitive demands of a job. Many variants of CTA exist.

## Key facts

- Cognitive task analysis is conducted through observations of operations, interviews with subject matter experts, and reviews of available documentation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p67]
- The book describes Goal-Directed Task Analysis (GDTA) as a form of cognitive task analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p83]
- GDTA focuses on dynamic information requirements relevant in a particular domain rather than static system knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p83]
- Many variants of CTA exist including observations, interviews with SMEs, and reviews of available documentation. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p67]
