# Perceptual Inference

## Summary

Perceptual inference is the process by which the brain generates predictions and compares them with sensory input. Solms emphasises that perception is fundamentally self-generated—the world as we experience it is a virtual reality constructed from cortical representations, not a direct reflection of external reality. The cortex generates predictions and sensory signals provide prediction errors for updating.

## Key facts

- What we perceive is a virtual reality constructed from the mind's own building materials, not simply what is 'there' externally. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p197]
- Binocular rivalry demonstrates that perception is inference—what we experience depends on our prior hypotheses, not just the retinal input. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p202-203]
- The brain assigns qualities like colour to the world by asking questions about relative light intensities and wavelengths. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p197]

## Inferences

- Inference: Neurological patients like Patient WB (who sees the world rotated 180 degrees) provide evidence that normal perception requires the brain to make corrective inferences that we are normally unaware of.
