# Natural Planning Model

## Summary

The Natural Planning Model describes how the brain naturally plans any task through five sequential phases: (1) defining purpose and principles, (2) outcome visioning, (3) brainstorming, (4) organizing, and (5) identifying next actions. This model serves as the foundation for effective project planning in GTD, distinguishing it from artificial or reactive planning approaches.

## Key facts

- The Natural Planning Model has five phases: Purpose, Outcome Visioning, Brainstorming, Organizing, and Identifying Next Actions. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p99]
- The brain is described as the most brilliant and creative planner, automatically processing these five phases for every task. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p99]
- Purpose defines success, creates decision-making criteria, aligns resources, motivates, clarifies focus, and expands options. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p105]
- Principles define the parameters of action and criteria for excellence of conduct. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p108]
- Vision provides the blueprint of what the project will look like when successfully completed. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p108]
- The Natural Planning Model contrasts with the Reactive Planning Model, which starts with action before defining purpose. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p102]
- The Natural Planning Model consists of five phases: defining purpose and principles, outcome vision, brainstorming, organizing, and identifying next actions. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p117]
- The key steps in organizing are: identify the significant pieces, sort by components/sequences/priorities, and detail to the required degree. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p117]
- The final stage of planning involves decisions about allocating and reallocating physical resources to get the project moving by identifying the next action. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p118]
- A project is sufficiently planned for implementation when every next-action step has been decided for every front that can be moved on without depending on another component first. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p118]
- If greater clarity is needed, one should shift thinking up the natural planning scale from action to organization to brainstorming to vision to purpose. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p120]
- If more action is needed, one should move down the model to identify next actions and clarify outcomes. [raw/books/productivity/Getting Things Done_ The Art of Stress - Fre - Allen, David.pdf#p121]
