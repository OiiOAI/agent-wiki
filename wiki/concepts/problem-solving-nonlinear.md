# Problem Solving (Nonlinear)

## Summary

The nonlinear account of problem solving describes it as a constructive process with four interactive components: problem detection, problem representation, option generation, and evaluation. Unlike stage models, these components influence each other rather than proceeding linearly.

## Key facts

- Most natural goals are ill-defined, meaning experienced people may not agree on how to satisfy the goal, making standard linear problem-solving approaches ineffective. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p163]
- Standard advice on problem solving is aimed at well-defined goals and can actually interfere with solving ill-defined problems. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p164]
- Problem solving is a constructive process where solutions are built from leverage points and the nature of the goal can be clarified during problem-solving efforts. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p157]
- Goals affect how we evaluate courses of action, and evaluation helps us learn to set better goals in a bidirectional relationship. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p158]
- The judgment of solvability—using experience to assess whether a problem can be solved without too much work—is an experiential ability essential to problem solving. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p160]

## Inferences

- Inference: Problem solving and decision making blur in natural settings, with problem solving being a subclass of decision making when formulation of new courses of action is needed, and decision making being a subclass of problem solving when comparing courses of action is required.
