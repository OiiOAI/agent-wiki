# Hannibal as Psychological Symbol

## Summary

Freud's identification with Hannibal represents a complex psychological symbol combining childhood hero worship, Jewish identity, and resistance to anti-Semitism. Following in Hannibal's footsteps, including not seeing Rome, became symbolic of unattainable aims and Jewish determination against oppression.

## Key facts

- Hannibal, whom Freud came to resemble in not seeing Rome and moving into the Campagna when expected in Rome, had been the favorite hero of his later schooldays. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1459]
- To Freud's youthful mind, Hannibal and Rome symbolized the conflict between the tenacity of Jewry and the organization of the Catholic Church. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1459]
- The increasing importance of the effects of the anti-Semitic movement upon emotional life helped fix the thoughts and feelings of those early days. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1459]
- Rome and Karlsbad come to be identified as symbols of unattainable aims in Freud's correspondence and dream analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1458]
