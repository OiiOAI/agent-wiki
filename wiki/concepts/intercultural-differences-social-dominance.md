# Intercultural Differences in Social Dominance Appraisal

## Summary

Human cultures vastly differ in how they value personality traits related to social dominance. In individualistic cultures, self-construal emphasizes agency and social dominance, whereas in collectivistic cultures, self-construal emphasizes communion and social connection. Neuroimaging studies have shown that neural correlates of social dominance expressed by body postures are reversed in American versus Japanese subjects.

## Key facts

- In individualistic cultures, the relevant dimension of self-construal is agency, defined as concern with personal effectiveness and social dominance. In collectivistic cultures, the relevant dimension is communion, defined as concern with personal integration and social connection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p211]
- European American children are more motivated to solve anagrams when they could choose the category of problem compared to when their mother chose, whereas Asian American children displayed the opposite pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p211]
- In Americans, mPFC and caudate nucleus exhibited stronger BOLD responses to dominant stimuli relative to subordinate stimuli; in Japanese, these same regions exhibited the opposite pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p217]
- Endogenous serotonin reuptake capacity covaried with individualism-collectivism and the steepness of social hierarchies from one country to another. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p217]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cross-cultural differences in the perception of social dominance may be partly based on polymorphisms of genes engaged in serotonergic and dopaminergic transmission.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms driving these cultural differences in neural responses to dominance cues remain to be fully elucidated.
