# Teeter-Totter Principle

## Summary

The teeter-totter principle describes the equilibrium between overcompetence on one hand and overchallenge on the other. Overcompetence leads to complacency, autopilot behavior, disengagement, and boredom. Overchallenge leads to stress, overwhelm, and inability to be creative or responsive. Optimal development occurs at the edge of one's ability—where one is neither complacent nor overwhelmed. This zone is reached through incrementally advancing beyond comfort levels, expanding both breadth (range of activities) and depth (quality of engagement).

## Key facts

- Overcompetence occurs when one could do something with eyes closed, no longer experiencing expansion in skills or possibility. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p134]
- Overchallenge occurs when juggling complexities that inhibit ability to be creative, appropriately responsive, or to thrive. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p134]
- The zone of optimal development is reached when living at the edge of ability—neither overcompetent nor overwhelmed. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p135]
- We move to edge of ability through incrementally advancing beyond level of competence and comfort. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p135]

## Inferences

- Inference: The teeter-totter suggests that sustainable growth requires continuous micro-adjustments rather than static balance.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The text does not specify how one measures whether they are in the optimal zone versus just near it.
