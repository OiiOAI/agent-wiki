# Human Brain Uniqueness

## Summary

The proposition that the human brain has many unique features beyond simple size differences, including specific genes for brain development (microcephalin, ASPM), unique cortical architecture, greater connectivity, and microscopic differences in areas like the planum temporale. Gazzaniga argues that despite historical assumptions of continuity with other animals, evidence shows qualitative differences in human brain structure and function.

## Key facts

- Human brains are 4-5 times larger than expected for an average mammal of comparable size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p20]
- The neocortex in humans is 76% of brain weight in chimpanzees but even larger in humans, compared to 16% in hedgehogs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p26]
- Area 10 in the lateral prefrontal cortex is almost twice as large in humans as in apes, involved with memory, planning, cognitive flexibility, and abstract thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p28]
- The planum temporale shows microscopic uniqueness in the left hemisphere of humans - minicolumns are larger and intercolumnar spaces are wider compared to chimpanzees and rhesus monkeys. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p32]
- The prefrontal white matter is disproportionately larger in humans than other primates, suggesting higher connectivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p29]
- Microcephalin and ASPM are genes that regulate brain size, and both show evidence of accelerated evolution during Homo sapiens evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p23]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery of predecessor cells in the developing human brain (described by Pasko Rakic in 2006) that may not exist in other animals further supports human brain uniqueness

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: While humans and chimpanzees share 98.6% of DNA nucleotide sequence, the 1.44% difference in chromosome structure generates changes in most proteins, with 83% of coding sequences showing differences at the amino acid level
