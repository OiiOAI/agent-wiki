# The Regal Moon

## Summary

The Regal Moon addresses recognising who is in charge in different areas of life and acting accordingly. The concept introduces Monarchs who wear the crown and have final say within a defined Realm. Three types of leadership are identified: wearing the crown yourself (you are the Monarch), another person wearing the crown (you are a subject), and joint leadership. The chapter explores regal rules, advisors, subjects, professional versus personal relationships, teams, and the importance of respecting the crown when it is on someone else's head.

## Key facts

- Any system involving people needs clearly defined philosophies, procedures, and 'rules' to resolve differences of opinion, with Humans discussing ideas and typically coming to joint opinions while Chimps give opinion, criticise others, and rarely give way. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p162]
- The Human and Chimp will both reach a settlement if there are clear rules and a definite agreed leader who has the ability to make the final decision; effective leaders listen to and respect the opinion of others. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p162]
- You are the Monarch of your home Realm where you decide who enters and you decide on the rules; when you enter someone else's home you have entered their territory and their Realm, and they are the Monarch. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p163]
- Regal rules are behaviours and attitudes you have decided to abide by as a Monarch—they are not for other people to abide by, and serve as a reminder to your Chimp. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p163]
- Monarchs have a responsibility to appoint advisors who will offer advice (not commands); one factor differentiating successful from unsuccessful people is the ability to recognise when they need help and get it from the best source. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p163]
- Professional relationships have prescribed boundaries especially interpersonal ones, with confidentiality paramount and no emotional judgements made, whereas personal relationships have no prescribed boundaries and are emotionally based with flexible boundaries. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p166]
- When you enter a personal relationship with someone, some part of the relationship is in a joint Realm where decisions are necessarily joint ones, while other parts are not in the joint Realm and need individual decisions. [raw/books/psychology/The Chimp Paradox_ How Our Impulses and Emotions Can - Peters, Dr Steve.pdf#p166]
