# 40-Hertz Oscillations

## Summary

40-Hertz oscillations refer to rhythmic neural firing patterns in the 35-75 Hz frequency range observed in the visual cortex, first documented by German researchers Wolf Singer and Charles Gray in Frankfurt, and independently by Reinhard Eckhorn in Marburg. These oscillations are hypothesized to solve the binding problem by synchronizing the firing of neurons that represent different attributes of a single visual object.

## Key facts

- Oscillations in the 35- to 75-Hertz range were observed in the cat's visual cortex by Singer, Gray, and colleagues at Frankfurt, and independently by Eckhorn and colleagues at Marburg. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p259]
- Neurons firing in such oscillations fire 'on the beat' of the local oscillations and often do so in approximate synchrony with fellow neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p259]
- Crick and Koch proposed that synchronized firing at gamma oscillation frequencies might be the neural correlate of visual awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p260]
- Synchronized oscillations can occur between neurons in different cortical areas and also between the two hemispheres of the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p259]
- These oscillations are transient and often depend on the duration of the visual signal used; correlated oscillations between neuron sets may persist for only a few hundred milliseconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p261]
- 40-Hertz oscillations have been observed in alert monkeys when moving bars are used as visual input but not when patterns of semirandom moving dots are shown. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p261]
- The oscillations have also been clearly seen in the motor somatosensory cortex of alert monkeys, especially when performing intricate manual tasks demanding attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p261]

## Inferences

- Inference: The observation that oscillations appear in figure-ground discrimination tasks (moving bars but not random dots) suggests they may be involved more in object segmentation than in general visual awareness.
- Inference: Crick suggests the main function of attentional mechanism would be to select an object for attention and then help synchronize the relevant neuron coalition corresponding to the brain's best interpretation of that visual input.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact behavioral function of 40-Hertz oscillations remains unclear - the difference between responses to moving bars versus random dots has not been explained.
- Uncertain: Whether oscillations are essential for consciousness or an epiphenomenon is not yet determined.
