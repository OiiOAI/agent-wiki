# Ignore the Real World

## Summary

The concept dismisses objections based on 'the real world' as self-defeating excuses for not trying. The authors argue that people who invoke the real world expect fresh concepts to fail, are filled with pessimism, want to drag hopeful people down, and represent an excuse rather than reality. They counter with examples of 37signals doing things the real world says are impossible: having employees spread across eight cities on two continents, attracting millions of customers without salespeople or advertising, and revealing their formula for success.

## Key facts

- The authors describe 'the real world' as an excuse, justification for not trying, and a tomb that pessimistic people want to drag others into. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p14]
- The authors cite 37signals' successes that defy real-world expectations: employees in eight cities on two continents, millions of customers without salespeople or advertising, and sharing their formula for success. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p14]
- The authors conclude that 'the real world isn't a place, it's an excuse' and 'has nothing to do with you.'. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p14]

## Inferences

- Inference: The real world objection is often invoked by those who have failed at similar ventures and project their limitations onto others.
