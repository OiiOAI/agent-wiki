# Hypnotism

## Summary

Hypnotism and suggestion are discussed in Volume I through Freud's early reviews, prefaces, and clinical observations. The texts document Freud's growing engagement with Bernheim's Nancy school and Charcot's Salpêtrière approaches to hypnotism, culminating in his own experiments and his 1891 paper 'Hypnosis.' Freud's review of Forel's work on hypnotism (1889) marked his first publication on the subject, while his review of Berkhan's experiments on deaf-mutism showed interest in hypnosis as a means of increasing sensory excitability.

## Key facts

- Freud's review of Berkhan's experiments on hypnotizing deaf-mutes (1887) was his very first publication on hypnotism, describing how hypnosis restored partial hearing in several boys. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p77]
- Freud's review of August Forel's Hypnotism (1889) explored the phenomena of grand hypnotisme described by Charcot, which had been met with 'more doubt than recognition' in German-speaking countries. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p46]
- The paper 'Hypnosis' (1891) is listed as one of the major pre-psychoanalytic works in Volume I, following the reviews and prefaces on hypnotism and suggestion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p13]
- Freud's 1892 report 'On Hypnosis and Suggestion' was an exception to the general rule that reports of Freud's lectures at medical societies were excluded from the SE/RSE. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p20]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's engagement with hypnotism was a transitional step between his neurological work and his psychological investigations, as the phenomena of suggestion pointed toward the role of unconscious mental processes.
