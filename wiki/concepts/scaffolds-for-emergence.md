# Scaffolds for Emergence

## Summary

Scaffolds for emergence distinguish temporary, purpose-built structures that support emergence from permanent frameworks that define structure. Drawing on architectural and biological metaphors (tissue regeneration scaffolds, cyborg tissues), scaffolds support the natural emergence of organizational outcomes without imposing deterministic forms. This concept directly contrasts with traditional theories of change that rely on permanent frameworks.

## Key facts

- A framework is a skeletal structure that gives form to that which it supports, while a scaffold is a temporary structure for supporting something until that something can stand on its own. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p341]
- Biological scaffolds in regenerative medicine support cell attachment and migration and can deliver nutrients or exert biological influences on cell behavior, eventually dissolving as tissue regenerates. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p342]
- Cyborg tissues at Harvard combine living cells with embedded nanowires and transistors, creating a sensor network while cells behave normally—the scaffold becomes part of the system. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p343]
- Designing for emergence involves designing ecologies of change—integrated, interacting sets of mechanisms that aim to steer a complex adaptive system while it is in motion. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p347]
- John Holland's four characteristics of emergence are: systems that learn through feedback, component mechanisms that interact without central control, flexible interactions, and building blocks that aggregate into more complex levels. [raw/books/strategy/Cynefin - Weaving Sense - Making into the Fa - Dave Snowden.pdf#p351]
