# Adult neurogenesis in dentate gyrus

## Summary

Adult neurogenesis in the dentate gyrus is the continued production of new excitatory granule neurons throughout adulthood. These new neurons become incorporated into neural circuits and are specifically required for pattern separation. Procedures that stimulate neurogenesis enhance the ability to discriminate between closely related environments, suggesting potential therapeutic approaches for age-related memory loss.

## Key facts

- Adult neurogenesis continues throughout adulthood in the excitatory granule neurons of the dentate gyrus, one of only two brain regions where new neurons are generated throughout life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
- Adult-born granule neurons are specifically required for pattern separation, as blocking neurogenesis by X-ray irradiation or administration of DNA synthesis inhibitors impairs contextual memory tasks requiring discrimination between similar environments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
- Procedures that stimulate neurogenesis enhance the ability of mice to perform pattern separation during contextual memory tasks requiring discrimination between closely related environments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
- Methods to enhance neurogenesis are being explored as potential treatments for different types of age-related memory loss. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1564]
