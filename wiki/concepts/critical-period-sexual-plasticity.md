# Critical Period in Sexual Development

## Summary

Critical periods are brief windows of time in childhood when new brain systems and maps develop with the help of stimulation from one's environment. Freud discovered that early childhood, not puberty, is the first critical period for sexuality and intimacy, and that children are capable of passionate, protosexual feelings. During these periods, patterns of relating and attaching to others can get 'wired' into the brain and repeated in adulthood.

## Key facts

- Freud discovered critical periods for sexual plasticity, arguing that an adult's ability to love intimately and sexually unfolds in stages beginning in infancy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p107]
- Children are capable of passionate, protosexual feelings, crushes, and loving feelings from early childhood. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p107]
- Freud discovered that sexual abuse of children is harmful because it influences the critical period of sexuality in childhood, shaping later attractions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p108]
- If the parent is warm, gentle, and reliable, the child will frequently develop a taste for that kind of relationship later on. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p108]
- Early patterns of relating and attaching to others, if problematic, can get 'wired' into our brains in childhood and repeated in adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p108]
- Adolescence represents another critical period of sexual plasticity in which the brain reorganizes again, so that the pleasure of sex becomes intense enough to override any disgust. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p109]

## Inferences

- Inference: The existence of critical periods for sexual development means that therapeutic interventions may be more effective when they work with the brain's natural plasticity rather than against established neural pathways.
- Inference: The concept helps explain why some individuals develop atypical sexual preferences or problematic attachment patterns.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact timing and duration of critical periods for different aspects of sexual development are not precisely established.
