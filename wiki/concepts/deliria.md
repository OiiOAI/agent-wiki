# Deliria

## Summary

Freud introduces the term 'deliria' to describe psychical structures that emerge during the secondary defensive struggle against obsessional thoughts. These are hybrids between reasonable considerations and pathological thought—they accept certain premises of the obsession they are combating while using the weapons of reason. Deliria represent a compromise formation where conscious thought misapprehends the obsessional material. Examples include the patient's rationalization of changing 'aber' to 'abér' as strengthening the mute 'e', when the truth was approximation toward 'Abwehr' (defence), or the patient's magical word that unconsciously united his semen with the name of his beloved.

- Deliria are hybrids between reasonable considerations and pathological thought, accepting premises of the obsession while using weapons of reason. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3331]

- Deliria constantly tend to form new connections with the part of the obsession that is not present in consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3348]

## Inferences

- Inference: The term 'deliria' fills a nosological gap, distinguishing these hybrid formations from both normal reasoning and purely obsessional content.
