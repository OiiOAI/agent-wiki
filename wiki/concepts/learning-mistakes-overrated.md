# Learning from Mistakes Is Overrated

## Summary

The concept challenges the common wisdom that failure is a necessary precursor to success. The authors argue that other people's failures have nothing to do with you, that learning from mistakes only tells you what not to do again without revealing what to do next, and that success gives real ammunition for future endeavors. They cite a Harvard Business School study showing that already-successful entrepreneurs have a 34 percent success rate with future companies while entrepreneurs whose companies failed have only a 23 percent success rate, similar to first-time entrepreneurs.

## Key facts

- The authors argue that learning from mistakes only reveals what not to do again without showing what should be done next. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p16]
- A Harvard Business School study found that already-successful entrepreneurs have a 34 percent follow-on success rate, while entrepreneurs whose companies failed have only a 23 percent success rate, similar to first-time entrepreneurs. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p16]
- The authors argue that evolution doesn't linger on past failures but builds upon what worked, and that success is the experience that actually counts. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p16]

## Inferences

- Inference: Success may beget success through learned behaviors, accumulated confidence, and proven methodologies rather than through failure-derived lessons.
