# Negative Feedback

## Summary

Negative feedback is the control principle underlying purposive machine behavior, including biological nervous systems. A purpose machine measures discrepancy between current and desired states, working harder when discrepancy is larger, thereby automatically reducing the gap. The Watt steam governor is the classic example; biological brains use negative feedback extensively for coordinating muscle contractions with external events.

## Key facts

- Negative feedback machines have a measuring device that detects discrepancy from a 'desired' state, working harder as discrepancy increases to reduce it—this is why it is called 'negative' feedback. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p78]
- The Watt governor uses spinning balls whose centrifugal force, resisted by gravity, controls steam valve opening: faster rotation leads to valves shutting, slowing the engine automatically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p78]
- Guided missiles exemplify sophisticated purpose machines using negative feedback, feed-forward, and prediction to pursue targets without consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p79]
- Sense organs translate external patterns into neurone pulse codes, and the brain must coordinate muscle contractions with these sensory events for adaptive behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p77]
- The 'desired state' of a Watt governor is simply the state to which it tends to return—no consciousness is required for apparent purposive behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p79]

## Inferences

- Inference: The common misconception that guided missiles are 'really' controlled by humans parallels the misconception that genes 'really' control organism behavior—both overlook the autonomy of the intermediate mechanism.
