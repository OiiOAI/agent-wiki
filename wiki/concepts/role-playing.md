# Role-playing

## Summary

Role-playing refers to the universal human tendency to wear social masks and perform different roles depending on the audience. The text cites Shakespeare's observation that 'all the world is a stage' and emphasizes that this theatrical quality is essential to social functioning. People learn to conceal insecurities, flatter important figures, and present the best possible front. The key insight is that we must both accept role-playing as necessary while also developing skills to see through others' masks.

## Key facts

- Shakespeare's quote 'All the world is a stage, and all the men and women merely players' captures the theatrical quality of social life where everyone wears masks. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p107]
- People learn at an early age how to get what they want from parents by putting on certain looks that elicit sympathy or affection. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p107]
- We become good actors without awareness—we imagine we are almost always sincere while subtly changing what we say, tone of voice, and body language to suit each situation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p107]
- The word 'personality' comes from the Latin 'persona,' meaning 'mask,' highlighting that public identity involves wearing masks. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p131]
- Emperor Augustus exemplified master impression management, projecting humility while holding tight to military control, and reportedly called life 'the farce' on his deathbed. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text distinguishes between two tasks: accepting role-playing as necessary for social functioning, and developing skills to decode others' true feelings beneath their masks.
