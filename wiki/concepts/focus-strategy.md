# Focus (Strategy)

## Summary

Focus in strategy has two meanings: first, it denotes the coordination of policies that produces extra power through their interacting and overlapping effects; second, it denotes the application of that power to the right target. A focused strategy attacks a segment of the market with a business system supplying more value to that segment than competitors can. Focus involves coordinating policies around a clear target rather than pursuing multiple unrelated goals.

## Key facts

- Focus denotes the coordination of policies that produces extra power through their interacting and overlapping effects, and denotes the application of that power to the right target. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p61]
- Crown Cork & Seal's focus was on shorter production runs, serving smaller customers, rush orders, and less production per customer—creating bargaining power opposite to the captive producers in the industry. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p60]
- By concentrating on a carefully selected part of the market, Crown crafted a competitive advantage—Crown wasn't the biggest can maker but made the most money. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p60]
- Most complex organizations don't focus their resources; instead they pursue multiple goals at once without concentrating enough resources to achieve a breakthrough in any of them. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p61]
