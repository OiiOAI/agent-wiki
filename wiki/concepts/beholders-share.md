# Beholder's share

## Summary

The beholder's share is the concept that perceptual experience involves the observer imaginatively 'completing' what is perceived. First introduced by Alois Riegl and popularized by Ernst Gombrich, it highlights that perception involves top-down inference — there is no 'innocent eye'.

## Key facts

- The beholder's share is the part of perceptual experience contributed by the perceiver, not found in the artwork or world itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p118]
- Ernst Gombrich argued there is no 'innocent eye' — all visual perception is based on classifying concepts and interpreting visual information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p118]
- One cannot perceive that which one cannot classify. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p118]
- Impressionist paintings attempt to remove the artist from the act of painting, imparting raw materials for perceptual inference rather than the output of this process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p119]
- Impressionist paintings can be understood as exercises in reverse engineering the human visual system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p119]

## Inferences

- Inference: The beholder's share connects directly to predictive processing: perceptual predictions are the 'completion' the brain contributes, and this applies to all perception, not just art.
