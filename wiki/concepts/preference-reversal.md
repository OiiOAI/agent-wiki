# Preference Reversal

## Summary

Preference reversal occurs when the relative attractiveness of two options reverses depending on whether they are evaluated separately (single evaluation) or compared directly (joint evaluation). In single evaluation, emotionally salient features dominate and produce one ranking; in joint evaluation, normative features dominate and may produce the opposite ranking. This phenomenon challenged expected utility theory and was confirmed by economists David Grether and Charles Plott.

## Key facts

- In Lichtenstein and Slovic's classic experiment, most participants chose betting option B (high probability of winning $40) over option A (lower probability of winning $160), but when asked to set a selling price for each option, assigned a higher price to option A—directly contradicting their choice. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p459]
- In a robbery scenario, victims shot at the store they frequented were awarded lower compensation than those shot at an unfamiliar store in single evaluation, because 'if only I had gone to my usual store' is more readily imagined in the latter case, triggering stronger emotional response. In joint evaluation, almost all participants agreed compensation should be identical. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p457]
- David Grether and Charles Plott, both prominent economists, published their own study in the American Economic Review attempting to explain away preference reversals; after testing 13 alternative theories, only one remained: that the psychologists were right and that individual choices systematically depend on the context in which they are made—a direct challenge to the coherence axioms of rational choice theory. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p460]
- In Hsee's dictionary experiment, two identical dictionaries (same content, same year) were compared: dictionary A was 'like new' and dictionary B had a torn cover. In single evaluation, A was valued higher (condition matters); in joint evaluation, B was preferred because content quantity (20,000 entries vs. 10,000) dominated the cosmetic condition. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p465]

## Inferences

- Inference: Joint evaluation activates System 2 more strongly, producing more normatively consistent but emotionally detached assessments; single evaluation reflects System 1 responses that are heavily influenced by salience, vividness, and emotional intensity rather than by the most normatively relevant attributes.
