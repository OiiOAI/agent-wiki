# Destructive Deduction

## Summary

Destructive deduction is the mental operation of shattering the correspondence between a domain or concept and its constituent elements. This 'unstructuring' creates disorder and chaos from meaning and order, and is related to the general-to-specific chain of deduction, analysis, and differentiation (as exemplified by differential calculus).

## Key facts

- Destructive deduction involves shattering the correspondence of each domain or concept with its constituent elements—imagining the existence of parts but pretending the domains they were previously associated with do not exist. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- The result of destructive deduction is many particulars swimming in a sea of anarchy, replacing meaning and order with uncertainty and disorder. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- Destructive deduction is related to the idea chain of general-to-specific, deduction, analysis, and differentiation. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
- The differential calculus proceeds from the general-to-specific (from a function to its derivative), illustrating deduction and analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p445]
- Destructive deduction is the crucial step that permits creative induction by separating particulars from their previous domains—without this unstructuring, creation of new structure cannot proceed. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p446]
