# Soil Mineral Depletion

## Summary

Soil mineral depletion refers to the progressive loss of essential minerals from agricultural topsoil, reducing the mineral content of crops and thereby the mineral intake of people who eat them. Factors contributing to depletion include years of monoculture and tilling, the use of NPK-only fertilizers (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium) that omit other essential minerals, the 'dilution effect' from higher-yield crop varieties, climate change increasing atmospheric CO₂, and the use of pesticides and herbicides. Research from 1940 to 2002 documented average losses of 19% in magnesium, 29% in calcium, 37% in iron, and 62% in copper in foods. A 2018 analysis of soil assays from 30% of Canada's arable land found that only magnesium consistently met the lowest acceptable mineral levels, with 12 of 14 minerals below acceptable levels in the majority of samples.

## Key facts

- A 2008 summary of studies from 1940 to 2002 documented average nutrient losses in foods of 19% in magnesium, 29% in calcium, 37% in iron, and 62% in copper. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p92]
- A 2018 analysis of 40 soil assays from fields across the four western Canadian provinces (representing 30% of Canada's arable land) found magnesium was the only mineral meeting the lowest acceptable level in 98% of samples; iron met the lowest level in 52% of samples; the other 12 of 14 minerals fell below acceptable levels in the majority of samples. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p93]
- Most fertilizers contain only NPK (nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium), omitting many other essential minerals that plants absorb from the soil and that humans need from food. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p93]
- Rising atmospheric CO₂ levels reduce the micronutrient density of crops; spring wheat studies showed more than 7% decline in protein and significant decreases in iron and manganese under higher CO₂, alongside increased carbohydrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p98]
- Glyphosate's first patent was as a descaling agent to remove mineral deposits; it interferes with the shikimate pathway in both plants and some gut bacteria, potentially reducing mineral bioavailability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p95]
- Organic and non-organic adjacent fields showed no difference in mineral content in the Canadian soil assay study, suggesting organic status alone does not guarantee mineral density. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p93]
