# Polanyi's Paradox

## Summary

Polanyi's Paradox is an observation by philosopher Michael Polanyi summarized as 'We know more than we can say.' It refers to tacit knowledge—tasks we can easily perform but cannot fully explain, such as driving a car or recognizing a face. The book argues this tacit knowledge represents the final frontier on which humans outperform machines, making it the unique value humans bring to creative work.

## Key facts

- Michael Polanyi observed that there are many tasks we can easily perform as humans that we cannot fully explain, such as driving a car or recognizing a face. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p202]
- This problem of self-ignorance has been a major roadblock in the development of artificial intelligence. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p202]
- The tacit knowledge represented by Polanyi's Paradox is the final frontier on which humans outperform machines—jobs relying on tacit knowledge will be the last to be automated. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p202]
- You know things about how the world works that you cannot fully put into words, and your Second Brain helps pull this tacit knowledge into conscious awareness. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p202]
