# Adaptive Automation

## Summary

Adaptive automation systematically allocates control between human and computer based on factors such as critical events, detection of human performance below criterion, psychophysiological monitoring, or performance models. This approach aims to reduce complacency and improve monitoring by periodically returning control to the human operator. Research shows adaptive automation reduces workload and can improve situation awareness when based on operator physiological engagement.

## Key facts

- Adaptive automation exploits the fact that in most automated systems operators need to assume manual control at times, systematically allocating control at intervals to reduce complacency and improve monitoring. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p203]
- AA can be triggered by: occurrence of critical events, detection of human performance below criterion, psychophysiological monitoring, or use of performance models to predict optimal intervention times. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p203]
- Prinzel et al. developed an operator engagement index based on EEG that automatically adjusted automation level in direct correspondence to operator engagement, significantly improving overall performance. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p204]
- The duration of automated versus manual control cycles contributes most to workload reduction, while level of automation contributes most to changes in situation awareness. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p204]

## Inferences

- Inference: Adaptive automation interfaces need to clearly indicate current mode and upcoming manual control periods to avoid SA challenges associated with mode changes.
