# Cultural Feminism

## Summary

Cultural feminism celebrates women's distinctive ways of being and explores the social value of women's difference from men. It theorizes that women possess distinctive virtues—cooperation, caring, pacifism, nonviolence, an ethic of care—that represent a healthier template for social organization than masculine ways. The most influential version is Carol Gilligan's argument that women operate from an 'ethic of care' (focused on achieving outcomes where all parties' needs are noticed) while men operate from an 'ethic of justice' (focused on protecting equal rights).

## Key facts

- Cultural feminism is less focused on explaining the origins of difference and more on exploring and celebrating the social value of women's distinctive ways of being. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p361]
- First Wave cultural feminists such as Margaret Fuller, Frances Willard, Jane Addams, and Charlotte Perkins Gilman argued that society needed women's virtues of cooperation, caring, pacifism, and nonviolence. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p361]
- Carol Gilligan contrasts the 'ethic of care' (seen as female, focusing on achieving outcomes where all parties feel their needs are noticed and responded to) with the 'ethic of justice' (seen as male, focusing on protecting the equal rights of all parties). [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p362]
- Cultural feminism suggests that women's ways of being and knowing may be a healthier template for producing a just society than are those of an androcentric culture for male ways of knowing and being. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p362]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cultural feminism has allowed cultural feminists to sidestep rather than resolve problems posed by the essentialist thesis, since it celebrates difference without fully confronting whether those differences are immutable.
