# Centenarian Decathlon

## Summary

The Centenarian Decathlon is a framework developed by Peter Attia for organizing physical training goals around the activities one wants to be able to perform in later decades of life. Rather than training for specific athletic events, the goal is to maintain capability across multiple physical domains (strength, endurance, balance, flexibility) to ensure one can perform essential and desired activities well into old age.

## Key facts

- The Centenarian Decathlon is a framework for identifying the physical tasks one wants to be able to perform in their ninth or tenth decade of life. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p238]
- Key events in the Centenarian Decathlon include: hiking 1.5 miles on hilly terrain, getting up off the floor using maximum one arm for support, picking up a young child, carrying groceries, lifting a 20-pound suitcase overhead, balancing on one leg for 30 seconds, climbing four flights of stairs in 3 minutes, opening a jar, and doing 30 consecutive jump-rope skips. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p238-239]
- Because muscle strength declines by 8-17% per decade, to maintain capability at age 80, one must be able to lift approximately 50-55 pounds now to pick up a 30-pound child then. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p240]
- To hike on a hilly trail comfortably in later decades requires a VO2 max of roughly 30 ml/kg/min now. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p239]
- The Centenarian Decathlon emphasizes training broadly (like a decathlete) rather than specializing in one domain. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p242]
- The three dimensions optimized through the Centenarian Decathlon are aerobic endurance/efficiency, strength, and stability. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p244]

## Inferences

- Inference: By setting goals for extremely old age, individuals will naturally train at levels well above average, providing a safety margin against age-related decline.
