# Radial Glial Cell

## Summary

Radial glial cells are elongated neuroepithelial cells that span the developing cerebral wall from ventricular to pial surface. They were traditionally thought to be scaffolding for neuronal migration but are now known to be neural stem cells capable of generating both neurons and astrocytes. Radial glia express Nestin and BLBP and differentiate into astrocytes at later stages. Each radial glial cell can support the migration of up to 30 generations of cortical neurons through its long basal process.

## Key facts

- Radial glial cells have cell bodies in the ventricular zone and processes extending to the pial surface, serving as both neural progenitors and migration scaffolds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1239]
- Radial glial cells undergo asymmetric division generating both neurons and additional radial glial cells, and can generate astrocytes at later stages. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1239]
- A single radial glial cell scaffold can support migration of up to 30 generations of cortical neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1245]
- Notch signaling from newborn neurons promotes radial glial identity through Hes bHLH proteins and neuregulin signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1241]
