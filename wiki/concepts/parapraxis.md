# Psychical Mechanism of Forgetfulness

## Summary

Freud's first published analysis (1898) of what came to be called parapraxes—disturbances in psychical functioning such as forgetting proper names. Through his famous Signorelli analysis, Freud demonstrated that forgetting involves repression: a suppressed thought (about death and sexuality in Bosnia) made the name Signorelli inaccessible and led to substitute names Botticelli and Boltraffio. This same mechanism underlies pathological processes in hysteria, obsessional neurosis, and paranoia.

## Key facts

- In the case of forgetting the name Signorelli, Freud's suppressed story about Bosnian Turks' attitudes toward death and sexuality made the name inaccessible to memory. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1183]
- The substitute names Botticelli and Boltraffio were determined by their assonance with Bosnia and by the place name Trafoi. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1184]
- The mechanism causing forgetting (repression and displacement) is the same as that which produces hysterical symptoms, obsessional thoughts, and paranoic paramnesias. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1186]
- The best procedure for getting hold of a missing name is to divert attention from the task, after which the missing name shoots into the mind. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1181]
- Parapraxes include slips of the tongue, forgetting of names, misreading, and other faulty actions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2006]
- Freud argues that parapraxes are motivated by unconscious processes and are not mere coincidences. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2006]
- Two main factors contribute to parapraxes: the uninhibited stream of associations and the relaxation of inhibitory attention. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2052]
- Parapraxes have a sense, intention, and purpose rather than being random errors. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4833]
- Parapraxes arise from mutual interference between two different intentions: a disturbed intention and a disturbing intention. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4844]
- The disturbing intention must have been 'forced back' or suppressed from conscious expression before it can manifest as a disturbance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4848]
- Parapraxes constitute a half-success and half-failure for each of the two intentions involved. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4849]
- The forgetting of intentions can be traced to an opposing current of thought, which is unwilling to carry out the intention. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4838]

## Inferences

- Inference: Normal parapraxes and pathological neurotic symptoms share the same unconscious mechanism, suggesting a continuum between normal and abnormal psychology.
- Inference: Parapraxes demonstrate that unconscious mental processes can influence conscious behavior without the person's awareness, since the disturbing purpose is often denied or unrecognized by the speaker.
- Inference: The study of parapraxes represents a 'first product of psychoanalysis' that extends psychology's understanding of mental phenomena beyond what was previously recognized.
