# Interoceptive Predictive Processing

## Summary

Interoceptive predictive processing is a theoretical framework proposing that feelings and emotions result from the brain integrating basic information about bodily states and general arousal with higher-level predictions of their most probable causes. Rather than emotions having distinct physiological signatures, they are constructed moment-by-moment from a mixture of cultural influences, situational evidence and expectations, and individual bodily states.

## Key facts

- According to interoceptive predictive processing, feelings and emotions are what result when we integrate basic information about bodily states and general arousal with higher-level predictions of their most probable causes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p99]
- No such simple analogs exist as distinct bodily fingerprints for different emotional states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p98]
- A single kind of process combines inner and outer sources of information, generating a context-reflecting amalgam that is experienced as emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p99]
- The very same bodily information can feel very different according to how we represent the larger context in which the bodily signals arise. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: The anterior insular cortex (AIC) plays a major role in this process due to its dense connectivity allowing integration of multiple interoceptive information sources [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p99]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact neuronal mechanisms by which interoceptive and exteroceptive predictions are combined requires further research
