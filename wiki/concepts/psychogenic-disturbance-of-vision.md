# Psychogenic Disturbance of Vision

## Summary

In this paper (1910), Freud presents his psychoanalytic theory of psychogenic visual disturbances, building on earlier work on hysteria. He argues that these disturbances result from repression of the sexual component drive of scopophilia (pleasure in looking). When the eye serves dual functions—both for self-preservation (practical seeing) and sexuality (erotic looking)—repression of the sexual drive can cause general disturbance of vision. The paper connects to broader psychoanalytic theory regarding the conflict between ego drives and sexual drives, and includes discussion of the role of somatic compliance in predisposition to neurosis.

## Key facts

- Freud states that appropriate experiments have shown that people who are hysterically blind 'do nevertheless see in some sense, though not in the full sense,' compelling the distinction between conscious and unconscious mental processes. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3648]
- Freud explains that psychoanalysis traces mental life to 'an interplay between forces that favour or inhibit one another,' with repression being 'analogous to a condemnatory judgement in the field of logic.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3649]
- The paper introduces Freud's view that 'the ego feels threatened by the claims of the sexual drives and fends them off by repressions; these, however, do not always have the desired result, but lead to the formation of dangerous substitutes for the repressed and to burdensome reactions on the part of the ego.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3650]
- Freud argues that the eye serves dual functions—perceiving alterations important for self-preservation and perceiving charms that lead to objects being chosen as love objects. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3650]
- The paper cites Schiller: 'all the organic drives that operate in our mind may be classified as hunger or love.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3650]
- Freud notes that civilization 'originates mainly at the cost of the sexual component drives, and that these must be suppressed, restricted, transformed and directed to higher aims.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3650]
- The paper includes a letter from Freud to Édouard Claparède clarifying that 'libido' means only the energy of the sexual drives, not the equivalent of all psychical driving force—Jung, not Freud, held the broader view. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3649]

## Inferences
