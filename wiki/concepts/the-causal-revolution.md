# The Causal Revolution

## Summary

The Causal Revolution refers to the transformation of causality from a concept shrouded in mystery into a mathematical object with well-defined semantics and well-founded logic, beginning roughly in the 1980s. It has impacted virtually every data-informed scientific discipline.

## Key facts

- Pearl characterizes the development of causal inference as a 'revolution' that has changed thinking across many sciences. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L25]
- The Causal Revolution turned the scientific neglect of causal questions into a thing of the past. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L43]
- Questions about causation like 'Did X cause Y?' were considered unscientific in reputable journals as recently as two decades ago. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L52]
- The calculus of causation consists of two languages: causal diagrams and a symbolic algebra for queries. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L75]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Causal Revolution was stalled for over half a century after Galton and Pearson's failure to address causal questions, during which time causal vocabulary was 'virtually prohibited' in statistics.
