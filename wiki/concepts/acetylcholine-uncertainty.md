# Acetylcholine and Uncertainty

## Summary

Acetylcholine (ACh) signals expected uncertainty corresponding to known variability or stochasticity in the environment. ACh enhances thalamocortical synaptic transmission while suppressing intracortical transmission, promoting bottom-up sensory-driven processing. In sustained attention tasks, ACh depletion causes over-confidence in 'rarity' priors (decreased hits), while ACh elevation causes under-valuation of rarity priors (increased false alarms).

## Key facts

- ACh signals expected uncertainty corresponding to known variability in the behavioral context, while norepinephrine signals unexpected uncertainty due to drastic environmental changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p139]
- ACh selectively enhances thalamocortical synapses via presynaptic nicotinic receptors and strongly suppresses intracortical synaptic transmission through postsynaptic muscarinic receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p143]
- In sustained attention tasks, abnormally low ACh leads to selective increase in error rate on signal trials (decreased hits), while abnormally high ACh leads to increased error rate on no-signal trials (increased false alarms). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p146]
- ACh binds to two major receptor classes: nicotinic (ionotropic, fast-acting) and muscarinic (metabotropic, diverse and sustained effects). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p140]
- The basal forebrain cholinergic system projects to cortex via nucleus basalis magnocellularis (NBM), with additional projections from medial septum to hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p141]
