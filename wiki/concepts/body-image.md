# Body Image

## Summary

Body image is a mental construct produced by brain maps that projects sensation onto the body. It is distinct from the physical body itself and can be distorted or modified through neuroplasticity. Ramachandran demonstrated that body image is surprisingly malleable, and that we can experience artificial objects as part of our bodies through synchronized sensory input.

## Key facts

- We always experience pain as projected into the body, but as phantoms show, we don't need a body part or even pain receptors to feel pain—we need only a body image. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p194]
- Ramachandran stated: 'Your own body is a phantom, one that your brain has constructed purely for convenience.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p194]
- Anorexics experience their bodies as fat when they are on the edge of starvation, demonstrating a distorted body image. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p195]
- Body dysmorphic disorder causes people to experience body parts that are within normal range as defective, leading to shame and often unsuccessful plastic surgery. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p195]
- In a rubber hand illusion experiment, Ramachandran demonstrated that synchronized stroking of a fake hand and hidden real hand causes the dummy hand to become part of the subject's body image. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p195]
- Ramachandran created an illusion where a subject's sensory body image expanded to include a tabletop after simultaneous tapping of the tabletop and hidden hand in identical rhythm. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p196]
- When Ramachandran bashed a tabletop that had become part of a subject's body image, the subject's stress response went through the roof, as measured by galvanic skin response. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p196]

## Inferences

- Inference: Body image modifications like those in phantom limbs, anorexia, and body dysmorphic disorder suggest that 'neuroplastic surgery' to change body image might be more effective than physical surgery for certain conditions.
