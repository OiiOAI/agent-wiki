# fMRI Neuroeconomics

## Summary

fMRI neuroeconomics combines functional magnetic resonance imaging with economic decision-making tasks to identify brain regions involved in value computation, reward processing, and strategic interaction. The technique measures blood-oxygen-level-dependent (BOLD) responses correlated with neural activity during economic choices.

## Key facts

- An fMRI neuroeconomics experiment correlates brain activity with economic decision making by measuring cerebral blood flow, which is correlated with underlying neuronal activations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p57]
- Neuroeconomic experiments typically require multiple plays of the same game and averaging of signals because the BOLD signal is weak with typical responses being just a few percentage points from baseline. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p57]
- McCabe et al. (2001) conducted the first published imaging study of economic exchange using trust game variants. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: MRI provides non-invasive access to brain function during economic decision-making, complementing invasive neurophysiology in non-human primates.
