# Affect

## Summary

Affect refers to hedonically valenced feelings—states that feel 'good' or 'bad'—which distinguish affective states from other mental states like perception. Affects are the subjective manifestation of drives, telling us how well or badly things are going in relation to our biological needs. They arise from error signals indicating deviations from biologically preferred states, and their primary function is to guide voluntary behavior in conditions of uncertainty. Affects are always conscious; an unconscious feeling is a contradiction in terms.

## Key facts

- Affects are hedonically valenced—they feel 'good' or 'bad'—which distinguishes them from sensory states like vision and hearing. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p93]
- Valence reflects the biological value system: it is 'good' to survive and reproduce, 'bad' not to do so. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p94]
- Feelings make creatures do something necessary; they are measures of demands for work. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p94]
- Freud defined 'drive' as 'a measure of the demand made upon the mind for work in consequence of its connection with the body'—affects are their subjective manifestation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p95]
- Affects convey which biological things are going well or badly for us, and arouse us to do something about them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p95]
- Feelings are always conscious; a feeling of which you are unconscious is not a feeling. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p96]
- Felt needs are prioritised over unfelt ones because you can only do one thing at a time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p97]
- Voluntary behavior must be conscious and grounded in a value system—the thing that determines 'goodness' versus 'badness'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p97]
- The deepest biological function of feeling is to guide our behavior in conditions of uncertainty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p97]
