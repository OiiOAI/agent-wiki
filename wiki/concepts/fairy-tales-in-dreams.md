# Fairy Tales in Dreams

## Summary

Freud's analysis of how fairy tale elements appear in dreams, with patients producing significant fairy tales as associations. The 'Rumpelstiltskin' and wolf/goat fairy tales were connected to patients' unconscious wishes and fears.

## Key facts

- Elements and situations derived from fairy tales are frequently found in dreams, and in interpreting them the patient will produce the significant fairy tale as an association. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3999]
- In one case, a manikin's description fitted the dreamer's father-in-law, who then led to the story of 'Rumpelstiltskin' who danced around and lost his claim to the queen's first child. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4000]
- The 'Wolf Man' had a dream of white wolves on walnut trees derived from fairy tale pictures, producing an animal phobia of wolves. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4002]
- The wolf fairy tales (Little Red Riding-Hood and The Wolf and the Seven Little Goats) are connected to the castration complex and infantile fear of the father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4004]
