# Misplaced Salience

## Summary

Misplaced salience occurs when less important information is made to look more important through highly salient cues (flashing lights, movement, bright colors), distracting from more important information. People will generally attempt to seek out information relevant to their goals, but attention is also caught by highly salient information. This creates the 'Las Vegas Strip phenomenon'—with so much competing information drawing attention, it is difficult to process any of it well.

## Key facts

- Salience is the compellingness of certain forms of information, largely determined by physical characteristics like red color, movement, flashing lights, loud noises, larger shapes, and near objects. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p56]
- The 'Las Vegas Strip phenomenon' occurs when too much information draws attention, making it difficult to process any of it well. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p57]
- False alarms create unnecessary distractions that can act to degrade SA of other information the person is attempting to attend to. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p57]
- Misplaced salience is entirely in the hands of the designer in engineered systems but is often overused or used inappropriately. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p58]
