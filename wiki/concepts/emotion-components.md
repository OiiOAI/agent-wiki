# Emotion (Components)

## Summary

Emotion is a broad term encompassing a range of processes including subjective experience (feelings), expression (facial, vocal, bodily), bodily responses (autonomic, hormonal, neuromodulatory), action tendencies, and evaluation/appraisal of events. Unlike mood, which is diffuse and low-intensity, emotions are characterized as synchronized, discrete responses to events. Emotions can be categorized by basic types (Ekman's six basic emotions) or by dimensions (valence and arousal in the Circumplex model).

## Key facts

- Emotion includes multiple synchronized components: subjective experience (feelings), expression (facial, vocal, bodily), bodily responses (autonomic, hormonal), action tendencies, and evaluation/appraisal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p234]
- Feelings are the consciously accessible subjective experience of emotion, but are neither necessary nor sufficient for emotion to occur; emotion can occur without feeling and feeling can occur without full emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p234]
- Mood differs from emotion: mood is a diffuse, relatively enduring, low-intensity state characterized primarily by subjective feelings; emotions are discrete responses to specific events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p236]
- The Circumplex model characterizes emotion along two dimensions: arousal (activation-deactivation) and valence (pleasant-unpleasant). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p238]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between emotion components suggests that neuroimaging studies claiming to identify 'emotion' from BOLD responses may lack specificity unless they measure multiple components
- Inference: Preference shares features with emotion (subjective affective state, action tendencies) but differs in that emotions also have expression and bodily response features and are more time-limited
