# Epigenetic inheritance

## Summary

Epigenetic inheritance refers to the inheritance of nongenetic structures within the cell through mechanisms other than DNA replication. Three types have been distinguished: steady-state inheritance (self-regulation of gene expression), structural inheritance (3D structure serving as template), and chromatin-marking inheritance (methylation patterns on DNA). Epigenetic inheritance undermines the equation of biological identity through time with the transgenerational bridge of DNA.

## Key facts

- Steady-state inheritance is based on self-regulation of gene expression within the cell, where a gene produces a product facilitating its own continued activity, with sufficient quantity transmitted to daughter cells during division. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p174]
- Structural inheritance involves a three-dimensional structure serving as a template for identical structures in daughter cells; variations in cytoskeletal and cortical organization can be inherited through cell division. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p174]
- Chromatin-marking inheritance is based on inheritance of methylation patterns on DNA; a particular DNA sequence can have several different heritable methylation patterns imposed on it, replicated by a special methylation replication system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p174]
- Organisms inherit not simply a genome but an entire developmental matrix: organisms structure the environment of their progeny, so environmental conditions are passed on inseparably with genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p175]

## Inferences

- Inference: Symbiosis provides another example that not all inheritance is a function of gene lineages: hereditary symbioses involve transmission of whole functioning populations of symbionts in addition to symbiont DNA.
