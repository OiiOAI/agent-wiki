# Premortems

## Summary

A premortem is a decision-making technique, introduced by Gary Klein, that involves imagining a project or decision has failed catastrophically and then working backward to determine what could have caused that failure. Unlike a postmortem (conducted after failure), the premortem is performed prospectively before a decision is finalized, allowing teams to identify and mitigate potential failure modes. Duke discusses how premortems counter overconfidence and groupthink by legitimizing the expression of doubts and concerns. The technique pairs well with backcasting (imagining success) by providing comprehensive coverage of both positive and negative future scenarios.

## Key facts

- Premortems were introduced by Gary Klein as a method where teams imagine a project has already failed and identify potential causes. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2095-L2105]
- The premortem is conducted before a decision is finalized, unlike a postmortem which analyzes failures after they occur. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2095-L2105]
- Premortems help counter overconfidence and groupthink by legitimizing the expression of doubts. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2095-L2105]
- Gabriele Oettingen's WOOP method (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) provides a structured approach to implementing mental contrasting for premortem-like analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L2230-L2240]
