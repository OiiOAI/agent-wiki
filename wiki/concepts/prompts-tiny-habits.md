# Prompts (Tiny Habits)

## Summary

Prompts are the invisible drivers of behavior in the Tiny Habits method—the cues that remind us to act. They are the third element of the Fogg Behavior Model (B=MAP), and unlike motivation and ability which exist on a spectrum, prompts are binary: you either notice them or you don't. The book identifies three types of prompts: Person Prompts, Context Prompts, and Action Prompts (Anchors), with Action Prompts being the most effective for habit formation.

## Key facts

- No behavior happens without a prompt. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p117]
- There are three types of prompts: Person Prompts, Context Prompts, and Action Prompts. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p122]
- Person Prompts rely on something inside you to do a behavior, such as bodily urges or memory, but are unreliable for habits. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p123]
- Context Prompts are environmental cues like sticky notes, app notifications, or colleagues reminding you to act. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p124]
- Action Prompts (Anchors) are behaviors you already do that can remind you to do a new habit—the most effective prompt type. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p127]
- When too many Context Prompts exist, people become desensitized and fail to heed them, similar to living next to train tracks. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p125]
- Finding the right Anchor requires matching physical location, frequency, and theme/purpose with the new habit. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p132]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book predicts that Context Prompts will become less effective over time as people become increasingly desensitized and better at filtering them out.
- Inference: Businesses that help customers create Action Prompts will have a competitive advantage over those relying on Context Prompts.
