# 2D:4D Digit Ratio

## Summary

The ratio of the second finger to the fourth finger length, which is influenced by prenatal testosterone exposure. While the second finger is usually shorter than the fourth, this difference is greater in men than women. The ratio is demonstrable in third-trimester fetuses and correlates with fetal testosterone exposure, serving as a marker for prenatal androgen levels.

## Key facts

- The 2D:4D ratio is greater in men than women, first noted in the 1880s and demonstrable in third-trimester fetuses. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p226]
- The more fetal testosterone exposure (as assessed by amniocentesis), the more pronounced the masculine ratio. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p226]
- Men with more masculine 2D:4D ratios tend toward higher aggression, math scores, ADHD, and autism, and decreased risk of depression and anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p227]
- The sex difference in the ratio occurs in other primates and rodents, and no one knows why this difference exists. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p226]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 2D:4D ratio constitutes some of the strongest evidence that individual differences in fetal androgen exposure predict individual differences in adult behavior.
- Inference: These effects are small and variable, producing meaningful relationships only with large numbers of individuals.
