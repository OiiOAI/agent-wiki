# Mediation

## Summary

Mediation analysis investigates whether the effect of a cause on an outcome operates directly or indirectly through intermediate variables (mediators). For example, if drug D affects lifespan L only through its effect on blood pressure Z, then Z is a mediator and the effect of D on L is indirect. Understanding mediation pathways has both scientific interest—revealing causal mechanisms—and practical applications, such as developing alternative drugs that achieve the same effect by targeting the mediator more cheaply or with fewer side effects. Modern causal inference reduces mediation analysis to an algebraic exercise using the do-calculus.

## Key facts

- Mediation addresses whether an effect is direct or indirect through an intermediate variable. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L258]
- A mediator is an intermediate variable through which a cause affects an outcome. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L258]
- The blood pressure example illustrates mediation: drug D → blood pressure Z (mediator) → lifespan L. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L258]
