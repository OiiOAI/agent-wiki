# Fausses reconnaissances

## Summary

A psychoanalytic concept describing the phenomenon where a patient insists they have already told the analyst something, when in fact they have only intended to tell it. Freud traced this to an unconscious resolution that was never carried out, with the recollection of intention being confused with recollection of performance.

## Key facts

- Fausses reconnaissances ('déjà raconté') occur when patients claim to have already told the analyst something they have only intended to tell. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4304]
- The explanation for this phenomenon is that the patient really had an intention of giving the information, made remarks leading up to it, but was prevented by resistance from carrying it out, and afterwards confused recollection of intention with recollection of performance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4304]
- Freud reported the case of a patient who had an hallucinatory childhood memory of cutting off his little finger, which he claimed to have already told Freud, but had actually only related a screen memory about a pocket-knife brought by his uncle. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4306]

## Inferences

- Inference: Fausses reconnaissances reveal the operation of repression, where the patient's resistance prevents direct communication of significant material, causing them to mistake preparatory attempts for completed actions.
- Inference: The phenomenon demonstrates how unconscious intentions can be confused with conscious memories, supporting Freud's broader claim about the unreliability of ordinary memory.
