# Triangulation

## Summary

Triangulation is a boundary problem where Person A is angry at Person B but tells Person C instead, creating false relationships and preventing resolution. The authors cite biblical passages commanding direct confrontation with those who have offended.

## Key facts

- Triangulation occurs when Person A is angry at Person B but does not tell Person B; instead, Person A calls Person C to complain about Person B. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p135]
- The result is that Person C becomes the confidant of both A and B, who have not resolved their conflict, leaving C with two compromised 'friendships.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p135]
- The authors state: 'This is a clear lack of boundaries because Person A is not owning his anger. The person with whom A is angry deserves to hear it straight from him.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p136]
- The authors cite Proverbs 28:23: 'He who rebukes a man will in the end gain more favor than he who has a flattering tongue.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p136]
- Matthew 5:23-24 is cited: 'If you are offering your gift at the altar and there remember that your brother has something against you, leave your gift there... First go and be reconciled to your brother.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p136]
- The authors provide a simple rule: 'Never say to a third party something about someone that you do not plan to say to the person himself.'. [raw/books/business/Boundaries - Henry Cloud & John Townsend.pdf#p137]

## Inferences

- Inference: Triangulation is identified as a primary boundary problem in dysfunctional families of origin.
