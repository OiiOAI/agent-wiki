# Don't Be a Hero

## Summary

The concept advises against heroics and extended effort on tasks that are taking far longer than expected. The authors argue that when a task worth two hours takes four hours and is only a quarter done, continuing wastes time that could be spent on other things. They recommend quitting when the cost exceeds the benefit, and instituted a rule at 37signals that any task taking more than two weeks requires bringing in other people to review it. Walking away from sunk costs is presented as rational rather than failure.

## Key facts

- The authors advise quitting when a task takes far longer than expected rather than continuing into 'hero mode' to justify the time already spent. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p91]
- 37signals has a rule that if anything takes longer than two weeks, other people must be brought in to review and give feedback. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p91]
- The authors argue that walking away from sunk costs is often the rational choice because the time already spent cannot be recovered. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p91]
- Continuing to work on something not worth it cuts off feedback that could reveal an obvious solution or reveal the work should be abandoned. [raw/books/business/Rework - Jason Fried.pdf#p91]
