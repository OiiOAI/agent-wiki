# Dramaturgical Analysis

## Summary

Dramaturgical analysis is a variant of symbolic interactionism developed by Erving Goffman that compares social interaction to theatrical performance. Goffman saw commonalities between theatrical performances and the "acts" people put on in daily life. Interaction is seen as fragile, maintained by social performances, with poor performances or disruptions being great threats. Key concepts include the "front region" (where actors are concerned with appearances, costumes, and props) and the "back region" (where actors can retire to prepare and shed their roles). The theory is consistent with symbolic interactionism's focus on actors, action, and interaction, using the theater metaphor to shed new light on small-scale social processes.
