# Mediation in NVC

## Summary

NVC mediation focuses on creating connection between conflicting parties rather than analyzing issues or proposing solutions. The mediator's role is to help parties express needs, hear each other, and arrive at strategies to meet those needs, avoiding taking sides or accomplishing personal goals.

## Key facts

- The mediator's objective is not to get the parties to do what we want them to do. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p236]
- When expressing empathy toward one side, the other side may accuse the mediator of favoritism; this calls for emergency first-aid empathy. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p237]
- Keep the conversation in the present; conflict resolution can only happen right now. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p239]
- Use role-play to speed up the mediation process when parties tell the same stories repeatedly. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p240]
- Role-play is simply putting ourselves in the other person's shoes; nobody gets it right all the time, and that's fine. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p241]

## Inferences

- Inference: The mediator needs to 'keep score' by paying attention to what has been said, using visual tracking to note needs and reassure parties that their needs will be addressed.
