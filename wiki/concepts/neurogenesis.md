# Neurogenesis

## Summary

The process of stem cells dividing and developing into functional new brain cells (neurons) in the adult brain, firmly established in 1998 and believed to be confined to part of the hippocampus and the subventricular zone associated with smell.

## Key facts

- Neurogenesis is the process of stem cells dividing and developing into functional new brain cells, or neurons, in the brain. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p224]
- That this happens in the adult human was firmly established in 1998. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p224]
- Neurogenesis is believed to be confined to part of the hippocampus and another brain area called the subventricular zone, which is associated with the sense of smell. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p224]
- Neurogenesis occurs in certain regions of the adult mammalian brain, contrary to the previous dogma that new neurons cannot form in adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Evidence for adult neurogenesis has accelerated research on stimulating neurogenesis and replacing neurons following injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Therapeutic interventions may retain or replace injured central neurons through stimulation of neurogenesis in regions of injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Neurogenesis is the process of creating new neurons from neuronal stem cells and continues until the day we die. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p253]
- Neuronal stem cells were first discovered in rats by Joseph Altman and Gopal D. Das in 1965, but their work was disbelieved at the time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- Fernando Nottebohm discovered that songbirds grow new brain cells each season in the area responsible for song learning, inspiring research in mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- Elizabeth Gould was the first to discover neuronal stem cells in primates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- Eriksson and Gage used BrdU marker staining to show that living neurons form in humans until the very end of life, finding new baby neurons in the hippocampi of terminally ill patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- Neuronal stem cells have also been found active in the olfactory bulb and dormant in the septum, striatum, and spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p254]
- Mice raised in enriched environments for 45 days showed a 15% increase in hippocampal volume and 40,000 new neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p255]
- Mice raised in enriched environments for 10 months in the second half of their lives showed a fivefold increase in hippocampal neurons and performed better on learning and intelligence tests. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p255]
- Running on a wheel doubled the number of new neurons in the hippocampus of mice after one month. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p255]
- Physical exercise creates new neurons, while learning extends the life of existing new neurons—these work in complementary ways. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p256]
- Exercise stimulates the production and release of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor), which plays a crucial role in plastic change. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p258]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery that neurogenesis occurs in the adult brain suggests that endogenous repair mechanisms could potentially be harnessed for therapeutic purposes.
- Inference: Neurogenesis appears to be triggered by novel environments and new learning, suggesting that exposure to new experiences may be essential for maintaining brain health through neuron production.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The functional significance and therapeutic potential of adult neurogenesis in humans requires further investigation.
- Uncertain: The search continues for neuronal stem cells in other parts of the human brain beyond the hippocampus, olfactory bulb, septum, striatum, and spinal cord.
