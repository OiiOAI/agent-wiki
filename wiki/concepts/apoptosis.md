# Apoptosis in Neurons

## Summary

Apoptosis is a genetically programmed form of cell death in which neurons round up, form blebs, condense chromatin, and fragment their nuclei. Neurotrophic factors suppress this latent death program present in all neurons. The apoptotic pathway is conserved from invertebrates to humans and involves caspases and Bcl-2 family proteins.

## Key facts

- Apoptosis (Greek, 'falling away') involves cells rounding up, forming blebs, condensing chromatin, and fragmenting their nuclei—distinct from necrosis which results from acute traumatic injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1151]
- The cell death pathway was first elucidated in C. elegans, where ced-9 antagonizes ced-3 and ced-4 to regulate cell survival versus death. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1151]
- Caspases are cysteine proteases that cleave specific aspartic acid residues, with initiator caspases (8, 9, 10) activating effector caspases (3, 7) which cleave ~1% of cellular proteins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1152]
- The survival of mammalian neurons depends on the balance between antiapoptotic (Bcl-2) and proapoptotic (BAX, BAK) members of the Bcl-2 family. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1152]
