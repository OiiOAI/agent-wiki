# Contagion Bias

## Summary

Contagion bias describes how we are incapable of ignoring connections we feel to certain items, believing intangible forces can transfer. Most people would not wear a freshly laundered sweater Hitler once wore. Paul Rozin's research showed people hesitated to throw darts at photos of loved ones.

## Key facts

- Paul Rozin found that subjects who had to shoot darts at photos of loved ones were much less accurate than control groups, behaving as if a mystic force prevented them from hitting the photos. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p126]
- The prospect of wearing a sweater Hitler once wore puts people off even though there is not a single molecule of Hitler's sweat on it after dry cleaning. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p125]
