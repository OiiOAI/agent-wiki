# The Subjectivity-Objectivity Dilemma

## Summary

The English faculty at Montana State College presented Phædrus with a dilemma: Does Quality exist in the things we observe (objective), or is it subjective, existing only in the observer? If Quality is objective, scientific instruments should detect it. If subjective, it is merely 'whatever you like.' Phædrus identified three classic logical refutations: taking the left horn, taking the right horn, or going between the horns by denying these are the only choices.

## Key facts

- The faculty asked whether Quality exists in observed objects or only in the observer—Phædrus recognized this as an ancient logical construct known as a dilemma. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p194]
- Phædrus recognized that every dilemma affords not two but three classic refutations: take the left horn, take the right horn, or go between the horns. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p195]
- Phædrus refuted scientific materialism by showing that concepts like the number zero cannot be derived from mass-energy, yet digital computers function exclusively in terms of ones and zeros. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p199]
- Phædrus removed the word 'just' from the statement 'Quality is just what you like,' showing it was a purely pejorative term whose logical contribution was nil. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p197]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dilemma was designed as a 'finisher-offer' that would impale Phædrus no matter how he answered.
