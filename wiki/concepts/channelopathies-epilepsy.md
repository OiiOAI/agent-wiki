# Channelopathies in Epilepsy

## Summary

Channelopathies are diseases caused by mutations in ion channel genes that affect neuronal excitability. In epilepsy, mutations in voltage-gated sodium, potassium, calcium, and chloride channels, as well as ligand-gated channels like GABAA receptors and nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, have been identified as causes of monogenic epilepsies. These mutations affect channel gating, expression, or trafficking.

## Key facts

- More than 120 genes have been linked to an epileptic phenotype, approximately half discovered in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Voltage-gated Na+ channel mutations (NaV1.1, NaV1.2) are associated with GEFS+ and severe myoclonic epilepsy of infancy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- GABAA receptor mutations in α1, β3, and γ2 subunits are linked to GEFS+, juvenile myoclonic epilepsy, and childhood absence epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Mutations in voltage-gated Ca2+ channels (CaV2.1/P/Q-type) cause absence epilepsy, as demonstrated in totterer mice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The first autosomal dominant human epilepsy gene identified was the α4-subunit of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor causing autosomal dominant nocturnal frontal lobe epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]

## Inferences

- Inference: The identification of channelopathies as major causes of monogenic epilepsies supports the fundamental role of ion channel function in controlling neuronal excitability and seizure susceptibility.
