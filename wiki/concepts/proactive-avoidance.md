# Proactive Avoidance

## Summary

Proactive avoidance refers to behaviors and thoughts that directly engage with anxiety triggers to change their impact and enable the organism to exert control over them. Unlike maladaptive passive avoidance, proactive avoidance involves active coping strategies—like relaxation, escape to regroup, or controlled reexposure—that reinforce successful regulation of defensive responses. Research in rats showed that active behavioral control over threat cues prevents spontaneous recovery and reinstatement of fear, making it more effective than passive extinction alone.

## Key facts

- Active behavioral engagement and control appear more effective than extinction in preventing the return of defensive reactions to threat cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p428]
- Resilient individuals tend to have a large repertoire of active coping options and are adept at choosing appropriate ones for specific contexts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p428]

## Inferences

- Inference: Proactive avoidance may work by preventing the CS from driving the lateral amygdala to central amygdala pathway, instead directing outputs to the basal amygdala and nucleus accumbens where the CS functions as a negative reinforcer.
