# The Looking-Glass Self

## Summary

The looking-glass self is a concept developed by Charles Horton Cooley describing how people possess consciousness and how it is shaped in continuing social interaction. The concept posits that individuals develop their sense of self through seeing themselves through the eyes of others—imagining how they appear to others, imagining their judgment of that appearance, and developing their self-feeling based on this imagined judgment. This concept is central to Cooley's social-psychological interests and aligns with the symbolic interactionist tradition that emerged from the Chicago school.
