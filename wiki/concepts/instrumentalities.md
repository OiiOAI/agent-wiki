# Instrumentalities

## Summary

Instrumentalities are the linkages between behavior and desired outcomes that are necessary for motivation. Without appropriate instrumentalities—meaningful contingencies between behavior and outcomes—there will not be productive, motivated behavior. Deci contrasts the American system's instrumentalities (efficiency-focused, with extrinsic rewards linked to performance) with the Bulgarian system's lack of such linkages, which resulted in widespread lethargy. However, instrumentalities are a double-edged sword: they facilitate motivation but are also the means through which control exerts its negative effects.

## Key facts

- Instrumentalities are the linkages between behavior and desired outcomes that are necessary for motivation; people must see a relationship between their behavior and desired outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p59]
- In Bulgaria, there were no instrumentalities—no meaningful contingencies between behavior and outcomes—resulting in widespread lethargy and minimal productive behavior. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p58]
- Instrumentalities are a double-edged sword: they are the basis for facilitating motivation, but also the means through which control can have profoundly negative effects. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p59]
- Control uses instrumentalities to pressure people to behave in particular ways, and extrinsic-reward contingencies are what allow control to work as a strategy for motivating productivity. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p60]
- For extrinsic motivation to work, there must be clarity about what behaviors are expected and what outcomes will result from those behaviors. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p63]
