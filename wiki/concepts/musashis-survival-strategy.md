# Musashi's Survival Strategy

## Summary

Miyamoto Musashi's famous claim to have won 60 consecutive duels without defeat is reframed as a strategy to eliminate chance entirely rather than simply maximize win probability. If his odds of winning any single fight were 97%, the mathematical probability of surviving all 60 fights would only be 16%. Therefore, Musashi's strategy was not about increasing the odds of victory but about arranging conditions so that survival was virtually guaranteed—a "sure thing" that left nothing to chance.

## Key facts

- Musashi's claimed 97% probability of winning any individual fight would result in only 16% chance of surviving 60 fights if relying on luck. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p150]
- Musashi's strategy was framed not in terms of increasing odds, but of arranging a sure thing that eliminated chance. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p150]
