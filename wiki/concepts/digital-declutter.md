# Digital Declutter

## Summary

Digital declutter is the process of organizing and reducing digital clutter in one's digital life, including computers, phones, files, and internet usage. Since 2013, total digital media usage in the world has increased by 40%. The process involves cleaning up desktop screens, organizing files, reducing apps, managing social media accounts, and decluttering email inboxes. Digital minimalism requires ongoing periodic attention as digital entropy naturally causes clutter to accumulate over time.

- Since 2013, the total digital media usage in the world has increased by 40%. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p42]
- The average person spends at least three hours with their phone or mobile device each day. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p42]
- For every five minutes that people spend each day, one minute is spent on social media. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p42]
- For every two minutes that people spend nowadays, one minute is used for online entertainment including watching videos, music, and games. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p42]
- Total smartphone usage in the last three years has doubled. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p42]
- Average adults check their emails around 45 times each day. [raw/books/productivity/Digital Minimalism in Everyday Life - Williams, James W.pdf#p61]
