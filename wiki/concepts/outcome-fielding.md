# Outcome fielding

## Summary

The process of determining whether outcomes belong in the 'skill bucket' (influenced by our decisions) or the 'luck bucket' (outside our control). This initial sorting decision is itself a bet with significant consequences for learning. Fielding outcomes accurately is essential for learning from experience, but humans predictably err in systematic ways.

## Key facts

- An outcome belongs in the skill bucket if making the same decision would predictably result in the same outcome, or if changing the decision would predictably result in a different outcome. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L860-L867]
- When Nick the Greek won with 7-2, he fielded the outcome into the skill bucket; when he lost (more frequently), he wrote it off as bad luck, a fielding error that prevented him from ever questioning his beliefs. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L878-L884]
- Outcomes are rarely all skill or all luck; even egregious mistakes can be affected by luck, and doing everything right can still result in negative outcomes due to luck. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L888-L897]
- Treating outcome fielding as a bet forces us to consider alternative causes more seriously and examine our beliefs more closely. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1190-L1200]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ability to field outcomes accurately is what separates those who learn from experience from those who don't, even when both have identical amounts of experience. This is why experience is necessary but not sufficient for expertise.
