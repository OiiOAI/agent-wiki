# Micromapping

## Summary

Micromapping is a technique using microelectrodes so small they can be inserted inside or beside a single neuron to detect when it fires. Developed by Vernon Mountcastle in the 1950s, it allowed neuroscientists to 'listen in' on individual neurons rather than thousands at once. Merzenich used this technique to map brain regions with unprecedented precision, discovering plasticity in adult brains.

## Key facts

- Microelectrodes are so small they can be inserted inside or beside a single neuron and detect when an individual neuron fires its electrical signal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p59]
- Micromapping is about a thousand times more precise than current brain scans, which detect bursts lasting one second in thousands of neurons, while neuronal signals last a thousandth of a second. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p59]
- Merzenich mapped the hand area of monkey brains by making 500 microelectrode insertions over several days. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p59]
- The technique requires extremely tedious surgery under a microscope with microsurgical instruments, which is why it hasn't replaced brain scans despite its precision. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p59]
