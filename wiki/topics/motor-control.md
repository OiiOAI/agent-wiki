# Motor Control

## Summary

Motor control encompasses the neural mechanisms by which the brain generates and coordinates movement. Llinás argues that continuous-through-time control of movement combined with simultaneous independent control of individual muscles leads to physiologically untenable functional overhead. The solution involves discontinuous pulsatile control operating at 8-12 Hz, controlling muscle collectives rather than individual muscles, and using an over-complete pool of functional synergies. The inferior olive functions as a timing mechanism for this system.

## Key facts

- Continuous-through-time control of movement combined with simultaneous independent control of individual muscles leads to physiologically untenable functional overhead for the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p55]
- The execution of rapid voluntary movements consists of two components: a feedforward, ballistic approximation of the movement's endpoint, followed by feedback fine-tuning using sensory cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p42]
- The olivocerebellar system is proposed as the prime candidate for a neural assembly capable of optimizing and simplifying motor control—temporally pulsatile and rapidly, dynamically self-reorganizing in the spatial domain. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p55]
