# Neural Networks

## Summary

Neural networks are computational models composed of interconnected neurons that process information through both sequential and parallel computation. This chunk covers two major types: perceptrons for visual processing and associative memory networks for memory storage. Neural network models explore how intelligence emerges from interactions between neurons and how learning occurs through synaptic modification.

## Key facts

- Neural network models share two principles: immediate experience is rooted in patterns of action potentials, and learning is based on long-lasting modifications of synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1633]
- Perceptrons lack synaptic loops and are feedforward networks; cell assemblies contain loops and are recurrent networks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1634]
- Networks with loops are called recurrent or feedback networks; feedforward networks have no synaptic loops. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1634]
- Balanced networks of excitatory and inhibitory neurons can generate the irregular, asynchronous activity seen in vivo. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1659]
- Inhibition-stabilized networks have excitatory recurrence strong enough to be unstable by itself but stabilized by feedback inhibition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1662]
- Neural networks are mathematical models for understanding brain function that include attractor networks, Hopfield-Tank networks, perceptrons, and associative memory networks based on Hebbian plasticity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1721]
- Neurons function as computational devices performing Boolean logical function computing, with McCulloch-Pitts models and perceptrons computing conjunctions and disjunctions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1721]
- Recurrent networks can behave like feedforward networks with balanced excitation and inhibition, exhibiting paradoxical effects including surround suppression in visual cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1721]
- Hebbian plasticity underlies associative memory networks where synaptic strengthening occurs based on correlated activity, with multiple stable states from synaptic loops. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1721]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dichotomy between selectivity and invariance in visual processing may be reconciled through hierarchical processing in neural networks.
- Inference: Recurrent networks can exhibit paradoxical effects where adding excitation to inhibitory neurons decreases their firing rate.
- Inference: Neural network models bridge computational neuroscience and cognitive science by providing frameworks for understanding perception, memory, and decision-making at the neural level.
