# Italian Confucianism

## Summary

Italian Confucianism refers to the social phenomenon in parts of Italy where family loyalty takes precedence over civic engagement and broader social trust, resembling Confucian cultural patterns in East Asia. This concept, developed through studies of southern Italian communities like Montegrano (Chiaromonte), describes a pattern where strong extended family bonds coexist with weak civic institutions and limited trust beyond kinship networks.

## Key facts

- Edward C. Banfield studied a southern Italian community he called Montegrano (actual name Chiaromonte) to understand the moral basis of a backward society. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Banfield documented how amoral familism—acting only for the material benefit of one's nuclear family—characterized the community. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Banfield observed that residents could not cooperate for mutual benefit even when it would be to their collective advantage. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Robert Putnam's study documented the North-South distribution of civic community characteristics across Italy, with stronger civic engagement in the North. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]

## Inferences

- Inference: The term 'Italian Confucianism' draws an analogy between Italian family-centered social structures and East Asian Confucian values, both prioritizing family loyalty over broader civic trust.
