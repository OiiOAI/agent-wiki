# Acquisition and Control of Fire

## Summary

Freud's psychoanalytic interpretation of the myth of Prometheus, proposing that the acquisition of fire involved renouncing the homosexually-tinged desire to extinguish fire with urine, representing a renunciation of drive that became necessary for civilization.

## Key facts

- Freud proposed that to gain control over fire, men had to renounce the homosexually-tinged desire to put it out with a stream of urine. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7045]
- The acquisition of fire was regarded as a crime in myths because the gods possessed what humans had to renounce; in analytic terms, the life of the drives was defrauded. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7046]
- Prometheus was punished by being chained to a rock while a vulture ate his liver daily, with the liver representing the seat of passions and desires. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7047]
- Freud connected the physiological fact that urination and sexual excitation are mutually incompatible—'as incompatible as fire and water.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7049]

## Inferences

- Inference: The myth of Prometheus encodes a historical truth about the renunciation of drive that was necessary for civilization, with the punishment representing the resentment that cultural restrictions inevitably arouse.
- Inference: The phoenix myth, like the Prometheus liver, describes the indestructibility of libidinal desires that are quenched but daily revived.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that it may be difficult to distinguish between historical elements in the myth and purely imaginative symbolic elements.
