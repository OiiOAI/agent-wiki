# The Growth and Guidance of Axons

## Summary

The Growth and Guidance of Axons describes how neurons extend axons and dendrites to form synaptic connections. Neuronal polarity is established through cytoskeletal rearrangements involving Par protein complexes. The growth cone at the axon tip transduces molecular cues into cytoskeletal changes. Guidance cues include extracellular matrix molecules (laminins), cell adhesion molecules (cadherins, immunoglobulin superfamily), and secreted signals (netrins, semaphorins, slits, ephrins). Retinal ganglion axons reach the optic tectum through a series of discrete decision points. Ephrin-Eph gradients create the retinotopic map through repellent signaling.

## Key facts

- Axons and dendrites acquire distinct molecular identities early in development, with Tau protein in axons and MAP2 in dendrites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1261]
- The Par complex (Par3, Par6, aPKC) is required for establishing neuronal polarity; mutants generate processes intermediate between axons and dendrites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1262]
- Growth cones use filopodia to sense guidance cues and transduce them into actin cytoskeletal rearrangements through second messengers including calcium and cyclic nucleotides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1265]
- Guidance cues act through at least six mechanisms: extracellular matrix adhesion, cell surface adhesion, fasciculation, chemoattraction, contact inhibition, and chemorepulsion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1270]
- Retinal axons navigate through the optic chiasm by responding to ephrin-B from midline glial cells; temporal axons expressing EphB are deflected ipsilaterally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1274]
- Ephrin-A gradients in the tectum repel posterior retinal axons expressing high EphA, creating the anteroposterior retinotopic map. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1276]
