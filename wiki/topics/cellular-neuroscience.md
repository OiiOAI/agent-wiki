# Cellular Neuroscience

## Summary

Cellular neuroscience examines the electrical and molecular properties of individual neurons that allow them to function as the fundamental units of neural computation. Key topics include membrane potential arising from ionic concentration gradients, action potentials as all-or-none signals, synaptic transmission via neurotransmitter release, and electrotonic coupling through gap junctions. The electrical activity of neurons—oscillations, resonance, and coherence—forms the foundation for all higher brain functions including consciousness.

## Key facts

- Neurons constitute a specialization of eukaryotic cells that allowed evolution of natural computation by cellular ensembles, carrying information and constructing an internal world that simulates external reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p86]
- The resting membrane potential is typically -70 mV with respect to the outside of the cell, maintained by semipermeable membranes and ion pumps. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p90]
- Synaptic potentials are graded potentials that vary from fractions of a millivolt to tens of millivolts, produced by neurotransmitter release from vesicles, and summate to potentially trigger action potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p93]
