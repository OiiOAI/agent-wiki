# Emotion Regulation

## Summary

The process of managing emotions through both reappraisal of emotion-eliciting stimuli and developing integrated regulatory processes for behavior, enabling full emotional experience while maintaining a sense of choice about expression.

## Key facts

- Emotions are reactions to real or imagined stimuli in the current situation or in memory, and the stimuli to which people have emotional reactions do not have universal meanings. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188]
- People give their own meanings to stimuli, and these meanings derive from how the stimulus relates to the people's needs, wants, and expectations. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p188]
- Becoming autonomous involves developing integrated regulatory processes for managing behavior when emotions have been stimulated. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p191]
- Emotions serve as a cue that signals a discrepancy between people's current state and some standard they hold, allowing them to ask whether they need to find a way to get what they want or whether their expectations are unnecessary. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p192]
- Being integrated and autonomous means allowing oneself to feel emotions—all the emotions—and then deciding what to do with them. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p193]
- Being autonomous means neither blocking awareness of emotions because of introjected admonitions nor letting them be overpowering; it means experiencing them fully and feeling a sense of choice about how to express them. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p193]

## Inferences

- Inference: Depression and aggrandizement are not pure emotions but have cognitive overlays involving self-derogation or self-aggrandizement that are maladaptive, distinguishing them from basic emotions like joy, sadness, and anger.
