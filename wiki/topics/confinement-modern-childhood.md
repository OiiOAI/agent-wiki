# The Confinement of Modern Childhood

## Summary

Children in the Western world have experienced a dramatic transformation in how they spend their time, characterized by a drastic reduction in free, unsupervised outdoor play. By 2003, only 10 percent of U.S. children spent any time playing freely outdoors on a regular basis. This confinement, combined with reduced physical activity, is hypothesized to contribute significantly to rising attention problems.

## Key facts

- By 2003, only 10 percent of children in the U.S. spent any time playing freely outdoors on a regular basis. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p257]
- Only 73 percent of American elementary schools now have any form of recess, down significantly from previous decades. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p257]
- Professor Joel Nigg explains that aerobic exercise 'expands the growth of brain connections, the frontal cortex, and the brain chemicals that support self-regulation and executive functioning,' making these findings 'definite.'.
