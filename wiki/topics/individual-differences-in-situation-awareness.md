# Individual Differences in Situation Awareness

## Summary

This chapter presents experimental research by Gugerty and Tirre examining how individual differences in cognitive abilities affect situation awareness (SA) in real-time tasks, particularly driving. Three experiments using a PC-based driving simulator measured SA through recall probes, time-to-passage probes, performance probes, and scene-interpretation probes. Working memory emerged as the primary cognitive predictor of SA, with correlations ranging from .44 to .74 across experiments. Additional predictors included multilimb coordination, temporal processing, and visual processing abilities. The findings support the theoretical view that SA depends critically on working memory capacity and time-sharing ability.

## Key facts

- Working memory capacity was the primary cognitive predictor of SA across all three experiments, with correlations ranging from r = .44 to r = .74 with various SA measures. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p270]
- Multilimb coordination correlated significantly with SA measures (r = .31 to .54), likely reflecting its association with time-sharing ability. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p274]
- Temporal processing predicted SA ability regardless of whether temporal tests used visual or auditory stimuli, with both duration discrimination and rate extrapolation contributing significantly. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p282]
- SA measures correlated highly with g/WM factor and slightly less with ASVAB g factor, supporting the view that working memory and tasks with heavy working memory requirements may be the core of psychometric general intelligence. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p271]
- In Experiment 3, the six predictors (g/WM and five perceptual-motor/temporal/visual factors) together accounted for 52.9% of the variance in the SA composite score (R = .73). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p281]
- Both dynamic visual processing (road-sign test) and static visual processing (visual search) made unique contributions to predicting SA. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p282]

## Inferences

- Inference: The substantial common contribution between working memory and multilimb coordination suggests these abilities share underlying cognitive demands related to time-sharing or resource management.
- Inference: The finding that SA correlates with g (general cognitive ability) and working memory supports the theoretical claim that SA involves conscious, controlled processing limited by working memory capacity.
- Inference: The researchers hypothesized that SA may also have an automatic component that might not be limited by working memory, meriting further investigation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: In Experiment 3, g/WM failed to make a significant unique contribution to SA variance, possibly due to a weakened measurement approach compared to Experiments 1 and 2.
- Uncertain: The visual processing factors operating in the dynamic driving environment were described as 'admittedly incomplete' by the authors.
- Uncertain: Factor analysis did not reveal the hypothesized 2-factor structure (scene perception vs. local scene comprehension); instead, a single factor adequately described the SA data.
