# Bozeman, Montana

## Summary

Bozeman, Montana is the destination where Phædrus had taught at a college (the 'Church of Reason') during the 1950s. The college was caught in a political struggle during an era of ultra-right-wing politics, with a governor trying to fire professors and eliminate academic freedom. The narrator is returning to this place with tension and archeological feelings, knowing he will encounter DeWeese, who knew Phædrus.

## Key facts

- Phædrus taught at a college in Bozeman during the 1950s, a time when ultra-right-wing politics was rising in Montana, with a governor who was state coordinator for the John Birch Society. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p123]
- The governor had a list of fifty 'radical' college professors he intended to fire, though Phædrus would have been included simply for asking basic questions. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p87]
- At the school, Phædrus was regarded as controversial and was 'something of a troublemaker,' but was never censured in proportion to the trouble he made because his motives were ultimately rational. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p127]
- Eleanor Roosevelt was once 'banned' from the Bozeman college for being 'too controversial.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p87]
