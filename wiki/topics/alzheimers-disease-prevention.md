# Alzheimer's Disease Prevention

## Summary

Alzheimer's disease prevention encompasses lifestyle and medical interventions aimed at reducing risk or delaying onset of Alzheimer's and related neurodegenerative diseases. Key strategies include exercise (particularly endurance and strength training), dietary approaches (Mediterranean diet, ketogenic diet), sleep optimization, managing metabolic health, and addressing modifiable risk factors. Prevention is emphasized as the primary approach since no effective treatments exist for established disease.

## Key facts

- Exercise is considered the single most powerful tool for preventing cognitive decline, acting through multiple vascular and metabolic mechanisms. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p209]
- People in the lowest quartile of grip strength have a 72% higher incidence of dementia compared to those in the top quartile. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p209]
- Sleep is critical for brain health as deep sleep enables the brain to clear intracellular waste that accumulates between neurons. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p211]
- Hearing loss is associated with increased Alzheimer's risk, potentially because auditory deprivation leads to brain atrophy and social withdrawal. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p212]
- Poor oral health and P. gingivalis infection correlate with increased inflammatory markers (IL-6) and have been found in Alzheimer's patient brains. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p212]
- Sauna use (at least 4 sessions per week, 20+ minutes at 179°F/82°C) may reduce Alzheimer's risk by approximately 65% and ASCVD risk by 50%. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p212]
- For high-risk APOE e4/e4 patients like Stephanie, metabolic health optimization (glucose metabolism, inflammation, oxidative stress) is the first priority. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p208]
- A Mediterranean-style diet with monounsaturated fats, fewer refined carbohydrates, and regular fatty fish consumption is recommended. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p208]
- Higher doses of omega-3 fatty acid DHA may be required for e4/e4 carriers due to e4-induced metabolic changes and blood-brain barrier dysfunction. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p208]
- The four guiding principles for Alzheimer's prevention are: (1) what's good for the heart is good for the brain, (2) what's good for the liver is good for the brain, (3) time is key—prevention must start early, and (4) exercise is the most powerful tool. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p213]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cognitive reserve (built through education, experience, complex skills like music or language) may help some individuals resist symptoms of Alzheimer's disease longer.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The evidence linking vitamin D supplementation to reduced Alzheimer's risk is correlational and causality has not been established.
- Uncertain: The precise role of alcohol in Alzheimer's disease remains controversial.
