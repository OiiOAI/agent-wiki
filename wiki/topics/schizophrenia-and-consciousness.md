# Schizophrenia and Consciousness

## Summary

Dehaene proposes that schizophrenia may be a 'disease of consciousness' targeting the long-distance connections that support the global neuronal workspace. The disorder appears to affect specifically the conscious integration of information, leaving subliminal processing largely intact while dramatically impairing conscious access, self-monitoring, and metacognitive judgments.

## Key facts

- Schizophrenia affects functions associated with the conscious global workspace: social beliefs, self-monitoring, metacognitive judgments, and even elementary access to perceptual information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p313]
- Schizophrenic patients exhibit a dramatic overconfidence in their bizarre beliefs, with metacognition and theory of mind so seriously impaired that patients fail to distinguish their own thoughts, knowledge, actions, and memories from those of others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p313]
- Schizophrenic patients' conscious memories can be flagrantly wrong, yet their implicit unconscious memories may remain completely intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p314]
- Schizophrenics' threshold for conscious access is elevated: they need much more sensory evidence before reporting conscious seeing, though their unconscious processing (including subliminal priming at 29 milliseconds) remains intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p314]
- Diffusion tensor imaging reveals massive anomalies of long-distance bundles of axons in schizophrenia, particularly impairing the corpus callosum and connections linking prefrontal cortex with cortex, hippocampus, and thalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p315]
- Drugs such as phencyclidine (PCP) and ketamine, which block NMDA receptors essential for top-down transmission, cause a transient schizophrenia-like psychosis, supporting the hypothesis that NMDA receptor dysfunction underlies schizophrenia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p316]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Fletcher-Frith proposal explains positive symptoms: when top-down predictions fail due to impaired long-distance connections, sensory inputs are never satisfactorily explained, error signals remain, and patients continually concoct far-fetched interpretations of their surroundings [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p317].

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The explanation for positive symptoms (hallucinations and delusions) is based on theoretical proposals by Fletcher and Frith that require further empirical validation [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p316-p317].
