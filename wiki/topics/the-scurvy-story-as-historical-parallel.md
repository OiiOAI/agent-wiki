# The Scurvy Story as Historical Parallel

## Summary

The historical treatment of scurvy demonstrates how long it takes for scientific nutritional evidence to become policy. Despite James Lancaster's 1601 demonstration that citrus prevented scurvy, and James Lind's controlled trial in 1747, it took until 1865 for British policy to require citrus on all ships—a 264-year gap from initial evidence.

## Key facts

- In 1601, James Lancaster demonstrated that three spoonfuls of lemon juice daily prevented scurvy in sailors. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p298]
- In 1747, James Lind conducted a controlled trial showing citrus cured scurvy, but the British Admiralty waited 48 years before requiring citrus on navy ships. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p299]
- It took until 1865 for British policy to require citrus on all merchant ships, 264 years after initial scientific proof. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p299]
- Sailors with scurvy showed weakness, bleeding gums, loose teeth, bruising, and depression before death. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p298]
- Nutrient treatment research for mental disorders traces back to the 1920s, with multinutrient research accelerating around 2000. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p299]

## Inferences

- Inference: The historical parallel suggests that despite accumulating evidence for nutritional treatment of mental health, policy changes may take decades to implement.
