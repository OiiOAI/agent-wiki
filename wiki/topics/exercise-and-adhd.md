# Exercise and ADHD

## Summary

This chapter section presents evidence that exercise functions as an effective treatment for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder by addressing the underlying dysfunction in the brain's attention system. The attention system relies on dopamine and norepinephrine signaling across multiple brain regions including the locus coeruleus, amygdala, nucleus accumbens, basal ganglia, cerebellum, and prefrontal cortex. Exercise immediately increases these neurotransmitters and, with regular practice, raises baseline levels by stimulating growth of new receptors. Complex motor activities such as martial arts, gymnastics, and dance dance revolution are particularly effective because they engage overlapping attention and movement pathways simultaneously. Case studies demonstrate that exercise can enable patients to reduce or discontinue medication in some cases, with subjects reporting enhanced focus during and after exercise sessions.

## Key facts

- Approximately 4 percent of American adults (thirteen million people) have ADHD, which is the most-studied disorder in medicine and clearly not an attitude problem, as evidenced by its strong familial inheritance (91% concordance in identical twins). [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p120]
- The 1990 Zametkin PET scan study demonstrated that adults with ADHD show 10 percent less brain activity during attention tests than controls, with the largest deficit in the prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p120]
- Exercise increases dopamine levels in the basal ganglia by creating new dopamine receptors, acting similarly to ADHD medications like Ritalin. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p131]
- Dishman's research found boys showed improved motor reflex inhibition after maximal exercise, while girls improved more after submaximal exercise (65-75% of maximum heart rate). [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p132]
- A small Hofstra University study found ADHD boys participating in martial arts twice weekly improved behavior, homework completion, class preparation, grades, and rule-breaking compared to those on typical aerobic exercise programs. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p122]
- Chronic exercise improves the tone of the locus coeruleus, making individuals less prone to startle and reducing irritability. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p131]
- The cerebellum, which governs balance and fluidity, contains half of the brain's neurons and is often smaller and dysfunctional in ADHD; complex exercise increases BDNF more dramatically in the cerebellum than simple running. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p125]

## Inferences

- Inference: The strong relationship between movement and attention, evidenced by overlapping neural pathways, explains why complex physical activities like martial arts produce greater improvements than aerobic exercise alone for ADHD symptoms.
- Inference: Patients who exercise regularly may require lower doses of stimulant medication due to exercise's direct effects on dopamine and norepinephrine systems.
- Inference: The prefrontal cortex does not fully develop until the early twenties, explaining why hyperactivity symptoms often subside with age and why ADHD can be compensated through learned strategies.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: While anecdotal evidence suggests the focusing effects of exercise last about an hour to ninety minutes, researchers have not quantified this precisely.
