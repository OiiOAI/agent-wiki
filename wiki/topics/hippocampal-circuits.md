# Hippocampal Memory Circuits

## Summary

The hippocampal formation contains distinct subregions (dentate gyrus, CA3, CA2, CA1) that process different aspects of explicit memory. The trisynaptic pathway (entorhinal cortex → dentate gyrus → CA3 → CA1) and direct pathway carry spatial and contextual information that is transformed through pattern separation and completion mechanisms.

## Key facts

- The hippocampus processes information through both indirect (trisynaptic) and direct pathways from entorhinal cortex to CA1, with the trisynaptic pathway involving dentate gyrus → CA3 → CA1. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1387]
- CA3 pyramidal neurons have strong recurrent excitatory connections with each other (recurrent collaterals) that are thought to contribute to associative memory storage and pattern completion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1387]
- CA1 pyramidal cells are the major output neurons of the hippocampus, projecting back to deep layers of entorhinal cortex and forward to the subiculum. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1387]
- The dentate gyrus performs pattern separation (creating distinct representations of similar experiences), while CA3 performs pattern completion (retrieving complete memories from partial cues). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1406]
- CA2 is important for social memory and receives strong input from hypothalamic nuclei releasing oxytocin and vasopressin. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1388]
