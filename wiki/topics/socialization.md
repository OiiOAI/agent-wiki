# Socialization

## Summary

Socialization is the process through which individuals take on the values of society. Deci and Flaste contrast two approaches: the controlling perspective that views socialization as something done to people, programming their behavior, versus the SDT perspective that views humans as having inherent tendencies to grow and develop, with internalization being a proactive process individuals do with help from caregivers. Effective socialization requires that children experience satisfaction of their basic psychological needs—for autonomy, competence, and relatedness—within the social context.

## Key facts

- Socialization can be viewed as something that gets done to people—a matter of writing people's life scripts and molding them into society—or as a proactive process individuals do with help from caregivers. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p103]
- Developing human beings need psychological nutriments to do what they do naturally, including satisfaction of basic needs for autonomy, competence, and relatedness. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p109]
- Children face the challenge of responding to society's beckoning without being overwhelmed or suffocated, finding a way to become related to the social world while achieving a sense of integrity. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p109]
- Autonomy support from significant others helps individuals naturally take on and assimilate aspects of culture that result in making fertile contributions while becoming truly free.
