# Good Strategy vs. Bad Strategy

## Summary

A central theme of Rumelt's book Good Strategy/Bad Strategy is distinguishing between effective and ineffective strategic approaches. Good strategy involves a clear diagnosis, a set of coherent actions, and a focus on actionable proximate objectives, while bad strategy is characterized by fluff, blue-sky objectives, and failure to face real problems.

## Key facts

- Gary Hamel states that Rumelt 'reminds managers that the essence of strategy is a clear and differentiated point of view that supports forceful and coherent action'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p3]
- Brian Farrell notes that the book 'explains what is, and what is not, good strategy and good strategic thinking'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p3]
- James Roche identifies strategy 'sins' as including fluff, blue-sky objectives, and not facing the problem. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p3]
- Gary Hamel notes that 'Most companies have strategies that are quixotic, muddled, and undifferentiated', suggesting widespread prevalence of bad strategy. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p3]
- Chuck Harrington states that 'A strategy is the set of actions an organization or team should implement and, just as important, the actions an organization should avoid'. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p3]
- A good strategy does more than urge forward toward a goal; it honestly acknowledges challenges and provides an approach to overcoming them. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p15]
- Good strategy almost always looks simple and obvious and does not require thick decks of PowerPoint slides. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p14]
- A talented leader identifies one or two critical issues—the pivot points that can multiply the effectiveness of effort—and focuses and concentrates action and resources on them. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p14]
- The two essential aspects of good strategy are: (1) having a coherent strategy that coordinates policies and actions, and (2) the creation of new strengths through subtle shifts in viewpoint. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p17]

## Inferences

- Inference: The diagnosis and action components are identified by reviewers as the distinguishing features that separate good strategy from bad strategy.
- Inference: Good strategy is unexpected because most organizations do not have one, and competitors do not expect you to have one.
- Inference: The power of good strategy comes from directly tackling fundamental problems with focused and coordinated actions.
