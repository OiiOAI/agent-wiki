# Queer Theory

## Summary

Queer theory grew out of key publications, academic conferences, political organizations, and texts largely during the early 1990s. Its theoretical roots lie in feminist studies, literary criticism, social constructionism, and poststructuralism. Politically, it has roots in queer politics and groups such as ACT UP and Queer Nation. Academically, it has strong roots in Michel Foucault, Judith Butler, Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, and Teresa de Lauretis. Queer theory argues that identities are not fixed and stable but are historically and socially constructed processes that are fluid and contested. It approaches the homosexual/heterosexual divide as a figure of knowledge and power that orders desires, behaviors, social institutions, and social relations. It is a broader intellectual project than gay and lesbian studies.
