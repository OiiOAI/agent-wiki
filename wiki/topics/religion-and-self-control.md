# Religion and Self-Control

## Summary

Religious activity increases longevity by approximately 25% according to psychologist Michael McCullough's analysis of over three dozen studies. Religion affects self-control through two central mechanisms: building willpower through regular practices like prayers, meditation, and fasting; and improving monitoring through the sense of being watched by God and the religious community. Religious meditations involve effortful regulation of attention that builds mental discipline. People subliminally exposed to religious words become slower to recognize temptation-related words, suggesting religion is associated with tamping down temptations. McCullough advises agnostics to find their own 'sacred' values.
