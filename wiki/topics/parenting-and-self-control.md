# Parenting and Self-Control

## Summary

Effective parenting for self-control emphasizes consistency, structure, and discipline over unconditional praise. The three basic facets of punishment are severity, speed, and consistency—with consistency being most important. Research by Deborah Carroll (Nanny Deb) on reality TV shows that children thrive with clear rules, structure, and appropriate rewards and punishments. Asian-American parents demonstrate alternative approaches through setting high goals, enforcing standards, and treating strict parenting as a form of devotion. The self-esteem movement's emphasis on praise without achievement has not produced better outcomes than traditional discipline.

## Key facts

- Consistency is by far the most important facet of punishment—parents should discipline quickly and every single time, but in a restrained manner. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p183]
- Nanny Deb (Deborah Carroll) found that children need structure and clear rules, and that it is harder to get parents to stay on track than to help children. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p182]
- When children help draw up rules, they see them as personal commitments instead of parental whims and are more likely to respect them. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p189]
