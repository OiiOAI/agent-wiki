# Social Implications of Brain Emotional Systems

## Summary

This topic explores how understanding brain emotional systems might inform social institutions and cultural practices. The chapter discusses implications for education (e.g., concerns about medicating impulsive children), economics, social contracts, and the potential for designing social systems that minimize negative emotions and maximize positive social-emotional functions like nurturance and altruism.

## Key facts

- Social systems that fail to recognize the importance and natural dynamics of intrinsic emotional urges are bound to make graver mistakes than those that do. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p169]
- Social systems will flourish if they minimize the impact of negative emotions on members of a society. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p169]
- The brain contains circuits for social play and dominance, and no successful social system has stifled the dictates of those circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p169]
- Human sense of sympathy may be intrinsically weaker than the sense of retribution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p169]
- The most effective trading strategy is fairness: to punish trading partners only if they have cheated, but then to forgive rapidly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p170]
- In anonymous megasocieties, the ancient stricture to do unto others as you would have them do unto you may be gradually losing force. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p170]

## Inferences

- Inference: Emotional education could potentially counter human potential for evil by cultivating new perspectives that explicitly recognize the nature and importance of all basic emotions.
- Inference: New social systems may prosper only to the extent that they harmonize with the positive emotions of the greatest number.
