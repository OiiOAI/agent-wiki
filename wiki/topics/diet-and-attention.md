# Diet and Attention

## Summary

Research indicates that modern diets are significantly impairing attention through three main mechanisms: causing energy spikes and crashes from high glycemic foods, depriving the brain of nutrients needed for development and function, and introducing chemicals (like food dyes) that act on the brain like drugs. Nutritionists argue that returning to whole foods could dramatically improve attention.

## Key facts

- Dale Pinnock explains that eating typical breakfast foods like Frosties and white bread causes blood sugar to 'go really high, really quickly' and then crash, causing 'brain fog' and inability to focus. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p214]
- A 2009 Dutch study found that over 70 percent of children with attention problems who cut out preservatives and artificial dyes improved their ability to pay attention, with an average improvement of 50 percent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p216]
- A 2007 Southampton study found that children who drank beverages containing common food additives were significantly more likely to become hyperactive, leading to bans of these dyes in many European countries but not the U.S. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p217]
- Dr. Drew Ramsay states that the brain 'gets built from foods' and requires a broad range of key nutrients, particularly omega-3s found in fish, to grow and function properly. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p217]
