# Government Dietary Guidelines

## Summary

Government dietary guidelines are produced by advisory committees to influence food purchasing and preparation in institutions receiving government funding. The US guidelines recommend a Mediterranean-style diet emphasizing vegetables, fruits, whole grains, dairy, and variety of protein foods while limiting saturated fats, added sugars, and sodium.

## Key facts

- US dietary guidelines recommend a healthy eating pattern including a variety of vegetables, whole fruits, at least half whole grains, fat-free or low-fat dairy, and a variety of protein foods. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p233]
- The guidelines limit saturated fats and trans fats, added sugars, and sodium. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p233]
- Over 100 countries have established dietary guidelines, all emphasizing variety, adequate water, and lots of fruits and vegetables. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p234]
- The American plate size has increased from 8.5 inches (800 calories) in the 1960s to 12 inches (1,900 calories) in the 21st century. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p234]
- The Mediterranean diet emphasizes olive oil consumption, while US guidelines merely mention oils. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p234]
