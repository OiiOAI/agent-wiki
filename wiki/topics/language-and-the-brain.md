# Language and the Brain

## Summary

The study of how language is represented in the brain, based initially on clinical studies of aphasia and later on brain imaging techniques. Key findings include the localization of language production to Broca's area and language comprehension to Wernicke's area in the left hemisphere, the distributed nature of language processing, the age-dependence of language acquisition affecting cortical organization, and the involvement of the right hemisphere in affective aspects of language (prosody).

## Key facts

- The first important evidence for localization of cognitive abilities came from studies of how the brain produces language. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p61]
- Broca's area controls the production of intelligible speech and lies near the region of the motor area controlling mouth and tongue movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- Wernicke's area processes auditory input for language and is important for understanding speech, lying near the primary auditory cortex and angular gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- Wernicke's area communicates with Broca's area by a bidirectional pathway, part of which is the arcuate fasciculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- If a second language is acquired in adulthood, it is represented in a distinct region within Broca's area separate from the native language; if acquired early, both share common representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p65]
- The affective aspects of language (prosody) are represented in the right hemisphere, mirroring the organization of logical content in the left. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p67]
- Damage to the right temporal area corresponding to Wernicke's area leads to disturbances in comprehending emotional aspects of speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p67]

## Inferences

- Inference: The neural networks for language are far more extensive and complex than those described by Broca and Wernicke alone, as revealed by modern imaging studies.
- Inference: Functional MRI studies show that thinking about a word's meaning in the absence of sensory inputs activates a different area in the left frontal cortex than reading or speaking.
