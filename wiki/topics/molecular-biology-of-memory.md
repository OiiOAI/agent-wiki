# Molecular Biology of Learning and Memory

## Summary

The molecular biology of learning and memory encompasses the cellular and molecular mechanisms by which experiences are encoded in the nervous system. Research on Aplysia has revealed that short-term memory involves functional changes in synaptic strength mediated by second messenger systems like cyclic AMP and protein kinase A, while long-term memory requires gene activation, new protein synthesis, and structural changes including growth of new synaptic connections.

## Key facts

- Kandel's work demonstrated that memory derives from changes in synapses: short-term memory from functional changes and long-term memory from structural changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p233]
- The neurotransmitter for the gill-withdrawal reflex is glutamate, the major excitatory transmitter in the mammalian brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p234]
- Serotonin acts as a modulatory transmitter to enhance glutamate release from presynaptic terminals of sensory neurons during sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p234]
- There are two kinds of neural circuits: mediating circuits that produce behaviors and modulating circuits that modify the strength of connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p235]
- The CREB switch for converting short-term to long-term memory is the same in Aplysia, Drosophila, bees, mice, and people. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p278]
- The cyclic AMP pathway is not unique to memory; it is used in the gut, kidney, liver, and even single-celled organisms like E. coli. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p245]

## Inferences

- Inference: The conservation of molecular mechanisms across species suggests that fundamental processes of learning and memory have been preserved throughout evolution.
