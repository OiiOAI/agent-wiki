# Origin of Monotheism

## Summary

Freud's comprehensive theory tracing Jewish monotheism to Egyptian origins—specifically Akhenaten's Aten religion carried forward by Moses. The theory posits that monotheism underwent a traumatic suppression (Moses' murder), latency period (Yahweh worship), and eventual resurgence through the Prophets, paralleling neurotic symptom formation.

## Key facts

- Freud asserts that Jewish monotheism originated with Akhenaten's Aten religion, which Moses introduced to the Jewish people after Akhenaten's death. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7229]
- The 'great Prophets' are described as men who tirelessly preached the old Mosaic doctrine—that the deity disdained sacrifice and ceremonial and asked only for faith and a life in truth and justice. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7222]
- Freud identifies dualities in Jewish history: two groups that formed the nation, two kingdoms, two gods' names, two religious founders—both called Moses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7223]

## Inferences

- Inference: The first duality—the traumatic experience of Moses' murder that one portion of the people endured while another escaped—accounts for all subsequent dualities in Jewish history.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledges that 'we have here dealt with only a single instance from the copious phenomenology of religions' and cannot illuminate other religions fully.
