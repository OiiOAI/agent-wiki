# Learning Happiness

## Summary

Happiness is learned as a skill, not inherited. Key principles include accepting reality, eliminating desires, finding peace through presence, and building good habits. The book explores that happiness comes from internal peace rather than external achievements, and that desires are contracts for unhappiness.

## Key facts

- Peace and happiness are skills that can be learned, like fitness or nutrition. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p119]
- Happiness requires presence; most people spend their time planning the future or regretting the past. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p111]
- Happiness requires peace; envy is the enemy of happiness. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p112]
- Every desire is a chosen unhappiness; desire is a contract you make with yourself to be unhappy until you get what you want. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p114]
- Success does not earn happiness; after a certain point, happiness is internal. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p115]
- Happiness is built by habits; the people you spend the most time with shape your happiness. [raw/books/philosophy/The Almanack of Naval Ravikant_ A Guide to - Eric Jorgenson.pdf#p119]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book suggests that happiness practices include meditation, exercise, avoiding caffeine, minimizing social media, and surrounding yourself with positive people.
