# Autism and Socioemotional Brain Systems

## Summary

Early childhood autism is characterized by severe failures in socialization, communication, and imagination. Current theoretical perspectives suggest these children do not develop a 'theory of mind' and may have dysfunction in neural systems mediating social emotions. Research suggests autistic children may have excessive endogenous opioid activity, and treatment with opioid antagonists like naltrexone has shown benefits in approximately half of autistic children by reducing hyperactivity, stereotypies, self-injurious behaviors, and promoting social activities.

## Key facts

- Autism is characterized by severe failures in socialization, communication, and imagination, with an innate inability to form biologically provided affective contact with people. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Autistic children do not develop a 'theory of mind'—the ability to recognize thoughts and feelings in others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Autism is now agreed to be a neurobiological disorder originating in the second trimester of pregnancy during brain stem and limbic circuit development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Autistic children have too many densely packed small neurons within parts of the limbic system, suggesting apoptosis did not progress normally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- About half of autistic children have elevated endogenous opioids, which could explain their pain insensitivity and self-injurious behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Naltrexone (opioid receptor blocker) improves about half of autistic children by reducing hyperactivity, stereotypies, self-injurious behaviors, and promoting social activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p147]
- Rough-and-tumble play may be the only type of social activity some autistic children favor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p157]
- Autistic children typically exhibit high levels of rough-and-tumble play, consistent with elevated opioid activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p157]

## Inferences

- Inference: Autism may involve dysfunction in the social-bonding and separation-distress systems of the brain
- Inference: Children with autism may have a primary deficit in the ability to experience social emotions and perceive emotional dynamics in others
- Inference: The fact that only roughhousing play is favored by autistic children is consistent with excessive opioid activity hypothesis

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise genetic and environmental factors contributing to autism remain unclear
- Uncertain: It is unknown whether abnormalities in oxytocin or vasotocin systems contribute to autism
- Uncertain: Whether early intervention with neural growth factors could help is speculative
