# Rhetoric Teaching Methods

## Summary

This topic covers Phædrus's innovative teaching approaches in freshman rhetoric and technical writing at a Montana university. His methods included: withholding grades while secretly recording them; using 'demonstrator' essays developed in real-time with students; refusing to define Quality while teaching students to recognize it empirically; and breaking the imitation compulsion by assigning students to describe specific, narrow objects. These experiments revealed that brighter students were less desirous of grades while weaker students depended on them.

## Key facts

- Phædrus discovered that imitation was 'a real evil that had to be broken before real rhetoric teaching could begin,' leading him to assign students to write about specific objects like 'the upper left-hand brick' of the Opera House. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p162]
- In his grade-withholding experiment, 'The A students were 2 to 1 in favor of the system. The B and C students were evenly divided. And the D's and F's were unanimously opposed.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p168]
- Phædrus taught that Quality 'cannot be defined' but students 'know what Quality is,' trapping them 'into making quality judgments for himself' which 'was just exactly this and nothing else that taught him to write.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p177]
