# Sexual Aetiology of the Neuroses

## Summary

Freud's theory that sexual factors constitute the essential aetiology of the neuroses. In neurasthenia, the aetiology involves present-day sexual practices; in psychoneuroses (hysteria, obsessional neurosis, paranoia), the aetiology lies in infantile sexual experiences which the patient has forgotten and which only become pathogenic when recalled as unconscious memories.

## Key facts

- In every case of neurosis there is a sexual aetiology; but in neurasthenia it is of a present-day kind, whereas in psychoneuroses it lies in early childhood. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1160]
- The events and influences which lie at the root of every psychoneurosis belong to an epoch of life which is long past—to the time of early childhood—which is why the patient knows nothing of them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1159]
- Defence achieves its purpose if there are infantile sexual scenes present in the form of unconscious memories, and if the idea to be repressed can be brought into logical or associative connection with an infantile experience. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1116]

## Inferences

- Inference: The distinction between neurasthenia (current sexual practices) and psychoneuroses (childhood experiences) suggests different temporal relationships between aetiology and symptom onset.
