# Consciousness Studies

## Summary

Consciousness studies is an interdisciplinary field tackling the question of how the brain gives rise to subjective experience. The book argues that a major obstacle has been the cortical fallacy — the assumption that consciousness is a cortical function — which has misdirected research for over a century. Solms proposes that consciousness should be understood through the lens of affective neuroscience, which emphasises that feeling (not cognition) is the primary content of consciousness, and that feelings originate from ancient subcortical systems shared with other vertebrates. The field also grapples with the problem of other minds — how we can know whether other beings are conscious — and with the distinction between the 'level' of consciousness (wakefulness) and its 'contents' (experience).

## Key facts

- Consciousness studies confronts two fundamental puzzles: the mind/body problem (how the brain gives rise to the mind) and the problem of other minds (how we can know whether other beings are conscious). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p17]
- The field distinguishes between 'consciousness as the waking state' and 'consciousness as experience' (phenomenal consciousness), and between the quantitative 'level' and qualitative 'contents' of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p59]
- Three major scientific responses to consciousness have emerged: behaviourism (which excluded subjectivity), cognitive psychology (which treated the mind as a computer), and cognitive neuroscience (which uses brain imaging to study neural correlates). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p18]
- Solms argues that the cortical fallacy has been perpetuated by theoretical inertia rather than scientific evidence, and that contemporary cognitive neuroscience now recognizes that much mental processing is unconscious. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p67]
- Contemporary binding theories (global workspace, re-entrant loops, integrated information) fail to explain why binding should give rise to experience, and may lead to pan-psychism — the view that even computers or inanimate systems might be conscious. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p84]

## Inferences

- Inference: The field of consciousness studies has reached an impasse by treating consciousness as a cortical phenomenon while simultaneously discovering that cortical processing is largely unconscious. The resolution proposed here is that consciousness is not about cortical information processing but about subcortical feeling.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the proposed relocation of consciousness from cortex to brainstem will gain consensus within the consciousness studies community remains to be seen.
