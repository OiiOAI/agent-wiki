# Neurodegenerative Diseases

## Summary

Neurodegenerative diseases are characterized by progressive loss of neuronal function and structure. Major categories include Alzheimer disease, Parkinson disease, and the trinucleotide repeat diseases such as Huntington disease and the spinocerebellar ataxias. Common pathogenic mechanisms include protein misfolding and aggregation, mitochondrial dysfunction, altered gene expression, and impaired protein clearance. Despite different genetic causes, these disorders share overlapping cellular pathways that represent potential therapeutic targets.

## Key facts

- Major degenerative diseases of the nervous system affect more than 6 million people in the United States and more than 25 million throughout the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1544]
- Neurodegenerative diseases can be divided into inherited and sporadic (unknown etiology) forms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1544]
- Protein misfolding is a common pathogenic mechanism across adult-onset neurodegenerative diseases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1554]
- Mitochondrial dysfunction is common in neurodegenerative diseases, with specific proteins regulating mitochondrial quality control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1556]
- Studies in mouse models have demonstrated that neuronal dysfunction is reversible in several neurodegenerative diseases when expression of the mutant gene is modulated.
