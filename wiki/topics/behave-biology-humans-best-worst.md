# Sapolsky's 'Behave': Biology of Human Behavior

## Summary

Robert Sapolsky's 'Behave: The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst' (2017) examines the biological causes of human behavior, working backward in time from seconds before a behavior to millions of years of evolutionary history. This chunk covers chapters 4-7: the endocrinology of behavior (testosterone, oxytocin, vasopressin, stress hormones), neuroplasticity (LTP, LTD, adult neurogenesis), adolescence and frontal cortex development, and childhood development including Piagetian cognition, moral development, attachment, and childhood adversity.

## Key facts

- The book's approach is to examine behaviors working backward in time: seconds before, minutes/hours, days/months, and evolutionary timescales. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p104]
- The book argues that hormones rarely determine behavior outright but instead amplify existing tendencies in a context-dependent manner. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p107]
- Neuroplasticity demonstrates that brains change throughout life, making tangible the idea that people can change. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p155]
- The frontal cortex is the last brain region to fully mature, explaining many adolescent behavioral characteristics. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p157]
- Childhood adversity produces convergent biological changes (elevated glucocorticoids, amygdala enlargement, frontal atrophy) increasing risk for multiple adult problems. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p201]

## Inferences

- Inference: The central thesis is that all behaviors have biological substrates that interact with environmental experiences across multiple timescales, meaning that understanding behavior requires integration of neuroscience, endocrinology, development, and evolution
- Inference: The book emphasizes that biology is not destiny but rather sets the range of possible responses to environment, with experience determining where within that range any individual falls
