# Workplace Violence Prevention

## Summary

Violence occurring in work settings includes revenge killing, domestic violence at the workplace, date stalking, and rage killings. Common predictors include perceived humiliation, loss of dignity, and employee dismissal handled poorly. The book emphasizes that obvious warning signs are frequently ignored and that early recognition through pre-incident indicators allows for intervention.

## Key facts

- Workplace violence encompasses revenge killing when employees feel humiliated, domestic violence when spouses pursue victims at work, date stalking, and rage killings. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p98]
- The ESL shooting by Richard Farley killed 7 people and wounded 10; Farley had been fired after refusing to stop pursuing a coworker who had rejected him. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p98]
- USAir Flight 1771 was crashed by David Burke after being fired, killing 43 people; he had a history of violence, had left a death threat on his supervisor's answering machine, and retained his airport ID badge after termination. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p100]
- Wayne Lo at Simon's Rock College killed 2 and wounded 6 after school officials failed to act on a suspicious package and an anonymous warning about his intentions. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p100]
- Pat Sherill, known as 'Crazy Pat,' killed 14 coworkers at the postal facility in 1986, helping create the 'going postal' stereotype, though postal service violence rates are actually better than most industries. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p99]
- Effective background checks give employers the opportunity to learn important information about a candidate before problems occur. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p108]
- Checking references and former employers is described as an absolutely critical duty of every employer. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p108]
- The most important thing references can give is other references, called 'developed sources'—people who know the applicant but were not listed as references. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p109]
- Appropriate supervision can be stated in six words: praise for performance—correction for errors. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p111]
- A problem employee is easier to terminate before he makes a substantial emotional investment in the job. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p112]
- At the instant of firing, a power shift occurs and the fired employee gains options he could never have used while trying to keep his job. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p115]
- The worst possible reaction to a threat is a counter-threat, as violence is interactional and may escalate into a contest employers rarely win. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p115]

## Inferences

- Inference: The postal service stereotype is misleading; with hundreds of thousands of employees, statistical likelihood means more incidents of every kind, including violence.
- Inference: The book argues that employers who experience the worst outcomes typically fail in at least one of three areas: hiring, supervision, or termination timing.
- Inference: The failure to take obvious steps like calling references is described as an 'epidemic in America.'
