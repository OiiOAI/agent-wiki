# Brain-Language Co-Evolution

## Summary

Deacon argues that human brain evolution can only be adequately explained through Baldwinian evolutionary processes, where the use of symbolic communication created new selection pressures that shaped brain structure over approximately 2 million years. The remarkable expansion of the human brain, particularly the prefrontal cortex, was not the cause of symbolic language but a consequence of it. This co-evolution produced an extensive array of perceptual, motor, and learning predispositions that together constitute human language adaptations.

## Key facts

- The remarkable expansion of the brain that took place in human evolution and indirectly produced prefrontal expansion was not the cause of symbolic language but a consequence of it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p366]
- Symbolic communication introduced selection for greater prefrontalization, more efficient articulatory and auditory capacities, and a suite of other ancillary capacities that eased language acquisition and use. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p366]
- The co-evolution of languages with respect to human neurological biases may not just be a plausible source for emergent universals of grammar, it may be the only plausible source. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p366]
- The evolution of specific neural adaptations should tend toward functions recruited for multiple uses rather than strict domain specificity, especially for neural adaptations one or two steps removed from perceptual and motor domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p379]
- The increase in brain size in hominid evolution is a record both of the relative ease with which symbols were able to be acquired by a given fossil species and of the prior effects of selection on this ability. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p371]
- The evolution of language took place neither inside nor outside brains but at the interface where cultural evolutionary processes affect biological evolutionary processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p442]
- Two and a half million years of sustained selection in an unprecedented socio-ecological niche maintained by unprecedented communicational and cognitive tricks have shaped both physical brain changes and the mental and cultural world that co-evolved with them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p441]
- The genetic basis for symbol-learning abilities has been driven to fixation, becoming a universal trait of the species under intense selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p445]
- The changes in human brain proportions span a critical learning threshold standing between indexical associations and symbolic reference. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p445]

## Inferences

- Inference: Language adaptations (LAs) unlike a monolithic language acquisition device (LAD) consist of many subtle perceptual, motor, and learning predispositions that together guide the language learning process.
- Inference: The co-evolutionary perspective recognizes that human neurobiology and psychology cannot be understood without recognizing their shaping by symbolic reference as an evolutionary idea.
