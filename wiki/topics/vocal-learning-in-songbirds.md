# Vocal Learning in Songbirds

## Summary

Songbirds are vocal learners that must learn their complex songs from adult tutors, similar to how humans learn speech. The songbird song system includes specialized forebrain nuclei (HVC, RA, Area X, LMAN) for song learning and production. Song learning occurs during sensitive periods and requires auditory feedback. Song-selective neurons in the song system respond preferentially to the bird's own song.

## Key facts

- Deaf songbirds sing highly simplified isolate songs that lack normal song structure, demonstrating that song is learned rather than innate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p758]
- Songbirds require auditory feedback to refine their songs; birds deafened after sensory learning produce highly abnormal songs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p758]
- The song system in birds includes HVC (premotor nucleus), RA (robust nucleus of the arcopallium), Area X (basal ganglia nucleus), and LMAN (lateral magnocellular nucleus of the anterior nidopallium). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p760]
- Song-selective neurons in the songbird brain respond more strongly to the bird's own song than to other equally complex auditory stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p760]
- Songbirds have sensitive periods for song learning; birds not exposed to song in the first several months of life develop isolate song even if later exposed to normal adult song. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p759]
