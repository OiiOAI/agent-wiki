# Animal Emotion

## Summary

Barrett examines whether animals experience emotions like humans do. She concludes that while many animals (especially mammals) have interoception and feel affect, they likely lack the emotion concepts and social reality necessary for human-style emotions. Animals can learn concepts but primarily action-based ones, not the goal-based concepts humans use. The apparent emotions we see in animals are often mental inference fallacies—humans projecting their own concepts onto animal behavior.

## Key facts

- Macaques have an interoceptive network with some of the same parts as humans, structured to function by prediction; they likely experience affect. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p278]
- Dogs appear to express affect through tail wagging: larger movements to the right during pleasant events like seeing their owner, to the left for unpleasant events. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p288]
- Dogs don't have human emotion concepts like anger, guilt, and jealousy; it's conceivable one dog could develop some emotion-like concept, but without language it couldn't teach the concept to other dogs. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p290]
- Dogs may have some emotion-like concepts; scientists suspect very social animals like dogs and elephants have some concept of death and can experience grief rooted in attachment neurochemistry. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p293]
- Animals with small affective niches cannot form emotion concepts; a lion cannot hate a zebra when hunting; the reality of their emotion is for the moment only within human perceivers. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p300]

## Inferences

- Inference: The question 'Is a growling dog angry?' is actually two questions: 'Was the dog angry from the boy's perspective?' (yes, he constructed that perception) and 'Was the dog angry from his own perspective?' (almost certainly no, lacking human emotion concepts).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether bonobos and perhaps chimpanzees can form their own sort of emotion concepts remains uncertain.
