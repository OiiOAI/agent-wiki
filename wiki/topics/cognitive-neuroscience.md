# Cognitive Neuroscience

## Summary

Cognitive neuroscience emerged from the merger of cognitive psychology with cellular neurobiology in the 1970s-1980s. It focuses on the biology of internal representations and draws on electrophysiological studies of sensory information processing and brain imaging. This field made possible the study of cognitive processes at the cellular and molecular level.

## Key facts

- During the 1970s and 1980s, behaviorists and cognitive psychologists began collaborating with brain scientists, giving rise to cognitive neural science focused on the biology of internal representations. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p308]
- Cognitive neural science draws on two lines of inquiry: the electrophysiological study of how sensory information is represented in animal brains, and the imaging of sensory and other complex internal representations in intact, behaving human beings. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p308]
- The merger of cognitive psychology and cellular neurobiology revealed an actual physical representation—an information-processing capability in the brain—that leads to behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p315]
