# Vocal communication systems

## Summary

Vocal communication systems in vertebrates funnel communicative behaviors into a final common pathway consisting of structures in the central midbrain and brain stem. The motor programs for most innate mammalian vocalizations are highly modular and relatively invariant from birth. Humans have achieved a unique superimposition of cortical motor control on these visceral motor systems, enabling voluntary vocal control and speech. This involves the coordination of respiratory premotor regions, nucleus ambiguus (laryngeal control), hypoglossal nucleus (tongue), and facial/trigeminal motor nuclei (lips/jaw).

## Key facts

- Communicative behaviors in a wide range of vertebrate species are funneled into a final common pathway consisting of structures in the central midbrain and brain stem. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p248]
- Vocalization requires coordinated activation of clusters of motor neurons controlling muscles for breathing, tension of the larynx, and movements of oral and facial muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p250]
- The motor programs for most innate mammalian vocalizations are highly modular, relatively invariant from birth, with learning playing little or no role in their form. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p253]
- In speech, skeletal motor components of the oral tract take the foreground against a comparatively more stable vocal-respiratory background, inverting the relationship seen in animal calls. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p265]
