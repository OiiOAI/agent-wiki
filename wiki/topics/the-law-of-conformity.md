# The Law of Conformity

## Summary

The Law of Conformity describes the unconscious transformation humans undergo when operating in groups, where they imitate others, think differently to fit in, feel emotions infected by group mood, and become prone to risks and irrational behavior. This social personality can dominate individual identity, leading to loss of uniqueness and independent thinking. The solution requires self-awareness and understanding of how group dynamics operate, enabling superior social actors to cooperate while retaining independence and rationality.

## Key facts

- When people operate in groups, they do not engage in nuanced thinking and deep analysis; only individuals with calmness and detachment can do so. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p493]
- People in groups feel emotional and excited, with a primary desire to fit in and believe what others believe. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p493]
- Group thinking tends to be simplistic: good versus evil, with us or against us. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p493]
- Creating chaos makes groups more likely to fall into primitive patterns of thinking due to the fear of confusion and uncertainty. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p493]
- The social force is neither positive nor negative; it is simply a physiological part of human nature. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p502]
- The social force can exert a downward pull into more primitive behaviors unsuited to modern conditions. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p502]
- The problem is not experiencing the social force but being in denial of its existence. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p502]
- Humans need more than anything group intelligence, which includes understanding how groups affect thinking and emotions and the laws and dynamics they operate by. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p503]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Law of Conformity explains why intelligent individuals often make poor decisions in groups, as demonstrated by historical events like the Cultural Revolution where educated students engaged in irrational violence.
