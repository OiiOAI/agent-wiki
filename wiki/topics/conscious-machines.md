# Conscious Machines

## Summary

Dehaene argues that creating artificial consciousness in computers is a logically possible and exciting scientific research direction. He proposes a software architecture with three critical functions current computers lack: flexible communication through a global workspace, powerful learning algorithms for plasticity, and autonomy to set its own goals and generate spontaneous thoughts.

## Key facts

- Dehaene sees no logical problem with the possibility of simulating consciousness in a computer, considering it an exciting avenue of scientific research and a grand challenge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p320]
- A simulated consciousness would contain many specialized programs dedicated to specific functions, with some taking inputs from inside the system to provide introspection and self-knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p320]
- Three critical functions current computers lack: flexible communication through a global workspace, powerful learning algorithms for plasticity, and autonomy to set its own goals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p321]
- Ned Block argues the workspace machinery may explain conscious access but is inherently incapable of explaining qualia—the subjective states or raw feelings of 'what it is like' to experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p321]
- David Chalmers distinguishes the 'easy problem' of consciousness (explaining cognitive functions) from the 'hard problem' of how physical processes give rise to subjective experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p322]
- Dehaene argues Chalmers 'swapped the labels': the 'easy' problem is hard, while the hard problem will evaporate once intuition is educated by cognitive neuroscience and computer simulations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p322]
- Dehaene dismisses quantum mechanics proposals for consciousness (Penrose-Hameroff, Eccles) as having no solid neurobiology, noting the warm brain is incompatible with quantum computing requirements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p324]

## Inferences

- Inference: A machine with free will is not a contradiction in terms: whenever a neuronal architecture exhibits autonomy and deliberation, it exercises a form of free will, even if deterministic [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p325].

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The detailed blueprint for conscious machines remains vague; much work is needed [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p321].
