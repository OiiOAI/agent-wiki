# Brain Preservation of H.M.

## Summary

Following Henry Molaison's death on December 2, 2008, researchers executed a seven-year plan to preserve and study his brain. This included in situ MRI scanning at Mass General, removal of the brain by neuropathologist Matthew Frosch, ex vivo imaging at 3 Tesla and 7 Tesla scanners, and transportation to UC San Diego where the brain was cut into 2,401 slices of 70 microns each. The resulting histological slides and digital images are being used to create a three-dimensional model available online.

## Key facts

- Henry died on December 2, 2008, at age 82 at Bickford nursing home. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p287]
- An in situ MRI scan was performed for nine hours at the Martinos Center, generating 11 gigabytes of brain images—16 times more data than typical living-participant scans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p294]
- Neuropathologist Matthew Frosch removed Henry's brain the morning after his death, with the skull removed using an electric saw. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p295]
- Henry's brain was photographed in situ and after removal before being preserved in formaldehyde solution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p296]
- Ten weeks after preservation, the brain was imaged at both 3 Tesla and 7 Tesla MRI scanners. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p298]
- Jacopo Annese at UC San Diego cut the brain into 2,401 slices of 70 microns each over 53 hours in December 2009. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p300]
- The brain cutting was streamed live on the web, with 400,000 people watching over three days. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p300]
- Digital images from the cutting will be used to create a massive three-dimensional model of Henry's brain for the Digital Brain Library Project at UCSD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p302]
- Henry's identity was revealed publicly for the first time after death, with his name Henry Gustave Molaison announced in the New York Times obituary on December 5, 2008. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p297]

## Inferences

- Inference: The unprecedented scale of H.M.'s brain preservation and the public interest it generated (400,000 online viewers, front-page obituary) reflected his unique status as the most important single-case study in neuroscience history.
- Inference: Postmortem microscopy will reveal whether residual hippocampal tissue existed and whether perirhinal and parahippocampal cortex were functional, potentially explaining his unexpected semantic learning abilities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact cause of Henry's dementia in later years—Alzheimer disease versus vascular dementia—remained to be determined through neuropathological examination.
- Uncertain: Whether the brain slicing captured all relevant tissue without distortion could only be assessed after the analysis was complete.
