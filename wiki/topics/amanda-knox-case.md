# The Amanda Knox Case

## Summary

The Amanda Knox case in Perugia, Italy (2007-2015) illustrates the transparency problem. Knox was an innocent person who acted 'guilty'—she was quirky, inappropriate, and didn't display expected grief behaviors after her roommate Meredith Kercher was murdered. Police and prosecutors wrongly convicted her based on her demeanor rather than evidence. Gladwell argues there was never physical evidence linking her to the crime and the investigation was 'shockingly inept.'

## Key facts

- Amanda Knox was twenty years old when her roommate Meredith Kercher was murdered in Perugia, Italy on November 1, 2007. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p99]
- There was never any physical evidence linking Knox or her boyfriend Sollecito to the crime. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p99]
- Knox showed no expected grief behaviors—she sat with feet on her boyfriend's lap, caressed, kissed, and laughed with him at the police station. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p104]
- When told Kercher 'bled to death,' Knox responded angrily 'She fucking bled to death!'—prosecutors saw this as coldness rather than anger. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p104]
- At the police station, Knox did a split on the floor while waiting for an interview, and when told to take off her shoes, said 'Ta-dah'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p105]
- Lead investigator Edgardo Giobbi said guilt was established 'by closely observing the suspect's psychological and behavioral reaction'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p105]
- Knox was finally declared innocent by the Italian Supreme Court eight years after the crime. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Amanda Knox case demonstrates 'mismatch'—her behavior (quirky, inappropriate) didn't match expectations for an innocent person, leading to wrongful conviction
