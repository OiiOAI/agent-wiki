# Sex Determination and Differentiation

## Summary

Sex determination in mammals is controlled by chromosomal sex (XX or XY) directing gonadal differentiation. The Y chromosome bears the SRY gene, which initiates male development by inducing SOX9 and testis formation. Testes produce testosterone and Müllerian inhibiting substance (MIS) that masculinize the fetus. In the absence of SRY, female development occurs by default.

## Key facts

- In mammals, XY genotype drives differentiation of testes whereas XX leads to ovarian differentiation, with SRY on the Y chromosome being the crucial male determinant. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1359]
- Testosterone produced by testes induces Wolffian duct differentiation into male internal genitalia, while MIS causes regression of Müllerian ducts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1360]
- In the absence of circulating testosterone, Wolffian ducts resorb and Müllerian ducts differentiate into female internal genitalia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1360]
- Complete androgen insensitivity syndrome (CAIS) results from loss-of-function mutations in the androgen receptor, causing XY individuals with testes to develop as phenotypically females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1362]
