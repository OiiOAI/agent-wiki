# East Asian Stock Markets

## Summary

Historical development of stock exchanges in Japan, Taiwan, South Korea, and Hong Kong. Fukuyama provides founding dates and growth trajectories for major East Asian stock exchanges, noting their varying levels of development and the crash of the Tokyo market in 1989-1991.

## Key facts

- The Tokyo Stock Exchange was founded in 1878, briefly closed during World War II, and reopened in 1949 under the American occupation. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p453]
- The Tokyo stock market crash of 1989-1991 reduced that market's total valuation by approximately sixty percent. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p454]
- The Taiwan Stock Exchange, founded in 1961, grew very slowly with only 102 companies listed by 1980. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p453]
- Most South Korean banks were denationalized between 1980 and 1983. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p454]

## Inferences

- Inference: The varying development trajectories of East Asian stock markets—with Japan's exchange dating to the 19th century while Taiwan's remained small decades after founding—reflect different financial system priorities, where some economies relied more on bank-based rather than market-based financing.
