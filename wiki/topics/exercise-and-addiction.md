# Exercise and Addiction

## Summary

This chapter section presents evidence that exercise serves as both treatment and protection against addiction by engaging the brain's reward system in healthy ways and building alternative neural pathways. The Odyssey House rehabilitation program in New York incorporates running and marathon training as core components, with participants staying in treatment twice as long as non-active residents. Exercise works through multiple mechanisms: it provides alternative dopamine stimulation that competes with drug cravings, reduces stress that triggers relapse, builds self-efficacy and routine that counter hopelessness, and creates new neural connections that override addictive memories. Research shows that even ten minutes of exercise can blunt cravings, and for smokers, five minutes of intense exercise doubles or triples the interval to the next cigarette. Exercise directly counteracts reward-deficiency syndrome, a genetic condition affecting approximately 25% of the general population that reduces dopamine receptor density and increases vulnerability to addiction.

- A 2004 London study showed that intense exercise significantly reduced alcohol cravings in forty hospitalized detox patients, with effects varying by exercise intensity. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p146]
- Exercise can fend off cigarette cravings for fifty minutes and double or triple the interval to the next smoke break. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p146]
- Approximately 25% of the general population has the D2R2 allele that reduces dopamine receptors in the reward center, compared to 70% of alcoholics with cirrhosis and 80% of addicts who also abuse multiple substances. - A rat study showed exercise-and-morphine groups performed better on memory tests than controls, indicating exercise offsets the mind-dulling effects of drugs, and dramatically reduced withdrawal symptoms including wet dog shakes, writhing, and diarrhea. - The prefrontal cortex doesn't fully develop until the late twenties, potentially explaining why most people who become addicted experiment with drugs during adolescence when inhibition hasn't fully developed. ## Inferences

- Inference: Exercise provides inoculation against addiction because engaging in physical activity is directly antithetical to drug-addictive behavior, which involves neglecting physical health, mental acuity, and self-care.
- Inference: The basal ganglia operates on autopilot for established habits; exercise can reprogram this reflexive system by building alternative neural connections that compete with addictive memory pathways.
