# Cerebellum

## Summary

The cerebellum constitutes only 10% of the total volume of the brain but contains more than one-half of its neurons. It performs feedforward control of movement, incorporates internal models of the motor apparatus, contributes to timing control, and participates in motor skill learning through synaptic plasticity at multiple sites including the cerebellar cortex and deep cerebellar nuclei.

## Key facts

- The cerebellum contains more than one-half of all neurons in the brain despite comprising only 10% of total brain volume. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p909]
- The cerebellum is divided into three lobes by deep transverse fissures: anterior lobe, posterior lobe, and flocculonodular lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p912]
- Three pairs of deep nuclei are embedded in the cerebellum: the fastigial nucleus, interposed nucleus (emboliform and globose), and dentate nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p912]
- The cerebellum has three functional divisions: vestibulocerebellum (flocculonodular lobe), spinocerebellum (vermis and intermediate hemispheres), and cerebrocerebellum (lateral hemispheres). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p913]
- The vestibulocerebellum receives vestibular and visual inputs and projects to vestibular nuclei for balance and eye movement control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p913]
- The spinocerebellum receives input from the spinal cord and controls posture and locomotion through projections to the fastigial nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p916]
- The cerebrocerebellum connects reciprocally with cerebral cortex through pontine nuclei and thalamus, participating in movement planning and execution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p917]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cerebellum's repetitive microcircuit architecture suggests it performs similar computational operations on different inputs across all regions.
- Inference: The extensive connectivity between cerebellum and cerebral cortex (motor, prefrontal, parietal regions) explains its involvement in both motor and cognitive functions.
