# Hobbes vs Rousseau Debate

## Summary

The Hobbes vs Rousseau debate asks whether human nature is fundamentally violent and selfish (Hobbes) or cooperative and peaceful (Rousseau). In modern terms, it concerns whether warfare and violence are ancient or recent phenomena in human history. Archaeologists debate evidence of prehistoric warfare (mass graves, projectile wounds), while anthropologists study violence in contemporary hunter-gatherer societies. The consensus leans toward a nuanced view: hunter-gatherers were capable of murder but warfare was rare until the advent of agriculture about 10,000 years ago.

## Key facts

- Lawrence Keeley's 1996 book War Before Civilization argued that archaeological evidence shows warfare is broad and ancient. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p317]
- Steven Pinker's 2011 book The Better Angels of Our Nature argued that violence has declined over the last half millennium while acknowledging ancient warfare. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p318]
- The oldest massacre site is the 12,000- to 14,000-year-old Jebel Sahaba site in northern Sudan, with fifty-nine skeletons, nearly half with stone projectiles embedded in bones. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p318]
- Keeley estimates 90 to 95 percent of tribal societies engage in warfare when not pacified by outside government forces. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p321]
- Fry and Söderberg's 2013 Science review found warfare in only a minority of pure nomadic hunter-gatherers, with 86% experiencing lethal violence. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p334]
- Keeley tacitly concludes that nomadic hunter-gatherers are the exceptions to his 90 to 95 percent warfare estimate. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p337]

## Inferences

- Inference: The evidence suggests warfare was rare until agriculture created surplus, sedentary living, and unequal resource distribution.
