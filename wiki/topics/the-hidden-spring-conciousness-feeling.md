# The Hidden Spring: Consciousness and Feeling

## Summary

This topic synthesises Solms' central thesis that consciousness is fundamentally affective rather than cognitive. He argues that the primary function of consciousness is feeling (affect), not perception or cognition, and that feelings arise from the need to prioritise competing homeostatic demands in uncertain environments. The book connects evolutionary biology, neuroscience, psychoanalysis, and physics through the free energy principle.

## Key facts

- Consciousness is fundamentally affective; its primary function is feeling rather than perceiving or remembering. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p242]
- Feelings evolved from simple affects like 'feeling too hot' to increasingly elaborate forms over deep predictive hierarchies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p195]
- Feelings are fluctuating, existentially valued, subjective states with differentiated qualities and degrees of confidence—the stuff of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p196]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book proposes that consciousness cannot exist before brains evolved, suggesting its origins around 525 million years ago with vertebrate evolution, though rudimentary affective precursors may have existed earlier.
