# Globalization Theory

## Summary

Globalization theory examines the spread of worldwide practices, relations, consciousness, and organization of social life. This chapter covers major theoretical perspectives including Giddens's 'runaway world,' Beck's cosmopolitanism, Bauman's 'space war,' cultural theories (differentialism, convergence, hybridization), economic theories (transnational capitalism, Empire), and political theories.

## Key facts

- Globalization is the spread of worldwide practices, relations, consciousness, and organization of social life affecting nearly every nation and billions of people. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p475]
- Globalization theory emerged partly as a reaction against modernization theory's Western bias and the idea that the rest of the world had no choice but to become like the West. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p475]
- Cultural globalization can be analyzed as either the spread of common codes and practices (homogeneity) or as the creation of cultural hybrids through interaction of global and local inputs (heterogeneity). [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p476]
- Hardt and Negri's 'Empire' describes a postmodern global sovereignty with no single nation at its center but a diffused network of power. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p493]
- Samuel Huntington's 'Clash of Civilizations' thesis argues that post-Cold War conflicts occur along cultural and civilizational fault lines. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p481]

## Inferences

- Inference: Globalization theory represents a major shift in social theory away from nation-centered analysis toward transnational and global perspectives.
- Inference: The central debate in cultural globalization theory concerns whether the world is becoming more homogeneous, maintaining eternal differences, or creating new hybrid forms.
