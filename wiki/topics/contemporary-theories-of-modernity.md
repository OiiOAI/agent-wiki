# Contemporary Theories of Modernity

## Summary

This chapter examines several contemporary theoretical perspectives that continue to see the contemporary world in modern terms, including Giddens's 'juggernaut of modernity,' Beck's 'risk society,' Bauman's analysis of 'modernity and the Holocaust,' Habermas's 'unfinished project of modernity,' and Castells's 'informationalism and network society.' These theorists reject postmodernity and seek to understand the distinctive characteristics of late or high modernity.

## Key facts

- Giddens describes modernity as a 'juggernaut,' a runaway engine that collectively humans can drive to some extent but which threatens to rush out of control. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p452]
- Giddens identifies four institutional dimensions of modernity: capitalism, industrialism, surveillance capacities, and military power. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p454]
- Bauman argues the Holocaust serves as a paradigm of modern bureaucratic rationality and was a 'normal' product of modernity. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p461]
- Habermas sees modernity as an 'unfinished project' with far more to be done before considering postmodernity. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p465]
- Castells examines the emergence of informational capitalism and the network society based on the information technology revolution. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p470]

## Inferences

- Inference: All these theorists share a rejection of postmodernity and an attempt to understand the distinctiveness of contemporary modernity.
- Inference: The theories range from emphasizing risks and dangers (Giddens, Beck, Bauman) to emphasizing communicative and informational transformations (Habermas, Castells).
