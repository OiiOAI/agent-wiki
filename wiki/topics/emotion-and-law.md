# Emotion and the Law

## Summary

Barrett argues that the U.S. legal system is built on outdated assumptions from the classical view of emotion—beliefs that neuroscience has debunked. These assumptions include: emotions are distinct from reason, emotions have universal biological fingerprints, emotional loss of control reduces culpability, remorse can be detected in faces, the dispassionate judge is possible, and emotional harm is less serious than physical harm. The legal system's reliance on these myths leads to unjust outcomes.

## Key facts

- Some people are punished undeservedly and others escape punishment based on an outdated theory of the mind rooted in belief rather than science. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p242]
- The legal system assumes emotions are part of human 'animal nature' causing foolish and violent acts unless controlled by rational thought. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p243]
- The story of emotion as the primitive part of human nature to be controlled by the more advanced rational parts is the 'triune brain' myth whose roots go back to Plato. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p245]
- The law considers emotional damage to be less serious than physical damage, even though emotional harm can shorten telomeres and therefore lifespan. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p264]
- Barrett proposes five teaching points: emotions are not expressed in objectively detectable ways; affective realism colors all evidence; automatic events are not necessarily emotional or outside control; brain structure claims in court are junk science; and emotion stereotypes must not be mistaken for biological reality. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p268]

## Inferences

- Inference: Barrett suggests it may be time to reevaluate trial by jury as the basis for determining guilt and innocence, given that affective realism makes impartial juries impossible.
- Inference: The legal system's view of volition versus awareness of volition reflects a fundamental misunderstanding of how the brain works.
