# Reinforcement Learning in the Brain

## Summary

Reinforcement learning models provide a computational framework linking behavioral and neural data on reward learning. The temporal-difference prediction error theory of dopamine proposes that phasic dopamine encodes reward prediction errors for learning. Different brain regions (ventral vs. dorsal striatum) may implement different RL algorithms (Critic vs. Actor), and distinct value systems (goal, habit, Pavlovian) guide behavior in different contexts.

## Key facts

- RL models identify hidden variables controlling behavior (state values, prediction errors) that can be searched for in brain activity, enabling quantitative comparison between models and neural data. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p329]
- fMRI prediction-error signals in ventral and dorsal striatum have been replicated across multiple studies, supporting the RL framework's validity for brain function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p330]
- Three distinct value systems (Pavlovian, goal, habit) guide behavior based on different neural substrates: amygdala/OFC/ventral striatum for Pavlovian values, dorsomedial striatum for goal values, and dorsolateral striatum for habit values. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p384]
