# The ADHD Debate

## Summary

The ADHD debate centers on whether attention deficit hyperactivity disorder is primarily a biological/genetic disorder requiring pharmaceutical treatment, or whether attention problems are largely environmental responses to stress, trauma, poor nutrition, pollution, and other societal factors. This controversy has significant implications for how society responds to children and adults who struggle to focus.

## Key facts

- Between 2003 and 2011, ADHD diagnoses in the United States soared by 43 percent overall and 55 percent among girls. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p230]
- Thirteen percent of adolescents in the U.S. have been diagnosed with ADHD, with the majority being prescribed stimulant drugs. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p230]
- Professor Stephen Hinshaw claims genetics account for 75 to 80 percent of ADHD, based on twin studies. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p233]
- Critic Dr. Jay Joseph argues that twin studies are unreliable because identical twins experience more similar environments than non-identical twins, which could explain the genetic correlation findings. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p252]
- SNP heritability studies find that approximately 20 to 30 percent of attention problems relate to genetics, a much lower figure than twin studies suggest. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p253]

## Inferences

- Inference: The divergence between twin study estimates (75-80% genetic) and SNP heritability estimates (20-30% genetic) suggests the genetic contribution to ADHD has been significantly overstated.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: There is genuine scientific disagreement about the causes of ADHD; the book notes that serious scientists disagree on even the most basic questions about whether ADHD exists as a biological illness.
