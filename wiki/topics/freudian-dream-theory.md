# Freudian Dream Theory

## Summary

Freudian dream theory, as presented in The Interpretation of Dreams, posits that dreams are the (disguised) fulfillment of repressed wishes, processed through the dream-work involving condensation, displacement, considerations of representability, and secondary revision. Dreams serve to preserve sleep by transforming disturbing stimuli into acceptable imagery. The manifest content of dreams differs from the latent dream thoughts, which are revealed through psychoanalysis. Affects in dreams remain largely unmodified while ideational content undergoes distortion.

## Key facts

- The dream-work consists of condensation, displacement, considerations of representability, and secondary revision. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p388]
- Affects experienced in dreams are in no way inferior to those experienced in waking life in terms of their intensity and reality. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p411]
- Analysis shows that ideational material undergoes displacements while affects remain unaltered during dream distortion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p412]
- Absurdity in dreams serves to represent criticism, ridicule or derision present in the dream thoughts. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p388]
- Judgements appearing in dreams are derived from the dream thoughts rather than representing independent intellectual activity during sleep. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p398]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms by which affects remain unmodified while ideational content is displaced, and whether this principle applies uniformly across all types of dreams.
