# Omega-3 Fatty Acids and Mental Health

## Summary

Omega-3 fatty acids (EPA and DHA) are crucial for brain health and found in oily fish, shellfish, plant oils, flaxseed, walnuts, and algae. Research supports benefits for mood disorders and ADHD, with optimal doses of 1-2g EPA daily for depression and at least 0.5g EPA daily for ADHD.

## Key facts

- For depression, optimal EPA dose is 1-2g daily from pure EPA or EPA/DHA formula greater than 2:1 ratio. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p264]
- For ADHD, optimal EPA dose should be at least 0.5g/day. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p264]
- Fish oil capsules rarely provide therapeutic doses even at maximum label-recommended intake. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p264]
- Mercury was not detected in any of 10 fish oil products tested. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p265]
- Vegetarian sources like hemp seed or flaxseed provide ALA, not DHA and EPA. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p264]

## Inferences

- Inference: Many commercial fish oil supplements may not provide adequate EPA/DHA levels for mental health benefits despite front-label claims of total fish oil content.
