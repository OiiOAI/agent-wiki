# Romanticism and Right Hemisphere Values

## Summary

Romanticism (late 18th–19th century) emerged as a compensatory response to Enlightenment rationalism, representing a shift toward right hemisphere dominance. Its key features include: acceptance that opposites can both be true (coniunctio oppositorum), emphasis on depth over surface, individual difference over generalization, embodied experience over abstraction, melancholy and longing over optimism, and the implicit over the explicit. Romanticism was not a reaction against the Enlightenment but an 'aufhebung'—a preservation and transcendence that included Enlightenment values while restoring balance with right hemisphere qualities. The transition was 'seamless,' not revolutionary.

## Key facts

- For the Romantics, the coming together of opposites into a fruitful union forms the basis of beauty and of truth itself, expressed by Hölderlin as 'the one differentiated in itself.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p487]
- Romanticism is concerned with a 'how'—a whole disposition towards the world—rather than a 'what' of fixed beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p485]
- The sublime expands and extends the being of the beholder: 'every Person upon seeing a grand Object is affected with something which as it were extends his very Being, and expands it to a kind of Immensity.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p500]
- Depth in Romantic art—evoked through half-light, transitional seasons, vast distances—metaphorizes both connection with the Other and separation from it, giving rise to 'pleasurable melancholy.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p498]
- The Romantic appreciation of whatever can be only partly discerned—for unfinished sketches, half-light, twilight scenes, mountains in mist—recruits the right hemisphere's superiority for processing degraded or partial information. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p507]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Romantic emphasis on 'inexhaustibility' and 'unembraceability' as markers of profundity reflects the right hemisphere's engagement with the essentially incomplete, infinite nature of experience as delivered by the right hemisphere.
- Inference: The seamless transition from Enlightenment to Romanticism suggests the seeds of Romanticism were contained within the Enlightenment itself, needing only the right hemisphere's compensatory response.
