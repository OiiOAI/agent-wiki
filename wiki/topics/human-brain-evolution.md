# Human Brain Evolution

## Summary

Human brain evolution represents a unique departure from both general primate patterns and other mammalian trends. Unlike the primate shift (which results from reduced body growth), human encephalization involves disproportionate expansion of dorsal forebrain structures from early fetal development. This quantitative change produced qualitative shifts in brain organization through axonal displacement, ultimately enabling the cognitive capacities required for language acquisition.

## Key facts

- Human fetal brain/body growth follows the primate trajectory prenatally but diverges postnatally through extended brain growth without corresponding body growth extension. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p185]
- Human brains grow as though in a primate with an adult body size exceeding 1,000 pounds, yet our body growth resembles a chimpanzee's. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p186]
- The locus of human brain change was indeed the brain (not body reduction), as evidenced by fossil evidence showing absolute increases in both brain and body size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p185]
- Human cerebral cortex is roughly twice as big as predicted for typical ape brain structures and three times as big as predicted for brain stem and spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p198]
- Human prefrontal cortex is approximately twice the size predicted for an ape brain of human brain size and about six times larger than chimpanzee prefrontal cortex in absolute terms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p235]
- Absolute brain size may have been a limiting factor in language evolution because larger, longer-lived species could employ more flexible and indirect learning strategies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p175]

## Inferences

- Inference: The human brain represents a 'mismatch' of parts: a brain developmental program appropriate for a giant ape combined with a body developmental program appropriate for a large chimpanzee.
- Inference: This 'mismatch' produced unique displacement effects in neural connectivity that may underlie language-related cognitive capacities.
