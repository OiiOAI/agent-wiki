# Exercise and Depression Treatment

## Summary

This chapter section presents the evidence that aerobic exercise functions as an effective antidepressant, matching the efficacy of SSRIs while demonstrating superior long-term outcomes. The landmark Duke University SMILE study found that exercise achieved equivalent remission rates to sertraline (Zoloft) after sixteen weeks, with follow-up data showing exercise patients had significantly lower relapse rates (8% versus 38% for medication). Exercise counteracts depression at multiple levels: regulating neurotransmitters (serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine), increasing BDNF and growth factors essential for neuroplasticity and neurogenesis, improving executive function in the prefrontal cortex, and reducing cortisol levels that otherwise damage hippocampal neurons. The connectivity theory of depression posits that the disorder represents a physical alteration of the brain's emotional circuitry where neuroplasticity grinds to a halt, and exercise restarts this adaptive capacity.

## Key facts

- The 1999 Duke SMILE study found that after sixteen weeks, exercise achieved equivalent remission rates (approximately 50%) to sertraline for major depression. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p103]
- At six-month follow-up, only 30% of the exercise group remained depressed versus 52% on medication and 55% for combination therapy, and among those who achieved remission, 8% of the exercise group relapsed versus 38% on medication alone. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p104]
- Exercise immediately elevates norepinephrine levels in certain brain areas, improving self-esteem and waking up the brain. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p102]
- Chronic exercise increases dopamine storage in the brain and triggers production of dopamine receptors in the reward center, providing satisfaction when accomplishing goals. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p102]
- BDNF protects neurons against cortisol in mood-controlling areas including the hippocampus and is essential for neuroplasticity and neurogenesis; exercise boosts BDNF at least as much as antidepressants. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p109]
- Trivedi's 2006 study showed that patients not responding to antidepressants lowered depression scores by 10.4 points after twelve weeks of exercise, with 5 of 8 completers achieving full remission. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p105]
- The Alameda County Study tracked 8,023 people over twenty-six years, finding that inactive individuals who increased their activity level were no more likely to be depressed than those who were active throughout. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p99]
- A 2006 Dutch study of 19,288 twins showed exercisers are less anxious, less depressed, less neurotic, and more socially outgoing. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: Exercise attacks depression from both directions simultaneously: bottom-up through brain stem stimulation providing energy and motivation, and top-down through prefrontal cortex effects that shift self-concept and restore executive function.
- Inference: The delayed effect of antidepressants (often three weeks) may relate to the time required for neurogenesis to occur in the hippocampus, suggesting exercise's immediate neurochemical effects and gradual structural effects work synergistically.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The authors note that about half of patients in exercise and depression studies drop out, potentially because those who are inactive and hopeless struggle to begin.
- Uncertain: Blumenthal could not conclude definitively whether exercise caused remission or whether less depressed patients were simply more likely to continue exercising.
