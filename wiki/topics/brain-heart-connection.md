# Brain-Heart Connection

## Summary

The relationship between cardiovascular health and cognitive function, where reduced cerebral blood flow adversely affects brain structure and cognition. Research indicates that conditions benefiting the heart often benefit the brain, with cholesterol, dietary fats, and vascular health playing interconnected roles in both organs.

## Key facts

- Reduced cerebral perfusion from cardiovascular disease adversely affects cognition and brain structure in older adults. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- The brain represents only 2% of body weight but receives 15% of cardiac output and consumes 20% of oxygen. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Higher total and LDL cholesterol levels are associated with better memory function in very elderly subjects without the APOE4 allele. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- High total cholesterol levels in late life are associated with reduced risk of dementia. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Changes in cerebral blood flow and carbohydrate metabolism occur during acute hyperketonemia. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p463]
- Dietary nitrate modulates cerebral blood flow parameters and cognitive performance in humans. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p463]
- Oxidized LDL modification is increased in vascular dementia and inversely associated with cognitive performance. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p463]
