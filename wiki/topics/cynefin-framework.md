# Cynefin Framework

## Summary

The Cynefin framework is a decision support framework developed by Dave Snowden that helps determine appropriate approaches based on the type of problem context. It is based on the principle of bounded applicability: there are few if any context-free solutions but many valid context-specific ones. The framework distinguishes between four domains (clear, complicated, complex, chaotic) plus a confused state, each requiring different types of analysis and response.

## Key facts

- Cynefin is Welsh for 'habitat' or 'place of familiarity' and describes the relationship between people and their environment. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p66]
- The framework has four primary domains: clear (ordered, cause and effect visible), complicated (ordered but requires expertise), complex (uncertain, dispositional), and chaotic (unordered, no predictable cause-effect). [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p60]
- In the clear domain, best practice applies; in complicated, good practice; in complex, exaptive practice; in chaos, novel practice. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p61]
- The confused domain is the state of not knowing which domain one is in, and is frequently confused with chaos. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p60]
- Cynefin is part of naturalising sense-making, linking natural science to understanding how humans interact with systems. [raw/books/strategy/Managing complexity (and chaos) in times of crisis Cynefin.pdf#p60]
