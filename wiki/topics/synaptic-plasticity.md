# Synaptic Plasticity

## Summary

Synaptic plasticity refers to the ability of synaptic connections to change in strength in response to patterns of activity. Cajal first proposed in 1894 that learning modifies the strength of synaptic connections. Kandel's work in Aplysia demonstrated that different patterns of stimulation—mimicking habituation, sensitization, and classical conditioning—produce different changes in synaptic strength. This includes homosynaptic depression (weakening with repeated use) and heterosynaptic facilitation (strengthening through modulatory signals), suggesting that synaptic plasticity is fundamental to learning and memory storage.

## Key facts

- Cajal proposed in 1894 that learning modifies the strength of synaptic connections between neurons through increased terminal branches and new collaterals. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p169]
- Jerzy Kornorski in 1948 proposed that learning produces 'plastic change' leading to 'permanent functional transformations in particular systems of neurons.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p170]
- Kandel showed that different forms of learning give rise to different patterns of neural activity and each pattern changes synaptic strength in a particular way. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p171]
- Cajal proposed that learning could change the strength of synapses between neurons, strengthening communication. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p210]
- Kandel's studies showed that learning leads to a change in the strength of synaptic connections between specific cells in the neural circuit. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p213]
- Per Andersen pioneered the modern study of synaptic plasticity in the mammalian brain in 1973. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p197]
- Changes in synaptic strength underlying learning may be great enough to reconfigure a neural network and its information-processing ability. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p215]
