# Women in Early Sociology

## Summary

A group of women formed a broad network of social reformers developing pioneering sociological theories alongside the Chicago school and European theorists like Durkheim, Weber, and Simmel. These women included Jane Addams, Charlotte Perkins Gilman, Anna Julia Cooper, Ida Wells-Barnett, Marianne Weber, and Beatrice Potter Webb. With the possible exception of Cooper, they can all be connected through their relationship to Jane Addams. Their theories are not recognized in conventional histories of the discipline as sociological theory—a testament to gender politics within sociology. Their hallmarks include: emphasis on women's experience as equal to men's; awareness of speaking from a situated, embodied standpoint; the idea that sociology's purpose is social reform; and the claim that the chief problem for amelioration was inequality. These women translated their views into social and political activism that shaped North Atlantic societies.
