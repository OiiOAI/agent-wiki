# Blood Testing Limitations for Nutrient Assessment

## Summary

Blood tests for nutrient levels do not reliably predict who will benefit from broad-spectrum multinutrient supplementation for mental health conditions. Research shows that approximately 72 percent of participants would have been excluded from treatment studies if deficiencies had been used as inclusion criteria, yet many benefited from supplements despite normal blood levels.

## Key facts

- Pre-treatment blood levels of vitamin B12, vitamin D, zinc, copper, folate, ferritin, potassium, sodium, calcium, and homocysteine did not reliably predict who would respond to multinutrient supplements. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p247]
- 72 percent of study participants would never have been allowed into multinutrient studies based on normal blood nutrient levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p248]
- Blood tests measure circulating nutrient levels, not what the brain actually needs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p248]
- Blood levels are typically maintained within narrow ranges through homeostatic mechanisms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p248]
- About 40 percent of those with normal nutrient levels benefited greatly when given broad-spectrum multinutrients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p248]

## Inferences

- Inference: The brain may have higher nutrient requirements than what blood tests measure, or may have localized deficiencies not reflected in systemic circulation.
