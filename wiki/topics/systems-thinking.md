# Systems Thinking

## Summary

Systems thinking is a way of understanding the world by seeing how elements are interconnected within structures that produce their own patterns of behavior over time. It provides the freedom to identify root causes of problems and see new opportunities. At a time when the world is more messy, crowded, interconnected, interdependent, and rapidly changing than ever before, systems thinking helps people reclaim intuition about whole systems.

## Key facts

- Systems thinking is a way of seeing and thinking that gives freedom to identify root causes of problems and see new opportunities. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p17]
- Systems thinkers see the world as a collection of stocks along with mechanisms for regulating levels through flows. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p42]
- Instead of seeing only how A causes B, systems thinkers wonder how B may also influence A, and how A might reinforce or reverse itself. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p51]
- Systems thinkers stop looking for who's to blame and instead ask 'What's the system?'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p52]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book positions systems thinking as complementary to, not replacing, reductionist analysis—each way of seeing reveals different truths about the world.
