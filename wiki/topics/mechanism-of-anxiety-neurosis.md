# Mechanism of Anxiety Neurosis

## Summary

Freud's theory that anxiety neurosis results from the deflection of somatic sexual excitation away from the psychical sphere, with subsequent abnormal employment of that excitation in subcortical reactions manifesting as anxiety symptoms. The sexual process model posits continuous somatic excitation that must reach threshold before becoming psychical libido, and inadequate action leads to accumulation and misdirection of excitation.

## Key facts

- Somatic sexual excitation is produced continuously and must reach a threshold to become a psychical stimulus; the adequate action for discharging this excitation is a spinal reflex act during copulation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p108]
- When adequate sexual action is prevented, somatic excitation accumulates, is deflected into other paths, and manifests subcortically as anxiety. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p110]
- Anxiety attacks represent the isolated and exaggerated forms of the normal copulatory symptoms of accelerated breathing, palpitation, sweating, and congestion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: This somatic theory of anxiety contrasted with the psychical origins of anxiety in hysteria, establishing a fundamental distinction between 'actual neuroses' and psychoneuroses.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledged that his theoretical discussion could only claim 'the value of a first, groping attempt' at understanding the mechanism.
