# Repairing the Damaged Brain

## Summary

This chapter examines why the central nervous system (CNS) of mammals has limited capacity for regeneration after injury, in contrast to the robust regeneration seen in the peripheral nervous system and lower vertebrates. It explores the cellular and molecular mechanisms that prevent or permit neural repair, including the roles of myelin-associated inhibitors (such as Nogo), glial scarring, intrinsic growth programs, and the potential for therapeutic intervention to promote recovery.

## Key facts

- Axotomy divides the axon into a proximal segment attached to the cell body and a distal segment that undergoes degeneration called Wallerian degeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- After axotomy, the distal axonal stump separates from the parental cell body, becomes irregular, and undergoes Wallerian degeneration within days. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- The cell body of a damaged neuron undergoes chromatolysis, characterized by swelling and eccentric nuclear positioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Synaptic terminals that contact a damaged neuron withdraw, and glial cell processes invade the synaptic site. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Injured neurons' inputs and targets can undergo transneuronal degeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1285]
- Santiago Ramón y Cajal famously stated that in the injured CNS 'Everything may die, nothing may be regenerated,' though he expressed hope that future science might change this 'harsh decree.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- The CNS of mammals regenerates poorly compared to the PNS, while the CNS of lower vertebrates shows robust regeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
- Neurogenesis occurs in certain regions of the adult mammalian brain, challenging the long-held dogma that new neurons cannot form in adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1286]
