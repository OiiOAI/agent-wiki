# Toxic Personality Types

## Summary

The text identifies and describes twelve toxic personality types that recur throughout history and can be particularly pernicious because they lure people in with appearances that present weaknesses as positives. These types include hyperperfectionists who cannot delegate, relentless rebels who cannot accept criticism, personalizers who take everything personally, drama magnets who create crises, big talkers who never finish anything, sexualizers with compulsive patterns, pampered princes/princesses, pleasers with passive-aggressive tendencies, saviors who need control, and easy moralizers who judge others.

## Key facts

- The Hyperperfectionist cannot delegate tasks; they have to oversee everything because their need for control stems from dependency issues—they cannot trust anyone. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p156]
- The Relentless Rebel cannot accept any criticism because it reeks of authority; everything must be on their terms and anyone who crosses them becomes the target of vicious humor. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p157]
- The Personalizer's sensitivity really only goes inward—they take everything personally, brood for days, and have long memories for slights. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p157]
- The Drama Magnet learned in childhood that the only way to get lasting attention was to involve parents in large problems, becoming addicted to creating crises. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p158]
- The Big Talker is secretly afraid of effort and responsibility; they talk well and impress people but run away when called to account. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p159]
- The Pleaser has developed niceness as a defense mechanism against harsh parents; behind the smiles and flattery is resentment and they become expert liars. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p160]
- The Savior had to become a caregiver to their own parent in childhood and gains satisfaction from rescuing people—but their need to control emerges if you assert independence. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p161]
- The Easy Moralizer secretly condemns what they are themselves drawn toward; they project negative qualities onto others and thrive in cultures of political correctness. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p161]

## Inferences

- Inference: All toxic types share a common feature: they disguise their weaknesses as strengths, making them difficult to identify until entanglement has already occurred.
