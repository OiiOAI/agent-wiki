# Postural Control

## Summary

Postural control involves two interrelated goals: equilibrium (maintaining stable balance to avoid falls) and orientation (aligning body segments with respect to each other and the environment). The nervous system uses automatic postural responses, anticipatory postural adjustments, and sensory integration to maintain posture through distributed neural circuits from spinal cord to cerebral cortex.

## Key facts

- Postural equilibrium maintains the body in stable equilibrium to resist external forces and prevent falls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p884]
- Postural orientation aligns body segments with respect to each other and the environment, such as maintaining the head vertical. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p884]
- Maintaining balance while standing requires keeping the downward projection of the body's center of mass within the base of support. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p885]
- Automatic postural responses have characteristic latencies of 40-60 ms in cats and 90-120 ms in humans following sudden disturbances. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p888]
- Muscles are activated in synergies (coordinated groups) during postural responses, reducing the number of neural commands needed for balance control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p890]
- Anticipatory postural adjustments precede voluntary movements to maintain stability, with leg muscles activated before arm muscles during pulling movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p892]
- Postural responses use stereotyped strategies including ankle strategy (rotating about ankles) and hip strategy (bending at hip joints). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p889]
- Somatosensory, vestibular, and visual information are integrated to form a body schema used for postural orientation and balance control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p898]

## Inferences

- Inference: The spinal cord alone is sufficient for antigravity support but insufficient for balance control, which requires supraspinal circuits.
- Inference: Dynamic weighting of sensory inputs allows the postural system to adapt to changing environmental conditions, relying more on somatosensory inputs on stable surfaces and more on vestibular inputs on unstable surfaces.
