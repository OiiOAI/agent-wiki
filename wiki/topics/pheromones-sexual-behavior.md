# Pheromones and Sexual Behavior

## Summary

Pheromones are chemicals produced by animals that affect the behavior of other members of the species. In rodents, pheromones detected by the vomeronasal organ (VNO) and main olfactory epithelium (MOE) control partner choice, mating behavior, and aggression. Female mice possess neural circuitry for male sexual behavior that is inhibited by VNO input and lack of testosterone.

## Key facts

- Pheromones are detected by neurons in the vomeronasal organ (VNO) for nonvolatile cues and the main olfactory epithelium (MOE) for volatile odors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1369]
- Mice lacking the Trpc2 ion channel have a nonfunctional VNO and often exhibit male sexual behavior toward members of either sex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1370]
- Female mice possess the neural circuitry for male sexual behavior that is normally inhibited by sensory input from the VNO and the lack of testosterone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1371]
- Humans lack a functional VNO, and most genes for VNO pheromone receptors are pseudogenes in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1375]
