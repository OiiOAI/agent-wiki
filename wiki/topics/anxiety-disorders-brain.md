# Anxiety Disorders and Brain Alterations

## Summary

Anxiety disorders involve alterations in several brain processes according to Grupe and Nitschke's uncertainty and anticipation model: (1) increased attention to threats (hypervigilance) with overactive amygdala; (2) impaired threat-safety discrimination with extinction deficits; (3) increased behavioral and cognitive avoidance; (4) heightened reactivity to threat uncertainty involving BNST, amygdala, hippocampus, and insula; (5) overvaluation of threat significance involving amygdala, NAcc, and prefrontal areas; and (6) disrupted behavioral and cognitive control involving the anterior cingulate cortex. The circuits altered include the amygdala, ventral striatum (NAcc), extended amygdala (BNST), hippocampus, PAG, and prefrontal cortex areas.

## Key facts

- Anxious people exhibit increased attention to threats (hypervigilance), often with overactive amygdala responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p164]
- People with anxiety disorders have impaired discrimination of threat from safety, leading to extinction deficits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p165]
- Excessive behavioral and cognitive avoidance prevents the brain from learning that conditions have changed and threats have passed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p165]
- Anxiety disorders involve heightened reactivity to uncertainty, implicating BNST, amygdala, hippocampus, and insula. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p166]
- The anterior cingulate cortex, altered in anxiety disorders, connects with the amygdala, BNST, and hippocampus and plays a role in behavioral control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p167]

## Inferences

- Inference: Therapeutic approaches that reduce excessive avoidance may work by allowing the brain to update its threat assessments through new learning.
- Inference: The prefrontal cortex-amygdala 'accelerator and brake' analogy helps explain both normal anxiety regulation and its failure in pathology.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise neural mechanisms by which cognitive behavioral therapy normalizes anxiety-related brain activity are still being elucidated.
