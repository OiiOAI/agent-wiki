# Attention and Pollution

## Summary

Growing scientific evidence suggests that exposure to various pollutants—including air pollution, pesticides, flame retardants, and endocrine-disrupting chemicals like BPA—is damaging human attention and contributing to rising rates of neurodevelopmental disorders including ADHD. Barbara Demeneix warns that 'there is no way we can have a normal brain today' due to the cocktail of chemicals surrounding us.

## Key facts

- Professor Barbara Demeneix states that at 'every stage of your life, different forms of pollution will affect your attention span' and that this is contributing to increasing rates of ADHD. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p221]
- Barbara Maher found that living in polluted cities causes 'repeated chronic insult to your brain,' leading to inflammation and eventually damage to neurons. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p222]
- Professor Jordi Sunyer tested schoolchildren's ability to pay attention across Barcelona and found that worse pollution correlated with worse performance. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p222]
- Barbara Demeneix states that about two-thirds of over two hundred pesticides on the market in Europe affect either brain development or thyroid hormone signaling. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p227]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The book acknowledges that some scientists believe these dangers are overstated, noting that the American Council on Science and Health has disputed Barbara Demeneix's claims. However, this group has been funded by chemical and agricultural companies with vested interests in the debate.
