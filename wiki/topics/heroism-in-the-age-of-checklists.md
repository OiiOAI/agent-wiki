# Heroism in the Age of Checklists

## Summary

The redefinition of heroism in complex fields where teamwork, procedure adherence, and discipline are as important as individual daring and improvisation.

## Key facts

- The crew of US Airways Flight 1549 demonstrated an ability to adhere to vital procedures when it mattered most, remain calm under pressure, recognize where improvisation was needed and where it was not, and function as a coordinated team—qualities described as what heroism means in the modern era. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p128]
- Before takeoff, Sullenberger and Skiles—who had never flown together—ran through checklists, introduced themselves and the cabin crew, and did a briefing discussing plans and potential concerns, transforming from individuals into a prepared team. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p124]
- Aviation adds a fourth professional expectation—discipline in following prudent procedure and functioning with others—that is almost entirely outside the lexicon of most professions including medicine, which prioritizes autonomy. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p129]
- The author suggests the resistance to checklists in medicine and finance is visceral, feeling like an embarrassment beneath one's dignity, running counter to deeply held beliefs about how the truly great handle complexity. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p122]

## Inferences

- Inference: The 'Miracle on the Hudson' required not just pilot skill but the accumulated institutional knowledge of thousands of checklist iterations, simulation training, and crew resource management—a collective achievement disguised as individual heroism.
