# Multicultural Social Theory

## Summary

Multicultural social theory emerged closely tied to postmodernism, especially its emphasis on the periphery and its tendency to level the intellectual playing field. Its rise was foreshadowed by feminist sociological theory in the 1970s. Multicultural theory takes diverse forms including Afrocentric theory, Appalachian studies, Native American theory, and theories of masculinity. Key characteristics include: rejection of universalistic theories that support those in power; seeking to be inclusive and empower disempowered groups; lack of value-neutrality; seeking to disrupt the social and intellectual world; lack of clear boundaries between theory and other narratives; critical edge (both self-critical and critical of others); recognition of work being limited by historical, social, and cultural context.

## Key facts

- Multicultural theory rejects universalistic theories that tend to support those in power and seeks to empower those who lack clout. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p118]
- Multicultural theorists are not value-free; they often theorize on behalf of those without power and work to change social structure, culture, and prospects for individuals. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p118]
- Multicultural theorists seek to disrupt both the social world and the intellectual world, making it far more open and diverse. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p118]
- There is ordinarily a critical edge to multicultural theory—it is both self-critical and critical of other theories and the social world. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p119]
