# Mood Disorders

## Summary

Mood disorders encompass unipolar depression and bipolar disorder, characterized by disturbances in emotional state that significantly impact functioning. Unipolar depression involves depressed mood and loss of pleasure (anhedonia) lasting at least two weeks, while bipolar disorder alternates between mania and depression. Both disorders involve dysfunction in brain regions including the anterior cingulate cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus, as well as alterations in monoaminergic neurotransmitter systems.

## Key facts

- Unipolar depression often begins early in life; approximately one-half of cases occur in those younger than 25 years of age. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1455]
- Bipolar disorder generally begins in young adulthood and is characterized by swings between mania and depression, with manic episodes featuring euphoria or irritability, increased energy, decreased need for sleep, and poor judgment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1456]
- The anterior cingulate cortex, amygdala, and hippocampus are consistently implicated in mood disorders through neuroimaging studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1457]
- Depression is the leading cause of disability worldwide. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1456]
- After puberty, depression occurs more commonly in females with a ratio of 1.7:1 in the United States, independent of culture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1456]
- Suicide is the eighth leading cause of death in the United States, with more than 90% of suicides associated with mental illness, depression being the leading cause.
