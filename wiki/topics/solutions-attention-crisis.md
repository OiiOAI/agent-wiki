# Solutions to the Attention Crisis

## Summary

The book explores multiple levels of solutions, distinguishing between inadequate individual approaches (cruel optimism) and more promising systemic changes. Individual strategies like notification management and understanding internal triggers are acknowledged as helpful but insufficient. Systemic solutions focus on changing tech business models through regulation, including banning surveillance capitalism, transitioning to subscription models, or public ownership models similar to the BBC or public utilities.

## Key facts

- Aza Raskin argued that 'We could just ban surveillance capitalism' — prohibiting business models that track users to exploit their weaknesses for advertising. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p170]
- Historical precedents include banning lead paint (which damaged brains and focus) and CFCs (which damaged the ozone layer) — both were widespread but harmful, and both were successfully banned. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p170]
- A subscription model where users pay a small monthly fee would change incentives so platforms work for users rather than advertisers. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p171]
- Tristan proposed immediate changes tech companies could implement: batching notifications into one daily summary, removing infinite scroll, and adding 'who wants to meet up nearby' functionality. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p172]
- Aza said the essential changes 'don't require genius — it's actually technically not hard' to redesign social media once financial incentives align with user wellbeing. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p172]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book positions regulation as the bridge between the problematic present and a better technological future, enabling market forces to serve human flourishing once externalities are properly priced.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How realistic political change is given industry lobbying power remains uncertain.
