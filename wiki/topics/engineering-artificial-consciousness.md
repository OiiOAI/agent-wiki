# Engineering Artificial Consciousness

## Summary

This topic covers the scientific project described by Mark Solms for engineering artificial consciousness based on the integration of psychoanalytic theory, affective neuroscience, and Karl Friston's Free Energy Principle. It encompasses the three-stage experimental methodology, testing criteria, ethical framework, and implications for understanding consciousness.

## Key facts

- Solms argues consciousness can be produced artificially by reverse-engineering its functional organisation, and this is the only way to falsify his hypotheses about why and how consciousness arises. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p266]
- If consciousness can be engineered, Solms predicts it will be done somewhere regardless of his involvement, as the component insights are already in the public domain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p267]
- The approach must be non-commercially funded to prevent exploitation, and the project should proceed immediately to maintain ethical control. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p267]
- Success should be measured by the system's survival in novel unpredicted environments, coinciding with the functioning of a need-prioritisation mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p263]

## Inferences

- Inference: Solms sees this as a race against time - if ethical researchers don't pursue this, others with commercial motives will, risking harmful consequences.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether artificial consciousness can be achieved within Solms' lifetime or the near future remains an open empirical question.
