# Economic Trust Case Studies

## Summary

Fukuyama presents multiple case studies illustrating how trust and its absence affect economic performance. High-trust cases include Mazda and Daimler-Benz being rescued by their traditional banking partners (Sumitomo Trust and Deutsche Bank), Nucor's worker solidarity during recession, Toyota's worker empowerment system, and German factory flexibility. Low-trust cases include southern Italy (where wealthy citizens refused to fund public goods), France (rule-bound workplace relations), and African-American inner-city communities (absence of rotating credit associations compared to Korean immigrants).

## Key facts

- Sumitomo Trust rescued Mazda and Deutsche Bank rescued Daimler-Benz during financial crises, sacrificing immediate profitability to save the institutions. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p17]
- In 1950s southern Italy, wealthy citizens refused to found schools, hospitals, or factories despite capital and labor abundance, believing it was the state's obligation. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p19]
- French shop foreman relations with workers are regulated by rules from Paris because the French tend not to trust superiors to make honest personal evaluations. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p19]
- Small businesses in American inner cities are seldom owned by African-Americans due to absence of strong community and mutual trust, unlike Korean businesses organized around families and rotating credit associations. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p20]
