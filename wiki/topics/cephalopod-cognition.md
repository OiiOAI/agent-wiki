# Cephalopod Cognition

## Summary

Cephalopods (octopuses, cuttlefish, squid) exhibit sophisticated cognitive abilities including perceptual constancies, navigation, curiosity-driven exploration, and complex signaling through dynamic skin patterns. Their nervous systems are distributed throughout their bodies, with arms exhibiting partial autonomous control.

## Key facts

- Octopuses demonstrate perceptual constancies by correctly identifying object size regardless of viewing distance, factoring out retinal image size changes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p81]
- Octopuses navigate using looping paths that return to their dens, traveling through murky water for fifteen minutes or more. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p82]
- Octopuses exhibit a mixed-control system where arms have partial autonomous control from local neural tissue, creating a hybrid relationship between central brain and peripheral action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p84]
- Giant cuttlefish skin contains approximately ten million chromatophores, controlled directly by the brain through neurons, creating a dynamic pixel-like display system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p90]
- Octopus skin contains photoreceptor genes and can independently sense light and change color patterns even when separated from the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p98]
- Cephalopods tend and guard wounded sites, suggesting pain experience, though their response to injury varies with circumstances. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p84]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cephalopod cognition evolved through feedback between their flexible bodies and nervous systems—flexible bodies created evolutionary pressure for better behavioral control, which rewarded larger nervous systems.
- Inference: The distributed nervous system of octopuses means their subjective experience may be fundamentally different from centralized animals.
