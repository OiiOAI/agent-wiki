# Economics Education Critique

## Summary

Munger's critique of academic economics identifies nine main defects: fatal unconnectedness leading to man-with-a-hammer syndrome; failure to follow the fundamental full-attribution ethos of hard science; physics envy causing false precision; too much emphasis on macroeconomics and not enough on microeconomics; too little synthesis within economics; extreme and counterproductive psychological ignorance; too little attention to second- and higher-order effects; not enough attention to the concept of febezzlement; and not enough attention to virtue and vice effects.

## Key facts

- Munger identifies nine main defects in academic economics, including fatal unconnectedness, failure to follow hard science ethos, physics envy, overemphasis on macroeconomics, and psychological ignorance. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p388-422]
- Business school graduates cannot solve simple microeconomic problems because they lack synthesis skills learned through multidisciplinary thinking. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p399-403]
- Economics professors display too little attention to second-order and higher-order effects, as demonstrated by Medicare cost forecasts being off by more than one thousand percent. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p411]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger argues that economics is better than other soft sciences at multidisciplinary thinking but still 'lousy' and needs fundamental reform.
