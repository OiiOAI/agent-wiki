# Central Nervous System Organization

## Summary

The central nervous system consists of the spinal cord and brain, organized into six major regions: medulla, pons, midbrain, cerebellum, diencephalon, and cerebral hemispheres. These regions contain distinct nuclei and fiber pathways that process sensory information, generate motor commands, and support cognitive functions.

## Key facts

- The central nervous system is described along three major axes: rostral-caudal, dorsal-ventral, and medial-lateral. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p389]
- The spinal cord extends from the base of the skull to the first lumbar vertebra and contains dorsal and ventral horns of gray matter surrounded by white matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p389]
- The brain stem consists of the medulla, pons, and midbrain, which mediate sensation and motor control of the head, neck, and face. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p391]
- The cerebellum contains more neurons than any other single subdivision of the brain and is involved in motor coordination, motor learning, and cognitive functions including language. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p392]
- The diencephalon contains the thalamus, which relays sensory information to the cerebral cortex, and the hypothalamus, which regulates homeostasis and reproductive behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p392]
- The cerebral hemispheres consist of the cerebral cortex, underlying white matter, and three deep-lying structures: basal ganglia, amygdala, and hippocampal formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p393]
