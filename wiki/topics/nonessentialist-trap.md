# Nonessentialist Trap

## Summary

Nonessentialist traps are psychological and behavioral patterns that prevent people from eliminating nonessential commitments. Key traps include sunk-cost bias (continuing investment in losing propositions), the endowment effect (overvaluing what one owns), status quo bias (continuing simply because that's how it's always been), and the planning fallacy (underestimating time requirements).

## Key facts

- The endowment effect causes mug owners to refuse to sell for less than $5.25 while non-owners would pay only $2.25-$2.75 for identical mugs. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p138]
- Status quo bias explains why one company used an employee evaluation system for ten years without anyone questioning its origin or usefulness. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p142]
- Zero-based budgeting requires justifying every expense from scratch rather than using last year's budget as a baseline. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p142]

## Inferences

- Inference: These traps share a common feature: they prioritize the past over the future, preventing rational assessment of whether current commitments serve present goals.
