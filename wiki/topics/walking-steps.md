# Walking and Daily Steps

## Summary

Walking is presented as the foundational movement practice for maintaining health, with the authors recommending 8,000 to 10,000 steps per day (ideally 12,000 or more). The chapter covers the health risks of sedentary behavior, benefits of walking including better body mechanics, improved circulation, better sleep, enhanced brain function, and stress reduction, plus practical strategies for accumulating more steps.

## Key facts

- The authors recommend 8,000 to 10,000 steps per day, with 12,000 or more as an ideal target. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1098-L1099]
- Hunter-gatherer ancestors are estimated to have walked 12,000 to 17,000 steps daily. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1068-L1072]
- Americans average only 5,117 steps daily (about 2.5 miles), compared to Australians at 9,695 and Japanese at 7,168. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1076-L1078]
- A 2010 American Cancer Society study found that sitting more than six hours daily increased mortality risk by 37% for women and 18% for men. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1044-L1048]
- Sedentary behavior is defined as activity below 1.5 METs, which should be avoided for more than 30 minutes at a time. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1053-L1056]
- A 2020 study found that 8,000 daily steps was associated with 51% lower risk of death compared to 4,000 steps. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1103-L1106]
- Taking 12,000 steps per day was associated with a 65% lower risk of death compared to 4,000 steps. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1103-L1106]
- The 10,000 steps per day recommendation originated from a Japanese pedometer company in 1965. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1100-L1103]
- Walking helps offset the biomechanical effects of sitting by taking hips and quads out of right-angle positions. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1134-L1138]
- Cartilage receives nutrients via joint movement, which helps nourishing fluid flow in and out. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1142-L1145]
- A 2021 study found that 30 minutes of exercise reduced early death risk by 80% only when sitting time was under 7 hours; no reduction occurred for those sedentary 11-12 hours daily. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1160-L1165]
- Walking increases blood flow to the brain and triggers neurochemical release including serotonin and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF). [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1182-L1188]
- Harvard psychiatrist Dr. John Ratey has called physical activity 'Miracle-Gro' for the brain and compared it to 'a little bit of Prozac and a little bit of Ritalin'. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1188-L1192]
- A Stanford University study found that walking increased creativity by 60% compared to sitting. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1194-L1198]
- 200 minutes of walking per week has been associated with fewer symptoms of depression. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1174-L1176]
- The Walking School Bus is a protocol for gathering children to walk to school, endorsed by the U.S. Department of Transportation. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1218-L1221]

## Inferences

- Inference: Walking is positioned as more important than high-intensity exercise because it addresses the fundamental problem of sedentariness throughout the day, rather than just providing a 30-60 minute workout that can be negated by prolonged sitting.
