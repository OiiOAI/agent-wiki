# Molecular Biology of Cognition

## Summary

The molecular biology of cognition is the study of how molecular mechanisms underlie learning, memory, and cognitive processes. Kandel's work established that similar molecular pathways—cAMP, PKA, CREB—are used for both implicit memory in Aplysia and explicit memory in mice, demonstrating evolutionary conservation. This field bridges molecular biology with cognitive science and psychiatry.

## Key facts

- Despite significant behavioral differences between implicit and explicit memory, some aspects of implicit memory storage in invertebrates have been conserved over millions of years in the mechanisms by which explicit memory is stored in vertebrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p304]
- Several key molecular mechanisms of memory are shared by all animals, including cAMP, protein kinase A, and CREB. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p304]
- This compound approach—extending from molecules to mind—opened up the possibility of a molecular biology of cognition and attention and completed the outlines of a synthesis that led to a new science of mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p317]

## Inferences

- Inference: Kandel's work established a unified molecular framework for understanding both implicit (unconscious) and explicit (conscious) memory storage, suggesting that complex cognitive processes can be reduced to specific molecular pathways.
- Inference: The discovery that similar molecular mechanisms (cAMP, PKA, CREB) operate in both simple invertebrates and complex mammals supports the idea that fundamental biological processes are evolutionarily conserved.
