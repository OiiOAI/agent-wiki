# New Science of Mind

## Summary

A synthesis emerging from the merger of cognitive psychology, neuroscience, and molecular biology that examines mental processes on the biological level. The new science emerged through three mergers: cognitive psychology with behaviorist psychology (1960s), cognitive psychology with neuroscience (1970s), and cognitive neuroscience with molecular biology (1980s).

## Key facts

- The new science emerged from the merger of philosophy of mind, behaviorist psychology, and cognitive psychology in the 1960s. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p22]
- In the 1970s cognitive psychology merged with neuroscience to create cognitive neuroscience, introducing biological methods to the study of mental processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p23]
- In the 1980s cognitive neuroscience incorporated molecular biology, resulting in a 'molecular biology of cognition.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p23]
- Brain imaging technologies—PET and fMRI—enabled scientists to observe brain activity during mental functions in living humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p23]
- The most valuable insights into the human mind in the late 20th century came not from philosophy, psychology, or psychoanalysis alone, but from their merger with brain biology. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p13]

## Inferences

- Inference: The new science of mind represents a paradigm shift from viewing mental processes as inaccessible to experimental science toward treating them as molecular and cellular phenomena open to rigorous investigation.
