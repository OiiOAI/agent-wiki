# Ultra-Processed Foods and Mental Health

## Summary

Ultra-processed foods are nutrient-poor products that displace whole foods from the diet. Research including the SUN study found associations between ultra-processed food consumption and shorter telomere length, suggesting accelerated cellular aging. These foods typically contain excessive sodium, unhealthy fats, and chemicals while being low in fiber and essential nutrients needed for brain function.

## Key facts

- Over 70 percent of the sodium Americans eat comes from packaged, prepared, and restaurant foods, not the salt shaker. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p238]
- The SUN study found a relationship between shorter telomere length and quantity of ultra-processed food consumed by 886 older adults. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p239]
- Ultra-processed foods are nutrient-poor, replacing minerals and vitamins the brain needs with empty calories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p239]
- Chemicals in ultra-processed foods may include PFAS (per- and poly-fluoroalkyl substances), which do not get metabolized or broken down. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p239]
- Improving overall nutrition buffers the effects of exposure to harmful chemicals and protects from many environmental harms. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p240]

## Inferences

- Inference: The absence of nutrients in processed foods means the brain is not adequately fed even when the stomach is full.
