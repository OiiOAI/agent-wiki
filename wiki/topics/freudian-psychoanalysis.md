# Freudian Psychoanalysis

## Summary

The psychological theory and therapeutic method developed by Sigmund Freud, including the structural model of the psyche (ego, id, superego), the theory of the unconscious, and the technique of psychoanalytic treatment. This chunk presents Freud's comprehensive overview of psychoanalytic theory as developed up to 1926, including the concepts of transference, resistance, repression, the Oedipus complex, and infantile sexuality.

## Key facts

- Freud described psychoanalysis as a part of psychology—a depth psychology or psychology of the unconscious—not a specialized branch of medicine. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6458]
- Freud stated that psychoanalysis began by inferring the content of sexual childhood from the analysis of adults, then confirmed these findings by undertaking analyses on children themselves. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6424]
- Freud distinguished psychoanalysis from other psychotherapeutic procedures by its use of interpretation, fighting resistances, and handling the transference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6433]
- The therapeutic aim of psychoanalysis is to restore the ego, free it from its restrictions, and give it back the command over the id which it has lost owing to its early repressions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6415]
- Freud believed psychoanalysis might have applications beyond medicine, including to the study of civilization, religion, art, and the social order. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6454]
