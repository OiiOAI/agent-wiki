# Self-Determination Theory

## Summary

Self-Determination Theory (SDT), developed by Edward Deci and Richard Ryan, is a theory of human motivation based on the premise that humans have three basic psychological needs: autonomy, competence, and relatedness. The theory posits that intrinsic motivation and well-being are enhanced when these needs are satisfied, and diminished when they are frustrated. SDT distinguishes between autonomous motivation (volitional, self-endorsed) and controlled motivation (pressured by external or internal forces). The theory emphasizes that social contexts can either support or thwart these basic needs, with autonomy-supportive contexts promoting optimal development and functioning.
