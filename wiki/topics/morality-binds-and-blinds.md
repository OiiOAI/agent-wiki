# Morality Binds and Blinds

## Summary

The third principle of moral psychology explored in The Righteous Mind, explaining how morality serves dual functions: binding people into cohesive groups and simultaneously blinding them to alternative moral perspectives. This section examines the evolutionary origins of human groupishness through group selection theory, the hive hypothesis (explaining experiences of self-transcendence in groups), and the function of religion as a team sport that enables large-scale cooperation.

## Key facts

- The third principle states: 'Morality binds and blinds'—morality creates cohesion within groups while creating blindness to the virtues of other groups. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p207]
