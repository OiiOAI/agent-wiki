# Motor unit disorders

## Summary

Motor unit disorders are classified by which component of the motor unit is affected: motor neuron diseases (cell body), peripheral neuropathies (axon and myelin), neuromuscular junction disorders, and primary muscle diseases (myopathies). They can be distinguished clinically by pattern of weakness, presence of fasciculations, reflexes, sensory involvement, and laboratory findings including EMG, nerve conduction studies, and muscle biopsy.

## Key facts

- The motor unit consists of a motor neuron and all the muscle fibers it innervates; diseases can affect the cell body, axon, neuromuscular junction, or muscle fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p359]
- Distal limb weakness most often indicates a neurogenic disorder; proximal limb weakness signals a myopathy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p360]
- Fasciculations (visible twitches) and fibrillations (detected by EMG) are signs of neurogenic diseases resulting from involuntary contractions of motor units and denervated single fibers, respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p360]
- Peripheral neuropathies are classified as demyelinating (slowed conduction velocity) or axonal (normal conduction velocity), and may be acute (Guillain-Barré) or chronic. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p362]
