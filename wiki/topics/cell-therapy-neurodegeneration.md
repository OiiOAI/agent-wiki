# Cell Therapy for Neurodegenerative Disease

## Summary

Cell-based therapies for neurodegenerative diseases include transplantation of neural stem cells, embryonic stem cells (ES cells), and induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells. These approaches aim to replace lost neurons or oligodendrocytes. ES cells and iPS cells can be directed to differentiate into specific cell types such as dopaminergic neurons for Parkinson's disease or motor neurons for ALS. iPS cells offer advantages of patient-specific generation without embryo use, potentially avoiding immunological rejection.

## Key facts

- Neural precursors transplanted into the adult brain differentiate into neurons that are more characteristic of the transplant site than their site of origin, suggesting the local environment plays a major role in determining neuronal cell type. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1350]
- ES cells can be directed to differentiate into spinal motor neurons or dopaminergic neurons for potential transplantation in ALS or Parkinson's disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1350]
- iPS cells generated from patient skin fibroblasts can be reprogrammed and directed to motor neuron fate, offering patient-specific therapy that bypasses immunological concerns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1351]
- Transplantation of ES cell-derived oligodendrocyte precursors into injured spinal cords enhances remyelination and improves locomotor ability in experimental animals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1352]
