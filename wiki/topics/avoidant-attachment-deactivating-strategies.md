# Avoidant Attachment and Deactivating Strategies

## Summary

Having an avoidant attachment style makes it difficult to translate verbal and nonverbal signals into a coherent understanding of a lover's mental state. Avoidant individuals train themselves not to care about how the person closest to them is feeling, believing it's not their task and that partners need to take care of their own emotional well-being. This leads partners of avoidants to complain about insufficient emotional support and results in less connectedness, warmth, and satisfaction. Avoidants use powerful deactivating strategies including the phantom ex, the search for 'the one,' nitpicking, and other mechanisms to keep intimacy at bay.

## Key facts

- Avoidant individuals are not good at translating verbal and nonverbal signals during everyday interactions into a coherent understanding of their lover's mental state. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p101]
- Avoidants train themselves not to care about how the person closest to them is feeling, believing this is not their task and that partners need to take care of their own emotional well-being. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p101]
- The eight actions avoidants can take to stop pushing love away include: learning to identify deactivating strategies, de-emphasizing self-reliance, finding a secure partner, being aware of misinterpretation tendencies, making a relationship gratitude list, nixing the phantom ex, forgetting about 'the one,' and adopting distraction strategies. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p105-107]
- People with avoidant attachment overwhelmingly assume their inability to find happiness in relationships has little to do with themselves and much to do with external circumstances like meeting the wrong people or not finding 'the one.'. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p105]
- Avoidants rarely search inside themselves for reasons for dissatisfaction and even more rarely seek or accept help when partners suggest it, making change unlikely without self-reflection or counseling. [raw/books/psychology/Attached_ Are you Anxious, Avoidant or Sec - Amir Levine.pdf#p105]
