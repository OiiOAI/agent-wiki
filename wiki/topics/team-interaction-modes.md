# Team Interaction Modes

## Summary

Team Interaction Modes are three distinct patterns for how software teams collaborate to deliver value: collaboration, X-as-a-Service, and facilitating. Chapter 7 of Team Topologies emphasizes choosing specific interaction modes to enhance software delivery and help teams provide and evolve services to other teams.

## Key facts

- Team Topologies identifies three primary team interaction modes: collaboration, X-as-a-Service, and facilitating. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2191]
- Collaboration is a powerful driver for innovation but can reduce flow. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2193]
- X-as-a-Service helps other teams deliver quickly but only works when the boundary is suitable. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2194]
- Facilitating helps teams avoid cross-team challenges and detects problems. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2195]
- Team Interaction Modes address how teams interact and when to change teams and their interactions. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2213-L2217]
- The chapter emphasizes that arranging teams into patterns alone is not enough for high effectiveness. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2217-L2221]
- Three essential team interaction modes are defined: collaboration, X-as-a-Service, and facilitating. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2300-L2310]
- A combination of all three team interaction modes is likely needed for most medium-sized and large enterprises. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2310-L2320]
- One team might use two different interaction modes for two different teams with which it works simultaneously. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2320-L2330]
- Using a well-defined set of team interaction patterns avoids ambiguity present in many team-software relationships, producing more coherent subsystem boundaries and APIs. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2290-L2300]
- Intermittent collaboration produces better solutions than constant interaction according to research by Bernstein and colleagues. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L2250-L2270]
