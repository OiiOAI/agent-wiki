# Disorders of Consciousness

## Summary

Disorders of consciousness encompass a spectrum of conditions from coma (unarousable unresponsiveness) through vegetative state/unresponsive wakefulness (arousal without awareness) and minimally conscious state (limited but reproducible signs of awareness) to locked-in syndrome (full consciousness with complete motor paralysis). Brain imaging studies by Adrian Owen revealed that some patients diagnosed as vegetative show normal brain responses when asked to imagine playing tennis, demonstrating hidden consciousness. New diagnostic tools including the local-global auditory test and TMS-EEG are transforming clinical assessment of these patients.

## Key facts

- Coma is defined as prolonged loss of capacity to be roused with eyes closed and no signs of awareness, lasting an hour or more. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p254]
- Vegetative state (unresponsive wakefulness) preserves sleep-wake cycles but shows no signs of awareness, with occasional involuntary movements like smiles or cries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p256]
- Minimally conscious state is characterized by rare, inconsistent, limited responses suggesting residual comprehension and volition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p257]
- Locked-in syndrome leaves consciousness intact while paralyzing all voluntary muscles except vertical eye movements and blinking, as exemplified by Jean-Dominique Bauby. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p258]
- Adrian Owen demonstrated that some vegetative-state patients activate normal brain networks when asked to imagine tennis or navigating their home, revealing hidden consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p261]
- Deep brain stimulation of the central thalamus has helped some minimally conscious patients regain stable consciousness in controlled studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p284]

## Inferences

- Inference: The existence of responsive vegetative-state patients implies that clinical diagnosis alone is insufficient and brain-based measures are essential for accurate assessment
- Inference: Deep brain stimulation may work by restoring the vigilance signal that brings cortical activity closer to the threshold for conscious ignition

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact proportion of vegetative patients with hidden consciousness remains unknown
- Uncertain: Why some patients recover with stimulation while others do not is not fully understood
