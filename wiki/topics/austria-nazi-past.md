# Austria's Response to National Socialism

## Summary

Kandel organized a 2003 symposium in Vienna titled 'Austria's Response to National Socialism: Implications for Scientific and Humanistic Scholarship.' The symposium addressed Austria's role in the Holocaust (Austrians comprised 8% of the Reich population but provided 30% of officials working to eliminate Jews, including Eichmann and Kaltenbrunner), its claim to victimhood despite enthusiastic participation in the Holocaust, and the devastating intellectual loss when about 50% of the medical faculty was dismissed for being Jewish. The symposium also highlighted continued difficulties, including the financial struggles of the Jewish Kultusgemeinde.

## Key facts

- Despite claiming victim status, Austria (8% of the Reich population) accounted for more than 30% of officials working to eliminate Jews, with Austrians commanding four Polish death camps. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p412]
- About 50% of the University of Vienna medical faculty was dismissed for being Jewish after Hitler's arrival, and Viennese medicine has never recovered from this 'cleansing'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p413]
- Eduard Pernkopf, dean of the medical faculty from 1938–1943 and a Nazi since 1932, was allowed after the war to finish his Atlas of Anatomy, thought to be based on dissection of concentration camp victims. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p414]
- The number of Jews in Vienna is now small—about 9,000 officially registered with another 8,000 possibly unregistered—compared to the original community. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p417]

## Inferences

- Inference: Austria never underwent the soul-searching and cleansing that Germany did after the war, partly because the Allies validated Austria's claimed innocence to ensure neutrality during the Cold War.
