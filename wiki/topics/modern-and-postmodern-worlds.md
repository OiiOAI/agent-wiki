# The Modern and Post-Modern Worlds

## Summary

This topic encompasses McGilchrist's analysis of how the modern and post-modern eras represent a progressive domination of left-hemisphere modes of apprehending the world. Beginning with the Industrial Revolution's creation of a world in the left hemisphere's 'likeness', through the self-conscious era of modernism with its characteristic hyperconsciousness and fragmentation, to post-modernism's complete draining away of meaning through self-referential discourse.

## Key facts

- Virginia Woolf noted that 'on or about December 1910 human character changed', marking the beginning of the modern era. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p533]
- Modernity is marked by social disintegration deriving from the Industrial Revolution and Comte's vision of society as an aggregation of atomistic individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p534]
- Weber called this the disenchanted world—a world emptied of meaning by rationalistic, technical and bureaucratic ways of thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p534]
- With post-modernism, meaning drains away as art becomes a game where empty signifiers refer only to themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p578]

## Inferences

- Inference: The modern and post-modern condition represents the progressive triumph of left-hemisphere representation over right-hemisphere 'presence' and authentic experience
