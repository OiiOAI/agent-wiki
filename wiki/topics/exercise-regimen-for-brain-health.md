# Exercise Regimen for Brain Health

## Summary

Optimal brain health from exercise requires a balanced regimen combining aerobic, strength, and flexibility training. Low-intensity exercise (55-65% max heart rate, like brisk walking) burns fat and increases serotonin. Moderate-intensity (65-75%) builds blood vessels and releases growth factors like VEGF and FGF-2. High-intensity (75-90%) produces human growth hormone and dramatically boosts BDNF. The recommendation is six days a week: four moderate aerobic sessions plus two sessions mixing high-intensity intervals with strength training. Heart rate monitors help accurately gauge intensity.

## Key facts

- Low-intensity exercise (55-65% max heart rate) burns fat as fuel, pumps up free tryptophan for serotonin production, and changes distribution of norepinephrine and dopamine. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p204]
- Moderate-intensity exercise (65-75% max heart rate) releases VEGF and FGF-2 that trigger production of new blood vessels and encourage neurogenesis within two hours. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p205]
- High-intensity exercise (75-90% max heart rate) triggers human growth hormone (HGH), which burns belly fat, layers on muscle, and pumps up brain volume. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p207]
- A single sprint for 30 seconds generated a sixfold increase in HGH that peaked two hours after the sprint. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p208]
- Interval training with two 3-minute sprints in a 40-minute treadmill run improved learning ability with 20% faster vocabulary word acquisition. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p208]
- The recommended regimen is 6 days a week: 4 moderate aerobic sessions of 30-60 minutes plus 2 sessions of 20-30 minutes with high-intensity intervals and strength training. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p202]

## Inferences

- Inference: HGH is called the fountain of youth because levels naturally decrease to a tenth of childhood levels by middle age, and exercise can spike these levels back up, potentially reversing brain volume loss from aging.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Interval training isn't recommended for those without an established aerobic base; six months of consistent aerobic activity is suggested before adding high-intensity intervals.
