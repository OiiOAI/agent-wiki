# Patterning the Nervous System

## Summary

Patterning the Nervous System describes how the early neural tube becomes subdivided into distinct functional regions. The process involves secreted inductive signals (BMPs, Wnts, FGFs, hedgehogs) that create concentration gradients, establishing transcription factor domains. Rostrocaudal patterning is controlled by Wnt gradients and secondary organizing centers (isthmic organizer, zona limitans intrathalamica). Dorsoventral patterning uses Sonic hedgehog from the floor plate and BMPs from the roof plate. Hox genes specify motor neuron subtypes at different axial levels.

## Key facts

- The neural plate is induced from ectoderm by signals from the organizer region (dorsal lip of the blastopore), demonstrated by Spemann and Mangold's 1924 transplantation experiments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1219]
- The default fate of ectodermal cells is neural differentiation; BMPs from neighboring cells repress this and promote epidermal fate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1220]
- Wnt protein gradients pattern the rostrocaudal axis: low Wnt at rostral levels (due to endodermal Wnt inhibitors), high Wnt caudally from mesoderm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1221]
- The isthmic organizer at the midbrain-hindbrain boundary secretes FGF8 and Wnt1, patterning dopaminergic neurons (midbrain) and serotonergic neurons (hindbrain). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1222]
- Shh acts as a morphogen with concentration thresholds that specify five cardinal ventral progenitor domains expressing different homeodomain transcription factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1225]
- Motor neurons at different rostrocaudal levels acquire distinct identities controlled by Hox gene expression patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1228]
- Mouse Hoxb1 mutations change facial motor neuron fate to trigeminal-type, demonstrating Hox genes determine motor neuron identity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1230]
- The neocortex develops through interactions between FGF8 signaling and cross-repressive transcription factors Pax6 and Emx2. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1233]
