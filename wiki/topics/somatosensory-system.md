# Somatosensory System

## Summary

The somatosensory system transmits information coded by receptors distributed throughout the body, serving three major functions: proprioception (sense of body posture and movement), exteroception (sense of direct interaction with the external world including touch and thermal senses), and interoception (sense of function of major organ systems). All somatic senses are mediated by dorsal root ganglion neurons that perform transduction and encoding of stimuli into electrical signals and transmission to the central nervous system. The system includes mechanoreceptors, thermoreceptors, nociceptors, and proprioceptors.

## Key facts

- The somatosensory system serves three major functions: proprioception for awareness of body posture and movements, exteroception for direct interaction with the external world including touch, and interoception for sense of internal organ function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p449]
- Somatosensory information from the skin, muscles, joint capsules, and viscera is conveyed by dorsal root ganglion neurons innervating the limbs and trunk or by trigeminal sensory neurons that innervate cranial structures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p449]
- Peripheral nerves are classified into functional groups based on axon diameter, myelination, conduction velocity, and whether they are sensory or motor, with Group I axons having the largest diameter and fastest conduction.
