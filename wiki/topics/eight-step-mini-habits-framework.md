# Eight-Step Mini Habits Framework

## Summary

An eight-step methodology for building new habits through mini habits—extremely small versions of desired behaviors that are easy to start and hard to fail at. The steps guide users from selecting appropriate mini habits through implementation to recognizing when a behavior has become automatic.

## Key facts

- Step 1 involves choosing mini habits and creating a habit plan. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L182]
- Step 2 involves using the Why Drill on each mini habit to identify underlying motivations. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L183]
- Step 3 involves defining habit cues—triggers that initiate the habit behavior. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L184]
- Step 4 involves creating a reward plan to reinforce habit completion. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L185]
- Step 5 involves writing everything down—making habits and plans concrete through documentation. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L186]
- Step 6 emphasizes thinking small—maintaining the tiny nature of mini habits to reduce resistance. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L187]
- Step 7 involves meeting your schedule while dropping high expectations to reduce performance anxiety. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L188]
- Step 8 involves watching for signs of habit—recognizing when a behavior has become automatic. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#L189]

## Inferences

- Inference: The framework follows a logical progression: select habits, understand motivation, set triggers, add rewards, document, maintain small scope, manage expectations, and monitor progress.
