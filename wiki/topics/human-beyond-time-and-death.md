# The Human Beyond Time and Death

## Summary

This topic explores humanity's relationship with time across different dimensions: the phases of life (youth through old age), relationships with present generations, connections to past generations, and anticipation of future generations. The text argues that although time is linear and death inevitable, humans can transform their subjective experience of time—making it feel more cyclical, stepping outside the stream to experience forms of timelessness, and not remaining locked in generational perspectives. Key figures cited include Leonardo da Vinci and Goethe as examples of those who transcended their era.

## Key facts

- Time is a human creation, a way to measure its passage, and our experience of it is subjective and changeable. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p676]
- We have the power to consciously slow down or speed up our experience of time, making our relationship to it more malleable than we think. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p676]
- Leonardo da Vinci and Goethe are cited as examples of figures who consciously transcended their era in their experience of time. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p676]
- When young, one can develop more inner distance and premature wisdom; when older, one can retain natural curiosity by dropping smugness. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p677]
- Benjamin Franklin challenged his mind and body as he aged and retained a childlike, jovial disposition into his seventies and eighties. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p678]
- The past is not dead—all that we think and experience is shaped by the struggles of past generations. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p679]
- Early Christians of the first century revolutionized thinking by devotion to ideals; troubadours of the twelfth century introduced romantic love. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p679]
- Goethe anticipated a theory of evolution decades before Darwin and foresaw many political trends of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p681]
- Goethe was able to imagine advances of technology and their effects on spirit, and his prophetic powers were legendary among friends. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p681]
- Goethe stated: 'A man's shortcomings are taken from his epoch; his virtues and greatness belong to himself.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p682]

## Inferences

- Inference: The text suggests that by understanding how the past lives on within us, we can feel connected to an 'unbroken chain' of humanity that transcends individual lifetimes.
