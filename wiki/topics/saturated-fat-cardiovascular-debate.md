# Saturated Fat and Cardiovascular Disease Debate

## Summary

A longstanding controversy regarding the relationship between saturated fat consumption and cardiovascular disease risk. Traditional advice to reduce saturated fat has been challenged by meta-analyses showing no significant association between saturated fat and cardiovascular disease, and re-analysis of the Minnesota Coronary Experiment further questioning established dietary guidelines.

## Key facts

- Meta-analysis of prospective cohort studies found no evidence that saturated fat is associated with cardiovascular disease. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- The Minnesota Coronary Survey (1968-73) tested the effect of lipid lowering by diet on cardiovascular risk. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Re-evaluation of the traditional diet-heart hypothesis was conducted using recovered data from the Minnesota Coronary Experiment. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p462]
- Saturated fatty acids may affect coronary heart disease risk through modulation by replacement nutrients. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p463]
- Reducing saturated fat intake is associated with increased levels of LDL receptors on mononuclear cells. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p463]

## Inferences

- Inference: The re-evaluation of the Minnesota Coronary Experiment suggests that the original conclusions supporting low-saturated-fat diets may have been premature or incomplete.
