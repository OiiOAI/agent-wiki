# Predicting Brain

## Summary

The predicting brain is Barrett's core neuroscientific framework. Rather than passively receiving sensory input, the brain constantly generates predictions about what will happen next. When predictions match sensory input, experiences are created. The brain's most powerful predictions come from body-budgeting regions, which is why affect strongly shapes all perception (affective realism). This framework explains how the same brain can produce vastly different minds depending on cultural context.

## Key facts

- The brain is always a whirlwind of parallel predictions that compete with one another to determine actions and experiences. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p244]
- The two-system brain (quick intuitive process versus slower deliberative one) is an illusion created by a century-old flawed experimental design that disrupts the brain's natural prediction process. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p246]
- All behavior stems from the brain; no human actions, thoughts, or feelings exist apart from firing neurons, so a biological explanation does not automatically release someone from responsibility. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p252]
- The human brain evolved in the context of human cultures to create more than one kind of mind; Western cultures experience thoughts and emotions as fundamentally different, while Balinese and Ilongot cultures do not. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p305]
- The three inevitabilities of the mind are affective realism, concepts, and social reality—all universal based on brain anatomy but variable in content across cultures. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p308]

## Inferences

- Inference: A brain with high complexity and degeneracy brings distinct advantages: it can create and carry more information, is more robust and resistant to injury, and makes survival and gene propagation more likely.
