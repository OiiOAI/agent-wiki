# The Attention Crisis

## Summary

The attention crisis refers to the documented decline in humanity's collective and individual ability to focus deeply. Hari argues this is not primarily a personal failing but a systemic problem being inflicted by powerful forces including Big Tech, consumer capitalism, and modern lifestyle factors. The crisis threatens both individual well-being and democracy itself.

## Key facts

- Professor Joel Nigg suggests we may be developing 'an attentional pathogenic culture'—an environment where sustained focus is extremely hard for all of us. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p17]
- Professor Roy Baumeister—author of the book Willpower—admitted his own control over attention feels weaker than before, and he finds himself playing Candy Crush Saga on his phone. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p14-15]
- James Williams of Oxford warns that losing attention means losing the ability to 'do what matters in any domain—any context in life.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p19]
- Hari argues: 'You are living in a system that is pouring acid on your attention every day, and then you are being told to blame yourself.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p17]
- The book identifies 12 causes: speed/switching, flow destruction, exhaustion, reading collapse, mind-wandering disruption, tracking technology (x2), cruel optimism, stress, diet, pollution, ADHD, and child confinement. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p5]

## Inferences
