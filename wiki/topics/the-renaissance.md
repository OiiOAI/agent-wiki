# The Renaissance

## Summary

McGilchrist interprets the Renaissance as a resurgence of right-hemisphere engagement with the world, characterized by renewed interest in the natural world, the rediscovery of perspective, increased empathy and emotional complexity, and the rise of individuality. The period showed a standing back that enabled fresh perception: in science, looking carefully at things 'as they are' rather than as authority dictated; in painting, seeing what we see rather than what we know. This was marked by harmony and balance between hemispheres, with artistic achievements emerging from their cooperation. However, McGilchrist notes it was not simply a resurgence of the right hemisphere—the standing back itself demanded abstraction and generalisation, favoring the left.

## Key facts

- The Renaissance showed renewed interest in the world at large, a thirst for knowledge of the natural and historical world, with accent on 'how things are' rather than 'how they ought in theory to be.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p413]
- The rediscovery of perspective enabled a view of the world from an individual standpoint, enhancing the sense of the individual as standing within the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p415]
- Shakespeare's characters are 'so stubbornly themselves' that their individuality subverts the stereotyped pattern of their literary and historical sources. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p420]
- The Renaissance saw the first artist to deploy caricature of an individual: Annibale Carracci, who said 'a good caricature, like every work of art, is more true to life than reality itself.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p420]
- McGilchrist notes it was not simply a resurgence of the right hemisphere: the standing back demanded abstraction and generalisation, initially favoring the left. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p414]
