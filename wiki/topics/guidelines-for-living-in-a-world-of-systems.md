# Guidelines for Living in a World of Systems

## Summary

Meadows presents fifteen practical guidelines for successfully navigating complex systems, encompassing attention to system behavior, mental model examination, information stewardship, language precision, holistic perspective, feedback policy design, collective good prioritization, system wisdom listening, responsibility location, learning humility, complexity celebration, time horizon expansion, interdisciplinary defiance, caring boundary expansion, and moral goal preservation.

## Key facts

- The fifteen guidelines are: (1) Get the beat of the system, (2) Expose your mental models to the light of day, (3) Honor, respect, and distribute information, (4) Use language with care and enrich it with systems concepts, (5) Pay attention to what is important, not just what is quantifiable, (6) Make feedback policies for feedback systems, (7) Go for the good of the whole, (8) Listen to the wisdom of the system, (9) Locate responsibility within the system, (10) Stay humble—stay a learner, (11) Celebrate complexity, (12) Expand time horizons, (13) Defy the disciplines, (14) Expand the boundary of caring, (15) Don't erode the goal of goodness. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p227]
- Interdisciplinary communication works only if there is a real problem to be solved and representatives are more committed to solving the problem than to being academically correct. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p215]
- Meadows argues that many Native American cultures considered effects on the seventh generation in their decisions, and that longer time horizons are associated with better survival chances. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p214]
