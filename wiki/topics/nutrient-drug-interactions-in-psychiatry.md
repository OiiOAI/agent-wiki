# Nutrient-Drug Interactions in Psychiatry

## Summary

Combining psychiatric medications with broad-spectrum multinutrient formulas can cause increased side effects, interpreted as nutrients amplifying medication effects. The solution is cross-titration: gradually increasing nutrients while decreasing medications. Research shows that people who cross-titrated benefited far more than those who continued high-dose medications.

## Key facts

- Side effects increase when patients add nutrients to psychiatric medications, sometimes interpreted as nutrients amplifying medication effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p275]
- People who cross-titrated (reducing medications while increasing nutrients) benefited far more than those continuing high-dose medications. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p276]
- Grapefruit interferes with cytochrome P450 enzymes, increasing blood levels of over 85 different medications. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p277]
- Vitamin D and niacin can inhibit various CYP enzymes, changing medication breakdown rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p277]
- Antibiotics can cause microbiome imbalance, reducing nutrient absorption; increasing multinutrient dose during antibiotic treatment may be helpful. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p278]

## Inferences

- Inference: The liver enzyme inhibition by both grapefruit and certain nutrients suggests a shared mechanism for nutrient-drug interactions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise dosing protocols for safe cross-titration in all medication types.
