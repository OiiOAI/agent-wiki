# Self-Compassion

## Summary

Self-compassion in NVC involves evaluating ourselves in ways that engender growth rather than self-hatred. It uses the process of NVC mourning and self-forgiveness after making mistakes, translating 'have to' to 'choose to', and acting only in service to our own needs and values rather than out of duty, extrinsic rewards, or avoidance of guilt, shame, and punishment.

## Key facts

- NVC's most important use may be in developing self-compassion; when we are internally violent toward ourselves, it is difficult to be genuinely compassionate toward others. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p174]
- We use NVC to evaluate ourselves in ways that engender growth rather than self-hatred. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p175]
- The word 'should' implies there is no choice and when used with ourselves, we resist learning. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p177]
- Self-judgments, like all judgments, are tragic expressions of unmet needs. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p178]
- We want to take action out of the desire to contribute to life rather than out of fear, guilt, shame, or obligation. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p184]
- Be conscious of actions motivated by the desire for money or approval, and by fear, shame, or guilt; know the price you pay for them. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p189]

## Inferences

- Inference: The process of translating 'have to' to 'choose to' involves acknowledging we are doing things because we choose to, not because we have to, and identifying the intention behind our choice.
