# People v. Brock Turner

## Summary

In January 2015, Stanford swimmer Brock Turner was caught sexually assaulting Emily Doe by two Swedish graduate students cycling past a fraternity house. Turner was convicted of three felony counts and sentenced to six months in prison. The case became emblematic of campus sexual assault and alcohol myopia—Doe had no memory of the encounter due to blackout-level intoxication, and consent becomes impossible when both parties are drunk.

## Key facts

- Two Swedish graduate students, Peter Jonsson and Carl-Fredrik Arndt, witnessed Turner on top of an unconscious Doe near a dumpster outside a Stanford fraternity. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p109]
- Turner had a blood-alcohol level of 0.171; Doe had a level of 0.249—Turner was twice the legal limit, Doe was three times. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p132]
- Emily Doe had no memory of meeting Turner, dancing with him, leaving with him, or any sexual activity. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p129]
- Turner told police 'I think I was kind of blacked out' during the encounter. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p130]
- Turner was convicted of assault with intent to commit rape of an intoxicated person, sexual penetration of an intoxicated person, and sexual penetration of an unconscious person. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p117]
- Turner was sentenced to six months in prison and must register as a sex offender for life. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p117]
- A Washington Post/Kaiser poll found 18% of college students believed 'does not say no' establishes consent. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: The case illustrates that alcohol myopia makes consent impossible when both parties are extremely intoxicated—neither can properly assess the other's desires or their own judgment
