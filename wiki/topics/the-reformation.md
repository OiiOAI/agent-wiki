# The Reformation

## Summary

McGilchrist interprets the Reformation as representing the slide from right-hemisphere concerns (authenticity, direct experience) toward left-hemisphere dominance (abstraction, dematerialization, systematization). Initially, Luther sought authenticity and return to experience over authority. But his followers took this to mean the outer world was empty, and authenticity lay only in the inner world alone—transforming the visible world from 'presentation' to mere 're-presentation.' The Reformation favored the word over the image, literal over metaphorical meaning, abstraction over particularity, and produced iconoclasm, bureaucratization, and a new emphasis on systematized doctrine. McGilchrist sees the cardinal Christian tenet 'The Word is made Flesh' as reversed: 'The Flesh is made Word.'

## Key facts

- Luther sought authenticity and a return to experience as opposed to reliance on authority, decrying the emptiness when outer and inner worlds are divorced. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p434]
- His followers took this to mean the outer world was empty, transforming the visible world from 'presentation' to mere 're-presentation,' governed by the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p435]
- The Reformation favored the word over the image, literal over metaphorical meaning, and the abstract over the particular—a triumph of left-hemisphere cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p436]
- Images were defaced and replaced by written texts; Koerner notes 'pictures cannot speak' was held against them—a failing being their silence. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p440]
- McGilchrist summarizes: 'the cardinal tenet of Christianity—the Word is made Flesh—becomes reversed, and the Flesh is made Word.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p446]
