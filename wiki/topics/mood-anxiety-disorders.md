# Disorders of Mood and Anxiety

## Summary

This chapter covers mood disorders including major depressive disorder and bipolar disorder, as well as anxiety disorders including panic disorder, PTSD, generalized anxiety disorder, and phobias. These conditions share genetic and environmental risk factors and overlapping neural structures including the amygdala, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex, and insular cortex. Treatment approaches include antidepressant drugs, psychotherapy, ECT, and newer neuromodulation techniques.

## Key facts

- Mood and anxiety disorders frequently co-occur and share overlapping genetic and environmental risk factors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1548]
- All widely used antidepressant drugs increase activity in monoaminergic systems, particularly serotonin and norepinephrine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1559]
- Functional neuroimaging studies have identified abnormally functioning neural circuits in mood and anxiety disorders, particularly within the emotional salience and cognitive control networks.
