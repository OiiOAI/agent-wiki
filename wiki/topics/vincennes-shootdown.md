# Vincennes Shootdown

## Summary

The Vincennes shootdown refers to the July 3, 1988 incident where the USS Vincennes mistakenly shot down Iran Air Flight 655, killing all 290 passengers. The case is widely studied as an example of faulty decision making under stress, though Klein argues the crew was working with inaccurate data rather than decision biases.

## Key facts

- On July 3, 1988, the USS Vincennes fired two missiles at Iran Air Flight 655, which had taken off from Bandar Abbas airport, killing all 290 people aboard. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p110]
- The crew believed they were facing an Iranian F-14 based on a Mode II IFF response, but this identification was mistaken due to a human error where the IFF system correlated Flight 655 with a military aircraft that happened to take off simultaneously. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p116]
- The Fogarty report concluded that stress, task fixation, and unconscious distortion of data may have played a major role, with crew members apparently distorting data to fit a preconceived scenario. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p117]
- The USS Sides, viewing the same picture without being under gunboat attack, correctly identified the track as a commercial airliner, suggesting situational factors were critical. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p116]
- The critical problem was that altitude trend data was not shown on the large screen displays; crew members had to study changes in four-digit numbers on a separate monitor during 189 seconds of decision time. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p119]
- Roberts and Dotterway (1995) concluded that due to a track number recycling error, some crew members were monitoring a descending U.S. Navy A-6 several hundred miles away instead of the ascending Airbus. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p119]

## Inferences

- Inference: Klein argues against the 'decision bias' explanation, noting that if the Vincennes had not fired and had been attacked, researchers would have claimed they suffered from 'base rate neglect'—demonstrating that the bias framework can explain any outcome post hoc.
- Inference: The case demonstrates how computer interface design can contribute to errors: the altitude information format made it difficult to track trends under stress.
