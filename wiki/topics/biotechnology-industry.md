# Biotechnology Industry

## Summary

The biotechnology industry emerged in the 1970s-1980s from advances in recombinant DNA technology, transforming both academic science and pharmaceutical development. Companies like Genentech, Biogen, and Memory Pharmaceuticals translated basic research into therapeutic applications for neurological and psychiatric disorders, while also creating new career pathways for scientists.

## Key facts

- In 1976, Robert Swanson persuaded Herbert Boyer to form Genentech, the first biotechnology company focused on commercializing genetically engineered proteins; the company is now worth about $20 billion. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p329]
- By 1980, Genentech successfully produced human insulin, establishing the usefulness of genetic engineering for medicine and triggering widespread change in academic attitudes toward commercialization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p332]
- The biotechnology industry transformed academic attitudes toward commercialization; academics found that science could lead to medically useful drugs and that they could do well financially by doing good. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p332]
- The biotechnology industry now stands as a parallel career pathway for scientists; since the quality of science at the best companies is very high, scientists will move freely between academic science and the biotechnology industry. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p342]

## Inferences

- Inference: The biotechnology revolution created a new model for translating basic scientific discoveries into clinical applications, fundamentally changing the relationship between academia and industry.
- Inference: The emergence of biotech companies focused on cognitive enhancement raises ethical questions about cognitive enhancement in normal people that society must address.
