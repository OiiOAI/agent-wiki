# Entrepreneurial Profitability

## Summary

Entrepreneurial profitability refers to the challenge and practice of small business owners actually retaining profit from their operations. The book argues that approximately 80% of businesses fail, with lack of profitability being the primary reason cited for business discontinuation. The core problem is the traditional accounting formula that treats profit as whatever remains after expenses, combined with human tendencies to spend available resources.

## Key facts

- The Small Business Administration states there are 28 million small businesses in the United States generating $25 million or less in annual revenue. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p16]
- Globally, the number of small businesses exceeds 125 million. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p16]
- Eight out of ten businesses fail, with lack of profitability being the number one reason cited for business discontinuation according to Babson College research. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p17]
- Most entrepreneurs live check-to-check and panic-to-panic with their businesses despite growing revenue. [raw/books/business/Profit First - Mike Michalowicz.pdf#p35]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prevalence of check-to-check business operations suggests that traditional accounting education and advice fails to account for human behavioral tendencies around money
