# Exercise and Anxiety Treatment

## Summary

This chapter section presents research evidence demonstrating that aerobic exercise effectively treats both the state and trait components of anxiety disorders. High-intensity exercise works more quickly than low-intensity exercise in reducing anxiety sensitivity by teaching the brain that physical arousal symptoms (elevated heart rate, rapid breathing) do not necessarily lead to anxiety attacks. The mechanisms include reduced muscle tension interrupting the anxiety feedback loop, increased production of calming neurotransmitters (serotonin, GABA), elevated BDNF supporting neural plasticity, and ANP secretion from heart muscle cells that modulates the HPA axis. A landmark 1997 randomized controlled trial by Andreas Broocks compared exercise directly to the antidepressant clomipramine for panic disorder, finding both treatments achieved equivalent remission rates by ten weeks, with exercise showing superior long-term maintenance at six-month follow-up.

## Key facts

- In Broman-Fulks' 2004 study, high-intensity exercise (60-90% max heart rate) reduced anxiety sensitivity more effectively than low-intensity exercise (50% max heart rate), with effects appearing after just the second session. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p79]
- A 2005 study of 198 Chilean high school students found that those who exercised three 90-minute sessions per week showed 14% improvement in anxiety scores versus 3% for controls, alongside 8.5% versus 1.8% fitness improvements. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p80]
- Exercise produces calming neurochemical changes: free fatty acids from fat metabolism compete with tryptophan for transport proteins, increasing tryptophan entry to the brain where it synthesizes serotonin. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p79]
- Atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP), produced by heart muscle cells during exercise, reduces panic attacks by modulating the HPA axis and dampening the sympathetic nervous system. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p88]
- In Broocks' 1997 ten-week randomized controlled trial, exercise achieved equivalent remission rates to clomipramine for panic disorder, with exercise patients showing lower relapse rates at six-month follow-up. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p85]
- In Ströhle's 2005 study, thirty minutes of treadmill running significantly reduced panic attacks compared to quiet rest, by a ratio of two to one. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p86]
- People with anxiety disorders show normal amygdala response to genuinely frightening stimuli but cannot discriminate between danger and safety when responding to nonthreatening stimuli. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p82]

## Inferences

- Inference: The seven mechanisms by which exercise combats anxiety (distraction, reduced muscle tension, building brain resources, teaching different outcomes, rerouting circuits, improving resilience, and setting free) collectively address both the acute state of anxiety and the underlying trait.
- Inference: The combination of medication for immediate safety and exercise for fundamental change represents an optimal treatment strategy, particularly for panic disorder.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: While ANP is strongly implicated as a link between exercise and reduced anxiety, the authors note correlations do not equal causality.
