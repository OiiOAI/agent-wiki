# Patient D.F.

## Summary

D.F. was a patient with visual agnosia following damage to the ventral visual stream (inferotemporal cortex). She was unable to consciously perceive object properties such as orientation but could perform accurate visuomotor actions like posting a card through a slot. This case provided key evidence for the dual-stream hypothesis of visual processing.

## Key facts

- Patient D.F. developed visual agnosia after damage to the ventral stream projecting to the inferotemporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- D.F. was unable to indicate the orientation of a slot either verbally or with her hand when asked to perform a perceptual judgment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- However, when asked to perform a simple action like putting a card through the slot, D.F. oriented her hand appropriately without difficulty. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
- This double dissociation between perception and action supports the idea that dorsal and ventral visual streams process information for different purposes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p724]
