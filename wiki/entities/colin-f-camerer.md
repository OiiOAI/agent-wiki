# Colin F. Camerer

## Summary

Colin F. Camerer is a behavioral economist at Caltech known for pioneering behavioral game theory and its integration with neuroscience. His work on cognitive hierarchy models, eye-tracking in strategic games, and neural correlates of decision making has been foundational to neuroeconomics. He authored the seminal book 'Behavioral Game Theory: Experiments on Strategic Interaction' (2003).

## Key facts

- Camerer developed the cognitive hierarchy model of games, which assumes heterogeneous bounded rationality and limited steps of strategic thinking across players. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p195]
- Camerer's experience-weighted attraction (EWA) learning model provides a unified framework incorporating reinforcement learning and belief-based learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p200]
- Camerer's research shows that using eye-tracking and pupil dilation can predict truthful communication in sender-receiver games. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p203]
- Camerer and Ho developed the cognitive hierarchy model showing that choices in p-beauty contest games are better predicted by limited reasoning than by Nash equilibrium. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p196]
