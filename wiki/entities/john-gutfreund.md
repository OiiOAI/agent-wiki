# John Gutfreund

## Summary

Former CEO of Salomon Inc. who failed to promptly report trading improprieties to federal authorities, leading to the firm's near-collapse in 1991. Munger uses his case to illustrate the failure of the general counsel's appeals to morality versus self-interest.

## Key facts

- Salomon's general counsel knew that CEO Gutfreund should have promptly told the federal authorities about trading improprieties, but Gutfreund delayed because the task was unpleasant. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p278]
- The general counsel had very little constituency within Salomon except for the CEO—if the CEO went down, the general counsel was going down with him, so his career was on the line. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p278]
- The general counsel lost his job because he didn't apply elementary psychology; he failed to recognize that what works best is to appeal to a man's interest rather than to morality. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p279]
- CEOs don't like the idea of being ruined, disgraced, and fired, and appealing to these self-interests would have worked to get Gutfreund to do the right thing. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p279]
