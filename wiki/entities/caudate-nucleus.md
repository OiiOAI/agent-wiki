# Caudate Nucleus

## Summary

The caudate nucleus is a brain structure located deep in the center of the brain that functions as the 'automatic gearshift,' allowing thoughts to flow from one to the next. In OCD, the caudate becomes extremely 'sticky' and does not shift gears properly, preventing the normal disengagement from obsessive thoughts and compulsions. Brain scans show that the caudate in OCD patients is overactive because it is stuck and being inundated with signals from the orbital frontal cortex.

## Key facts

- The caudate nucleus sits deep in the center of the brain and serves as the 'automatic gearshift,' allowing thoughts to flow from one to the next. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- In OCD, the caudate becomes extremely 'sticky' and does not shift gears automatically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- Because the caudate doesn't shift the gear automatically, the orbital frontal cortex and the cingulate continue to fire off their signals, increasing the mistake feeling and anxiety. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- The malfunctioning caudate is probably overactive because it is stuck and still being inundated with signals from the orbital frontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- OCD can be caused by infections that swell the caudate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p177]
- Schwartz wondered whether patients could shift the caudate 'manually' by paying constant, effortful attention and actively focusing on something besides the worry. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p177]
- Each time patients try to shift gears, they begin fixing their 'transmission' by growing new circuits and altering the caudate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p180]
