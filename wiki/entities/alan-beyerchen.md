# Alan Beyerchen

## Summary

Alan Beyerchen is a scholar who produced a path-breaking study titled 'Clausewitz, Nonlinearity, and the Unpredictability of War' (1992) that appears on Boyd's reading list. Beyerchen emphasizes the relevance of the new sciences for the study of war, arguing that Clausewitz implicitly understood war as a non-linear phenomenon despite using mechanical Newtonian metaphors.

## Key facts

- Beyerchen's 1992 study 'Clausewitz, Nonlinearity, and the Unpredictability of War' is described as path-breaking and appears on Boyd's reading list. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]
- Beyerchen argues that Clausewitz understood war is inherently a non-linear phenomenon, perceiving and articulating it as an energy-consuming phenomenon involving competing and interactive factors. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p170]
- Beyerchen asserts that Clausewitz had a profound sense of how understanding of phenomena is truncated by the bounds placed on them for analytical convenience. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p173]
- According to Beyerchen, the core cause of analytical unpredictability in war is the very nature of interaction itself. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: Beyerchen's work provided intellectual validation for Boyd's own views in which fundamental uncertainty plays a dominant role, helping convince Boyd of the soundness of his approach.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Beyerchen's interpretation that Clausewitz 'understood' non-linearity reflects Clausewitz's explicit intent or represents retroactive theoretical attribution.
