# Queen Elizabeth I

## Summary

Queen Elizabeth I (1533-1603) ruled England from 1558 until her death, transforming a weakened, debt-ridden nation torn by religious conflict into a prosperous European power. Facing the 'entitlement curse' as a young, inexperienced female ruler with no political credibility, she deliberately rejected the sense of entitlement typical of monarchs and instead earned trust through relentless work, personal sacrifice, and deep empathy with her ministers and subjects. Her mastery of authority—balancing vision with empathy, taming rivals like Robert Essex, and outmaneuvering Philip II of Spain—demonstrates the principles of authentic leadership detailed in Greene's framework.

## Key facts

- Elizabeth was twenty-five years old at her coronation on January 14, 1559, with England in bad financial shape, heavily in debt, and recovering from civil war between Catholics and Protestants. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p536]
- Her mother Anne Boleyn was executed by Henry VIII on trumped-up charges when Elizabeth was not yet three years old. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p536]
- Queen Mary I imprisoned Elizabeth in the Tower of London in 1554 on charges of conspiring against the Crown, nearly having her executed. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p536]
- Elizabeth chose William Cecil as her most trusted minister, who was thirteen years older and had served under her half-brother Edward VI. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p538]
- Cecil planned to slowly take over power and arrange Elizabeth's marriage to a foreign prince who would rule as king. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p538]
- Elizabeth worked past midnight daily, was completely hands-on, and knew more about the country's financial state than her ministers. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p539]
- Over years, Elizabeth played potential suitors (France, Habsburgs) against each other to secure English interests without ever marrying. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p540]
- Mary, Queen of Scots was beheaded on February 8, 1587, after being convicted of treason for her involvement in plots to assassinate Elizabeth. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p542]
- The Spanish Armada was defeated in July 1588; the English lost no ships and had only around a hundred casualties while Spain lost most of its fleet and over twenty thousand soldiers. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p544]
- Pope Sixtus V exclaimed upon the Armada's defeat: 'Just look how well she governs! She is only a woman, only mistress of half an island, and yet she makes herself feared by Spain, by France, by the Empire, by all!'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p544]
- Robert Devereux, 2nd Earl of Essex, became Elizabeth's favorite but later attempted a coup in February 1601, leading to his execution for treason. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p547]
- Elizabeth adopted the opposite of entitlement—feeling no sense of privilege, keeping her weakness in mind, earning trust through action, and demonstrating she was not selfish. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p549]
- She practiced what she preached, reducing court expenditures and sacrificing her own income, setting the highest standards for herself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p549]
- Her authority derived from earning credibility rather than demanding it, balancing vision with empathy. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p559]
