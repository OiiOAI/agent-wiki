# Little Hans

## Summary

Little Hans was a five-year-old boy whose phobia of horses was analyzed by his father under Sigmund Freud's guidance. The case, published by Freud in 1909, provided early clinical evidence of the Oedipus complex, castration anxiety, and the mechanisms of anxiety hysteria in childhood. Hans developed intense fear of horses, particularly of them biting or falling down, which was later understood as representing his conflicted feelings toward his father and his sexual theories about childbirth.

## Inferences

- Inference: Little Hans's case provided clinical evidence for Freud's theory of the Oedipus complex in childhood, demonstrating that the child desired his mother and wished harm to his father.
- Inference: The case showed that childhood phobias serve to displace and disguise unconscious conflicts, particularly those related to the Oedipal situation.
- Inference: Hans's analysis demonstrated how sexual theories in children (such as babies being like 'lumf') arise from incomplete knowledge and fantasy.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud noted uncertainty about whether Hans had overheard his parents having intercourse, which might explain some of his sadistic impulses.
- Uncertain: The exact factors that triggered the transformation of libido into anxiety remain unclear—possibly intellectual inability to solve reproductive questions, or constitutional intolerance of sexual excitement.
