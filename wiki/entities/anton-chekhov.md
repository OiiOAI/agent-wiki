# Anton Chekhov

## Summary

Anton Chekhov (1860-1904) was a Russian playwright and short story writer who overcame a brutal childhood marked by physical abuse from his father, family abandonment, and poverty to become one of Russia's most celebrated writers. His case study in Greene's work illustrates the transformation from a constricted, fearful attitude to an expansive, generous one through self-awareness and work.

## Key facts

- Chekhov faced near-daily physical beatings from his father Pavel Yegorovich, who justified the abuse as divine will and necessary for humility. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p253]
- In 1875, Chekhov's older brothers left for Moscow, and his father fled to avoid bankruptcy, leaving 16-year-old Anton alone in Taganrog to fend for himself. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p255]
- Chekhov survived by hiring himself out as a tutor to multiple families, often going hungry for days and living in a single corner of a room. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p255]
- Chekhov experienced a transformative epiphany when he analyzed his father's history, realizing his father had also been a victim of harsh treatment, leading to sudden feelings of empathy and love that freed him from resentment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p257]
- Chekhov moved his entire family from a cramped basement tenement to a larger apartment with a view, funded by his medical school scholarship and careful budgeting. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p258]
- In 1890, Chekhov undertook a three-month journey to Sakhalin Island to investigate prison conditions, immersing himself in interviews with hardened criminals and witnessing severe torture and degradation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p259-260]
- Chekhov was diagnosed with tuberculosis around 1884 but refused to retire or change his lifestyle, continuing to write and practice medicine until his death in 1904. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p260]

## Inferences

- Inference: Chekhov's transformation from constricted to expansive attitude was triggered by crisis (being left alone) and deliberate self-analysis of his father's behavior.
- Inference: Greene uses Chekhov as a paradigm for how anyone can alter their attitude through understanding and acceptance rather than repression or bitterness.
