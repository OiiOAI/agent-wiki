# Williams Syndrome

## Summary

Williams syndrome is a rare genetic condition caused by heterozygous deletion of chromosome region 7q11.23 (about 1.5 Mb and 27 genes). Individuals typically exhibit normal language, extreme sociability, lack of reticence with strangers, profound impairment in spatial processing, overall intellectual disability, and high rates of anxiety. The patterns of impairment suggest that language and social skills can be separated from other brain functions.

- The disorder occurs in 1 in 10,000 live births and is characterized by mild to moderate intellectual disability, connective tissue abnormalities, and cardiovascular defects. - The behavioral phenotype includes increased sociability, preserved language abilities, and affinity for music, contrasting with impaired visuospatial capabilities. - Individuals with Williams syndrome display a nearly universal loss of reticence with strangers but have high general anxiety and isolated phobias. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1532]
