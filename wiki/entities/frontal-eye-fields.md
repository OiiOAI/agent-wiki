# Frontal eye fields

## Summary

The frontal eye fields (FEF) are a brain region in the frontal cortex that functions as a topographic map of eye movements, similar to LIP and superior colliculus. FEF projects to the superior colliculus and is reciprocally connected with other areas in the saccadic control system. Activity in FEF is related to the probability that a movement will be executed, suggesting it encodes RSV signals for choice.

## Key facts

- Frontal eye fields consist of a roughly topographic map broadly similar in function to LIP and SC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Frontal eye fields project both to the superior colliculus and to other areas in the saccadic system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- FEF activity is related to the probability that a movement will be executed by the animal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]

## Inferences

- Inference: FEF likely encodes RSV signals similar to LIP, participating in the decision-making circuit for saccadic choices.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The specific role of FEF relative to LIP in the decision process remains to be fully distinguished.
