# Camillo Golgi

## Summary

Camillo Golgi (1843-1926) was an Italian physician who developed the Golgi staining method using silver salts to reveal entire nerve cell structures under the microscope. He initially concluded that nerve cells act together in a continuous web or syncytium, opposing Ramón y Cajal's neuron doctrine.

## Key facts

- Golgi developed a method of staining neurons with silver salts that revealed their entire cell structure under the microscope. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p8]
- Based on his studies, Golgi concluded that nerve cells are not independent cells isolated from one another but act together in one continuous web of tissue or syncytium. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p8]
- Camillo Golgi was an Italian neuroscientist who invented a technique for staining brain tissue in 1873. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p704]
- The Golgi stain stained all branches of dendrites and axon terminals of single neurons without spreading to adjacent neurons. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p704]
