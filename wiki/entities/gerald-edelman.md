# Gerald Edelman

## Summary

Gerald Edelman (1929-2014) was an American biologist and neuroscientist who proposed the theory of 'neural Darwinism' (1987), framing global brain functions on Darwinian-like theory of neuronal group interactions. Unlike Terrence Deacon's approach, Edelman assumed that the entire source of informational variety available during cognition derives from early nonspecificity of axonal and dendritic growth.

## Key facts

- Edelman proposed the theory of 'neural Darwinism' in 1987, framing global brain functions on Darwinian-like theory of neuronal group interactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Edelman hypothesized that initial nonspecificity of connections provides the total initial variety upon which selection processes in the form of learning can later act. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Edelman assumed that the entire source of informational variety available during cognition derives from the early nonspecificity of axonal and dendritic growth. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
