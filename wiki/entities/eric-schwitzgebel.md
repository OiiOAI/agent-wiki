# Eric Schwitzgebel

## Summary

A philosopher who empirically tested whether moral philosophers are more virtuous than other academics. His surveys and surreptitious methods measured charity giving, voting, calling mothers, blood and organ donation, and conference cleanup behavior. He found moral philosophers behave no better than other philosophers on any measure. He also scrounged library missing-book lists and found ethics books more likely to be stolen than other philosophy books.

## Key facts

- Schwitzgebel measured how often moral philosophers give to charity, vote, call their mothers, donate blood, donate organs, and respond to student emails. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p105]
- Moral philosophers are no better than other philosophers or professors on any of these behavioral measures. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p105]
- Academic books on ethics are more likely to be stolen or never returned from libraries than books in other areas of philosophy. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p106]
- Schwitzgebel has found no single measure on which moral philosophers behave better than other philosophers. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p106]
