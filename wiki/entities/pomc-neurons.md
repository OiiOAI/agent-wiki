# POMC Neurons

## Summary

POMC (proopiomelanocortin) neurons are satiety-promoting neurons in the arcuate nucleus that decrease hunger and stimulate sympathetically-driven energy expenditure. They release α-MSH, which activates MC4R receptors on downstream PVH neurons, causing satiety. POMC neurons are antagonistically opposed to AgRP neurons in the neural circuits controlling energy balance. Genetic deficiency of POMC causes massive obesity.

## Key facts

- POMC neurons release α-MSH, which activates MC4R receptors on PVH satiety neurons, decreasing hunger. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1084]
- POMC neurons project to both the paraventricular hypothalamus (controlling hunger) and sympathetic preganglionic neurons in the spinal cord (controlling energy expenditure). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1084]
- Fasting inhibits POMC neurons while feeding activates them, opposite to the effects on AgRP neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1084]
- Genetic deficiency of POMC protein causes massive obesity in humans and mice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1084]
