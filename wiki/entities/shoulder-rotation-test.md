# Shoulder Rotation Test

## Summary

A subjective assessment test for shoulder mobility where the individual lies faceup, places arms at 90-degree angles, and presses the backs of their wrists into the floor while breathing normally. The test gauges whether shoulder rotation allows sufficient power generation rather than measuring absolute strength.

## Key facts

- The test requires lying faceup with knees bent, feet flat, arms out to side with elbows at 90 degrees, palms facing up. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1315-L1317]
- The individual rolls shoulders back in sockets and pushes backs of wrists down for five inhales and exhales. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1317-L1318]
- The test is not a measure of strength but rather a gauge of whether rotation allows power generation. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1308-L1310]
- The authors recommend retesting immediately after first Rotator Cuff Mobilization, then after one week of practice. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1323-L1325]
