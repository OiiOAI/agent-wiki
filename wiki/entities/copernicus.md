# Copernicus

## Summary

Carse uses Copernicus as an illustration of how myth provokes explanation. Though Copernicus's heliocentric theory projected an order into the heavens that remained unchallenged, Carse argues that what Copernicus dispelled were not myths but other explanations. The story in Copernicus's stating of facts—the ancient saga of the solitary wanderer, the peregrinus who risks anything for the sake of surprise—is the myth. Copernicus was a traveler who went with a hundred pairs of eyes, daring to look again at all that is familiar in hope of vision. What resounds most deeply in his life is the journey that made knowledge possible, not the knowledge that made the journey successful.
