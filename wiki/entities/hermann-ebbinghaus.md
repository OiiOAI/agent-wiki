# Hermann Ebbinghaus

## Summary

Hermann Ebbinghaus (1850-1909) was a German psychologist who pioneered the experimental study of memory. His 1885 monograph on forgetting established foundational methodology and revealed the famous forgetting curve.

## Key facts

- Ebbinghaus encountered Fechner's experimental methods in a Parisian bookstore in the late 1870s, inspiring him to apply scientific methods to studying memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p29]
- He tested his own memory for thousands of nonsense syllables over intervals from one hour to one month. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p29]
- His 1885 monograph 'Über das Gedächtnis' shaped the field of memory research for decades. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p29]
- Ebbinghaus pioneered the scientific study of memory in the late 19th century. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p16]
- In his 1885 treatise On Memory, Ebbinghaus could only memorize 64 trigrams in 45 minutes before exhaustion, headache, and other symptoms set in. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p17]
- Ebbinghaus discovered the forgetting curve showing that half of learned trigrams were forgotten within 20 minutes and two-thirds within a day. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p17]
