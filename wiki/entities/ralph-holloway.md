# Ralph Holloway

## Summary

Ralph Holloway is a pioneering figure in the analysis of fossil hominid brain casts at Columbia University. He is cited by Deacon as questioning the assumption that intelligence or brain size is a simple unitary trait, arguing that the brain cannot be treated as a 'unitary organ with a simple behavioral task to accomplish such as intelligence, language, adaptive behavior.' He also noted that human primary visual cortex is unexpectedly small relative to brain size.

## Key facts

- Holloway wonders 'how can we imagine that the brain is some unitary organ with a simple behavioral task to accomplish' and advocates looking to neurobiological details. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p157]
- Holloway was among the first researchers to notice that human primary visual cortex occupies less surface than predicted for a primate brain of human size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p232]
- Holloway (1979) showed that human visual areas were disproportionately smaller than predictions extrapolated from the trend observed in other primates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p515]
- Holloway interpreted a posterior shift of the lunate sulcus in australopithecine endocasts as indicating that australopithecines had a proportional reduction of visual areas, similar to modern human brains. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p515]
- Holloway concluded that australopithecines exhibited reorganization toward a 'humanlike' pattern, even prior to evolutionary increases in brain size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p515]
