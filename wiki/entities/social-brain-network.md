# Social Brain Network

## Summary

The Social Brain Network refers to the interconnected set of brain regions involved in social cognition. Key nodes include the medial prefrontal cortex (MPFC), temporoparietal junction (TPJ), superior temporal sulcus (STS), amygdala, and posterior cingulate cortex. Gray matter volume in these regions correlates with social network size, and their functional connectivity is associated with individual differences in social abilities.

## Key facts

- Gray matter volume in the amygdala and subregions of temporal and frontal cortex correlates with social network size in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p186]
- Animals housed in large social groups had more gray matter volume in bilateral mid-superior temporal sulcus and rostral prefrontal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p185]
- The MPFC, TPJ, STS, amygdala, and posterior cingulate cortex form key nodes of the social brain network connected through white matter pathways including the cingulum bundle and extreme capsule. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p190]
