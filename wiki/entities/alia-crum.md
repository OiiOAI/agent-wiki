# Alia Crum

## Summary

Alia Crum is a psychologist and professor at Stanford University who conducted groundbreaking research on mindsets and health effects. As Langer's student at Harvard, she investigated how believing work is exercise (chambermaid study) and later demonstrated that the perception of activity—regardless of actual exercise—predicts mortality over 21 years of tracking.

## Key facts

- With Octavia Zahrt, Crum surveyed more than 60,000 people over 21 years and found people who did not perceive themselves as active were more likely to die regardless of actual exercise amount. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p106]
- Crum and Langer's chambermaid study showed that telling hotel workers their work was exercise led to significant health improvements. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p105]
- In the milkshake study, thinking participants had consumed a high-calorie shake led to a steep drop in ghrelin (hunger hormone), consistent with feeling full. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p133]
- Crum tested whether genetic testing information about a 'tiring' gene affected performance—beliefs controlled performance regardless of actual genes. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p111]
