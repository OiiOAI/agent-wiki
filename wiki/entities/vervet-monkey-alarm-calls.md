# Vervet Monkey Alarm Calls

## Summary

Vervet monkey alarm calls, studied by Robert Seyfarth and Dorothy Cheney, are distinct vocalizations produced in response to different predators (eagles, leopards, snakes). These calls were initially thought to be analogous to words or names for predators, but Deacon argues they exemplify indexical rather than symbolic reference—they correlate with predators but don't represent them symbolically. The calls evolved through disruptive selection because appropriate defensive responses to different predators are mutually exclusive.

## Key facts

- Vervet monkeys produce distinct alarm calls for eagles, leopards, and snakes, with each call eliciting different defensive behaviors appropriate to that predator. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p57]
- Deacon argues that vervet alarm calls are indexical rather than symbolic—dogs learning 'come' can learn by association, but this differs from true symbolic understanding. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p58]
- The referential specificity of alarm calls evolved through disruptive selection because appropriate defensive behaviors for different predators are mutually exclusive. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p57]
