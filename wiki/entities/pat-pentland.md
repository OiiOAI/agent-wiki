# Pat Pentland

## Summary

Pat Pentland authored 'Center of Gravity Analysis and Chaos Theory' (1993), one of three papers on war and chaoplexity included in Boyd's list. His main argument is that considering the paradigm shift toward chaos theory, the key to analyzing enemy centers of gravity is to incorporate the real and dynamic complexities of the natural world. Pentland incorporates Boyd's OODA loop, acknowledging that this model anticipated many tenets of chaos theory.

## Key facts

- Pentland's main argument is that considering the insights provided by the paradigm shift, the key to analysis of enemy centers of gravity is to incorporate the real and dynamic complexities of the natural world explained by chaos theory. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p173]
- Pentland incorporates Boyd's OODA loop, acknowledging that this model, although developed in the 1970s, anticipated many tenets of chaos theory and is consistent with it. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p173]
- Pentland disaggregates an adversary's system into elements of power existing in three dimensions: source, linkage, and manifestation, where each element is both a center of gravity and a strange attractor. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p173]
- Chaotic systems are resilient; any chaotic system can maintain its structure and even organize into higher levels of organization as long as energy can be drawn from their environment. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p174]

## Inferences

- Inference: Pentland's framework suggests that military planning should account for the self-compensating and interdependent characteristics of complex systems, requiring planners to anticipate multiple compensatory responses.
