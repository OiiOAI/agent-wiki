# Martindale, Colin

## Summary

Colin Martindale is a researcher known for his work on the Cognitive Disinhibition Hypothesis of creativity and the neural correlates of creative thinking. His research includes EEG studies on cortical arousal during creative and intellectual tasks, and theoretical work on the relationship between attention and creativity.

## Key facts

- Martindale (1999) proposed the Cognitive Disinhibition Hypothesis, suggesting creative people adjust their focus of attention depending on task demands rather than having a stable disposition toward defocused attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p420]
- Martindale and Hines (1975) measured EEG alpha-wave activity while subjects completed creativity and intelligence tests, finding that creative people showed lower arousal during creativity tests but higher arousal during intelligence tests. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p421]
- Martindale and Hasenfus (1978) investigated the link between flexibility in attention and cortical activity in creative story generation, finding defocusing during inspiration phase only when instructed to be creative. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p421]
- Martindale argued that creative people lack the self-control necessary to regulate their focus of attention in biofeedback tasks, suggesting the regulatory process is automatic rather than requiring self control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p424]
