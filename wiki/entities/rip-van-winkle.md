# Rip van Winkle

## Summary

Washington Irving's character who represents the delicate case of the unconscious returning hero. Rip moved into the adventurous realm unconsciously, as all do every night in sleep. He returned with nothing to show for the experience but his whiskers—twenty years had passed, his wife was dead, and he was a stranger in his own land. Campbell contrasts him with Oisin and Kamar al-Zaman to illustrate degrees of return success.

## Key facts

- Rip van Winkle moved into the adventurous realm unconsciously, as all do every night when we go to sleep. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p170]
- Though we are refreshed and sustained by nightly visits to the source-darkness, our lives are not reformed by them; we return like Rip with nothing to show but our whiskers. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p171]
- When Rip returned from his sleep, twenty years had passed, his wife was dead, he knew no one, and was nearly mobbed as a Tory spy. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p171]
