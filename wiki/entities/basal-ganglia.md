# Basal Ganglia

## Summary

The basal ganglia are subcortical structures involved in planning and decision-making in Active Inference. The striatum (caudate nucleus and putamen) is the input nucleus, with medium spiny neurons dividing into D1-expressing (direct pathway) and D2-expressing (indirect pathway) types. Dopamine enhances activity of D1 neurons and attenuates D2 neurons. The direct pathway mediates disinhibition of behavior while the indirect pathway suppresses implausible policies.

## Key facts

- The striatum comprises the caudate nucleus and putamen and is the input nucleus of the basal ganglia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
- Medium spiny neurons divide into D1-expressing neurons (direct pathway) and D2-expressing neurons (indirect pathway), where dopamine enhances D1 and attenuates D2 activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
- The direct pathway goes from striatum to internal globus pallidus with a single inhibitory synapse, while the indirect pathway goes from striatum to external globus pallidus to subthalamic nucleus to internal globus pallidus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p100]
- In Active Inference, posterior probabilities over policies are computed based on their expected free energy, following the direct pathway from layer V cortex through striatum to basal ganglia output nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p101]
- Striatal dopamine can be thought of as modulating the balance between inferring what to do and what not to do. [raw/books/neuroscience/Active Inference _ The Free Energy Principle in Mind, Brain, - Thomas Parr; Giovanni Pezzulo; Karl J Friston; MIT Press.pdf#p102]
