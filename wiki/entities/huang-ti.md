# Huang Ti

## Summary

The third of China's August Three and the first emperor in human form. His distinguishing endowment was the power to dream—in sleep he could visit remote regions and consort with immortals. Campbell presents him as the perfect human spirit who governed China for 100 years in a golden age, teaching the control of forces in people's own hearts. He represents the stage of the cosmogonic cycle where the work is specifically human: control of the passions and elaboration of civilization's arts.

## Key facts

- Huang Ti's mother conceived him when she beheld a golden dazzling light around the Great Bear constellation; the child could talk when seventy days old and succeeded to the throne at eleven years. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p250]
- Huang Ti fell into a dream lasting three entire months during which he learned the lesson of the control of the heart. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p250]
- During his reign the phoenix and unicorn appeared in the gardens of the Empire, in attestation to the perfection of his reign. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p251]
