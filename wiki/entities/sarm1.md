# SARM1

## Summary

SARM1 is a NAD+ hydrolase that serves as the central executioner of axon degeneration. Upon activation following injury or disease, SARM1 triggers rapid NAD+ depletion, leading to energy failure and cytoskeletal breakdown. SARM1 is normally inhibited by NMNAT enzymes; when NMNAT2 levels fall after axotomy, SARM1 is disinhibited. Genetic deletion of SARM1 protects axons from degeneration in multiple injury and disease models.

## Key facts

- SARM1 is a key regulator of axon degeneration; its activation triggers NAD+ depletion and energy crisis leading to axon fragmentation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1286]
- SARM1 is normally inhibited by NMNAT enzymes; following axotomy, NMNAT2 degrades rapidly, disinhibiting SARM1 and triggering degeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1286]
- Deletion of SARM1 protects axons from degeneration in both in vitro and in vivo injury models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1286]

## Inferences

- Inference: SARM1 activation may contribute to axon degeneration in neurodegenerative diseases including ALS, Parkinson's, and Alzheimer's, suggesting therapeutic potential for SARM1 inhibitors.
