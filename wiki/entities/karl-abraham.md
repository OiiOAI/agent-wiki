# Karl Abraham

## Summary

Karl Abraham (1877–1925) was a German psychoanalyst and a close collaborator of Freud. He made important contributions to psychoanalytic theory, including his work on dementia praecox. Abraham distinguished himself from the mainstream psychoanalytic movement only after Freud's death, when he shifted emphasis toward character and character formation. His contributions during Freud's lifetime included the 1908 paper describing dementia praecox as characterized by a lack of libidinal cathexis of objects.

## Key facts

- In 1908, Karl Abraham, after exchange with Freud, pronounced the main characteristic of dementia praecox to be that in it the libidinal cathexis of objects was lacking. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
- Abraham answered that in dementia praecox, the libido turned away from objects is turned back onto the ego and this reflexive turning back is the source of the megalomania. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
- Abraham's first explanations of dementia praecox were accepted in psychoanalysis and became the basis of the attitude to the psychoses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5171]
