# Medial Superior Olive

## Summary

The medial superior olive (MSO) is a key structure in the superior olivary complex that generates a map of interaural time differences for localizing low-frequency sounds in the horizontal plane. Its neurons detect coincident inputs from the two ears transmitted through delay lines of differing lengths, creating a topographic representation of sound source location.

## Key facts

- The medial superior olive generates a map of interaural time differences using delay lines and coincidence detection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p741]
- Cochlear nerve fibers tuned to frequencies below 4 kHz and their bushy cell targets encode sounds by firing in phase with the pressure waves (phase-locking). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p741]
- The principal neurons of the medial superior olive have two tufts of dendrites: one extending to the lateral face contacted by ipsilateral bushy cells, the other projecting to the medial face contacted by contralateral bushy cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p743]
- Each medial superior olive forms a map of the location of sound sources in the contralateral hemifield. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p743]
