# Mark Solms

## Summary

Mark Solms is a South African neuroscientist and neuropsychoanalyst who has spent his career investigating the brain mechanisms of consciousness and dreaming. He is best known for discovering that dreaming and REM sleep are doubly dissociable — that dreaming is driven by the mesocortical-mesolimbic dopamine circuit rather than the cholinergic brainstem mechanisms that generate REM sleep. He is director of neuropsychology at the Neuroscience Institute of the University of Cape Town, trained as a psychoanalyst, and is the founder of the field of neuropsychoanalysis.

## Key facts

- Solms is director of neuropsychology in the Neuroscience Institute of the University of Cape Town, honorary lecturer in neurosurgery at the Royal London Hospital School of Medicine, and an honorary fellow of the American College of Psychiatrists. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p2]
- Solms conducted doctoral research on 361 neurological patients, systematically investigating how damage to different parts of the brain affected dreaming. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p28]
- Solms discovered that dreaming and REM sleep are doubly dissociable — dreaming is abolished by damage to the ventromesial frontal white matter (involving the mesocortical-mesolimbic dopamine pathway) while REM sleep is generated by cholinergic brainstem nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p29]
- In 1987, Solms decided to train as a psychoanalyst, motivated by his research findings and his belief that subjective reports had a vital role in neuropsychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p36]
- Solms coined the term 'neuropsychoanalysis' and founded the International Neuropsychoanalysis Society with Jaak Panksepp in 2000. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p48]
- Solms's clinical case of Mr S, a patient with Korsakoff psychosis, demonstrated that confabulations are motivated by wish-fulfilment, linking dopaminergic SEEKING mechanisms to Freud's concept. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p42]
- Solms was born on the Skeleton Coast of Namibia, and his older brother Lee suffered a traumatic brain injury at age six that profoundly altered his personality — an experience that motivated Solms's lifelong investigation of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p15]
- Solms began working with Karl Friston to develop a theory of consciousness based on Friston's free energy principle. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p12]
- Solms formulated a biological mechanism of consciousness centred on the periaqueductal gray (PAG) of the upper brainstem. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p256]
- He assembled an interdisciplinary team including physicists, computer scientists, biomedical engineers and neuroscientists to work on engineering artificial consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p256]
- Solms distinguishes his approach from traditional AI by emphasizing consciousness rather than intelligence, and by engineering a self-evidencing system with no objective goal other than self-preservation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p256-257]
- He predicts that consciousness arises only when a system can flexibly prioritise multiple needs through precision optimisation on a contextual basis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p259]
- Solms argues that if artificial consciousness can be created, it should not be commercially funded due to risks of exploitation resembling slavery. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p264]
- Mark Solms was affiliated with London Hospital Medical College and University College London at the time of writing the book. [raw/books/neuroscience/The_Neuropsychology_of_Dreams_A Clinico - Anatomical Study.pdf#p4]
- He conducted a systematic clinico-anatomical study of dreaming over several years starting in 1985, examining 361 neurological and neurosurgical patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/The_Neuropsychology_of_Dreams_A Clinico - Anatomical Study.pdf#p21]
- Solms collaborated with Dr. Karen Kaplan-Solms, who contributed fundamentally to the scientific project. [raw/books/neuroscience/The_Neuropsychology_of_Dreams_A Clinico - Anatomical Study.pdf#p19]
- He acknowledges Professor Michael Saling for helping plan the study and Dr. Oliver Turnbull for assisting in almost every aspect of the research. [raw/books/neuroscience/The_Neuropsychology_of_Dreams_A Clinico - Anatomical Study.pdf#p19]

## Inferences

- Inference: Solms's career trajectory — from dream research to psychoanalytic training to collaboration with Friston — reflects his commitment to integrating subjective experience into neuroscience rather than excluding it.
- Inference: Solms's discovery that dreaming is driven by the mesocortical-mesolimbic dopamine circuit rather than cholinergic brainstem mechanisms effectively vindicates Freud's broad claim that dreams are driven by wishes, even though Freud's specific neuroanatomical views were incorrect.
- Inference: Solms believes that consciousness is not synonymous with intelligence and can exist in rudimentary forms, which informs his strategy of engineering elementary consciousness first.
- Inference: Solms' background combining neuropsychology and likely psychoanalytic training (given his 1997 publication timing and subject matter) positions him to bridge these traditionally separate disciplines in dream research.
