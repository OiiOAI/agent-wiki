# Richard Thaler

## Summary

Richard Thaler is an American economist, Nobel laureate in Economic Sciences (2017), and the 'father of behavioral economics.' He discovered the endowment effect, coined the term, and collaborated extensively with Kahneman and Tversky at Stanford and the University of British Columbia. His work on mental accounting, loss aversion, and fairness norms established behavioral economics as a major field and influenced both academic theory and practical policy.

## Key facts

- Thaler discovered the endowment effect while observing Professor Richard Rosett, a wine collector who would not sell a bottle for under $100 but would not pay more than $35 for an identical bottle—a gap Thaler recognized as inconsistent with standard economic theory of a single valuation. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p378]
- When Thaler read an early draft of Prospect Theory, he immediately recognized that the asymmetric value function (loss aversion) could explain the endowment effect and other puzzles he had collected, marking the first major application of Prospect Theory to a specific economic anomaly. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p379]
- Thaler spent a year at Stanford with Kahneman and Tversky, and a second productive year with Kahneman at the University of British Columbia funded by the Russell Sage Foundation, co-authoring foundational papers on fairness norms and the endowment effect with Jack Knetsch. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p380]
- Thaler received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2017 for his contributions to behavioral economics and acturoeconomics, having substantially built on Kahneman and Tversky's foundational work. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p383]
- Thaler and Sunstein's book Nudge (2008) introduced 'libertarian paternalism' and the terms 'Econ' and 'Human' to distinguish rational-agent assumptions from actual human behavior. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p535]
- Thaler and Benartzi's Save More Tomorrow plan (2003) has improved retirement savings for millions of workers by tying contribution increases to pay raises, avoiding the immediate loss framing that causes resistance to change. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p536]
- Thaler coined the terms 'mental accounting,' 'nudge,' and 'libertarian paternalism,' and is a founding figure of behavioral economics. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p478]
