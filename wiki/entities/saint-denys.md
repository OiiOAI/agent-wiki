# Jean Marie Léon d'Hervey de Saint-Denys

## Summary

Jean Marie Léon d'Hervey de Saint-Denys (1822–1892) was a French professor of ethnography at the College de France and a pioneering nineteenth-century dream researcher. He began recording his dreams at age 13 and eventually filled 22 volumes with dream reports accompanied by colored drawings. He developed techniques for lucid dreaming to explore dreams from within, proposed that dream images are visual representations of ideas, and introduced concepts of abstractions and superimposition of images that were later renamed displacement and condensation by Freud.

## Key facts

- Saint-Denys began recording his dreams at age 13 and filled 22 volumes with dream reports and colored drawings by the time his book was published. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p29]
- He proposed that dream images are visual representations of ideas passing through the dreamer's mind, analogous to how a train of thought can change course. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p29]
- Saint-Denys introduced the concepts of abstractions and superimposition of images, later renamed by Freud as displacement and condensation. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p31]
- He used scented handkerchiefs to trigger dreams associated with specific travel memories, demonstrating that sensory stimuli could evoke specific dream content. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p31]
- Saint-Denys described methods for achieving lucid dreaming and used this awareness to explore dream formation from within the dream state. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p31]
- His book Les Rêves et les Moyens de les Diriger (1867) is described as the author's all-time favorite book on dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p29]
