# Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy

## Summary

Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy is an X-linked recessive inherited myopathy caused by mutations in the DMD gene (the largest human gene) that severely reduce dystrophin protein levels. It affects only males, begins in early childhood, progresses rapidly with patients in wheelchairs by age 12 and death typically in the third decade. Dystrophin normally links cytoskeletal actin to extracellular matrix proteins through the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex.

## Key facts

- Duchenne muscular dystrophy is caused by mutations that severely reduce dystrophin protein, a skeletal muscle protein conferring tensile strength. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The DMD gene is the second largest human gene, spanning about 2.5 million base pairs with at least 79 exons encoding a 14-kb mRNA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Most boys with DMD have deletions in the DMD gene introducing premature stop codons; about a third have point mutations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- ASO therapy promoting exon skipping can generate a shortened but partially functional dystrophin protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Gene therapy using AAV to deliver mini-dystrophin is feasible because full-length dystrophin is too large for AAV packaging. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery of the dystrophin gene and its protein product in the mid-1980s by Louis Kunkel opened new understanding of muscular dystrophy biology and revealed the dystrophin-glycoprotein complex linking cytoskeleton to extracellular matrix.
