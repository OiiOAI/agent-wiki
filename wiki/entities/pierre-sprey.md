# Pierre Sprey

## Summary

Pierre Sprey was a civilian analyst in the Office of the Secretary of Defense's Systems Analysis office, one of McNamara's 'Whiz Kids.' A brilliant mathematician and engineer who entered Yale at age 15, he became John Boyd's intellectual partner and the second member of what would become the Fighter Mafia. His interdiction bombing study and subsequent alliance with Boyd would eventually influence the design of the F-16.

## Key facts

- Sprey entered Yale at age 15 and graduated with majors in French literature and mechanical engineering. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p197]
- Sprey studied mathematical statistics and operational research at Cornell. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p197]
- Sprey wrote a heretical report saying interdiction bombing could not prevent Soviet forces from overrunning Europe. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p196]
- Sprey was nonrated (not a pilot) and had never served in the armed services. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p198]
- Boyd and Sprey met for hours discussing combat histories and fighter tactics, finding immediate common interests. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p199]
- Sprey began critiquing Boyd's briefings with irrefutable logic, which Boyd called the 'Pierre Sprey buzz saw'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p201]
- Boyd began calling Sprey at 4:00 AM with new E-M iterations, and their collaboration continued late into the night. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p201]
- Sprey designed the A-X (later A-10 Warthog) close air support aircraft with extreme attention to survivability and cost constraints. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p234]
- He wrote a famous letter to General James Ferguson criticizing gold-plating of the F-X. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p227]
- Sprey worked secretly on the lightweight fighter project, calling it 'the Lord's work'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p244]
- He was known for instituting strict design discipline, limiting RFP responses to 30 pages. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p236]
