# Genome-Wide Association Study

## Summary

Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) are hypothesis-free approaches that assay polymorphisms at every gene in the genome simultaneously. In schizophrenia research, GWAS involving nearly 40,000 cases and 113,000 controls identified 108 risk loci. This approach has proven highly reliable for identifying common risk alleles in complex disorders.

## Key facts

- GWAS in schizophrenia involving nearly 40,000 cases and 113,000 controls identified 108 risk loci. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p97]
- Individual genetic variants identified in schizophrenia GWAS typically account for less than 25% increase in risk. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p97]
