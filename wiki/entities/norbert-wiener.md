# Norbert Wiener

## Summary

Norbert Wiener was a mathematician and philosopher who founded the field of cybernetics. His 1948 book 'Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine' introduced the concept of organizations as 'sensing mechanisms.'

## Key facts

- Norbert Wiener authored 'Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine,' first published in 1948. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L494-L495]
- Wiener's work introduced the concept of using the organization as a 'sensing mechanism,' which influenced the Team Topologies book's approach to organizational sensing. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L493-L495]
- Norbert Wiener published 'Cybernetics' in 1948, defining it as 'the science of communication and control in the animal and the machine.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p113]
- Wiener proposed that the same general principles that controlled a thermostat may also be seen in economic systems, market regulation, and political decision-making systems. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p113]
- Wiener, von Neumann, and Turing applied cybernetics as a model for the brain, developing the 'computational theory of mind' that fueled the cognitive revolution. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p117]
- Wiener defined feedback as 'a method of controlling a system by reinserting into it the results of past performance,' controlling based on actual rather than expected performance. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p114]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wiener's cross-disciplinary approach to cybernetics established a template for Boyd's similarly interdisciplinary strategic framework.
- Inference: The computational theory of mind that Wiener helped develop provided the intellectual foundation for viewing cognition as information processing—a key assumption in Boyd's OODA loop.
