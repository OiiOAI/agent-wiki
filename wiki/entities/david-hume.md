# David Hume

## Summary

The Scottish philosopher David Hume argued that all knowledge derives exclusively from the senses (empiricism). His reasoning led to conclusions that if followed strictly, both 'substance' and 'causation' are merely imagined rather than empirically derived—they exist only as habits of mind when one thing repeatedly follows another. This led to the apparent conclusion that nature and nature's laws are creations of our own imagination.

## Key facts

- Hume submitted that if one follows the strictest rules of logical induction and deduction from experience, one must conclude that a child devoid of all senses would have no thoughts whatsoever after eighteen years. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p109]
- According to Hume, since all knowledge comes through sensory impressions and there's no sensory impression of substance itself, there is no knowledge of substance—it's entirely within our own minds. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- Hume concluded there's no evidence for causation in our sensations; like substance, causation is merely something we imagine when one thing repeatedly follows another. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- Kant said it was Hume who 'aroused me from my dogmatic slumbers' and caused him to write the Critique of Pure Reason. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- Hume proposed his definition of causation in 1748. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L253]
- Hume provided two definitions of causation: regularity and counterfactual. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L255]
- The first definition is based on regularity: the cause is regularly followed by the effect. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L255]
- The second definition is counterfactual: 'if the first object had not been, the second never had existed'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L255]
