# Hox genes

## Summary

Hox genes are a family of selector genes comprising typically eight genes clustered on one chromosome, activated in particular compartments of the body plan from front to back along the anteroposterior axis. Each Hox gene contains a homeobox sequence specifying a homeodomain of sixty amino acids that regulates gene activation in the developing embryo. The remarkable colinearity between gene order on the chromosome and anteroposterior order of activation suggests a common ancestor for all bilaterally symmetrical animals.

## Key facts

- Hox genes are clustered on one chromosome in an order corresponding to the anteroposterior order of their activation compartments in the body plan, exhibiting remarkable colinearity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p194]
- Once regionally activated, each Hox gene becomes auto-activating and self-maintaining through a regulatory circuit including its homeodomain transcription factor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p194]
- Similar Hox genes with the same colinear order are found in arthropods, chordates, annelids, and molluscs; genes at corresponding locations in different animals more closely resemble each other than genes at different locations in the same animal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p195]
- Hox genes, along with the sog/chordin and dpp/BMP gene pairs and a pax-6 associated light-sensitive organ, were proposed to have been present in 'Urbilateria,' the hypothetical ancestor of arthropod and mammalian lineages before the Cambrian explosion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p196]

## Inferences

- Inference: Given the antiquity and universality of Hox genes, it has been proposed they define a common 'zootypic' body plan for all metazoans.
