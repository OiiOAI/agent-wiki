# Chambermaid Study

## Summary

The chambermaid study tested whether changing mindset about physical activity would affect health outcomes. When chambermaids were told their physically strenuous work constituted exercise, they showed significant improvements in weight, BMI, blood pressure, and waist-to-hip ratio compared to controls who received only general health information, despite no change in actual work performed.

## Key facts

- Chambermaids were taught that their work was exercise, comparing making beds to rowing machines and mopping to upper body workouts. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p105]
- There were no differences in how hard or long the chambermaids worked nor in their eating over the monthlong intervention—the only change was belief. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p105]
- The experimental group showed significant weight loss, decreased BMI, lower blood pressure, and decreased waist-to-hip ratio. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p105]

## Inferences

- Inference: someone exercising at a gym sees themselves as socializing rather than exercising, they probably won't benefit much; someone who sees their daily activities as exercise could experience benefits.
