# Inanna

## Summary

The supreme goddess of ancient Sumerian cuneiform tablets who descended from the heavens into the hell region of her sister-opposite Ereshkigal. She was turned into a corpse and hung upon a stake. After three days, her messenger Ninshubur secured the 'food of life' and 'water of life' from Enki, and Inanna arose. She ascended with demons who demanded a substitute for each person she encountered. Campbell presents her as an Oriental sister of Amaterasu, illustrating the rescue-from-without pattern.

## Key facts

- Inanna descended naked to the seven judges of the underworld and was turned into a corpse hung upon a stake. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p165]
- After three days Ninshubur secured the 'food of life' and 'water of life' from Enki, and the food and water were sprinkled sixty times on the corpse, upon which Inanna arose. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p166]
- Inanna is the same deity known successively as Ishtar, Astarte, Aphrodite, and Venus, associated with the planet, moon, heavens, and fruitful earth. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p165]
