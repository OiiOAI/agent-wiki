# Henri Poincaré

## Summary

Henri Poincaré (1854–1912) was a French mathematician cited by Dehaene as evidence that unconscious processing extends to sophisticated mathematical reasoning. Poincaré reported that he solved mathematical problems while relaxing or traveling, with solutions appearing suddenly without conscious effort—suggesting unconscious mental work preceded awareness of the result.

## Key facts

- Poincaré described how the idea for non-Euclidean geometry transformations came to him suddenly while getting on an omnibus, with 'perfect certainty'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p104]
- Dehaene uses Poincaré's examples to argue that 'consciousness is overrated' and that unconscious processing includes sophisticated mathematics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p104]
- Poincaré concluded that the axioms of geometry are conventions; our choice among possible conventions is guided by experimental facts but remains free and is limited only by the necessity of avoiding contradiction. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p225]
- Poincaré stated that one geometry cannot be more true than another; it can only be more convenient. Geometry is not true, it is advantageous. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p225]
- Poincaré explained that a scientist does not choose facts at random but seeks to condense much experience and thought into a slender volume. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p227]
- Poincaré identified the 'subliminal self' as the entity that selects mathematical solutions on the basis of 'mathematical beauty,' the harmony of numbers and forms. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p228]
- The narrator discovered that Phædrus's metaphysical conclusions and Poincaré's scientific conclusions matched perfectly at their boundaries, producing a complete structure of thought capable of uniting Science and Art. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p230]
