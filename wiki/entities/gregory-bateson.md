# Gregory Bateson

## Summary

Gregory Bateson (1904-1980) was an English anthropologist, social scientist, and cyberneticist who developed a model of mind based on systems-theoretical principles. Influenced by Wiener, Bateson's criteria of mind included: minds as aggregates of interacting parts; interaction triggered by difference rather than energy; circular chains of determination; and mental processes interpreting preceding events as transforms. He connected evolution theory with mental processes, asserting that understanding mental process requires looking at biological evolution and vice versa. His interdisciplinary approach influenced cognitive science, family therapy, and organizational theory.

## Key facts

- Bateson developed a model of mind with criteria including: (1) mind as aggregate of interacting parts; (2) interaction triggered by difference related to information; (3) mental process requires circular chains of determination; (4) effects of difference are transforms of preceding events. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p118]
- Bateson asserted that 'if you want to understand mental process, look at biological evolution and conversely if you want to understand biological evolution, go look at mental process.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p118]
- Bateson introduced the element of difference as the key to which we respond, an insight that influenced later concepts of information and novelty in complexity theory. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p152]
- Like Boyd, Bateson argued for a thoroughly interdisciplinary approach focused on the very principles of organization that inform thinking about organisms and culture. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p118]

- Inference: Bateson's integration of evolution and mental processes provided an intellectual bridge that Boyd used to connect Darwinian concepts with cognitive processes in his strategic framework.
