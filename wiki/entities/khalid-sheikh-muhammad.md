# Khalid Sheikh Muhammad

## Summary

Khalid Sheikh Muhammad (KSM) was the alleged mastermind of the 9/11 attacks who was captured by the CIA in 2003 and subjected to enhanced interrogation techniques. His case illustrates the problems with extracting information through coercive methods.

## Key facts

- KSM was captured in Islamabad, Pakistan in 2003 and subjected to walling, sleep deprivation, and waterboarding by Mitchell and Jessen for approximately three weeks. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p142]
- Waterboarding did not work on KSM—he was able to open his sinuses so water flowed out, and even fell asleep during one session while being interrogated. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p142]
- KSM made a detailed confession in March 2007 at Guantánamo Bay listing 31 Al-Qaeda operations, but former CIA officers like Robert Baer concluded he was 'making things up.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p148-149]
- Some of KSM's claims were demonstrably false—for example, he claimed to have surveilled the Plaza Bank building in Seattle, but the bank wasn't founded until years after his arrest. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p149]

## Inferences

- Inference: KSM's case demonstrates the core problem with coercive interrogation: extended stress may produce compliance but not accurate information. His confession to numerous crimes may have been designed to gain importance or end his ordeal rather than reflect true memories.
