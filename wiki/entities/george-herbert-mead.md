# George Herbert Mead

## Summary

George Herbert Mead (1863-1931) was the most important thinker associated with the Chicago school and symbolic interactionism, though he was a philosopher rather than a sociologist. He started teaching philosophy at the University of Chicago in 1894 and taught there until his death in 1931. Although he published comparatively little during his lifetime, his courses in social psychology profoundly influenced many graduate students in sociology. Students combined Mead's ideas with those from the sociology department to create symbolic interactionism. After Mead's death, students published his lecture notes as "Mind, Self and Society" (1934), which became the main intellectual pillar of symbolic interactionism. Mead extended the principles of psychological behaviorism to analyze the mind, accepting behaviorism's focus on the individual while disagreeing that consciousness was not amenable to scientific study.
