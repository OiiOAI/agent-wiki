# Lambert-Eaton syndrome

## Summary

Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome is a disorder of neuromuscular transmission associated with small-cell lung cancer. It is caused by antibodies against voltage-gated Ca2+ channels in presynaptic terminals. Unlike myasthenia gravis, it shows facilitation rather than decrement with repetitive stimulation.

## Key facts

- Lambert-Eaton syndrome is caused by antibodies against voltage-gated Ca2+ channels in presynaptic terminals, leading to reduced Ca2+ influx and impaired ACh release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p371]
- The first postsynaptic potential is abnormally small, but subsequent responses increase in amplitude so the final summated potential is two to four times the amplitude of the first. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p371]
- The syndrome is often associated with small-cell carcinoma of the lung, possibly through molecular mimicry between tumor cell and nerve terminal Ca2+ channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p371]
