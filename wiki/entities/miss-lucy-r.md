# Miss Lucy R.

## Summary

A thirty-year-old English governess in Vienna, referred to Freud in 1892, suffering from chronic suppurative rhinitis, loss of smell, recurrent subjective olfactory hallucinations (smell of burnt pudding, then cigar smoke), and depression. Her case is presented by Freud as a model instance of acquired hysteria in a person of sound heredity, illustrating the mechanism of conversion through repression of incompatible ideas.

## Key facts

- Miss Lucy R. presented with loss of sense of smell, recurrent subjective olfactory hallucinations (initially of burnt pudding, later of cigar smoke), depression, fatigue, and heaviness in the head. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p634]
- The smell of burnt pudding originated when the children rushed at her to prevent her reading a birthday letter from her mother, while the pudding they were cooking was forgotten and burnt—a scene evoking conflicting affects of affection for the children and the desire to leave them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p641]
- Freud inferred that among the determinants of her trauma was an unconscious love for her employer, the children's father, which she had intentionally repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p643]
- The deeper traumatic scene involved the children's father shouting at an old accountant for trying to kiss the children, which revived her memory of an earlier scene where the father had furiously reprimanded her for allowing a lady to kiss the children on the mouth—crushing her hopes that he might love her. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p645]
- After the last analysis, the patient recovered suddenly two days later, reporting the weight was no longer on her mind and she felt well, though she still loved her employer but accepted her prospects as none. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p646]
- The case demonstrated that before hysteria can be acquired for the first time, an idea must be intentionally repressed from consciousness and excluded from associative modification, with the repressed idea then becoming pathogenic. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p642]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's analysis of Miss Lucy R. represents a model instance of acquired hysteria where the essential condition was the repression of an incompatible idea (her love for her employer) leading to conversion of the excitation into somatic symptoms (olfactory hallucinations).

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the improvement in her nasal sensitivity to pain and reflexes was due entirely to psychical analysis or partly to treatment of the underlying organic condition (caries of the ethmoid bone).
