# Arrowsmith School

## Summary

The Arrowsmith School is a Toronto-based educational institution founded in 1980 by Barbara Arrowsmith Young and Joshua Cohen. It uses brain exercises based on neuroplasticity principles to strengthen weak brain functions rather than simply teaching compensations for learning disabilities.

## Key facts

- The Arrowsmith School was opened in Toronto in 1980 by Barbara Arrowsmith Young and Joshua Cohen. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- Students undergo up to forty hours of assessments to determine precisely which brain functions are weak and whether they might be helped. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- Accepted students sit quietly working at their computers on brain exercises. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- Some students diagnosed with attention-deficit as well as learning disorders were on Ritalin when they entered and could come off medication as their exercises progress. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- The school uses computer exercises with complex multi-handed clocks to strengthen symbol relationship abilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- Children study Urdu and Persian letters to strengthen visual memories using unfamiliar shapes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- Children wear eye patches on their left eyes and trace intricate lines, squiggles, and Chinese letters to strengthen the right side of the brain where they have problems. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- The school teaches that speaking, writing, and reading difficulties often share a common cause: weakness in the brain function that coordinates and strings together movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p47]
- Tracing exercises improve children in speaking, writing, and reading simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p48]
- Students listen to CDs and memorize poems to improve weak auditory memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p48]
- A student who came at thirteen with math and reading at third-grade level, told by Tufts University he would never improve, graduated reading and doing math at tenth-grade level after three years at Arrowsmith. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p49]
- A student who came at sixteen reading at first-grade level was reading at seventh-grade level after fourteen months. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p49]
