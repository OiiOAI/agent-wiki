# Wilder Penfield

## Summary

Wilder Penfield (1891–1976) was a pioneering American-Canadian neurosurgeon and founder of the Montreal Neurological Institute (The Neuro) at McGill University. Born in Spokane, Washington, he studied at Princeton and Oxford (as a Rhodes Scholar), and Johns Hopkins Medical School. He developed the 'Montreal Procedure'—operating on epilepsy patients while awake to map and remove seizure-causing tissue. Penfield's work, along with his colleagues Brenda Milner and Herbert Jasper, established the anatomical basis of memory function and demonstrated that temporal-lobe seizures originated in the amygdala and hippocampus. His institute produced fundamental discoveries about brain organization and cognitive function.

- He mentored Brenda Milner, who went on to study Henry Molaison. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p41]
- Penfield operated on patients F.C. and P.B. before Henry, whose cases also revealed the hippocampus's role in memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p35]
- In the 1950s, Wilder Penfield discovered that déjà vu could be artificially generated by electrically stimulating areas in the temporal lobes of epilepsy patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p112]
- When Penfield stimulated areas in the temporal lobes, patients reported feelings like 'things seem familiar' or 'as though I had been through this before.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p112]
- Penfield's findings suggested the brain can generate an intensely strong feeling of familiarity even if a specific memory does not come to mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p112]
- Penfield co-advised Brenda Milner during her graduate studies at McGill. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p112]
