# Lee Alexis Wilson Case

## Summary

The 1969 murder of Lee Alexis Wilson, a 23-year-old university coed who was beaten, strangled, and sexually assaulted near the Art Institute in Chicago during daylight hours. The case became a landmark example of pluralistic ignorance, as thousands of people passed by the scene and one witness heard her scream but did not investigate because no one else seemed to be paying attention.

## Key facts

- Lee Alexis Wilson, 23, was beaten and strangled near one of Chicago's most popular tourist attractions. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p257]
- Her nude body was found in dense shrubs alongside the Art Institute wall by a 12-year-old boy. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p257]
- Police theorized she was attacked while sitting or standing by a fountain in the Art Institute's south plaza. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p257]
- Thousands of persons passed the site during the attack; one man heard a scream around 2 PM but did not investigate because no one else seemed to be paying attention. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p257]

## Inferences

- Inference: This case demonstrates how pluralistic ignorance can prevent bystander intervention even in serious emergencies, as individuals look to others' apparent lack of concern as evidence that no help is needed.
