# Stereotype Content Model

## Summary

The Stereotype Content Model (Fiske) proposes that we categorize others along two axes: warmth (friend or foe) and competence (effective or not). This produces four quadrants—high warmth/high competence (Us, pride), low warmth/low competence (homeless, disgust), high warmth/low competence (elderly, pity), and low warmth/high competence (Jews in Europe, envy). The emotions evoked predict behavioral responses.

## Key facts

- High warmth/high competence (Us) evokes pride; low warmth/high competence evokes envy; high warmth/low competence evokes pity; low warmth/low competence evokes disgust. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p425]
- The low warmth/high competence category includes Asian Americans (by white America), Jews (in Europe), and ethnic Chinese (in Indonesia)—all seen as cold, clever, clannish, but competent. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p425]
- The transition from high warmth/high competence to low warmth/low competence (e.g., a successful friend who becomes homeless) evokes disgust mingled with bafflement. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p426]
- Persecution of low warmth/high competence groups often involves first degrading them to low warmth/low competence—parading resented elites in dunce caps, forcing Jews to wear degrading symbols before murder. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p426]
