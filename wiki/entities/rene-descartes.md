# René Descartes

## Summary

René Descartes (1596–1650) was a French philosopher who developed the theory of mind-body dualism. Dehaene presents him as a pioneering scientist who was 'fundamentally a reductionist' with a mechanical model of the body, yet concluded that consciousness must be immaterial because machines could not mimic flexible language use and reasoning. His 'Descartes's Challenge'—how matter could produce the flexibility of consciousness—remains a central problem that Dehaene's book addresses.

## Key facts

- Descartes theorized dualism: the conscious mind is made of a nonmaterial substance that eludes normal laws of physics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p13]
- Descartes proposed a mechanical model of the body, comparing it to a collection of organs like church organs with animal spirits flowing through pipes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p13]
- Descartes argued machines could not flexibly use language: 'How could a machine ever respond to a query, as even the dumbest person can?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p16]
- Dehaene argues the global neuronal workspace resolves Descartes's challenge by providing a 'free-willing' machine capable of flexible thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p27]
