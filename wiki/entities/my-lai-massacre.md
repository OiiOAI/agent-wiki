# My Lai Massacre

## Summary

The My Lai Massacre was the mass murder of civilians—infants, toddlers, children, and adults—from the village of My Lai in South Vietnam on March 16, 1968, committed by U.S. Army soldiers under the command of Lieutenant William Calley. The massacre became significant in social psychology research on obedience when a national survey after Calley's trial revealed that 51 percent of Americans said they would shoot villagers if similarly ordered.

## Key facts

- Lieutenant William Calley ordered his soldiers to kill all inhabitants of My Lai, Vietnam—from infants and toddlers through their parents and grandparents. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- A national survey after Calley's trial showed 51 percent of Americans responded that if ordered, they too would shoot all residents of a Vietnamese village. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- The My Lai survey was conducted by Kelman and Hamilton (1989). [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
