# Richard Axel

## Summary

Richard Axel (born 1946) is a molecular biologist who collaborated with Kandel at Columbia University. He developed a general method of transferring any gene into any cell in tissue culture called co-transfection. In 2004, he and Linda Buck won the Nobel Prize for their work on the sense of smell.

## Key facts

- Axel met Kandel in 1977 at a tenure committee meeting and expressed interest in working on the nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p260]
- Axel developed a general method called co-transfection for transferring any gene into any cell in tissue culture. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p261]
- Richard Axel and Linda Buck were awarded the Nobel Prize in 2004 for discovering that there are about a thousand different receptors for smell in the mouse nose. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p263]
- Their collaboration with Kandel and Paul Scheller focused on the molecular biology of egg laying behavior in Aplysia. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p262]
