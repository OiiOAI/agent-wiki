# Graham Brown

## Summary

Graham Brown was an early 20th-century neuroscientist who championed the view that the spinal cord is organized not reflexologically but on a self-referential basis by central neuronal circuits that provide the drive for electrical pattern generation required for organized movement. His 1911, 1914, and 1915 studies of locomotion in deafferented animals demonstrated that organized gait could still be produced without sensory input from the legs.

## Key facts

- Graham Brown (1911, 1914, 1915) proposed that the spinal cord is organized on a self-referential basis by central neuronal circuits providing the drive for organized movement, based on studies of locomotion in deafferented animals. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p12]
- Brown demonstrated that animals with severed sensory pathways could still produce organized gait, leading him to propose that sensory input reflexively resets rhythm but does not generate walking itself. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p12]
- Brown described reciprocal neuronal organization as 'half-paired centers' where mutual interaction generates the left/right limb pacing that constitutes locomotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p12]
