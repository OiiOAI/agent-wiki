# Ludwig Wittgenstein

## Summary

Austrian-British philosopher (1889-1951) whose later work McGilchrist discusses alongside Heidegger as reaching similar conclusions about language, context, and limits of analysis. Wittgenstein emphasised the primacy of context over rules and system building, the importance of practice over theory, and became increasingly apophthegmatic as he struggled with the idea that philosophy was not possible outside of poetry.

## Key facts

- Wittgenstein emphasised the primacy of context over rules and system building, of practice over theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p226]
- Wittgenstein was sceptical of the scientific method for its tendency to 'reduce' and the deceptive clarity of its models. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p226]
- Wittgenstein said 'Everything is what it is and not another thing' - an expression of commitment to the sheer quiddity of each individual thing. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p226]
- Wittgenstein spoke of the need to struggle towards objectivity, yet the reality we aim to reveal is itself not precise, so that the artificial precision of our language betrays us. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p227]
- Wittgenstein ended by trying to work out honourable terms on which philosophy might surrender to poetry. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p223]

## Inferences

- Inference: Both Wittgenstein and Heidegger, starting from left-hemisphere modes of operation (language, abstraction, analysis), were compelled to acknowledge the primacy of right-hemisphere understanding.
