# Head Direction Cells

## Summary

Head direction cells are neurons discovered by James Ranck in 1984 that fire robustly when an animal's head points in a specific direction. Each of the 360 degrees has a different set of neurons firing with equal probability. The head direction system consists of many neurons in a serially connected brain network including the brainstem, mammillary bodies, anterodorsal thalamic nucleus, post subiculum, entorhinal cortex, lateral septum, and parietal cortex. Remarkably, these neurons continue to fire in organized sequential patterns during sleep, demonstrating that the system is maintained by self-organized mechanisms.

## Key facts

- James Ranck discovered head direction cells in 1984 in the post subiculum, observing that neurons fire robustly when the animal's head points in a specific direction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p126]
- The head direction system consists of neurons in the brainstem, mammillary bodies, anterodorsal thalamic nucleus, post subiculum, entorhinal cortex, lateral septum, and parietal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p107]
- During REM sleep, head direction neurons replicate sequential activity patterns seen in waking, with the virtual head direction moving at the same speed as during waking. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p108]
- During non-REM sleep, head direction-related neuronal activity drifts ten times faster than during waking or REM sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p108]

## Inferences

- Inference: Head direction neurons maintain organized activity without external sensory inputs, demonstrating that self-organized mechanisms can improve the brain's interpretation of the external world through interpolation and extrapolation.
