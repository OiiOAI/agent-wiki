# Barbara Burks

## Summary

Barbara Burks (1903-1943) was a pioneering researcher who, as a Stanford graduate student in 1926, became the first person outside Sewall Wright's work to use path diagrams for causal analysis. Her dissertation on nature versus nurture used these diagrams to estimate that only 35% of IQ variation is inherited. She discovered the collider bias independently and formulated criteria against conditioning on effects of causes. Despite her qualifications, she was never offered a university position.

## Key facts

- Barbara Burks was a Stanford graduate student who independently invented path diagrams in 1926. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2640-L2650]
- Her dissertation showed only 35% of IQ variation is inherited, contrary to her advisor Terman's eugenics views. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2650-L2660]
- Burks identified the collider bias problem in 1926, anticipating what would later be called M-bias. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2655-L2665]
- She published 'On the Inadequacy of the Partial and Multiple Correlation Technique' as a student. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2665-L2675]
- Burks never obtained a university professorship despite her qualifications. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2675-L2680]
- She died by suicide in 1943 at age 40. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2678-L2680]
