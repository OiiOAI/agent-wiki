# Ivan Pavlov

## Summary

Ivan Pavlov was a Nobel Prize-winning Russian physiologist known for his research on conditioned reflexes. He also characterized responses to novel and surprising experiences, calling them the 'What is it?' reflex, which represents the brain's fundamental orienting response to the unexpected.

## Key facts

- Ivan Pavlov noticed that animals had a characteristic response to any changes or new things in the environment, which he called the 'What is it?' reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p132]
- Pavlov believed the orienting response, seen in all sorts of animals, is foundational for inquisitiveness, which fuels the scientific method and humanity's drive to understand the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p137]
- Pavlov described the biological significance of the orienting response: without it, life would hang by a thread; in humans it has developed into inquisitiveness, the parent of scientific method. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p137]
