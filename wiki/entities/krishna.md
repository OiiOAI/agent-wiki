# Krishna

## Summary

Krishna is a major Hindu deity and world savior whose miraculous infancy among cowherds features numerous exploits including the slaying of the demon Putana and the lifting of Mount Govardhan. His legends illustrate the hero as world redeemer, culminating in the slaying of his tyrannical uncle Kans.

## Key facts

- Putana came in the shape of a beautiful woman with poison in her breasts; Krishna drew so hard that he sucked away her life, and she fell dead, reassuming her huge and hideous form. When the foul corpse was cremated, it emitted a sweet fragrance because the divine infant had given the demoness salvation. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p257]
- When Krishna was playing in the yard, his foster parent Yasoda was warned he was eating clay. When she peered inside his mouth, she beheld the whole universe, the 'Three Worlds,' before the moment passed from her mind. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p257]
- Krishna taught the cowherds that Indra is not a supreme deity and redirected their worship to Mount Govardhan, saying prosperity depends on natural laws rather than the god of heaven. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p257-p258]
- When Indra sent a deluge of storm clouds, Krishna lifted Mount Govardhan with his little finger and bid the people take shelter beneath; the rain struck the mountain, hissed, and evaporated for seven days. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p258]
- After slaying the wrestlers sent against him, Krishna leapt to the royal dais, dragged the tyrant Kans by the hair, and killed him. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p275]
- Krishna comforted the wives of the dead Kans with his primal wisdom: 'Mother, do not grieve. No one can live and not die. To imagine oneself as possessing anything is to be mistaken; nobody is father, mother, or son. There is only the continuous round of birth and death.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p275]

## Inferences

- Inference: Krishna's legend exemplifies the world redeemer archetype, where the hero descends from divine origins, undergoes obscurity among common people, and ultimately reveals cosmic truths through transformative deeds.
