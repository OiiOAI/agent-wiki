# Erving Goffman

## Summary

Erving Goffman (1922-1982) is often considered the last major thinker associated with the original Chicago school and is arguably the most influential twentieth-century American sociologist. Between the 1950s and 1970s, he published works that gave birth to dramaturgical analysis as a variant of symbolic interactionism. His best-known work, "Presentation of Self in Everyday Life" (1959), developed dramaturgical theory by seeing commonalities between theatrical performances and the "acts" people put on in daily life. In this view, interaction is fragile, maintained by social performances, with poor performances or disruptions being threats. He developed concepts of "front region" (parallel to stage front) and "back region" (where actors can retire to prepare and shed their roles). Dramaturgical analysis is consistent with symbolic interactionism's focus on actors, action, and interaction.
