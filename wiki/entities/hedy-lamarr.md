# Hedy Lamarr

## Summary

Hedy Lamarr (1914-2000) was a pioneering Hollywood actress and inventor. Born in Austria, she began inventing at age five when she took apart and reassembled a music box. During World War II, she co-invented frequency hopping technology with composer George Antheil, which was ahead of its time. This patent—using 88 channels to prevent torpedo signal jamming—later became foundational for Wi-Fi, GPS, and Bluetooth technology. She received the Electronic Frontier Foundation's Pioneer Award in 1997, becoming the first woman to win what is known as one of the 'Oscars of inventing.'
