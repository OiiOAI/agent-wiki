# Arvid Carlsson

## Summary

Arvid Carlsson at the University of Göteborg discovered dopamine as a brain neurotransmitter and showed that both Parkinson's disease and schizophrenia involve dopamine dysregulation. His work established the dopamine hypothesis of schizophrenia and earned him the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

## Key facts

- In 1960, Arvid Carlsson discovered dopamine as a neurotransmitter in the brain and showed that lowering dopamine concentration produces a model of Parkinson's disease. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p364]
- Carlsson noticed that overly large doses of dopamine produced psychotic symptoms resembling schizophrenia and suggested the underlying cause is excessive dopamine transmission. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p364]
- Carlsson suggested antipsychotic drugs produce their therapeutic effect by blocking dopamine receptors, reducing dopamine transmission along critical neural pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p365]
