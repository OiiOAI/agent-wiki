# Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis

## Summary

Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis is a rare autoimmune disease discovered in 2007 at the University of Pennsylvania in which the immune system selectively destroys NMDA receptors. This disease provides a dramatic model showing how a single molecule, when wiped out, suffices to cause a selective loss of mental health and eventually consciousness itself, progressing from an artificial form of schizophrenia to complete loss of consciousness.

## Key facts

- Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis was discovered in 2007 at the University of Pennsylvania, with patients presenting with anxiety, agitation, bizarre behavior, delusional or paranoid thoughts, and visual or auditory hallucinations—an acute, acquired, quickly evolving form of schizophrenia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p318]
- Within three weeks, patients' consciousness declined: EEG exhibited slow brain waves as in coma, they became motionless, ceased to respond, and several died within months. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p318]
- The disease involves an autoimmune response selectively destroying the NMDA receptor for the neurotransmitter glutamate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p318]
- When neuronal culture was exposed to patient serum, NMDA synapses vanished within hours but returned as soon as the lethal serum was removed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p319]

## Inferences

- Inference: This disease may serve as a model condition whose molecular mechanisms shed light on psychiatric diseases, their onset, and their link to conscious experience, potentially providing the first medical condition where a disease selectively disrupts the long-distance connections underlying conscious experience [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p319].
