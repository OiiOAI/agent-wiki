# Prometheus

## Summary

In Greek mythology, Prometheus (meaning 'forethought') was the Titan who stole fire from heaven and gave it to mortals, bringing numeracy, literacy, and technical skill. For this crime, Zeus chained him to a rock where his liver was daily torn out by a bird, only to regenerate for the next day's torment. McGilchrist interprets the myth as an allegory of the two hemispheres: Prometheus is the emissary who takes to himself the power of the Master, and his punishment represents the consequences of hubristic self-aggrandizement. In Aeschylus' Prometheus Bound, the god justifies himself as humanity's deliverer, yet the tragedy recounts the pain that comes from attempting to seize what belongs to another realm.
