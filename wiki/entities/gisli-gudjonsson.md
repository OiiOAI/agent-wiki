# Gisli Gudjonsson

## Summary

Gisli Gudjonsson is a clinical psychologist who developed the concept of 'interrogative suggestibility' and created a scale for measuring it. His work on false confessions, including the 'memory distrust syndrome,' has been influential in legal contexts. He found that individuals who retract confessions are more suggestible than those who steadfastly deny crimes.

## Key facts

- Gudjonsson coined the term 'memory distrust syndrome' to describe how suspects can begin to distrust their own memory under coercive questioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p163]
- Gudjonsson developed a scale for measuring 'interrogative suggestibility'—the tendency to change claims about the past in response to misleading information and suggestive questions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p164]
- Gudjonsson found that people who made criminal confessions they later retracted were more influenced by suggestive questions than 'deniers' who refused to acknowledge involvement despite forensic evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p165]
