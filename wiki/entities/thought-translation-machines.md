# Thought Translation Machines

## Summary

Thought translation machines are devices that read people's thoughts by detecting the electrical signatures of imagined actions in the motor cortex and translating them into commands for external devices. Developed by Miguel Nicolelis and John Chapin at Duke University, these machines work because the brain physically changes as we think in ways that can be tracked electronically. The technology was demonstrated with rats learning to control water dispensers through thought alone, and monkeys controlling robotic arms six hundred miles away. In 2006, Matthew Nagle became the first human to use such a device, controlling a computer cursor and robotic arm with his thoughts after paralysis from a spinal cord injury.

## Key facts

- Nicolelis and Chapin trained rats to press a bar to get water, then disconnected the bar from water release; after connecting to neurons, rats learned to get water by imagining pressing the bar. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p212]
- Belle the owl monkey was trained to control a robotic arm in a lab six hundred miles away using only her thoughts, detected 300 milliseconds before actual movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p213]
- Monkeys were taught to use only thoughts to move a robotic arm in any direction in three-dimensional space to perform complex reaching and grasping movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p213]
- In July 2006, Matthew Nagle, paralyzed in all four limbs, was the first human to use the device; after four days of practice he could move a cursor, open e-mail, control TV, play games, and control a robotic arm. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p214]
- The goal is to implant a microelectrode array with batteries and a transmitter the size of a baby's fingernail in the motor cortex to control robotic arms, wheelchairs, or muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p214]
