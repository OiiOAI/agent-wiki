# James C. Davies

## Summary

James C. Davies is a social scientist and political theorist who proposed a theory about the causes of revolution. His thesis holds that revolutions are most likely to occur not among the chronically oppressed, but among people who have experienced a period of improving conditions followed by a sharp reversal. This 'Davies Thesis' was supported by evidence from the French, Russian, and Egyptian revolutions, Dorr's Rebellion, the American Civil War, and the 1960s urban black riots in America.

## Key facts

- Davies states that revolutions are most likely where a period of improving economic and social conditions is followed by a short, sharp reversal in those conditions. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p231]
- According to Davies, it is not the traditionally most downtrodden people who are especially liable to revolt, but those who have been given at least some taste of a better life. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p231]
- Davies gathered evidence from the French, Russian, and Egyptian revolutions as well as Dorr's Rebellion, the American Civil War, and the urban black riots of the 1960s. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p232]
- Davies analyzed how two decades after WWII brought dramatic political and economic gains to American blacks, followed by a reversal that contributed to 1960s urban violence. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p232]

## Inferences

- Inference: Davies's theory represents a specific application of the broader psychological principle that newly experienced scarcity (in this case, loss of social and economic progress) is more motivating than chronic deprivation.
