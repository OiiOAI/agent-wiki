# Thomas Bayes

## Summary

Thomas Bayes (1702–1761) was a Presbyterian minister, philosopher, and statistician who lived in Tunbridge Wells, England. He never published the theorem immortalizing his name; his 'Essay towards Solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances' was presented to the Royal Society two years after his death by Richard Price.

## Key facts

- Thomas Bayes lived from 1702 to 1761 and was a Presbyterian minister, philosopher, and statistician. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p100]
- Bayes' theorem was published posthumously, presented to the Royal Society by Richard Price two years after Bayes' death. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p100]
- Bayes' name is tied to 'inference to the best explanation' — how to make optimal inferences under uncertainty. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p100]
- Pierre-Simon Laplace also contributed significantly to Bayesian mathematical development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p100]
- Bayes derived the formula P(S|T) P(T) = P(T|S) P(S), enabling estimation of inverse probabilities from forward probabilities. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L590-L610]
- Bayes showed that inverse probability is harder for humans to assess intuitively because causation flows forward from cause to effect, making backward inference require more information. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L575-L590]
- Bayes's rule can be interpreted as a normative rule for updating beliefs in response to evidence, asserting that more surprising evidence should increase conviction in its cause. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L590-L605]
- Bayes's rule in its simplest form represents the minimal Bayesian network with one link between hypothesis and evidence. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L530-L545]
