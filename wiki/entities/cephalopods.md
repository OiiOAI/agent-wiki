# Cephalopods

## Summary

Cephalopods are a group of mollusks including octopuses, cuttlefish, squid, and nautiluses. They are remarkable for having evolved large, complex nervous systems—an island of mental complexity in the sea of invertebrate animals. Their evolution from shelled ancestors to active predators represents an independent experiment in the evolution of large brains, having diverged from vertebrates approximately 600 million years ago.

## Key facts

- Cephalopods are a subgroup within mollusks, related to clams and snails but having evolved large nervous systems and complex behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p13]
- Cephalopods independently evolved camera eyes with a lens that focuses an image on a retina, the same design as vertebrate eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p60]
- Early cephalopods evolved from cap-shaped shellfish by filling their shells with gas to become buoyant, enabling them to rise from the sea floor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p38]
- Cephalopods invented jet propulsion by directing water through a tube-like siphon and freed their foot for grasping objects, sprouting tentacles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p39]
- The lineage split into two main branches: an eight-armed group including octopuses and a ten-armed group including cuttlefish and squid. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p40]
- Cephalopods never went onto land, unlike other mollusks, and represent a non-social form of intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p55]

## Inferences

- Inference: Because the human-cephalopod common ancestor was so simple and lies so far back, cephalopods represent an independent experiment in the evolution of large brains rather than sharing cognitive heritage with vertebrates.
