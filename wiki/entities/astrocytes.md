# Astrocytes

## Summary

Astrocytes are a major class of glial cells in the central nervous system characterized by their star-like shape and broad end-feet on their processes. They owe their name to their irregular, roughly star-shaped cell bodies and large numbers of processes. Astrocytes support neurons and modulate neuronal signaling in several ways: they buffer extracellular K+ concentrations, regulate neurotransmitter levels (particularly glutamate), maintain the blood-brain barrier, and actively participate in synapse formation and function. They express many voltage-gated ion channels and neurotransmitter receptors, enabling bidirectional communication with neurons.

## Key facts

- Astrocytes are divided into two main classes: protoplasmic astrocytes in gray matter with processes closely associated with synapses and blood vessels, and fibrillary astrocytes in white matter that contact axons and nodes of Ranvier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p154]
- Astrocytes express high-affinity transporters that rapidly clear glutamate from the synaptic cleft, and once within the glial cell, glutamate is converted to glutamine by glutamine synthetase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p154]
- Astrocytes secrete synaptogenic factors including thrombospondins, hevin, and glycipans that promote the formation of new synapses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p159]
- Astrocytes ensheathe small arterioles and capillaries throughout the brain, forming contacts between astrocyte process ends and the basal lamina around endothelial cells, supporting the blood-brain barrier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p159]
- Following brain injury and disease, astrocytes undergo reactive astrocytosis involving changes in gene expression, morphology, and signaling, with recent studies identifying both beneficial and harmful reactive astrocyte subtypes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p159]
