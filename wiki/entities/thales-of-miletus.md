# Thales of Miletus

## Summary

Pre-Socratic philosopher and mathematician from Miletus (Phoenician stock) who demonstrated antifragility through optionality. Taleb uses Thales's olive press contract as the archetype of asymmetry—the first recorded option. Aristotle mistakenly attributed Thales's success to superior astronomical knowledge, but Taleb argues the actual lesson is the power of the option structure itself, not prediction.

## Key facts

- Thales put a down payment on the seasonal use of every olive press in Miletus and Chios at low rent; when harvest was bountiful, he profited substantially by renting the presses at his terms. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p224]
- Aristotle claimed Thales used superior astronomical knowledge to predict the olive harvest; Taleb identifies this as the central error—Thales exploited the asymmetry of the option, not prediction. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p225]
- The olive press contract is the archetype of an option: 'the right but not the obligation' for the buyer, 'the obligation but not the right' for the seller—pure asymmetry. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p226]
- Thales did not need to understand messages from the stars; he only needed the asymmetry of the contract structure to profit regardless of his forecasting accuracy. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p225]

## Inferences

- Inference: Taleb's reanalysis of Thales demonstrates that success often comes from structural advantages (optionality) rather than superior knowledge, a theme that recurs throughout Antifragile.
