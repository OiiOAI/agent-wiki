# Northwest Airlines Flight 255

## Summary

Northwest Airlines Flight 255 (MD-82) crashed on August 16, 1987, shortly after departure from Detroit Metropolitan Airport, killing 154 persons. The accident was caused by the crew's failure to extend flaps and slats during takeoff, resulting in inadequate lift. The investigation illustrates how flight crew situation awareness regarding aircraft status can be faulty even when relevant information is available and checklists are provided.

## Key facts

- The accident occurred on August 16, 1987, when Northwest Airlines MD-80 Flight 255 crashed shortly after departure from Detroit Metropolitan Airport. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
- The crash killed 154 persons. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
- Initial witness reports described flames being emitted from the engines, but DFDR data subsequently showed that flaps and slats had been retracted during takeoff. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
- Without flaps and slats extended, the airfoils were incapable of providing necessary lift for the initial climbout. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
- The CVR revealed that pilots failed to check the status of flaps and slats after their taxi checklist was interrupted by an ATC clearance. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
- The crew believed almost up to impact that the difficulty in climbout was due to weather factors, not an improper aircraft configuration. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p119]
