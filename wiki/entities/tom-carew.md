# Tom Carew

## Summary

Tom Carew was a physiological psychologist from UC Riverside who joined Kandel's lab in 1971 and opened up the study of long-term memory. He became good friends with Schwartz, Spencer, and Kandel, soaking up the culture of the group including shared interests in art, music, and scientific gossip.

## Key facts

- Carew joined Kandel's lab at NYU in 1971, describing himself as a gifted, energetic, and gregarious physiological psychologist. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p203]
- Carew and Kandel found that long-term memory in Aplysia requires repeated training interspersed with periods of rest. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p203]
- They discovered that forty stimuli administered consecutively result in habituation lasting one day, but ten stimuli every day for four days produce habituation lasting weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p203]
- Carew, Kupfermann, and Kandel demonstrated that the gill-withdrawal reflex can be modified by nonassociative learning with short- and long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p204]
- In 1983, they succeeded in producing classical conditioning of the gill-withdrawal reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p204]
