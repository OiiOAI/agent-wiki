# Steven Mann

## Summary

Steven Mann is a scholar who published 'Chaos Theory and Strategic Thought' (Parameters, 1992), one of three papers on war and chaoplexity in Boyd's list. Mann lists four factors shaping the criticality of a strategic system: initial shape of the system, underlying structure, cohesion among actors, and conflict energy of individual actors.

## Key facts

- Mann lists four factors of the environment of a strategic entity which shape the criticality of a system: (1) initial shape of the system, (2) underlying structure of the system, (3) cohesion among the actors, (4) conflict energy of the individual actors. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]
- Mann states that effective treaties and compacts can slow the progress of a system towards criticality, but that absolute stability is unattainable in international affairs. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]
- Mann proposes using criticality to one's advantage by aiming to shape the broad context of security affairs, with policy efforts centered on achieving cohesion and mitigating conflict energy. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]
- Mann concludes that we must develop an encompassing definition of strategy not simply as a match of means to ends but as a match of paradigm to the particular strategic challenge. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p169]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mann's framework suggests that policy makers have some influence only over the latter two factors (cohesion and conflict energy), implying that strategic planning should focus primarily on these controllable variables.
