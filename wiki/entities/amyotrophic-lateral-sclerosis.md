# Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

## Summary

Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS) is a progressive motor neuron disease affecting both upper and lower motor neurons, causing muscle weakness, wasting, fasciculations, and eventual paralysis. Most cases (90%) are sporadic with unknown cause, while about 10% are inherited, often due to mutations in genes such as C9orf72, SOD1, or TDP-43.

## Key facts

- ALS affects both upper and lower motor neurons, causing weakness, wasting, fasciculations, and hyperreflexia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- About 10% of ALS cases are inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Greater than 25% of inherited ALS cases arise from expansions in the C9orf72 gene (hexanucleotide repeat). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Mutations in SOD1 encode copper/zinc superoxide dismutase and account for about 20% of familial ALS cases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- TDP-43 is an RNA-interacting protein that is normally intranuclear but mislocalizes to the cytosol in most ALS cases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Motor neurons supplying ocular muscles and bladder sphincters are typically spared in ALS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- ALS involves both upper and lower motor neurons, causing weakness with wasting, fasciculations, hyperreflexia, and Babinski sign. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p361]
- Approximately 10% of ALS cases are inherited as dominant traits, with 25% of familial cases caused by mutations in the SOD1 gene encoding copper/zinc superoxide dismutase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p361]
- The disease is progressive and ultimately affects muscles of respiration; there is no effective treatment and it is fatal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p361]
- Mice expressing high levels of mutant SOD1 develop adult-onset motor neuron disease and die, while mice with high levels of normal SOD1 do not develop paralysis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p361]

## Inferences

- Inference: The selective vulnerability of certain motor neurons while others are spared suggests that different neuronal populations have distinct molecular characteristics that confer protection or susceptibility.
- Inference: The dominantly inherited nature of SOD1-linked ALS, where mutant protein causes disease even with normal wild-type protein present, suggests the mutant acquires a toxic property rather than simply losing function.
