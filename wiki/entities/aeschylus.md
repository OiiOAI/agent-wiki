# Aeschylus

## Summary

Aeschylus (c. 525–456 BC), generally accepted as the founder of Greek tragedy, fought at Marathon and Salamis. He was an initiate at the Eleusinian mysteries and received divine inspiration via a dream in which Dionysus exhorted him to write tragedy. His description of Prometheus' fate is profoundly moving and compassionate, yet recounts the pain from hubristic attempt to seize what belongs to another realm. Schlegel considered Prometheus Bound to be 'Tragedy herself.' Sophocles said of Aeschylus: 'Aeschylus does what is right without knowing it'—a statement McGilchrist sees as indicating Aeschylus's debt to right-hemisphere processing.

## Key facts

- Aeschylus is generally accepted as the founder of Greek tragedy and was so designated by A. W. Schlegel: 'In full panoply she sprung from his head, like Pallas from the head of Jupiter.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p380]
- He was a brave soldier who fought at Marathon and Salamis, so proud of this that his epitaph referred to his participation at Marathon but not to his pre-eminence as a playwright. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p380]
- According to Pausanias, Dionysus appeared to Aeschylus in a dream and exhorted him to write tragedy; his art was 'inextricably bound to the world of religion and its mysteries.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p381]
- Sophocles said of him, 'Aeschylus does what is right without knowing it'—which McGilchrist sees as indicating debt to the workings of the right hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p381]
- Schlegel considered Prometheus Bound to be 'Tragedy herself': 'The other productions of the Greek Tragedians are so many tragedies; but this I might say is Tragedy herself.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p380]
