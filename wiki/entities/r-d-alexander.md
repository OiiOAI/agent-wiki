# R. D. Alexander

## Summary

Richard D. Alexander is an American evolutionary biologist who proposed the 'parental manipulation' theory as an alternative explanation for altruism—suggesting parents might manipulate offspring into being altruistic for the parents' genetic benefit. He also argued that parents must always win parent-offspring conflicts because genes for juvenile selfishness would reduce the individual's fitness when it becomes an adult. The book critically evaluates and rejects these arguments.

## Key facts

- Alexander proposed 'parental manipulation' as an alternative evolutionary cause of altruistic behavior, independent of kin selection—suggesting parents manipulate offspring into altruism for the parents' genetic benefit. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p170]
- Alexander argued that parents will always win parent-offspring conflicts because a gene for juvenile selfishness would reduce the individual's fitness when it becomes an adult parent, as its own children would inherit the same selfish gene. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p171]
- Dawkins counters Alexander's argument by showing it rests on false genetic asymmetry: the relatedness between parent and child (50%) is the same as between siblings, so applying the same logic in reverse would prove children always win, which cannot both be true. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p171]
