# Albert Einstein

## Summary

Albert Einstein delivered a 1918 speech quoted in the book regarding the motives that bring people to science. Phædrus identified with Einstein's description of those who enter the temple of science to escape everyday life and pursue truth from 'a finely tempered nature.' Einstein's views on intuition and sympathy as sources of scientific knowledge particularly influenced Phædrus's own questioning of scientific method.

## Key facts

- Einstein described scientists who come to the temple of science seeking escape from everyday life with its 'painful crudity and hopeless dreariness' and 'fetters of one's own shifting desires.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p93]
- Einstein stated, 'The supreme task is to arrive at those universal elementary laws from which the cosmos can be built up by pure deduction. There is no logical path to these laws; only intuition, resting on sympathetic understanding of experience, can reach them.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p94]
- Einstein said, 'Evolution has shown that at any given moment out of all conceivable constructions a single one has always proved itself absolutely superior to the rest.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p95]
