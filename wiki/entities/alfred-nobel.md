# Alfred Nobel

## Summary

Alfred Nobel (1833–1896) was a Swedish inventor who developed dynamite and accumulated a fortune of 31 million Swedish kronen. His will established the Nobel Prizes, first awarded on December 10, 1901. Nobel specified five fields: physics, chemistry, physiology or medicine, literature, and peace. The prizes were to honor those who had 'conferred the greatest benefit on mankind' during the preceding year.

## Key facts

- Alfred Nobel was born in Stockholm in 1833, left Sweden at age nine, and developed more than three hundred patents, with dynamite making his fortune. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p403]
- In 1866 Nobel discovered that liquid nitroglycerin absorbed by kieselguhr became stable as dynamite, which could be shaped into sticks needing only a detonator to explode. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p403]
- When Nobel died on December 10, 1896, he left an estate of 31 million Swedish kronen, equivalent to $9 million, establishing prizes for physics, chemistry, physiology or medicine, literature, and peace. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p403]
- The first Nobel Prizes were awarded on December 10, 1901, the fifth anniversary of Nobel's death, with each award being 9 million Swedish kronen. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p404]
