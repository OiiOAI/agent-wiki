# Operational Error

## Summary

An Operational Error (OE) in air traffic control occurs when a controller allows less than the applicable minimum separation criteria between aircraft (or between an aircraft and an obstruction). Separation minima standards are defined in the ATC Handbook (7110.65F) and supplemental instructions. At flight levels between 29,000 ft and 45,000 ft, controllers must maintain either 2,000 ft vertical separation or 5 nautical miles horizontal separation. Below 29,000 ft under IFR, the requirements are 1,000 ft vertical or 5 nautical miles horizontal separation. OEs are severity-classified based on point values assigned to horizontal and vertical separation parameters at the time of closest approach: major errors (≥20 points), moderate errors (14-19 points), and minor errors (≤13 points).

- At flight levels 29,000-45,000 ft, en route controllers must maintain either 2,000 ft vertical separation or 5 mile horizontal separation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p92]
- Below FL 290 under IFR, en route controllers must maintain either 1,000 ft vertical separation or 5 mile horizontal separation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p92]
- A major error is defined as less than 0.5 mi horizontal separation and less than 500 ft vertical separation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
- OE severity is calculated by a subroutine of the OE database using point assignments for horizontal and vertical separation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
