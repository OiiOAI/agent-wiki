# Attractiveness and Legal Sentencing Research

## Summary

Research examining how physical attractiveness influences legal outcomes demonstrates that attractive defendants receive more favorable treatment in the justice system. Studies show that even when attractive defendants are found guilty, they are less likely to be sentenced to prison compared to less attractive defendants convicted of similar offenses.

## Key facts

- Attractive defendants, even when found guilty, are less likely to be sentenced to prison than less attractive defendants. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p258]
- A New York City study of jail inmates with facial disfigurements found that those given plastic surgery were significantly less likely to return to jail one year after release, compared to those who did not receive surgery. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p258]
- The plastic surgery effect on recidivism was equally true for criminals who did NOT receive traditional rehabilitative services as for those who did, suggesting cosmetic surgery might be at least as effective as rehabilitation. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p258]
- Pennsylvania data (Stewart, 1980) challenged the rehabilitation argument for surgery, suggesting that making an ugly criminal more attractive may reduce chances of being sent to jail rather than reducing the likelihood of committing another crime. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p258]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Pennsylvania data suggests the appearance-recidivism link may operate through juror/judge perceptions rather than actual behavioral change, raising ethical questions about cosmetic interventions in prison systems.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The 1968 New York study (Kurtzburg et al.) did not include heroin addicts in its positive findings, suggesting the effect may not generalize to all types of offenders.
