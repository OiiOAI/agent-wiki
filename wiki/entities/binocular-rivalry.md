# Binocular Rivalry

## Summary

Binocular rivalry is a visual phenomenon that occurs when different images are presented to each eye simultaneously. Consciousness alternates between the two images approximately every few seconds, allowing researchers to dissociate conscious perception from physical stimulation. It has become a key tool for studying the neural correlates of consciousness.

## Key facts

- Binocular rivalry occurs when different visual stimuli are presented simultaneously to each eye, causing awareness to alternate between them rather than perceiving both simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p484]
- During binocular rivalry, perceptual experience fluctuates while the physical stimulus remains constant, providing a unique opportunity to study neural correlates of consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p484]
- Waves of neural activity propagate across primary visual cortex (V1) correlated with perceptual changes during binocular rivalry, traveling at approximately 2 cm/s across the cortical surface. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p488]
- When subjects are distracted and do not consciously perceive rival stimuli, waves of activity are still evident in V1 but not in V2 and V3, suggesting V1 activity alone is insufficient for conscious perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p488]
- Higher-order visual areas in the inferior temporal lobe and areas in parietal and prefrontal cortex also show activity correlated with perceptual alternations during binocular rivalry. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p488]

## Inferences

- Inference: Conscious perception during binocular rivalry requires activity in higher-order visual and prefrontal areas beyond V1, suggesting that consciousness emerges from distributed processing across multiple brain regions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanism by which attention modulates the competition between rival stimuli remains unclear.
