# John Blunt

## Summary

John Blunt (1665-1733) was an English businessman who became one of the lead directors of the South Sea Company. Rising from humble origins as a shoemaker's son, he orchestrated the infamous South Sea Bubble of 1720, a speculative mania that ended in financial disaster for thousands of investors.

## Key facts

- Blunt was the son of a humble shoemaker and from early on aimed to ascend to the highest levels of English society through the financial revolution. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p190]
- In October 1719 he proposed that the South Sea Company take over the English government's massive debt of £31 million and privatize it as tradeable shares. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p191]
- The scheme depended entirely on the share price rising continuously, which it did, reaching over £1,000 by June 1720. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p195]
- He was knighted in June 1720 as the share price peaked, becoming a baronet and England's most celebrated celebrity. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p195]
- When the Bubble Act he helped pass backfired and the scheme began collapsing, he launched increasingly desperate measures including promises of 30% Christmas dividends. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p195]
- After the crash, he was hounded in the streets, nearly killed by an assassin, and spent the rest of his life in Bath on modest means after Parliament seized his earnings. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p196]

## Inferences

- Inference: Blunt's downfall illustrates how shortsightedness—his inability to look beyond the immediate success—led to ignoring the fundamental lack of real assets backing the scheme.
