# Joseph Stalin

## Summary

Joseph Stalin (1879–1953) was the Premier of the Soviet Union who exemplifies the Complete Control Narcissist type. He initially charmed lieutenants with apparent warmth and accessibility, but as his power grew he became increasingly paranoid, controlling, and prone to purging and executing former friends and colleagues.

## Key facts

- Early in his reign, Stalin charmed lieutenants by encouraging the familiar 'you' form in Russian, making himself accessible, and listening with intense interest. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p74]
- Stalin placed his arm around lieutenants when escorting them out, creating an illusion of intimate bonds. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p74]
- Stalin had Sergey Kirov, his closest confidant and Politburo member, assassinated in December 1934. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p76]
- During the great purge of the late 1930s, hundreds of thousands were arrested, including almost all of Stalin's top lieutenants who were tortured for confessions. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p77]
- Stalin increasingly spoke of himself in the third person, as if he had become an impersonal and infallible revolutionary force. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p77]
- Stalin delighted in forcing lieutenants to drink heavily during late-night gatherings at his house, enjoying their loss of self-control. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p78]
- Stalin died in 1953 from a stroke; his lieutenants neglected to help him or call a doctor due to their fear and resentment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p79]

## Inferences

- Inference: Stalin's childhood included a father who beat him mercilessly and a cold, unloving mother, which Greene suggests contributed to his deep narcissistic development.
