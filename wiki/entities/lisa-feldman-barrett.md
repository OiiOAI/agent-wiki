# Lisa Feldman Barrett

## Summary

Lisa Feldman Barrett is a psychologist and neuroscientist whose groundbreaking book 'How Emotions Are Made' introduced the concept of body budgeting—the idea that the brain tracks and anticipates the use and replenishment of key resources for maintaining bodily functioning.

## Key facts

- Lisa Feldman Barrett is a psychologist and neuroscientist who wrote the groundbreaking book 'How Emotions Are Made.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
- Barrett captures the bedrock role of predictions in maintaining viable bodily state using the metaphor of body budgeting, tracking income and expenditure of resources like water, salt, and glucose. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p96]
- Barrett argues that the whole of our mental lives reflects the brain's busy and deeply anticipatory body-budgeting activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p98]
- Barrett proposes that every brain region central to the generation and experience of emotion is a body-budgeting region. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p98]
- Barrett describes depression as a disorder of allostasis involving inefficient energy regulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p105]
- Barrett coined the term affective realism to describe how inner bodily sensations may be taken as evidence for active threat in perception. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p128]
- Barrett is a Distinguished University Professor at Northeastern University and directs the Interdisciplinary Affective Science Laboratory. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p14]
- Her graduate research initially failed to replicate well-established findings, but she discovered that people often cannot distinguish between feeling anxious and depressed, leading to her concept of emotional granularity. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p14]
- Barrett proposes that the theory of constructed emotion incorporates elements of social construction, psychological construction, and neuroconstruction. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p49]
- Lisa Feldman Barrett is a University Distinguished Professor of Psychology at Northeastern University. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p546]
- She holds appointments at Harvard Medical School and Massachusetts General Hospital in psychiatry and radiology. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p546]
- She received an NH Director's Pioneer Award for groundbreaking research on emotion in the brain. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p546]
- She authored the book 'How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain' published by HMH. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p547]
- Barrett argues 'emotions are made and not triggered,' meaning each person builds their own culturally-specific 'operating instructions' for their face over their lifetime. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]
- Barrett is described as 'one of the leaders in challenging the Ekman view' of universal emotions. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]
- Her book 'How Emotions Are Made' (2017) presents her theory that the face is 'a symbol of how different human beings are, not how similar we are.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p211]
- Barrett co-authored a paper in Psychological Science in the Public Interest reconsidering emotional expressions and challenging the view that emotion can be reliably inferred from facial movements. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p212]

## Inferences

- Inference: Barrett's work bridges neuroscience, psychology, and clinical practice, providing a unifying framework for understanding how prediction errors and body-budgeting underlie both normal emotional experience and psychiatric conditions [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p105]
- Inference: Barrett's work challenges the evidentiary basis of lie detection and other systems that assume facial expressions reliably reveal inner emotional states.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which Barrett's findings replicate across diverse cultural contexts requires further investigation
