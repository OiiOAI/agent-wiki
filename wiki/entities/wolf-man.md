# Wolf Man

## Summary

A wealthy young Russian patient of Sigmund Freud, known in psychoanalytic literature as the 'Wolf Man'. He came to Freud in January 1910 at age 23, and his treatment lasted until July 1914. His infantile neurosis began at age 3.5 with seduction by his elder sister, included the famous wolf dream at age 4, and developed into an obsessional neurosis with religious content. He later suffered from a severe adult neurosis that was initially diagnosed as manic-depressive insanity. After his treatment with Freud, he lived for many decades and left extensive autobiographical memoirs.

## Key facts

- He was born on December 24, 1886 (by the Julian calendar), which corresponded to January 6, 1887 by the Georgian calendar. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5277]
- He began psychoanalytic treatment with Freud in February 1910 at approximately 23 years of age. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5270]
- His health had broken down in his eighteenth year after a gonorrhoeal infection, and he was entirely incapacitated when treatment began. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5270]
- His elder sister, two years his senior, later committed suicide and was diagnosed with probable dementia praecox. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5282]
- The patient showed evidence of conspicuous neuropathic heredity, including an uncle with severe obsessional neurosis and his sister with dementia praecox. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5282]
- Muriel Gardiner compiled and edited 'The Wolf Man and Sigmund Freud' (1971), containing the patient's autobiographical material. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5266]
- The patient became very rich through legacies from his father and uncle, but had no idea how much he possessed, what his expenditure was, or what balance was left over. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5329]
- During his later illness he suffered from disturbances of his intestinal function which were very obstinate; spontaneous evacuations did not occur for months at a time unless sudden excitement intervened. - When it became possible during analytic treatment to liberate his shackled homosexuality, each piece of homosexual libido which was set free sought out some application in life and some attachment to the great common concerns of mankind. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5327]
- His identification with his father became the source of an intense unconscious hostility towards him reaching the pitch of a death wish, and of a sense of guilt which reacted against it. - He displayed a most extraordinary unwillingness to give the name of Matrona, his later love interest, asserting the reason was that it was a purely peasant name, though the shame was really displaced from the earlier Grusha scene. ## Inferences

- Inference: His object choice in adult life (falling in love exclusively with servants) was determined by his sister's rejection of his incestuous advance during puberty.
- Inference: The pseudonym 'Wolf Man' derives from the central wolf dream that triggered his infantile anxiety neurosis.
- Inference: The patient represented a contrast between his agreeable personality, acute intelligence, and nice-mindedness on one hand, and his completely unbridled drive activity on the other.
- Inference: His symptoms were strung together as though derived from a primal scene of a particular kind, though he may have constructed this on the model of his anal phantasy of rebirth.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact nature of the primal scene—whether it was a real observation or a phantasy—remained unresolved.
- Uncertain: The patient's age when he first showed symptoms of neurosis (when exactly the appetite disturbance began) was not exactly fixed.
