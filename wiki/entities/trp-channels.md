# Transient Receptor Potential Channels

## Summary

TRP (Transient Receptor Potential) channels are a family of ion channels expressed in sensory neuron terminals that mediate thermal and chemical sensations. They form nonselective cation channels with four identical protein subunits, each containing six transmembrane alpha-helices. Different TRP receptors respond to different temperature ranges: TRPM8 responds to cold (below 25 degrees C), TRPA1 to frigid temperatures (below 17 degrees C), TRPV3 to warm temperatures (above 35 degrees C), and TRPV1/TRPV2 to hot temperatures (above 43-52 degrees C). TRP channels are polymodal sensory integrators responding to temperature, pH, osmolarity, and chemical irritants.

## Key facts

- TRP channels form nonselective cation channels that mediate inward depolarizing current and are encoded by genes belonging to the same superfamily as voltage-gated channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p463]
- TRPM8 receptors respond to temperatures below 25 degrees C and are perceived as cool or cold; TRPA1 receptors have thresholds below 17 degrees C. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p463]
- TRPV1 and TRPV2 receptors respond to temperatures exceeding 45 degrees C and mediate sensations of burning pain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p464]
- TRP channels are polymodal sensory integrators that respond directly to changes in temperature, pH, or osmolarity, as well as to noxious substances such as capsaicin or toxins.
