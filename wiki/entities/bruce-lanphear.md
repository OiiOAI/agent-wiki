# Bruce Lanphear

## Summary

Bruce Lanphear is a professor of health sciences at Simon Fraser University in Canada who conducted groundbreaking research on the effects of lead exposure on children's cognitive abilities. His studies in Rochester, New York revealed that lead poisoning was causing widespread attention problems in children, and demonstrated the power of regulatory action to address environmental toxins.

## Key facts

- In Rochester, New York, he found that one in three children had lead poisoning, and for Black children it was one in two. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p223]
- He demonstrated that children exposed to lead were two and a half times more likely to meet criteria for ADHD. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p223]
- He argues chemicals should be treated as 'like drugs' and tested for safety before use, rather than assumed innocent until proven toxic. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p228]
- The EPA estimates 68 million children were exposed to toxic levels of lead in the U.S. from leaded petrol alone between 1927 and 1987. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p223]
