# Dominic Cinelli Case

## Summary

Dominic Cinelli was a violent criminal with a 30-year history who convinced the Massachusetts Parole Board in 2008 that he was reformed. The board unanimously voted to free him based partly on his apparent remorse. Within months he committed new robberies and fatally shot a police officer. This case illustrates that remorse detection is fundamentally unreliable because emotions have no objective criteria.

## Key facts

- Cinelli convinced the parole board he was no longer criminal; the board unanimously voted to free him in November 2008. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p255]
- Cinelli soon embarked on new robberies and fatally shot a police officer; he was later killed during a shootout with police. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p255]
- It's possible Cinelli authentically felt remorse while testifying, but once released his old model of the world resurfaced with his old predictions, and his remorse evaporated. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p255]
- There is no objective criterion for feelings of remorse, anger, sadness, fear, or any other emotion relevant to a trial. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p255]
