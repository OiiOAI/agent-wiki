# Libet's Readiness Potential Experiments

## Summary

Benjamin Libet's experiments at UCSF in 1983 examined the timing relationship between conscious intention and brain activity during voluntary movement. Following up on Kornhuber's discovery of the 'readiness potential' occurring 1 second before voluntary movement, Libet found that the readiness potential appeared 200 milliseconds before a person felt the urge to move. This raised profound questions about free will: if the brain decides before conscious awareness, where is free will? Libet proposed that consciousness is recruited just before action to approve or veto the movement, giving us 'free won't' even if not 'free will'.

## Key facts

- Hans Kornhuber discovered that voluntary movement is preceded by a 'readiness potential' in the brain occurring 1 second before the movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p397]
- Libet found that the readiness potential appeared 200 milliseconds before a person felt the conscious urge to move, allowing prediction of the decision from brain activity before conscious awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p397]
- Libet proposed that the process of initiating voluntary action occurs unconsciously, but consciousness is recruited just before action to approve or veto it, giving us 'free won't'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p398]
- Gregory and Ramachandran drew strict limits on the free will argument, pointing out that 'our conscious mind may not have free will, but it does have free won't'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p398]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Libet experiments support the view that much of our cognitive and affective life is unconscious, echoing Freud's concept of psychic determinism.
