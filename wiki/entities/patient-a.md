# Patient A

## Summary

Patient A was a young man who sought treatment for depression after becoming involved with a woman who encouraged him to abuse her. His history included relationships with emotionally unstable, demanding women who thrilled him, while 'nice' girls bored him. His alcoholic mother had been seductive and violent, and their relationship was highly sexualized. He experienced fusion of sex and aggression in his brain maps, and through therapy learned to redifferentiate these maps, eventually achieving a healthy marriage.

## Key facts

- Patient A was attracted to women who were already attached to other men and emotionally out of control, who were either demanding and possessive or castratingly cruel. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p103]
- His mother was a severe alcoholic who was frequently needy, seductive, given to emotional storms and violent rages, and the relationship with her was highly sexualized. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p103]
- A. experienced an almost perfect fusion in his mind of sex with aggression; he felt that to love someone was to consume her and that sexual intercourse was a violent act that excited him. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p130]
- Doidge treated Patient A by pointing out instances of physical tenderness and getting him to find instances where aggression was untainted with sex, allowing him to form two different brain maps over time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p131]
- By the end of therapy Patient A was in a healthy, satisfying, happy marriage; his character and sexual type had been radically transformed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p132]

## Inferences

- Inference: Patient A's case demonstrates that neuroplastic principles can be applied therapeutically to treat problematic sexual patterns acquired during critical periods.
- Inference: The treatment approach of redifferentiating fused brain maps may have applications beyond this single case.
