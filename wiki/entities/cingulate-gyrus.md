# Cingulate Gyrus

## Summary

The cingulate gyrus is located in the deepest part of the cortex and is responsible for triggering the dreadful anxiety associated with the 'mistake feeling.' Once activated by the orbital frontal cortex, it sends signals to both the gut and the heart, causing the physical sensations of dread. In OCD, the cingulate is hyperactive and locked in the 'on position' together with the orbital frontal cortex.

## Key facts

- The cingulate gyrus is located in the deepest part of the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- The cingulate triggers the dreadful anxiety that something bad is going to happen unless we correct the mistake. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- The cingulate sends signals to both the gut and the heart, causing the physical sensations we associate with dread. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- Once the orbital frontal cortex has fired the 'mistake feeling,' it sends a signal to the cingulate gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- In OCD, both the orbital frontal cortex and the cingulate turn on and stay on as though locked in the 'on position.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]

## Inferences

- Inference: The cingulate gyrus serves as a bridge between cognitive error detection and the body's physical stress response, explaining why OCD produces both mental anguish and physical symptoms of anxiety.
