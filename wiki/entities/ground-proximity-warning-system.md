# Ground Proximity Warning System

## Summary

The Ground Proximity Warning System (GPWS) is an aircraft alerting system designed to warn pilots of proximity to terrain. The system has been criticized for high false alarm rates at certain airports due to its inability to account for flight path information. A study found that 26% of controlled flight into terrain (CFIT) accidents involved no response, slow response, or incorrect response to GPWS alarms.

## Key facts

- 26% of controlled flight into terrain (CFIT) accidents over a 17-year period involved no response, slow response, or incorrect response to GPWS alarms. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p148]
- GPWS produces frequent false alarms at certain airports where the approach flight path brings aircraft near terrain even though pilots are on safe trajectories. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p151]
- Pilots delay response to GPWS alarms approximately 73% of the time while seeking confirming evidence. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p152]
