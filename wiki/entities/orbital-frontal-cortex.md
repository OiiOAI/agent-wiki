# Orbital Frontal Cortex

## Summary

The orbital frontal cortex is part of the frontal lobe located on the underside of the brain, just behind the eyes. It is responsible for detecting mistakes, generating the 'mistake feeling' that tells us something is wrong. In OCD, brain scans show that the more obsessive a person is, the more activated the orbital frontal cortex becomes, and it remains locked in the 'on position' together with the cingulate gyrus.

## Key facts

- The orbital frontal cortex is part of the frontal lobe on the underside of the brain, just behind the eyes, and is involved in detecting mistakes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- The orbital frontal cortex generates the 'mistake feeling,' that nagging sense that something is wrong. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- Scans show that the more obsessive a person is, the more activated the orbital frontal cortex is. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- Once the orbital frontal cortex fires the 'mistake feeling,' it sends a signal to the cingulate gyrus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- In OCD, the orbital frontal cortex turns on and stays on as though locked in the 'on position.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- Because the caudate doesn't shift gears automatically, the orbital frontal cortex continues to fire off signals, increasing the mistake feeling and anxiety. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]

## Inferences

- Inference: The orbital frontal cortex's role in mistake detection suggests it is part of a broader error-monitoring system that, when dysfunctional, leads to persistent doubt and checking behaviors.
