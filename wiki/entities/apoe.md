# APOE

## Summary

APOE (apolipoprotein E) is a gene that exists in three common variants: e2, e3, and e4. The APOE e4 variant significantly increases the risk of Alzheimer's disease, with two copies (e4/e4) associated with up to twelve times greater risk than the common e3/e3 genotype. APOE also plays important roles in cholesterol transport and glucose metabolism in the brain.

## Key facts

- APOE e4/e4 genotype is associated with up to twelve times greater risk of developing Alzheimer's disease compared to e3/e3 carriers. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p186]
- APOE e4/e4 occurs in only about 2-3% of the population. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p186]
- APOE e2 appears to protect against Alzheimer's disease: approximately 10% reduced risk for e2/e3, and 20% for e2/e2. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p186]
- The APOE e4 protein differs from e3 by just one amino acid but appears less efficient at moving cholesterol into and especially out of the brain. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p206]
- APOE e4 may cause early breakdown of the blood-brain barrier, making the brain more susceptible to injury and degeneration. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p206]
- APOE e4 also significantly raises the risk of Lewy body dementia and Parkinson's disease with dementia. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p193]
- The e3 mutation appeared approximately 225,000 years ago, while e2 is a relative latecomer from the last 10,000 years; e4 was the original human allele. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p206]
- A woman with one copy of e4 is four times more likely to develop Alzheimer's than a man with the same genotype. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p206]
- The Klotho gene variant (KL-VS) appears to protect carriers of e4 from developing dementia. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p187]

## Inferences

- Inference: APOE e4 may have persisted evolutionarily because it promoted beneficial inflammation in environments with high infectious disease burden.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The clinical significance of dramatic blood glucose spikes after eating in young e4 carriers on continuous glucose monitoring is unclear.
