# Wisconsin Card Sort Test

## Summary

The Wisconsin Card Sort Test is a neuropsychological assessment that evaluates executive function and cognitive flexibility. Subjects must discover and apply sorting rules (number, shape, or color) based on feedback, then adapt when rules change without warning. It is highly sensitive to dorsolateral prefrontal cortex damage.

## Key facts

- In the Wisconsin Card Sort Test, subjects select cards from a deck and place them next to one of four samples, discovering by trial and error whether the correct strategy is based on number, shape, or color of symbols. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p456]
- Patients with dorsolateral prefrontal cortex lesions persist in using unsuccessful strategies, making perseverative errors even after rules change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p456]
- Patients with prefrontal damage also make capricious errors, abandoning successful rules unnecessarily, as if conscious oversight of behavior has been weakened. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p456]
- The test was developed by Adam Love and Barry Gordon and published in 1948, becoming one of the most widely used tests of executive function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p456]

## Inferences

- Inference: Performance on the Wisconsin Card Sort Test depends on the ability to inhibit prepotent responses and flexibly update rules based on feedback, functions attributed to the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex.
