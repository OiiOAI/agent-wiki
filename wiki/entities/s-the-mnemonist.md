# S. (The Mnemonist)

## Summary

S. was a mnemonist studied by Aleksandr Luria who possessed synesthetic memory abilities, experiencing vivid images triggered by words, sounds, and sensory impressions. His exceptional figurative memory enabled remarkable feats but interfered with understanding poetry, abstract concepts, and scientific material. He could control autonomic functions such as heart rate and body temperature through visualization.

## Key facts

- S. was studied by Luria over many years, with records dating from at least 1934 to 1938. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p131]
- S. could not understand poetry because each expression gave rise to images that conflicted with one another, creating chaos. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p131]
- S. could alter his pulse rate from a resting 70-72 beats per minute to 100 or slow it to 64-66 by imagining himself running or falling asleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p149]
- S. could raise the temperature of his right hand by two degrees and lower his left hand by one and a half degrees through visualization of hot and cold objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p150]
- S. reported using imaginative techniques to eliminate pain, visualizing pain as an orange-red thread that he could cut and make smaller. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p151]
- S. experienced a split between the 'I' who issued orders and a visualized 'he' who carried them out, persisting throughout his life. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p153]
- S. stated he could only understand what he could visualize, and when this proved impossible he would see 'puffs of steam' or 'lines' instead. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p141]
- S. changed jobs dozens of times, regarding all of them as merely 'temporary' while waiting for something particularly fine to happen. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p158]

## Inferences

- Inference: S.'s synesthetic experiences involved multiple senses simultaneously—when hearing music, he felt the taste of it on his tongue, suggesting cross-modal sensory connections.
- Inference: S.'s EEG showed depression of alpha waves when imagining bright light, suggesting his imagined experiences produced measurable neurological effects.
- Inference: S.'s difficulty with abstract concepts like 'nothing' and 'infinity' stemmed from his inability to form visual images of them, demonstrating the importance of visualization for his comprehension.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanisms behind S.'s autonomic control abilities were not fully explained by physiological research, as the experiments yielded 'negligible and rather indirect information'.
