# Ventromedial Hypothalamus

## Summary

The ventromedial hypothalamus (VMH) is a key brain region for long-term energy balance regulation. It contains metabolically sensitive neurons that integrate body energy status and modulate feeding behavior and body weight.

## Key facts

- The VMH contains insulin-sensitive neurons that may provide a long-term metabolic signal for body energy status. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]
- VMH-lesioned animals fail to detect hunger effectively, exhibit reduced motivation to work for food, and become obese only with palatable foods. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]
- The VMH appears to contain a long-term metabolic detector rather than a short-term meal-terminating satiety mechanism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]
- Local glucose infusions into the VMH decrease daily food intake cumulatively without affecting immediate meal size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p99]
- Gold-thio-glucose selectively destroys the VMH partly due to local insulin sensitivity that allows toxic accumulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]
- The VMH sits astride the pituitary stalk in an ideal position to orchestrate hormonal changes controlling body energy transactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: The VMH may regulate feeding through the GABA shunt of the Krebs cycle, linking energy metabolism to neural inhibition of foraging.
