# Alan Hodgkin

## Summary

Alan Hodgkin (1914-1998) was a British physiologist who, together with Andrew Huxley, developed the quantitative mathematical model of the action potential (the Hodgkin-Huxley model) using the voltage-clamp technique on the squid giant axon. He shared the 1963 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Huxley and Bernard Katz.

## Key facts

- Hodgkin and Huxley used voltage-clamp recordings to measure Na+ and K+ currents and developed empirical equations describing their voltage and time dependence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p260]
- Hodgkin and Katz demonstrated that the action potential amplitude is reduced when external Na+ concentration is lowered, establishing that Na+ influx causes the rising phase.
