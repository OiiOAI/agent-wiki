# F-X Project

## Summary

The F-X was the U.S. Air Force's troubled fighter development project that would eventually become the F-15 Eagle. John Boyd's work on this project represented his attempt to apply Energy-Maneuverability principles to actual aircraft design, fighting against institutional pressure for larger, heavier, swing-wing multi-role aircraft.

## Key facts

- The F-X was designed as a swing-wing behemoth of approximately 70,000 pounds. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p185]
- The proposed F-X reminded many of the troubled F-111. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p185]
- Boyd wanted to reduce the F-X weight to approximately 35,000 pounds with only one engine. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p206]
- Boyd rejected the initial F-X design package, saying he 'could fuck up and do better'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p194]
- Boyd insisted the F-X have an internal gun while Wright-Pat wanted only missiles. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p206]
- The F-X was the first U.S. fighter designed with maneuvering specifications and dogfighting in mind. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p205]
