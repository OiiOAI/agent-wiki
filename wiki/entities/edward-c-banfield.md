# Edward C. Banfield

## Summary

Edward C. Banfield (1915-1999) was an American political scientist and sociologist who conducted pioneering research on the relationship between social trust and economic development. His study of a southern Italian community he called Montegrano (actually Chiaromonte) resulted in the influential work 'The Moral Basis of a Backward Society' (1958), which documented the phenomenon of amoral familism.

## Key facts

- Banfield conducted his field research in the 1950s studying a community in southern Italy. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- His book 'The Moral Basis of a Backward Society' (1958) examined why certain communities fail to develop economically despite resources. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Banfield identified 'amoral familism' as the dominant social pattern in the community, where individuals acted only for their nuclear family's benefit. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]

## Inferences

- Inference: Banfield's work became foundational for understanding how social trust patterns affect economic development, influencing later scholars like Robert Putnam.
