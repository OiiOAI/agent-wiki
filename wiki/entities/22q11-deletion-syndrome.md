# 22q11 Deletion Syndrome

## Summary

22q11 deletion syndrome is the most common chromosomal abnormality associated with schizophrenia and childhood-onset schizophrenia. The deletion was initially described as velocardiofacial syndrome or DiGeorge syndrome. Studies suggest that multiple genes within this interval, each conferring relatively small individual effects, are responsible for the psychiatric phenotype. Loss and gain of the same region can lead to contrasting social phenotypes.

## Key facts

- The 22q11 deletion is the most common chromosomal abnormality associated with schizophrenia and childhood-onset schizophrenia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p95]
- The specific genes within the 22q11 deletion region responsible for the psychiatric phenotype have not been definitively established. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p95]
