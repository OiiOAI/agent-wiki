# William J. LeMessurier

## Summary

William J. LeMessurier was the structural engineer who designed the steel skeleton of the Citicorp Tower in Manhattan (built 1977, seventh-tallest building in the world at the time). He discovered a critical design flaw that could have led to catastrophic building failure: the wind braces were not adequately designed for diagonal (quartering) winds, and the joints had been changed from welded to bolted connections without his knowledge. He conducted the investigation himself, confirmed the problem through wind tunnel analyses, and then 'blew the whistle' on himself to get the repairs made before Hurricane Ella threatened New York. The repairs involved welding two-inch-thick steel plates over more than 200 vulnerable bolted joints, conducted secretly during evening hours over one month.

## Key facts

- LeMessurier discovered the problem by connecting two pieces of information: the wind braces were not as effective as designed against quartering winds, and the joints had been changed from welded to bolted. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p146]
- The building code only required calculations for perpendicular winds, not diagonal winds. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p146]
- The design team had classified diagonal wind braces as trusses to evade stringent safety specifications for joint strength. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p147]
- Weather data showed that dangerous quartering wind conditions occurred in New York City on average every sixteen years. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p147]
- Repairs involved welding two-inch-thick steel plates over more than 200 vulnerable joints during evening hours (5 PM to 4 AM). [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p147]
- LeMessurier titled his report 'Project SERENE' (Special Engineering Review of Events Nobody Envisioned). [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p146]
