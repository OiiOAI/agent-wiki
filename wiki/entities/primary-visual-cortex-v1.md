# Primary Visual Cortex (V1)

## Summary

The primary visual cortex (V1, striate cortex, Brodmann area 17) is the first cortical area to receive visual information from the lateral geniculate nucleus of the thalamus. It contains neurons with orientation selectivity, ocular dominance columns, and cytochrome oxidase blobs. V1 plays critical roles in both local analysis of orientation and global integration of visual features through horizontal connections.

## Key facts

- V1 receives input from the lateral geniculate nucleus, with parvocellular inputs projecting to layer IVCβ and magnocellular inputs to layer IVCα. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p620]
- V1 has a visuotopic organization systematically representing the visual field, with the fovea having the largest cortical magnification factor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p618]
- The dominant feature of V1 functional organization is the columnar arrangement of cells with similar properties extending from cortical surface to white matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p618]
- More area in V1 is dedicated to the central 10° of visual space than to all the rest, reflecting the greater resolution needed at the fovea. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p618]
