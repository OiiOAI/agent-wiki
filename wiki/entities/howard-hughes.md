# Howard Hughes

## Summary

Howard Hughes Jr. (1905–1976) was an American business magnate, film producer, and aviator whose life exemplifies the formation of compulsive character patterns. After losing both parents in his teens, he revealed a compulsive need for total control that manifested in micromanagement across his film and aircraft businesses, leading to repeated spectacular failures despite his inherited wealth.

## Key facts

- Howard Hughes's mother died in 1922 when he was 17, and his father died two years later, leaving him alone at 19 with complete control of the family fortune. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p133]
- After his parents' deaths, he immediately became abusive and rebellious against relatives who had tried to guide him, eventually cutting off almost all contact with them. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p134]
- His film Hell's Angels cost $3.8 million and lost $2 million, but the flying and action sequences thrilled audiences and created his legend as a maverick. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p135]
- During World War II, Hughes Aircraft failed to deliver any of the 100 photo-reconnaissance planes ordered for $43 million due to internal chaos and his compulsive micromanagement. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p137]
- His Spruce Goose flew only once for about a mile before being permanently grounded, representing his pattern of grandiose projects failing to meet expectations. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p137]
- When he bought RKO Pictures, he promised executives no interference, then immediately began replacing actresses and micromanaging productions, repeating his Hell's Angels pattern. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p138]
- His helicopter contract lost $90 million due to the same disorganization that plagued his earlier aircraft ventures—he had not learned a single lesson from past failures. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p139]
- At death in 1976, he weighed only 93 pounds, had lived in sealed hotel rooms fearing germs, and was addicted to pain pills and narcotics. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p139]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hughes's pattern of compulsive control behavior was established in childhood—his smothering mother created dependency anxieties that he later compulsively tried to overcome through total control of external circumstances.
