# Mike Wyly

## Summary

Mike Wyly was a decorated United States Marine Corps colonel who became John Boyd's sixth Acolyte and played a pivotal role in introducing maneuver warfare doctrine to the Marine Corps. He was instrumental in bringing Boyd's 'Patterns of Conflict' briefing to the Amphibious Warfare School, creating the Marine Corps' reading list, and helping to write FMFM-1 Warfighting, the Marine Corps' new tactics manual. He was forced into early retirement after confronting senior officers over maneuver warfare.

## Key facts

- Mike Wyly was a Marine Corps officer whose uncle, Captain Donald Duncan, led the 96th Company of the 6th Marines at Belleau Wood on June 6, 1918, and died attacking a German machine-gun nest. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p368]
- Wyly entered the Naval Academy as a private in the Marine Corps reserves and took his commission in the Marines. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p368]
- As a psychological-warfare officer in Vietnam in 1965, Wyly served a tour and observed that Marines were tough and disciplined but questioned whether they understood the war. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p369]
- In 1969 Wyly returned to Vietnam as a twenty-nine-year-old captain commanding Delta Company, 1st Battalion of the 5th Marines, which had the nickname 'Dying Delta.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p369]
- Wyly recommended James Webb for the Navy Cross after Webb led patrols demonstrating extraordinary courage, earning him the reputation among Marines that he could 'walk on water.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p369]
- Wyly called John Boyd in 1980 and asked about his theory of warfare; Boyd said it was not a theory but a five-hour briefing called 'Patterns of Conflict.'. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p372]
- Boyd's first briefing at the Amphibious Warfare School in January 1980 lasted eight hours, with students staying past nightfall, demonstrating the power of his ideas. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p373]
- Wyly became the sixth Acolyte of John Boyd and worked with him to introduce reading lists at the Amphibious Warfare School, a radical step for the Marine Corps. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p374]
- Wyly distributed hundreds of copies of Boyd's 'A Discourse on Winning and Losing' (the 'Green Book') to Marine officers at Quantico. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p375]
- Wyly was fired as head of tactics at the Amphibious Warfare School on February 25, 1982, his forty-second birthday. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p379]
- Boyd arranged for Wyly's transfer to the Office of the Secretary of Defense via a personal message from Deputy Secretary Frank Carlucci. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p380]
- Wyly served in Carlucci's office for two years in the 'black hole' and was promoted to full colonel during this time. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p380]
- Wyly wrote seminal articles on maneuver warfare for the Marine Corps Gazette, including 'Thinking Beyond the Beachhead,' born from Boyd's observation about focusing on the enemy rather than terrain. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p382]
- Wyly and G.I. Wilson arranged for James Webb to meet Al Gray, leading to Gray's appointment as Commandant of the Marine Corps. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p383]
- Wyly was assigned to help create the Marine Corps University and was given a mandate by Commandant Gray to codify new warfighting doctrine. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p384]
- Boyd and Wyly helped write FMFM-1 Warfighting, which became official Marine Corps doctrine, though Gray insisted it be numbered '1' to show a new beginning. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p385]
- Wyly was passed over for promotion to brigadier general and forced to retire after confronting a two-star general who belittled maneuver warfare presentations. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p388]
- Wyly received no retirement ceremony, no Legion of Merit, and no letter from the commandant when he left the Marine Corps. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p391]
- After retirement, Wyly bought a farm in Maine, started 'Wilderness Vineyard,' and turned around the struggling Bossov Ballet Theater using Marine Corps management principles. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p436]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wyly's experience in Vietnam, where he saw young Marines learning guerrilla tactics to survive, prepared him to appreciate and champion Boyd's maneuver warfare concepts.
- Inference: The Bradley controversy and the Gulf War both demonstrated the validity of the testing and tactical reforms championed by Boyd and his Acolytes.
