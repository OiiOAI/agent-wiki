# Al and Burt Dialogue on Smoking

## Summary

This detailed NVC demonstration shows Al, a nonsmoker, expressing concern to his best friend Burt about his smoking habit using nonviolent communication. The dialogue illustrates expressing feelings and needs without judgment, making genuine requests (not demands), and empathically listening to Burt's fears about failure and what others might think.

## Key facts

- Al expressed his concern saying 'I'm scared your damned cigarettes are going to kill you' while explicitly stating he wasn't judging Burt. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p129]
- Burt revealed he had tried to quit many times and was afraid people would think less of him if he failed again. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p129]
- Al empathized with Burt's fear of failure and self-image concerns, saying 'I don't think less of you. And if you tried and failed again, I still wouldn't.'. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p129]
- Burt said he didn't like smoking anymore and felt like a pariah in public. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p130]
- Al maintained his commitment to the relationship quality regardless of Burt's response, saying 'I'll still like you as much. It's just that I want to like you for longer.'. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p131]
- Because Al's request was a genuine request, not a demand, he maintained awareness of his commitment to the quality of the relationship regardless of Burt's response. [raw/books/psychology/Nonviolent Communication_ A Language of Li - Marshall B. Rosenberg.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dialogue demonstrates that genuine requests differ from demands in that they leave space for refusal without threatening the relationship, allowing for authentic dialogue about difficult topics.
