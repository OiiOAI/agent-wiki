# Milgram, Bickman, and Berkowitz (1967) Sky-Staring Experiment

## Summary

A landmark social psychology experiment conducted by Stanley Milgram, Leonard Bickman, and Leonard Berkowitz in New York City, demonstrating the powerful effect of social proof on crowd behavior. When four people stared at an empty spot on a building, 80 percent of passersby stopped to look up within sixty seconds, compared to virtually no one stopping when a single person performed the behavior.

## Key facts

- The experiment was conducted by three New York social psychologists on a busy sidewalk. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p255]
- When four people stared upward, 80 percent of all passersby lifted their gaze to the empty spot. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p255]
- The pressure to look up at least briefly was nearly irresistible for pedestrians who did not join the group. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p255]

## Inferences

- Inference: The experiment demonstrates that social proof can override individual judgment and logical assessment of a situation, causing people to conform to group behavior even when there is nothing to see.
