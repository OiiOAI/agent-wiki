# Prazosin

## Summary

Prazosin is an alpha-1-adrenergic antagonist that has become the most frequently recommended pharmacological treatment for trauma-related nightmares in PTSD sufferers. It works by blocking brain receptors that bind noradrenaline, thereby reducing noradrenaline activity during sleep and restoring the neurochemical environment needed for normal dream processing.

## Key facts

- Murray Raskind discovered prazosin's effects on PTSD nightmares in 2000 when two combat veterans reported dramatic reductions after being treated with the drug for an unrelated medical condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p212]
- Prazosin is an alpha-1-adrenergic antagonist—a drug that blocks receptors that bind noradrenaline and mediate its action. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p212]
- By decreasing noradrenaline activity, prazosin restores the neurochemical environment required by NEXTUP to accomplish its work of memory processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p213]
- Reduction in chronic trauma-related nightmares with prazosin is associated with clinically significant improvements in both sleep quality and daytime functioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p213]
