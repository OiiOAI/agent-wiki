# Something in the Air Study

## Summary

The 'Something in the Air' study tested whether rooms previously occupied by meditators would affect subsequent occupants' cognitive performance. Participants who entered rooms where meditators had been practicing insight meditation showed faster reaction times than those entering rooms where people had watched stressful videos or rooms that were unoccupied, suggesting mindfulness leaves some kind of residue.

## Key facts

- Participants who entered a room where meditators had practiced insight meditation reported the room felt more enlivening and appealing than those entering unoccupied rooms. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p171]
- Reaction times in a tapping exercise were fastest for those in the meditation room, with no meaningful difference between the stressful video room and unoccupied room. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p171]
- Meditation was insight-style where one becomes aware of thoughts and sensations without focusing on them. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p171]

## Inferences

- Inference: Every internal action may be accompanied by an external signifier—be it odor, perspiration, or energy emitted—that can affect subsequent occupants of a space.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The mechanism for room effects is unknown and requires further investigation before conclusions can be drawn.
