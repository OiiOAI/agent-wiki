# Sudbury Valley School

## Summary

Sudbury Valley School is a democratic school in Massachusetts founded in 1968 with no teachers, no classes, no curriculum, no homework, and no tests. Children learn entirely through self-directed play and pursuit of their own interests. Professor Peter Gray's research tracked alumni, finding over 50 percent went on to higher education and were remarkably successful in finding interesting employment. The school is cited as evidence that children can learn naturally through play without traditional schooling.

## Key facts

- Sudbury Valley School opened in 1968 with no teachers, no classes, no curriculum, no homework, and no tests—children learn what they want and choose how to spend their time. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p276]
- Children ages 4-11 spend most of their time playing elaborate games they create, building forts, and engaging in months-long mythological games like clans fighting goblins. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p277]
- All school rules are created and voted on by daily meeting where everyone from four-year-olds to adult staff has one vote. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p277]
- Students breaking rules are tried by a jury representing the whole age range of children, who decide punishment. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p277]
- Professor Peter Gray found that over 50 percent of alumni went on to higher education and almost all were remarkably successful in finding employment that interested them. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p278]
- Gray found that 4 out of 11 students who had been judged to have serious learning difficulties before arriving at Sudbury went on to receive college degrees. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p279]
- Gray argues the modern school was designed in the 1870s to train children to sit still and do what they're told, preparing them to work in factories. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p279]
- The author notes Sudbury Valley charges fees between $7,500 and $10,000 a year, so parents already have financial advantages that could contribute to children's success. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p279]

## Inferences

- Inference: The success of Sudbury alumni suggests that self-directed learning through play can produce comparable or better outcomes than traditional schooling for many children.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The school's tuition creates selection bias—parents who can afford it are likely already advantaged, so outcomes cannot be attributed solely to the school model.
