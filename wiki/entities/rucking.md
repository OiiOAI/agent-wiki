# Rucking

## Summary

Rucking is the practice of carrying weight in a backpack while walking, derived from the military term 'rucksack.' It combines strength training and cardiovascular exercise in one activity and serves as a lower-impact alternative to running. The authors recommend carrying household items in a backpack or opting for duffel bags over rolling suitcases when traveling.

## Key facts

- Rucking comes from the military term 'rucksack' for backpack. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1284-L1287]
- The authors sometimes carry a 30-pound sandbag when walking. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1305-L1308]
- The authors prefer duffel bags over rolling suitcases when traveling to incorporate carrying into their routine. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1305-L1308]
- GORUCK is a company that sells backpacks specifically designed for rucking with pockets for weighted plates. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1310-L1314]
- Jason McCarthy, founder of GORUCK and former U.S. Army Special Forces member, describes rucking as 'strength and cardio in one'. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1312-L1315]
- Chimpanzees required 75% more energy to walk on all fours compared to upright humans on a treadmill. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1276-L1280]

## Inferences

- Inference: Rucking may serve as a substitute for running for people who find running too high-impact, particularly as they age.
