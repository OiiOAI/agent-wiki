# Wade Marshall

## Summary

Wade Marshall (1907–1972) was the first scientist to map the detailed sensory representation of touch and vision in the cerebral cortex. Working at the National Institute of Mental Health, he discovered that sensory information is organized topographically in the brain—that neighboring areas on the body surface are represented in neighboring areas of the somatosensory cortex. He supervised Eric Kandel during Kandel's early research career from 1957 to 1960, providing crucial support and freedom to explore.

## Key facts

- Marshall discovered that the internal representation of touch is organized spatially, with neighboring areas on the body surface preserved in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p122]
- Marshall showed that the retina's light receptors are represented in an orderly way in the primary visual cortex, and that the temporal lobe has a sensory map for sound frequencies. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p125]
- Marshall provided Eric Kandel with laboratory space and allowed him the freedom to study how learning and memory are achieved in brain cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p127]
