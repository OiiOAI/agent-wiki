# Hydra

## Summary

Hydra is a simple freshwater polyp about one centimeter long with a ring of tentacles surrounding its mouth and a footpad at the other end. Despite having neurons organized in a diffuse nerve net without a brain, Hydra exhibits a repertoire of coordinated behaviors including feeding, locomotion, and tentacle movements. It represents an early stage in the evolution of nervous system control of behavior.

## Key facts

- Hydra is a simple freshwater polyp shaped like a fat hollow cigar, about one centimeter long, with tentacles at one end and a footpad at the other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p102]
- Hydras do not have brains; their neurons are organized in a diffuse nerve net in contact with the two cell layers that make up the body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p102]
- Hydras are capable of only a small number of basic movements: longitudinal contraction, longitudinal extension, radial contraction, bending, releasing footpad, extending tentacles, retracting tentacles, and opening and closing mouth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p103]
- The nerve net comprises three or four intercalated subnetworks that control the animal's small repertoire of actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p103]
- Hydras engage in spontaneous behaviors, and their nervous system shows endogenous patterns and oscillations of activity even in the absence of external stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p105]
- In Greek mythology, Hydra is a serpent-like creature with numerous heads, each of which grows back two when cut off—making harm what it likes and representing antifragility. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p55]
- Heracles defeated Hydra by cauterizing the wounds after severing heads, thereby disrupting the antifragile recovery and regrowth process. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p84-85]
- The Hydra metaphor illustrates that political movements and rebellions can be highly antifragile, and repressing them with brute force is a sucker game—making them stronger by attempting to destroy them. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p72]
