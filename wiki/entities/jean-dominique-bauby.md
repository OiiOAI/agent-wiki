# Jean-Dominique Bauby

## Summary

Jean-Dominique Bauby (1960-1996) was the editor of the French fashion magazine Elle who suffered a brain stem stroke in 1995, leaving him with locked-in syndrome. He was only able to communicate by blinking his left eyelid, and dictated his memoir 'The Diving-Bell and the Butterfly' letter by letter. His vivid account of intact consciousness trapped in a paralyzed body provides compelling evidence for the autonomy of consciousness from motor output. He founded the French Association of Locked-In Syndrome and survived for 15 months after his stroke.

## Key facts

- Bauby suffered a stroke to his brain stem in December 1995, disconnecting his cortex from motor output pathways while leaving consciousness intact. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p250]
- He dictated his memoir 'The Diving-Bell and the Butterfly' by blinking his left eyelid while an assistant recited letters in sequence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p250]
- His book describes a rich mental life including vision, touch, smell, and imagination, imprisoned in a motionless body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p250]
- Bauby founded the French Association of Locked-In Syndrome, which is now run by patients using computer interfaces. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p251]
- He survived for 15 months after his stroke, dying of pneumonia three days after his book was published in 1997. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p250]

## Inferences
