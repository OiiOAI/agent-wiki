# Jerome Kerviel

## Summary

Jerome Kerviel was a rogue back office employee at Societe Generale who engaged in massive unauthorized trading in 2008, hiding exposures from the main computer system. When discovered over a weekend, the bank was forced into a fire sale of 70 billion dollars worth of stocks, causing 6 billion dollars in losses and contributing to a worldwide market drop of nearly 10 percent.

## Key facts

- On January 21, 2008, Societe Generale rushed to sell approximately 70 billion dollars worth of stocks in a fire sale, causing 6 billion dollars in losses. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p353]
- Kerviel was a rogue back office employee playing with humongous sums and hiding exposures from the main computer system. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p353]
- The problem was primarily size and fragility from size — if ten smaller banks with proportional Micro-Kerviels had existed, total losses would have been close to nothing. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p354]
- Taleb had presented Black Swan risks to Societe Generale executives in Prague weeks before the incident, warning about pseudo risk techniques, and was heckled by Kerviel's boss. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p354]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Kerviel case illustrates that postmortem analyses attributing problems to 'bad controls' or 'greed' missed the primary issue — size. Large institutions face nonlinear squeeze costs that smaller institutions avoid.
