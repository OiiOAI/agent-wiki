# Inferior Temporal Cortex

## Summary

The inferior temporal cortex is the primary center for object recognition in the primate brain. It receives visual information from earlier stages of the ventral visual stream and contains neurons that encode complex visual stimuli. Damage to this region causes visual agnosia. The cortex is organized into functionally specialized columns and contains dedicated face-processing areas.

## Key facts

- The inferior temporal cortex is the primary center for object recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p612]
- Damage to the inferior temporal cortex can produce specific failures of object recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p612]
- The inferior temporal cortex comprises at least two main functional subdivisions: posterior temporo-occipital cortex and anterior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p612]
- Neurons in the inferior temporal cortex encode complex stimulus features and possess large, centrally located receptive fields. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p615]
- The inferior temporal cortex contains face-selective regions organized into face patches, a few millimeters in diameter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p616]
- The inferior temporal cortex stores information about the shapes and identities of objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p615]
- A portion of the inferior temporal cortex represents faces; damage to this region results in prosopagnosia, the inability to recognize faces. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p615]
- Clinical evidence identifies the inferior temporal cortex as essential for object recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p621]
- The inferior temporal cortex comprises the lower bank of the superior temporal sulcus and the ventrolateral convexity of the temporal lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p674]
- The pathway to inferior temporal cortex extends ventrally and anteriorly from V1 through V2, V4, and the temporo-occipital junction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p674]
- Neurons in the inferior temporal cortex have large, centrally located receptive fields and encode complex stimulus features rather than simple orientation or color. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p675]
- Damage to the posterior inferior temporal cortex causes apperceptive agnosia, whereas damage to the anterior inferior temporal cortex causes associative agnosia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p676]
- Face-selective neurons constitute a highly specialized class that may be located in exclusive clusters in the inferior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p677]
- The inferior temporal cortex projects to perirhinal and parahippocampal cortices, which are involved in long-term memory storage and retrieval. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p677]
- Many inferior temporal neurons exhibit invariance in their selective responses to changes in stimulus size, position, and contrast polarity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p679]

## Inferences

- Inference: The inferior temporal cortex is functionally subdivided into posterior and anterior regions, with the anterior subdivision representing a higher processing stage than the posterior subdivision.
- Inference: Face-selective neurons in the inferior temporal cortex likely contribute directly to face recognition, given that prosopagnosia specifically impairs face identification while preserving other visual functions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact mechanism of viewpoint invariance is uncertain; individual neurons are tuned for specific viewing angles rather than showing complete viewpoint invariance, suggesting population coding or processing in higher cortical areas.
