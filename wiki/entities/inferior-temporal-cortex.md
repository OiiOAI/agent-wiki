# Inferior Temporal Cortex

## Summary

The inferior temporal cortex is the primary center for object recognition in the primate brain. It receives visual information from earlier stages of the ventral visual stream and contains neurons that encode complex visual stimuli. Damage to this region causes visual agnosia. The cortex is organized into functionally specialized columns and contains dedicated face-processing areas.

## Key facts

- The inferior temporal cortex is the primary center for object recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p612]
- Damage to the inferior temporal cortex can produce specific failures of object recognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p612]
- The inferior temporal cortex comprises at least two main functional subdivisions: posterior temporo-occipital cortex and anterior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p612]
- Neurons in the inferior temporal cortex encode complex stimulus features and possess large, centrally located receptive fields. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p615]
- The inferior temporal cortex contains face-selective regions organized into face patches, a few millimeters in diameter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p616]
