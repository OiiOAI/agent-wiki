# Capgras Syndrome

## Summary

Capgras syndrome is a delusion in which patients believe that someone close to them has been replaced by an impostor who is similar but not identical in appearance. This condition may represent the mirror phenomenon of prosopagnosia, where face recognition is intact but the emotional response to familiar faces is absent.

## Key facts

- In Capgras syndrome, patients firmly believe that someone close to them has been replaced by an impostor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1525]
- Capgras syndrome may result from intact face recognition circuitry combined with damaged emotional response circuitry, so patients recognize the face but lack the emotional response that normally confirms identity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1525]
- Patients with Capgras syndrome do not have normal autonomic responses to familiar faces. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1525]
