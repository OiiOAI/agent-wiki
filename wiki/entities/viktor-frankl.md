# Viktor E. Frankl

## Summary

Viktor Emil Frankl (1905–1997) was an Austrian psychiatrist and neurologist who survived the Holocaust, including imprisonment in Auschwitz and affiliated concentration camps. He was the developer of logotherapy, a meaning-centered form of psychotherapy, and author of Man's Search for Meaning, which documents his concentration camp experiences and philosophical insights.

## Key facts

- Frankl was assigned prisoner number 119,104 during his imprisonment. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p15]
- Frankl was not employed as a psychiatrist in camp except for the last few weeks; most of his time was spent digging and laying tracks for railway lines. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p15]
- Frankl received a Christmas 1944 'premium coupon' gift worth twelve cigarettes, which could be exchanged for twelve soups. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p15]
- Frankl received an immigration visa to the United States but chose to stay with his parents in Vienna after seeing a piece of marble from a burned synagogue with a Hebrew letter representing one of the Ten Commandments. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p11]
- Frankl chose to stay because his father explained the letter stood for 'Honor thy father and thy mother that thy days may be long upon the land'. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p11]
- Frankl was survived by his wife, who was in another part of Auschwitz during his time there. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p40]
- Frankl reconstructed his lost manuscript mentally during nights in the camp, scribbling key words in shorthand on tiny scraps of paper. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p35]
- Frankl volunteered for medical duties in the typhus camp, calculating that dying as a doctor would be more purposeful than dying as an unproductive laborer. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p44]
- Frankl made his will to his friend Otto, stating he had loved his wife more than anyone and that their short time married outweighed everything they had gone through. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p48]
- Frankl nearly escaped with a colleague but chose to return to stay with his dying countryman and patients. [raw/books/psychology/Man's Search for Meaning_ Young Adult Edit - Viktor E. Frankl.pdf#p51]
- Viktor Frankl was a Jewish psychiatrist who survived Auschwitz and other Nazi death camps. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p308]
- Frankl observed that those who survived longest in the death camps were often not the physically fittest but those most connected to a sense of meaning and purpose. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p308]
- Frankl's core value was being helpful; throughout his time in the camps he helped other prisoners cope with suffering. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p309]
- Frankl wrote: 'Everything can be taken from a man but one thing: the last of the human freedoms—to choose one's attitude in any given set of circumstances, to choose one's own way.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p309]
- Frankl documented his concentration camp experiences in his book 'Man's Search for Meaning.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Happiness Trap_ How to Stop Struggling - Russ Harris.pdf#p308]
