# David Weisburd

## Summary

David Weisburd is a criminologist who pioneered the 'Law of Crime Concentration'—the finding that crime is highly concentrated in a small percentage of geographic locations, supporting the coupling theory.

## Key facts

- While walking the 72nd Precinct in Brooklyn, Weisburd discovered that crime was concentrated on just one or two streets despite the neighborhood being considered uniformly 'bad.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p160-161]
- In Minneapolis, Weisburd and Lawrence Sherman found that 3.3% of street segments accounted for over 50% of police calls. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p161]
- This pattern repeated in cities across the U.S. and internationally—including Tel Aviv—leading Weisburd to formulate the 'Law of Crime Concentration.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p161-162]
- Weisburd argues that police should focus resources on the specific hot spots where crime occurs rather than spreading patrols evenly throughout a jurisdiction. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p176-177]
- Weisburd formulated the Law of Crime Concentration based on his research showing crime is highly concentrated at specific street segments. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p224]
- Weisburd and his colleague Claire White have been running a multimillion-dollar hot spot research project in Baltimore since 2012, studying 450 street segments. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p223]
- Weisburd conducted research in Jersey City showing that crime displacement does not necessarily occur when police focus on hot spots—the benefits of policing diffuse to surrounding areas. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p225]
- Weisburd's work on crime and place is compiled in books including 'The Criminology of Place' (2012) and 'Place Matters: Criminology for the Twenty-First Century' (2016). [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p223]

## Inferences

- Inference: Weisburd's research provides a scientific foundation for coupling theory in criminology, suggesting that effective policing requires geographic precision rather than broad-based patrols.
