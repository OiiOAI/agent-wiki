# Bjorn Merker

## Summary

Bjorn Merker is a neuroscientist who joined a self-help group for caregivers of children with hydranencephaly in 2003 and observed that these children — born without a cerebral cortex — were not in a vegetative state but showed emotional and orienting reactions to their environment, responding with pleasure and aversion appropriate to the situation. His findings challenged the assumption that consciousness requires a cortex.

## Key facts

- In 2003, Merker joined a self-help group for caregivers of hydranencephalic children and observed that these children showed emotional and orienting reactions, expressed pleasure by smiling and laughter, and aversion by fussing and crying. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p53]
- Merker reported that hydranencephalic children respond most vigorously to the voices and actions of familiar people, show preferences for certain situations, and exhibit initiative within the limitations of their motor disabilities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p54]
- Merker expressed concern that the assumption that hydranencephalic children are 'vegetative' might have become a self-fulfilling prophecy, caused by treating them as completely insensate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p53]
- Merker noted that these children exhibit absence seizures, which parents describe in terms like 'she is off talking with the angels', and parents can recognise when the child 'is back'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p53]
- Merker was one of three neuroscientists (alongside Panksepp and Damasio) who shared Solms's view that neuroscience lacked focus on the embodied nature of lived experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p14]

## Inferences

- Inference: Merker's observations of emotionally responsive hydranencephalic children provide evidence that the cortex is not required for conscious feeling, supporting Solms's argument that consciousness arises from subcortical structures.
