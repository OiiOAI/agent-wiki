# Sleep Perception Study

## Summary

A Harvard study demonstrated that perceptions of sleep duration affect alertness and brain activity more than actual sleep duration. Participants' bedside clocks were programmed to alter their perception of how long they had slept, and reaction times and EEG measurements showed that perceived sleep, not actual sleep, determined alertness levels.

## Key facts

- When participants thought they slept for 8 hours but had only slept 5, their reaction times were quicker than when they knew they had only 5 hours of sleep. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p107]
- When people slept 8 hours but thought they only slept 5, performance was worse than when they actually slept 8 hours and thought they had. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p108]
- Alpha brain waves correlated more with perceived sleep duration than actual sleep duration—thinking sleep was restricted made the brain act as if it was. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p108]
