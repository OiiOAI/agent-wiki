# Daniel Kahneman

## Summary

Daniel Kahneman is a Nobel Prize-winning psychologist and economist cited in Essentialism for his research on behavioral economics, particularly the sunk cost effect, loss aversion, and decision-making biases.

## Key facts

- Kahneman co-authored 'Anomalies: The Endowment Effect, Loss Aversion, and Status Quo Bias' published in Journal of Economic Perspectives in 1991. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p237]
- He co-authored 'Intuitive Prediction: Biases and Corrective Procedures' with Amos Tversky in 1979. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p239]
- His research is cited in the Uncommit chapter to explain sunk cost and status quo biases. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p237]
- Kahneman and Tversky found that people place unwarranted emphasis on salient information when forecasting. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p184]
- Kahneman coined the term "planning fallacy" for our tendency to underestimate time and overestimate benefits. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p199]
- Kahneman found zero correlation between investment advisors' year-to-year performance rankings—rewarding luck rather than skill. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
- Kahneman noted: "It's hard to imagine that people enthusiastically buy books written by business leaders who are, on average, only slightly better than the norm.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
