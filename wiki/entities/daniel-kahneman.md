# Daniel Kahneman

## Summary

Daniel Kahneman is a Nobel Prize-winning psychologist and economist cited in Essentialism for his research on behavioral economics, particularly the sunk cost effect, loss aversion, and decision-making biases.

## Key facts

- Kahneman co-authored 'Anomalies: The Endowment Effect, Loss Aversion, and Status Quo Bias' published in Journal of Economic Perspectives in 1991. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p237]
- He co-authored 'Intuitive Prediction: Biases and Corrective Procedures' with Amos Tversky in 1979. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p239]
- His research is cited in the Uncommit chapter to explain sunk cost and status quo biases. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p237]
- Kahneman and Tversky found that people place unwarranted emphasis on salient information when forecasting. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p184]
- Kahneman coined the term "planning fallacy" for our tendency to underestimate time and overestimate benefits. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p199]
- Kahneman found zero correlation between investment advisors' year-to-year performance rankings—rewarding luck rather than skill. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
- Kahneman noted: "It's hard to imagine that people enthusiastically buy books written by business leaders who are, on average, only slightly better than the norm.". [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p206]
- Kahneman received the 2002 Nobel Prize in Economics, which he stated would have been shared with Tversky had Tversky not died in 1996. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p18]
- Kahneman collaborated with Amos Tversky for 14 years beginning in 1969 at Hebrew University. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p11]
- Kahneman described himself as more intuitive and rooted in perceptual psychology, while Tversky was more logical and theoretically oriented. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p13]
- Kahneman spent a year at University of Michigan early in his career, where he researched cognitive effort and pupil dilation. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p45]
- Kahneman and Tversky discovered the core flaw in Bernoulli's expected utility model while discussing Tversky's book chapter on measuring the utility of money through small-stakes gambles, when Kahneman—then not yet fully educated in utility theory—questioned whether studying responses to penny-scale gambles could reveal anything meaningful about wealth valuation. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p360]
- Kahneman and Tversky concluded that prospect theory would define outcomes as gains and losses rather than states of wealth, building on Harry Markowitz's earlier (and ignored) suggestion linking utility to changes in wealth rather than absolute wealth levels. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p361]
- Kahneman received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2002, shared with Vernon Smith, for having integrated insights from psychological research into economic science, especially concerning human judgment and decision-making under uncertainty. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p381]

## Inferences
