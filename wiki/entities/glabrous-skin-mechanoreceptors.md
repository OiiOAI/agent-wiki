# Mechanoreceptors in Glabrous Skin

## Summary

The hand has four types of mechanoreceptors responsible for touch: Meissner corpuscles (RA1), Merkel cells (SA1), Pacinian corpuscles (RA2), and Ruffini endings (SA2). These receptors differ in morphology, location in the skin, receptive field size, and physiological responses. Type 1 fibers (RA1, SA1) terminate in clusters of small receptor organs in superficial skin layers, while type 2 fibers (RA2, SA2) innervate single large receptors in deeper tissue. Slowly adapting fibers respond to sustained indentation while rapidly adapting fibers respond primarily to motion and vibration.

- The hand has four types of mechanoreceptors: Meissner corpuscles (RA1), Merkel cells (SA1), Pacinian corpuscles (RA2), and Ruffini endings (SA2). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p467]
- Slowly adapting (SA) fibers respond to steady skin indentation with a sustained discharge, whereas rapidly adapting (RA) fibers stop firing when indentation becomes stationary.
