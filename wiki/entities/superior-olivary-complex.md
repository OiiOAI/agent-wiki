# Superior Olivary Complex

## Summary

The superior olivary complex in the brainstem contains nuclei that perform the first comparisons of binaural acoustic information. The medial superior olive detects interaural time differences for low-frequency sound localization, while the lateral superior olive detects interaural intensity differences for high-frequency localization.

## Key facts

- The medial superior olive generates a map of interaural time differences through delay lines and coincidence detection as proposed by Lloyd Jeffress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p705]
- Large spherical bushy cells from the ventral cochlear nucleus provide phase-locked input to the medial superior olive for encoding sounds below 4 kHz. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p705]
- The lateral superior olive detects interaural intensity differences by balancing ipsilateral excitation from small spherical bushy cells with contralateral inhibition from the medial nucleus of the trapezoid body. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p709]
- Globular bushy cells form calyces of Held on neurons of the medial nucleus of the trapezoid body, enabling fast and reliable synaptic transmission for precise timing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p709]
- Olivocochlear neurons provide feedback to the cochlea: medial olivocochlear neurons hyperpolarize outer hair cells, while lateral olivocochlear neurons terminate on afferent fibers from inner hair cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p710]
