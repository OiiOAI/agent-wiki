# General Motors

## Summary

General Motors is an American automobile manufacturer that exemplifies organizational dysfunction and strategic failure. By 1985, GM had become moribund, lagging behind Toyota and revived Ford and Chrysler. Executive Alan described the situation: honest analysis replaced by spin doctoring, rampant careerism, insufferable bureaucracy, and lack of trust. Employees directed energy inward rather than outward to the marketplace. GM filed for bankruptcy in 2009, the largest ever for an industrial firm, with $82 billion in assets and $173 billion in liabilities. The subsequent ignition-switch fiasco, linked to 124 deaths, revealed that engineers knew about the switch problems but failed to connect them to airbag-deployment issues. CEO Mary Barra subsequently killed the matrix structure, flattened the organization, and refocused on electric vehicles.

## Key facts

- By 1985, GM had become moribund, lagging ever further behind upstart Toyota and a revived Ford and Chrysler. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p203]
- GM executive Alan described honest analysis replaced by spin doctoring, rampant careerism, insufferable bureaucracy, and lack of trust. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p204]
- GM filed for bankruptcy in 2009, the largest ever for an industrial firm, with $82 billion in assets and $173 billion in liabilities. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p204]
- The ignition-switch fiasco was linked to 124 deaths; GM engineers knew about the switch problems but failed to connect them to airbag-deployment problems. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p205]
- CEO Mary Barra killed the matrix structure, flattened the organization, fired 15 employees for cause, and dropped brands including Saturn, Hummer, Pontiac, and Saab. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p206]

## Inferences

- Inference: GM's dysfunction stemmed from organizational structure and culture that prevented talent from addressing known problems, rather than lack of knowledge about what needed to be fixed.
