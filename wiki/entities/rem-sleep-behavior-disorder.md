# REM Sleep Behavior Disorder

## Summary

REM sleep behavior disorder (RBD) is the opposite of sleep paralysis—it occurs when the muscle paralysis normally present during REM sleep fails to develop, allowing people to physically act out their dreams. First identified in 1986 by Carlos Schenck and Mark Mahowald, RBD affects about one adult in 200, particularly men over age 50. Over 80 percent of RBD patients go on to develop Parkinson's disease or other forms of dementia.

## Key facts

- RBD was first identified in 1986 by Carlos Schenck and Mark Mahowald in Minneapolis. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p221]
- RBD affects approximately one adult in 200, particularly men over age 50. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p222]
- More than 80 percent of patients with RBD go on to develop Parkinson's disease or other forms of dementia, with an average delay of 14 years between RBD diagnosis and neurodegenerative diagnosis. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p222]
- RBD is a harbinger of life-altering neurodegenerative diseases, though symptoms can be effectively managed with medication. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p222]

## Inferences

- Inference: RBD's link to Parkinson's disease suggests both conditions result from deteriorations within the same brain networks that control movement, highlighting the relationship between dream-related brain systems and motor control.
