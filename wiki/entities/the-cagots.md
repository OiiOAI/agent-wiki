# The Cagots

## Summary

The Cagots were a minority in France whose persecution began in the 11th century and continued into recent times. They were required to live outside villages, dress distinctively, sit separately in church, and do menial jobs—yet they didn't differ in appearance, religion, accent, or names, and no one knows why they were pariahs. During the French Revolution, Cagots burned their own birth certificates to destroy proof of their status.

- The Cagots were required to live outside villages, dress distinctively, sit separately in church, and do menial jobs, yet had no known distinguishing physical, religious, or linguistic features.
