# Nicola Clayton

## Summary

Nicola Clayton is a professor at the University of Cambridge who conducted pioneering research on episodic-like memory in Florida scrub jays, demonstrating that they can answer 'what, where, when' questions about caching events and adjust strategies based on experience.

## Key facts

- Clayton found that Florida scrub jays (Aphelocoma coerulescens) will cache different types of food in different places at different times and selectively retrieve food that degrades before food that stores well. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p323]
- Clayton and colleagues showed that scrub jays adjust caching strategies to minimize potential stealing by recaching food in private when observed by other birds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p323]
- She suggests that scrub jays may be showing evidence of knowing what another jay knows—theory of mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p324]
- Clayton calls scrub jay memory 'episode-like' because it is unclear whether it reflects semantic knowledge or true episodic memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p323]
