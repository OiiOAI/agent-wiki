# Pericles

## Summary

Pericles was an Athenian statesman and orator who led Athens during its golden age from approximately 461–429 BC. He exemplifies rationality in leadership, developing strategies to control emotions and make decisions based on what served the greater good of Athens rather than personal glory or factional interests.

## Key facts

- Pericles led Athens as a statesman for over three decades, beginning around 463 BC. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p26]
- Pericles came from an aristocratic family but allied himself with the lower and middle classes of Athens, including farmers, sailors, and craftsmen. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p27]
- Pericles promoted a restrained speaking style in the Assembly, working to convince audiences through logical arguments rather than emotional rhetoric. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p28]
- During the Spartan ultimatum of 432 BC, Pericles advocated for a defensive war strategy rather than direct confrontation on land. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p28]
- Pericles commissioned the Parthenon and other monumental buildings as part of transforming Athens into a golden age center of arts and sciences. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p27]
- Pericles conceptualized rationality as worshipping 'nous' (mind/intelligence), embodied in the goddess Athena. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p32]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene uses Pericles as the ideal exemplar of rationality because his policies led to unprecedented prosperity for Athens, demonstrating that rational leadership produces tangible positive results.
