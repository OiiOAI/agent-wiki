# Ryanair

## Summary

Ryanair was founded in 1984 by Tony Ryan to compete with Aer Lingus and British Airways on the London-Dublin route. After initial failure and bankruptcy in 1992, the company restructured under Michael O'Leary using Southwest Airlines as a model. The company exemplifies strategy as a journey through sequential challenges from initial failure to European dominance to pandemic disruption.

## Key facts

- Ryanair was founded in 1984 by Tony Ryan who had built one of Europe's largest airplane leasing companies. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p61]
- The original strategy failed—competing against state-subsidized carriers on the London-Dublin route led to bankruptcy in 1992. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p62]
- The crux of the initial failure was the staying power of established players on major routes. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p62]
- Ryanair switched to flying from Dublin to Luton rather than London's Gatwick, serving nonmajor airports. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p62]
- In 2019, Ryanair had revenues of €7.7 billion and profit after tax of €885 million. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p64]
