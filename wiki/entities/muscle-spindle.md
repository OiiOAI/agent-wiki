# Muscle Spindle

## Summary

The muscle spindle is the principal receptor for proprioception, consisting of a bundle of thin intrafusal muscle fibers aligned parallel to larger skeletal muscle fibers and enclosed within a capsule. The intrafusal fibers are entwined by sensory axons that detect muscle stretch through mechanoreceptive ion channels linked to the cytoskeleton. The spindle is also innervated by motor axons that regulate contractile force and receptor sensitivity. The depolarizing receptor potential recorded in group Ia fibers innervating the muscle spindle is proportional to both the velocity and amplitude of muscle stretch.

- The intrafusal muscle fibers contain dynamic nuclear bag fibers, static nuclear bag fibers, and nuclear chain fibers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p765]
- Primary (Ia) sensory axons spiral around all intrafusal fiber types and are sensitive to both the rate and magnitude of stretch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p765]
- Secondary (II) sensory axons innervate chain and static bag fibers and signal primarily steady-state muscle length. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p765]
- Gamma motor neurons innervate the contractile polar regions of intrafusal fibers and adjust spindle sensitivity by maintaining tension on the central sensory region during muscle contraction.
