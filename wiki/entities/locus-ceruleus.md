# Locus Ceruleus

## Summary

The locus ceruleus is a noradrenergic nucleus (A6 cell group) in the pons that plays a key role in attention, arousal, and stress responses. Its neurons project widely throughout the brain and spinal cord.

## Key facts

- The locus ceruleus (A6) provides the major noradrenergic input to the dorsal horn of the spinal cord for pain modulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1046]
- Locus ceruleus neurons exhibit phasic firing during focused attention and tonic firing during search for new behavioral goals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1052]
- In the phasic mode, bursts of firing occur just before the monkey responds to stimuli to which it has been attentive, facilitating selective attention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1052]
- Performance is optimal when baseline locus ceruleus activity is moderate; performance is poor at low levels (not alert) and at high levels (incompatible with focused attention). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1052]
