# James Lind

## Summary

James Lind was an 18th-century Scottish sea captain who conducted one of the earliest controlled experiments on scurvy in 1747. His study established that citrus fruit prevented scurvy, leading to the British Navy's adoption of lime juice rations. However, because the mechanism (vitamin C) was unknown, subsequent substitutions and processing methods destroyed the active ingredient, and the knowledge was forgotten, contributing to scurvy deaths in polar expeditions decades later.

## Key facts

- James Lind conducted the first controlled experiment on scurvy in 1747. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2585-L2595]
- Lind's study showed citrus fruit prevented scurvy, though the mechanism (vitamin C) was unknown. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2590-L2598]
- By 1800, scurvy was controlled in the British Navy through citrus rations. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2595-L2600]
- Scurvy returned in Antarctic expeditions because lime juice was substituted and processed in ways that destroyed vitamin C. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2600-L2610]
