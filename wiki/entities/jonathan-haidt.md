# Jonathan Haidt

## Summary

Jonathan Haidt is a psychologist at the University of Virginia who studies moral psychology and the social intuitionist model. He proposed that moral judgments arise from gut intuitions followed by post-hoc rationalization, and identified five universal moral modules.

## Key facts

- Haidt's research on incest scenarios demonstrated that moral intuitions operate automatically and precede rational reasoning, with people saying 'it feels wrong' even when no rational argument can be constructed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p124]
- Haidt and Craig Joseph identified five universal moral modules: reciprocity, suffering/harm, hierarchy, in-group/out-group coalitions, and purity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p138]
- Haidt proposed the social intuitionist model of moral reasoning, where moral judgments result from quick intuitions followed by post-hoc reasoning that seeks rational explanations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p151]
- Haidt stated that moral emotions include not only altruism and niceness but also emotions leading to ostracism, shaming, and vengeance, all part of human moral nature. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p139]
