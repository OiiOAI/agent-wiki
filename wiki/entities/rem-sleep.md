# REM Sleep

## Summary

REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep is a unique brain and body state characterized by rapid jerky eye movements, muscle atonia (paralysis), and wake-like brain wave activity. Discovered in 1951 by Eugene Aserinsky, REM sleep is strongly associated with vivid dreaming and recurs approximately every 90 minutes throughout the night, with REM periods getting longer as the night progresses.

## Key facts

- During REM sleep, heart rate, blood pressure, and breathing vary widely, and men have prolonged erections while women experience clitoral swelling. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
- The brain's electrical activity during REM sleep becomes indistinguishable from that of an awake person. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
- REM sleep is accompanied by near-complete loss of muscle tone (atonia), effectively paralyzing the dreamer. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
- REM periods increase in length as the night progresses, while deep N3 sleep decreases and may disappear by mid-night. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p60]
- REM sleep was also known as paradoxical sleep because the EEG appeared to indicate wakefulness while the person was actually asleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p63]
