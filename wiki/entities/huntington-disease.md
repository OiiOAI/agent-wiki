# Huntington Disease

## Summary

Huntington disease is an autosomal dominant neurodegenerative disorder caused by expansion of CAG trinucleotide repeats encoding glutamine in the huntingtin protein. The disease involves degeneration of the striatum, leading to chorea, cognitive impairment, and affective disturbance. Normal alleles have 6-34 repeats while disease-causing alleles have 36 or more repeats, with longer repeats causing earlier onset and more severe disease through the phenomenon of anticipation.

## Key facts

- Huntington disease affects 5 to 10 people per 100,000 and is an autosomal dominant disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- The disease is caused by expansion of a translated CAG repeat encoding glutamine in the huntingtin protein, with normal alleles having 6-34 repeats and disease-causing alleles having 36 or more. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- Disease severity, age of onset, and speed of progression correlate with repeat length; individuals with 36-39 repeats have later onset, while those with more than 40 repeats have earlier onset. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- The pathological hallmark is degeneration of the striatum, with the caudate nucleus more affected than the putamen. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]
- Antisense oligonucleotides targeting huntingtin mRNA are currently in clinical trials for Huntington disease treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1557]
- Studies in mouse models have shown that neuronal dysfunction in Huntington disease is reversible when expression of the mutant gene is turned off. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1557]
- Huntington's disease is caused by a defect in the HTT gene on chromosome 4, with a DNA segment repeated up to 120 times in affected individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- While Parkinson's hallmark is too little movement, Huntington's hallmark is too much involuntary, jerky movement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- Huntington's patients showed impaired sequence learning even though their motor function was sufficient to perform the test. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p164]

## Inferences

- Inference: The caudate nucleus, affected early in Huntington's, is a critical substrate for sequence-specific motor learning.
