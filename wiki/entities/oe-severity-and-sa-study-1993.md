# Rodgers and Nye 1993 Study

## Summary

This study conducted by Rodgers and Nye (1993) examined Operational Errors (OEs) occurring in ARTCCs between 1988-1991 (n=1,053 errors). The study had five purposes: examining the relationship between error severity and controller workload, determining which causal factor categories were associated with OE severity, analyzing the relationship between OE severity and aircraft profile/altitude, determining how variables were distributed among individual ARTCCs, and identifying factors during major OEs. Key findings included that controller awareness of error development was associated with less severe errors, and that causal factors related to SA (particularly radar display issues at 59% and communication errors at 36%) were significant contributors.

## Key facts

- The Rodgers and Nye (1993) study analyzed 1,053 OE reports from 1988, 1989, 1990, and approximately two-thirds of 1991. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p94]
- Controller awareness of error development was associated with less severe operational errors. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p92]
- Radar display issues were involved in 59% of minor or moderate errors at en route facilities. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p96]
- Communication errors were involved in 36% of errors, with readback issues accounting for 20%. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p96]
- The five main causal factor categories are: data posting (13%), radar display (59%), coordination (15%), communication (36%), and position relief briefing deficiencies (3%). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p96]
- The number of aircraft being worked ranged from 8.1 for minor errors at FL 290 or below to 10.6 for moderate errors above FL 290. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: The high prevalence of radar display issues (59%) suggests that SA in ATC is heavily dependent on accurate visual information presentation
- Inference: The association between controller awareness and error severity suggests that maintaining SA may prevent minor errors from escalating to major ones
