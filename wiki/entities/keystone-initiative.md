# Keystone Initiative

## Summary

The Keystone Initiative was a statewide implementation of Pronovost's checklist approach across Michigan ICUs, coordinated by the Michigan Health and Hospital Association starting in 2003. It achieved dramatic reductions in central line infections and served as a model for large-scale healthcare quality improvement.

## Key facts

- The Michigan Health and Hospital Association approached Pronovost in 2003 to test his central line checklist throughout the state's ICUs. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p38]
- In early 2004, Michigan ICU infection rates were higher than the national average, with Sinai-Grace experiencing more central line infections than 75 percent of American hospitals. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p38]
- Each participating hospital assigned a project manager and participated in twice-monthly conference calls with Pronovost. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p39]
- Executives were required to visit units at least once monthly to hear staff complaints and help solve problems, discovering that chlorhexidine soap was unavailable in most ICUs. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p39]
- Within three months, Michigan ICU central line infection rates decreased by 66 percent, with most ICUs cutting quarterly infection rates to zero. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p40]
- In the first 18 months, Michigan hospitals saved an estimated $175 million and more than 1,500 lives. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p40]
- Results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine in December 2006. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p40]

## Inferences

- Inference: The initiative's success relied on multiple factors beyond the checklist itself: executive engagement, communication infrastructure, and removing supply barriers.
