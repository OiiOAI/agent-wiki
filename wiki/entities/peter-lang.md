# Peter Lang

## Summary

Peter Lang was a psychologist who proposed the three-response system theory of fear and anxiety, involving behavioral avoidance, physiological responses, and verbal behavior. He developed the concept of fear structures (schemas) representing fear in the brain as programs for escaping or avoiding danger. His work influenced emotional processing theory and prolonged exposure therapy.

## Key facts

- Lang's three-response system theory identified behavioral avoidance, physiological responses, and verbal behavior as components of fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p369]
- Lang proposed that fear structures contain propositions about threats, physiological changes, actions, and meanings that are stored in memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p378]
