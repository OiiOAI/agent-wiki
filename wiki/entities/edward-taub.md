# Edward Taub

## Summary

Edward Taub is a neuroscientist who developed constraint-induced (CI) movement therapy, a plasticity-based treatment for stroke patients and others with brain damage. Born in Brooklyn in 1931, he graduated high school at age 15 and studied behaviorism at Columbia University under Fred Keller. As a graduate student, he overturned Sherrington's reflexological theory through deafferentation experiments with monkeys, establishing that voluntary movement can occur without sensory feedback. His work was delayed for years due to the infamous Silver Spring monkeys controversy, during which PETA accused him of animal cruelty. He was ultimately exonerated and now works at the University of Alabama, where he continues to develop and apply CI therapy.

## Key facts

- Edward Taub was born in Brooklyn in 1931 and graduated from high school at age 15. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p144]
- At Columbia University, Taub studied behaviorism with Fred Keller, who was B.F. Skinner's intellectual lieutenant. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p144]
- Taub disproved one of his mentor Keller's theories using his 'rat diary' observation method while still a graduate student. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p145]
- Taub overturned Sherrington's reflexological theory of movement by showing that deafferented monkeys could move their limbs when their good arms were restrained. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p147]
- Taub proposed the phenomenon of 'learned nonuse,' explaining why stroke patients don't use affected limbs even when recovery is possible. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p149]
- In 1981, Alex Pacheco, a cofounder of PETA, volunteered in Taub's lab and then raided it, leading to the Silver Spring monkeys controversy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p151]
- Taub was charged with 119 counts of animal cruelty but was exonerated in a unanimous decision by the Maryland Court of Appeals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- Taub was hired by the University of Alabama in 1986 and opened a CI therapy clinic. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- Studies show that 80 percent of stroke patients who have lost arm function can improve substantially with CI therapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p155]
- Taub collaborated on demonstrating that after CI therapy, the brain map for an affected arm doubles in size. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p157]

## Inferences

- Inference: Taub's development of CI therapy was based on his animal research showing that forced use of affected limbs could overcome learned nonuse.
- Inference: The Silver Spring monkeys controversy, while damaging to Taub personally, ultimately provided the animals needed for landmark neuroplasticity experiments.
