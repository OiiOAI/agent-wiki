# David Phillips

## Summary

Dr. David Phillips is a sociologist at the University of California at San Diego who developed the theory of the Werther effect—the phenomenon where publicized suicide stories trigger imitative suicides and accidents. His research demonstrates that certain troubled people, upon learning of another's self-inflicted death, kill themselves in imitation, and some disguise these deaths as accidents.

## Key facts

- Phillips found that within two months after front-page suicide stories, an average of fifty-eight more people than usual killed themselves in regions where the story was highly publicized. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p136]
- Phillips argued that excess deaths following publicized suicides can be explained as copycat suicides, with some individuals purposely causing car or plane wrecks to disguise their suicides. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p136]
- Analysis of heavyweight championship fights showed that when a black fighter lost, the homicide rate rose for young black males but not white males; when a white fighter lost, the reverse occurred. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p139]
