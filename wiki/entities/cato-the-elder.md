# Cato the Elder

## Summary

Cato the Elder (Marcus Porcius Cato) was a Roman statesman Taleb describes as having the bottom-line mind of Fat Tony but with high civic sense, respect for tradition, and commitment to moral rectitude. He was highly allergic to Greek culture, philosophers, and doctors. Plutarch quotes him calling Socrates 'a mighty babbler who tried to make himself tyrant of his country in order to destroy its customs and entice its citizens into holding views contrary to law and order.'

## Key facts

- Cato had the bottom-line mind of Fat Tony but with much higher civic sense, respect for tradition, and commitment to moral rectitude. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p322]
- Cato was allergic to things Greek — exhibited in his allergy to philosophers and doctors. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p322]
- Plutarch quotes Cato: 'Socrates was a mighty babbler who tried to make himself tyrant of his country in order to destroy its customs and entice its citizens into holding views contrary to law and order.'. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p322]
- Cato's commitment to democracy led him to believe in both freedom and the rules of custom, in combination with fear of tyranny. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p322]
