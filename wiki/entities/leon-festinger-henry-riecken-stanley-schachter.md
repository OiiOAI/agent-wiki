# Leon Festinger, Henry Riecken, and Stanley Schachter

## Summary

These University of Minnesota researchers conducted participant observation of a doomsday cult in Chicago, studying how believers responded when their prophecy of a world flood failed to occur. Their 1956 book 'When Prophecy Fails' documented how the group became MORE committed and began actively recruiting after their beliefs were disconfirmed.

## Key facts

- The researchers joined the group incognito and placed additional paid observers among the cult's ranks to document behaviors before and after the predicted flood date. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p114]
- After midnight passed without the predicted saucer arrival or flood, the group shifted from secretive behavior to actively seeking publicity and recruiting new members to generate social proof for their beliefs. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p119]
- The group's sudden shift to active recruitment was explained by the need to establish social proof for their beliefs after physical evidence had disconfirmed them. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p120]
