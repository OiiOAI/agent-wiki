# Rick Perry

## Summary

Rick Perry, former Texas governor, experienced a famous blocking episode during the November 9, 2011 Republican presidential debate when he could not recall the name of the third federal agency he would eliminate. He struggled for 45 seconds before concluding 'the third one I can't... sorry... oops!' He later recalled the Department of Energy. This case exemplifies classic name blocking: age-related increased vulnerability, lack of recent retrieval, partial knowledge, and the role of stress in retrieval failure.

## Key facts

- During the 2011 Republican debate, Perry said 'it's three agencies of government when I get there that are gone,' then named Commerce and Education before failing to recall the third for 45 seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p119]
- Perry was eight months past his sixty-first birthday at the time, old enough that age could plausibly have heightened his risk of blocking. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p120]
- Perry had partial knowledge of the name: when Ron Paul suggested EPA and the moderator asked if it was correct, Perry knew enough to reject the suggestion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p120]
- Research using the Trier Social Stress Test shows that evaluation-related stress results in a significant increase in tip-of-the-tongue states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p120]
