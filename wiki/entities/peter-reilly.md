# Peter Reilly

## Summary

Peter Reilly is a case study in memory distrust syndrome. He discovered his murdered mother's body, immediately notified police, but was eventually convinced through coercive questioning that he must have committed the crime. He signed a written confession and was convicted, only to be exonerated two years later when new evidence proved he could not have committed the murder.

## Key facts

- Peter Reilly came home to discover his murdered mother's body, immediately notified the police, but was identified as a suspect and failed a polygraph test. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p163]
- Though initially denying the murder, Reilly eventually became convinced that he must have committed the crime and signed a written confession. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p163]
- Two years later, Reilly was exonerated by new evidence showing that he could not have murdered his mother. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p163]
