# Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex

## Summary

The ventromedial prefrontal cortex (orbital and medial sectors of the prefrontal lobe) is identified by Damasio as the critical brain region where reasoning and emotion intersect. Damage to this region produces the characteristic pattern seen in Phineas Gage and Elliot: impaired personal and social decision-making despite otherwise intact cognition and emotional flatness.

## Key facts

- The ventromedial prefrontal region was damaged in both Phineas Gage and patient Elliot, producing impaired decision-making in personal and social domains. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p52]
- Damage to ventromedial prefrontal cortices consistently correlates with impairments of both reasoning/decision-making and emotion/feeling, against an otherwise intact neuropsychological profile. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p81]
- The ventromedial prefrontal cortex contains high concentrations of serotonin-2 receptors, and these concentrations correlate with adaptive social behavior in primates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p76]
- This region is interconnected with the amygdala, another structure involved in emotion and decision-making processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p71]

## Inferences

- Inference: The convergence of decision-making and emotional processing in this region supports Damasio's thesis that emotion is integral to rational decision-making rather than separate from it
