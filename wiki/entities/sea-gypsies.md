# Sea Gypsies

## Summary

The Sea Gypsies are nomadic people living in the Burmese archipelago and off the west coast of Thailand who can see clearly underwater without goggles. Their children dive down thirty feet or more to harvest food and have learned to constrict their pupils 22 percent more than normal—a seemingly hardwired reflex that they have trained to change. Researcher Anna Gislen demonstrated that this ability can be taught to Swedish children, proving neuroplasticity of what was thought to be an innate reflex.

## Key facts

- Sea Gypsies learn to swim before they learn to walk and live over half their lives in boats on the open sea. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- Their children dive down, often thirty feet beneath the water's surface, and pluck up food including small morsels of marine life. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- By learning to lower their heart rate, they can stay underwater twice as long as most swimmers without diving equipment. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- They can see clearly at great depths without goggles because they have learned to control the shape of their lenses and constrict their pupils 22 percent. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- Researcher Anna Gislen found they were more than twice as skillful as European children at reading placards underwater. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- This ability is not genetic; Gislen has since taught Swedish children to constrict their pupils to see underwater. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- Pupil adjustment had been thought to be a fixed, innate reflex controlled by the brain and nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p292]
- When the 2004 tsunami struck, the Sea Gypsies survived because they saw the sea receding unusually, noticed dolphins swimming for deeper water, and heard cicadas fall silent—perceiving the whole picture holistically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p308]
