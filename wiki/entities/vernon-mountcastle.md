# Vernon Mountcastle

## Summary

Vernon Mountcastle was a neuroscientist at Johns Hopkins University who proposed in his 1978 essay 'The Mindful Brain' that all cortical columns perform the same fundamental algorithm, differing only in what they connect to. This elegant proposal, comparing to Darwin's insight about life's diversity arising from one evolutionary algorithm, profoundly influenced Jeff Hawkins' research and forms the foundation of the Thousand Brains Theory.

## Key facts

- In 1978, Mountcastle published 'The Mindful Brain,' containing an essay proposing that all cortical columns perform the same basic function. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p33]
- Mountcastle argued that what makes cortical regions different is not their intrinsic function but what they are connected to; connect to eyes and you get vision. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p35]
- Mountcastle proposed that the fundamental unit of the neocortex is the cortical column, and discovering the cortical algorithm would explain all intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p36]
- Mountcastle's proposal is compared to Darwin's discovery that life's diversity stems from one evolutionary algorithm. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p35]
- In 2005, Mountcastle invited Hawkins to work at Johns Hopkins, an offer Hawkins could not accept due to family and business commitments. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p38]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mountcastle's proposal was radical because it implied that vision and language are fundamentally the same process operating on different inputs, which many found hard to accept.
