# Neanderthals

## Summary

Neanderthals were a local variant population of archaic Homo sapiens, genetically isolated for perhaps a hundred thousand years before contact with anatomically modern populations. Evidence suggests they had brain sizes slightly above modern values and may have possessed vocal apparatus more modern than previously suspected, challenging assumptions that their demise was due to cognitive or linguistic inferiority.

## Key facts

- Neanderthals had a brain size slightly above modern values with internal brain structure proportions consistent with symbolic capacity equal to anatomically modern humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p402]
- The discovery of a Neanderthal hyoid bone provides evidence that they may have had a more modern vocal apparatus than previously suspected. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p402]
- Chatelperronian artifacts associated with the last Neanderthals in France include diverse tool types, bone and ivory industry, and artistically crafted adornments. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p402]
- Anatomically modern human fossils in Africa date to between 75,000 and 115,000 years ago, putting them in the same time range as some of the oldest Neanderthal fossils. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p400]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Neanderthal demise more likely resulted from disease and genetic isolation rather than cognitive or cultural inferiority, paralleling the decline of isolated indigenous populations after contact with Old World populations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact nature and sophistication of Neanderthal symbolic communication.
