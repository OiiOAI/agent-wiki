# Caenorhabditis elegans

## Summary

C. elegans is a microscopic nematode worm approximately one millimeter in length with the best-characterized nervous system of any creature. Adult hermaphrodites have exactly 302 neurons while males have 387. The worm exhibits sophisticated behaviors including sensory integration, motor control, and various forms of learning, representing a major evolutionary step toward individual agency through its ability to develop knowledge based on its own experience.

## Key facts

- C. elegans is a microscopic worm at one millimeter in length with the best-characterized nervous system of any creature. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p105]
- Adult hermaphrodites have exactly 959 somatic cells and 302 neurons, while adult males have 1,033 cells including 387 neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p106]
- Neuronal subtypes include sensory neurons, motor neurons, and interneurons arranged in a more hierarchical fashion than Hydra's nerve net. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p106]
- Command neurons initiate movements, and once activated, motor sequences happen by themselves without needing central direction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p106]
- C. elegans can learn through association, developing long-lasting preferences based on experience with environmental conditions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p109]
- Individual worms can learn from their own experience and develop their own reasons for choosing one action over another. [raw/books/neuroscience/Free Agents _ How Evolution Gave Us Free Will - Kevin J_ Mitchell.pdf#p110]
