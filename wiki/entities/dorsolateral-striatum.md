# Dorsolateral Striatum

## Summary

The dorsolateral striatum supports habit-based behavior and stimulus-response learning. It receives cortical input from sensory-motor areas and dopaminergic input from the substantia nigra, and projects to the globus pallidus, positioning it to influence motor output.

## Key facts

- The dorsolateral striatum supports habit-based behavior rather than goal-directed action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p235]
- Dorsolateral striatum lesioned rats show disrupted habit learning but normal taste aversion learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p236]
- Activation of the posterior putamen increases when free operant responses to cues become outcome-insensitive. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p236]
- Putamen activity increases over the course of learning, consistent with the idea that it may exert more control over behavior with experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p237]
- Once behaviors are well learned, they may become impervious to negative consequences due to dorsolateral striatum-mediated habit formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p237]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dorsolateral striatum may cause habitual behavior even when it is no longer adaptive, as in addiction where drug cues elicit habitual responses.
