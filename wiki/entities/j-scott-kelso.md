# J. Scott Kelso

## Summary

J. Scott Kelso is a neuroscientist known for his work on coordination dynamics and the dynamical approach to brain and behavior. With Haken and Bunz, he developed a dynamic-systems model of finger coordination demonstrating phase transitions from in-phase to anti-phase patterns at critical frequencies. His work shows that coordination dynamics is informational and semantically meaningful, with order parameters capturing coherent relations among different kinds of things. He has applied phase-transition models to motor skill learning, speech perception, visual perception, and cortical coordination.

## Key facts

- Kelso, with Haken and Bunz, developed a dynamic-systems model of rhythmic finger movement showing spontaneous switching from in-phase to anti-phase patterns. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p50]
- At low speeds there are two comfortable coordination patterns (bistability): in-phase or anti-phase finger movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p50]
- As speed increases, the in-phase pattern becomes unstable and the fingers spontaneously switch to anti-phase at a critical frequency (bifurcation). [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p50]
- Kelso argues that coordination dynamics is informational from the start; order parameters capture coherent relations among different kinds of things. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p66]
- Order parameters in biological systems are functionally specific, context-sensitive informational variables. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p66]
- Kelso has extended phase-transition models to motor skill learning, speech perception, visual perception, and dynamic coordination among cortical areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p51]
