# Numenta

## Summary

Numenta is an independent research company founded by Jeff Hawkins in 2002 (as RNI moved to Berkeley) to develop a theory of how the neocortex works and apply brain principles to machine learning. Over fifteen years old at the time of the book's writing, Numenta combines the focus of a startup with the patience required for fundamental research, having made major discoveries in 2010 (dendrite spikes as predictions) and 2016 (reference frames).

## Key facts

- Numenta was founded as a research company with the primary goal of developing a theory of how the neocortex works. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p22]
- Numenta's secondary goal is to apply what is learned about brains to machine learning and machine intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p22]
- The company operates with startup-like flexibility and focus while requiring patience, since the first major discovery came in 2010, five years after founding. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p22]
- Numenta first published its sequence memory theory in 2011, with a peer-reviewed paper in 2016 titled 'Why Neurons Have Thousands of Synapses.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p56]
