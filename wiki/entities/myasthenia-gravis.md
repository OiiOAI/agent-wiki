# Myasthenia Gravis

## Summary

Myasthenia Gravis is an autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction where antibodies attack nicotinic acetylcholine receptors or muscle-specific tyrosine kinase (MuSK), reducing receptor density and impairing synaptic transmission. It causes variable, fatigable weakness particularly affecting cranial muscles, with symptoms that fluctuate over time. Treatment includes acetylcholinesterase inhibitors and immunosuppression.

## Key facts

- Myasthenia gravis results from antibodies against nicotinic acetylcholine receptors or MuSK at the neuromuscular junction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Anti-ACh receptor antibodies interfere with synaptic transmission by reducing receptor numbers or impeding ACh interaction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Myasthenia gravis always affects cranial muscles (eyelids, eye muscles, oropharyngeal muscles) as well as limb muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Symptoms vary over the course of a single day, from day to day, or over longer periods with remissions and exacerbations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- About 15% of adult patients have thymomas, and myasthenia often co-occurs with other autoimmune diseases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Neostigmine (an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor) reverses the decremental response of compound muscle action potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Myasthenia gravis is caused by antibodies against nicotinic acetylcholine receptors that reduce the number of functional receptors and interfere with ACh interaction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p366]
- Four characteristic features: affects cranial muscles and limbs; severity varies during the day and over longer periods; no denervation signs on EMG; weakness reversed by acetylcholinesterase inhibitors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p366]
- Antibodies cross-link ACh receptors, triggering their degradation and complement activation leading to focal lysis of the postsynaptic membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p369]
- Approximately 15% of adult patients have thymomas, and myasthenia often co-occurs with other autoimmune diseases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p367]

## Inferences

- Inference: The variability in symptoms over time and the association with other autoimmune conditions strongly suggests an immune dysregulation component beyond simple antibody production against neuromuscular junction proteins.
- Inference: The reduction in functional ACh receptors decreases the probability that released ACh will find a receptor before being hydrolyzed, reducing end-plate potential amplitude to near threshold.
- Inference: The enlarged synaptic cleft and simplified junctional folds in myasthenia further reduce transmission efficiency by promoting diffusion of ACh away from the synapse.
