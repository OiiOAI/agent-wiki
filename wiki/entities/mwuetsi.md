# Mwuetsi (Moon Man)

## Summary

The cosmic male figure from Wahungwe Makoni mythology of South Rhodesia who was placed at the bottom of a lake by Maori (God) and later emerged onto the earth. He was given two wives—Massassi (morning star) and Morongo (evening star)—each for two years, and through them generated grasses, trees, animals, and finally human beings. Campbell uses this to illustrate the Virgin Birth concept and the cosmogonic progression through sexual generations.

## Key facts

- Mwuetsi the Moon Man was placed on the bottom of Dsivoa lake and given Massassi the morning star as wife for two years. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p237]
- Through Massassi, Mwuetsi generated grasses, bushes, and trees until they grew to touch the sky; through Morongo he generated animals and humans. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p238]
- When Morongo slept with the snake and stopped giving birth, the world sickened; Mwuetsi's children strangled him and buried him, choosing another man to be king, and the symbolic parental figures subsided into the original abyss. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p239]
