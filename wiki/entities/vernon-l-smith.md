# Vernon L. Smith

## Summary

Vernon L. Smith is a Nobel Prize-winning economist known for pioneering experimental economics. He shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences with Daniel Kahneman for his contributions to experimental economics, particularly laboratory studies of market mechanisms. His work has been influential in the development of neuroeconomics through studies of trust games and the neural basis of market exchange.

## Key facts

- Smith shared the Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences with Kahneman in 2002 for his contributions to experimental economics, particularly the use of controlled experiments. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p9]
- Smith's experimental economics began with the viewpoint that economic principles should apply everywhere, as principles in natural and physical sciences are presumed to. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p4]
- Smith collaborated with McCabe on brain imaging studies of the trust game, examining behavior and neural activation in strategic games. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p9]
- Smith proposed three interdependent orders of brain/mind decision making: the internal order of the mind, the social order of exchange, and the extended order of market institutions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p15]

## Inferences

- Inference: Smith's contributions established that carefully controlled experiments could reveal principles of economic behavior, paving the way for neuroeconomic experiments that combine economic methodology with neural measurement.
