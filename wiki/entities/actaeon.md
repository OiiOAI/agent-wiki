# Actaeon

## Summary

Young Greek huntsman who inadvertently saw the goddess Diana bathing naked at noon. As punishment for this unprepared viewing of the divine, she transformed him into a stag and he was torn apart by his own hounds—a warning against viewing the goddess without spiritual preparation.

## Key facts

- Actaeon saw Diana at the fateful moment when the sun breaks in its youthful strong ascent, balances, and begins the plunge to death. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p91]
- Diana flung water into Actaeon's face and declared 'Now you are free to tell, if you can, that you have seen the goddess nude'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p92]
- Diana transformed Actaeon into a stag with antlers, great neck, eartips, and hooves, and his own hounds later tore him apart. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p92]
- Actaeon represents the unprepared man spiritually incapable of bearing the revelation of the form that must be beheld without infantile overtones of desire, surprise, and fear. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p94]
