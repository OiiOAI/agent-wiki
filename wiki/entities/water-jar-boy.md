# Water Jar Boy

## Summary

Water Jar Boy is a Pueblo hero born from a water jar rather than a mother's womb. After a long period of obscurity as a jar without limbs, he broke free upon striking a rock, revealing himself as a fully adorned warrior. He later discovered his father was the water snake Avaiyo'pi'i living in a spring.

## Key facts

- A young Pueblo woman felt a splash of mud on her leg; after some days she gave birth not to a baby but to a little round water jar with two things sticking out. Her father became very fond of the jar, which was found to be moving and growing, and could talk after twenty days. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p256]
- Water Jar Boy asked his grandfather to take him rabbit hunting despite having no legs or arms. When he hit a rock while chasing a rabbit, he broke and a boy jumped up wearing beads, turquoise earrings, a dance kilt, moccasins, and a buckskin shirt. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p259]
- When Water Jar Boy asked 'Who is my father?' his mother could not tell him. He found his father at a spring, the water snake Avaiyo'pi'i, who had come out of the spring to meet him. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p271]
- After finding his father, his mother became sick and died. The boy went to live with his father in the spring, who explained he made the mother sick so she would die and 'come over here to live with me.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p282]

## Inferences
