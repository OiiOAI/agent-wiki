# Fraxile X Syndrome

## Summary

Fragile X syndrome is an X-linked neurodevelopmental disorder caused by mutations that reduce expression of fragile X mental retardation protein (FMRP). Affected children may have intellectual disability, poor social cognition, high social anxiety, repetitive behavior, and about 30% of boys meet criteria for autism spectrum disorder. FMRP regulates activity-dependent mRNA translation in neurons, linking synaptic plasticity to higher-order cognitive processes.

## Key facts

- Fragile X syndrome results from mutations that reduce expression of fragile X mental retardation protein (FMRP). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p94]
- About 30% of boys with fragile X syndrome meet diagnostic criteria for autism spectrum disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p94]
- FMRP regulates the translation of mRNAs into proteins in neurons in a process regulated by neuronal activity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p94]
- Fragile X syndrome results from expansion of CGG repeats beyond 200 in the FMR1 gene on the X chromosome, causing gene methylation and silencing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1531]
- FMRP is a selective RNA-binding protein that blocks translation of mRNA until protein synthesis is required, regulating local dendritic protein synthesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1531]
- The prevalence of fragile X syndrome is approximately 1 in 4,000 boys and 1 in 8,000 girls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1531]
- Approximately 30% of boys with fragile X meet all diagnostic criteria for ASD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1531]
- Antagonists of metabotropic glutamate receptor 5 (mGluR5) may lessen excess protein translation in fragile X syndrome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1531]
