# Autism Spectrum Disorder

## Summary

Autism spectrum disorders are developmental syndromes affecting approximately 2-3% of the population with a male predominance of approximately 3:1. They are characterized by impairment in reciprocal social communication and stereotyped interests/repetitive behaviors, with symptoms emerging in the first 3 years of life. Genetics studies have identified over 100 associated genes, with about 45 reaching high confidence. Many risk genes are involved in synaptic formation/function and are targets of fragile X mental retardation protein.

## Key facts

- Autism spectrum disorders affect approximately 2% to 3% of the population with a male predominance of approximately 3:1. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p96]
- Large-scale studies of de novo mutations in autism spectrum disorders have identified more than 100 associated genes, with about 45 reaching the highest confidence level. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p96]
- Risk genes for autism spectrum disorder are significantly overrepresented for genes involved in synaptic formation and function and in regulation of transcription. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p96]
- ASD is defined by two core diagnostic features: impaired social communication and stereotyped behaviors with highly restricted interests. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1524]
- The prevalence of ASD is estimated to be at least 1.5% in developed countries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1524]
- The male-to-female ratio in ASD is approximately 2:1 to 3:1 male excess. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1524]
- About half of all individuals with ASD also have intellectual disability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1524]
- Approximately 10% of clearly affected children show improvement with little or no evidence of social disability later in life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1524]
- Theory of mind impairment, termed 'mind blindness,' is thought to underlie social deficits in ASD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1526]
- The mentalizing system involves medial prefrontal cortex, temporoparietal region, amygdala, and inferior temporal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1527]
- Individuals with ASD tend to fixate on people's mouths instead of showing the normal preference for eyes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1529]
- About 10% of individuals with ASD demonstrate savant skills compared to about 1 in 1,000 individuals with other forms of intellectual disability. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1529]
- Monozygotic twins show 60% to 90% concordance for ASD, while dizygotic twins show 10% to 30% concordance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1530]
- There is no credible evidence that immunizations contribute to ASD risk. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1530]
