# t Gene

## Summary

The t gene in mice is one of the best-known segregation distorters. When a male mouse has only one t gene, up to 95% of his sperm contain the t gene rather than the expected 50%. Mice with two t genes die young or are sterile (t is lethal in homozygous state). When t spreads through a population, homozygotes accumulate and the population can be driven extinct.

## Key facts

- The t gene in mice is a segregation distorter: when a male mouse has only one t gene, up to 95 per cent of his sperm contain the t gene. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p276]
- t is said to be 'lethal' in the homozygous state: mice with two t genes either die young or are sterile. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p276]
- The t gene spreads so fast through a population that large numbers of individuals inherit it in double dose, die or become sterile, and the whole local population can be driven extinct. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p277]
