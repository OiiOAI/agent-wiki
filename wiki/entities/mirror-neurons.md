# Mirror Neurons

## Summary

Mirror neurons are neurons in the premotor cortex (area F5) discovered by Giacomo Rizzolatti and colleagues at the University of Parma that fire both when a monkey performs a particular action and when it watches another monkey or experimenter performing the same action. These neurons serve dual functions—active during intentional action and during interpretation of others' intentional actions. They may form the neurophysiological basis of social interactions by linking action and its meaning or intent, whether committed by self, others, or through mental simulation.

## Key facts

- Giacomo Rizzolatti and colleagues at the University of Parma discovered mirror neurons in the premotor cortex (F5) of macaque monkeys, observing they fire both during action performance and action observation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p129]
- A small subset of mirror neurons show strong correlation between observed and executed actions in both general action (e.g., grasping) and executed action (e.g., precision grip). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p129]
- Human fMRI studies show inferior frontal cortex and rostral right superior parietal lobe activate during both action execution and observation, with the opercular region representing observed action as a motor goal without defining precise movement details. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p130]
- During internalized states, the motor system puts action planning in a 'motor format' so that it resembles real action even when not accompanied by overt behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mirror neurons represent a spectacular example of brain disengagement from sensory input and motor reafferentation, similar to how hippocampal neurons are active during both encoding and recall of experience.
