# SRY Gene

## Summary

SRY (sex-determining region on Y) is the master regulatory gene on the Y chromosome that directs male sex determination by inducing expression of SOX9 and initiating the cascade that differentiates the embryonic gonad into testes rather than ovaries.

## Key facts

- SRY is the sex-determining gene on the Y chromosome whose activity is required for masculinization of the embryonic gonads. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1359]
- SRY encodes a transcription factor that induces SOX9 expression, which is required for differentiation of testes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1360]
- Inactivation or deletion of SRY leads to complete sex reversal: XY individuals develop as phenotypically females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1359]
- When SRY translocates to another chromosome, XX individuals develop as phenotypically males (XXSRY). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1359]
