# Mormonism

## Summary

Chapter 24 of Fukuyama's Trust examines Mormonism as a case study in 'rugged conformism' — a distinctive American combination of religious community, economic entrepreneurship, and social conservatism. The religion emerged in the early 19th century under Joseph Smith and grew into a global movement notable for high birth rates, low illegitimacy, strong welfare programs, and entrepreneurial success among its members.

## Key facts

- Mormon families average 4.61 children, which is twice the US national average. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p442]
- The rate of illegitimate teenage births in Utah is 48 per 1,000 live births, compared to 155 per 1,000 nationally — less than one-third the national average. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p442]
- Joseph Smith's 112 revelations, 88 dealt with economic matters. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p442]
- In practice, only half of young Mormon men, and a smaller percentage of women, go on mission. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p442]
- The fast offering required church members to donate the whole of their incomes to the church, which would return a portion as it saw fit. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p443]
- Mormons have been highly entrepreneurial, founding companies like WordPerfect. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p443]
- Ray Noorda initially could not get any banks in Salt Lake City to loan him money when he first tried to turn Novell around, due to the Mormon practice of avoiding debt. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p443]
- By the year 2000, there were projected to be more Spanish- than English-speaking Mormons; large non-European Mormon communities exist in Polynesia, the Philippines, and Africa. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p444]
- Mormons in Utah constitute only one million out of the nine million worldwide. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p444]

## Inferences

- Inference: Fukuyama uses Mormonism to illustrate 'rugged conformism' — the paradox that intense religious community and strict doctrinal conformity can coexist with and even promote individual economic initiative
- Inference: The combination of high social cohesion, strong family structures, and economic individualism in Mormonism demonstrates that trust-based communities need not undermine personal ambition

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The source notes that the fast offering 'ultimately proved unworkable' but still remains 'something of an ideal' — the degree to which it is currently practiced is unclear
