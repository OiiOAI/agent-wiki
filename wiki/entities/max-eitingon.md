# Max Eitingon

## Summary

Max Eitingon (1881-1943) was a psychoanalyst who created the Berlin Psychoanalytical Policlinic, supporting it initially out of his own resources. The Berlin Policlinic was an important institution for training analysts and providing treatment to those who could not afford private analysis.

## Key facts

- Max Eitingon created the Berlin Psychoanalytical Policlinic and supported it initially out of his own resources. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6183]
- Eitingon served as an adviser and collaborator to Dr S. Radó in editing the Zeitschrift. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6191]
- Eitingon established the Berlin Psychoanalytic Institute from his own resources in 1920, the first such institute to serve three functions: making analysis accessible to the poor, providing theoretical teaching, and advancing knowledge through clinical application. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6792]
- Freud notes that an institute of this kind was indispensable but that they would have waited in vain for assistance from the State or interest from the University. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6792]
