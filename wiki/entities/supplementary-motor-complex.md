# Supplementary Motor Complex

## Summary

The supplementary motor complex (SMC) consists of the supplementary motor area (SMA) proper and the presupplementary motor area (pre-SMA). Located in the medial wall of the frontal cortex, the SMC is involved in contextual control of voluntary behavior, including self-initiated movements, movement suppression, executive control, and motor sequence organization. Lesions produce deficits in movement initiation, suppression, and sequence learning.

## Key facts

- The supplementary motor complex (SMC) contains two areas with distinct cytoarchitectonic characteristics, axonal connections, and functional properties: a more caudal SMA proper and a more rostral pre-SMA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Electrical stimulation of SMC below the threshold for movement initiation can evoke an introspective sense of an urge to move that does not arise during M1 stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- Lesions of SMC produce problems initiating desired movements or suppressing undesirable movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p828]
- The readiness potential, a slow cortical potential arising in the frontal cortex up to 0.8 to 1.0 second before movement onset, has its peak in the SMC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p829]
- The SMC has been implicated in executive control of behavior, including operations required to switch between different actions, plans, and strategies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p830]
- Neurons in both SMA and pre-SMA discharge before and during voluntary movements, but unlike M1 neurons, the activity of most SMA neurons is less tightly coupled to particular actions and appears associated with more complex, coordinated motor acts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p829]
- Pre-SMA neurons often begin to discharge much earlier in advance of movement onset and are less tightly coupled to movement execution compared to SMA neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p829]
- Some SMC neurons discharge only when a particular movement occurs in a specific position in a sequence or when a particular pair of consecutive movements occurs regardless of their position. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p830]
