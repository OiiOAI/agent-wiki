# Cisco Systems

## Summary

Cisco Systems was founded by Stanford staff members Andy Bechtolsheim and William Yeager, who created a 'blue box' device to interconnect proprietary computer networks using different protocols. After an acrimonious split with Stanford in 1987, Cisco received rights to the software for $167,000. The company grew to become, briefly in 2000, the most valuable corporation in the world by riding four simultaneous waves of change: software centrality, corporate networking growth, IP networking, and the public Internet explosion.

## Key facts

- Cisco Systems originated at Stanford University in the early 1980s when Ralph Gorin requested a way to interconnect Apple, Alto, and DEC computer networks. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p73]
- The original solution, called the 'blue box,' was engineered by Stanford staff members Andy Bechtolsheim and William Yeager. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p73]
- Cisco received full legal rights to the software in 1987 for a payment of $167,000 and the promise of discounts on Cisco products. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p73]
- By 2000, Cisco briefly became the most valuable corporation in the world. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p73]
- Cisco outsourced hardware manufacturing while concentrating on software, sales, and service. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p73]
- Cisco captured two-thirds of the corporate networking market during the 1993 Internet boom. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p73]
