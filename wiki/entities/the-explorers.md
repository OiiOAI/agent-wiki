# The Explorers

## Summary

One of three post-infidelity outcomes identified by Esther Perel. Explorers are couples for whom the affair becomes a catalyst for transformation. When faced with the collapse of their known world, they home in on each other with intensity they haven't experienced in years. They can experience a combustive rekindling of desire mixing anxiety and lust. Their marriages become richer, more interesting, though potentially less secure, integrating novelty, mystery, and risk into the familiar.

## Key facts

- Explorers 'home in on each other with a level of intensity they haven't experienced in years' and it is 'not uncommon for them to experience a combustive rekindling of desire that is a potent mixture of anxiety and lust'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p295]
- Perel notes Explorers have 'an uncanny ability to express and accept a wide range of feelings without demanding premature closure' and their 'tolerance for ambiguity and uncertainty opened up a space for exploration'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p295]
- Madison and Dennis's language shifted from 'you' and 'me' to 'our,' talking about 'When we had our crisis' rather than 'When you did this to me'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p296]
- Dennis says: 'I'm not sure at all where this is going to take us, but dull it certainly is not.' The explorers added 'novelty to the enduring, mystery to the familiar, and risk to the predictable'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p296]
- Perel summarizes: 'To repair is to re-pair.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p296]
