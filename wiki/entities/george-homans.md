# George Homans

## Summary

George Homans (1910-1989) was a wealthy Bostonian who received his bachelor's degree from Harvard in 1932. During the Great Depression he attended L. J. Henderson's course on Vilfredo Pareto's theories, which shaped his anti-Marxist orientation. He co-authored "An Introduction to Pareto" in 1934, which made him a sociologist. As a junior fellow at Harvard until 1939, he absorbed more sociology and later joined Parsons's department of social relations. Homans was highly critical of Parsons's style of theorizing, arguing that theory should be built from empirical observations rather than general theoretical categories. In the 1950s he developed exchange theory based on B. F. Skinner's psychological behaviorism, publishing "Social Behavior: Its Elementary Forms" in 1961. His basic view was that sociology should focus on individual behavior and interaction, reinforcement patterns, and the history of rewards and costs that lead people to act as they do.
