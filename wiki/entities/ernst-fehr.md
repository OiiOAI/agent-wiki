# Ernst Fehr

## Summary

Ernst Fehr is a Swiss economist at the University of Zurich known for his work on experimental economics, social preferences, fairness, and cooperation. His research has demonstrated that a substantial fraction of people exhibit social preferences and are willing to incur costs to punish norm violations. He has been a key figure in establishing the neuroeconomics of social behavior.

## Key facts

- Fehr's work established that neither concerns for others' welfare nor fairness and reciprocity can be ignored in social interactions, contrary to standard economic assumptions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p217]
- Fehr and Gächter demonstrated that altruistic punishment—the willingness to costly punish free-riders—maintains cooperation in public goods games. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p177]
- Fehr and Schmidt developed the inequity aversion model showing that many people dislike unequal outcomes per se. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p216]
- Fehr's cross-cultural studies show large variations in social preferences across small-scale societies depending on cultural and institutional features. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p220]
- Fehr and Gächter's public goods game showed that 84% of subjects paid to punish free riders even when no reciprocity was possible. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p200]
- Their experiment demonstrated that punishment of bad behavior promotes virtue; when punishment was removed, cooperation collapsed. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p200]
