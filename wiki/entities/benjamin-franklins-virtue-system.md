# Benjamin Franklin's Virtue System

## Summary

In his twenties, Benjamin Franklin created a system of 13 virtues (Temperance, Silence, Resolution, Frugality, Industry, Sincerity, Justice, Moderation, Cleanliness, Tranquility, Chastity, Humility, and Order) that he tracked on weekly charts. He focused on one virtue per week, marking failures with black pencil marks. Though he never achieved 'moral perfection' or a clean chart, he concluded he became 'a better and a happier man' through the endeavor.

## Key facts

- Franklin tracked 13 virtues on weekly charts, focusing on one virtue per week. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p64]
- He made black pencil marks for each failure and eventually wore holes in the paper from repeated erasing. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p65]
- Franklin concluded he became 'a better and a happier man' through attempting the system even though he never achieved perfection. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p70-71]
