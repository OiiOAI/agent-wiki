# Julius Caesar

## Summary

Carse invokes Julius Caesar as an illustration of the contradiction of finite play at its highest form: playing so successfully that all need for play is erased. Caesar originally sought power in Rome because he loved to play the dangerous style of politics common to the Republic, but he played the game so well that he destroyed all his opponents, making it impossible for him to find genuinely dangerous combat. His word became irresistible and for that reason he could speak with no one, and his isolation was complete. Carse cites Syme: 'We might almost say this man was looking for an assassination.'
