# Richard Wrangham

## Summary

Professor of biological anthropology at Harvard University and author of 'Demonic Males.' Wrangham argues that human violence and male aggression have roots in our common ancestry with chimpanzees, based on similarities in patrilineal social structure and lethal raiding behavior.

## Key facts

- Wrangham states that only chimpanzees and humans share the suite of behaviors: patrilineal, male-bonded communities with female dispersal and lethal raiding into neighboring territories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p77]
- 30% of male chimps die from aggression in Gombe National Park, the same percentage found in Yanomami villages. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p78]
- Wrangham hypothesizes that pride evolved during countless generations in which males who achieved high status were able to turn social success into extra reproduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p81]
