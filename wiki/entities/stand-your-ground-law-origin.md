# Stand Your Ground Law Origin

## Summary

Florida's controversial 'Stand Your Ground' law was justified partly by the story of James Workman shooting an apparent intruder. The true backstory: Workman shot Rodney Cox, an African American FEMA employee who was simply doing his job in the hurricane-damaged area. Workman's affective realism caused him to perceive deadly threat where none existed, but this accurate story did not become part of the law's justification.

## Key facts

- The true backstory: Workman's trailer was in a hurricane-damaged area, and the man he shot was a FEMA employee, Rodney Cox, who was African American. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p258]
- Workman, under the influence of affective realism, perceived that Cox meant him harm and opened fire on an innocent man. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p258]
- The inaccurate first story (elderly couple defended against intruder) became a primary justification for Florida's Stand Your Ground law. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p258]
