# Sophists

## Summary

The Sophists were ancient Greek humanist teachers who sought to teach beliefs of men rather than principles, with the object of improving men. They taught that all principles, all truths, are relative: 'Man is the measure of all things.' Phædrus discovers they were actually teaching aretē (excellence), the precursor to Quality. Plato and Socrates condemned the Sophists as enemies of Truth, but Phædrus sees this conflict as a war between those who upheld the Good (Sophists) and those who upheld the True (Dialecticians). The Sophists lost this philosophical war, resulting in Quality being subordinated to Reason in Western thought.

## Key facts

- The Sophists taught that all principles, all truths, are relative: 'Man is the measure of all things.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p319]
- The Sophists were not teaching ethical relativism but aretē (excellence), using rhetoric as their medium. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p322]
- Plato's condemnation of the Sophists was motivated by fear they threatened humanity's first grasp of the idea of Truth. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p319]
- Phædrus sees Truth winning over the Good as the reason modern people have difficulty accepting Quality. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p317]
