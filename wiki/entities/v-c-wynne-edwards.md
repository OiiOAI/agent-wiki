# V. C. Wynne-Edwards

## Summary

V.C. Wynne-Edwards was a Scottish ornithologist and ecologist who championed the theory of group selection to explain animal behaviors like birth control, territoriality, and dominance hierarchies. He proposed that animals deliberately and altruistically reduce their birth rates for the good of the group, and that epideictic displays (large aggregations) serve as population censuses. His ideas were controversial and opposed by mainstream evolutionary biologists who favored individual-level selection explanations.

## Key facts

- Wynne-Edwards proposed that individual animals deliberately and altruistically reduce their birth rates for the good of the group as a whole, invoking group selection because normal natural selection cannot give rise to evolution of lower-than-average reproductive rates. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p145]
- Wynne-Edwards interpreted territoriality as animals fighting over 'token prizes' (territories) rather than actual resources, where winning a territory functions like winning a 'ticket or licence to breed'—limiting total population births to the number of available territories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p146]
- He interpreted dominance hierarchies similarly, seeing high social rank as a 'ticket of entitlement to reproduce,' where individuals fight over status and accept that low-ranking individuals should not breed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p147]
- Wynne-Edwards coined the term 'epideictic behavior' to describe aggregations like starling murmurations, proposing they serve as deliberate population censuses linking density perception to reproductive control. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p148]
- Dawkins acknowledges that Wynne-Edwards's theory is 'on the face of it, rather plausible,' but argues the evidence is not good—the examples can equally well be interpreted on selfish gene lines. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p148]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wynne-Edwards's ideas attracted controversy because they seemed to explain altruistic birth control, but the book argues his group selection explanations are unnecessary when individual-level selection (selfish gene theory, kin selection) can explain the same phenomena.
