# Washington Post

## Summary

Berkshire's investment in the Washington Post in 1973-74 exemplified the ideal investment: a dominant business (top hand in a one-winner game) purchased at a deep discount (one-fifth of value) with high-integrity management.

## Key facts

- Berkshire bought Washington Post at about twenty percent of value to a private owner—purchased on a Ben Graham-style basis at one-fifth of obvious value. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p204]
- The investment combined top hand in a game that was clearly going to end with one winner (newspaper consolidation) plus management with lot of integrity and intelligence—described as an 'absolute damn dream.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p204]
- The Katharine Graham family were very high-class people, making it a dream situation; this was a once-in-forty-years type denouement in the markets (1973-74). [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p204]
- That investment is up about fifty times over cost; Munger advises not to count on getting any investment in a lifetime quite as good as Washington Post in 1973-74. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p205]
