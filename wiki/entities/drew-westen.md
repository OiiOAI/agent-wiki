# Drew Westen

## Summary

Political psychologist who conducted fMRI research in 2004 capturing partisan brains in action during the U.S. presidential election. Studied 15 highly partisan Democrats and 15 highly partisan Republicans viewing contradictory statements by Bush and Kerry. Found that threatening information about one's own candidate activated emotion-related brain areas, with no increase in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex activity (cool reasoning), supporting Hume's model over Jefferson's dual-process model.

## Key facts

- Westen used fMRI to study partisan brains watching 18 sets of slides showing candidate contradictions and resolutions during the 2004 election. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p103]
- Threatening information (own candidate's hypocrisy) immediately activated emotion-related brain areas associated with negative emotion and punishment responses. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p104]
- There was no increase in dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (dlPFC) activity—the main area for cool reasoning tasks. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p104]
- When partisans were released from the threat by the final slide, the ventral striatum (brain's reward center) started humming with dopamine. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p104]
- The data supported Hume's model with emotional and intuitive processes running the show, not Jefferson's dual-process model where reasoning processes information equally. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p104]
