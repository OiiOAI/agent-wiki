# Wolfram Schultz

## Summary

Wolfram Schultz is a neuroscientist who pioneered single-unit recordings from midbrain dopamine neurons in awake, behaving monkeys. His laboratory's findings demonstrated that dopamine neurons encode reward prediction errors consistent with temporal difference learning models, fundamentally linking computational neuroscience to the neurobiology of reward.

## Key facts

- Schultz's lab showed that dopamine neurons respond to unexpected rewards but not to predicted rewards, with responses shifting to predictive cues during conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p325]
- Schultz and colleagues demonstrated that dopamine neurons respond to predicted rewards in proportion to the magnitude and probability of the predicted reward, consistent with economic value. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p326]

## Inferences

- Inference: Schultz's work established the empirical foundation for the reward prediction error theory of dopamine function.
- Inference: His research demonstrated that individual dopamine neurons code reward predictions in a remarkably homogeneous manner.
