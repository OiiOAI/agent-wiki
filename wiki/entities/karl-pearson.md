# Karl Pearson

## Summary

British statistician (1857-1936) who completed Galton's work and expunged causation from statistics. He founded Biometrika journal and the Biometrics Lab at University College London. Pearson argued that causation was merely perfect correlation and that data was all there was to science, dismissing intervention and counterfactuals as scientifically meaningless.

## Key facts

- Pearson argued that causation is simply perfect correlation (coefficient of 1 or -1) and that science can only describe patterns of observation without demonstrating inherent necessity. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L510-L530]
- Pearson founded Biometrika journal in 1901 and established the Biometrics Lab at University College London in 1911 using Galton's endowment. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L525-L540]
- Pearson discovered Simpson's paradox through skull measurements showing that combining two uncorrelated populations could produce spurious correlations. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L545-L560]
- Pearson demanded loyalty from followers and drove dissenters away, creating what historians describe as a 'schismatic sect' hostile to causal thinking. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L530-L545]
