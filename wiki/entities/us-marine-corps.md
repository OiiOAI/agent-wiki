# US Marine Corps

## Summary

The US Marine Corps adopted Boyd's ideas and incorporated them into their doctrine. Starting in 1979, Marine Corps officers were exposed to Boyd's ideas through lectures and discussions. Boyd played an active role in the Marines' doctrinal change from attrition-style warfare toward maneuver warfare. In 1994, the Marine Corps adopted nonlinear dynamics and complexity theory as underlying basis for their maneuver warfare doctrine.

## Key facts

- From 1979 onwards, Marine Corps officers later achieving high ranks were exposed to Boyd's ideas through lectures and discussions. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p80]
- Boyd's ideas were incorporated in experiments at the Amphibious Warfare School and in discussions within the Marine Corps Development Center, which aimed to find alternatives to attrition-style warfare with linear tactics. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p80]
- Boyd argued for non-linear tactics, avoiding and bypassing enemy positions, venturing deep into enemy territory without concern for flanks – the prize was time, surprise and shock rather than territory. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p81]
- In 1994 the US Marine Corps adopted nonlinear dynamics and complexity theory, realizing they provided an underlying basis for the Marine doctrine of maneuver warfare embodied in the capstone manual Warfighting. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p81]
- Boyd was bestowed the distinguished title of 'honorable marine' and the US Marine Corps University houses Boyd's collection of papers at specific request by the Commandant of the US Marine Corps. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p81]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Marine Corps was the first US service to formally adopt Boyd's systems-theoretical and complexity perspective as the foundation for their doctrinal framework.
