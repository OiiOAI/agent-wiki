# Schizophrenia

## Summary

A psychiatric disorder discussed in relation to consciousness and unconscious processing. The book examines how schizophrenia may involve disrupted consciousness signatures, particularly in relation to hallucinations and meaning processing.

## Key facts

- Schizophrenia is associated with disrupted unconscious processing and abnormal gamma-band synchronization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p254-259]
- Hallucinations in schizophrenia may result from failure of internal monitoring systems that normally distinguish self-generated from external information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p254-258]
- Masking experiments reveal abnormal processing in schizophrenia patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p254-255]
- Schizophrenia involves disrupted meaning processing at the unconscious level. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p255-257]
- Schizophrenia affects about 1% of all young adults, causing a pattern of thought disorders and emotional withdrawal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p96]
- GWAS studies have identified 108 risk loci for schizophrenia from nearly 40,000 cases and 113,000 controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p97]
- The complement C4 gene is associated with schizophrenia risk, and mice with C4 knockouts had deficits in synaptic pruning during development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p97]
- Schizophrenia affects 0.25% to 0.75% of the population worldwide. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1536]
- Males are more commonly affected than females with a sex ratio of approximately 3:2, and onset is often earlier in males. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1536]
- Schizophrenia typically begins during the late teen years or early to mid-twenties. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1536]
- Schizophrenia is characterized by loss of gray matter in prefrontal, temporal, and parietal regions of cerebral cortex with counterbalancing increases in cerebral ventricle size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1539]
- Gray matter loss in schizophrenia results from decreased dendritic arborization and dendritic spines rather than cell death. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1541]
- The strongest genetic association with schizophrenia lies within the MHC locus encoding complement factor C4, with elevated C4A expression associated with increased risk. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1544]
- All current antipsychotic drugs produce their therapeutic effects by blocking D2 dopamine receptors in the forebrain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1545]

## Inferences

- Inference: Schizophrenia may represent a disorder of consciousness signatures, where the brain's ability to generate normal conscious markers is impaired
- Inference: The overpruning hypothesis suggests schizophrenia may result from abnormal and excessive synaptic pruning during adolescence, supported by the association with complement factor C4.
