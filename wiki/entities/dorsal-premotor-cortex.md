# Dorsal Premotor Cortex (PMd)

## Summary

The dorsal premotor cortex (PMd), part of the lateral premotor cortex, plays a crucial role in planning sensory-guided arm movements. It contains neurons that signal intended motor acts including spatial target location and movement direction during instructed-delay periods. PMd can prepare multiple potential motor actions before a final decision, reflects decisions not to move, and is involved in applying rules that govern behavior. It works in concert with parietal reach region (PRR) in motor planning.

## Key facts

- The dorsal premotor cortex (PMd) is involved in planning sensory-guided movement of the arm based on external sensory inputs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p832]
- PMd delay-period activity can reflect the intention to reach to a particular location with either the contralateral or ipsilateral arm in an extrinsic spatial coordinate framework. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p832]
- PMd can prepare multiple potential motor actions prior to the final decision about which action to take, possibly limited to no more than three to four simultaneous potential actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p832]
- Many PMd neurons generate directionally tuned activity during an instructed-delay period when instructed to reach to a target, but decrease their activity when instructed to refrain from reaching. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p833]
- PMd is implicated in the acquisition of new movement-related associations or rules linking specific symbolic cues to particular actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p834]
- Neural activity in PMd after visual test images is correlated more strongly to the motor decision than the perceptual decision, but also reflects the behavioral rule during the delay period. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- PMd activity during passive observation of visual events processes observed sensory events to predict their ultimate outcome for the monkey, such as the likelihood of a free juice reward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
- Reach-related neurons in parietal area PRR contribute to the preparation for two potential motor actions before the final action decision is made, similar to PMd. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p832]
