# The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (2000)

## Summary

Kandel received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine on October 9, 2000, sharing it with Arvid Carlsson and Paul Greengard for their work on signal transduction in the nervous system. The prize recognized that Parkinson's disease results from failure in synaptic release of dopamine treatable with L-DOPA; that second messengers activate protein kinases; and that neurotransmitter signaling creates short- and long-term memory. The ceremony took place on December 10, 2000, at the Stockholm Concert Hall.

## Key facts

- Kandel was awakened at 5:15 A.M. on October 9, 2000, by Hans Jörnvall, secretary general of the Nobel Foundation, informing him of the prize. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p400]
- The prize was shared with Arvid Carlsson and Paul Greengard for 'signal transduction in the nervous system', with Carlsson's research showing Parkinson's is due to failure in synaptic release of dopamine treatable by L-DOPA. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p407]
- Kandel's contribution was showing how transmitters through second messengers and protein phosphorylation create short- and long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p407]
- At the banquet, Kandel spoke about the biology of mind bridging sciences and humanities, stating 'the biology of mind bridges the sciences—concerned with the natural world—and the humanities—concerned with the meaning of human experience.'.
