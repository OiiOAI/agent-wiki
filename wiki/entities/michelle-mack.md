# Michelle Mack

## Summary

Michelle Mack is a woman born with only a right hemisphere, whose left hemisphere failed to develop in utero. Despite this catastrophic brain challenge, she achieved remarkable neuroplastic reorganization, with her right hemisphere taking over essential functions including speech and language. Born November 9, 1973, she demonstrates both extraordinary savant abilities and specific cognitive challenges that illuminate the boundaries of brain plasticity.

## Key facts

- Michelle Mack was born on November 9, 1973, with only a right hemisphere; her left hemisphere never developed in utero. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p261]
- Her left carotid artery, which supplies blood to the left hemisphere, may have become blocked while Michelle was still a fetus, preventing that hemisphere from forming. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p261]
- Her right hemisphere took over essential mental functions including speech and language, which normally reside in the left hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p262]
- She holds a part-time job, reads, enjoys movies and her family, votes in elections, and follows basketball. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p262]
- Michelle has extraordinary calculating skills — savant skills — including the ability to calculate the day of the week for any date she remembers (back to the mid-1980s) at lightning speed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p272]
- She has difficulty understanding abstract thought, proverbs, metaphors, and extracting the main point from stories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p270]
- She uses nonsense words and singing, such as 'ACTING BEES!', as a way to discharge feelings and maintain control when frustrated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p269]
- She is hyper-sensitive to sound and touch, and her mother Carol cuts tags off her clothing to prevent sensory overload. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p264]
- She has supercharged hearing to compensate for her right visual field blindness (since visual information from the right goes to the missing left hemisphere). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p263]
- She enjoys repetitive activities like data entry, folding one thousand church flyers in a half hour, and playing solitaire at lightning speed by visualizing the entire deck. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p270]
- She associates each day of the week with a vivid scene from her past, which helps her remember dates and events with extraordinary clarity going back to the fall of 1984. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p274]
- She is being studied at the National Institutes of Health under Dr. Jordan Grafman. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p267]

## Inferences

- Inference: Michelle's right hemisphere likely processed speech before visual-spatial abilities because she was partially blind and barely able to crawl as an infant, so speech learning preceded seeing and walking.
- Inference: Her superior event registration may have developed because there was no left hemisphere to inhibit it after the main point was extracted.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact cause of her left hemisphere failing to develop remains unknown; doctors speculate a blocked left carotid artery but nothing was confirmed.
