# Jim Jones

## Summary

Reverend Jim Jones was the undisputed leader of the People's Temple cult who ordered the mass suicide of 910 people in Jonestown, Guyana in November 1978. Cialdini analyzes his methods as demonstrating mastery of social proof principles, particularly through the strategic relocation of the group to an environment of maximum uncertainty and exclusive similarity.

## Key facts

- Dr. Louis Jolyon West stated that the Jonestown incident 'wouldn't have happened in California' but occurred because members lived 'in total alienation from the rest of the world in a jungle situation in a hostile country.'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p141]
- Cialdini argues Jones's 'real genius' was his understanding that he could not regularly persuade all members single-handedly, but could persuade some, then let social proof convince the rest. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p142]
- Jones's masterstroke was relocating the community to Guyana, where conditions of uncertainty and exclusive similarity would make social proof operate maximally in his favor. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p142]
