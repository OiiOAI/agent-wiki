# John Maynard Smith

## Summary

John Maynard Smith (1900-1977) was a British evolutionary biologist who introduced Evolutionarily Stable Strategy (ESS) theory to biology, applying game theory to evolutionary questions of animal conflict and cooperation. His collaboration with G.R. Price and G.A. Parker produced foundational work on aggressive behavior models including the hawk-dove game and war of attrition.

## Key facts

- Maynard Smith introduced the ESS concept, tracing its intellectual origins to W.D. Hamilton and R.H. MacArthur. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p99]
- In collaboration with G.R. Price and G.A. Parker, Maynard Smith developed the hawk-dove model of animal aggression using game theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p99]
- Maynard Smith and Parker extended ESS analysis to asymmetric contests, showing that arbitrary asymmetries can give rise to stable conventions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p108]
- Dawkins credits Maynard Smith with bringing game theory into biology and predicts the ESS concept will revolutionize ecology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p115]
- Maynard Smith identified 'paradoxical' ESS strategies, such as attacking larger opponents and fleeing smaller ones, though he considered these of mainly academic interest. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: Maynard Smith's application of game theory to biology marked a paradigm shift, moving from qualitative descriptions of animal behavior to formal mathematical modeling of evolutionary dynamics.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Maynard Smith's paradoxical strategies have been observed in nature beyond the Oecobius spider example cited by Dawkins.
