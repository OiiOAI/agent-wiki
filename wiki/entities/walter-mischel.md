# Walter Mischel

## Summary

Walter Mischel (1930-2018) was a Stanford psychologist who developed the famous 'marshmallow test' in the 1960s to study delayed gratification in children. Children were presented with a marshmallow and told they could eat it immediately or wait 15 minutes for a second marshmallow. Mischel found that marshmallow wait time predicted later academic success, social competence, and health outcomes decades later. His work demonstrated the importance of distraction and reappraisal strategies rather than pure willpower in self-regulation, and showed that willpower is not a fixed trait but can be taught.

## Key facts

- Mischel's marshmallow test showed that about a third of children waited the full 15 minutes, with the rest averaging 11 minutes of waiting. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p192]
- Marshmallow wait time predicted higher SAT scores, more social success, resilience, less aggression and oppositional behavior in high school. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p193]
- Forty years post-test, marshmallow champs showed superior frontal function, more PFC activation during frontal tasks, and lower BMIs. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p193]
- Younger children used 'hot ideation' strategies (thinking about how good the marshmallow tastes) which backfired, while older children used distraction and reappraisal. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p192]

## Inferences

- Inference: The predictive power of the marshmallow test suggests that early childhood self-regulation capacity has lasting effects on life trajectories, potentially through its influence on academic achievement, relationship quality, and health behaviors
