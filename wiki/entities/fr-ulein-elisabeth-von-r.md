# Fräulein Elisabeth von R.

## Summary

A twenty-four-year-old Hungarian woman examined by Freud in 1892 for pains in her legs and difficulties in walking that had developed over two years. Her case illustrates the mechanism of conversion for purposes of defence, where an incompatible idea (erotic love for a young man) was repressed and its affect converted into physical pain in the legs, with the pain serving as a mnemic symbol. Freud used this case to demonstrate the cathartic treatment method through 'layer by layer' excavation of buried psychical material.

## Key facts

- Fräulein Elisabeth von R. suffered from pains in her legs with difficulties in walking, with an ill-defined hyperalgesic area on the anterior surface of the right thigh, though prick of a needle was met with unconcern. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p660]
- When her hyperalgesic skin and muscles were pressed or pinched, her face assumed an expression of pleasure rather than pain—a sign that stimulation had touched upon a hysterogenic zone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p661]
- The first hysterical pains were linked to an evening when she was accompanied home by a young man she secretly loved, followed by her return to her sick father's bedside where she reproached herself for the time spent on enjoyment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p669]
- The mechanism was one of conversion for the purpose of defence: the erotic idea was repressed from association and the affect was used to intensify a physical pain present simultaneously. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p670]
- The specific localization of pain in the right thigh was explained by the fact that her father used to rest his leg on that spot while she renewed his bandage—a hundred repetitions that established the hysterogenic zone. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p671]
- During abreaction, pain would appear when a memory was called up and disappear when the essential and decisive part of the story was told—a pain she used as a compass to guide the analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p671]
- Her right leg became painful when memories concerned nursing her sick father and her relations with her youthful friend; her left leg became painful when memories concerned her dead sister and her two brothers-in-law. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p672]
- The first permanent appearance of her pains followed a warm bath at the health resort, shortly after a long walk and following a period of yearning and wishful thinking about her own loneliness compared to her married sisters' happiness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p673]
- The patient's astasia-abasia was built up through cathexis of new regions in the legs by each fresh pathogenic theme, lasting accumulation of cathexis linking leg functions with feelings of pain, and symbolic expression of her inability to 'take a single step forward.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p135]
- At the moment she looked at her sister lying dead, another thought shot through Elisabeth's mind: 'Now he is free again and I can be his wife.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p139]
- The first conversion occurred while nursing her father, when her duties as nurse came into conflict with her erotic desires; under the pressure of lively self-reproaches she decided in favour of her duties and brought about her hysterical pain. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p146]
- After treatment, Elisabeth married someone unknown to Freud and was regarded as cured. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p143]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's approach to Elisabeth von R. involved 'layer by layer' excavation of psychical material, comparing it to the technique of excavating a buried city.
- Inference: The resistance she offered to reproducing memories corresponded to the energy with which the incompatible idea had been forced out of her associations.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Freud acknowledged a gap in the explanation—whether the conversion of psychical excitation into physical pain occurred at the moment perceived, or whether the pain was not consciously noticed at the time but represented an earlier organic rheumatic condition later utilized by the neurosis.
- Uncertain: Whether the first attack of pains during her father's illness was genuinely hysterical or merely rheumatic could not be definitively established.
