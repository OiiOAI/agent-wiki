# John Snow

## Summary

Dr. John Snow (1813-1858) was a physician who pioneered epidemiology by tracing a cholera outbreak in London to contaminated water from the Broad Street pump. His work, though initially ignored, established waterborne transmission of cholera.

## Key facts

- Snow investigated the 1853-1854 cholera epidemic in London, challenging the prevailing miasma theory. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2265-L2275]
- Snow identified that districts served by Southwark and Vauxhall Company (drawing water downstream from sewers) had death rates eight times higher than those served by Lambeth Company (upstream intake). [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2295-L2305]
- Snow described the water company situation as a natural experiment: 'No experiment could have been devised which would more thoroughly test the effect of water supply.'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2305-L2320]
- Snow concluded that if Southwark and Vauxhall had moved its intake upstream, more than 1,000 lives would have been saved. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L2310-L2315]
- John Snow investigated the cholera outbreak, which was very little appreciated during his lifetime. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3729-L3731]
- John Snow's one-paragraph obituary in Lancet did not even mention his investigation of cholera. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3729-L3731]
- The premier British medical journal 'corrected' its obituary of John Snow 155 years later. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3731-L3732]
- John Snow is associated with the Broad Street pump and modern epidemiology. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3733-L3733]
