# Thomas Kuhn

## Summary

Thomas Kuhn (1922-1996) was a philosopher of science whose 1962 book The Structure of Scientific Revolutions had profound influence on sociology. His paradigm theory challenged cumulative conceptions of scientific development and became a major variety of My metatheorizing in sociology.

## Key facts

- Kuhn's The Structure of Scientific Revolutions was published in 1962 and grew out of philosophy. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p561]
- Robert Friedrichs published the first important Kuhnian work in sociology in 1970: A Sociology of Sociology. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p561]
- Kuhn viewed cumulative scientific development as a myth and sought to debunk it. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p563]
- Kuhn's theory of scientific change involves the cycle: Paradigm → Normal Science → Anomalies → Crisis → Revolution → New Paradigm. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p564]
- Normal science is a period of accumulation of knowledge in which scientists work to expand the reigning paradigm. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p564]
