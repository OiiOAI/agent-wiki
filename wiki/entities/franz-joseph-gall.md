# Franz Joseph Gall

## Summary

Viennese physician and neuroanatomist who in 1800 proposed two radically new ideas: that the brain is the organ of the mind with all mental functions emanating from it, and that the cerebral cortex contains many organs with specific regions controlling specific functions. His work led to phrenology and influenced Broca's neuropsychology.

## Key facts

- In 1800 Gall proposed that the brain is the organ of the mind and that all mental functions emanate from the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- He argued that the cerebral cortex did not function as a single organ but contained many organs with particular regions controlling specific functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- Gall enumerated at least 27 distinct regions or organs of the cerebral cortex, assigning intellectual processes to the front of the brain and instinctive characteristics to the back. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- Gall's experimental approach to localization was naive, as he relied on physiognomy and skull bumps rather than examining brain lesions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
- His studies of localized brain functions led to phrenology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p58]
