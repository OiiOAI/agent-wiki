# Striatum

## Summary

The striatum, comprising the caudate nucleus and putamen, is a brain structure critical for motor-skill learning. It receives signals from cortical and lower brain regions and sends outputs back to the same areas via the thalamus. Research on Parkinson's and Huntington's disease patients revealed that different parts of the striatum support different aspects of motor learning.

## Key facts

- The striatum includes the caudate nucleus and the putamen, two collections of neurons under the cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- The striatum receives messages from specific cortical areas and sends signals back to the same areas via the thalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- Parkinson's disease primarily affects the putamen, while Huntington's disease primarily affects the caudate nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- Parkinson's patients showed impaired mirror tracing but normal sequence learning, indicating the putamen supports some but not all motor skills. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p164]
- Huntington's patients showed impaired sequence learning, providing evidence that the caudate nucleus is critical for sequence-specific learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p164]
- Patients with Parkinson disease (basal ganglia dysfunction) perform poorly on the probabilistic classification task but normally on declarative memory tasks, whereas amnesic patients with medial temporal lobe lesions show the opposite pattern. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1506]
- The neostriatal habit learning system in humans was demonstrated by Knowlton, Mangels, and Squire using probabilistic classification tasks, where subjects predict outcomes based on cue-outcome associations that are probabilistic rather than deterministic. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1506]
- LTP at corticostriatal synapses requires NMDA receptors and CREB, and CREB impairment in the striatum selectively disrupts striatum-dependent habit learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1534]

## Inferences

- Inference: Different motor skills engage separate cognitive and neural processes within the striatum, with the caudate nucleus and putamen supporting distinct aspects of procedural learning.
