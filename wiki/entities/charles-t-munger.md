# Charles T. Munger

## Summary

Charles T. Munger (1924-2023) was an American investor, businessman, and philanthropist who served as vice chairman of Berkshire Hathaway. Known for his multidisciplinary approach to thinking and investing, Munger developed the concept of 'Multiple Mental Models' and delivered numerous speeches on worldly wisdom, decision-making, and psychology that were compiled in Poor Charlie's Almanack.

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger's reference to inverting (following Jacobi) suggests he regularly applies mathematical and problem-solving techniques to business and life challenges.
- Inference: Munger's mention of Lord John Maynard Keynes as a model for independence indicates intellectual admiration for Keynes beyond his economics.
- Inference: Munger's multidisciplinary approach to problem-solving was influenced by his early exposure to physics and his admiration for how physicists approach the unknown.
- Inference: The Mungers' ability to weather the Great Depression through mutual support shaped Munger's later philosophy of partnership and long-term relationships.
- Inference: Munger's approach to raising his eight children included using moral tales and 'downward spiral' stories to teach ethics and consequences.
- Inference: Munger's influence on Buffett's investment philosophy was significant but Munger himself downplays this credit, stating the Buffett record would have been similar without him.
