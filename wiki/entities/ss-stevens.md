# S. S. Stevens

## Summary

S.S. Stevens demonstrated in 1953 that subjective experience of sensation intensity follows a power function rather than Fechner's logarithmic law, establishing Stevens' law (I = K(S–S₀)ⁿ). He also introduced magnitude estimation and formalized four scales of measurement.

## Key facts

- In 1953 Stevens demonstrated that subjective experience of sensation intensity is best described by a power function over extended ranges, formalized as I = K(S–S₀)ⁿ. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
- For pressure on the hand, the relationship between stimulus magnitude and perceived intensity is linear (power function with unity exponent n=1). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
- Stevens introduced the technique of magnitude estimation, where subjects use numerical scales to rate the intensity of sensations experienced by stimuli of different amplitude. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p504]
- Stevens formalized four scales of measurement: nominal (named categories), ordinal (ranked), interval (meaningful distances), and ratio scales (zero has meaning). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p504]
