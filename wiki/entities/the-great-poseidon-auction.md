# The Great Poseidon Auction

## Summary

The Great Poseidon Auction was the 1973 open-bid auction for television rights to the movie The Poseidon Adventure, resulting in Barry Diller's ABC paying $3.3 million—a $1 million loss expected. CBS president Robert Wood admitted to bidding irrationally in a 'fever' that overcame his initial cost-benefit analysis. This case demonstrates how competition for scarce resources can override rational decision-making.

## Key facts

- In 1973, Barry Diller (then ABC vice president for prime-time programming) agreed to pay $3.3 million for a single television showing of The Poseidon Adventure. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p236]
- The $3.3 million figure exceeded the previous highest price for a one-time movie showing of $2 million for Patton, and ABC expected to lose $1 million. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p236]
- This was the first time a motion picture had been offered to networks in an open-bid auction, creating direct competition for the scarce resource. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p237]
- The auction format was the brainchild of producer Irwin Allen and 20th Century Fox vice president William Self. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p237]
- After winning, Diller announced that ABC would 'never again enter into an auction situation,' speaking 'as if it could have escaped only from between clenched teeth.'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p237]
- Robert Wood admitted: 'We were very rational at the start... But then the bidding started. ABC opened with two million. I came back with two point four... And the fever of the thing caught us. Like a guy who had lost his mind, I kept bidding.'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p237]

## Inferences

- Inference: The auction case illustrates that open competition for scarce resources can create a psychological state that overrides normal cost-benefit analysis, resulting in decisions that rational actors would not make.
