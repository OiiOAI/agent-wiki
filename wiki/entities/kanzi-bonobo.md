# Kanzi

## Summary

Kanzi is a bonobo (pygmy chimpanzee) studied by Sue Savage-Rumbaugh who demonstrated unprecedented symbolic abilities, including 90% correct responses to novel syntactically complex spoken English requests and spontaneous symbolic use of lexigrams. Crucially, he acquired these abilities incidentally while his foster mother was being trained, suggesting immaturity was the key factor.

## Key facts

- Kanzi achieved approximately 90% correct responses to novel, syntactically correct but pragmatically anomalous spoken English requests like 'Put the soap on the apple.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p133]
- Kanzi learned lexigram communication incidentally while his foster mother Matata was being explicitly trained, not through explicit instruction. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p133]
- Kanzi demonstrated spontaneous syntactic regularization in his lexigram output, showing consistent word order. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p133]
- Deacon argues Kanzi's success supports the view that immaturity, not species-specific language adaptations, explains early symbolic learning advantages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p134]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Kanzi's abilities represent a genuine subspecies difference (bonobo vs. common chimp) or are attributable solely to age at exposure.
