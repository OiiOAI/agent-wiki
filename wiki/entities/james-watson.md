# James D. Watson

## Summary

James D. Watson is a Nobel Prize-winning geneticist who co-discovered the double-helix structure of DNA. He is cited in this text making controversial statements about genetic enhancement, suggesting that modifying the human genome to improve traits is justifiable and that conditions like low intelligence should be classified as diseases warranting genetic intervention.

## Key facts

- James Watson co-discovered the double-helix structure of DNA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p391]
- At a 1998 conference on human germ-line engineering, Watson stated: 'No one really has the guts to say it, but if we could make better human beings by knowing how to add genes, why shouldn't we?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p391]
- Watson stated on a British documentary: 'If you are really stupid, I would call that a disease'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p391]
- Watson expressed the view that 'the lower 10 percent who really have difficulty, even in elementary school' could potentially benefit from genetic intervention. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p391]
- Watson stated that 'many of the psychological differences between people (and the similarities) have biological roots'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p391]
