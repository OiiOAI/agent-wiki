# Coleoids

## Summary

The coleoids are the group of cephalopods that includes octopuses, cuttlefish, and squid. They diverged from their last common ancestor approximately 270 million years ago during the Permian period, splitting into the octopod group (including octopuses and deep-sea Vampyromorpha) and the decapod (ten-footed) group (including squid and cuttlefish).

## Key facts

- The most recent common ancestor of octopuses, cuttlefish, and squid lived 270 million years ago, not 170 million years ago as previously believed based on fossil evidence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
- The evolutionary split led to an 'octopod' group on one side, including octopuses and the deep-sea Vampyromorpha, and a 'decapod' (ten-footed) group on the other, including squid and cuttlefish. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
- Backdating the split by 100 million years makes it much more likely that cephalopods evolved complex nervous systems at least twice, once in the octopus lineage and once in the cuttlefish/squid lineage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
- The split occurred in the Permian period, before the dinosaurs, when life in the oceans was very different. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
