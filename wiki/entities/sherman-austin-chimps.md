# Sherman and Austin

## Summary

Sherman and Austin were common chimpanzees trained by Sue and Duane Rumbaugh on a lexigram keyboard system. Their learning trajectory—from thousands of trials for initial associations, through explicit error-training, to spontaneous categorical generalization—provided a step-by-step model of the transition from indexical to symbolic reference.

## Key facts

- Sherman and Austin required thousands of trials to learn initial verb-noun lexigram combinations, and some initially repeated only the most recently learned combination. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p90]
- The experimenters had to explicitly train the chimps to avoid incorrect associations (e.g., treating keyboard position as relevant), using a hierarchy of error-not-rewarded trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p91]
- After learning the combinatorial logic, Sherman and Austin could correctly respond to new food lexigrams on their first trial, whereas initial learning required hundreds of trials. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p92]
- Unlike Lana, Sherman and Austin recoded individual lexigram-object associations into symbolic categories ('food', 'tool') that superseded rote associations. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p97]
