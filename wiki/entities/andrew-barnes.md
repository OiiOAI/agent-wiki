# Andrew Barnes

## Summary

Andrew Barnes is the founder of Perpetual Guardian, a New Zealand company that became the first major business to permanently implement a four-day work week after a successful trial. A former financier in the City of London, he experienced firsthand the damaging effects of overwork before implementing radical changes to his own company's working practices.

## Key facts

- He worked in the City of London after financial deregulation in 1987, where working hours were extreme and being seen before 7:30 a.m. or after 7:30 p.m. was expected. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p200]
- In 2018, he implemented a two-month trial of the four-day work week at Perpetual Guardian, paying employees for five days but asking them to work only four. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p202]
- The trial found social media use at work fell by 35 percent, while engagement and teamwork went up by 30 to 40 percent, and stress levels dropped by 15 percent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p204]
- The four-day week was made permanent after the trial demonstrated productivity was maintained. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p204]
