# Neuromuscular Junction

## Summary

The neuromuscular junction is the specialized synapse between motor neurons and skeletal muscle fibers, where acetylcholine release triggers muscle contraction through activation of nicotinic ACh receptors.

## Key facts

- Motor axon terminals at the neuromuscular junction contain synaptic vesicles clustered at active zones, with voltage-gated Ca2+ channels that trigger transmitter release upon action potential arrival. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p255]
- ACh receptors are concentrated at the crests of secondary junctional folds at a surface density exceeding 10,000/μm². [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p257]
- The end-plate potential at the neuromuscular junction is approximately 75 mV, large enough to exceed threshold and trigger an action potential in the muscle fiber. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p255]
- The high safety factor of neuromuscular transmission ensures reliable signal transmission, critical for functions like breathing and escape responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]

## Inferences

- Inference: The combination of a very large EPSP and low threshold at the end-plate results from both the high density of ACh receptors and the high density of voltage-gated Na+ channels at the bottom of junctional folds.
