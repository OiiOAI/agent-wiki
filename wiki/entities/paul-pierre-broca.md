# Paul Pierre Broca

## Summary

Paul Pierre Broca (1824-1880) was a French neurologist who identified specific cortical areas concerned with language, founding neuropsychology as an empirical science. In 1861 he described the case of Leborgne, a patient who could not speak but could understand language, revealing a lesion in the left frontal lobe, now called Broca's area.

## Key facts

- Broca identified Broca's area in the posterior inferior region of the left frontal lobe as critical for speech production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Broca studied eight patients with similar language deficits, all with lesions in the left cerebral hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- In 1864 Broca announced 'Nous parlons avec l'hémisphère gauche!' (We speak with the left hemisphere!). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Broca founded neuropsychology by correlating clinical evidence of aphasia with brain lesions discovered post mortem, distinguishing it from Gall's phrenology.
