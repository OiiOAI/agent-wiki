# Karl Friston

## Summary

Karl Friston is a British neuroscientist who coined the term 'active inference'. His work on the free energy principle and predictive processing has been highly influential in connecting Bayesian brain theories with neural implementation.

## Key facts

- Karl Friston coined the term 'active inference' for minimizing prediction error through action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p112]
- Active inference is a term Friston introduced to describe how actions can fulfill perceptual predictions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p112]
- Karl Friston is an eminent British neuroscientist who developed the 'free energy principle' and its application to neuroscience through active inference. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p189]
- The first thing that strikes you about the free energy principle is that it's a really big idea bringing together concepts from biology, physics, statistics, neuroscience, engineering, machine learning, and elsewhere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p189]
- For Friston, the free energy principle explains all features of living systems, from a single bacterium to brain details to body plans of animals, reaching as far as the broad strokes of evolution. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p189]
- Friston describes the view from the FEP as organisms gathering and modelling sensory information so as to maximize the sensory evidence for their own existence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p193]
- Karl Friston is described as 'the world's most influential living neuroscientist' and bears the distinction of being the world record holder for most cited scientific paper author. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p12]
- Friston is best known for reducing brain functions to a basic physical necessity to minimise something called free energy, which Solms describes as the deepest theoretical foundation for the book's argument. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p12]
- Before collaborating with Solms, Friston had largely equated mental functions with cortical ones; Solms's work extended his statistical-mechanical framework into the brainstem. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p14]
- Solms states that their combined theory may be called 'the free energy theory of consciousness'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p12]
- Karl Friston is the scientific director at the Wellcome Centre for Human Neuroimaging at Queen Square in London. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p138]
- Friston's h-index is 235—the highest of any neuroscientist. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p139]
- Friston's original claim to fame was 'statistical parametric mapping', which enabled the analysis of functional neuroimaging. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p139]
- His work on 'predictive coding' and the Free Energy Principle brought him much more renown. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p139]
- Friston and Solms collaborated on a paper setting out their shared view on incorporating consciousness under the Free Energy Principle. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p140]

## Inferences

- Inference: Friston's free energy principle provides a physical foundation for consciousness that, when combined with Solms's neuropsychological findings, suggests consciousness is engineerable — artificially producible.
