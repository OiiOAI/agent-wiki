# Jeremy Bentham

## Summary

Jeremy Bentham (1748–1832) was an English philosopher and legal reformer, founder of utilitarianism. McGilchrist discusses him as exhibiting features suggesting mild autism and right-hemisphere deficits: social awkwardness, pedantic speech patterns ('antejentacular circumgyrations'), preference for inanimate objects (he named his stick Dapple and his teapot Dick), and limited human feelings. J.S. Mill wrote that Bentham 'never knew prosperity and adversity, passion nor satiety' and had 'no internal experience nor external.' Bentham invented the words 'international,' 'codify,' and 'maximise' and designed the Panopticon prison.

## Key facts

- Bentham 'probably never talked to women at all, except for his cook and housemaid,' and 'courted women with a clumsy jocularity.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p468]
- Mill wrote that Bentham 'had neither internal experience nor external' and 'never knew prosperity and adversity, passion nor satiety.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p469]
- Bentham defined 'the community is a fictitious body,' reflecting left-hemisphere preference for categorical abstraction over lived human connection. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p468]
- Bentham wrote that poetry was inferior to the game of push-pin because 'between poetry and truth there is a natural opposition: false morals and fictitious nature.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p470]
- Bentham designed the Panopticon, a prison enabling total surveillance where prisoners would be under watch without knowing when they were being observed—'the powerful all-surveying, all-capturing eye achieved its apotheosis.'.
