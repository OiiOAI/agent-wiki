# Ruth Steinhagen

## Summary

Ruth Steinhagen was a young woman who shot baseball player Eddie Waitkus in 1949, becoming one of the first media-age stalkers to attack a public figure. Her case established patterns that would later characterize similar attackers.

## Key facts

- Ruth Steinhagen shot Eddie Waitkus on June 14, 1949, at the Edgewater Beach Hotel in Chicago. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p156]
- Steinhagen was obsessed with Eddie Waitkus, learning Lithuanian (his heritage), obsessing over the number 36 (his jersey number), and collecting press clippings about him. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p156]
- Steinhagen was examined by two psychiatrists who concluded nothing was wrong with her except that she should forget about Waitkus. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p156]
- After shooting Waitkus, Steinhagen told police: 'I liked him a great deal and knew I could never have him, and if I couldn't have him neither could anybody else. I've always wanted to be in the limelight. I wanted attention and publicity for once.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p157]
- Experts initially deemed Steinhagen insane and committed her to a mental facility; three years later she was freed after being declared sane. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p157]
