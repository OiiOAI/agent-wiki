# Beaver Dam

## Summary

Beaver dams are cited as examples of extended phenotypes that extend over hundreds of yards. The lake created by a dam serves to protect the beaver's lodge from predators and provides a convenient waterway for travelling and transporting logs. Like caddis houses, beaver dams are phenotypic effects of beaver genes subject to Darwinian selection.

## Key facts

- Beaver lakes are phenotypic effects no less than the beaver's teeth and tail, and have evolved under the influence of Darwinian selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p289]
- Selection favoured beaver genes that made good lakes for transporting trees, just as it favoured genes that made good teeth for felling them. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p289]
- Beaver lakes are extended phenotypic effects of beaver genes that can extend over several hundreds of yards. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p289]
