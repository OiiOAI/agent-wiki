# Anosognosia

## Summary

Anosognosia is a neuropsychological condition in which patients with left-side paralysis from right-hemisphere brain damage are unable to acknowledge their deficit, reporting they are 'fine' despite blatant paralysis. Along with this denial of illness, these patients show reduced emotional responses to their situation and impaired decision-making in personal and social matters. The condition results from damage to somatosensory cortices in the right hemisphere that disrupts the brain's representation of body state.

## Key facts

- Patients with anosognosia from right-hemisphere damage cannot sense their left-side paralysis automatically and internally through the body's sensory system, and they show no appropriate emotional distress about their condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p83]
- Mirror-image left-hemisphere damage causing right-side paralysis is not accompanied by anosognosia—patients are fully aware of their deficit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p82]
- Anosognosic patients also show impaired reasoning and decision-making in personal and social contexts, similar to patients with prefrontal damage, and are indifferent to their health status. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p86]
- The condition results from damage to right-hemisphere somatosensory cortices (insula, areas 3/1/2, S2) and their connections with thalamus, basal ganglia, and prefrontal cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p85]

## Inferences

- Inference: Anosognosia demonstrates that awareness of one's own body state depends on right-hemisphere somatosensory systems, and that this self-representation is prerequisite for appropriate emotional responses and sound decision-making about one's own situation.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The full neuroanatomical correlates of anosognosia are still being worked out; the text describes this as an ongoing research project.
