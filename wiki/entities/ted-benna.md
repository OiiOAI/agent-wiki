# Ted Benna

## Summary

Ted Benna is credited with inventing the 401(k) retirement plan in 1980. As an employee-benefits consultant frustrated with helping primarily business owners and top management rather than average employees, Benna recognized that a new tax code provision (Section 401(k) of the Tax Reform Act of 1978) could allow employees to shelter regular income from taxes. His plan included employer matching contributions and pre-tax income contributions, which particularly benefited middle-class workers. After being rejected by insurance companies and struggling for media attention, Benna's company implemented the plan on itself, discovering that lower-paid employees were highly enthusiastic participants. The 401(k) plan eventually became one of the most significant retirement vehicles in America.

## Key facts

- Benna invented the 401(k) retirement plan in 1980 by recognizing how the recently passed Tax Reform Act of 1978 could benefit all employees, not just those receiving incentive bonuses. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p203]
- The 401(k) provision was originally a compromise between banks and the Treasury Department regarding how much high-salaried officers could shelter. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p204]
- Benna was motivated by frustration that his work primarily helped business owners and top management rather than average employees. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p204]
- Insurance companies rejected Benna's offer to sell the concept for $1,000,000. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p205]
- The key ingredients for the discovery were the Congressional opportunity, Benna's familiarity with retirement plans, and his personal interest in helping lower-paid workers. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p202]

## Inferences

- Inference: The discovery of the 401(k) was largely accidental—Benna had worked on a retirement benefits plan for a different bank a few years before, allowing him to contrast situations when he encountered the new provision
