# Rosa Parks

## Summary

Rosa Parks was an African American civil rights activist whose 1955 refusal to give up her bus seat in Montgomery, Alabama, became a catalyst for the civil rights movement. Contrary to the popular image of her as generally assertive, Parks described herself as 'too timid to say no' in other contexts, suggesting her courageous decision on the bus grew from deep moral conviction rather than general personality traits.

## Key facts

- Rosa Parks's refusal to give up her bus seat was not part of a general assertive tendency; she once said she was 'too timid to say no' when asked to become NAACP secretary. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p124]
- Parks described feeling 'a determination cover my body like a quilt on a winter night' when the bus driver ordered her to move. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p124]
- Parks said at the time of her arrest that it was 'the very last time that I would ever ride in humiliation of this kind'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p124]
- Rosa Parks is cited as a civil rights icon who refused to give up her bus seat, cited from multiple sources including Juan Williams's 'Eyes on the Prize.'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p235]
- She died at age 92 in 2005, referenced from Mark Feeney's Boston Globe obituary. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p235]
- Donnie Williams and Wayne Greenhaw's 'The Thunder of Angels' is cited for accounts of the Montgomery Bus Boycott. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p235]
