# Psychopathy

## Summary

Psychopathy is a personality disorder characterized by lack of empathy, narcissism, impulsivity, selfishness, and instrumental use of others. Research shows psychopathic individuals have deficits in emotional processing, reduced amygdala response during emotional tasks, and reduced gray matter in amygdala and hippocampus. Unlike autism, psychopathy may spare cognitive perspective-taking while eliminating emotional empathy.

## Key facts

- Psychopathy is characterized by lack of empathy, narcissism, impulsivity, selfishness, and instrumental use of others. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]
- Behavioral studies with psychopathic populations have found selective emotional dysfunction including impairment in aversive conditioning, autonomic response to threat, and altered autonomic response to sad and fearful expressions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]
- Neurophysiological evidence shows reduced amygdala response during emotional memory and aversive conditioning tasks, as well as reduced gray matter in amygdala and hippocampus in psychopaths. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]
- Birbaumer et al. (2005) reported hypoactivation in amygdala and AI in psychopathic individuals during a fear-conditioning task. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]
- Conduct disorder in adolescents, which often develops into antisocial personality disorder, is associated with reduced activity in amygdala and AI, with empathy scores correlating negatively with gray-matter volume in bilateral AI. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p250]

## Inferences

- Inference: Psychopaths may lack empathy but retain cognitive perspective-taking abilities, explaining their capacity for manipulation while lacking emotional concern for others.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether psychopaths have deficits in their ability to mentalize (theory of mind) remains unclear from existing behavioral studies.
