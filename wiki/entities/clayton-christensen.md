# Clayton Christensen

## Summary

Clayton Christensen was a Harvard Business School professor and author of 'The Innovator's Dilemma.' He exemplified essentialist boundary-setting by declining to work on weekends, declaring his Saturdays belonged to his wife and children and his Sundays to God. He noted that making one exception could lead to making many exceptions.

## Key facts

- Christensen declined a partner's request to work on Saturday by saying he had committed every Saturday to his wife and children. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p153]
- When the partner rescheduled for Sunday, Christensen declined again, saying he had given Sunday to God. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p154]
- Christensen reflected: 'If I had made an exception then I might have made it many times'. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p154]
- Clayton Christensen gave a talk to students at Stanford Law School in 2013, which McKeown based part of the Limit chapter on. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p239]
- Some minor details in the story were changed. [raw/books/productivity/Essentialism_ The Disciplined Pursuit of L - Mckeown, Greg.pdf#p239]
