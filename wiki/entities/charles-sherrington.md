# Charles Sherrington

## Summary

Sir Charles Scott Sherrington (1857-1952) was a pioneering neuroscientist who championed the reflexological view of the central nervous system, viewing the brain as a complex input/output system driven by momentary environmental demands. He won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1932 for his work on the function of neurons. Sherrington coined the term 'synapse' and described the brain as an 'enchanted loom' generating electrical activity.

## Key facts

- Sherrington viewed the working organization of the central nervous system as fundamentally reflexological—a complex input/output system driven by environmental demands, providing impetus for the study of central reflexes and synaptic transmission. - Sherrington described the brain's electrical activity as the 'enchanted loom,' producing well-defined electrical storms representing the mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p8]
