# Charles Sherrington

## Summary

Sir Charles Scott Sherrington (1857-1952) was a pioneering neuroscientist who championed the reflexological view of the central nervous system, viewing the brain as a complex input/output system driven by momentary environmental demands. He won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1932 for his work on the function of neurons. Sherrington coined the term 'synapse' and described the brain as an 'enchanted loom' generating electrical activity.

## Key facts

- Sherrington viewed the working organization of the central nervous system as fundamentally reflexological—a complex input/output system driven by environmental demands, providing impetus for the study of central reflexes and synaptic transmission. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p12]
- Sherrington described the brain's electrical activity as the 'enchanted loom,' producing well-defined electrical storms representing the mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p8]
- Sherrington noted that 'la' cannot be repeated more than 11 times per second, establishing that the limit for repetitive voluntary movement is approximately 11 Hz. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p35]
- Sherrington proposed that simple reflexes are the basic units for movement and that complex movements can be produced by combining simple reflexes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p791]
- Sherrington proposed the motor unit as the basic functional unit by which the nervous system controls movement in 1925. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p819]
- Sherrington described reciprocal innervation as excitation of one group of muscles accompanied by inhibition of their antagonists. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p843]
