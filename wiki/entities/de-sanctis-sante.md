# Sante de Sanctis

## Summary

Sante de Sanctis (1862–1935) was an Italian scientist at the University of Rome, La Sapienza, who published I Sogni: Psychological and Clinical Studies of an Alienist in 1899. He developed a multipronged approach to studying dreams including questionnaires, systematic awakenings, repeated observations, and statistical analyses. He proposed that dreams result from the interaction of the dreamer's fundamental state, current state, and immediate experiences, anticipating modern integrative models of dream function.

## Key facts

- De Sanctis developed a multipronged approach to dream study including detailed questionnaires, systematic sleep awakenings, repeated observations, and statistical analysis. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p34]
- He studied the dreams of children, the elderly, criminals, patients with epilepsy, the mentally ill, and healthy adults, finding that waking emotions play a vital role in dream construction. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p34]
- De Sanctis showed that dreaming is less common during deep early-night sleep and more vivid in lighter late-night sleep, anticipating REM sleep findings. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p35]
- He proposed that to be properly understood, a dream must be viewed as the sum of the dreamer's past experiences, current state, and immediate sleep experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p35]
- He distinguished between brain structures responsible for starting dreams and those responsible for elaborating dream contents, anticipating Hobson and McCarley's activation-synthesis model. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p35]
