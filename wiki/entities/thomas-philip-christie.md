# Thomas Philip Christie

## Summary

Thomas Philip Christie (born 1935) was a civilian mathematician at Eglin AFB who became John Boyd's most important collaborator. His expertise in applied mathematics, computer skills, and bureaucratic navigation enabled Boyd's energy-maneuverability theory to be fully developed and institutionalized.

## Key facts

- Christie grew up in Pensacola, the first of five children of an abusive alcoholic father, and excelled academically and in baseball before escaping on a scholarship to Spring Hill College. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p141]
- Christie earned a master's degree in applied mathematics from NYU in 1962 after being selected as one of few civilians for Air Force graduate school. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p142]
- Christie's job was to develop Air Force bombing tables to break the service's final connection to Army Air Corps methods. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p142]
- Christie possessed the greatest collection of aircraft-performance data in the Air Force, including thrust data, angle of attack data, and computations about fuel, altitude, and airspeed. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p143]
- Boyd and Christie met in an Officers Club bar in 1962, where Boyd immediately began explaining his excess power theory and Christie understood its potential. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p143-144]
- Christie and Boyd recognized parallels in their childhoods: embarrassing poverty, dysfunctional families, athletic prowess, and an overpowering desire to succeed. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p145]
