# Susumu Tonegawa

## Summary

Susumu Tonegawa won the Nobel Prize in 1987 for determining the genetic basis of antibody diversity. After conquering immunology, he turned to neuroscience to study learning and memory using genetically modified mice. He collaborated with Kandel on studies showing that knocking out a single gene inhibits both LTP and spatial memory.

## Key facts

- Susumu Tonegawa won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1987 for determining the genetic basis of antibody diversity, an extraordinary contribution to immunology. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p301]
- With this accomplishment behind him, Tonegawa wanted to turn to the brain; Kandel encouraged him to study learning and memory rather than consciousness, which was too difficult and poorly defined for a molecular approach at that time. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p302]
- Tonegawa and Kandel each created different lines of genetically modified mice lacking key proteins for LTP and presented back-to-back talks at Cold Spring Harbor in 1992 showing knocking out a single gene inhibits both LTP and spatial memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p301]
