# Wild Salmon

## Summary

Wild salmon is the ninth Genius Food, a rich source of EPA and DHA omega-3 fats and astaxanthin. Consumers of wild fish exhibit superior cognitive aging, better memory function, and even possess bigger brains. The astaxanthin in wild salmon (derived from krill) provides potent antioxidant effects and has been shown to switch on genes that protect against DNA damage, including the FOXO3 longevity pathway.

## Key facts

- Consumers of wild fish exhibit superior cognitive aging and better memory function, and even possess bigger brains. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p339]
- Cognitively normal older people who ate seafood more than once per week had reduced decline of verbal memory and slower rates of decline in perceptual speed over five years compared to those eating less. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p339]
- Wild salmon is low in mercury and a rich source of both EPA and DHA omega-3 fats. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p339]
- Astaxanthin is a powerful carotenoid that has been shown to boost cognitive function, protect the skin from sun damage, protect the eyes, and convert blood lipids to a more cardioprotective profile. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p340]
- Astaxanthin's unique molecular structure allows it to shield cell membranes from oxidative stress and has been shown to switch on the FOXO3 longevity pathway. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p340]

## Inferences

- Inference: Regular consumption of wild salmon may support both cognitive function and longevity through multiple mechanisms.
