# Alden Spencer

## Summary

Alden Spencer (1931–1977) was an American neuroscientist who collaborated with Eric Kandel at the National Institute of Mental Health from 1958 to 1960. He made major contributions to understanding the hippocampus, showing that pyramidal cells fire spontaneously and that action potentials can originate in dendrites. Later joining Kandel at NYU and Columbia, Spencer examined the modification of simple reflex responses by learning and the perception of touch before turning to other research areas.

## Key facts

- Spencer and Kandel obtained the first intracellular signals ever recorded from the hippocampus, finding that certain hippocampal neurons fire spontaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p151]
- They found that action potentials in pyramidal cells of the hippocampus can originate at more than one site within the cell, including the dendrites—contrary to prevailing assumptions. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p152]
- Spencer was committed to mammalian neurophysiology and believed that solutions found in invertebrates might not apply to the vertebrate brain unless verified there. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p154]
- After his stay at NIH, Alden accepted a position as assistant professor at the University of Oregon Medical School but became frustrated because teaching was too time-consuming. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- Kandel tried without success to help Spencer get a position at Harvard before recruiting him to NYU. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- Spencer loved New York City, which provided an outlet for his and Diane's love of music. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- Spencer occupied the laboratory next to Kandel's and they talked daily about neurobiology until his untimely death eleven years later. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- No one else influenced Kandel's thinking on science as much as Spencer did. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- Spencer was diagnosed with ALS in fall 1973 and died in November 1977. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p230]
- The Alden Spencer Lectureship and Award was established in 1978, given annually to a great scientist under fifty. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p231]
