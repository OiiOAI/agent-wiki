# Avalokiteshvara

## Summary

One of the most powerful Bodhisattvas of Mahayana Buddhism, known as 'The Lord Looking Down in Pity' who regards all sentient creatures suffering the evils of existence. He permeates the whole texture of existence with divine grace, appearing in human form with two arms or in superhuman forms with four to a thousand arms, always holding the lotus of the world.

## Key facts

- Avalokiteshvara paused on the threshold of Nirvana and vowed to bring all creatures to enlightenment before entering the void. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p121]
- The prayer 'Om mani padme hum' means 'The jewel is in the lotus' and is addressed to Avalokiteshvara. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p121]
- In China and Japan, Avalokiteshvara appears in female form as Kwan Yin and Kwannon, representing the Madonna of the Far East. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p122]
- The Bodhisattva has androgynous character, conducting the mind beyond objective experience into a symbolic realm where duality is left behind. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p122]

## Inferences

- Inference: The pause on the threshold of Nirvana represents a realization that the distinction between eternity and time is only apparent, made by the rational mind but dissolved in perfect knowledge
