# Lithium in Bipolar Disorder Treatment

## Summary

Lithium was discovered by John Cade in 1949 as an effective treatment for bipolar disorder. It effectively treats acute episodes of mania and stabilizes mood by reducing the frequency of cycling into mania and depression. The most likely molecular target is inhibition of glycogen synthase kinase type 3β (GSK3β), a component of the Wnt signaling pathway. Lithium remains a cornerstone of bipolar disorder treatment despite an incomplete understanding of its mechanism.

## Key facts

- In 1949, John Cade discovered the calming effects of lithium in guinea pigs and soon thereafter in a small clinical trial in bipolar patients. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1567]
- Lithium is effective in treating acute episodes of mania and in stabilizing mood by reducing the frequency of cycling into mania and depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1567]
- The most likely molecular target of lithium is inhibition of glycogen synthase kinase type 3β (GSK3β), a component of the Wnt signaling pathway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1567]
- The mechanisms by which lithium exerts beneficial effects on mania and mood cycling are not fully known due to its many actions at therapeutic concentrations.
