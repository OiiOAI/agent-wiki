# Carl Cotman

## Summary

Director of the Institute for Brain Aging and Dementia at the University of California, Irvine. Cotman conducted pioneering research showing that exercise elevates brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) in the hippocampus, establishing a direct biological connection between physical activity and cognitive function.

## Key facts

- Cotman is director of the Institute for Brain Aging and Dementia at the University of California, Irvine. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p39]
- In 1995, Cotman designed a study showing that voluntary exercise in mice elevated BDNF levels, with increases correlating to distance run. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p39]
- Cotman expected BDNF changes to appear in motor-sensory areas of the brain but found the spike occurred in the hippocampus instead. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p39]
- Cotman had to pay $1,000 apiece for stainless steel running wheels that would pass university protocol for his research. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p40]
- Cotman states that 'exercise improves the rate of learning' and suggests that being in good shape allows people to learn and function more efficiently. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p41]
