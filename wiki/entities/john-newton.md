# John Newton

## Summary

John Newton (1725-1807) was an Anglican clergyman best known for composing the hymn 'Amazing Grace' and as a prominent British abolitionist who mentored William Wilberforce. Notably, he spent decades as a slave ship captain and investor in the slave trade before his conversion to abolitionism, illustrating the slow, imperfect nature of moral progress.

## Key facts

- John Newton captained a slave ship and worked in the slave trade for approximately six years, stopping only due to declining health rather than moral awakening. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p674]
- Newton invested in slave-trading ventures even after becoming an evangelical preacher, and did not publish a pamphlet denouncing slavery until thirty-four years after stopping being a slaver. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p675]
- Newton became the major abolitionist voice in England and lived to see England ban the slave trade in 1807. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p675]
