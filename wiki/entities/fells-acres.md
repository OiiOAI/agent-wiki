# Fells Acres Daycare Case

## Summary

The Fells Acres daycare case involved the 1984 conviction of Gerald Amirault, his sister Cheryl Amirault LeFave, and their mother Violet Amirault for molestation of children at their family-run daycare center in Malden, Massachusetts. The case became controversial due to suggestive interviewing techniques used with children, leading to eventual release of the Amiraults after prolonged legal battles. Twenty-nine memory researchers signed an amicus brief supporting the defense's claims about child suggestibility.

## Key facts

- The Amiraults were convicted in 1984 of molesting children at Fells Acres daycare center, with accusations involving not only sexual abuse but also bizarre claims including bloody torture, murder, and forced eating of dead babies. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p173]
- Fells Acres had operated for eighteen years without any accusations of impropriety prior to the initial charges, and no adult visitors ever noticed anything amiss. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p174]
- Cheryl Amirault LeFave was ultimately freed on time served in late October 1999, while Gerald Amirault remained in prison until 2004 when released on parole. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p180]
- Violet Amirault died of cancer in September 1997 while the case was still being litigated. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p174]
