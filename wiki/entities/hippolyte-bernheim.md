# Hippolyte Bernheim

## Summary

Hippolyte Bernheim (1840–1919) was Professor of Medicine at Nancy and founder of the Nancy school of hypnotism. With Liébeault, he developed the suggestion theory of hypnosis, arguing that all hypnotic phenomena arise from suggestion. His book 'De la suggestion et de ses applications à la thérapeutique' (1886) was translated by Freud into German in 1888–89. Bernheim maintained that hypnosis is merely a product of suggestion and can be achieved in the majority of healthy people.

## Key facts

- Bernheim holds that all hypnotic manifestations are psychical phenomena, effects of suggestions; he asserts that hypnosis is not peculiar to neuropaths but can be achieved with little trouble in the great majority of healthy people. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p105]
- Freud's translation of Bernheim's 'De la suggestion et de ses applications à la thérapeutrique' (1886) was published in German in 1888–89. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p79]
- Freud wrote a substantial preface to this translation (1888) defending Charcot's physiological phenomena while acknowledging Bernheim's psychological contributions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p81]
- Bernheim himself reported that he does not achieve the same results in his private practice as he does with hospital patients. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p135]
- In a letter to Fliess (August 29, 1888), Freud wrote: 'I do not share Bernheim's views, which seem to me one-sided, and in my preface I have tried to defend Charcot's point of view.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p67]
- Bernheim maintained that suggestion was the essential element in hypnotic phenomena, that hypnosis itself was a result of suggestion, and he preferred to practice suggestion in a waking state. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5200]
- Freud was a pupil of Bernheim's, whom he visited at Nancy in 1889, and whose book on suggestion he translated into German. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5200]
- Bernheim's suggestibility was nothing other than the tendency to transference, somewhat too narrowly conceived, not including negative transference. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5198]
- Bernheim never knew that his suggestibility depended on sexuality, on the activity of the libido. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5198]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's critical engagement with Bernheim's work, even while translating it, shows his independent theoretical stance and foreshadows his move beyond simple suggestion theory.
