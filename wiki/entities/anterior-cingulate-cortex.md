# Anterior Cingulate Cortex

## Summary

The anterior cingulate cortex is a forebrain zone devoted to elaborating higher manifestations of social emotional processes, particularly those related to the PANIC system and social-emotional sensitivities. It may also contain brain mechanisms that generate the urge to speak.

## Key facts

- The psychic tension that leads to panic disorders and agoraphobia is markedly diminished following cingulate cortex damage in humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- Changes in the arousability of the cingulate cortex have recently been implicated in the genesis of depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p167]
- The urge to speak appears to be elaborated by the anterior cingulate cortex, a zone that also mediates social motivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p166]
- Damage to the anterior cingulate produces akinetic mutism, where apparent desire but not competence to talk has disappeared. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p175]
- The anterior cingulate cortex, along with supplementary motor area (M2) and third motor area (M3), is proposed as the 'fountainhead region' for the energy of both movement and thought. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p71]
- Patients with damage to this region (e.g., Mrs. T) become motionless and speechless with neutral facial expression, and later report they 'had nothing to say'—not because of imprisonment but because there was no real thinking or reasoning occurring. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p93]
- This region is involved in movement, emotion, and attention, and damage causes a 'virtual suspension of the animation of action and of thought process such that reason is no longer viable.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain.pdf#p71]

## Inferences

- Inference: The anterior cingulate cortex represents a higher sphere of influence for social emotional processes, converging on emotional and SELF representation zones of the midbrain.
- Inference: The anterior cingulate may serve as a critical integration hub that provides the attentional and motivational 'energy' necessary for both cognitive processing and motor output, explaining why its damage produces the appearance of willed paralysis.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise mechanisms by which the anterior cingulate contributes to volition and thought animation remain to be fully elucidated.
