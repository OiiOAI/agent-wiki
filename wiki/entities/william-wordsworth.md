# William Wordsworth

## Summary

William Wordsworth (1770–1850) is presented as the central exemplar of Romantic poetry's engagement with the right hemisphere's world. His concept of 'spots of time' describes visionary experiences that are unwilled, reciprocal, and paradoxically require the conscious mind to be occupied elsewhere. McGilchrist argues these experiences depend on the right hemisphere, citing their association with religious awe, large visual masses, childhood memory, and the sense of ultimate meaning. Wordsworth's poetry aims to redeem the everyday through metaphor, allowing the right hemisphere to circumvent the familiarizing tendencies of ordinary language.

## Key facts

- Wordsworth wrote that 'I know of no book or system of moral philosophy written with sufficient power to melt into our affections, to incorporate itself with the blood & vital juices of our minds.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p492]
- Wordsworth's 'spots of time' are 'spots of time … Which with distinct pre-eminence retain / A vivifying Virtue,' by which 'our minds / Are nourished and invisibly repair'd.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p516]
- Wordsworth noted that visionary power 'comes unsought' and 'I approach, and then they close'—the mind must be intent on something other than the scene that enters into the heart. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p517]
- Wordsworth described how the 'hiding-places of my power / Seem open; I approach, and then they close'—vision requires both effort and relaxation, effort opening 'the windows of the soul' but explicit intention obscuring sight. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p517]

## Inferences

- Inference: McGilchrist interprets Wordsworth's description of 'spots of time' as describing the relationship between hemispheres: narrowly focused left-hemisphere attention frees the right hemisphere to see the scene 'afresh.'
