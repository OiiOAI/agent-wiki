# Fannie Mae

## Summary

Fannie Mae (Federal National Mortgage Association) was a giant government-sponsored lending firm that Taleb identified as 'sitting on a barrel of dynamite' in 2003. Using fragility detection analysis of their risk reports (obtained by journalist Alex Berenson), Taleb saw that an upward move in economic variables led to massive losses while downward moves led to small profits — monstrous concavity indicating inevitable blowup.

## Key facts

- In 2003, Taleb examined Fannie Mae's risk reports and immediately saw their exposures were severely concave — losses accelerating with deviation. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p367]
- A move upward in an economic variable led to massive losses; a move downward led to small profits, with acceleration of harm. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p367]
- Taleb publicly declared Fannie Mae fragile and later the entire banking system was in the same situation — leading to total collapse as a certainty. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p368]
- Fannie Mae eventually collapsed, leaving US taxpayers with hundreds of billions of dollars in losses. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p366]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Fannie Mae case was the practical application that crystallized Taleb's understanding of fragility — the acceleration of harm visible in the risk reports mapped directly to concave exposure functions.
