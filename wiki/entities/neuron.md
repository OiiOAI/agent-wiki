# Neuron

## Summary

Neurons are the signaling units of the nervous system. A typical neuron has four morphologically defined regions: cell body (soma), dendrites, axon, and presynaptic terminals. The human brain contains approximately 86 billion neurons. Neurons are classified structurally as unipolar, bipolar, or multipolar, and functionally as sensory neurons, motor neurons, or interneurons. Despite morphological differences, molecular mechanisms of electrical signaling are surprisingly similar across neuron types.

## Key facts

- The human brain contains approximately 86 billion neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p103]
- Neurons are classified into three structural types: unipolar, bipolar, and multipolar. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p106]
- Neurons are functionally classified as sensory neurons, motor neurons, or interneurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p106]
- Neurons have a high degree of morphological and functional asymmetry: receptive dendrites at one end and a transmitting axon at the other, which is the structural basis for unidirectional neuronal signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p122]
- Neurons are both electrically and chemically excitable, containing specialized ion channels and receptors that facilitate the flow of specific inorganic ions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p122]
- There are about 100 distinct types of neurons, each expressing a restricted set of genes producing specific molecules including enzymes, structural proteins, membrane constituents, and secretory products. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p123]
- The cytoplasm of neurons contains cytosol and membranous organelles including mitochondria, peroxisomes, the rough and smooth endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi complex, secretory vesicles, endosomes, and lysosomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p124]
- Ribosomes, rough endoplasmic reticulum, and the Golgi complex are generally excluded from axons; axons are rich in synaptic vesicles and their precursor membranes instead. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p125]
