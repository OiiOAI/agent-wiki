# J.F.C. Fuller

## Summary

J.F.C. Fuller was a British military theorist who influenced Boyd's thinking through his concepts of 'brain warfare' and the three spheres of war (physical, mental, moral). Fuller argued that paralysis should be the aim in war and that the mental and moral dimensions should be the prime target of military operations.

## Key facts

- Fuller argued that action without will loses coordination and without a directing brain an army is reduced to a mob – the fighting power of an army lies in its organization which can be destroyed either by wearing it down or by rendering inoperative his power of command ('brain warfare'). [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p61]
- Fuller insisted that 'brain warfare' was the most effective and efficient way to destroy the enemy's military organization and hence its military strength, producing instantaneous effects of a 'shot through the head' rather than slow attrition. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p61]
- Fuller adopted the concept of three spheres of war: physical (destruction of physical strength/fighting power), mental (disorganization of mental processes/thinking power), and moral (disintegration of will to resist/staying power). [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p61]
- Fuller recognized that technological, tactical and doctrinal developments lead to counter-developments – war and its tools developed in an evolutionary scheme he called 'the constant tactical factor' which lay at the root of the law of military evolution. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p61]
- Boyd incorporated Fuller's ideas in Patterns of Conflict which contains sections in which specific styles of warfare are contrasted with their logical counters, including Marxist, Leninist and Maoist versions of revolutionary warfare. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p61]
