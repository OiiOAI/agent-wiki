# James H. Schwartz

## Summary

James H. Schwartz (born 1932) is a biochemist who was a housemate and friend of Kandel at Harvard summer school in 1951. He obtained an M.D. at NYU and a Ph.D. in biochemistry at Rockefeller University. He pioneered the biochemistry of Aplysia and made major contributions to the molecular underpinnings of learning and memory.

## Key facts

- Schwartz studied enzyme mechanisms and the chemistry of bacteria at Rockefeller University. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p195]
- Schwartz joined Kandel at NYU in spring 1966, establishing a reputation as an outstanding young scientist. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p195]
- Schwartz began studying specific chemical transmitters used for signaling by different Aplysia nerve cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p195]
- Schwartz collaborated with Kandel on the biochemical analysis of memory storage, testing whether long-term memory requires protein synthesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p237]
- Together, Kandel, Schwartz, and Paul Greengard discovered that cyclic AMP second-messenger signaling is turned on by serotonin during sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p241]
- James H. Schwartz died on March 13, 2006. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p8]
- Schwartz worked with Werner Maas in the microbiology department at NYU and carried out important studies on feedback inhibition in bacterial metabolism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p8]
- Schwartz turned to studying the nerve cells of the snail Aplysia because they were so large and uniquely identifiable that they seemed likely candidates for a study of neuronal biochemical identity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p8]
- Schwartz was recruited back to NYU in 1965 as an Assistant Professor in the Department of Microbiology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p8]
- The idea of going from molecules to behavior was the organizing theme of the first edition of Principles of Neural Science, which Schwartz co-edited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p8]
