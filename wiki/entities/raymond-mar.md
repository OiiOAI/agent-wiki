# Raymond Mar

## Summary

A professor of psychology at the University of Toronto who has conducted pioneering research on how reading fiction affects human consciousness and empathy. His studies found that people who read more novels are better at reading other people's emotions.

## Key facts

- Raymond Mar's research found that the more novels you read, the better you were at reading other people's emotions — a huge effect that did not appear with nonfiction reading. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p95]
- Mar describes reading as creating a 'unique form of consciousness' where attention fluctuates both outward toward the words and inward toward what those words represent. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p95]
- His research found that children who are read more storybooks by their parents show better ability to read other people's emotions. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p96]
- Mar notes that long TV series can be just as effective as novels in boosting empathy, but shorter shows do not have this effect. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p97]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mar's work suggests that sustained engagement with complex narratives about other people's inner lives expands empathy over time.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Mar acknowledges controversy in his field — empathetic people may be more drawn to reading fiction, though he believes both directions of influence are true.
