# Wealthfront

## Summary

Wealthfront was originally called kaChing and began as an online fantasy league for amateur investors. After discovering that only seven amateur managers qualified to manage real assets and conversion rates were near zero, the company pivoted to become a financial services platform connecting investors with professional managers. Today it manages over $180 million on the platform.

## Key facts

- Wealthfront originally launched as kaChing, a fantasy league allowing anyone to build a virtual portfolio based on real market data to identify talented amateur traders. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p157]
- kaChing attracted more than 450,000 gamers in its initial launch, a vanity metric that could have led to false conclusions. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p158]
- When Wealthfront was ready to launch its paid financial product, only seven amateur managers had qualified as worthy of managing other people's money. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p158]
- The conversion rate from gamers to paying customers was close to zero, far below the ideal model prediction. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p158]
- Wealthfront pivoted by abandoning the gaming customers and focusing on providing a service allowing customers to invest with professional managers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p160]
- Today Wealthfront has over $180 million invested on the platform with over forty professional managers and was named one of Fast Company's ten most innovative companies in finance. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p161]
