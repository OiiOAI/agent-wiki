# The Acolytes

## Summary

The Acolytes were a dedicated group of military officers who followed John Boyd and worked to keep his memory alive and move his ideas into the mainstream of American thought. They met every Wednesday evening for almost thirty years in the Old Guard Room at the Officers Club at Fort Myer, Virginia.

## Key facts

- The core Acolytes were Tom Christie, Pierre Sprey, Chuck Spinney, Ray Leopold, and James Burton. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p6]
- Franklin "Chuck" Spinney served as the "go to" person for author Robert Coram during more than two years of research for this biography. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p6]
- The Acolytes believe Boyd's final work made him the most influential military thinker since Sun Tzu wrote The Art of War 2,400 years ago. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p13]
- They met every Wednesday night for almost thirty years in the Officers Club at Fort Myer. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p13]
- The group included Chet Richards, a consultant and lecturer on Boyd's ideas as applied to business. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p7]
- The term 'Acolytes' described the followers who gathered around Boyd and devoted themselves to his goals. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p232]
- Pierre Sprey demonstrated what sort of men these Acolytes were when he took on the A-X project. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p232]
- Raymond Leopold became Boyd's protégé and was described as 'number one' in the office despite being the junior member. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p285]
- Chuck Spinney became the fourth Acolyte and would become the most well-known. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p290]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Acolytes' careers were forever changed, some say ruined, by their association with Boyd and the battles they fought together.
