# Almonds

## Summary

Almonds are the tenth Genius Food, offering three key brain benefits: prebiotic effects from their skins that nurture beneficial gut bacteria, rich polyphenol content providing antioxidant effects, and high vitamin E content that protects synaptic membranes from oxidation. A 2013 trial found that high doses of vitamin E led to significantly slower decline in patients with Alzheimer's disease.

## Key facts

- Almond skins have been shown to provide a prebiotic effect, increasing populations of beneficial bacterial species while reducing pathogenic ones. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p378]
- Almonds are a rich source of polyphenols—plant defense compounds that provide antioxidant effects to both humans and gut bacteria. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p378]
- Almonds are a powerful source of fat-soluble antioxidant vitamin E, which protects synaptic membranes from oxidation and supports neuroplasticity. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p378]
- Scientists have noted a link between decreasing serum levels of vitamin E and poorer memory performance in older individuals. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p379]
- A 2013 trial published in JAMA found that high doses of vitamin E led to significantly slower decline in patients with Alzheimer's disease. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p379]

## Inferences

- Inference: Due to their vitamin E content, almonds may support synaptic health and potentially slow cognitive decline.
