# Paul Ekman

## Summary

Paul Ekman is a psychologist who identified six basic emotions (anger, fear, disgust, surprise, happiness, sadness) with universal facial expressions across cultures. His Facial Action Coding System (FACS) became a standard tool for assessing emotional processing. His work supported the basic emotions theory that certain emotions are genetically programmed and expressed identically regardless of cultural background. He consulted for the CIA on detecting lying and was the model for the TV show 'Lie to Me.'

## Key facts

- Ekman identified six basic emotions with characteristic universal facial expressions: anger, fear, disgust, surprise, happiness, and sadness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p181]
- His Facial Action Coding System (FACS) became a standard tool for assessing brain processing of emotions in research and clinical settings. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p182]
- Ekman consulted for the CIA to train agents in recognizing true emotions and detecting deception. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p181]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: LeDoux notes that basic emotions theory has been challenged on grounds that facial expressions are not as singular and automatic as assumed.
