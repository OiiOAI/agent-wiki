# Phineas Gage

## Summary

Phineas P. Gage (1823–1861) was a railroad construction foreman who survived a remarkable brain injury in 1848 when an iron tamping rod was driven through his skull. His subsequent profound personality change—from an efficient, responsible worker to an erratic, socially inappropriate man—provided the first evidence that specific brain damage could impair social reasoning while leaving intellect intact.

## Key facts

- On September 13, 1848, Gage sustained a brain injury when an iron bar (13.25 pounds, 3 feet 7 inches long) was driven through his left cheek, through the base of his skull, and out the top of his head. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p24]
- Before the accident, Gage was described as efficient, capable, temperate, with considerable energy of character and a well-balanced mind. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p28]
- After the injury, Gage exhibited marked personality changes: fitful, irreverent, indulging in gross profanity, impatient of restraint, capricious and vacillating, with the animal passions of a strong man but intellectual capacity of a child. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p28]
- Gage's employers refused to give him his job back, stating the change in his mind was so marked. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p28]
- Gage worked briefly at horse farms, then was featured as a circus attraction at Barnum's Museum in New York, displaying his wounds and the tamping iron. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p29]
- Gage died on May 21, 1861 at age 38, likely from status epilepticus, in San Francisco. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p30]
- Modern reconstruction using Brainvox technique confirmed damage was most extensive in the ventromedial prefrontal cortices bilaterally, sparing motor and language areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p52]
- Gage could not make good decisions, and the choices he made were actively disadvantageous rather than simply neutral or diminished. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p31]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between intact intellect and impaired social behavior in Gage suggested that brain systems dedicated specifically to social reasoning exist separately from general cognitive machinery
- Inference: Gage's case anticipated that normal social conduct requires particular brain regions, a concept that was radical for its time
