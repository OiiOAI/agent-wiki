# Aromatase

## Summary

Aromatase is the enzyme that converts testosterone to estradiol (17-β-estradiol). It is expressed at high levels in the ovaries but not in testes, explaining sexual dimorphism in circulating testosterone and estrogen levels. Aromatase is also expressed in various brain regions, and many effects of testosterone on neurons are mediated after its conversion to estrogen.

## Key facts

- Aromatase catalyzes the conversion of testosterone to estradiol, and its differential expression in ovaries versus testes produces sex differences in circulating hormone levels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1263]
- Aromatase is expressed in discrete neuronal populations in the preoptic hypothalamus, bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST), and medial amygdala. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1264]
- Many brain masculinizing effects of testosterone occur after local conversion to estrogen by aromatase in neural tissue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1263]
