# Bell Palsy

## Summary

Bell palsy is an isolated injury of the facial (VII) nerve, commonly caused by viral infections. It results in weakness of facial muscles on the affected side, with the face pulling toward the unaffected side due to unopposed contralateral muscles.

## Key facts

- Bell palsy causes the face to pull toward the unaffected side because of weakness of muscles on the side of the lesion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1031]
- Later symptoms include drooping of the ipsilateral corner of the mouth, food falling out of the mouth, and inability to close eyelids on the affected side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1031]
- Loss of blinking may result in drying and injury to the cornea. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1031]
- Sound may have a booming quality in the ipsilateral ear because the stapedius muscle fails to tense the ossicles in response to loud sounds (impaired stapedial reflex/hyperacusis). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1031]
- Taste may be lost on the anterior two-thirds of the tongue on the ipsilateral side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1032]
- If caused by herpes zoster infection of the geniculate ganglion, small blisters may form in the outer ear canal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1032]
