# Leonardo da Vinci

## Summary

Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519) was an Italian Renaissance polymath whose fields of interest included painting, sculpture, architecture, science, music, mathematics, engineering, literature, anatomy, geology, astronomy, botany, and cartography. In Freud's psychoanalytic study 'Leonardo da Vinci and a Memory of his Childhood' (1910), Leonardo serves as a case study for Freud's theories on childhood sexuality, sublimation, and the psychical genesis of homosexuality.

## Key facts

- Leonardo da Vinci (1452–1519) was admired even by his contemporaries as one of the greatest men of the Italian Renaissance. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3504]
- Leonardo was a universal genius 'whose outlines can only be surmised – never defined'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3504]
- Leonardo was born in 1452 in the little town of Vinci between Florence and Empoli; he was an illegitimate child, his father was Ser Piero da Vinci, a notary, and his mother was Caterina, probably a peasant girl. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3519]
- Leonardo spent the first years of his life (between three and five) with his mother Caterina before being received into his father's household. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3528]
- Leonardo's sexual activity was exceptionally reduced, and he never married or had known sexual relations with women. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3511]
- A charge of forbidden homosexual practices was brought against Leonardo while he was an apprentice in Verrocchio's studio, though he was acquitted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3511]
- Leonardo surrounded himself with handsome boys and youths whom he took as pupils, the last being Francesco Melzi who accompanied him to France and was named as his heir. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3512]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud infers that Leonardo's childhood spent with his mother Caterina, combined with his father's absence during those early years, was of decisive influence in the formation of his inner life.
- Inference: Freud infers that Leonardo sublimated his sexual drives into the drive to research, which explains both his scientific achievements and his relative lack of sexual activity.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Leonardo had actual sexual relationships with his male pupils remains uncertain; Freud notes that 'Without sharing in the certainty of his modern biographers, who naturally reject the possibility that there was a sexual relationship between him and his pupils as a baseless insult to the great man, we may take it as much more probable that Leonardo's affectionate relations with the young men... did not extend to sexual activity.' [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3512]
