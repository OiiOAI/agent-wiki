# Military Reform Movement

## Summary

The Military Reform Movement was an amorphous group of serving and retired military officers, journalists, academics, industry experts, and politicians who shared an intent to improve how the US Department of Defense did business after the Vietnam War. Boyd was a key figure whose Patterns of Conflict briefing became the means by which the movement's philosophy was spread.

## Key facts

- The Military Reform Movement consisted of serving and retired military officers, journalists, academics, industry experts, and politicians including Newt Gingrich, Gary Hart, Sam Nunn, and Dick Cheney. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p75]
- Boyd became one of the key persons of the reform movement, which rallied against spiraling complexity and costs of military equipment driven by industrial interests and faith in technology. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p75]
- Boyd remained active as a non-paid consultant to the Office of the Secretary of Defense after retiring on September 1, 1975. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p75]
- The expanding network of the reform movement exposed increasing numbers of people to Boyd's ideas, which he continued to expand in Patterns of Conflict through briefings to growing audiences. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p75]
- Boyd's hundreds of Patterns of Conflict briefings around the Pentagon and throughout the US military prepared the ground for a different approach to warfare. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p87]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Military Reform Movement provided the institutional context in which Boyd's ideas could mature and spread, with the movement's concerns about attritional warfare directly influencing his emphasis on maneuver warfare.
