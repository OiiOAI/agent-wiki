# Caddis House

## Summary

Caddis larvae build tubular houses from materials picked up from river beds, including sticks, dead leaves, snail shells, and carefully selected stones. These houses represent a key example of the extended phenotype, as the stones chosen are phenotypic effects of caddis genes, even though the stones themselves are not living material.

## Key facts

- Caddis larvae build tubular houses for themselves using cement of their own manufacture, from materials picked up from the bed of the stream. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p279]
- Caddis larvae choose stones carefully, rejecting those too large or too small, rotating each stone until it achieves the snuggest fit. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p279]
- Stone hardness is an extended phenotypic effect of the caddis's genes: it is legitimate to speak of a gene as affecting the hardness of the stones in a caddis house. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p281]
