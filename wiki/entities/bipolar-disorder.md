# Bipolar Disorder

## Summary

Bipolar disorder is characterized by swings of mood between mania and depression. It requires at least one episode of mania for diagnosis and has a prevalence of approximately 1% worldwide with equivalent incidence in males and females. Manic episodes feature elevated mood, increased energy, decreased need for sleep, and impaired judgment, often accompanied by psychotic symptoms.

- The incidence of bipolar disorder is equivalent in males and females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1549]
- Bipolar disorder generally begins in young adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1551]
- The depressive episodes in bipolar disorder are often more difficult to treat than those in unipolar depression. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1551]
- Bipolar disorder can be treated with lithium and several anticonvulsant drugs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1566]
