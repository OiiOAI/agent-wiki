# Bernie Madoff

## Summary

Bernard 'Bernie' Madoff was an American financier who orchestrated the largest Ponzi scheme in history, defrauding investors of an estimated $64.8 billion. His case illustrates how professionals defaulted to truth, ignoring numerous red flags and suspicious characteristics of his operation.

## Key facts

- Madoff claimed his investment strategy was based on 'gut feel' for market timing, which SEC investigator Peter Lamore found suspicious but could not prove fraudulent. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p56]
- Renaissance Technologies conducted an internal investigation and concluded 'none of it seems to add up,' yet reduced rather than eliminated their stake in Madoff's fund. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p55]
- SEC official Robert Sollazzo did not find Madoff's 'gut feel' explanation 'necessarily ridiculous,' exemplifying institutional default to truth. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p56]
- The hedge fund Renaissance Technologies was founded by mathematicians and code-breakers but still failed to fully act on suspicions about Madoff. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p58]
