# Moses

## Summary

In Freud's psychoanalytic interpretation of Michelangelo's statue, Moses represents a figure who has overcome violent impulse in service of a higher mission. Freud portrays Moses not as about to act in rage, but as having already controlled his anger to preserve the Tables of the Law.

## Key facts

- In Freud's interpretation, Moses is depicted after the moment when he would have leapt up to punish the worshippers of the Golden Calf. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4334]
- Freud argues Moses remained seated in frozen wrath and in pain mingled with contempt, controlling his passion to preserve the Tables. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4334]
- Freud suggests Michelangelo created a Moses superior to the biblical and traditional Moses, who actually broke the Tables in his anger. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4337]
