# Charles Morgan

## Summary

Charles Morgan was a psychiatrist who researched stress responses at SERE schools and later worked for the CIA, arguing that interrogation techniques like sleep deprivation compromise the reliability of information obtained.

## Key facts

- Morgan conducted research at Army SERE schools showing that even simulated interrogations caused stress hormone changes comparable to actual combat or major surgery. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p143-144]
- After stressful questioning in SERE training, 80% of subjects drew complex figures 'piecemeal, like a prepubescent kid'—indicating their prefrontal cortex had shut down temporarily. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p146]
- In a study, 20 of 52 SERE students falsely identified an innocent physician as the camp commandant in a lineup, even though he had been on vacation in Hawaii during the training. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p147]
- Morgan told the CIA that sleep deprivation is 'like trying to get a better signal out of a radio that you are smashing with a sledgehammer'—it compromises rather than improves information quality. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p149]

## Inferences

- Inference: Morgan's research suggests that coercive interrogation methods designed to make prisoners talk may actually make the information they provide less reliable, creating a fundamental paradox for interrogation programs.
