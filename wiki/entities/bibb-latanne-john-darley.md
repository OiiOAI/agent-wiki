# Bibb Latane and John Darley

## Summary

Bibb Latane and John Darley were New York-based psychology professors who investigated the Genovese murder case and developed the influential bystander effect theory. Their research demonstrated that the presence of other bystanders decreases individual responsibility to help, and that pluralistic ignorance causes groups to interpret ambiguous emergencies as non-emergencies.

## Key facts

- Latane and Darley suggested that no one helped Catherine Genovese precisely because there were so many observers, due to diffusion of responsibility and pluralistic ignorance. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p124]
- Their staged emergency experiments found that a college student having an epileptic seizure received help 85% of the time with a single bystander but only 31% of the time with five bystanders present. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p125]
- When witnesses were convinced an emergency existed, aid was very likely—Florida accident scenes received help 100% of the time when the need was clear, regardless of group size. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p125]
