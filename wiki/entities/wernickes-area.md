# Wernicke's Area

## Summary

Wernicke's area is a modest-size region of the neocortex traditionally associated with language comprehension. Like Broca's area, it is located only on the left side of the brain and has faster-conducting neurons due to extra myelin insulation.

## Key facts

- Wernicke's area is thought to be responsible for language comprehension and is located only on the left side of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p87]
- There is disagreement over the exact location and extent of Wernicke's area, and its functions overlap somewhat with Broca's area rather than being neatly differentiated. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p87]
- The number and density of synapses is greater in the language regions compared to their equivalents on the right side of the brain, though this could mean these areas have learned more things. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p87]

## Inferences

- Inference: The greater synaptic density in language areas might reflect the complexity of language learning rather than fundamental functional differences from other neocortical regions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact boundaries and functional specialization of Wernicke's area continue to be researched.
