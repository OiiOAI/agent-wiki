# Clive Wearing

## Summary

Clive Wearing is a British musicologist who suffered devastating brain damage from herpes encephalitis in 1985, destroying his hippocampus bilaterally. He has one of the most profound amnesias ever documented, with no ability to form new memories or recall old ones, existing in a permanent present of 7-30 seconds.

## Key facts

- Clive Wearing is a British musicologist who worked as a choirmaster and edited works of Orlande de Lassus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p158]
- In March 1985 he suffered herpes encephalitis that wrought massive damage to his hippocampus in both cerebral hemispheres. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p158]
- He exists in a permanent present of between seven and thirty seconds. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p158]
- His diaries show repeated 'first awakenings' — entries like 'Now I am really, completely, awake' crossed out and rewritten minutes later. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p159]
- Despite destroying his narrative self, other aspects of self remain intact: body ownership, first-person perspective, voluntary action, and when playing music he is 'wholly alive'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p159]

## Inferences

- Inference: Clive Wearing's case demonstrates that the narrative self (dependent on autobiographical memory) is dissociable from embodied self aspects like body ownership and voluntary action.
