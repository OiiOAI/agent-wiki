# Fast ForWord

## Summary

Fast ForWord is a plasticity-based computer training program developed by Scientific Learning Corporation (founded 1996) for language-impaired and learning-disabled children. The program exercises basic brain functions involved in language from decoding sounds to comprehension. Children typically work on it for 1 hour 40 minutes daily, five days a week, for several weeks. Remarkably, children who had a lifetime of cognitive difficulties improved after only 30 to 60 hours of treatment.

## Key facts

- Fast ForWord was developed by Scientific Learning Corporation, founded by Merzenich, Paula Tallal, Bill Jenkins, and Steve Miller in 1996. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p80]
- The program includes seven brain exercises teaching children to distinguish short sounds, identify consonant-vowel combinations like 'ba' and 'da,' hear frequency glides, and remember and match sounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p81]
- Fast ForWord slows down the 'fast parts of speech' so language-disabled children can hear them clearly, then gradually speeds them up. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p81]
- The 1996 study published in Science showed children with language impairments made significant progress, achieving normal or better-than-normal language scores and keeping gains six weeks after training. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p82]
- The average child advanced 1.8 years of language development in six weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p82]
- Stanford brain scans showed dyslexic children using different brain areas for reading before training, then normalizing after Fast ForWord with increased activity in the left temporo-parietal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p82]
- The program also helped some autistic children improve language and autistic symptoms including attention spans, sense of humor, eye contact, and social connection. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p85]

## Inferences

- Inference: Fast ForWord's success with autism suggests language and autistic symptoms may be different expressions of a common underlying problem.
