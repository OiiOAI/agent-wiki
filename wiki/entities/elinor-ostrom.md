# Elinor Ostrom

## Summary

Elinor Ostrom was an American political economist who won the Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences in 2009 for her analysis of economic governance, especially the commons. She challenged the conventional wisdom that common resources must be regulated by either private owners or central authorities. Her research demonstrated that local communities can develop effective self-governance mechanisms when allowed to communicate and establish their own rules, including penalties for free riders.

## Key facts

- Ostrom won the Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences in 2009 for her analysis of economic governance, especially common-pool resources. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p164]
- Ostrom showed in laboratory studies that groups allowed to communicate and develop their own methods of fining free riders can manage communal resources almost perfectly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p164]
- She challenged the conventional assumption that common resources must be either privatized or nationalized, demonstrating viable third options through empirical research. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p164]
