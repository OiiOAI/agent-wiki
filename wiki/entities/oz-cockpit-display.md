# Oz Cockpit Display

## Summary

The Oz project presents a graphical cockpit display that directly portrays complex flight envelope relationships, replacing the need for pilots to mentally calculate aircraft limitations. The display shows a bent-wing visual where pilots keep a horizontal bar (strut) between wing edges representing speed limits. Research shows pilots learn to fly more quickly with the Oz display than traditional cockpit displays, retain desired flight control better under turbulence, and have more mental resources available for other tasks.

## Key facts

- The Oz display presents a graphical image showing thrust available and in use through a strut between bent wings representing aircraft speed limits. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p100]
- The drag curve in the Oz display is automatically adjusted for aircraft configuration (weight, landing gear, flaps) and density altitudes, eliminating the need for pilot mental calculations. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p100]
- Research shows pilots can learn to fly much more quickly with the Oz display than traditional cockpit displays and retain desired flight control under heavy turbulence. [raw/books/strategy/Designing for Situation Awareness _ An Approach to - Jones, Debra G_; Endsley, Mica R.pdf#p100]
