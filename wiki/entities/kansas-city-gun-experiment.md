# Kansas City Gun Experiment

## Summary

The Kansas City Gun Experiment was a 1992-1993 policing study that demonstrated the effectiveness of focused patrol in crime hot spots. Four officers conducted aggressive proactive policing in a tiny 0.64 square mile area, reducing gun crimes by half.

## Key facts

- The experiment focused on District 144 in Kansas City, which had a homicide rate twenty times the national average and averaged one violent felony per day. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p173]
- Officers worked nights (7pm-1am) for 200 consecutive days, conducting 1,090 traffic citations, 948 vehicle stops, 616 arrests, 532 pedestrian checks, and seizing 29 guns. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p175]
- Gun crimes in the target area fell by 50%, while comparison areas saw no change—demonstrating that focused, aggressive patrol in hot spots was effective. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p174]
- The experiment inspired widespread adoption of proactive policing nationwide, including 'Operation Pipeline' which taught local police to conduct traffic stops to catch drug couriers. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p175-176]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gladwell argues that the Kansas City experiment's lessons were often lost in translation—when exported nationwide, the focused geographic approach was replaced by broad aggressive policing that created encounters like the Sandra Bland stop.
