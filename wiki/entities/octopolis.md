# Octopolis

## Summary

Octopolis is a research site discovered by scuba enthusiast Matthew Lawrence in a bay on the east coast of Australia, approximately fifty feet down. It features a shell bed with about a dozen octopuses living in excavated dens around an encrusted rock-like object. The site provides opportunities to observe complex social behaviors including wrestling matches, bullying, and individual recognition of human divers.

## Key facts

- Octopolis is located about fifty feet down in a bay on the east coast of Australia. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p51]
- The site features a bed of scallop shells about two yards in diameter with an encrusted rock-like object in the middle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p51]
- Octopuses at the site were observed engaging in wrestling matches and bullying behavior where larger octopuses jumped on and wrestled with smaller ones in dens. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p50]
- Octopuses at the site showed curiosity toward research equipment, adding stakes to dens and exploring cameras and air hoses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p51]
- Octopolis was discovered by Matt Lawrence in 2009 and hosts multiple octopuses year-round in an area just a few yards across. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p141]
- The site was seeded by a metal object that accumulated scallop shells, enabling stable dens in otherwise unsuitable sandy bottom. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p148]
- Octopuses at Octopolis exhibit high levels of social interaction including 'high-five' arm probes, aggressive displays, and herding behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p144]
- The 'Nosferatu' pose—rising dark and raising the mantle over the head—is used by dominant octopuses at Octopolis to intimidate rivals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p145]
- Octopolis represents a case of ecosystem engineering, where octopuses have reshaped their environment through shell-collecting behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p151]
