# Viracocha

## Summary

The universal god of prehistoric Peru, creator of all things, depicted with a sun tiara and thunderbolts in either hand, with tears descending from his eyes as rain. His synthesis of sun-god and storm-god traits combines the grace that creates with the energy that annihilates.

## Key facts

- Viracocha's tiara is the sun, he grasps a thunderbolt in either hand, and tears descend from his eyes as the rains that refresh the valleys of the world. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p117]
- Viracocha wandered earth as a beggar in rags and reviled, appearing incognito like Zeus and Mercury at Baucis and Philemon's home. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p117]
- The tears of Viracocha symbolize that the seminal waters of life given forth are simultaneously tears of God's eyes, combining world-discrediting insight with world-begetting affirmative. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p117]
