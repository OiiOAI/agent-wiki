# Edmund Husserl

## Summary

Edmund Husserl (1859-1938) was the founder of phenomenology, a philosophical method investigating the structures of consciousness and experience. He developed the phenomenological reduction and epoché as methods for suspending theoretical assumptions to examine how things appear in experience. His key concepts include intentionality, the noema-noesis structure, the life-world (Lebenswelt), and the distinction between static, genetic, and generative phenomenology. His work established the framework for investigating consciousness that later thinkers like Heidegger and Merleau-Ponty developed in different directions.

## Key facts

- Husserl developed the phenomenological reduction as a method of redirecting thought from unreflective immersion in the world to how the world appears to us. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p29]
- The epoché is the suspension or bracketing of both natural positing attitude and theoretical beliefs about objective reality. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p30]
- Transcendental phenomenology investigates the modes or ways in which objects are experienced and the a priori conditions for such experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p31]
- Husserl distinguished between receptive (paying attention to what affects us passively) and affectivity, with receptivity presupposing prior affection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p40]
- Husserl stated in 1927: 'As Hume correctly teaches, habit is not only our nurse, rather it is the function of consciousness that shapes and constantly further shapes the world.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p42]
- Husserl's late work moved from genetic to generative phenomenology, widening scope beyond self-other relation to include parameters of birth, death, and generational interconnectedness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Mind in Life _ Biology, Phenomenology, and the Sciences of - Evan Thompson.pdf#p43]
- Husserl came to the conclusion that there was an objective reality, but that it was constituted by what he called intersubjectivity - achieved through shared experience made possible by embodied existence alongside other embodied individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p208]
- Husserl distinguished between the body as Körper - a material object alongside other objects - and Leib - experienced as something lived from the inside. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p208]
- Husserl's roots of the European crisis of modernism lay in 'verirrenden Rationalismus' and 'Blindheit für das Transzendentale' - a mad rationalism and blindness to the transcendental. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p208]
