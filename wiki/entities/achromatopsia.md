# Achromatopsia

## Summary

Achromatopsia is a condition involving partial or complete loss of color vision, where patients see everything in shades of gray. The case of Jonathan I., a New York abstract painter, illustrates how this condition affects not just visual perception but also synesthesia (where music previously produced 'inner colors'), food appreciation, facial recognition, and even dream coloration. The damage was bilateral and affected a high level of the visual system in the ventromedial sector of the occipital lobe.

## Key facts

- Achromatopsia produces partial or complete loss of color vision, with patients seeing everything in shades of gray. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p179]
- Patient Jonathan I., a painter, lost both color vision and synesthetic color responses to music after his injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p179]
- Jonathan I. became abnormally sensitive to movement, claiming he could 'see a worm wriggling a block away' and read license plates from four blocks away at night. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p180]
- Achromatopsia in humans usually involves cortical damage at a fairly high level of the visual system (the ventromedial sector of the occipital lobe). [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p180]

## Inferences

- Inference: The increased sensitivity to movement in achromatopsia suggests the P system (involved in color and form) damage may leave the M system (involved in motion) operating with less competition.
