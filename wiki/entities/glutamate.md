# Glutamate

## Summary

Glutamate is the major excitatory neurotransmitter in the mammalian central nervous system. It acts on ionotropic glutamate receptors (AMPA, NMDA, kainate) and metabotropic glutamate receptors. Glutamate-mediated excitotoxicity can contribute to neuronal damage.

## Key facts

- Glutamate is the primary excitatory transmitter in the brain and spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p264]
- Three major subtypes of ionotropic glutamate receptors exist: AMPA, kainate, and NMDA, named after their selective agonists. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p265]
- High concentrations of glutamate can cause excitotoxicity through excessive Ca2+ influx, activating proteases and producing free radicals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p267]

## Inferences

- Inference: Glutamate's role as the primary excitatory transmitter makes it central to virtually all neural circuit computations.
