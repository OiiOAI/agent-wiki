# Donella Meadows

## Summary

Donella Meadows (1941–2001) was a scientist trained in chemistry and biophysics who became a pioneering figure in systems thinking and environmental modeling. She was lead author of The Limits to Growth (1972), recipient of a MacArthur Fellowship (1994), and founder of the Sustainability Institute (1996).

## Key facts

- Donella Meadows held a Ph.D. in chemistry and biophysics from Harvard University. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p251]
- In 1970, she joined the MIT team led by Dennis Meadows that produced World3, a global computer model exploring human population and economic growth dynamics on a finite planet. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p251]
- In 1972, she was lead author of The Limits to Growth, translated into twenty-eight languages, which sparked global debate about Earth's carrying capacity and human choices. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p251]
- Meadows wrote nine additional books on global modeling and sustainable development and wrote a weekly column titled 'The Global Citizen' for fifteen years. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p251]
- She was recognized as a Pew Scholar in Conservation and the Environment in 1991 and received a MacArthur Fellowship in 1994. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p251]
- She founded the Sustainability Institute in 1996 to apply systems thinking and organizational learning to economic, environmental, and social challenges. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p251]
- From 1972 until her death in 2001, Meadows taught in the Environmental Studies Program of Dartmouth College. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p252]
