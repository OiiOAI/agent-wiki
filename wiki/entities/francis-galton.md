# Francis Galton

## Summary

British scientist and cousin of Charles Darwin (1822-1911) who discovered regression to the mean while studying hereditary traits. His Galton board (quincunx) demonstrated normal distribution through random processes. Though seeking causal explanations for heredity, he ended up discovering correlation, which Karl Pearson later elevated into a philosophy that excluded causation from science.

## Key facts

- Galton discovered regression to the mean in 1877 while studying inheritance of height, observing that tall fathers have shorter sons and short fathers have taller sons. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L470-L490]
- The Galton board demonstrated that random processes (sums of coin flips) produce normal distributions, which Galton used as an analogy for inheritance of traits. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L470-L485]
- Galton initially sought causal explanations for regression to the mean but came to believe it had no causal basis, representing the first step toward divorcing statistics from causation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L490-L510]
- Galton coined the term 'correlation' to describe relationships like height and forearm length, where both are caused by genetic inheritance but neither causes the other. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L490-L505]

## Inferences

- Inference: Galton's quincunx was wrongly constructed for modeling inheritance because it allowed luck variations to accumulate across generations, contradicting the modern understanding that transitory luck has no impact on future generations.
