# Professor Flechsig

## Summary

Geheimrat Professor Flechsig of Leipzig was Daniel Paul Schreber's physician during his first stay at the University Clinic (1884–1885) and again during his second illness. Schreber developed elaborate delusions accusing Flechsig of 'soul murder,' and Freud interpreted this as Schreber's displaced homosexual feelings toward his doctor.

## Key facts

- Flechsig treated Schreber during his first illness (1884–1885) at the University Clinic in Leipzig, where Schreber spent six months. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3754]
- Schreber developed the delusion that Flechsig attempted 'soul murder' upon him, which Schreber compared to efforts of the Devil to gain possession of souls. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3752]
- In Schreber's delusion, Flechsig's soul became a 'leader of rays' in Heaven without dying or undergoing purification. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3752]
- Freud interpreted the accusation of 'soul murder' as originally a self-accusation later reversed by projection. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3763]
- Schreber maintained in his 'Open Letter to Professor Flechsig' that he did not intend to attack the doctor's honor, distinguishing the 'soul Flechsig' from the living man. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3753]
