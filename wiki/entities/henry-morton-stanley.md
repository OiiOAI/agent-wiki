# Henry Morton Stanley

## Summary

Henry Morton Stanley (1841-1904) was a Welsh-American explorer famous for finding Dr. Livingstone in Africa and for his remarkable self-control during expeditions through the Congo. Born John Rowlands in a workhouse, he developed sophisticated precommitment strategies and habits to maintain discipline in extreme conditions, earning the African nickname 'Bula Matari' (Breaker of Rocks).

## Key facts

- Stanley was born John Rowlands to an unmarried woman who abandoned him; he grew up in a workhouse and later fabricated his identity, taking the name Henry Morton Stanley from a kind American trader. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p135]
- At age eleven, Stanley experimented 'on Will' by imposing extra hardships on himself in the workhouse, including midnight prayer sessions and sharing his food with others. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p136]
- Stanley shaved every day even in 'Starvation Camp' in the Ituri Forest, saying he did so for 'self-discipline and self-respect,' turning shaving into an automatic habit that conserved willpower. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p144]
- Stanley concluded that 'self-control is more indispensable than gunpowder' and that persistent self-control 'is impossible without real, heartfelt sympathy for the natives with whom one has to deal.'. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p151]

## Uncertainties
