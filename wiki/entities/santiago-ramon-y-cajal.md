# Santiago Ramón y Cajal

## Summary

Santiago Ramón y Cajal (1852-1934) was a Spanish neuroanatomist who won the 1906 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his work on the structure of the nervous system. He discovered that neurons are elongated cells with long axons capable of spanning meters, and that long-distance projection cells are dense in the cortex. His meticulous drawings revealed the pyramidal neurons whose axons project to distant brain regions, forming the anatomical basis for the global neuronal workspace.

## Key facts

- Cajal discovered that neurons are enormously elongated cells with long axons capable of measuring up to meters in size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p215]
- He noted that long-distance projection cells are quite dense in the cortex, with pyramidal neurons sending axons to distant regions or the opposite hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p215]
- Axons from these projection neurons group into bundles forming cables of several millimeters in diameter and several centimeters in length. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p215]
- Modern MRI can now detect these crisscrossing fiber bundles in the living human brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p215]
