# Open-Label Placebo

## Summary

Open-label placebos are inert treatments given to patients who are told they are receiving placebos. Research dating back to 1965 and continuing through cancer fatigue studies shows that even when patients know they are taking placebos, the treatments still produce positive effects, supporting the view that positive expectations can heal regardless of transparency.

## Key facts

- In 1965, researchers found that giving placebos to people who knew it was a placebo did not render the pill ineffective. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p127]
- In cancer survivor studies, participants with lingering fatigue were given pills labeled as placebo for three weeks—despite the label, the pills still had positive effects. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p127]

## Inferences

- Inference: Open-label placebos work because patients are induced to have positive expectations—the approach should work as long as positive expectations are maintained.
