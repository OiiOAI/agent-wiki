# Plato

## Summary

Plato (c. 428–348 BC) developed philosophy that prioritized abstract Forms over phenomenal particulars, systematized knowledge, and influenced the West for two millennia. His doctrine of eternal Forms gave priority to the unchanging categorical type over imperfect sensory copies. He believed knowledge must be unfailing and general, hence inaccessible to changing particulars. McGilchrist interprets Plato's philosophy as paradigmatically left-hemisphere: prioritizing what is abstract, fixed, and categorizable over what is concrete, changing, and individual; preferring reason over experience, and denigrating the body and the sensory world. Yet Plato himself needed myth to explain what resisted dialectic, revealing ambivalence.
