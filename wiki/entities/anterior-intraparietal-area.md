# Anterior Intraparietal Area

## Summary

The anterior intraparietal area (AIP) is a region in the monkey brain located in the anterior part of the intraparietal sulcus. It plays a critical role in transforming visual information about objects into motor representations for grasping, containing neurons selective for object shapes and hand configurations.

## Key facts

- The anterior intraparietal area (AIP) contains neurons selective for objects of particular shapes and for the hand shapes required to grasp them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p471]
- A neuron in AIP that fires when the monkey sees a given object also fires when it prepares to grasp that object, demonstrating sensorimotor transformation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p450]
- Neurons in AIP are classified as motor-dominant (discharge equally in light and dark), visual-dominant (only during light), or visual-and-motor (stronger in light but fire in both conditions). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p472]
- AIP projects to premotor area F5, where canonical neurons encode potential motor acts congruent with the object affordances extracted in AIP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p473]
- AIP is part of the dorsal visual stream (ventro-dorsal branch) involved in visuomotor transformations for object interaction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p469]

## Inferences

- Inference: AIP serves as an interface between visual object processing and motor control for manipulation, transforming what an object is into how to grasp it.
