# Orexin

## Summary

Orexin (also called hypocretin) is a pair of neuropeptides (orexin-A and orexin-B) produced exclusively by neurons in the lateral hypothalamus. These peptides promote wakefulness and regulate REM sleep by activating monoaminergic neurons in the brainstem and inhibiting REM-promoting circuits. Loss of orexinergic neurons causes narcolepsy in humans and animals, making orexin replacement a potential therapeutic approach for narcolepsy.

## Key facts

- There are two orexin peptides derived from the same mRNA precursor, found only in cells in the posterior lateral hypothalamus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1141]
- Orexinergic neurons promote wakefulness by activating monoaminergic neurons in the locus ceruleus and dorsal raphe, and by activating REM-off GABAergic neurons in the periaqueductal gray matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1142]
- People with narcolepsy show a highly selective loss of more than 90% of their orexin neurons, while other types of hypothalamic neurons are spared. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1141]
- Narcolepsy is linked to immune genes and triggered by influenza, suggesting T lymphocytes target orexin neuropeptides in an autoimmune attack. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1141]
