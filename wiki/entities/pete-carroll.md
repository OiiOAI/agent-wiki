# Pete Carroll

## Summary

Pete Carroll is the head coach of the Seattle Seahawks who made one of the most controversial decisions in Super Bowl history in 2015. His call for Russell Wilson to pass instead of handing off to Marshawn Lynch was widely criticized as the 'worst play call in Super Bowl history,' but Duke uses this as an example of 'resulting'—judging decision quality by outcomes rather than process.

## Key facts

- Pete Carroll called a quarterback pass play on second down at the New England Patriots' one-yard line with 26 seconds remaining in Super Bowl XLIX, which was intercepted. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L224-L229]
- Analysis by Benjamin Morris on FiveThirtyEight.com and Brian Burke on Slate.com argued the decision was defensible, noting that out of 66 passes from the one-yard line that season, zero were intercepted. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L239-L243]
- Four days after the Super Bowl, Carroll appeared on the Today show acknowledging 'It was the worst result of a call ever' but noting 'The call would have been a great one if we catch it.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L259-L263]
- Duke describes Carroll as having made a good-quality decision that got a bad result due to bad luck, making him a victim of resulting. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L254-L259]
- The scenario planning analysis shows that a pass play gave Seattle three chances to score (including two runs after an incomplete pass) versus only two attempts with a run, while interception risk was only 2-3%. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1856-L1860]
- Very few commentators recognized the mathematical advantage of the pass play, even with extensive time to analyze the decision after the fact. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1862-L1864]
- Pete Carroll was head coach of the Seattle Seahawks during Super Bowl XLIX in 2015. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Carroll's decision to call a pass play instead of a running play near the goal line resulted in an interception by Malcolm Butler. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Headlines described the play-call as 'Worst Play-Call in Super Bowl History' across multiple major publications. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Some analysts argued the decision was not as unreasonable as headlines suggested, given situational probabilities. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]
- Carroll appeared on the Today show and conceded the result was the 'worst result of a call ever'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1909-L1939]

## Inferences

- Inference: Carroll understood that his critics were guilty of resulting and acknowledged the outcome while defending the decision process.
- Inference: The Carroll example illustrates how hindsight bias causes observers to treat a 2-3% outcome (interception) as having been obviously foreseeable and avoidable, despite it being a low-probability branch of the decision tree.
