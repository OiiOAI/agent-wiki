# Terry Real

## Summary

Terry Real is a family therapist and author who has written extensively about men in relationships. Perel cites his concept of 'intrusion trauma' to explain how boys who grow up with powerful, irresponsible, or abusive fathers become enmeshed with their mothers and as adults fear their own emotions, leading to difficulties with intimacy.

## Key facts

- Terry Real describes an 'unholy triangle' between 'the powerful, irresponsible, and/or abusive father, the codependent, downtrodden wife, and the sweet son caught in the middle.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p165]
- Real expands that sons caught in this pattern become unhealthily enmeshed with their mothers, and as adults they 'become afraid of their own range of emotions,' always taking responsibility for others' happiness. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p165]
- Real calls this childhood pattern 'intrusion trauma,' which 'lives not just in the psyche but in the body—hence its power to inhibit physical intimacy.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p165]
