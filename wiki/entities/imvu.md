# IMVU

## Summary

IMVU is a virtual world and avatar-based social platform founded by Eric Ries and his cofounders in 2004. It serves as the primary case study throughout The Lean Startup book. IMVU's early struggles with building the wrong product—an IM add-on that customers didn't want—led to a pivot toward a standalone virtual goods platform. The company became profitable with annual revenues exceeding $50 million by 2011 and over 60 million avatars created, demonstrating the effectiveness of validated learning and rapid experimentation.

## Key facts

- IMVU was founded in 2004 by Eric Ries and his cofounders with a vision to combine instant messaging with 3D avatar technology. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p13]
- The original IMVU product was an IM add-on designed to interoperate with existing networks like AOL AIM, built over six months of intense development. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p44]
- IMVU launched with a terrible, buggy product that nobody wanted to download—revealing the initial strategy was fundamentally flawed. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p45]
- Through customer interviews, IMVU discovered that customers wanted a stand-alone IM network and to make new friends online using avatars, not an add-on for existing networks. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p48-49]
- After pivoting, IMVU customers created more than 60 million avatars; the company generates annual revenues exceeding $50 million with over 100 employees. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p16]
- IMVU shipped new versions of the product dozens of times every single day, enabling rapid learning and iteration. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p14]
- At IMVU, engineers and designers worked together side by side on one feature at a time, releasing new versions immediately when features were ready to test with a relatively small number of customers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p176]
- IMVU made about fifty changes to its product every single day on average. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p176]
- IMVU developed a 'product immune system' that automatically removed defective changes, notified relevant teams, blocked further changes, and required root cause analysis before resuming. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p177]
- IMVU originally believed customers would use IMVU as an add-on to existing IM networks and grow virally, but customers wanted to make new friends rather than use it with existing friends, eliminating viral potential. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p200]
- IMVU grew through paid advertising because customers were willing to pay more for the product than it cost to reach them via advertising. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p201]
- IMVU is where Eric Ries served as CTO and developed the techniques that would become the Lean Startup methodology. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p208]
- At IMVU, a training program was built organically from a methodical approach to evolving processes, with mentors assigned to new employees and performance of both linked. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p211]
- IMVU shipped early products full of bugs and bad design that customers wouldn't even try, demonstrating that working too fast can cause waste when customers can't experience the product's benefits. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p213]
- IMVU used Five Whys to trace a server failure through five levels to discover that the real problem was a manager who didn't believe in training new engineers. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p215]
- IMVU was founded by five original co-founders: Wil Harvey, Marcus Gosling, Mat Danzig, Mel Guymon, and Eric Ries. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#L9849]
- Early conversations with customers at IMVU led to a pivot away from an add-on strategy. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#L9853-9854]
- A Harvard Business Review case study about IMVU was written by Bethany Coates under the direction of Professor Andy Rachleff. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#L9857-9858]
