# Sergeant Joseph Darby

## Summary

Sergeant Joseph Darby was a 24-year-old U.S. Army reservist who exposed the Abu Ghraib prisoner abuse scandal in Iraq. Initially he looked the other way as abuse became normalized among soldiers, even playing along when given a CD of abuse photos. After days of stressful deliberation, he handed the CD to a superior officer, leading to prosecution of soldiers involved. His story illustrates the power of values alignment in resisting dangerous groupthink, and the courage required to act from truth within oneself despite conformity pressures in military culture.

## Key facts

- Joseph Darby was 24 years old and a U.S. Army reservist when he was posted to Abu Ghraib prison in early days of the Iraq War. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p102]
- He initially played along when fellow guards gave him a CD of abuse photos: 'It was amusing at first.'. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p103]
- After realizing the abuse 'violated everything I personally believed in and all I'd been taught about the rules of war,' he handed over the CD to a superior officer. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p103]
- He was so afraid he'd be found out as the whistle-blower that at one point he slept with a gun under his pillow. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p103]
