# Frederick Winslow Taylor

## Summary

Frederick Winslow Taylor (1856-1915) was an American mechanical engineer and management consultant who pioneered scientific management. His work fundamentally changed industrial organization by applying scientific methods to work processes and labor management.

## Key facts

- Taylor developed the Principles of Scientific Management, first presented in a lecture in 1895. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p429]
- Taylor's approach to scientific management was a locus classicus for criticism of modern industrial society, paralleling Adam Smith's pin factory analysis. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p430]
- Alfred Chandler noted Taylor gave his first lecture on scientific management in 1895. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p429]

## Inferences

- Inference: Taylor's scientific management represents a systematic approach to organizing work that contrasts with traditional craft-based methods and has implications for understanding trust dynamics between workers and management.
