# Kent Berridge

## Summary

Kent Berridge is a professor of psychology and neuroscience at the University of Michigan who conducted extensive research demonstrating that dopamine is about motivation to seek rewards ('wanting') rather than the pleasure of receiving them ('liking'). His work fundamentally changed understanding of the brain's reward system.

## Key facts

- Kent Berridge ran countless experiments showing that interfering with dopamine reduced animals' ability to work for rewards but had no effect on their ability to enjoy rewards they received. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p103]
- Berridge demonstrated that to the brain, 'wanting' something is not the same as 'liking' it, challenging the view of dopamine as the 'pleasure chemical.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p103]
- In one study, Berridge showed that rats whose dopamine circuits had been activated would repeatedly touch a rod that gave them shocks, even learning to avoid it less, because they 'wanted' the stimulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Why We Remember_ Unlocking Memory's Power - Charan Ranganath.pdf#p103]
