# R. L. Trivers

## Summary

Robert L. Trivers is an American evolutionary biologist who made fundamental contributions to the understanding of social evolution, introducing the concepts of Parental Investment (1972), Parent-Offspring Conflict (1974), and Reciprocal Altruism. His work on sexual conflict provided the theoretical framework for understanding the evolutionary battle between males and females, including the 'cruel bind' situation where the first partner to desert gains advantage.

## Key facts

- Trivers introduced the concept of Parental Investment in 1972, defined as any investment by the parent in an offspring that increases the offspring's chance of surviving at the cost of the parent's ability to invest in other offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p158]
- Trivers's 1974 paper 'Parent-Offspring Conflict' analyzed the genetic conflict of interest between parents and offspring, showing children should grab more than their 'fair share' but only up to the point where cost to siblings exceeds double the benefit to themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p162]
- Trivers introduced the idea of reciprocal altruism ('You scratch my back, I'll ride on yours') as an explanation for altruistic behavior between unrelated individuals. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p203]
- Trivers proposed that both males and females have reasons to want prolonged 'engagement' periods before copulation—for females to assess male fidelity, and for males to ensure the female is not harboring another male's offspring (step-children). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p183]
- Trivers's 'cruel bind' describes the situation where the partner who is left behind is placed at a disadvantage, creating evolutionary pressure for both males and females to desert first—leading to an arms race over who can abandon earliest. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p184]

## Inferences

- Inference: Trivers's work provides the theoretical backbone for understanding family conflict, showing that altruism and cooperation within families coexists with genetic conflict rather than replacing it.
