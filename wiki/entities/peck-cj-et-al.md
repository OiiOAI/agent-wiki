# Peck CJ, Lau B, Salzman CD (2013)

## Summary

Peck et al. (2013) examined how neural activity in the amygdala is linked to spatial attention. They found that amygdala neurons combine information about the spatial configuration of visual stimuli and the rewards predicted by those stimuli, demonstrating that the amygdala influences attentional allocation toward motivationally significant stimuli.

## Key facts

- Amygdala neurons encoded both the spatial configuration and the reward value of motivational cues. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p45]
- A strong positive correlation between value and spatial selectivity was observed, demonstrating that individual amygdala neurons systematically combine information about space and reward value. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p45]
- The strength of the spatial signal in the amygdala is modulated by task demands, suggesting it is related to attentional engagement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p45]
