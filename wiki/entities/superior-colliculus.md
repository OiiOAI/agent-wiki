# Superior Colliculus

## Summary

The superior colliculus is a twin moundlike structure at the tectum of the brain stem, primarily engaged in processing visual information while also containing maps for auditory and somatosensory space and motor control for rapid eye movements during pursuit.

## Key facts

- The superior colliculus is located at the roof (tectum) of the brain stem and is primarily engaged in processing visual information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
- The deeper layers of the superior colliculus contain multimodal sensory maps for auditory and somatosensory space and motor control functions, especially spontaneous eye movements for rapid orientation during pursuit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
- The superior colliculus is where the first evolutionary appearance of sophisticated representation of self may emerge, containing multimodal sensory systems for elaborating simple orientation responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p47]
- The superior colliculus in the midbrain is a major visuomotor integration region, the mammalian homolog of the optic tectum in nonmammalian vertebrates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p872]
- The superficial layers of the superior colliculus receive direct input from the retina and a projection from the striate cortex, responding to visual stimuli in a retinotopic map of the contralateral visual field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p872]
- The intermediate and deep layers of the superior colliculus contain movement-related neurons that receive visual information from prestriate, middle temporal, and parietal cortices and motor information from the frontal eye field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p872]
- Movement-related neurons in the superior colliculus selectively discharge before saccades of specific amplitudes and directions, forming a movement field map in register with the visual map. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p873]
- The rostral portion of the superior colliculus facilitates visual fixation, containing neurons that inhibit movement-related neurons in more caudal parts and project to omnipause neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p874]
- The superior colliculus receives a powerful GABAergic inhibitory projection from neurons in the substantia nigra pars reticulata that fire spontaneously with high frequency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p874]
- Lesions of the entire superior colliculus render a monkey unable to make any contraversive saccades, though with time this ability can be recovered. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p873]
- When both the frontal eye field and the superior colliculus are damaged, the ability to make saccades is permanently compromised. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p875]
- Superior colliculus is a topographic map broadly similar in function to LIP and FEF. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Superior colliculus contains burst neurons that can fire in either a continuous low-frequency state or a fixed high-frequency burst state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- Inhibitory interconnections in the collicular map preclude burst-like activity at more than one location at a time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p498]
- The superior colliculus reflects instantaneous mapping between color and value even when this changes from trial to trial. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p499]

## Inferences

- Inference: The superior colliculus appears to be a critical structure for the winner-take-all operation that accomplishes movement selection.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: How the collicular choice mechanism generalizes to more abstract objects without specific motor mappings remains unclear.
