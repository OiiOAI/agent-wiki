# Nucleus of the Solitary Tract

## Summary

The nucleus of the solitary tract is a visceral sensory relay center in the medulla that receives input from cranial nerves VII, IX, and X. It processes taste information and general visceral sensory information and projects to autonomic control centers.

## Key facts

- The nucleus of the solitary tract receives special visceral afferents (taste) from the anterior two-thirds of the tongue via the facial nerve and from posterior parts via glossopharyngeal and vagus nerves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1037]
- General visceral afferents from the gastrointestinal tract terminate in topographic order in the middle portion of the solitary nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1037]
- The solitary nucleus projects directly to parasympathetic and sympathetic preganglionic motor neurons that mediate various autonomic reflexes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1037]
- The nucleus of the solitary tract receives baroreceptor information from the vagus (X) nerve and chemoreceptor information from the glossopharyngeal (IX) nerve. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1070]
