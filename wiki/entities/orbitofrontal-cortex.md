# Orbitofrontal Cortex (OFC)

## Summary

The orbitofrontal cortex (OFC) is known to play a prominent role in encoding the economic value of chosen options in a unit of common currency. It is part of an interconnected network that guides reward-related behaviors, encompassing striatum, medial prefrontal cortex, and ventromedial prefrontal cortex. OFC activity is modulated by social context, with neurons responding differently depending on whether rewards are for self only or shared with others.

## Key facts

- Neurons in the orbitofrontal cortex play a prominent role in encoding the economic value of a chosen option in the unit of common currency. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p163]
- The orbitofrontal cortex is known to be part of the interconnected network that guides reward-related behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p163]
- Value-sensitive neurons in the OFC responded with reduced firing when identical rewards were provided to a partner monkey, reflecting subjective value. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p183]
- Social influence of reward sharing and partner identity is taken into account in the valuation process operated by the OFC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p183]
- Cue-related and anticipatory responses in orbitofrontal cortex relate to monkeys' behavioral preference for associated outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p378]
- Neuroimaging studies reveal activations in OFC during both appetitive and aversive Pavlovian conditioning in response to CS presentation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p379]
- Valentin et al. (2007) found that medial and central OFC showed activity consistent with action-outcome learning during instrumental choice, with medial OFC particularly implicated in encoding goal values. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p383]
- OFC neurons encode the value subjects assign to the available goods during economic choice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p443]
- The representation of value in OFC is menu invariant - neuronal responses do not depend on what other goods are available. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p445]
- Activity in OFC encodes willingness to pay in everyday economic transactions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p436]
