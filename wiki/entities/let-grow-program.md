# Let Grow Program

## Summary

Let Grow is an organization founded by Lenore Skenazy to promote free play and independence for children. The program provides schools with a framework where children receive homework to do something new independently, without adult supervision, once a week or monthly. Children carry a card explaining to concerned adults why they're out alone. The program emerged from research showing that children today have been deprived of free play, structured time has exploded, and this deprivation harms attention development.

## Key facts

- Lenore Skenazy founded Let Grow to promote free play and freedom to explore for children. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p267]
- Schools signing up commit that once a week or once a month, a child's homework will be to go home and do something new independently, without adult supervision. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p269]
- Children are given a card to show to any adult who stops them, explaining: 'I'm not lost or neglected' and citing lower crime rates than in 1963. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p270]
- Roanoke Avenue Elementary on Long Island became one of the first schools to sign up for the program. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p269]
- Children in the program undertook diverse projects: lemonade stands, cleaning rivers to save turtles, cooking for parents, and climbing trees. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p270]
- One student called L.B., previously not academic and often distracted, chose to build a boat as his project, working intensely for weeks until successful. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p271]
- After building his boat, L.B. built a life-size wagon he could sleep in, then an amphibious wagon that floated, becoming increasingly ambitious. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p271-273]
- L.B.'s reading hugely improved because it was tied to his hobby—he was reading all the time about how to build things. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p271]
- Teacher Donna Verbeck observed that after the program, kids rose to the occasion and parents were rewired by seeing their children succeed independently. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p274]
- When parents see their kids do something without them watching or helping, they can't understand why they didn't trust their kids sooner. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p274]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Let Grow model demonstrates that providing children with autonomous, self-directed challenges can develop focus and intrinsic motivation more effectively than forced academic work.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The program's effectiveness has been observed in individual schools but systematic long-term studies are not yet cited in this chunk.
