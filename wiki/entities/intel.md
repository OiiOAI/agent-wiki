# Intel

## Summary

Intel is used as a case study for identifying Addressable Strategic Challenges (ASCs). The company faced multiple gnarly challenges in 2019-2020 including manufacturing delays, competition from AMD, missing the mobile market, and cultural complacency. The study group identified manufacturing and culture as the crux challenges with both high importance and high addressability.

## Key facts

- Intel had been dominant through Moore's Law scaling from 1000nm in 1984 to 14nm in 2014, six scaling steps each. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p76]
- By 2018, Moore's Law scaling appeared to be ending with costs ballooning, posing a large challenge to Intel's performance-edge strategy. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p77]
- Intel spent approximately $10 billion in a failed attempt to place Atom chips in mobile devices. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p78]
- AMD gained market share because its new Ryzen chips outperformed Intel's current offerings. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p77]
- CEO Robert Swan identified Intel's culture as a major challenge with managers hoarding information and battling over budgets. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p80]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Intel case demonstrates that multiple challenges can be systematically evaluated by scoring importance and addressability to identify ASCs.
