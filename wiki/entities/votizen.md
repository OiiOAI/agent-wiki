# Votizen

## Summary

Votizen was founded by David Binetti to tackle civic participation in the political process. The company went through multiple pivots—from a social network of verified voters to @2gov (a social lobbying platform) to a self-serve platform—demonstrating the acceleration of MVP cycles. The company raised $1.5 million from Peter Thiel and successfully delivered tens of thousands of messages to Congress.

## Key facts

- Votizen's first MVP cost just over $1,200 and took three months to build. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p140]
- After optimization through split testing, Votizen improved registration from 5% to 17% and activation from 17% to over 90%. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p142]
- Votizen pivoted from a social network of verified voters to @2gov, a social lobbying platform allowing customers to contact elected representatives via Twitter. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p145]
- After pivoting to @2gov, registration reached 42%, activation 83%, retention 21%, and referral 54%, but less than 1% were willing to pay. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p146]
- Votizen then pivoted to a platform model, building a self-serve sales platform where anyone could become a customer with just a credit card. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p150]
- The final pivot resulted in 51% sign-up rate, 92% activation, 28% retention, 64% referral, and 11% willing to pay 20 cents per message. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p150]
- Votizen raised $1.5 million from Peter Thiel and can process voter identity in real time for 94% of the U.S. population. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p152]
- Votizen's MVP acceleration demonstrated that each successive MVP took less time: first took eight months, next four months, then three, then one. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p152]
