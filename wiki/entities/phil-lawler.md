# Phil Lawler

## Summary

A visionary junior high physical education teacher who founded the Naperville 'New PE' movement in 1990. After reading about declining child health, Lawler shifted PE focus from team sports to cardiovascular fitness, grading students on effort rather than skill. He pioneered the use of heart rate monitors and created the Zero Hour PE program.

## Key facts

- Lawler started the Naperville revolution after reading a 1990 newspaper article reporting that U.S. children's health was declining. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p18]
- Lawler is a former college baseball pitcher who became a gym teacher to stay close to sports. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p18]
- He instituted a policy of running the mile once a week, which met with groans from students, complaints from parents, and notes from doctors. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p18]
- Lawler introduced personal bests as a grading principle, allowing any student who achieved a personal best to move up a letter grade regardless of athletic ability. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p18]
- Lawler obtained a heart rate monitor as a 'door prize' at a PE conference he organized and used it to discover that a non-athletic sixth-grader had an average heart rate of 187 during the mile run. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p19]
- Lawler retired from teaching in 2004 after being diagnosed with colon cancer but continued to lobby for daily physical education. [raw/books/health/Spark _ The Revolutionary New Science of Exercise and the - John J_ Ratey; Eric Hagerman.pdf#p20]
