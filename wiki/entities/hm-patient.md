# HM (Patient)

## Summary

HM is the most famous amnesic patient in neuroscience, studied extensively from 1954 onward after undergoing bilateral medial temporal lobectomy for intractable epilepsy. His profound inability to form new explicit memories while retaining the ability to learn motor skills provided the first clear evidence that implicit and explicit memory are mediated by distinct neural systems.

## Key facts

- HM underwent medial temporal lobectomy (Scoville 1954) and was studied extensively over many years, suffering loss of ability to acquire new memories of places, names, events, or people. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- Although HM could hold an image in mind briefly, attention diversion caused irretrievable loss until the experience was re-encountered as something entirely novel. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- Surprisingly, HM could be taught motor skills along a normal learning curve yet had no conscious recollection of ever having done the task before (Milner 1962). [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- After 30 trials over three days, HM attained and retained a difficult mirror-drawing skill but when presented with the task afterward had no idea he had ever done it. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
