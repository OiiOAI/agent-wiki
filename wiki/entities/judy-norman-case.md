# Judy Norman Case

## Summary

Judy Norman killed her husband after years of systematic beating and abuse. The Supreme Court of North Carolina rejected her self-defense claim and she remained convicted of voluntary manslaughter. The case illustrates how gender stereotypes affect legal outcomes: women are expected to be fearful victims rather than angry defenders, and women who kill are not acting like 'typical wives.'

## Key facts

- Norman's husband had systematically beaten and abused her for years before she killed him. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p249]
- The Supreme Court of North Carolina rejected the defense's claim she was acting in self-defense out of 'reasonable fear of imminent death or great bodily harm.'. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p249]
- She remained convicted of voluntary manslaughter, a lesser charge than murder, but still convicted. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p249]
- The case matches stereotypes: anger is stereotypically normal for men who are supposed to be aggressors, while women are supposed to be victims who should be afraid, not angry. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p249]
