# Caterina Sforza

## Summary

Caterina Sforza (1463–1509) was an Italian noblewoman and warrior countess of Forlì who exemplified the law of gender rigidity by developing both masculine and feminine qualities despite the restricted roles available to women in Renaissance Italy. Raised in the Sforza court with exceptional education including sword fighting and classical studies, she became famous for her bold political maneuvering, including taking control of Castel Sant'Angelo in Rome while seven months pregnant, and defending Ravaldino Castle against Cesare Borgia's forces. Her ability to blend masculine boldness with feminine charm fascinated both men and women of her era.

## Key facts

- Caterina Sforza was born in 1463, the illegitimate daughter of Duke Galeazzo Maria Sforza of Milan. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p394]
- Her father ordered her raised in the castle Porta Giovia with the finest education, including Latin, Greek, philosophy, sciences, and military history. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p394]
- She was trained in sword fighting and hunting wild animals, taught to think ahead and plot several moves in advance. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p395]
- At age ten in 1473, she was married to Girolamo Riario, nephew of Pope Sixtus IV, uniting Milan and Rome politically. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p396]
- In 1484 after the pope's death, she rode alone to Rome while seven months pregnant and seized Castel Sant'Angelo, holding it for eleven days. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p397-398]
- When assassins killed her husband in 1488 and threatened her children, she famously refused to surrender Ravaldino Castle, saying she could make more children. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p400]
- She was captured by Cesare Borgia in 1499 after being betrayed by her own soldier and was imprisoned for a year. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p404]
- Her final marriage was to Giovanni de' Medici (Lorenzo de' Medici), uniting two of Italy's most illustrious families. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p403]
- She died in 1509, weakened by her imprisonment, but continued receiving letters from admirers across Europe until her death. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p405]
