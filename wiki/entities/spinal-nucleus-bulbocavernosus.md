# Spinal Nucleus of the Bulbocavernosus

## Summary

The spinal nucleus of the bulbocavernosus (SNB) is a sexually dimorphic motor nucleus in the lumbar spinal cord that innervates the bulbocavernosus and levator ani muscles. In adult rats, males have many more SNB motor neurons than females, a difference arising from testosterone promoting survival of neurons and muscles that would otherwise die postnatally in females.

## Key facts

- The SNB is a sexually dimorphic motor center containing many more motor neurons in males than females in rats and humans (Onuf's nucleus). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1363]
- The sexual dimorphism in SNB arises because female motor neurons and target muscles die postnatally unless rescued by perinatal testosterone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1367]
- Testosterone acts on the bulbocavernosus muscle to produce trophic factors (possibly CNTF) that support SNB motor neuron survival, not directly on the neurons themselves. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1368]
- Androgens also act directly on SNB motor neurons in adulthood to increase dendritic arbor size; castration causes dendritic pruning and testosterone replacement restores branching. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1368]
