# Judith Rich Harris

## Summary

A psychologist without academic affiliation or doctorate who argued in 'The Nurture Assumption' that the importance of parenting in shaping a child's adult personality is exaggerated, and that peers are most influential once kids pass a surprisingly young age.

## Key facts

- Harris argued that parental influence is often mediated via peers—for example, being raised by a single mother increases risk of adult antisocial behavior due to typically lower income and tougher peer environments. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p212]
- Harris noted that children acquire the accent of their peers, not their parents, supporting peer influence on linguistic development. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p212]
- Harris's book was controversial, with the theme being distorted to 'Psychologist proves that parents don't matter.'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p212]
- Judith Rich Harris was born in 1938. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p612]
- Harris was kicked out of Harvard's PhD program in psychology because Harvard believed she lacked qualities ideal in psychological research. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p612]
- She was housebound throughout adult life by an unfixable autoimmune disease. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p612]
- She published The Nurture Assumption (1998) and No Two Alike (2006). [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p612]
- For her academic paper that became The Nurture Assumption, she won a prestigious medal awarded annually by the American Psychological Association for distinction in published writing. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p612]
- The medal she won was named after the man who signed her dismissal notice from Harvard. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p612]
- She demonstrated that peer pressure on the young is far more important, and parental nurture is much less important, than had been commonly recognized. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p611]
