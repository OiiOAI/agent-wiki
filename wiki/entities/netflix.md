# Netflix

## Summary

Netflix began in 1998 as the leading DVD rent-by-mail business and executed a dramatic strategic pivot to streaming by 2010. The book uses Netflix's 2018 strategic situation as a teaching case, examining challenges including rising content costs, new competitors (Disney, Apple), and international expansion opportunities. The crux analysis identifies leveraging international content creation as the key opportunity.

## Key facts

- Netflix was the leading DVD rent-by-mail business with the clever Cinematch prediction system and efficient logistics. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- Netflix commanded 76 percent of paid streaming market share versus Amazon Prime at 17 percent in 2018. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p25]
- Revenue was $11.7 billion in 2017 but the company showed net cash outflow of negative $1.8 billion. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- Netflix had 58 million international subscribers versus 53 million in the United States by 2018. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p24]
- The diagnosis identifies that Netflix can no longer count on contracting for existing content at reasonable prices from studios like Disney and WarnerMedia. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p31]

## Inferences

- Inference: Netflix's strategic challenge involves balancing negative cash flow with investment in original content to defend against new streaming competitors.
