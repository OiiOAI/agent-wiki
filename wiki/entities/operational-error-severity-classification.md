# OE Severity Classification

## Summary

Operational Error (OE) severity is categorized using a point system based on the closest proximity of involved aircraft in terms of horizontal and vertical distances at the time of occurrence. Points are assigned separately for vertical and horizontal separation, then summed. Severity thresholds differ based on whether the occurrence was at or above FL 290. Major errors (≥20 points) represent the most severe category, including occurrences with less than 0.5 nautical miles horizontal separation and less than 500 feet vertical separation.

## Key facts

- Severity classification uses points assigned for vertical separation (2-10 points) and horizontal separation (1-10 points). [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
- Major errors require 20 or more points total. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
- Moderate errors are classified as 14-19 points, minor errors as 13 or fewer points. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
- At or below FL 290, a minor error can involve less than 0.5 mi horizontal separation only if vertical separation is 900 ft or greater. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
- Above FL 290, all errors with vertical separation of 1,000 ft or greater are classified as minor regardless of horizontal separation. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
- Of 1,053 errors in the Rodgers and Nye study, only 15 were coded as major, with 7 resulting in near midair collision reports. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p95]
