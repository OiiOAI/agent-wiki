# Malala Yousafzai

## Summary

Malala Yousafzai is a Pakistani activist for female education who became internationally known after writing a pseudonymous blog for the BBC about life under Taliban rule. At age fifteen, she was shot in the head by a Taliban gunman for her advocacy. She survived and won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2014 at age seventeen. Her father, Ziauddin Yousafzai, is cited as an example of autonomy-supportive parenting—he encouraged her activism without clipping her wings.

## Key facts

- Malala began writing a pseudonymous blog for the BBC about her life in northwest Pakistan when she was eleven years old. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p191]
- The Taliban sent a gunman to kill Malala in 2012 when she was fifteen; he fired three times and one bullet struck her in the head. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p191]
- Malala's father Ziauddin said: 'She consoled us. We learned from her how to be resilient in the most difficult times.'. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p192]
- Malala's father stated: 'What has made Malala so special and so bold and so poised? Don't ask me what I did. Ask me what I did not do. I did not clip her wings, and that's all.'. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p192]
- Malala won the Nobel Peace Prize in 2014 at age seventeen, becoming its youngest-ever recipient. [raw/books/psychology/Emotional Agility - Susan David.pdf#p192]
