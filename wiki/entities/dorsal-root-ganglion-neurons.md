# Dorsal Root Ganglion Neurons

## Summary

Dorsal root ganglion (DRG) neurons are the primary sensory receptor cells of the somatosensory system. They are pseudo-unipolar cells whose cell bodies lie in ganglia adjacent to the spinal cord. Each DRG neuron has two branches: one projecting to the periphery with specialized terminals containing receptors for particular stimuli, and one projecting to the central nervous system forming the first synapses in somatosensory pathways. DRG neurons can be subdivided into five functional zones: the receptive zone, spike generation site, peripheral nerve fiber, cell body, and spinal or cranial nerve.

## Key facts

- Dorsal root ganglion neurons are the primary sensory receptor cells of the somatosensory system and perform two major functions: transduction and encoding of stimuli into electrical signals, and transmission of those signals to the central nervous system. - DRG neurons are pseudo-unipolar cells with the axon having two branches: one projecting to the periphery with specialized receptor terminals, and one projecting to the spinal cord or brain stem. - DRG neurons differ in the size of their cell soma, gene expression profile, conduction velocity of their axons, sensory transduction molecules, innervation pattern in the body, and physiological function.
