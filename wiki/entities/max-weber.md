# Max Weber

## Summary

Max Weber (1864-1920) was a German sociologist, philosopher, and political economist whose ideas profoundly influenced sociological theory. In the context of Fukuyama's Trust, Weber's work on authority types (charismatic, traditional, and legal-rational), bureaucratic organization, and instrumental rationality provides a framework for understanding how trust functions within formal organizations.

## Key facts

- Weber identified three pure types of legitimate authority: charismatic, traditional, and legal-rational. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p428]
- Weber described instrumental rationality as divorced from the rationality of ends. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p428]
- In Weber's conception of bureaucratic authority, the power to give commands is distributed stably and delimited by rules governing coercive means. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p428]
- Weber's major works cited include From Max Weber: Essays in Sociology (1946) and Economy and Society (1978). [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p428]

## Inferences

- Inference: Fukuyama draws on Weber's analysis of bureaucracy and authority to understand how institutional structures shape trust relationships in modern organizations.
