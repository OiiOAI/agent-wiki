# Heraclitus of Ephesus

## Summary

Heraclitus (fl. late sixth century BC) was from Ephesus, an opulent rival city to Miletus. His fragments survive with a taciturn, apophthegmatic, and paradoxical quality that has made them an endlessly rich resource for interpretation. He held that the nature of things is intrinsically hard to seek out; our natural assumptions lead us astray. He prioritized experience over opinion, insisted on the union of opposites ('war is father of all'), saw all things as in process of flux ('you cannot step twice into the same river'), and understood the logos as shared and reciprocal. McGilchrist sees him as grasping the essence of hemispheric balance while remaining aware of right-hemisphere primacy.

## Key facts

- Heraclitus flourished in the late sixth century BC and came from Ephesus, caring little for the philosophy of the Milesians. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p373]
- He held that the nature of things is intrinsically hard to seek out using normal tools: 'He who does not expect will not find out the unexpected, for it is trackless and unexplored.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p373]
- He prioritized experience over opinion: 'Whatever comes from sight, hearing, learning from experience: this I prefer'; 'Eyes are surer witnesses than ears.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p374]
- His famous pronouncement that 'war is father of all, and king of all' expresses the creative power of opposition—opposites define and bring each other into existence. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p375]
- McGilchrist interprets Heraclitus as grasping 'the essence of the balance between the hemispheres while remaining aware of the primacy of the right hemisphere's world.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p376]
