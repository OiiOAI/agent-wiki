# Paul Bach-y-Rita

## Summary

Paul Bach-y-Rita (1934-2006) was a pioneering neuroscientist and rehabilitation physician who became one of the first scientists to demonstrate and apply neuroplasticity—the brain's ability to reorganize itself. He developed sensory substitution devices that allowed one sense to replace another, such as enabling blind people to see through touch or tongue-based devices. His work challenged the prevailing localizationist view of the brain.

## Key facts

- Bach-y-Rita was both a basic scientist and a rehabilitation physician—a rare combination. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p21]
- In 1969, his article in Nature described a device enabling people blind from birth to see using tactile-vision substitution. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p21]
- He developed the tongue display unit (TDU), a plastic strip with electrodes placed on the tongue that replaced his earlier back-based tactile-vision device. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p29]
- His work was rejected multiple times because journals objected to the word 'plasticity' in his paper titles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p29]
- He switched from basic research back to medicine at age forty-four and did residencies in neurology and rehabilitation medicine. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p33]
- He was partial to five-dollar suits and Salvation Army clothes, drove a rusty twelve-year-old car, and spoke five languages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p25]
- He grew up in the Bronx and was four foot ten when entering high school due to a disease that stunted his growth for eight years. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p25]
