# William Hamilton

## Summary

William Hamilton was an evolutionary biologist who established the theory of kin selection in the 1960s at the London School of Economics and University College London, providing a mathematical framework to explain altruistic behavior through shared genes.

## Key facts

- Hamilton established kin selection theory showing that altruistic behavior could evolve when benefiting genetically related individuals, using the formula C < R × B. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p90]
- In Hamilton's formula, C represents cost to the actor, R represents genetic relatedness between actor and recipient, and B represents benefit to the recipient. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p90]
- Hamilton showed that individuals share 50% of DNA with siblings and children, 25% with grandchildren and nieces/nephews, explaining why helping close relatives passes shared genes forward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p93]
