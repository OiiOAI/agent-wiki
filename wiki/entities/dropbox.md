# Dropbox

## Summary

Dropbox is a file-synchronization service created by Drew Houston after he forgot his thumb drive in 2006. The company's software constantly scans files for changes and backs up sectors rather than entire files, keeping multiple devices synchronized. Dropbox was free for users needing less than 2 GB of storage, with tiered pricing for more. Growth was powered by the free tier and users sharing folders with others, creating network effects. By 2015, Dropbox was widely used with hundreds of millions of users. The company went public in 2018 with a market value of $10 billion. Competitors like Google Drive synch files one at a time rather than sector by sector, and Dropbox's non-tech-giant status attracts users concerned about data privacy.

## Key facts

- Dropbox was created by Drew Houston in 2006 after he forgot his thumb drive while traveling. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p198]
- Dropbox scans files for changes and backs up sectors (chunks) of files rather than entire files, keeping multiple devices synchronized. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p198]
- Dropbox went public in 2018 with a market value of $10 billion and over 600 million users. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p199]
- Google Drive synchronizes files one at a time rather than sector by sector, and its interface is less obvious than Dropbox's. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p199]
