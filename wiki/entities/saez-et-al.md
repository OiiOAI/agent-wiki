# Saez A, Rigotti M, Ostojic S, Fusi S, Salzman CD (2015)

## Summary

Saez et al. (2015) investigated how monkeys use cognitive strategies to update reinforcement expectations in changing environments. Using a serial reversal-learning task, they demonstrated that monkeys acquire abstract 'mental maps' of task structure, allowing them to infer contingencies for new stimuli without relearning.

## Key facts

- Monkeys adjusted anticipatory licking in a switch-like manner after recognizing that reinforcement contingencies had changed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
- After experiencing one CS switched, monkeys instantaneously switched anticipatory licking for the other CS without additional experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
- A linear decoder trained on spike counts showed that information about abstract context was encoded in the amygdala, ACC, and OFC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
- Errors in behavior were correlated with failure to maintain a representation of abstract context in the amygdala. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
