# Ilya Prigogine

## Summary

Ilya Prigogine (1917-2003) was a Belgian physicist and chemist who received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1977 for his work on dissipative structures. He discovered that systems far from thermodynamic equilibrium can spontaneously develop ordered structures through self-organization, with dissipation becoming a source of order rather than disorder. His work transformed understanding of non-equilibrium thermodynamics and provided foundations for chaos and complexity theory. Prigogine emphasized the 'radical change in our vision of nature towards the multiple, the temporal and the complex.'

## Key facts

- Prigogine received the Nobel Prize in 1977 for his theory of dissipative structures, discovering that classical thermodynamics only explained equilibrium structures while biological and social systems require different understanding. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p134]
- Prigogine stated: 'nonequilibrium is the source of order, nonequilibrium brings order out of chaos,' as systems driven far-from-equilibrium reach critical instability points where new ordered patterns emerge. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p135]
- Prigogine noted that cities and cells 'exist only because they are open' and 'cannot be separated from the fluxes that they incessantly transform,' unlike equilibrium structures like crystals. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p135]
- Prigogine asserted there is 'a radical change in our vision of nature towards the multiple, the temporal and the complex,' reflecting both the internal logic of science and cultural context. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p111]
- Boyd included sections from Prigogine's 'Order out of Chaos' in his Strategic Game briefing, using it to argue that equilibrium thermodynamics cannot explain nature's complexity. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p135]
