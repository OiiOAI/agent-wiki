# Niklas Luhmann

## Summary

Niklas Luhmann was a German sociologist who developed the Zettelkasten (slip-box) method and used it to become one of the most productive and innovative social theorists of the 20th century, writing 58 books and hundreds of articles. His approach emphasized that it is not possible to think systematically without writing.

## Key facts

- Luhmann wrote 58 books and hundreds of articles using his slip-box. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p88]
- Luhmann's slip-box contains about 90,000 notes, meaning he wrote six notes a day from when he started until he died. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p88]
- Luhmann stated: It is not possible to think systematically without writing. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p90]
- Luhmann said he never forced himself to do anything and only did what came easily to him. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p129]
- Luhmann almost never read a text twice yet was regarded as an impressive conversation partner. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p85]
