# Immanuel Wallerstein

## Summary

Immanuel Wallerstein (1930-2019) was an American sociologist who developed the modern world-systems theory. His multi-volume work 'The Modern World-System' (1974-2011) analyzing the global capitalist economy has been highly influential in sociology and related disciplines.

## Key facts

- Wallerstein received all his degrees from Columbia University, including a doctorate in 1959. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p196]
- Wallerstein was awarded the prestigious Sorokin Award for the first volume of 'The Modern World-System' in 1975. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p196]
- Wallerstein defined the world-system as a largely self-contained social system with a division of labor not circumscribed by political or cultural boundaries. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p194]
- The world-system consists of core areas that dominate and exploit, periphery areas that provide raw materials and are heavily exploited, and semiperiphery areas between them. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p195]
- Wallerstein argued that capitalism separated the political sector from the economic sector, unlike earlier world empires which were based on political domination. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p195]
- Three things were necessary for the rise of the capitalist world-economy: geographical expansion, development of different methods of labor control, and development of strong states. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p195]
