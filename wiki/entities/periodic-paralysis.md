# Periodic paralysis

## Summary

Periodic paralysis refers to rare inherited muscle diseases characterized by transient episodes of weakness or paralysis. They are caused by mutations in voltage-gated ion channels affecting muscle excitability. Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis involves Na+ channel defects causing attacks with elevated serum K+, while hypokalemic periodic paralysis involves Ca2+ channel defects causing attacks with low serum K+.

## Key facts

- During an attack, the resting potential of affected muscles depolarizes from normal −90 mV to approximately −60 mV, inactivating most Na+ channels and rendering the muscle fiber chronically refractory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p376]
- Hyperkalemic periodic paralysis is caused by missense mutations in the SCN4A gene encoding the voltage-gated Na+ channel pore-forming subunit, disrupting inactivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p377]
- Subtle defects in Na+ channel inactivation produce myotonia; more pronounced changes result in chronic depolarization and paralysis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p377]
- Hypokalemic periodic paralysis is usually caused by mutations in the CACNL1S gene encoding the main subunit of a voltage-sensitive Ca2+ channel in skeletal muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p377]

## Inferences

- Inference: The correlation between serum K+ levels and attack occurrence suggests that K+ concentrations directly influence membrane potential and channel function in these channelopathies.
