# S. Bernardi and D. Salzman

## Summary

S. Bernardi and D. Salzman are neuroscientists at Columbia University and the New York State Psychiatric Institute who co-authored Chapter 3 on appetitive and aversive systems in the amygdala.

## Key facts

- S. Bernardi and D. Salzman are affiliated with Columbia University, New York, and the New York State Psychiatric Institute, New York. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p33]
- Their chapter describes how the amygdala contains distinct appetitive and aversive networks of neurons that regulate and are regulated by diverse cognitive operations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p5]
- Paton et al. (including Bernardi and Salzman) established that the amygdala contains separate appetitive and aversive neural systems using trace-conditioning tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p34]
- Their research using optogenetics demonstrated that selective activation of appetitive or aversive amygdalar networks can elicit innate valence-specific autonomic responses and induce Pavlovian and operant learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p35]
