# Spinocerebellar Ataxias

## Summary

The spinocerebellar ataxias (SCAs) are a group of hereditary neurodegenerative disorders characterized by cerebellar dysfunction, including ataxia and dysarthria. These diseases are caused by expansion of CAG trinucleotide repeats in various genes, with 33 subtypes now identified. The length of the CAG repeat inversely correlates with age of onset, though the specific repeat length causing pathology varies by gene context.

## Key facts

- The spinocerebellar ataxias are characterized by dysfunction of the cerebellum, spinal tracts, and various brain stem nuclei. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1546]
- Ataxia and dysarthria are common to all SCAs and are signs of cerebellar dysfunction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1546]
- 33 spinocerebellar ataxia subtypes have been identified, caused by expansion of various trinucleotide or pentanucleotide repeats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1548]
- Age of onset within each SCA type depends on the number of CAG repeats, with longer repeats causing earlier onset. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1547]
- SCA6 is caused by the shortest CAG expansion: normal alleles have fewer than 18 repeats and pathological repeats have only 21-33 repeats. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1547]
- Reducing levels of disease-driving proteins through RNA interference has shown therapeutic potential in SCA mouse and Drosophila models. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1548]
