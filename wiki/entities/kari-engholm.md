# Kari Engholm

## Summary

Kari Engholm was a 35-year-old hospital executive in Dallas County, Iowa who in June 2001 forgot her 7-month-old daughter Clare in the back seat of her car, leading to the child's death. She was charged with felony neglect and involuntary manslaughter but was found not guilty in December 2001. Her case exemplifies absent-minded forgetting and prospective memory failure, illustrating how routine changes, preoccupation, and automatic behavior can combine in a 'perfect storm' of forgetting.

## Key facts

- Kari Engholm was a 35-year-old hospital executive who normally drove her son Eric to daycare but on one occasion also had to drive her daughter Clare to a babysitter. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p85]
- Kari dropped off Eric at daycare but drove directly to work without stopping to leave Clare with the babysitter, never realizing Clare was still in the car. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p85]
- At the end of a ninety-degree day, Kari discovered Clare had died in the back seat of the overheated car. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p85]
- Judge Paul Huscher rendered a verdict of not guilty in December 2001, reasoning that Kari had not acted with reckless disregard for Clare's safety. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p86]
