# Pierre Wack

## Summary

Pierre Wack was a scenario planner at Shell International Group Planning who, along with Ted Newland, made some of the most striking anticipations in modern business history—including predicting the rise of OPEC and the 1970s energy crisis from discernible patterns in 1970. He explained that certain aspects of future events are predetermined and that the real risk is not whether oil prices go high or low, but that they will sucker you into investment and then turn.

## Key facts

- Pierre Wack and Ted Newland of Shell Group Planning predicted the rise of OPEC and the ensuing energy crisis by recognizing patterns of incomes and populations in key oil-producing countries. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p45]
- Wack said certain aspects of future events are predetermined—for example, if there is a storm in the Himalayas, you can confidently predict flooding in the Ganges plain. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p45]
- Wack warned that standard scenario forecasting leads people to plan on medium outcomes and miss the real risk—that prices will sucker you into investment and then turn. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p45]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wack's approach to strategic anticipation was based on identifying predetermined downstream results of events already underway, not psychic prediction.
