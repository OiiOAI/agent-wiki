# The One Push-up Challenge

## Summary

The One Push-up Challenge is the author's personal origin story for the Mini Habits system. On December 28, 2012, unable to motivate himself for a 30-minute workout, Guise applied a creative thinking technique called 'False Faces' and decided to do the opposite: one single push-up. This one push-up led to a 20-minute workout, and the author continued requiring just one push-up per day, eventually building full gym habits and daily writing and reading routines.

## Key facts

- The One Push-up Challenge began on December 28, 2012, when the author could not motivate himself for a planned 30-minute workout. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p15]
- The author got the idea from Michael Michalko's 'Thinkertoys' creative thinking technique called False Faces, which considers the opposite of what you're thinking. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p16]
- The author thought the idea of one push-up was 'pathetic' but tried it after failing to do the 30-minute workout, and ended up completing a 20-minute workout. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p16]
- During the first workout, the author had to set seven micro-goals, using phrases like 'one more' and 'two more' to continue exercising. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p17]
- The author published 'The One Push-up Challenge' as a blog post that became one of his most popular, with people still reporting it helped them exercise consistently. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p17]
- From this one push-up, the author eventually developed the ability to do 16 pull-ups in a row, improved physique, and daily reading and writing habits. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p13]
- Stephen Guise started with one push-up per day for approximately six months, doing only about 1-20 push-ups daily during this period. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p128]
- Before starting the challenge, Guise felt 'above' doing one push-up, believing it was worthless for exercise. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p122]
- The one push-up requirement helped Guise eventually exercise for 30 minutes. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p122]
- After six months, he changed his goal to driving to the gym three times per week, which he credits to the self-discipline built from the push-up challenge. [raw/books/productivity/Mini Habits_ Smaller Habits, Bigger Results - Guise, Stephen.pdf#p128]

## Inferences

- Inference: The One Push-up Challenge demonstrates that the hardest part of any task is starting, and that starting extremely small can lead to natural momentum toward larger goals.
- Inference: The gradual progression from one push-up to gym visits suggests that tiny habits create a foundation of self-efficacy that makes larger changes feel more achievable over time.
