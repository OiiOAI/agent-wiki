# Inferior Colliculus

## Summary

The inferior colliculus is the major midbrain auditory relay where essentially all ascending auditory pathways converge. It contains a tonotopically organized central nucleus and receives excitatory and inhibitory inputs that shape responses to sound location and other features.

## Key facts

- All ascending auditory pathways from the brainstem converge in the inferior colliculus, making it a central hub in the auditory system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p711]
- The central nucleus of the inferior colliculus is tonotopically organized with low frequencies represented dorsolaterally and high frequencies ventromedially. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p711]
- Many neurons in the inferior colliculus are sensitive to both interaural time and intensity differences, encoding sound location in the horizontal plane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p711]
- Persistent inhibition from the dorsal nucleus of the lateral lemniscus in the inferior colliculus may contribute to the precedence effect by suppressing spurious echoes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p711]
- The inferior colliculus projects to both the thalamus and the superior colliculus, serving as both a convergence and branch point in auditory processing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p712]
