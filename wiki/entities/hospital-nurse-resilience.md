# Hospital Nurse Resilience Project

## Summary

Fogg and coach Linda were hired by a major research hospital to address nurse burnout. They trained nurses using Tiny Habits methods via video conferencing, focusing on stress-reducing tiny behaviors that could fit into 12-hour shifts. Key findings included that nurses typically wouldn't drink water during shifts to avoid bathroom breaks, and the training led to statistically significant improvements in resilience measures.

## Key facts

- The aspiration was framed positively as 'help nurses form new habits that make them more resilient' rather than addressing burnout directly. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p287]
- Nurses almost never took breaks, used aggravating old computer software, and typically wouldn't drink any water during 12-hour shifts. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p289]
- Nurses created recipes like: 'After I park at work, I will close my eyes and take three relaxing breaths' and 'After I dock my computer, I will take a sip of water'. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p290]
- Nurses started celebrating each other's successes—a quick clap for a colleague who took a drink of water, a high five after sitting in the break room. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p290]
- The research showed statistically significant improvements in areas including 'I practice stress-reducing habits daily' and 'I recognize when something goes well at work'. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p291]
- Fogg concluded that Tiny Habits was the only realistic path for people who are stressed, pressed for time, and overwhelmed. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p291]
