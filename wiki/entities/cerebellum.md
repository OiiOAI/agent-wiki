# Cerebellum

## Summary

The cerebellum (Latin for 'little brain') is a large, complex structure at the back of the brain under the visual cortex that plays critical roles in motor control and motor learning. It integrates sensory and motor information and is directly connected to the striatum and cortical areas. Henry's cerebellum was greatly reduced in size due to Dilantin medication, yet he still demonstrated some motor learning abilities.

## Key facts

- The cerebellum is directly connected to the striatum and to several areas in the cortex by means of closed circuits. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- Henry's cerebellum was greatly reduced in size due to Dilantin medication, a side effect of his antiepileptic treatment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- Patients with cerebellar degeneration are impaired at sequence learning and cannot use feedback to guide their movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p165]
- Henry showed normal prism adaptation despite marked cerebellar atrophy, though the specific circuits supporting this are unknown. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p166]

## Inferences

- Inference: Residual cerebellar tissue, particularly the deep cerebellar nuclei that transmit information to the cerebellum, may have supported some of Henry's preserved motor functions.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact cerebellar circuits that remained functional in Henry's brain were not determined until postmortem examination.
