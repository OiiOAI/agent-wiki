# Microglia

## Summary

Microglia are the brain's resident immune cells and phagocytes, composing approximately 10% of glial cells in the CNS. Unlike neurons, astrocytes, and oligodendrocytes, microglia do not belong to the neuroectodermal lineage but rather derive from myeloid progenitors in the yolk sac. They colonize the brain early in embryonic development and reside throughout all brain regions. During development, microglia help sculpt neural circuits by engulfing pre- and postsynaptic structures, and in the adult brain, their processes continuously survey the surrounding extracellular environment.

- Following injury and disease, microglia undergo dramatic increases in process motility and changes in morphology and gene expression, serving beneficial roles by phagocytosing debris and bringing immune cells into the CNS.
