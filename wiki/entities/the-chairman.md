# the Chairman

## Summary

The Chairman for the Committee on Analysis of Ideas and Study of Methods at the University of Chicago who took over the Ideas and Methods 251 class when the regular professor fell ill. He represented the Classical/Dialectical tradition and attempted to demonstrate the superiority of dialectic over rhetoric by controlling class discussion. Phaedrus challenged his interpretation of Plato's Phaedrus dialogue, twice trapping the Chairman dialectically. The Chairman's persona was 'overwhelming' but his authority collapsed when his knowledge was shown to be incomplete. The narrator describes him as a 'shepherd' presiding over 'classroom sheep,' part of the 'Church of Reason' that demands inability rather than ability from its followers.

## Key facts

- The Chairman took over the Ideas and Methods 251 class when the regular Professor of Philosophy was ill. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p329]
- The Chairman stuffed an old pipe for nearly half a minute before lighting it, demonstrating habitual behavior. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p330]
- The Chairman claimed Socrates swore to the Gods that his chariot allegory was the Truth, but Phaedrus countered that Socrates had previously stated it was an analogy. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p332]
- The Chairman was unable to refute Phaedrus's textual citation and became speechless, unable to think of a word to say. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p333]
- The Chairman asked Phaedrus for Aristotle's opinion on dialectic, expecting to trap him, but Phaedrus cited Aristotle stating dialectic comes before everything else—trapping the Chairman instead. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p334]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Chairman's inability to survive Phaedrus's challenges suggests Pirsig's view that dialecticians have never confronted a 'living Sophist'—only dead ones in historical texts, where they control the interpretation.
