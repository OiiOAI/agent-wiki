# Catherine Genovese

## Summary

Catherine Genovese was a 28-year-old woman murdered in Kew Gardens, Queens in March 1964 in an attack witnessed by 38 neighbors who failed to call police. Her case became famous as a catalyst for research into the bystander effect, with initial media coverage attributing the failure to urban apathy.

## Key facts

- Genovese's attacker chased and stabbed her three times over a thirty-five-minute period while thirty-eight neighbors watched from their windows without calling police. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p122]
- Only one witness called police, and that call came after Genovese was already dead. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p122]
- The case was initially reported by The New York Times as evidence of urban 'apathy,' a theory later challenged by Latane and Darley's psychological research. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p123]
