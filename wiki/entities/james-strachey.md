# James Strachey

## Summary

James Strachey (1887–1967) was the British psychoanalyst who translated Freud's complete psychological works into English under the general editorship of Anna Freud. His Standard Edition (1953–74), published by the Hogarth Press and the Institute of Psychoanalysis in London, became the canonical English reference for Freud's works for decades. The Revised Standard Edition (2024), edited by Mark Solms, retains Strachey's translations while revising them and adding extensive new editorial apparatus. Strachey's achievement was described by Solms as 'monumental' despite criticisms of his translational choices.

## Key facts

- James Strachey translated Freud's complete psychological works under the general editorship of Anna Freud, in collaboration with Alix Strachey and Alan Tyson, with Angela Richards as editorial assistant. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p8]
- Strachey's General Preface states that the SE was an 'amateur production' by individuals usually engaged in other occupations, without the support of any established academic institution. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p24]
- Strachey aimed to translate Freud as though he were an 'English man of science' of wide education born in the mid-nineteenth century, preferring English scientific conventions over literal German constructions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p25]
- Strachey stated his editorial principle: 'allowing Freud to be his own expositor,' avoiding reference to later commentaries, elaborations, or criticisms from any source. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p23]
- The Standard Edition was made possible by a subscription from the American Psychoanalytic Association, which subscribed in advance for approximately 500 sets before the first volumes were published. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p25]

## Inferences

- Inference: Strachey's choice to exclude all secondary literature from the SE, while limiting editorial apparatus to what could be derived from Freud's own writings, reflects a documentary approach prioritizing Freud's primary texts over interpretive frameworks.
