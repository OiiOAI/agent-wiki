# Raymond Leopold

## Summary

Raymond Leopold was a United States Air Force captain with a Ph.D. in electrical engineering who became one of John Boyd's Acolytes. He joined Boyd's office in 1973 and conducted a detailed budget analysis of the B-1 Bomber that revealed its costs would be more than double the congressional mandate. He was the first to receive Boyd's 'To Be or to Do' speech.

## Key facts

- Leopold was a graduate of the Air Force Academy who herniated a spinal disk and had his flying career ended. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p281]
- He earned his Ph.D. in electrical engineering while serving in the Air Force without assistance. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p281]
- Boyd gave him the 'To Be or to Do' speech and assigned him to analyze the Air Force budget with focus on the B-1. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p282]
- Leopold discovered the B-1 would cost $68 million per copy against a $25 million congressional mandate. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p284]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's protection of Leopold sleeping at his desk, while seemingly permissive, was actually recognition of his dedicated work.
