# Rotator Cuff

## Summary

A web of muscles and connective tissues surrounding the shoulder joint that provides support and enables arm and shoulder movement. The authors use an octopus-in-seashell metaphor to describe how the rotator cuff reaches from within the shoulder blade to steer, guide, position, organize, stabilize, and rotate the humerus.

## Key facts

- The rotator cuff is a web of muscles and connective tissues that support and allow movement of the arm and shoulder. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1340-L1342]
- The rotator cuff can be visualized as an octopus that has swum headfirst into a seashell (the shoulder blade), with its arms reaching out to control the humerus. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1343-L1346]
- Modern posture where shoulders slouch forward and upper back rounds disrupts the rotator cuff function, causing some 'arms' to stretch while others compress. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1353-L1357]
- Poor shoulder range of motion may be a root cause of golf and tennis elbow, hidden because hands and forearms compensate. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L1367-L1370]

## Inferences

- Inference: Restoring proper rotator cuff function through mobilization exercises can improve both shoulder mobility and resolve associated conditions like elbow pain.
