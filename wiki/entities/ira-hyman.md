# Ira Hyman

## Summary

Ira Hyman is a psychologist who successfully implanted false memories of childhood experiences in significant minorities of participants. His research found that 20-40% of participants produced false memories when encouraged to imagine events, with more than half describing them as 'clear' recollections. He linked vivid visual imagery to false memory creation.

## Key facts

- Hyman asked college students about childhood experiences that parents confirmed had or had not happened, finding that 20 to 40% of participants eventually described some memory of false events in later interviews. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p167]
- More than half of participants who produced false memories described them as 'clear' recollections including specific details of the central event. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p167]
- People who produced false memories scored higher on scales measuring vividness of visual imagery than individuals whose recollections were more accurate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p168]
- When Hyman instructed people to try to imagine an event if they could not recall it initially, more false memories resulted compared with allowing participants to sit quietly and think. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p168]
