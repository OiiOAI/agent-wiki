# Elena Bonner

## Summary

Soviet dissident and wife of Andrei Sakharov, who exemplified psychological freedom through her choice to return to internal exile in the USSR rather than remain in the relative political freedom of the West, demonstrating that true autonomy exists within one's psychological functioning rather than in external circumstances.

## Key facts

- In December 1985, Elena Bonner was allowed to leave her house arrest in the USSR to seek medical attention in the West and spent six months undergoing surgery before she returned to her life of imprisonment. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p210]
- Bonner could probably have stayed in the U.S. as a defector, but chose to return to internal exile because sharing life's experiences with her husband Sakharov was the most important thing to her. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p211]
- Elena Bonner was being autonomous; her decision came from what we call an integrated sense of self and was a true choice, an action not controlled by introjects or other pressures. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p211]
- As Bonner put it, 'together... we are still free to be ourselves,' demonstrating that in a system of political control, the Sakharovs displayed greater personal freedom than millions of people whose governments do not oppress them politically. [raw/books/psychology/Why we do what we do _ understanding self - motivation - Edward L_ Deci, Richard Flaste.pdf#p211]
