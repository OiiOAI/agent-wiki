# Elliot

## Summary

A patient with ventromedial prefrontal cortex damage studied by Antonio Damasio. Despite normal intelligence and memory, Elliot exhibited profound deficits in real-life decision-making, social behavior, and emotional processing. He described himself as a conservative, low-risk person yet made disastrous choices. His case provided crucial evidence for the somatic marker hypothesis, demonstrating that knowledge without feeling is insufficient for adaptive behavior.

## Key facts

- Elliot had normal language, intelligence, and memory but could not make appropriate decisions in daily life, losing his job, remarrying unsuccessfully, and losing all his money. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p193]
- Elliot drove calmly over ice patches in freezing rain without concern, then spent thirty minutes deliberating over which of two appointment dates to choose, enumerating reasons for and against each. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p213]
- Elliot performed the gambling task like his real-life decisions, systematically choosing disadvantageous decks and going bankrupt, even though he later knew which decks were bad. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p215]
- Elliot described himself as a conservative, low-risk person despite his disastrous behavioral pattern, demonstrating intact self-awareness without emotional guidance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p215]

## Inferences

- Inference: Elliot's dissociation between knowing what is right and doing what is right suggests that emotional tagging (somatic markers) is necessary for translating knowledge into action.
