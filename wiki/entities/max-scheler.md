# Max Scheler

## Summary

German philosopher (1874-1928) and colleague of Heidegger who explored the importance of value and feeling as constitutive of the phenomenological world. Scheler held that values form a hierarchy - from sensuous values through values of vitality and spiritual values to the holy - and that man is essentially ens amans, a being that loves. Our early experience, he argued, is intersubjective and does not include awareness of self as distinct from other.

## Key facts

- Scheler held that values are not feelings but reach us through the realm of feeling, much as colours reach us through sight - they are a pre-cognitive aspect of the existing world. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p229]
- Scheler argued that our early experience is intersubjective and does not include awareness of self as distinct from other - there is 'an immediate flow of experiences, undifferentiated as between mine and thine'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p229]
- For Scheler, man is essentially ens amans - a being that loves - with the attractive power of love being as fundamental as gravity in the physical universe. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p229]
- Scheler's hierarchy of values progresses from sensuous values through values of vitality (Lebenswerte) and spiritual values (geistige Werte) to das Heilige (the holy). [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p231]

## Inferences

- Inference: Scheler's higher values (wisdom, humility, virtue, courage, love) cannot be grasped by an effort of will and depend on the right hemisphere, whereas left-hemisphere modes are applicable only to values of the useful and pleasurable.
