# Irma (Specimen Dream Patient)

## Summary

The patient whose dream provided Freud's first successful self-analysis and demonstration of dream interpretation. Her dream involved Freud defending himself against accusations of failed treatment.

## Key facts

- The dream took place during summer 1895, after psychoanalytic treatment of a young lady named Irma, whose treatment had ended in partial success with the patient relieved of hysterical anxiety but retaining some somatic symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p106]
- In the dream, Freud reproached Irma for not accepting his 'solution' and she complained of pains in throat, stomach, and abdomen that were not her actual symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p107]
- Analysis revealed that Irma was substituted in the dream with her friend who also suffered from hysterical choking, and with Freud's own wife. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dream's purpose was to exculpate Freud from responsibility for Irma's continued pains by attributing them to organic causes (diphtheria), dirty syringes, and Otto's thoughtless injection.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The full extent of connections in the dream analysis was not fully disclosed, as Freud noted he had not reported everything that occurred to him during interpretation.
