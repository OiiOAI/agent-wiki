# Sewall Wright

## Summary

Sewall Wright (1889-1988) was a geneticist who invented path analysis in the 1920s, creating a direct ancestor of modern causal inference methods. Despite his contributions, Wright's causal methods were underappreciated in statistics and languished for decades.

## Key facts

- Sewall Wright (1889-1988) was a geneticist who invented path analysis in the 1920s. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L66]
- Wright's path analysis was a direct ancestor of the causal methods discussed in The Book of Why. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L66]
- Path analysis was badly underappreciated in statistics and languished for decades. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L67]
- Wright developed path analysis in 1920 to estimate the relative importance of hereditary versus developmental factors in guinea pig coat color variation. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L560-L580]
- Wright showed that 42% of coat color variation was due to heredity and 58% to developmental factors in randomly bred guinea pigs, versus 3% and 92% respectively in inbred families. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L580-L595]
- Wright demonstrated that correlation does not imply causation but that some correlations do imply causation when combined with qualitative causal assumptions. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L560-L575]
- Wright faced criticism from Henry Niles in 1921 and Karlin et al. in 1983 but defended path analysis throughout his career, stating 'There can be no such analysis without a model.'. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L595-L620]
- Wright's birth weight study showed that the apparent 5.66 grams per day growth rate was actually 3.34 grams per day when correctly disentangled from litter size effects. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L620-L650]
- Wright developed path analysis as a method for representing causal relationships using diagrams. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3434-L3434]
- His seminal 1920 paper on path diagrams appeared in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3433-L3433]
- His 1921 paper 'Correlation and causation' provided fuller exposition and the guinea pig birth-weight example. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3435-L3435]
- Wright responded to Karlin's critique in 1983 when he was over ninety years old. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3436-L3436]
- Wright lived long enough to see his biography by Provine (1986) published while he was still alive. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3432-L3432]
- Crow wrote two biographical sketches of Wright in 1982 and 1990 providing useful biographical perspective. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3432-L3432]
- Wright studied the relative importance of heredity and environment in determining the piebald pattern of guinea-pigs. [raw/books/strategy/The_Book_of_Why.epub#L3467-L3467]

## Inferences

- Inference: Wright's underappreciated work represents the 'first step toward causal inference' that 'remained the only step until the 1980s,' explaining why causal methods took so long to develop.
- Inference: Wright's persistent defense of path analysis over six decades anticipated modern causal inference by demonstrating that qualitative causal assumptions combined with data could yield non-obvious quantitative conclusions.
