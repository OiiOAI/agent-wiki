# Coco Chanel

## Summary

Coco Chanel (1883-1971) was a French fashion designer who founded the House of Chanel. Born Gabrielle Chanel in poverty, she rose from a childhood in a convent orphanage to become one of the most influential fashion designers of the 20th century, known for liberating women from corsets and creating timeless styles including the Little Black Dress, Chanel No. 5 perfume, and the cardigan.

## Key facts

- Born Gabrielle Chanel in 1883, her mother died of tuberculosis when she was eleven, and her father abandoned her and her siblings to a convent in central France. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p167]
- At age eighteen she left the convent for boarding school where she was trained as a seamstress. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p168]
- She initially pursued a career in theater under the name Coco, but realized she lacked sufficient talent for the success she desired. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p168]
- She moved to the château of Etienne Balsan, a wealthy man who supported courtesans, where she began wearing his men's clothing. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p169]
- She created an androgynous style using men's open-collared shirts, tweed coats, and straw boater hats, which attracted envy from other women at the château. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p169]
- With encouragement from her lover Arthur Capel, she opened a shop in Deauville selling hats and jersey clothing that became a sensation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p170]
- She created Chanel No. 5 perfume, named after herself with a number rather than a poetic title, packaged in a sleek modernist bottle. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p171]
- The perfume was marketed with a subliminal campaign, filling her Paris store with scent and slipping unlabeled bottles to wealthy clients, creating word-of-mouth buzz. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p172]
- During World War II she stayed in Paris during the Nazi occupation and was later disgraced for her political sympathies, fleeing to Switzerland in self-imposed exile. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p172]
- At age seventy in 1954, she launched a comeback, targeting the American market where her simple, classical styles created a new sensation. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p173]

## Inferences

- Inference: Chanel's childhood deprivation created a persistent restlessness and desire for what she could not have, which later fueled her creative ambitions.
- Inference: Her androgynous fashion designs reflected her discovery that what she truly wanted was the power and freedom that men possessed.
