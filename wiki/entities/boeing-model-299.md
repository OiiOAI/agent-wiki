# Boeing Model 299

## Summary

The Boeing Model 299 was a prototype long-range bomber that crashed during its 1935 test flight, killing two crew members. The crash was attributed to pilot error—forgetting to release a locking mechanism on controls—and led to the development of the pilot's checklist, which enabled safe operation of this complex aircraft.

## Key facts

- In October 1935, the Boeing Model 299 crashed during testing at Wright Air Field, killing pilot Major Ployer P. Hill and another crew member. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p32]
- The crash investigation revealed nothing mechanical was wrong; it was attributed to pilot error because Hill forgot to release a new locking mechanism on elevator and rudder controls. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p33]
- The Model 299 required the pilot to attend to four engines, retractable landing gear, wing flaps, electric trim tabs, and constant-speed propellers—too complex for one person. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p33]
- Test pilots created a pilot's checklist simple enough to fit on an index card, covering takeoff, flight, landing, and taxiing checks. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p33]
- With the checklist, pilots flew the Model 299 a total of 1.8 million miles without one accident, and the army ultimately ordered almost 13,000 aircraft, the B-17. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p33]
