# Ellen J. Langer

## Summary

Ellen J. Langer is a Harvard psychologist and professor who pioneered mindfulness research starting in the 1970s. She is known for the counterclockwise study, research on the illusion of control, decision-making theory, and the concept of mind-body unity as a determinant of health.

## Key facts

- Langer is a professor at Harvard University in the psychology department. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12]
- She conducted the counterclockwise study over 40 years ago, in which elderly men lived as their younger selves for a week in a retrofitted retreat, resulting in measurable physical improvements. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p12]
- Her early work on the illusion of control was the subject of her graduate dissertation at Yale and has been cited thousands of times. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p42-43]
- Her research on mindful noticing showed that a brief intervention—asking people to sort photos by novel psychological categories rather than obvious demographic labels—reduced implicit racial bias on the IAT by half. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p17-18]
- Langer was married secretly at age 16 and again publicly at age 19. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p29-30]
- Langer's mother was diagnosed with breast cancer at 56, experienced spontaneous remission, and died at 57, an experience that shaped her scientific career. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p10]
