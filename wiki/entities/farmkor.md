# FarmKor

## Summary

FarmKor is a disguised company that produces hardware and software for agriculture and food processing, tracking weather and ground chemistry to provide crops with optimal water and nutrients. It was used as the case study to illustrate the Strategy Foundry methodology.

## Key facts

- FarmKor produces and sells hardware and software for use in agriculture and the first stages of food processing. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p272]
- FarmKor products track weather and ground chemistry and try to provide crops with just the right amount of water and nutrients. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p272]
- FarmKor had equipment operating in ten countries and manufactured equipment in four locations around the world. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p272]
- The company went public in Europe in 1998, then through a management buyout in 2001, brought in new investors, and went public again in 2007. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p272]
- The ten challenges identified by the FarmKor Strategy Foundry included Diminished Advantage, Big Players' R&D, Founders' influence, Falling Revenue per Acre, Too Many Parts, Precision Farming trends, Regional Baronies, Less Talent per Acre, Nutrient Substitutes, and High-Tech Start-Ups. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p277-278]
- The key policy adopted was a shift toward serving high-value crops including rapeseed, gourmet mushrooms, saffron, nut trees, apple trees, plum trees, and high-end vineyards. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p281]

## Inferences

- Inference: FarmKor's case demonstrates how the Strategy Foundry process surfaces challenges and leads to focused strategic pivots toward higher-value activities.
