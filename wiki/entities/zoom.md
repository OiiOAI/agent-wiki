# Zoom Video Communications

## Summary

Zoom is a video conferencing company founded in 2011 by Eric Yuan and a team of engineers who left Cisco's WebEx division. Yuan left WebEx after becoming vice president of engineering there, frustrated that the product had not improved over time and that customers were not happy. Zoom was designed to be much easier to install, use, and maintain than WebEx, eliminating the complexities of signing up, opening accounts, and downloading programs. The company's free tier (up to 100 attendees for 40 minutes) and ease of use helped it spread rapidly, with traffic increasing 3,000 percent in May 2020 during COVID-19 lockdowns. Zoom went public and became valued at billions, with 'to Zoom' becoming a verb reflecting its cultural impact.

## Key facts

- Eric Yuan quit Cisco in 2011, taking engineers with him from WebEx to found Zoom. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p197]
- Yuan observed that WebEx customers were unhappy and the software base had not been upgraded since 2010. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p197]
- Zoom was free for up to 100 attendees for 40 minutes, and with COVID-19 lockdowns, traffic increased by 3,000 percent in May 2020. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p198]
- The crux of Zoom's technical challenge was eliminating complexities of signing up, opening accounts, downloading programs, and making video work in any browser regardless of firewall settings. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p197]

## Inferences

- Inference: Zoom's success came from identifying a crux problem (user friction) that competitors had accepted as normal and solving it through focused engineering effort.
