# Toyota

## Summary

Toyota is described as having the most advanced system of management in the world and the most advanced learning organization in history. Founded in the post-WWI economy where it could not compete with American mass production, Toyota pioneered small-batch manufacturing through innovations by Taiichi Ohno and Shigeo Shingo, eventually becoming the world's largest automaker in 2008.

## Key facts

- Toyota faced intense pressure from mass production because the Japanese car market was too small for economies of scale, and capital was not available for massive investments in large machines. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p173]
- Shigeo Shingo created SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Die) to enable smaller batch sizes, reducing changeover times from hours to less than ten minutes by reimagining and restructuring work. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p174]
- Toyota's famous andon cord allows any worker to stop the entire production line as soon as they notice a problem, such as a defect, if it cannot be corrected immediately. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p174]
- Toyota became the world's largest automaker in 2008, having built the most advanced learning organization in history with demonstrated ability to unleash employee creativity and produce innovative products over nearly a century. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p190]
