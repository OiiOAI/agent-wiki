# Behavioral Change Stairway Model

## Summary

The Behavioral Change Stairway Model (BCSM) is a five-stage framework developed by the FBI's Crisis Negotiation Unit that describes how negotiators move counterparts from initial contact through active listening, empathy, rapport, influence, and finally behavioral change.

## Key facts

- The BCSM proposes five stages: active listening, empathy, rapport, influence, and behavioral change. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p87]
- The model's origins trace to psychologist Carl Rogers and his concept of unconditional positive regard. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p87]
- Real change only comes when a therapist (or negotiator) accepts the client as they are, without conditions. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p87]
- The breakthrough moment occurs when unconditional positive regard is established and influence can begin. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p88]

## Inferences

- Inference: The BCSM applies to business negotiations because the psychological environment for real behavioral change is the same whether dealing with hostages, clients, or employees.
