# Vestibular Schwannoma

## Summary

A vestibular schwannoma is one of the most common intracranial tumors, arising from Schwann cells in the vestibular component of cranial nerve VIII. It typically presents with hearing loss as the primary symptom.

## Key facts

- Vestibular schwannoma is one of the most common intracranial tumors, arising from Schwann cells in the vestibular component of cranial nerve VIII. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1032]
- Most patients complain only about hearing loss, as the brain usually adapts to gradual loss of vestibular input from one side. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1032]
- A common tumor of the cerebellopontine angle is the vestibular schwannoma (sometimes erroneously called an acoustic neuroma). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1033]
- A large tumor of the cerebellopontine angle may impair function of nerves VII and VIII and may press on nerve V, causing facial numbness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1033]
