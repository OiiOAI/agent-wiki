# Maggie Bruck

## Summary

Maggie Bruck is a psychologist and expert on children's suggestibility and the reliability of children's testimony. Her research was critical in the Fells Acres daycare case, where she argued that interviewing techniques used with children led them to provide inaccurate reports. Twenty-nine memory researchers signed an amicus brief supporting her interpretation.

## Key facts

- Bruck documented that in Susan Kelley's interviews with Fells Acres children, she never began with open-ended questions like 'What happened?' and instead proceeded directly to specific questions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p175]
- Bruck and Stephen Ceci found that when children were repeatedly asked about events parents indicated had never occurred and encouraged to imagine them, 58% reported detailed recollections of at least one such event. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p176]
- In 1998, Maggie Bruck presented new research to Judge Isaac Borenstein highlighting important differences from earlier work available at the initial Amirault trial, which led him to rule in favor of a new trial. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p179]
