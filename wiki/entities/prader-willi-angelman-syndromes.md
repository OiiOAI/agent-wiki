# Prader-Willi and Angelman Syndromes

## Summary

Prader-Willi syndrome and Angelman syndrome are related imprinting disorders caused by deletions of the same region of chromosome 15q11-13, but differ depending on parental origin of the deletion. Prader-Willi syndrome (maternal deletion) involves mild intellectual disability, hypogonadism, and hypothalamic abnormality causing inability to feel satiated. Angelman syndrome (paternal deletion) involves profound intellectual disability and inappropriately happy demeanor. Both demonstrate genetic imprinting where only one parental allele is expressed.

## Key facts

- Prader-Willi and Angelman syndromes are caused by deletions of the same region of chromosome 15q11-13 but differ depending on parental origin: Prader-Willi involves paternal deletion while Angelman involves maternal deletion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1487]
- Prader-Willi syndrome is associated with mild intellectual disability, hypogonadism, and hypothalamic abnormality resulting in inability to feel satiated from hunger. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1487]
- Angelman syndrome is characterized by profound intellectual disability and inappropriately happy demeanor with frequent laughing and smiling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1487]
- The same genetic deletion produces different phenotypes due to differential patterns of imprinting of paternal and maternal alleles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1487]
- Angelman syndrome has been narrowed to a single gene encoding the E3 ubiquitin ligase enzyme. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1487]
