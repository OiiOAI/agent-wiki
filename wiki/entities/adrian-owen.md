# Adrian Owen

## Summary

Adrian Owen is a British neuroscientist who pioneered the use of fMRI to detect hidden consciousness in vegetative-state patients. In a landmark 2006 Science paper, he showed that a patient clinically diagnosed as vegetative could activate normal brain networks when asked to imagine playing tennis or navigating her home. This work demonstrated that some patients diagnosed as vegetative may be conscious but unable to communicate. He subsequently showed that brain imaging can establish two-way communication with such patients.

## Key facts

- In 2006 Owen published in Science showing that a vegetative-state patient activated normal language networks when listening to sentences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p260]
- When asked to imagine playing tennis, the patient's supplementary motor area activated on and off every 30 seconds as requested. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p261]
- When asked to mentally visit her apartment, she activated spatial navigation networks including parahippocampal gyrus and parietal cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p261]
- Martin Monti and Owen later showed that vegetative patients can use mental imagery to answer yes/no questions with 60-80% accuracy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p264]
- Owen noted that fMRI is impractical for routine clinical use and advocated for simpler EEG-based methods. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p266]
