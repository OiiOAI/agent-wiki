# Nucor Corporation

## Summary

Nucor Corporation is an American steel company that serves as a case study in Fukuyama's book illustrating how mutual trust between workers and management enables economic cooperation. During the 1983-1984 recession, Nucor implemented a policy of shared sacrifice, placing all employees from CEO to maintenance workers on reduced workweeks with corresponding pay cuts, while committing to no layoffs. This trust-based approach generated tremendous esprit de corps and contributed to Nucor becoming a major force in American steel.

## Key facts

- During the 1983-1984 recession, Nucor put all employees from CEO to maintenance worker on a two- or three-day workweek with corresponding pay cuts. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p17]
- Nucor committed to no worker layoffs during the recession. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p17]
- When the economy and company recovered, Nucor enjoyed tremendous esprit de corps that contributed to it becoming a major force in the American steel industry. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p17]
- Nucor's workers were willing to accept severe pay cuts because they believed managers were hurting as well and committed to not laying them off. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p18]
