# Hall and Van de Castle Dream Scoring System

## Summary

The Hall and Van de Castle (HVC) dream scoring system, developed in the 1960s, is the most well-known, best validated, and extensive dream content analysis method. It provides rules for scoring characters, settings, objects, actions, social interactions, successes/failures, and emotions in dreams.

## Key facts

- The HVC system was developed by Calvin Hall and Robert Van de Castle based on analyzing 1,000 dream reports from 200 college students. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p149]
- The system allows scoring of aggressive, friendly, and sexual interactions as a function of who initiated them, whether they were reciprocated, and their severity. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p149]
- The Hall and Van de Castle system includes five categories of dream emotion: happiness, sadness, anger, apprehension, and confusion. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p168]
- DreamBank is an online database of over 20,000 dreams, many already scored according to the HVC system, allowing scientists to search for specific words, phrases, or HVC variables. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p150]
- Using the HVC coding system, researchers have successfully extracted information about personalities, concerns, and activities from dream reports of individuals unknown to them. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p151]
