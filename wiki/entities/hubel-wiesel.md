# Hubel and Wiesel

## Summary

David Hubel (1924-2013) and Torsten Wiesel (1924-2024) were neurophysiologists who pioneered the study of visual cortex organization and experience-dependent plasticity. Their groundbreaking work demonstrated that visual experience during critical periods in early postnatal life is essential for normal development of binocular vision and ocular dominance columns. They received the 1981 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for their discoveries concerning information processing in the visual system.

## Key facts

- Hubel and Wiesel demonstrated that monocular deprivation during early postnatal life permanently shifts ocular dominance in the visual cortex of cats and monkeys. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1260]
- They showed that the structural basis of ocular dominance plasticity involves expansion and contraction of alternating ocular dominance columns representing each eye. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1261]
- Hubel and Wiesel demonstrated that synchrony of activity between inputs from the two eyes promotes cooperation and formation of binocular connections, while asynchronous activity promotes competition and segregation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1264]
