# Periaqueductal Gray

## Summary

The periaqueductal gray (PAG) is a brain structure surrounding the cerebral aqueduct in the midbrain that is hypothesized to be a primary source of the primal SELF and emotional consciousness. It contains representations of all emotional processes including pain, fear, anger, separation distress, and sexual and maternal behavior systems.

## Key facts

- The PAG is hypothesized to be the epicenter of the primordial SELF, containing both visceral and somatic maps of emotional processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p165]
- The PAG elaborates a visceral-type map of the body along with basic neural representations of pain, fear, anger, separation distress, sexual, and maternal behavior systems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p165]
- Extensive PAG damage produces spectacular deterioration of all conscious activities, while damage along the whole length of the PAG is required for global effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p166]
- Lower intensities of electrical stimulation in the PAG arouse animals to a greater variety of coordinated emotional actions than stimulation at any other brain location. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p166]
- The PAG is the most primal source of anguished pain and suffering during stressful circumstances and is where all forms of pain leave strong neuronal footprints as indicated by c-fos labeling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p166]

## Inferences

- Inference: Disruptions of consciousness would most likely occur when anesthetic agents act upon PAG tissues and connected reticular areas of the diencephalon.
- Inference: Consciousness would be compromised most when such agents are placed into PAG tissues up to the septal area.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether PAG tissue is sufficiently high along the neuroaxis to elaborate conscious awareness and intentionality is debated.
