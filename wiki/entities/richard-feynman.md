# Richard Feynman

## Summary

Nobel Prize-winning physicist Richard Feynman emphasized that understanding can only be confirmed by explaining something to others. He famously stated that he could only determine whether he understood something if he could give an introductory lecture on it. His notebooks were not records of his thinking process but the thinking process itself—he did the work on paper.

- Feynman said he could only determine whether he understood something if he could give an introductory lecture on it. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p82]
- The principle is that you must not fool yourself, and you are the easiest person to fool. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p82]
- Feynman stated: They aren't a record of my thinking process. They are my thinking process. I actually did the work on the paper. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p90]
- Feynman could not stand textbooks full of pseudo-explanations and teachers who used artificial examples instead of building on students' prior understanding. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p99]
- Richard Feynman was born in Far Rockaway, New York in 1918 and earned an undergraduate degree in physics from MIT. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p322]
- He shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1965 for his work on quantum electrodynamics. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p322]
- Feynman demonstrated the effect of cold temperatures on rubber O-rings in the Challenger Space Shuttle investigation. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p322]
