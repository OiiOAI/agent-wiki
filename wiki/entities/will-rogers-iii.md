# Will Rogers III

## Summary

Will Rogers III was the commanding officer of the USS Vincennes, an AEGIS cruiser. He was interviewed by Gary Klein about time-pressured decision making and was involved in two contrasting incidents: successfully handling harassing Iranian F-4s without firing, and the controversial shootdown of Iran Air Flight 655.

## Key facts

- Captain Rogers successfully assessed that Iranian F-4s circling near the Vincennes were not preparing to attack, reasoning that the pilots were too vulnerable and visible, and chose to jam their radar rather than fire. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p108]
- Rogers denied a request to fire missiles at what he believed was an Iranian F-14 at twenty nautical miles because the aircraft was not using aggressive radar systems, waiting for more information despite the risk. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p114]
- The USS Vincennes fired two missiles at Iran Air Flight 655 on July 3, 1988, after Captain Rogers concluded the track was a hostile F-14 based on faulty IFF data and reports that the aircraft was descending. [raw/books/strategy/Sources of Power How People Make Decisions by Gary A. Klein .epub.pdf#p110]

## Inferences

- Inference: The two incidents demonstrate that the same person can use mental simulation successfully one time yet meet failure the next, with the critical difference being the accuracy of the data available for simulation.
