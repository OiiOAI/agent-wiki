# Propranolol

## Summary

Propranolol is a beta-blocker medication that crosses the blood-brain barrier and blocks the release of stress-related hormones, thereby impairing the enhanced memory formation typically associated with emotional arousal. It has been investigated as a potential treatment for reducing intrusive memories of trauma both by blocking consolidation and by interfering with memory reconsolidation when administered during memory reactivation.

## Key facts

- Propranolol blocks the usual memory-enhancing effects of emotional arousal by preventing the action of stress-related hormones. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p240]
- A 2018 McGill/Harvard clinical trial found that six weekly sessions of propranolol combined with memory reactivation produced significantly greater PTSD symptom reduction than placebo with reactivation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p244]
- Kindt reported that a single dose of propranolol following traumatic memory reactivation eliminated PTSD symptoms in one patient and significantly reduced symptoms in two of three others in a pilot study. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p243]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: A 2015 study of 18 PTSD patients failed to find effects from a single propranolol/reactivation treatment, suggesting multiple sessions may be necessary.
