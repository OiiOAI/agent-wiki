# Astatotilapia burtoni

## Summary

Astatotilapia burtoni is an African cichlid fish endemic to Lake Tanganyika, used as a model system for studying social behavior and decision-making. Males exist as two reversible, socially controlled phenotypes: reproductively competent dominant (D) males and reproductively incompetent nondominant (ND) males, making it ideal for studying how social context shapes brain and behavior.

## Key facts

- A. burtoni males exist as one of two reversible, socially controlled phenotypes: dominant (D) males that are brightly colored with eye-bar and humeral patch, and nondominant (ND) males that are duller in coloration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p335]
- D males defend territories vigorously, represent 10-30% of the male population, and spend most time courting and spawning with females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p335]
- When a male ascends from ND to D status, hypothalamic GnRH1 mRNA increases rapidly, and GnRH1 neurons increase in volume by eightfold over several days. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p336]
- ND males attend closely to activity in the social scene, assessing when they might gain a territory by defeating a resident male. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p336]
- A. burtoni can perform transitive inference, choosing which male to attack based on observing pairwise fights and inferring implied hierarchy (A > B > C > D > E). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p338]
- Female A. burtoni who saw their preferred male win a fight rarely switched their choice, but those who saw their preferred male lose nearly always switched their choice. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p340]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ability to perform transitive inference in A. burtoni suggests that social rank assessment based on observation alone is evolutionarily conserved.
- Inference: Social ascent upon gaining a territory activates many behavioral, physiological, and molecular processes, allowing ascending males reproductive success.
