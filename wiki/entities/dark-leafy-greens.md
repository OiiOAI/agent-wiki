# Dark Leafy Greens

## Summary

Dark leafy greens are the seventh Genius Food, identified as one of the most important categories of brain-boosting foods. They are low in sugar, packed with vitamins, minerals, and phytonutrients including folate, magnesium, and fiber. These vegetables support the gut microbiome's production of butyrate and contain sulfoquinovose, a sulfur-bound sugar that directly feeds healthy gut bacteria.

## Key facts

- The word folate comes from the Latin word for 'foliage,' making it easy to remember to get it by eating leaves. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p269]
- Nearly 300 enzymes rely on magnesium, which is found at the center of the chlorophyll molecule giving plants their green pigmentation. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p269]
- Magnesium consumption is inadequate for 50 percent of the population. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p269]
- People who ate just two servings of dark leafy greens a day had brains that looked 11 years younger on scans. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p270]
- Dark leafy greens contain sulfoquinovose, a newly discovered sulfur-bound sugar molecule that directly feeds healthy gut bacteria. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p270]
- Dark leafy greens are the number one way to feed gut microbes and extract butyrate for reducing inflammation. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p270]

## Inferences

- Inference: Regular consumption of dark leafy greens may provide measurable cognitive benefits and slow brain aging.
