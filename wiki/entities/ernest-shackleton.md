# Ernest Shackleton

## Summary

Sir Ernest Henry Shackleton (1874–1922) was a British explorer who led the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition aboard the Endurance (1914–1917). After the ship became trapped in Antarctic ice and sank, his extraordinary leadership ensured the survival of all 27 crew members. His ability to monitor and manage group morale under extreme conditions exemplifies the 'healthy narcissist' who reads and regulates the moods of others.

## Key facts

- Shackleton ordered the abandonment of the Endurance in October 1915 after it had been trapped in an ice floe for over eight months. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p90]
- He understood that the men's attitude and morale were the primary factors that would determine life or death. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p94]
- Shackleton hid his own doubts and fears, projecting confidence through body language and humor to infect the crew with the proper spirit. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p90]
- He developed sharp attunement to the men's shifting moods, engaging each person according to their particular psychology—talking science with scientists and poetry with aesthetic types. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p91]
- Shackleton selected crew members for the lifeboat voyage to South Georgia Island based on psychological factors, assigning the grumpy carpenter McNeish to oversee boat safety to keep his mind occupied. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p92]
- When McNeish's spirits sank and he stopped rowing, Shackleton improvised by stopping the boat and ordering hot milk for everyone, sparing McNeish embarrassment. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p93]
- All 27 men survived and were rescued, making it one of the most remarkable survival stories in history. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p94]
- Sir Edmund Hillary reportedly said: 'For scientific leadership give me Scott; for swift, efficient travel, Amundsen; but when you are in a hopeless situation, get down on your knees and pray for Shackleton.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p94]
