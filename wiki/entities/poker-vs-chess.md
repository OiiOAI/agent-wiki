# Poker vs Chess

## Summary

The distinction between poker and chess is a central metaphor in the book. Chess represents decisions with complete information and minimal luck, where outcomes tightly correlate with decision quality. Poker represents real life with hidden information and significant luck, making it difficult to separate skill from chance. Duke argues life is more like poker than chess.

## Key facts

- In chess, there is no hidden information and very little luck—pieces cannot randomly appear or disappear, and if you lose, it must be because there were better moves you didn't make. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L404-L408]
- In poker, you can make the best possible decision at every point and still lose the hand because you don't know what cards will be dealt and revealed. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L412-L415]
- A novice could beat a world champion poker player in a single hand or several hands, but this could never happen in chess. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L418-L421]
- Von Neumann stated that in chess 'there must be a solution, a right procedure in any position,' but 'real games' involve bluffing and deception. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L396-L401]
- Duke argues that 'resulting' is a reasonable strategy for learning in chess but not in poker or life, where uncertainty gives room to deceive ourselves and misinterpret data. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L421-L425]

## Inferences

- Inference: The poker versus chess distinction explains why we struggle with decision-making in life—our brains evolved for quick pattern recognition in survival situations, but life requires dealing with uncertainty and luck that chess doesn't have.
