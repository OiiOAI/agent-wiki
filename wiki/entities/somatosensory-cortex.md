# Somatosensory Cortex

## Summary

The somatosensory cortex is identified by Buzsáki as a misnomer of the outside-in framework because it not only senses but also simulates the body. Evidence includes continued representation of missing body parts causing phantom limb sensations, false body part illusions from the rubber hand experiment, and the ability of somatosensory cortex to form equally direct and prominent motor control pathways. Michael Brecht's body model theory proposes that layer 4 of somatosensory cortex mirrors the entire body, not just sensory afferents, continuously updated by layer 6 inputs.

## Key facts

- The term 'somatosensory' cortex is a misnomer because it not only senses but also simulates the body, most clearly demonstrated by continued representation of missing body parts causing phantom limbs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p99]
- Activation of neurons in the 'somatosensory' cortex is as effective in controlling whisker movements as stimulation of the anatomically designated motor cortex, with direct anatomical projections to the spinal cord present in both rodents and monkeys. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p96]
- Michael Brecht's body model theory proposes that layer 4 of somatosensory cortex mirrors the entire body, continuously updated by layer 6 to layer 4 inputs comparing to an avatar rather than a mere sensory map. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p99]
- The rubber hand illusion demonstrates that illusory touch requires lack of grounding by action—moving one's own hand while the rubber hand is touched prevents the illusion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain from Inside Out - György Buzsáki MD, PhD.pdf#p99]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sensory cortices do not simply serve to veridically 'represent' the external world, as demonstrated by phantom limbs, blindsight, and the ability of blind people to imagine visual scenes.
