# Storythinking

## Summary

Storythinking is the biological use of story to think—the neural mechanism that powers the default mode network, enabling humans to envision alternative worlds and possible tomorrows. Researched by Anna Craft in preschools, storythinking was extended to adults, chimpanzees, mice, and crows, revealing it to be hundreds of millions of years old. It is classified today as a product of imagination, but this is backwards—story is the source of imagination. Storythinking works through causal thinking (reflecting on past events, asking 'why') and counterfactual thinking (imagining future events, asking 'what if'), connected in a feedback loop where diverse whys produce innovative what ifs.

## Key facts

- Anna Craft discovered that children naturally think in story, observing them imagining themselves as dogs digging holes, doctors healing bones, and dinosaurs trampling homes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p45]
- Story evolved millions of years before language, running through ancestors' brains to do what brains do: think. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p45]
- While logic computes what is probable, story creates what is possible—accelerating into the future and acting with initiative in uncertainty without requiring lots of reliable data. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p46]
- The feedback loop connects past (diverse whys) to future (creative what ifs) so that more creatively hypothesizing causes of yesterday produces more dynamic invention of tomorrow. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p47]
- Story is a sequence of actions where one event causes another; storythinking draws on the brain's motor intelligence because moto generates new actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p207]
- Storythinking differs from deduction, interpretation, critical thinking, and logic—it is narrative cognition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p207]
- Simple consciousness is spatial and sensory, reflecting its origins in vision; self-consciousness is temporal and imagined, beginning with awareness of self continuing in time—a mental story of cause → effect. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p207]
- To wake up, becoming aware of the world, is to think like an algorithm; but to become self-aware, discovering personal history and purpose, is to do what AI cannot: think in story. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p207]
- Storythinking enabled Neolithic ancestors to invent tools, plan hunts, and imagine gods. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p208]
- A plan is a path through life; new plans are new stories—if you can invent one, you can invent the other. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p253]
