# Tau Protein

## Summary

Tau is a microtubule-binding protein that stabilizes microtubules and plays a key role in intracellular transport, particularly in axons. In Alzheimer disease, tau becomes hyperphosphorylated, loses its association with normal microtubules (which disassemble), and forms paired helical filaments sequestered in neurofibrillary tangles. Tau aggregation spreading appears to be driven by Aβ accumulation and may occur transsynaptically in a prion-like manner.

## Key facts

- Tau is a microtubule-binding protein normally soluble that plays a key role in intracellular transport, particularly in axons by binding to and stabilizing microtubules. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- In AD, tau becomes hyperphosphorylated, loses association with normal microtubules (which begin to disassemble), and forms paired helical filaments sequestered in neurofibrillary tangles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- Filamentous deposits of hyperphosphorylated tau occur not only in AD but also in frontotemporal dementia, progressive supranuclear palsy, corticobasal degeneration, and chronic traumatic encephalopathy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Mutations in the tau gene cause frontotemporal dementia with Parkinsonism-17 (FTPD17), with tau aggregation and brain atrophy in specific regions in the absence of Aβ deposition. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Progressive symptoms of AD correlate much better with the number and distribution of tangles than with amyloid plaques, and tangles appear first in the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Aβ accumulation appears to drive tau aggregation and spreading in the brain, with evidence from experiments showing Aβ42 injection increases tangles in nearby neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Mice expressing both mutant APP and mutant tau develop much worse tau pathology than mice with either mutation alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Tau aggregates can spread from cell to cell in a prion-like manner, possibly transsynaptically, which may be interruptible with antibodies directed against tau. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Impairments in axonal transport from tau pathology compromise synaptic stability and trophic support. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]

## Inferences

- Inference: Tau pathology correlates more strongly with cognitive decline than Aβ plaques, suggesting that while Aβ may initiate disease, tau aggregation and spreading are major contributors to neurodegeneration.
