# Anne Sexton

## Summary

Anne Sexton (1928-1974) was an American poet who was a friend of Sylvia Plath. Like Plath, she struggled with mental illness and eventually committed suicide using carbon monoxide from a car engine in her garage.

## Key facts

- Anne Sexton was a preternaturally gifted poet who struggled with depression, alcoholism, and multiple suicide attempts throughout her life. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p164-165]
- She chose suicide by carbon monoxide from car exhaust, which she considered the 'woman's way out'—painless and leaving the body unmarked. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p167-168]
- She committed suicide in October 1974, just before U.S. regulations requiring catalytic converters (which reduce carbon monoxide emissions) on new cars took effect in 1975. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p167-168]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gladwell uses Sexton as another example of coupling—her chosen method of suicide happened to be available at that moment but became far less lethal within a year due to automotive regulations.
