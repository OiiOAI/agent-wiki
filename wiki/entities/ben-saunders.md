# Ben Saunders

## Summary

Ben Saunders is a British polar explorer and ultra-endurance athlete who completed the longest human journey ever recorded: an 1,800-mile round-trip on foot from Ross Island to the South Pole and back, taking 108 days. He emphasizes that curiosity journeys require meticulous preparation, deliberate practice, and breaking goals into manageable milestones.

## Key facts

- Saunders and Tarka L'Herpiniere completed an 1,800-mile round-trip journey on foot from Ross Island to the South Pole in 105 days, the longest human journey ever recorded. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p119-122]
- Saunders describes the journey as going a thousand kilometers further than anyone had ever walked before in these conditions, a step into the unknown. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p120]
- Saunders emphasizes that Antarctic preparation requires creating safety nets including aircraft, communications, and position-sharing capabilities. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p121]
- Saunders uses 90-minute walking intervals with breaks as mental milestones to maintain motivation during difficult stretches. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p139]
- Saunders states that planning must account for unpredictable environments and requires flexibility. [raw/books/productivity/Purposeful Curiosity_ the Power of Asking - Constantine Andriopoulos.pdf#p130]
