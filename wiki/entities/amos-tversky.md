# Amos Tversky

## Summary

Amos Tversky was an Israeli cognitive psychologist and mathematician who collaborated closely with Daniel Kahneman for 14 years, co-founding the heuristics and biases research program and Prospect Theory. Renowned for his brilliance and humor, Tversky died in 1996 at age 59. He was considered by many acquaintances to be the most intelligent person they had ever met.

## Key facts

- Tversky was described as brilliant, articulate, and charismatic, with a strong ability to remember and use jokes to explain ideas. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p11]
- Tversky died in 1996 at age 59. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p18]
- Kahneman and Tversky developed a routine of spending most workdays together, often taking long walks. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p13]
- Tversky was more logical and theory-oriented, while Kahneman was more intuitive and rooted in perceptual psychology. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p13]
- Tversky recognized the relevance of modern psychophysics to the question of how people value wealth changes, immediately grasping that the value function should be defined over gains and losses rather than final wealth states, a crucial insight that Kahneman had not yet articulated. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p360]
- Tversky introduced the 'Asian Disease' problem in a lecture to public health professionals, demonstrating that even experts are susceptible to framing effects: presenting outcomes as '200 people saved' versus '400 people will die' produces different risk preferences from the same objective data. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p475]
- Tversky introduced the work of von Neumann and Morgenstern to Kahneman, presenting it as celebrated, and also introduced the famous challenge posed by Maurice Allais—a test case that both highlighted the limitations of expected utility theory and motivated the development of Prospect Theory. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p405]
