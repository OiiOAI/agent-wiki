# Octopus

## Summary

Octopuses are cephalopod mollusks with large, complex nervous systems containing approximately 500 million neurons. They have evolved from shelled ancestors to soft-bodied predators with remarkable intelligence, distributed nervous systems, and sophisticated behaviors including tool use, play, and individual recognition of humans.

## Key facts

- A common octopus has about 500 million neurons in its body, roughly in the range of dogs and smaller mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p43]
- Octopuses have lost their shells entirely, enabling them to squeeze through holes about the size of their eyeballs and transform their body shape almost indefinitely. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p40]
- The majority of an octopus's neurons are found in their arms—nearly twice as many as in the central brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p56]
- Octopuses can recognize individual human beings and can do this even when the humans are wearing identical uniforms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p47]
- Octopuses have been observed turning off aquarium lights by squirting water at the bulbs to short-circuit the power supply. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p47]
- Each sucker on an octopus's arm may have 10,000 neurons to handle taste and touch. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p56]
- Even an arm that has been surgically removed can perform various basic motions, like reaching and grasping. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p56]
- The oldest possible octopus fossil dates from 290 million years ago, with clearer evidence around 164 million years ago. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p40]
- There are about 300 known octopus species, ranging from less than an inch to the giant Pacific octopus weighing 100 pounds and spanning twenty feet. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p40]

## Inferences

- Inference: The octopus represents a 'different embodiment' that does not fit standard embodied cognition theories, because it lacks fixed body shape, joints, and natural angles that other theories rely upon.
- Inference: The octopus's large nervous system may have evolved initially to solve coordination problems of controlling a soft, multi-armed body, with intelligence arising as a byproduct.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether octopuses experience REM-like sleep remains unclear, though cuttlefish appear to have REM sleep.
