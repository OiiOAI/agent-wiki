# Hippocampus

## Summary

The hippocampus is a medial temporal lobe structure critical for explicit (declarative) memory, including episodic and spatial memory. Information flows through the trisynaptic circuit: entorhinal cortex → dentate gyrus granule cells → CA3 pyramidal cells → CA1 pyramidal cells via Schaffer collaterals. CA1 neurons receive both direct and indirect (trisynaptic) inputs from entorhinal cortex. The hippocampus is the primary site of long-term potentiation (LTP) studies and spatial memory formation, as demonstrated by the Morris water maze and radial arm maze tasks.

## Key facts

- The trisynaptic pathway consists of three sequential excitatory connections: perforant path (entorhinal cortex layer II → dentate gyrus granule cells), mossy fiber pathway (granule cells → CA3 pyramidal cells), and Schaffer collateral pathway (CA3 → CA1 pyramidal cells). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1542]
- CA1 pyramidal neurons receive cortical information through two parallel pathways: a direct pathway from layer III entorhinal cortex neurons and an indirect trisynaptic pathway, both termed perforant pathways. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1541]
- Spatial memory depends on intact hippocampal LTP; NMDA receptor blockade in the hippocampus impairs learning of the hidden-platform (contextual) water maze but not the visible-platform version. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1553]
- The hippocampus and nearby structures in the inner parts of the temporal lobe are especially pertinent to the sin of transience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p39]
- Patient H.M. had the inner parts of his temporal lobe removed bilaterally in 1953, resulting in profound inability to form new memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p40]
- The hippocampus is among the earliest and hardest-hit structures in Alzheimer's disease, explaining patients' difficulty remembering recent experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p41]
- Levels of activity in the hippocampus predict subsequent remembering and forgetting during memory encoding. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p44]
