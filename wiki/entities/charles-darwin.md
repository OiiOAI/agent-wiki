# Charles Darwin

## Summary

Charles Darwin (1809-1882) was the English naturalist who developed the theory of evolution by natural selection in 1859, inspired by his childhood reading of Shakespeare and his encounter with Thomas Malthus's population theory. His concept of "Divergence of Character" — that every child differs from its siblings, creating variation upon which natural selection acts — became foundational to evolutionary biology.

## Key facts

- Darwin proposed the theory of evolution by natural selection in 1859. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p108]
- Darwin recalled that as a schoolboy he "used to sit for hours reading the historical plays of Shakespeare". [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p108]
- Darwin named his key evolutionary rule "Divergence of Character," recognizing that every child differs from its siblings with unique characteristics. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p110]
- The theory was "disproven" in the 1870s by Lord Kelvin and by the late nineteenth century was viewed as untenable by most scientists. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p108]
- Even Alfred Russel Wallace forsook Darwin's theory, declaring in 1889 that intellectual and moral faculties "could not have been so developed" by natural selection. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p108]
