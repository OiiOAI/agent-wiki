# Genentech

## Summary

Genentech (short for Genetic Engineering Technologies) was founded in 1976 by Robert Swanson and Herbert Boyer. It was the first biotechnology company focused on commercializing genetically engineered proteins for medical purposes. Starting with $1000 each, they proved recombinant DNA could produce medically important proteins including human insulin.

## Key facts

- In 1976, Robert Swanson, a twenty-eight-year-old venture capitalist, persuaded Herbert Boyer to join forces to form Genentech, the first biotechnology company focused on commercializing genetically engineered proteins. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p329]
- Swanson and Boyer each contributed $500 to the venture; the company is now worth about $20 billion. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p329]
- In 1977, Boyer developed gene-cloning methods that allowed synthesis of large amounts of somatostatin, establishing that recombinant DNA could produce medically important drugs. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p330]
- By 1980, Genentech successfully produced human insulin, demonstrating the practical usefulness of genetic engineering for medicine. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p332]
