# Robert Trivers

## Summary

Robert Trivers is a professor of anthropology at Rutgers University who developed the theory of reciprocal altruism and contributed understanding of how emotions mediate between intuitions and behavior in social exchange.

## Key facts

- Trivers explained reciprocal altruism: cooperation occurs when individuals can expect future returns, requiring ability to recognize individuals, remember favors, and evaluate cost-benefit equivalence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p94]
- Trivers proposed that species practicing reciprocal altruism evolved mechanisms to identify cheaters, otherwise the behavior could not have survived. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p94]
- Trivers helped establish that parental investment determines mating strategies, with the sex having higher investment (usually females in mammals) being choosier about mates. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p95]
- Robert Trivers wrote the foreword to the first edition of The Selfish Gene in 1976. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p14]
- Trivers' ideas dominate Chapters 8, 9, 10, and 12 of The Selfish Gene. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p14]
- In the foreword, Trivers announced his theory of the evolution of self-deception. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p14]
- Trivers is one of four named intellectual heroes of the book, along with W.D. Hamilton. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p14]
- Dawkins describes Trivers' foreword as a beautifully crafted introduction to the book. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p14]
- Trivers' papers of the early 1970s were among the most important inspirations for writing The Selfish Gene, with his ideas especially dominating Chapter 8. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p353]
- Trivers wrote in Social Evolution that he was unconsciously identifying with Arthur, a baboon: 'Arthur was a superb young male in his prime' in describing his excitement observing rival males. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p353]
- Trivers' 1974 paper on parent-offspring conflict has stood the test of time; more detailed mathematical and genetic models have confirmed his verbal arguments follow from accepted Darwinian theory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p353]
- With Hare, Trivers proposed that hymenopteran workers should prefer to rear sisters over brothers because sisters are more closely related, predicting a 3:1 female-biased sex ratio. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p372]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Alexander and Sherman disputed whether the 3:1 ratio fits well, preferring an alternative explanation for female-biased sex ratios.
