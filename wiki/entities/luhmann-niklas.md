# Niklas Luhmann

## Summary

Niklas Luhmann (1927–1998) was a German sociologist who developed the slip-box (Zettelkasten) note-taking system that enabled extraordinary productivity. A brewer's son who went to law school, he entered academia by submitting handwritten notes to sociologist Helmut Schelsky, who hired him as a professor at the newly founded University of Bielefeld in 1968 despite his lack of formal qualifications. He completed his doctoral and habilitation theses in under a year using his slip-box. Over 30 years he published 58 books and hundreds of articles across sociology, law, politics, economy, art, education, epistemology, and religion. His final work was the two-volume 'The Society of Society' (1997). He repeatedly stated he never forced himself to do anything he did not feel like, attributing his productivity entirely to the slip-box.

- Luhmann was born the son of a brewer, went to law school, and chose to be a public servant to avoid working for multiple clients. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p18]
- He entered academia by submitting handwritten notes to Helmut Schelsky, who then hired him as professor of sociology at the University of Bielefeld despite his lack of formal qualifications. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p18-19]
- He completed his doctoral thesis and habilitation thesis in less than a year using his slip-box, while simultaneously taking classes in sociology. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p19]
- He famously stated his research project would be 'theory of society, duration 30 years, costs zero,' and delivered the two-volume 'The Society of Society' (1997) almost exactly on schedule. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p19]
- Over 30 years he published 58 books and hundreds of articles; about half a dozen more books were published posthumously from nearly finished manuscripts. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p19]
- He wrote on law, politics, economy, communication, art, education, epistemology, and love, with many books becoming classics in their respective fields. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p19]
- He stated: 'I only do what is easy. I only write when I immediately know how to do it. If I falter for a moment, I put the matter aside and do something else.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p20]
- His standard answer to questions about his productivity from 1985 onward was: 'I, of course, do not think everything by myself. It happens mainly within the slip-box.'. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p20]
- Sociologist Johannes F.K. Schmidt concluded after extensive research that Luhmann's productivity could only be explained by his unique working technique (Schmidt 2013, 168). [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p20]

## Inferences

- Inference: Luhmann's claim that he 'never forced himself' suggests the slip-box method generates intrinsic motivation by ensuring every writing session starts from existing connections rather than from a blank slate, eliminating the need for willpower to initiate work.
