# They Saw a Game (Dartmouth-Princeton Study)

## Summary

A landmark 1954 psychology study by Albert Hastorf and Hadley Cantril using the November 23, 1951 Dartmouth-Princeton football game as a natural experiment. The game was dirty and penalty-laden; Princeton won 13-0. Students from each school watched the same film and completed questionnaires about infractions. The results showed that Princeton students saw Dartmouth commit twice as many flagrant penalties and three times the mild penalties as Princeton, while Dartmouth students saw each team commit equal infractions—demonstrating that 'we do not simply react to a happening' but behave according to what we bring to the occasion.

## Key facts

- The Dartmouth-Princeton game took place on November 23, 1951 at Princeton's Palmer Stadium; Princeton won 13-0. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L680-L682]
- The game was violent: Dartmouth received 70 yards in penalties, Princeton 25; a Princeton player was kicked, and two Dartmouth players broke legs. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L682-L688]
- Princeton star Dick Kazmaier left with a concussion and broken nose but returned for the final play and later won the Heisman Trophy. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L688-L692]
- Princeton students watching the film saw Dartmouth commit twice as many flagrant penalties and three times the mild penalties as Princeton. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L695-L698]
- Hastorf and Cantril concluded that we behave according to what we bring to the occasion, not simply reacting to events. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L698-L702]
