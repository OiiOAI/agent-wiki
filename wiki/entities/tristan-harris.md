# Tristan Harris

## Summary

A former Google engineer who became Google's first 'design ethicist' and later left to become a prominent critic of the tech industry's attention-hijacking practices. He was featured in the Netflix documentary 'The Social Dilemma' and co-founded the Center for Humane Technology.

## Key facts

- Tristan Harris argued that the attention crisis is 'not your fault — it's by design. Your distraction is their fuel.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p123]
- While at Google, Harris created a slide-deck warning colleagues about the company's impact on attention, titled 'I'm concerned about how we're making the world more distracted.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p124]
- He testified before the U.S. Senate stating 'I failed because companies don't currently have the right incentive to change.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p128]
- Harris concluded that 'No matter where you start [on YouTube], you end up more crazy' due to the platform's radicalizing recommendation algorithm. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p148]
- He described the algorithms as 'debasing the soil of society' and warned they are 'destroying our sense-making at a time when we need it the most.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p153]

## Inferences

- Inference: Harris's critique emphasizes that the problem is not technology itself but the deliberate design choices made by tech companies operating under surveillance capitalism incentives.
