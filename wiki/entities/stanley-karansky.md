# Stanley Karansky

## Summary

A ninety-year-old physician who participated in brain exercises developed by Merzenich's team through Posit Science. Born in 1916 in New York City, he served as an anesthesiologist, then retrained as a family practitioner, and after retiring has maintained an intellectually and physically active lifestyle including amateur astronomy and rock collecting.

## Key facts

- Stanley Karansky was born in 1916 in New York City and attended Duke University medical school. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p249]
- He served as a medical officer in the infantry in the European theater for almost four years during World War II. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p249]
- He practiced as an anesthesiologist until age seventy, then retrained as a family doctor and practiced for ten more years until age eighty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p249]
- At age ninety, he completed brain exercises developed by Merzenich's team through Posit Science, working on auditory memory exercises for an hour and a quarter, three times a week, for three months. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p249]
- He reported improvements in alertness, driving alertness, conversational ease, and handwriting after the exercises. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p250]
- He experienced ventricular fibrillation in 1982 at age 65, 'literally died on the sidewalk,' and was revived with CPR and defibrillation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p251]
- At age 81, he learned Russian and then traveled to Antarctica on a Russian scientific vessel. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p252]
- He maintains physical fitness with muscle exercises on CYBEX machines and 25-35 minutes of cycling three times per week. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p250]
- He describes himself as a lifelong self-educator who constantly explores new subjects including astronomy, rock collecting, history, and puzzles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p250]

## Inferences

- Inference: Karansky's case demonstrates that elderly individuals can experience cognitive benefits from targeted brain exercises, and that maintaining an active lifestyle with diverse intellectual pursuits may support brain health in aging.
