# W. E. B. Du Bois

## Summary

W. E. B. Du Bois (1868-1963) was a pioneering sociologist and civil rights leader best known for his founding and leadership roles in civil rights organizations including the NAACP. Born in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, he earned a Ph.D. from Harvard with study at the University of Berlin, where he encountered thinkers including Max Weber. Within sociology, Du Bois is known for "The Philadelphia Negro" (1899), a pioneering ethnography of Philadelphia's seventh ward. His major theoretical concepts include "double consciousness" (a sense of "two-ness" among African Americans), "the veil" (creating separation between races), and his overarching interest in the "race idea." His autobiographical works like "The Souls of Black Folk" (1903) developed theoretical ideas in the context of reflections on his own life. He became increasingly radical over his life, eventually joining the Communist Party, and died in Ghana in 1963.
