# Purkinje cells

## Summary

Purkinje cells are the sole output neurons of the cerebellar cortex. Their cell bodies form a single sheet in the Purkinje cell layer, and their fan-like dendrites extend into the molecular layer. Each Purkinje neuron receives input from only a single climbing fiber but is contacted by axons from 200,000 to 1 million granule cells through parallel fibers. Purkinje cell axons project to deep nuclei or vestibular nuclei where GABAergic output has inhibitory effects. They generate two types of spikes: simple spikes driven by mossy fiber input through parallel fibers, and complex spikes driven by climbing fiber input.

## Key facts

- Purkinje cells are 50 to 80 µm in diameter and their fan-like dendrites extend upward into the molecular layer where they receive inputs from climbing fibers and interneurons. - Each Purkinje neuron receives synaptic input from only a single climbing fiber but is contacted by axons from 200,000 to 1 million granule cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- Purkinje cell axons conduct the entire output of the cerebellar cortex, projecting to deep nuclei or vestibular nuclei where GABA has an inhibitory action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1018]
- In awake animals, Purkinje neurons emit a steady stream of simple spikes with spontaneous firing rates as high as 100 per second even when an animal is sitting quietly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1019]
- Purkinje neurons can fire at rates as high as several hundred spikes per second during active eye, arm, and face movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1019]

## Inferences

- Inference: The massive convergence of granule cell input onto Purkinje cells allows the mossy fiber-parallel fiber pathway to encode stimulus magnitude and duration through firing rate.
- Inference: The spontaneous high firing rate even at rest suggests Purkinje cells maintain a tonic inhibitory influence on downstream targets that can be modulated by sensory inputs.
