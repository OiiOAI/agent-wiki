# Gustav Fechner

## Summary

Gustav Fechner extended Weber's law to describe the relationship between stimulus strength and sensation intensity, proposing Fechner's law (I = K log(S/S₀)) in 1860. His development of quantitative psychophysical methods remains influential despite the eventual rejection of his assumption about summation of just noticeable differences.

## Key facts

- Fechner extended Weber's law to describe the relationship between stimulus strength (S) and sensation intensity (I) as I = K log(S/S₀), published in 1860. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p502]
- Fechner's assumption that sensation intensity could be equated with the sum of equal increments in just noticeable differences turned out to be incorrect, though his law was accepted for nearly a century. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
- Fechner developed three widely used psychophysical methods: method of constant stimuli, method of limits, and method of adjustment or reproduction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p503]
