# Stephen Luby

## Summary

Stephen Luby is a public health researcher who led a landmark CDC/HOPE study in Karachi slums that demonstrated how simple soap distribution could dramatically reduce childhood illness and death. The 2005 Lancet study showed 52 percent reduction in diarrhea and 48 percent reduction in pneumonia among children in test neighborhoods.

## Key facts

- Stephen Luby was a CDC researcher in Pakistan who conducted a study on child health in Karachi slums with 4 million people living in crowded, squalid conditions. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p71]
- Luby partnered with HOPE (a charitable organization) and Procter & Gamble to study whether antibacterial Safeguard soap could reduce illness. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p72]
- Field workers distributed soap weekly with instructions to use it in six situations: daily body washing, after defecation, after wiping infants, before eating, before food preparation, and before feeding. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p72]
- Results showed 52 percent reduction in diarrhea, 48 percent reduction in pneumonia, and 35 percent reduction in impetigo compared to control neighborhoods. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p73]
- Plain soap proved just as effective as antibacterial soap, and households already used an average of two bars per week—suggesting the intervention worked by making soap use systematic rather than simply providing soap. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p73]

## Inferences

- Inference: The study's success came from combining soap provision with behavior change instructions, making the intervention more than just a product distribution.
