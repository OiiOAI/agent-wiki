# Saul Kassin

## Summary

Saul Kassin is a psychologist whose research on false confessions demonstrated that under the right conditions, most people can be induced to confess to acts they did not commit. His experiments showed that approximately 70% of college students signed false confessions and 35% produced detailed false recollections of events that never occurred.

## Key facts

- Kassin's experiments showed that college students falsely accused of hitting the Alt key on a computer: nearly 70% eventually signed a false confession and 35% produced detailed false recollections of how they made the error. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p165]
- The effect was particularly striking in the group that typed quickly and heard a confederate 'witness' back up the experimenter: all of them signed the confession. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p165]
- Kassin distinguishes 'internalized false confessions'—where people mistakenly believe they committed a crime—from coerced-compliant false confessions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p184]
