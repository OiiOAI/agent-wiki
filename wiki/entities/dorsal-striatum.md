# Dorsal Striatum

## Summary

The dorsal striatum, comprising the caudate nucleus and dorsal putamen, receives dopaminergic input from substantia nigra pars compacta and is implicated in habit learning and action-selection policies. Lesion studies indicate it implements an Actor-like function for learning stimulus-response associations. fMRI studies show prediction-error correlates specific to instrumental (but not Pavlovian) learning in this region.

## Key facts

- Dopamine signal reported by dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra pars compacta to dorsal striatal target areas is used to learn an action-selection policy (as in the actor). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p328]
- O'Doherty et al. (2004) showed correlates of reward-prediction errors are seen in dorsal striatum only in instrumental tasks, not in yoked Pavlovian tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p329]
- Dorsolateral striatum lesions in rats abolish the onset of habitual behavior, rendering lesioned animals permanently goal-directed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p381]
