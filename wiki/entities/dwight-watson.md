# Dwight Watson

## Summary

Dwight Watson was a North Carolina tobacco farmer who, in March 2003, drove his jeep towing a John Deere tractor into the pond between the Washington Monument and the Vietnam Veterans Memorial, threatening to blow it up with claimed 'organophosphate' bombs to protest reduced tobacco subsidies. Voss led the negotiation, which resolved after 48 hours when the team identified Watson's Christian worldview and referenced 'the Dawn of the Third Day' (Jesus's resurrection), leading Watson to surrender. No explosives were found.

## Key facts

- In March 2003, Dwight Watson drove his tractor into the National Mall reflecting pool and threatened to blow it up to protest government policies affecting tobacco farmers. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p196]
- Watson was a veteran (military police in the 82nd Airborne) who said he would surrender if reinforcements didn't arrive within three days, providing normative leverage based on his military honor code. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p198]
- FBI agent Winnie Miller identified Watson as a devout Christian, leading Voss to reference 'the Dawn of the Third Day,' which prompted Watson to surrender after a 48-hour standoff. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p199]

## Inferences

- Inference: Watson's desire for national attention for his cause (positive leverage) combined with his desire to live (allowing him to end with honor) created the opening for resolution.
