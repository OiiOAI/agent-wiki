# H.M. (Henry Molaison)

## Summary

Henry Molaison (1926-2008), known by his pseudonym H.M., was a patient who underwent bilateral surgical removal of the medial temporal lobe (including hippocampus, amygdala, and adjacent structures) in 1953 for treatment of epilepsy. His profound and specific anterograde amnesia, combined with preserved short-term memory and ability to learn procedural tasks, revolutionized understanding of memory systems and established the critical role of the medial temporal lobe in forming new explicit memories.

## Key facts

- H.M. underwent bilateral removal of the hippocampal formation, amygdala, and parts of the multimodal association area of the temporal cortex in 1953 as treatment for epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1494]
- H.M. retained normal working memory (for seconds to minutes) but was unable to transfer new information from working memory into long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1495]
- H.M. retained long-term memory for events that had occurred before the operation, including his name, former job, and childhood events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1495]
- Despite anterograde amnesia, H.M. could learn skilled movements like mirror-tracing and showed improvement comparable to healthy subjects while having no conscious memory of the learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1453]
- H.M.'s case provided the first clear link between memory and the medial temporal lobe, including the hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1495]

## Inferences

- Inference: The dissociation between H.M.'s preserved procedural learning and absent declarative memory formation demonstrated that different neural systems support these two forms of memory, fundamentally reshaping neuroscience understanding of memory modularity.
