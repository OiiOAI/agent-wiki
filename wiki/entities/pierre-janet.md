# Pierre Janet

## Summary

Pierre Janet (1859-1947) was a French psychologist and neurologist whose work on hysteria significantly influenced Breuer and Freud. Janet proposed that splitting of personality rests on an innate psychological weakness ('insuffisance psychologique')—an inadequate capacity for synthesis. He described the 'restriction of the field of consciousness' in hysterical patients, analogous to restriction of the visual field, where every idea takes possession of their limited mental activity. Breuer criticized this view, arguing that hysterical patients appear weak-minded because their mental activity is divided and only part of its capacity is at the disposal of conscious thought. Freud also drew on Janet's work while developing his own concepts of the unconscious.

## Inferences

- Inference: Janet's emphasis on synthesis and consciousness anticipates later developments in structural models of the mind, while Breuer's critique foreshadows the psychoanalytic focus on dynamic unconscious processes.
- Inference: Freud suggests Janet unnecessarily forfeited much credit by retreating from his early recognition of the unconscious as a real psychological phenomenon.
