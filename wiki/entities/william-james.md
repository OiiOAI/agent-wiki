# William James

## Summary

William James (1842–1910) was the founding father of American psychology. His 1890 book 'The Principles of Psychology' is cited for his definition of attention and his observation that consciousness is only a 'thin veneer' over unconscious processes. He noted that we constantly overestimate the role of consciousness, and described consciousness as a 'stream' of loosely connected thoughts.

## Key facts

- William James defined attention as 'the taking possession by the mind, in clear and vivid form, of one out of what seem several simultaneously possible objects or trains of thought'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p34]
- James noted 'we must never take a person's testimony...that he has felt nothing, as proof positive that no feeling has been there'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p71]
- James described consciousness as the 'stream of consciousness'—an uninterrupted flow of loosely connected thoughts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p27]
- William James proposed that if one abstracted all feelings of bodily symptoms from a strong emotion, nothing would remain behind and a cold, neutral state of intellectual perception would be all that is left. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p149]
- James famously asked: 'Can one fancy the state of rage and picture no ebullution in the chest, no flushing of the face, no dilatation of the nostrils?'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p149]
- James stated: 'Every object that excites an instinct excites an emotion as well.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Descartes' error _ emotion, reason, and the human brain - Antonio R_ Damasio,pdf.pdf#p150]
