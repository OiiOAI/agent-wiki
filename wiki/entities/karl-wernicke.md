# Karl Wernicke

## Summary

Karl Wernicke (1848-1905) was a German neurologist who in 1876 published a seminal paper describing receptive aphasia, a failure of language comprehension distinct from Broca's expressive aphasia. He formulated the first coherent neural model of language with separate motor and sensory programs for language processing.

## Key facts

- Wernicke described a new type of aphasia characterized by failure of comprehension rather than speech production, with patients producing grammatical but senseless sentences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Wernicke's area is located in the posterior part of the cerebral cortex where the temporal lobe meets the parietal lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Wernicke formulated the first coherent neural model for language, proposing that language involves separate motor and sensory programs governed by distinct cortical regions connected via the arcuate fasciculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p17]
- Wernicke proposed the principle of distributed processing, suggesting that complex cognitive functions result from interconnections between several functional sites.
