# Karl Wernicke

## Summary

Karl Wernicke (1848-1905) was a German neurologist who in 1876 published a seminal paper describing receptive aphasia, a failure of language comprehension distinct from Broca's expressive aphasia. He formulated the first coherent neural model of language with separate motor and sensory programs for language processing.

## Key facts

- Wernicke described a new type of aphasia characterized by failure of comprehension rather than speech production, with patients producing grammatical but senseless sentences. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Wernicke's area is located in the posterior part of the cerebral cortex where the temporal lobe meets the parietal lobe. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p16]
- Wernicke formulated the first coherent neural model for language, proposing that language involves separate motor and sensory programs governed by distinct cortical regions connected via the arcuate fasciculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p17]
- Wernicke proposed the principle of distributed processing, suggesting that complex cognitive functions result from interconnections between several functional sites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p17]
- In 1876, Wernicke described another type of aphasia characterized by failure of comprehension rather than speech. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p63]
- Wernicke's patients could form words but could not understand language, unlike Broca's patients who could understand but not speak. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p63]
- The locus of Wernicke's aphasia was different from Broca's: the lesion occurred in the posterior cortex where the temporal lobe meets the parietal and occipital lobes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p63]
- Wernicke proposed that more complex cognitive functions result from interconnections between several functional sites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p63]
- He was the first to advance the idea of distributed processing, now a central tenet of neural science. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p63]
- His neural model for language correctly predicted a third type of aphasia—conduction aphasia—resulting from disconnection between receptive and expressive zones. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p63]
