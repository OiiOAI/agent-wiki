# Milton Erickson

## Summary

Milton Erickson (1901–1980) was a pioneering American psychiatrist who became one of the most influential psychologists of the twentieth century. After developing polio at age 17 that left him almost completely paralyzed, he discovered the 'second language' of nonverbal communication by observing his sisters. This experience transformed him into a master reader of human behavior, founding innovative approaches to hypnotherapy that focused on physical presence and unconscious cues.

## Key facts

- At age 17, Milton Erickson awoke to discover parts of his body suddenly paralyzed; diagnosed with polio, doctors told his mother he would be dead by morning. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p98]
- During his paralysis, he discovered that people's faces made movements and tones of voice revealed true feelings—he counted sixteen different forms of 'no' and observed how people said yes while shaking their heads no. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p99]
- He noticed veins in his sisters' necks pulsating when they stood over him, indicating nervousness, and discovered certain breathing rhythms indicated boredom followed by yawning. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p99]
- By focusing deeply on specific muscles and using imagination, he taught himself to stand, walk, and eventually regained complete movement. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p100]
- His psychiatric method focused on physical presence as an entrée into patients' mental life and unconscious, contrary to colleagues who focused on words and childhood回忆. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p101]
- He could determine people's states of mind by their breathing patterns and, by mirroring these patterns, could lead patients into hypnotic trance and create deep rapport. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p104]
- Erickson once identified that a beautiful young woman seeking therapy was actually a man by observing how she picked lint off her sleeve without making a natural detour around the breast area. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p103]
- He could detect when his secretary was menstruating by the heaviness of her typing, and guess career backgrounds by the quality of hands, heaviness of step, head tilt, and vocal inflections. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p104]
- Milton H. Erickson was a psychiatrist from Phoenix, Arizona. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- He was the mentor of John Weakland and wrote the introduction to 'Change'. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- His innovative techniques, especially in hypnosis, influenced all three authors. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
- In his introduction, he spoke of 'the currents of change already seething within the person and the family'. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L107-L686]
