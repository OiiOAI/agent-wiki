# Otto Loewi

## Summary

Otto Loewi (1873–1961) was a German-born physiologist who, working in Austria, provided the first evidence for chemical transmission at synapses. In a landmark experiment on a frog's heart, he showed that stimulating the vagus nerve released a chemical substance that could slow a second frog's heart rate when transferred. He and Henry Dale shared the 1936 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for this discovery. Loewi was forced to flee Austria in 1938 after being arrested by Nazis because he was Jewish; he transferred his Nobel Prize money to a Nazi-controlled bank and emigrated to New York University Medical School.

## Key facts

- Loewi demonstrated chemical synaptic transmission by stimulating the vagus nerve of a frog, collecting the fluid around its heart, and injecting it into a second frog, whose heart rate also slowed. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p104]
- The substance released by the vagus nerve was identified as acetylcholine. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p104]
- Loewi and Dale shared the 1936 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for establishing chemical transmission in the autonomic nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p104]
- Loewi was arrested by Austrian Nazis in 1938 because he was Jewish and was released two months later on condition he transfer his Nobel Prize to a Nazi-controlled bank and leave immediately. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p105]
