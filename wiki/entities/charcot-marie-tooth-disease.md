# Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease

## Summary

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease is a group of inherited peripheral neuropathies characterized by muscle weakness and wasting, loss of reflexes, and sensory loss in distal limbs. Type 1 is demyelinating with slowed nerve conduction, while Type 2 is axonal with normal conduction velocity. Various genetic forms involve mutations in myelin proteins (PMP22, P0, Connexin-32) or axonal proteins.

## Key facts

- CMT disease is characterized by muscle weakness and wasting, loss of reflexes, and loss of sensation in distal parts of the limbs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Type 1 CMT is demyelinating with slowed nerve conduction velocity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Type 2 CMT is an axonal neuropathy with normal nerve conduction velocity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Type 1A CMT involves a defect in peripheral myelin protein 22 (PMP22), while Type 1B involves the myelin protein P0. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- X-linked CMT is caused by mutations in the gene expressing Connexin-32, a subunit of gap junction channels at the nodes of Ranvier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
