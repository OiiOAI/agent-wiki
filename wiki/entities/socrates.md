# Socrates

## Summary

Socrates (c. 470–399 BC), Plato's teacher, demonstrated the falsity of his interlocutors' premises by leading them to contradictions. Yet McGilchrist notes he was torn between his intuitions and the awareness that he was no longer at liberty to trust them. In the Theaetetus, Socrates points out that Parmenides was the only one of 'the wise' to deny that all is change and motion. Nietzsche viewed Socrates as 'the first degenerate' because he had lost the ability to trust intuition: honest men do not carry their reasons exposed in this fashion. While awaiting death, Socrates' daemon told him to make music—perhaps acknowledging that 'things which I do not understand are not automatically unreasonable.'
