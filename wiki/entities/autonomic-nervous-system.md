# Autonomic Nervous System

## Summary

The autonomic nervous system controls involuntary functions of the body including the heart, smooth muscle, and exocrine glands. It is divided into three major divisions: sympathetic (thoracolumbar), parasympathetic (craniosacral), and enteric. The system maintains homeostasis by regulating blood pressure, body temperature, digestion, and other vital functions through coordinated reflex circuits.

## Key facts

- The autonomic nervous system contains approximately 900,000 motor neurons in the superior cervical ganglion alone, compared to only approximately 120,000 somatic motor nerve cells in the entire spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1108]
- The parasympathetic pathways arise from a cranial nerve zone in the brain stem and a sacral zone (S2-S4) in the spinal cord, surrounding a sympathetic zone that extends from T1 to L3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1109]
- Preganglionic neurons in all divisions of the autonomic nervous system synthesize and release acetylcholine (ACh), which acts on nicotinic ACh receptors in postganglionic neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1109]
- Sympathetic postganglionic neurons release norepinephrine at most neuroeffector junctions, while parasympathetic postganglionic neurons release acetylcholine. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1113]
- The enteric nervous system contains as many as 100 million neurons and can maintain basic gastrointestinal functions even after the splanchnic sympathetic and vagal parasympathetic pathways are cut. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1112]
- The enteric system includes two major plexuses: the myenteric plexus, which controls smooth muscle movements, and the submucous plexus, which controls mucosal function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1113]

## Inferences

- Inference: The massive numerical advantage of autonomic over somatic motor neurons reflects the great diversity and complexity of autonomically controlled target tissues (stomach, intestine, bladder, heart, lungs, vasculature) compared to the relative uniformity of skeletal muscle.
