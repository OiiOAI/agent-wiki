# Jones Realty v. Turnbull

## Summary

A real-life case study illustrating principled negotiation in action. Frank Turnbull, a tenant, discovered he had been overcharged $67 per month above the legal rent-controlled maximum for his apartment. Through repeated applications of principled negotiation techniques—including asking questions rather than making accusations, separating the people from the problem, and appealing to objective standards—Turnbull secured reimbursement from Mrs. Jones of Jones Realty while maintaining a civil relationship.

## Key facts

- Frank Turnbull rented an apartment from Jones Realty for $300 a month, but the maximum legal rent under rent control was $233—$67 less. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p59]
- Turnbull used the phrase 'Please correct me if I'm wrong' to establish a dialogue based on reason rather than confrontation. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p59]
- Turnbull successfully separated trust issues from principle by stating 'Trust isn't the issue here. The issue is the principle: Did we pay more than we should have?'. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p61]
- Turnbull presented his BATNA (staying in the apartment, withholding rent, suing for treble damages) as a possibility suggested by the Rent Control Examiner rather than a personal threat. [raw/books/strategy/Getting_to_Yes_Fisher_Ury.pdf#p63]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Turnbull case demonstrates that a tenant with less apparent power (financial resources, legal expertise) can successfully negotiate against a landlord by focusing on objective standards and maintaining a principled approach.
