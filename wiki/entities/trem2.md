# TREM2

## Summary

TREM2 is a cell surface receptor expressed exclusively in microglia in the brain. Rare mutations in TREM2 double or triple the risk for Alzheimer disease, similar to having one copy of the APOE4 allele. This finding suggests involvement of the innate immune system in AD pathogenesis.

## Key facts

- TREM2 is expressed only in microglia, and rare mutations in TREM2 double or triple AD risk, similar to having one copy of the APOE4 allele. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- CD33, another gene associated with AD risk, is also expressed only in microglia, suggesting the innate immune system is involved in AD pathogenesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]

## Inferences

- Inference: The identification of microglial genes (TREM2 and CD33) as AD risk factors suggests neuroinflammation plays a significant role in disease pathogenesis.
