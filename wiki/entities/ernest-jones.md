# Ernest Jones

## Summary

Ernest Jones (1879-1958) was a Welsh psychoanalyst who became the leading figure among English-speaking analysts and a foremost representative of psychoanalysis internationally. Freud wrote this tribute on Jones's 50th birthday in 1929, reviewing his contributions to psychoanalysis including the founding of British psychoanalytic institutions, editorship of psychoanalytic journals, and his scientific writings such as Papers on Psycho-Analysis and Essays in Applied Psycho-Analysis.

## Key facts

- Freud describes Ernest Jones as 'indisputably the leading figure among English-speaking analysts' and 'one of the foremost representatives of psychoanalysis as a whole.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6780]
- Jones delivered his first paper at the 1908 Salzburg Congress on 'Rationalization in Everyday Life,' introducing an important concept and indispensable term to psychoanalysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6779]
- Jones worked as a professor in Toronto and then as a physician in London, where he founded and taught a Branch Society, directed a Press, edited a journal, and headed a training institute. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p6779]
