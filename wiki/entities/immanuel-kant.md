# Immanuel Kant

## Summary

The eighteenth-century German philosopher Immanuel Kant sought to save scientific empiricism from Hume's self-devouring logic. His Critique of Pure Reason introduced the concept of a priori knowledge—mental categories like time and space that structure our experience. Phædrus studied Kant intensively, finding his logical fortifications formidable but eventually feeling trapped by the intellectual prison Kant's system represented, leading him toward Eastern philosophy.

## Key facts

- Kant wrote the Critique of Pure Reason to save scientific empiricism from Hume's conclusions while refusing to abandon empirical reason. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- Kant said, 'That all our knowledge begins with experience there can be no doubt,' but 'though all knowledge begins with experience it doesn't follow that it arises out of experience.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p110]
- Kant called his thesis that a priori thoughts are independent of sense data and screen what we see a 'Copernican revolution.'. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p113]
- Phædrus found Kant's aesthetics disappointing and angering, feeling they were 'ugly' in a way woven so deeply into his world there seemed no escape. [raw/books/philosophy/Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance - Robert M. Pirsig.pdf#p114]
