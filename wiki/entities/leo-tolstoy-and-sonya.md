# Leo Tolstoy and Sonya Tolstaya

## Summary

Leo Tolstoy and his wife Sonya Behrs (Tolstaya) exemplify the Narcissistic Couple pattern described in the book. Their 48-year marriage was marked by constant conflict, mutual accusations, and emotional manipulation, culminating in Tolstoy's dramatic escape from the estate and subsequent death.

## Key facts

- Days before their 1862 wedding, Tolstoy revealed his past affairs, gonorrhea, and gambling to 18-year-old Sonya through his diaries, causing her distress. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p85]
- Sonya served as Tolstoy's most trusted assistant, copying his books including War and Peace and Anna Karenina, and managing publishing business. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p85]
- At age 50, Tolstoy underwent a spiritual crisis, rejected his previous novels, and demanded the family adopt a peasant lifestyle and renounce copyright ownership. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p85]
- Tolstoy wrote The Kreutzer Sonata based on their marriage, portraying Sonya in the worst possible light. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p86]
- Sonya made four suicide attempts, including walking into snow and jumping into a pond, as the marriage deteriorated. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p86]
- Tolstoy secretly fled the estate in October 1910 with a doctor friend, and died eleven days later in a stationmaster's cottage. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p87]
- Sonya arrived at Tolstoy's deathbed but was kept outside until he was unconscious; she whispered 'Forgive me' before he died. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p87]
- A month after Tolstoy's death, Sonya reportedly said 'You know I killed him.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p88]
