# Stefania Albertani Case

## Summary

Stefania Albertani pled guilty to drugging, killing, and burning her sister. Her defense team used brain imaging showing fewer neurons in her insula and anterior cingulate gyrus to argue these brain differences caused her behavior, reducing her sentence from life to 20 years. Barrett criticizes this 'blob-ology' as junk science, noting these are highly connected hub regions active in nearly every mental function, and mere variation in brain structure is not an extenuating circumstance.

## Key facts

- Brain imaging revealed Albertani's cortex contained fewer neurons than controls in the insula and anterior cingulate gyrus, regions the defense claimed were associated with aggression and lowered inhibitions. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p251]
- Expert witnesses concluded a 'causal relationship' between her brain structure and crime was possible; her sentence was reduced from life to twenty years. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p251]
- The insula and anterior cingulate are among the most highly connected hubs in the brain, showing increased activation for just about every mental event from language to pain to math. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p252]
- It is not scientifically justified to claim any specific causal relationship between these regions and extreme aggression, or to claim variation in brain size translates into variation in behavior. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p252]
- All behavior stems from the brain; the wrong use of neuroscience is to argue a biological explanation automatically releases someone from responsibility—you are your brain. [raw/books/psychology/How Emotions Are Made_ The Secret Life of - Lisa Feldman Barrett.pdf#p252]
