# Sun Tzu

## Summary

Sun Tzu (c. 5th century BC) was an ancient Chinese military strategist and philosopher whose work The Art of War remains the earliest known documentation of the strategic school that underlies both Blitzkrieg and the Toyota Production System. His teachings emphasize deception, psychological warfare, winning without fighting, and understanding that war is fundamentally about human psychology rather than physical force. Key quotes used in the book include 'Numbers alone confer no advantage' and 'All warfare is based upon deception.' His work was tested during the chaotic Warring States Period and has been refined by commentators and practitioners for 2,500 years.

## Key facts

- Sun Tzu (c. 5th century B.C.) dismissed the fascination with size: 'Numbers alone confer no advantage'. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p30]
- 'All warfare is based upon deception'—Sun Tzu. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p33]
- Sun Tzu taught that the best strategy wins without ever engaging in battle at all. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p8]
- Sun Tzu's strategies evolved through testing in the chaotic Warring States Period. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p61]
- 'He whose ranks are united in purpose will be victorious'—Sun Tzu. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p42]
- In Sun Tzu tradition, bearers of bad news are saviors who should be recognized and rewarded for providing intelligence while there is time to act. [raw/books/strategy/Certain to Win - The Strategy of John Boyd, Applied to - Richards, Chet, Richards, Chester W.pdf#p52]
- Boyd considered The Art of War his 'Rosetta stone' – the work he returned to repeatedly and the only theoretical book on war he did not find fundamentally flawed. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p66]
- Boyd eventually owned seven translations of The Art of War, each with long passages underlined and copious marginalia. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p66]
- Sun Tzu's ideas form one of the starting points of Patterns of Conflict and reappear at the beginning of its concluding part. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p66]
- Sun Tzu mandated that war should be avoided as much and as long as possible; statecraft should aim to avoid war using diplomatic means first. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p66]
- The Chinese concept 'shih' refers to strategic configurations of power, constantly shifting according to internal and external environmental conditions. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p67]
- Sun Tzu's key strategic concepts include: according with the enemy, foreknowledge, cohesion, surprise, deception, formlessness, high tempo, variety and flexibility, orthodox and unorthodox (cheng/ch'i), and discerning the vacuous and substantial. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p69]
- The concept of cheng (orthodox) and ch'i (unorthodox) means the straightforward method and the crafty method or direct and indirect approach – characteristically, the unorthodox can become the orthodox depending on what the opponent expects. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p72]
- Sun Tzu's essence of strategy was: Preserve harmony, create chaos, and achieve victory by continually keeping the enemy off balance through a superior capability to adapt. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p74]
- The goal of warfare according to Sun Tzu was to destroy the conditions of prosperity and order that formed the link between a ruler and his people, undermining legitimacy. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p74]
