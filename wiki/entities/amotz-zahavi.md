# Amotz Zahavi

## Summary

Amotz Zahavi is an Israeli evolutionary biologist who proposed the 'handicap principle' as an explanation for apparently maladaptive sexually-selected traits like the peacock's tail. He argues that these handicaps demonstrate genetic quality because only genuinely fit individuals can survive despite them. Zahavi also proposed the 'Fox, fox' theory of alarm calls and a baby blackmail hypothesis, both covered critically in the book.

## Key facts

- Zahavi proposed the 'handicap principle': males with handicaps like long tails demonstrate their genetic quality because only genuinely fit males can survive despite the encumbrance, and females who choose such males get good genes for their offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p196]
- Zahavi's 'Fox, fox' theory suggests baby birds scream to deliberately attract predators to the nest as a form of blackmail, forcing parents to feed them more—but Dawkins argues this is unlikely because the blackmailing baby has too much to lose if a predator actually comes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p166]
- Dawkins expresses skepticism about the handicap principle, noting that mathematical geneticists including Maynard Smith have so far failed to make it into a workable formal model, though he acknowledges uncertainty about whether the theory might still be valid. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p197]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether Zahavi's handicap principle has been successfully formalized mathematically—Dawkins notes this has not yet been achieved but is not fully confident the principle is wrong.
