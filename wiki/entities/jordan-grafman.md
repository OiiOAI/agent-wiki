# Jordan Grafman

## Summary

Dr. Jordan Grafman is Chief of the Cognitive Neurosciences Section at the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS). A leading researcher in neuroplasticity and prefrontal cortex function, he has studied Vietnam head injury patients for twenty years and identified four types of brain plasticity. His personal history—his father's stroke-induced personality changes led to a troubled childhood for Grafman—motivated his career investigating how the brain can change and adapt.

## Key facts

- He is chief of the Cognitive Neurosciences Section at the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p275]
- He served for twenty years in the United States Air Force, directing the neuropsychological component of the Vietnam Head Injury Study. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p276]
- He identified four kinds of neuroplasticity: map expansion, sensory reassignment, compensatory masquerade, and mirror region takeover. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p279]
- His research found that a soldier's preinjury IQ was a very important predictor of how well he would recover lost brain functions, suggesting intelligence enables better reorganization. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p277]
- He integrates a nondoctrinaire version of localizationism with plasticity, viewing the brain as divided into sectors that acquire primary responsibilities but remain adaptable. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p278]
- His father had a stroke in the right-frontal cortex that caused personality changes, emotional outbursts, and social disinhibition, which profoundly affected Grafman's childhood. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p275]
- He is studying Michelle Mack to understand how her brain developed with only one hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p267]
- He believes that neurons on the border between brain areas are less committed to specific functions and are recruited by competing adjacent areas based on daily activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p278]
- He argues that boundary areas of the brain can expand quickly, within minutes, to respond to moment-by-moment needs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p278]
