# Lateral Intraparietal Area (LIP)

## Summary

The lateral intraparietal area (LIP) is a sensorimotor region of the posterior parietal cortex in the macaque monkey that plays a central role in saccadic eye movement selection and perceptual decision-making. Electrophysiological studies have shown that LIP neurons exhibit persistent activity during delay periods, encode value information, and display gradual buildup activity consistent with evidence accumulation during decision processes.

## Key facts

- LIP is anatomically connected to higher-order sensory areas such as the extrastriate visual cortex and sends efferents to saccade execution areas including the frontal eye fields and superior colliculus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p123]
- Individual LIP neurons respond to both visual stimulus presentation in a circumscribed region of visual space termed the response field (RF) and before and during eye movements to the same spatial location. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p123]
- LIP neurons exhibit persistent spiking activity dissociated from both stimulus presence and active eye movements in memory saccade tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p123]
- LIP activity increases with the magnitude of reward outcome and the probability of reinforcement associated with a saccade to the neural response field. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p124]
- In foraging environments, LIP activity tracks the trial-by-trial changing value of options as determined by recent reward history. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p124]
- LIP neurons simultaneously encode both reward magnitude and perceptual evidence in motion discrimination tasks with variable reinforcement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p124]
- In temporal discounting tasks, LIP activity reflects the individual-specific decrement of reward value driven by imposed delays to reward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p125]

## Inferences

- Inference: LIP appears to function as a decision variable representing subjective value, closely tied to the economic notion of expected utility derived from behavior.
- Inference: The observed relative value coding in LIP is driven by spatial context defined by available choice alternatives, suggesting contextual modulation as a fundamental characteristic of neural value coding.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The precise contribution of LIP to value coding versus other decision-related signals (such as motor preparation or attention) remains debated.
- Uncertain: The relationship between LIP value representations and the RPE signals attributed to dopamine neurons is not fully established.
