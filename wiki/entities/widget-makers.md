# Widget Makers

## Summary

Widget Makers is a fictional example business used throughout 'The E-Myth Revisited' to illustrate the principles of organizational development. The company was founded by brothers Jack and Murray Hopeful. The example demonstrates what happens when a business organizes around personalities rather than functions, and then shows how applying the Organization Chart and Position Contract methodology creates order from chaos.

## Key facts

- Widget Makers was started by Jack and Murray Hopeful, brothers who initially shared all work equally, taking turns at every task. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p167]
- When the business grew and they hired Jerry (a nephew) and Herb (Jack's wife's brother), the result was chaos because everyone was doing everything without clear accountability. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p169]
- Without an Organization Chart, everything hinges on luck and good feelings, on personalities and goodwill—alone these are the recipe for chaos and disaster. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p170]
- Jack and Murray reframed themselves as shareholders outside the business and employees inside the business, with Jack becoming COO and Murray VP/Marketing. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p177]
- They created Position Contracts for all positions on their Organization Chart, ultimately resulting in six job positions each. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p183]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Widget Makers example progresses through the same growth challenges many small businesses face, making it an effective teaching tool for organizational development concepts.
- Inference: The brothers' journey from chaos to order through systematic organization demonstrates the practical application of Gerber's theories.
