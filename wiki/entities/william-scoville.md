# William Beecher Scoville

## Summary

William Beecher Scoville (1906–1984) was an American neurosurgeon at Hartford Hospital who performed the bilateral medial temporal lobe surgery on Henry Molaison in 1953 to treat severe epilepsy. The surgery was intended to relieve Henry's seizures but resulted in profound anterograde amnesia.

## Key facts

- William Scoville performed bilateral medial temporal lobe surgery on Henry Molaison in 1953 to treat severe epilepsy, removing approximately 8 centimeters of brain tissue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p77]
- Scoville had originally estimated that he removed about 8 centimeters of hippocampal tissue, but later MRI imaging revealed the actual extent was just over 5 centimeters. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p81]
- Scoville's 1957 paper with Brenda Milner describing Henry's case launched the modern era of memory research. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: Scoville's surgery, though intended to help Henry, inadvertently created the most important case study in the history of memory neuroscience.
