# Frances Yates

## Summary

Frances Yates was a historian of memory and mnemonics, best known for her 1966 book 'The Art of Memory' which traced the development of mnemonic techniques from ancient Greece through the Renaissance. She attributed the loss of the Greek mnemonic tradition to the difficulties of barbarous ages and the incomplete transmission of classical texts.

## Key facts

- Historian Frances Yates attributed the loss of the Greek-initiated tradition of mnemonics to the difficulties of a barbarous age, where it was dangerous to assemble to listen to someone speak. - The main reference works on mnemonics were lost, and medieval studies of memory, mainly Aquinas's, were based on texts either incomplete or misinterpreted. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p178]
- Quintilian's Institutio oratoria, which offered the most concrete description of the method of loci, had its complete text found only in 1416 at the library of the Abbey of St. Gall in Switzerland.
