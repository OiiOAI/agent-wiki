# Neville Chamberlain

## Summary

Neville Chamberlain was the British Prime Minister who attempted to appease Adolf Hitler through personal diplomacy, famously returning from Munich in 1938 with a piece of paper he claimed guaranteed 'peace for our time.' His case illustrates how personal interaction with a stranger can make judgment worse, not better.

## Key facts

- Chamberlain flew to Germany three times in September 1938 to meet Hitler, believing personal interaction would help him assess Hitler's intentions. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p24]
- Chamberlain concluded after his first meeting that Hitler was 'a man who could be relied upon when he had given his word.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p24]
- Chamberlain famously waved the Munich agreement upon returning to Britain, saying 'I believe it is peace for our time.'. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p30]
- Hitler invaded the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939, less than six months after the Munich agreement, and Poland in September 1939. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p31]
