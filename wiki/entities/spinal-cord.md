# Spinal Cord

## Summary

The spinal cord is the conduit for somatosensory information from the trunk and limbs to the brain, and for motor commands from the brain to skeletal muscles. It contains an H-shaped gray matter region (with dorsal and ventral horns) surrounded by white matter organized into dorsal, lateral, and ventral columns. The cord is divided into cervical, thoracic, lumbar, and sacral regions with corresponding spinal nerves.

## Key facts

- The spinal cord has a core H-shaped region of gray matter where neuronal cell bodies are located, surrounded by white matter formed by myelinated axons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p124]
- The gray matter on each side is divided into dorsal (posterior) horns containing secondary sensory neurons and ventral (anterior) horns containing motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p124]
- The dorsal columns contain only ascending axons carrying somatosensory information to the brain stem, while lateral and ventral columns include both ascending and descending axons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p125]
- The spinal cord is divided into four major regions: cervical, thoracic, lumbar, and sacral, corresponding to embryological somites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p126]
- White matter proportion is highest at cervical levels (most ascending and descending fibers) and lowest at sacral levels, which have relatively more gray matter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p127]
- The ventral horn is larger at levels where motor nerves innervate the arms and legs, reflecting the greater number of motor neurons needed for limb dexterity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p127]
