# David McNeill

## Summary

Psychologist whose research on gesture provides key evidence for the primacy of the right hemisphere in thought. McNeill videotaped human interactions and analyzed the relationship between gesture language and speech, discovering that gestures slightly anticipate speech and reveal 'global-synthetic image' thought before linguistic structure develops. His research shows that gestures constitute thought—without them, thought would be altered or incomplete. Most significantly, when there is a mismatch between gesture and speech, it is always the gesture that carries the day, suggesting thought originates in the right hemisphere and is translated into language by the left.

## Key facts

- McNeill found that gestures slightly anticipate speech, indicating a 'global-synthetic image taking form' before linguistic structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p269]
- McNeill identifies two modes: gesture expresses 'global-synthetic' thought throughout, while speech expresses 'linear-segmented hierarchical linguistic structure'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p270]
- The right-hemisphere contribution has both temporal priority and ontological priority, since thought is originally 'largely imagistic' before becoming 'both imagistic and analytic' at utterance. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p270]
- McNeill writes 'Gestures do not merely reflect thought, but help constitute thought'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p270]
- When there is a mismatch between gesture and speech, it is the gesture that carries the day in 100 percent of cases. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p271]
- After right-hemisphere damage, speakers show tendencies both to decontextualise speech and to reduce gesture output. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p271]
