# Gabriele Oettingen

## Summary

Gabriele Oettingen is a German-American psychologist and professor who developed the mental contrasting technique and WOOP method. She conducted twenty years of research at institutions including the University of Pennsylvania (where she worked with Martin Seligman) and the Max Planck Institute for Human Development and Education in Berlin. Her work challenges conventional positive thinking advice by demonstrating empirically that positive fantasies detached from reality impede goal achievement.

## Key facts

- Oettingen was a postdoctoral fellow at the University of Pennsylvania in Philadelphia in 1986, where Martin E.P. Seligman served as her research advisor. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p15]
- Her early research examined cross-cultural differences in depressive behavior between East and West Germany during the Cold War. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p15]
- She conducted research at the Max Planck Institute for Human Development and Education in Berlin. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p62]
- Over twenty years, she conducted studies across Germany and the United States with participants of different ages, socioeconomic statuses, and cultures. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p19]
- Her first study on positive fantasies and weight loss was published in 1991 in a peer-reviewed journal. [raw/books/psychology/Rethinking Positive Thinking - Gabriele Oettingen.pdf#p18]
