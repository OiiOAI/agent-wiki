# Nicholas Dodman

## Summary

Nicholas Dodman is a veterinary specialist and professor at Tufts University who pioneered the diagnosis of 'ADHD' in animals and the prescription of stimulant drugs to treat behavioral problems in pets. He became known as the 'Pied Piper' of drugging animals for psychiatric problems, but argues this is a limited 'Band-Aid' solution to deeper environmental problems.

## Key facts

- He diagnosed a beagle named Emma with ADHD and prescribed Ritalin, which her owner said 'solved' the dog's problems. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p234-235]
- He has prescribed Ritalin and other stimulants to numerous animals he diagnoses with ADHD, and estimates 50 to 60 percent of owners coming to his clinic seek psychiatric medications for their pets. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p236]
- He admits that drugging animals for 'zoochosis' is a limited solution, comparing it to putting a polar bear on Prozac instead of giving it adequate space. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p237]
- Nearly half of all U.S. zoos now admit to giving psychiatric drugs to their animals. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p236]

## Inferences

- Inference: His work with animals diagnosed with ADHD raises questions about whether similar behaviors in children might also be responses to environmental constraints rather than inherent biological disorders.
