# Stanley Milgram

## Summary

Stanley Milgram (1933-1984) was an American social psychologist at Yale University best known for his obedience experiments conducted in the early 1960s. His research demonstrated the startling extent to which ordinary people will comply with authority figures, even when asked to harm others. His 1974 book 'Obedience to Authority' provided a comprehensive account of his findings and their implications for understanding events like the Holocaust.

## Key facts

- Milgram's initial obedience experiment was conducted in 1961 at Yale University, published in the Journal of Abnormal and Social Psychology in 1963. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- Milgram published his book 'Obedience to Authority' in 1974. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- Milgram originally planned to replicate his experiments in Germany, believing German citizens would provide higher obedience rates, but found American subjects showed sufficient obedience for scientific analysis. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
- Milgram found such extreme obedience in New Haven, Connecticut that he remarked 'I hardly saw the need of taking the experiment to Germany'. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p261]
