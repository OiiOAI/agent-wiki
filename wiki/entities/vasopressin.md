# Vasopressin

## Summary

Vasopressin is a mammalian neuropeptide that stimulates pair-bond formation and paternal behavior. The pattern of V1a vasopressin receptor expression differs between monogamous and nonmonogamous vole species, and this expression pattern is crucial for social behavior differences. Introducing prairie vole V1a receptors into mice increases their affiliative behavior.

## Key facts

- Vasopressin released in the brain of male prairie voles during mating stimulates pair-bond formation and paternal behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p92]
- Male prairie voles (monogamous) have high V1a vasopressin receptor expression in the ventral pallidum, while male montane voles (nonmonogamous) have low expression in this region. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p92]
- Introducing the V1a vasopressin receptor gene from prairie voles into male mice increases V1a expression in the ventral pallidum and increases affiliative behavior toward females. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p92]
