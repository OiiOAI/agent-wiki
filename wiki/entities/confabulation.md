# Confabulation

## Summary

Confabulation refers to false memories that some patients, particularly those with amnesia, generate when asked about their past. These fabricated memories can sometimes be extremely implausible and represent the creative reconstruction of memory without access to actual events.

## Key facts

- Some amnesic patients give elaborate accounts that do not correspond to reality when asked about past events, a phenomenon called confabulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1529]
- Confabulations occur when the process by which memories are screened becomes dramatically disturbed. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1529]
- The creative mechanisms needed to reconstruct memories are also involved when imagining events that might happen in the future, and patients with hippocampal damage show impaired ability to imagine new events. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1529]
