# David Blaine

## Summary

David Blaine is a self-described 'endurance artist' who has performed feats of willpower including standing 80 feet above Bryant Park for 35 hours, spending 63 hours encased in ice, fasting for 44 days, and holding his breath for 17 minutes. He believes that willpower is a muscle that can be strengthened through daily exercises like breath-holding drills, cold exposure, and setting and achieving small goals.

## Key facts

- Blaine held his breath for 17 minutes and 4 seconds on Oprah, experiencing extreme pain, numbness in his arm, and a heart that was skipping beats, yet continued past his breaking point. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p119]
- Blaine practiced daily breath-holding for a year, holding his breath for 48 minutes total during hourly sessions, resulting in pounding headaches that trained his body to tolerate carbon dioxide buildup. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p120]
- Blaine believes that 'getting your brain wired into little goals and achieving them helps you achieve the bigger things you shouldn't be able to do,' practicing rituals like stepping on drawings of bikers during runs. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p121]
- Blaine noted that between stunts he loses self-control entirely, gaining 50 pounds in three months and oscillating between perfect eating for 5 days and 'eating horrifically for 10 days.'. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p131]

## Inferences

- Inference: Blaine's feats demonstrate that while extreme willpower can be developed, maintaining it indefinitely without a specific goal is extremely difficult for everyone, including trained performers.
