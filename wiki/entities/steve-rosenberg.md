# Steve Rosenberg

## Summary

Dr. Steven A. Rosenberg is a surgical oncologist who pioneered cancer immunotherapy. He encountered a case of spontaneous cancer remission in 1968 that led him to hypothesize the immune system could fight cancer. After decades of work including CAR-T cell therapy development and checkpoint inhibitor research, his approaches yielded FDA-approved treatments. He performed surgery on President Reagan and was nominated for the Nobel Prize.

## Key facts

- Steve Rosenberg encountered James DeAngelo, a patient whose metastatic stomach cancer had spontaneously regressed, inspiring his career in cancer immunotherapy. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p147]
- Rosenberg developed CAR-T (chimeric antigen receptor T cell) therapy, which was approved by the FDA in 2017 as the first cell and gene therapies ever approved. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p166]
- He worked on interleukin-2 (IL-2) therapy, which in 1984 successfully treated a late-stage melanoma patient, showing the immune system could fight cancer. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p165]
- Rosenberg performed surgery on President Ronald Reagan in 1985, removing cancerous polyps from his colon. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p148]
- He is one of the pioneers who helped bring immunotherapy from fringe concept to mainstream cancer treatment. [raw/books/health/Outlive.pdf#p171]
