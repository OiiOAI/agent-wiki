# Hermann Balck

## Summary

Hermann Balck (1894-1982) was a German general whose writings on leadership strongly influenced Boyd's concept of moral conflict. Balck emphasized that cohesion during combat relied more on moral (human values) superiority than material superiority. He stressed that leaders must create bonds and breadth of experience based upon trust, lead by example demonstrating physical energy, mental energy, and moral authority, and support unconventional subordinates who demonstrate initiative.

## Key facts

- Balck emphasized the importance of leadership in creating moral strength among troops, requiring leaders to subordinate wide freedom for subordinates to exercise imagination and initiative while harmonizing within superior commanders' intent. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p224]
- Balck stressed that cohesion during combat relied more on moral superiority than material superiority. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p224]
- Balck emphasized creation of implicit connections or bonds based upon trust, not mistrust, that permit wide freedom for subordinates to exercise imagination and initiative yet harmonize within superior commanders' intent. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p245]
- Balck wrote about the benefit of internal simplicity that permits rapid adaptability. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p245]

## Inferences

- Inference: Balck's emphasis on trust-based implicit coordination directly influenced Boyd's reconceptualization of command and control as appreciation and leadership rather than explicit hierarchical control.
