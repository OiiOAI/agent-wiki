# Ernest Hartmann

## Summary

Ernest Hartmann was a former psychiatrist and professor at Tufts University who made significant contributions to understanding dreams and nightmares. He proposed the nighttime therapy theory of dreaming, suggesting dreams help weave emotional concerns and traumatic events into existing memory systems within the safety of sleep.

## Key facts

- Ernest Hartmann was a psychiatrist and professor at Tufts University who proposed that dreaming is a form of nighttime therapy. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Hartmann proposed that dreams create connections between new and old memories that are broader and looser than connections made during wakefulness. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- Hartmann introduced the concept of contextualizing images—picture-metaphors representing key emotional issues that change as the dreamer's emotions evolve. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
- According to Hartmann, the dreamer's emotions guide the connections made during dreams, rather than emotions occurring in reaction to the dream. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p114]
