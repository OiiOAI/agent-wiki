# Earl Miller

## Summary

Earl Miller is a neuroscientist at MIT who won top awards for his research on how the brain handles attention and multitasking. He argues that our belief in multitasking is a dangerous delusion, and that we have created 'a perfect storm of cognitive degradation.'

## Key facts

- Miller states that the human brain can only produce 'one or two thoughts' in conscious mind at once, due to fundamental brain structure that won't change. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p43]
- He explains that when we think we're multitasking, we're actually rapidly switching, and the brain 'papers over' the gaps to create a false sense of seamless consciousness. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p44]
- He identified three costs of switching: the 'switch cost effect' (performance drops), the 'screw-up effect' (increased errors from backtracking), and the 'creativity drain' (less opportunity for associative thinking). [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p44-46]
- Miller stated we have created in our culture 'a perfect storm of cognitive degradation, as a result of distraction.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p48]
- He recommends 'monotasking'—practicing focus on one thing at a time for gradually increasing intervals—as the solution: 'The brain is like a muscle. The more you use certain things, the stronger the connection's getting.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p48-49]
