# Milgram Experiment

## Summary

Stanley Milgram's obedience experiment tested whether ordinary people would inflict potentially lethal electrical shocks on others when instructed by an authority figure. Participants (teachers) were told to shock 'learners' for wrong answers, with voltage increasing to 450 volts. Despite learners' screams and protests, 65% administered maximum shocks when urged by a lab-coated scientist to continue.

## Key facts

- In Milgram's original experiment, 65% of participants administered the maximum 450-volt shock when urged by an authority figure (lab-coated scientist). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p475]
- Participants typically tried to stop, argued with the scientist, and wept in distress but ultimately obeyed, citing that the learner 'consented' to the risks. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p475]
- Gina Perry's analysis of Milgram's unpublished recordings found that teachers refused to shock much more frequently than originally reported, and many realized the learner was an actor. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p478]
- When the authority was in a different room, compliance decreased; when conducted in a warehouse rather than at Yale, compliance also declined. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p482]
- Milgram stated that if death camps were set up in the US, sufficient personnel could be found in any medium-sized American town. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p477]
- Milgram's study showed approximately 65% of subjects were willing to deliver what they believed were lethal electric shocks when instructed by an authority figure. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p1168]
- The Hofling hospital experiment showed that 21 out of 22 nurses, unaware they were in an experiment, were willing to give a dangerously high medication dose when ordered by an unknown doctor. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p1170]
- Milgram and Zimbardo knew each other as classmates in their high school in the Bronx. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p1169]

## Inferences

- Inference: The Milgram experiment was motivated by the Eichmann trial and the question of how ordinary Germans could 'just follow orders' during the Holocaust—a direct response to understanding the 'banality of evil.'

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The true compliance rate is disputed; Perry's analysis suggests original reports were inflated, though contemporary virtual-reality replications still show high compliance (~60%).
