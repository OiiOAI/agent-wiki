# Lyndon B. Johnson

## Summary

Lyndon Baines Johnson (1908-1973) was the 36th President of the United States who previously served as Master of the Senate. His rise from junior senator to Senate Majority Leader in just four years exemplified masterful political strategy based on patience, strategic relationship-building, and making others feel valued.

## Key facts

- After being elected to the Senate in 1948, he initially offended Senator Tom Connally by demanding immediately prestigious committee assignments and being rebuked for not understanding senatorial seniority. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p216]
- He completely transformed his approach, becoming deferential and patient, visiting senior senators in their offices, waiting sometimes an hour to speak with them. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p217]
- He cultivated a close relationship with Senator Richard Russell of Georgia, visiting his office daily, sharing dinners, and studying baseball and the Civil War together. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p219]
- Through Russell's influence and a strategic arrangement with Senator Tydings, he became chairman of the Armed Services subcommittee within his first two years. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p219]
- He mentored Senator Hubert Humphrey, teaching him Senate procedures and the art of compromise, while getting Humphrey to bridge him to northern liberals. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p221]
- In 1952, with Russell's backing, he became Senate Majority Leader at age forty-four, the youngest in history. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p223]
- He secured unprecedented power over committee appointments by gaining the trust of senior senators through relentless charm, favors, and strategic quid pro quos. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p224]

## Inferences

- Inference: Johnson recognized that his aggressive personality was a liability in the intimate, relationship-driven Senate and deliberately cultivated an inoffensive front.
- Inference: His strategy of making others feel valued and understood lowered their defensiveness, allowing him to gradually accumulate influence without appearing threatening.
