# Rett Syndrome

## Summary

Rett syndrome is an X-linked progressive neurodevelopmental disorder and one of the most common causes of intellectual disability in females. It results from mutations in the MeCP2 gene. Affected girls develop typically until 6-18 months of age, then fail to acquire speech, show regression in intellectual functioning, and display compulsive hand wringing. Many patients show periods of impaired social interaction that may be indistinguishable from autism spectrum disorder.

## Key facts

- Rett syndrome results from mutations in the methyl CpG binding protein 2 (MeCP2) gene. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p95]
- MeCP2 binds methylated DNA as part of a process that regulates mRNA transcription. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p95]
- Girls with Rett syndrome often show a period of markedly impaired social interaction that may be indistinguishable from autism spectrum disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p95]
- Rett syndrome is caused by loss-of-function mutations in the MECP2 gene encoding a transcriptional regulator that binds to methylated cytosine bases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1532]
- Girls with Rett syndrome have normal development until 6-18 months, then regress, losing speech and hand skills. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1532]
- The prevalence of Rett syndrome is approximately 1 in 10,000 live female births. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1532]
- Studies in mouse models show that overexpression of BDNF improves the Rett phenotype. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1532]
- Phase II human trials with BDNF-related compounds including insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1) are currently underway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1532]
- Studies of Rett syndrome have demonstrated the potential reversibility of developmental phenotypes even into adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1534]
