# GABA

## Summary

GABA (γ-aminobutyric acid) is the major inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain and spinal cord. It acts on GABAA receptors (ionotropic Cl− channels) and GABAB receptors (metabotropic G protein-coupled receptors). GABAA receptors are targets of many clinically important drugs including benzodiazepines and barbiturates.

## Key facts

- GABA is the major inhibitory transmitter in the vertebrate brain and spinal cord. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p273]
- GABAA receptors are ionotropic receptors that directly open Cl− channels upon GABA binding. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p273]
- GABAB receptors are metabotropic receptors that activate K+ channels indirectly through G proteins. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p273]
- GABAA receptors are the target of benzodiazepines, barbiturates, general anesthetics, and alcohol. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p277]
