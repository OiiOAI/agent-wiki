# Elizabeth Loftus

## Summary

Elizabeth Loftus is a prominent cognitive psychologist known for her extensive research on suggestibility and false memories. She pioneered experimental techniques showing how suggested details can seep into eyewitness recollections. Her 'lost in the mall' study was one of the first to implant full false memories experimentally. She was a central figure in the recovered memories debate of the 1990s.

## Key facts

- Loftus reported one of the first attempts to experimentally implant a mildly traumatic autobiographical incident in the 'lost in the mall' study, where a teenager named Chris produced a detailed recollection of being lost in a shopping mall at age five despite never having that experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p167]
- Loftus documented that approximately one-quarter of 24 participants falsely remembered being lost as a child in a shopping mall after several probing interviews. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p167]
- In collaboration with Giuliana Mazzoni, Loftus showed that dream interpretation procedures could produce false memories of early experiences in the majority of participants. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p168]
- In 1974, Elizabeth Loftus co-authored 'Reconstruction of automobile destruction: An example of interaction between language and memory' with John Palmer in the Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]
- Loftus wrote about the case of Steve Titus, another man incorrectly identified as a rapist by a victim who said 'That's the closest' at lineup but changed to 'I'm absolutely positive that's the man' during trial. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]
- Loftus later co-authored a book with Ronald Cotton about the wrongful conviction case involving Jennifer Thompson, advocating for changes in eyewitness-related practice and wrongful conviction legislation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]
- In 2003, Loftus published 'Our changeable memories: Legal and practical implications' in Nature Reviews Neuroscience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p175]
