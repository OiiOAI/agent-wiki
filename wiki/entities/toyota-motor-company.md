# Toyota Motor Company

## Summary

Toyota Motor Company, particularly its Takaoka assembly plant, serves as a key case study demonstrating how workplace trust enables economic performance. At the Takaoka plant, any of the thousands of assembly line workers can halt the entire production line by pulling a cord at their workstation. Workers use this power responsibly to improve overall productivity, demonstrating the economic benefits of trust-based management. The contrast with traditional Ford plants, where workers were never trusted with such power, illustrates Fukuyama's thesis about trust and economic vitality.

## Key facts

- At Toyota's Takaoka assembly plant, any worker can halt the entire plant by pulling a cord at their workstation. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p17]
- Toyota workers use their power to stop the line responsibly to improve overall productivity. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p18]
- Management trusted workers not to abuse their power, and workers repaid this trust. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p18]
- Ford workers, having adopted Japanese techniques, are now trusted with similar powers and have greater control over their workplace. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p17]
