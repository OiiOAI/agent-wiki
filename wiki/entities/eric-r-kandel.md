# Eric R. Kandel

## Summary

Austrian-born American neuroscientist (born 1929) who received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2000 for his work on the physiological basis of memory storage. His personal history—escaping Vienna during the Nazi era—shaped his lifelong scientific fascination with memory.

## Key facts

- Kandel was born in Vienna, Austria in 1929 to parents who owned a small toy store on the Kutschkergasse. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p19]
- His family experienced Kristallnacht in November 1938, when his father was arrested and their apartment was ransacked. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p20]
- He emigrated to the United States with his brother in April 1939 at age nine, eventually reuniting with his parents in New York. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p46]
- He attended Yeshivah of Flatbush, Erasmus Hall High School, and Harvard College, where he majored in modern European history and literature. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p48]
- He entered NYU Medical School in 1952, initially planning to become a psychoanalyst, but became interested in the biology of the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p57]
- He worked in Harry Grundfest's laboratory at Columbia University in 1955-56, where he learned to study the brain one cell at a time. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p67]
- He married Denise Bystryn in 1956; she encouraged his exploration of the biological basis of mental function. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p63]
- Kandel moved to NYU in December 1965, bringing Denise and their children closer to aging parents. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- Kandel was influenced by Lewis's lead in medical school to follow the Metropolitan Opera. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- Kandel recruited Alden Spencer to NYU in 1965, forming the nucleus of the Division of Neurobiology and Behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p194]
- In 1967, Kandel and Spencer published a major review entitled 'Cellular Neurophysiological Approaches in the Study of Learning.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p196]
- Kandel received the Lasker Award in basic medical sciences in 1983, shared with Vernon Mountcastle. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p271]
- Kandel moved to Columbia University's College of Physicians and Surgeons in 1974 to replace his mentor Harry Grundfest. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p259]
- Kandel was senior investigator of the neural science initiative at the Howard Hughes Medical Institute at Columbia. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p264]
