# Johann Weyer

## Summary

A Dutch physician who published 'De Praestigiis Daemonum' in 1563, advocating reform of the witch justice system by noting that elderly women might fail the tear test due to lachrymal gland atrophy rather than demonic possession. His argument exemplifies 'liberal reform' of irrational systems by identifying narrow technical exceptions rather than challenging the system's foundations.

## Key facts

- Weyer acknowledged the existence of witches and the general appropriateness of witch-fighting techniques, but raised the caveat that elderly people, especially women, sometimes have atrophy of their lachrymal glands making it impossible to cry tears. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p599]
- Weyer's reform proposal—ensuring that poor elderly people weren't executed simply because their tear ducts didn't work anymore—exemplifies adding scientific exceptions to an irrational system rather than replacing the system itself. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p599]

## Inferences

- Inference: Weyer's approach parallels modern neuroscience-based criminal justice reform, which identifies biological exceptions (insanity, brain damage, adolescence) to the general rule of criminal responsibility without fundamentally questioning the system's assumptions.
