# Ludwig von Bertalanffy

## Summary

Ludwig von Bertalanffy (1901-1972) was a biologist who developed General Systems Theory, providing the first coherent framework for understanding living systems as organized wholes. Coming from the organismic school of biologists, he challenged the dominance of physics in science and emphasized the crucial difference between physical and biological systems. His concept of 'open systems'—living systems maintaining themselves far from equilibrium through continuous exchange with their environment—revolutionized understanding of life and influenced fields from ecology to management theory. He advocated interdisciplinary approaches and a 'science of wholeness.'

## Key facts

- Bertalanffy is commonly credited with the first formulation of a comprehensive theoretical framework describing principles of organization of living systems, advocating a science of wholeness. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p115]
- Bertalanffy recognized that living systems are open systems that cannot be described by classical thermodynamics, solving a 19th-century dilemma about how evolution could be reconciled with the Second Law. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p115]
- He introduced the concept of homeostasis extended to external interactions between system and environment, describing living systems as maintaining 'steady state' through continual flow and change. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p115]
- Bertalanffy noted that the analytical procedure for understanding systems depends on conditions (non-existent interaction between parts, linear relationships) not fulfilled in actual systems. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p112]
- Bertalanffy remarked that 'in one way or another we are forced to deal with complexities, with 'wholes' or 'systems' in all fields of knowledge,' implying 'a basic re-orientation of scientific thinking.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p126]
