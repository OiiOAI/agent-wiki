# Jonathan Freedman

## Summary

Jonathan Freedman was a social psychologist who conducted research demonstrating the difference between behavioral compliance achieved through threats and genuine internalization of values. His study with second-to-fourth-grade boys showed that children who were simply told not to play with a toy maintained that behavior six weeks later, while children threatened with severe punishment did not.

## Key facts

- Freedman found that 21 of 22 boys threatened with severe punishment did not touch the robot while he was present, but 77% chose the robot six weeks later when he was absent. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p92]
- Boys given only the instruction that it was 'wrong' to play with the robot (no threat) showed dramatically different results: when given freedom to play with any toy six weeks later, only 33% chose the robot. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p93]
