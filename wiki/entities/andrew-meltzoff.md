# Andrew Meltzoff

## Summary

Andrew Meltzoff is a developmental psychologist at the University of Washington known for his foundational research on infant imitation. His work with M. Keith Moore demonstrated that the visual perception of motor movements and the production of those movements are linked rather than independently acquired, suggesting imitation is innate.

## Key facts

- Meltzoff and Moore's study suggested that visual perception of motor movements and execution of imitative movements are not separately acquired abilities but are somehow linked in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p169]
- Meltzoff's research showed that newborns from 42 minutes to 72 hours old can accurately imitate facial expressions, demonstrating that imitation must be innate. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p169]
- Meltzoff demonstrated that infants understand they are 'like other people' through imitation—babies will imitate human actions but not those of objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p169]
