# Asch Conformity Experiment

## Summary

Solomon Asch's conformity experiments (1950s) tested whether people would give obviously wrong answers to match group consensus. Participants judged line lengths alone first (1% error). In groups where 7 confederates unanimously gave wrong answers, participants conformed ~33% of the time—demonstrating the powerful pull of social conformity even when the correct answer was obvious.

## Key facts

- In Asch's experiment, participants judged line lengths correctly ~99% of the time alone, but conformed to obviously wrong group answers ~33% of the time. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p474]
- Conformity first emerges when at least 3 people unanimously contradict the subject; maximum conformity occurs with around half a dozen contradictors. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p482]
- It takes less than 200 milliseconds for the brain to register disagreement and less than 380 milliseconds for activation predicting opinion change. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p471]
- When discovering disagreement, vmPFC, anterior cingulate cortex, and nucleus accumbens activate—circuitry for reinforcement learning that treats 'different = wrong'. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p472]

## Inferences

- Inference: Asch's work was motivated by post-WWII efforts to understand how intelligent Germans could support Nazism—a search for the roots of 'conformity to absurdity.'
