# Prefrontal cortex

## Summary

The prefrontal cortex, particularly the dorsolateral and ventromedial regions, plays a central role in consciousness, working memory, decision-making, and executive control. It serves as a hub in the global neuronal workspace, enabling conscious information to be broadcast throughout the brain. Lesions cause deficits in foresight, planning, and conscious inhibition.

## Key facts

- The prefrontal cortex serves as the brain's conscious router, collecting information from various processors, synthesizing it, and broadcasting the result to other areas. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p136]
- Patients with prefrontal lesions exhibit hemineglect, abulia, akinetic mutism, anosognosia, and impaired autonoetic memory—disorders directly related to consciousness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p131]
- The ventromedial prefrontal cortex specializes in unconscious valuation and constantly monitors actions and computes their potential value outside conscious awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p107]
- The frontal pole and ventromedial prefrontal cortex show similar activation during self-judgments and judgments about others, suggesting a unified social decision-making system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p144]
- Theta-burst TMS disruption of bilateral prefrontal regions creates a temporary blindsight-like state where objective performance is preserved but subjective confidence is lost. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p199]

## Inferences

- Inference: The prefrontal cortex's role in consciousness likely emerged through evolution as a social decision-making hub, allowing information to be shared among group members via language.
