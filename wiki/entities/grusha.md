# Grusha

## Summary

Grusha was the patient's first nursery-maid, employed during his earliest years (before the nurse). Her name ('grusha' meaning pear in his language) connected to her later significance through the screen memory of a yellow-striped butterfly. The crucial scene with Grusha occurred when the patient was about two and a half: he saw her kneeling on the floor scrubbing, with her buttocks projecting, and he urinated in the room. She responded with a threat of castration. This scene activated the primal scene picture (the mother's posture during intercourse) and represented the patient's first masculine sexual response, as he copied his father's action of urination. The scene determined his subsequent object choice, as all later love objects became surrogates for her.

## Inferences

- Inference: All later love objects were surrogates for Grusha, who through the accident of her attitude had become the patient's first mother surrogate.
- Inference: The Grusha scene was the key that unlocked the patient's object choice, which had been compulsively determined from that early age.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the scene with Grusha was a real experience or a later construction cannot be definitively determined, though Freud considers it to have been a real childhood memory.
