# Hair Cells

## Summary

Hair cells are the receptor cells of the inner ear, both vestibular and cochlear. They possess hair bundles that detect mechanical stimuli and transduce these signals into electrical responses. Cochlear hair cells include inner hair cells (the primary sensory receptors, numbering about 3,500) and outer hair cells (about 12,000, which provide mechanical amplification). Hair cells use specialized ribbon synapses for neurotransmitter release and receive efferent innervation for modulation.

## Key facts

- The organ of Corti contains approximately 16,000 hair cells: a single row of about 3,500 inner hair cells and three rows of approximately 12,000 outer hair cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p712]
- Inner hair cells form the primary sensory receptors of the cochlea, innervated by at least 90% of the spiral ganglion cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p727]
- Outer hair cells receive extensive efferent innervation and provide mechanical amplification through electromotility. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p727]
- Each hair bundle comprises stereocilia arranged in a hexagonal array, with inner hair cell bundles having a linear configuration and outer hair cell bundles having a V shape. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p714]
- Hair cells use ribbon synapses characterized by synaptic ribbons (presynaptic dense bodies) surrounded by synaptic vesicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p725]
- Hair cells release neurotransmitter continuously at rest, with release rate modulated upward or downward by depolarization or hyperpolarization. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p726]

## Inferences

- Inference: The predominance of afferent innervation to inner hair cells (90%) despite outer hair cells outnumbering them indicates that inner hair cells are the main source of neural information for hearing, while outer hair cells primarily modulate the mechanical input to inner hair cells.
