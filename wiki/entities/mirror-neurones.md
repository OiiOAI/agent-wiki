# Mirror neurones

## Summary

Mirror neurones are neurons that are active both when we perform an action and when we observe someone else performing that same action. They were first identified in the left pars opercularis (part of Broca's area) and were initially thought to be a speciality of the left hemisphere, but subsequent research showed that both hemispheres contain mirror neurones and contribute to watching and imitating.

## Key facts

- Mirror neurones are active both when we perform an action and when we watch others perform it. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p89]
- The left pars opercularis (part of Broca's area), critical for speech production, contains mirror neurones involved in the imitation of finger movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p89]
- Both the left and right pars opercularis equally have mirror neurones, and both hemispheres contribute to the processing of watching and imitating. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p89]
- Which hemisphere is involved depends not only on what and where the action is that we are copying, but also on how instrumental ('object-directed') that action is; such actions excite the left hemisphere, while imitation of non-instrumental actions comes from the right temporal and frontal lobes. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p89]
- Mirror neurones are a means of understanding another's intentions, amongst other things, and are not just about copying actions; they form part of our capacity to understand others and empathise with them. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p90]
- In imitating other people's facial expressions, it is the right pars opercularis, with its mirror neurones, which is the critically important area. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p90]
