# Neurons

## Summary

Neurons are the fundamental functional units of the nervous system, distinguished by their polarization with receptive dendrites at one end and transmitting axons at the other. This morphological asymmetry restricts the predominant flow of voltage impulses to one direction, enabling precise unidirectional neuronal signaling. Neurons are both electrically and chemically excitable, containing specialized ion channels and receptors that redistribute charge across the membrane to generate electrical signals. They possess secretory properties allowing neurotransmitter release at synapses and a cytoskeletal structure enabling efficient transport between the cell body and terminals.

## Key facts

- Neurons have a high degree of morphological and functional asymmetry with receptive dendrites at one end and a transmitting axon at the other, the structural basis for unidirectional neuronal signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p180]
- Neurons are both electrically and chemically excitable through specialized ion channels and receptors that facilitate the flow of specific inorganic ions, creating electrical currents that alter membrane voltage. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p180]
- Neurons use four major classes of channels: resting channels generate the resting potential, sensory receptor channels respond to stimuli, ligand-gated channels respond to neurotransmitters, and voltage-gated channels produce action potentials. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p179]
- There are hundreds of distinct types of neurons depending on dendritic morphology, axonal projections, and electrophysiological properties, specified largely by the genes expressed by each neuronal cell type. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p180]
- Axons are rich in smooth endoplasmic reticulum, individual synaptic vesicles, and their precursor membranes, while excluding ribosomes, rough endoplasmic reticulum, and the Golgi complex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p185]
