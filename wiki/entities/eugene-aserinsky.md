# Eugene Aserinsky

## Summary

Eugene Aserinsky was a graduate student at the University of Chicago who, in 1951, discovered Rapid Eye Movement (REM) sleep while recording his son Armond's eye movements during sleep. This discovery, made accidentally when Aserinsky thought his sleeping son had woken up based on the recorded eye movement signals, fundamentally changed the scientific understanding of dreaming.

## Key facts

- Aserinsky discovered REM sleep on a December night in 1951 while recording his eight-year-old son Armond's eye movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p57]
- Aserinsky was a graduate student without a bachelor's degree who talked his way into the University of Chicago. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p57]
- Aserinsky and Kleitman published their REM sleep discovery in a two-page article in Science in 1953. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p58]
