# Major Depressive Disorder

## Summary

Major depressive disorder is characterized by depressive episodes with symptoms including persistent depressed mood, anhedonia, sleep disturbance, appetite changes, psychomotor changes, fatigue, feelings of worthlessness, difficulty concentrating, and suicidal thoughts. It has a lifetime risk of approximately 19% in the US and is a leading cause of disability worldwide.

## Key facts

- The lifetime risk of major depressive disorder in the United States is approximately 19%, with 8.3% of the population suffering from it within any 1-year period. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1549]
- Major depressive disorder occurs equally in males and females before puberty, but after puberty it occurs more commonly in females with a ratio of approximately 2:1. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1550]
- Major depressive disorder often begins early in life, with those who have a first episode in childhood or adolescence having high likelihood of recurrence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1550]
- Decreased hippocampal volume is associated with major depression and correlates with duration of depressive episodes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1559]
- Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) are effective treatments for major depressive disorder. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1561]
