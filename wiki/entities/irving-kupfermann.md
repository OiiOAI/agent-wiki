# Irving Kupfermann

## Summary

Irving Kupfermann was a gifted behaviorist trained at the University of Chicago who collaborated with Kandel on studying Aplysia behavior. He was described as 'a large brain at the end of a long thin rod' by one of his students. Kupfermann proved an extremely well-informed and creative student of behavior who was clever at designing experiments.

## Key facts

- Kupfermann was allergic to rodents and cats, which led him to work on pill bugs for his Ph.D. dissertation. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p199]
- Together, Kandel and Kupfermann explored the behavior of Aplysia, including feeding, locomotor activity, inking, egg laying, and sexual behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p200]
- In 1968, Kupfermann and Kandel settled on the gill-withdrawal reflex as the simplest behavior to study. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p201]
- Kupfermann and Kandel discovered that the gill-withdrawal reflex can be modified by habituation and sensitization. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p202]
- Kupfermann found five motor cells that controlled the gill-withdrawal reflex. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p207]
- Kupfermann died in 2002 from Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease at the prime of his scientific career. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p284]
