# Kurt Gödel

## Summary

Kurt Gödel (1906–1978) was an Austrian-American logician and mathematician whose 1931 incompleteness proof showed that any consistent formal system containing arithmetic of whole numbers is incomplete—containing true statements that cannot be deduced from the system's postulates. His work is cited as supporting the idea that consistency of any new system must be determined by constructing or uncovering another system beyond it.

## Key facts

- In 1931, Gödel revealed that it is impossible to embrace mathematics within a single system of logic. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p448]
- Gödel proved that any consistent system that includes the arithmetic of whole numbers is incomplete—there are true statements or concepts within the system that cannot be deduced from the postulates. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p448]
- Gödel also proved that even though a system is consistent, its consistency cannot be demonstrated within the system itself. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p448]
- Since Gödel, Gerhard Gentzen and others have shown that a consistency proof of arithmetic can be found by appealing to systems outside that arithmetic. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p449]
- Gödel's Proof indirectly shows that to determine the consistency of any new system, one must construct or uncover another system beyond it, repeating this cycle for more elaborate systems. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p449]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gödel's work supports the idea that inward-oriented efforts to improve match-up of concept with observed reality will eventually increase the degree of mismatch, requiring creation of new, broader systems.
