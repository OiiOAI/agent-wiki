# CREB-1

## Summary

CREB-1 (cAMP response element binding protein 1) is a transcription factor that plays a central role in long-term memory consolidation. It is activated by phosphorylation by PKA and other kinases, then binds to CRE elements in DNA to activate transcription of memory-related genes.

## Key facts

- The catalytic subunit of PKA phosphorylates CREB-1, which then binds to CRE (cAMP response element) sequences in the promoter region of target genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1369]
- Phosphorylated CREB-1 recruits CREB-binding protein (CBP) to the promoter region, and CBP acetylates histones to facilitate transcription. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1369]
- CREB-1 activates transcription of genes including ubiquitin carboxyterminal hydrolase and C/EBP, which are important for long-term facilitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1371]
- miRNA-124 normally suppresses CREB-1 protein levels; serotonin inhibits miRNA-124, leading to disinhibition of CREB-1 translation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1371]
