# S. (Mnemonist)

## Summary

The subject of Luria's 30-year study, known only as 'S.' A Jewish man who began as a failed musician (due to hearing impairment from ear disease) and later became a professional mnemonist. S. possessed extraordinary synesthetic memory capabilities, experiencing sounds as colors, tastes, and tactile sensations. He could retain unlimited material for years but struggled with abstract concepts, metaphors, and the practical problem of forgetting unwanted memories.

## Key facts

- S. was a newspaper reporter when discovered in the 1920s; he never took notes but could repeat entire assignments word for word. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p17]
- S. had synesthetic experiences from early childhood, where words settled in his mind as 'puffs of steam or splashes' connected to their sounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p31]
- Each sound S. heard produced experiences of light and color; vowels appeared as simple figures while consonants appeared as splashes with distinct forms, colors, and tastes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p34]
- S. distributed images along mental 'streets' or routes in his mind, often using familiar locations like Gorky Street in Moscow or his childhood home in Torzhok. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p41]
- S. developed a technique of eidetic images where he would abbreviate images and use symbolic forms to remember complex material including foreign languages and mathematical formulas. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p51]
- S. experienced great difficulty understanding metaphors and figurative language, being unable to 'get behind the surface images' of words. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p9]
- S. had no childhood amnesia; his memories from the earliest period were so vivid they could cause him acute malaise and chagrin. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p8]
- S. developed a technique to forget by realizing that if he did not want an image to appear, it would not—achieved through autosuggestion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p71]

## Inferences

- Inference: S.'s synesthesia was central to his memory, providing additional 'extra' information that guaranteed accurate recall through synesthetic confirmation.
- Inference: S.'s difficulty with abstraction and metaphors suggests that his highly concrete, figurative thinking prevented him from forming general concepts from particulars.
