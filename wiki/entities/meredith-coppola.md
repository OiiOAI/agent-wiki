# Meredith Coppola

## Summary

A woman murdered by her husband who lived six months longer than Nicole Brown Simpson, whose case shares similar patterns of domestic violence preceding murder. Used by de Becker to illustrate that spousal homicide is predictable and follows common patterns.

## Key facts

- Meredith Coppola was murdered by her husband, living six months longer than Nicole Brown Simpson, in a case with similar patterns of domestic violence preceding murder. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p117]
- De Becker states that 'if I told of all the women killed in America this year by a husband or boyfriend, the book you are holding would be four thousand pages long—and the stories would be stunningly similar.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p117]
- The book notes that while Nicole Brown's murder became famous, Coppola's case occurred 'thousands of miles from Brentwood' and received less attention despite sharing the same domestic violence patterns. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p117]

## Inferences

- Inference: The book uses Coppola's case to argue that spousal homicide is America's most predictable murder, and that the famous Simpson case is 'nothing more than an example of this common crime.'
