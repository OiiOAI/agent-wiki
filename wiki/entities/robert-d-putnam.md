# Robert D. Putnam

## Summary

Robert D. Putnam is an American political scientist best known for his work on social capital and civic engagement. His seminal book 'Making Democracy Work: Civic Traditions in Modern Italy' (1993), co-authored with others, documented the significant regional differences in civic culture between northern and southern Italy and their effects on institutional performance.

## Key facts

- Putnam's 'Making Democracy Work' (1993) studied civic traditions across Italian regions. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Putnam documented the North-South distribution of civic community characteristics in Italy. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Putnam found that regional differences in civic engagement correlated with economic development and government effectiveness. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p393]
- Putnam's article 'Bowling Alone' was published in Journal of Democracy 6 (1995): 65-78. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p448]
- The AARP, with 33 million members in 1993, is described by Putnam as the world's largest private organization after the Catholic church. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p448]
- Putnam documented that social capital and civic associations have been declining in America. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p448]
- Putnam observed that the growth of social fragmentation continues even when crime rates in some urban areas declined in the late 1980s and early 1990s. [raw/books/sociology/Trust - Francis Fukuyama.pdf#p448]
