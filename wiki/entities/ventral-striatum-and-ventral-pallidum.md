# Ventral Striatum and Ventral Pallidum

## Summary

The ventral striatum (VS) and ventral pallidum (VP) are components of the limbic circuit within the basal ganglia. The VS receives inputs from limbic structures including the amygdala, anterior insula, and orbitofrontal cortex. Both structures process both appetitive and aversive information and are involved in approach/avoidance behaviors and anxiety-related disorders when dysfunctional.

## Key facts

- The VS and VP function as an interface between motivation and action, processing both positive and negative motivational information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p52]
- The VS is involved in aversive processing, with selective activation in specific subterritories producing stereotyped behaviors and anxiety manifestations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p55]
- Bicuculline injections into VP induce compulsive grooming and licking/biting fingers, and escape behaviors specifically in aversive contexts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p56]
- The VS-VP pathway (indirect pathway in basal ganglia) contributes to aversive processing and avoidance behavior, with aberrant function potentially contributing to anxiety states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p58]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ventral striatopallidal pathway is critical for controlling approach/avoidance behavior in both normal and pathological contexts.
- Inference: Disruption of VS-VP function could underlie psychiatric disorders involving abnormal aversive processing and decision-making.
