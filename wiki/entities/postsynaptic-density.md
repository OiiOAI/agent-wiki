# Postsynaptic Density

## Summary

The postsynaptic density is a specialized protein complex at excitatory synapses containing glutamate receptors, scaffolding proteins like PSD-95, and signaling molecules that organize receptor localization and downstream signaling.

## Key facts

- A typical PSD is approximately 350 nm in diameter and contains about 20 NMDA receptors localized near the center and 10-50 AMPA receptors more peripherally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p282]
- PSD-95 is a membrane-associated protein with three PDZ domains that binds NMDA receptor GluN2 subunits and TARP proteins associated with AMPA receptors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p282]
- PSD-95 interacts with neuroligin, which contacts presynaptic neurexin across the synaptic cleft, an interaction important for synapse development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p282]
- The PSD contains dozens of proteins forming a complex network that links receptors to the actin cytoskeleton and organizes downstream signaling cascades. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p282]
