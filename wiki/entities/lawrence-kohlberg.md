# Lawrence Kohlberg

## Summary

Lawrence Kohlberg (1927-1987) was an American psychologist who developed the influential stage model of moral development in the 1950s-80s. His theory proposed three levels of moral reasoning (preconventional, conventional, postconventional), each with two stages, ranging from self-interest (avoiding punishment, seeking reward) through social conformity and law-abidingness to principled conscience that may supersede legal rules. His work was criticized for studying only males and for overemphasizing cognitive reasoning over emotional intuition in moral decision-making.

## Key facts

- Kohlberg proposed three levels of moral development: Preconventional (ego-oriented, ages ~8-10), Conventional (relationship-oriented, most adolescents and adults), and Postconventional (principle-oriented, rare). [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p187]
- Kohlberg's work was criticized for studying only males, prompting Carol Gilligan's challenge that females value care over justice in moral judgments. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p190]
- Kohlberg's cognitive emphasis on moral reasoning over intuition was challenged by social intuitionist views by Martin Hoffman and Jonathan Haidt. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p190]
- Kohlberg found that moral reasoning rarely predicts moral action—who does the right thing when it's hard. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: The finding that moral reasoning (Kohlberg's domain) poorly predicts moral action suggests that the cognitive approach to moral development captures only part of the phenomenon, with automatic processes and emotional responses also crucial

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether postconventional reasoning (stages 5-6) actually exists as a stable developmental stage or represents rare peak states is debated
