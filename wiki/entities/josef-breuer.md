# Josef Breuer

## Summary

Josef Breuer (1842–1925) was a Viennese physician and Freud's early collaborator on hysteria. Freud mentions that Breuer sometimes used hypnotism for therapeutic purposes even before Freud's Paris visit. Breuer developed the cathartic method of treatment, leading patients under hypnosis back to the psychical prehistory of ailments. Freud later combined Breuer's cathartic treatment with Weir Mitchell's rest cure.

## Key facts

- Freud became aware that his future collaborator Breuer was sometimes using hypnotism for therapeutic purposes while Freud was still a student. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p66]
- Breuer's cathartic method leads the patient under hypnosis back to the psychical prehistory of the ailment and compels him to acknowledge the psychical occasion on which the disorder originated. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
- Freud later recommended a combination of Weir Mitchell's rest cure with Breuer's cathartic treatment. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p98]
- From the very first Freud made use of hypnosis in another manner apart from hypnotic suggestion—Breuer's method of using hypnotism for tracing back the origin of symptoms. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p67]
- Breuer and Freud jointly published the 'Preliminary Communication' (1893a) on the psychical mechanism of hysterical phenomena. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p214]
- Breuer introduced the term 'hypnoid' to describe states whose content is cut off from the remaining content of consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p177]
- The therapy described consists in reviving the trauma in a state of somnambulism, then abreacting and correcting it, or bringing it into normal consciousness under hypnosis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p178]
- Breuer introduced the cathartic method through his work with Anna O., showing that hysterical symptoms disappeared when the patient described the pathogenic event with accompanying affect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p769]
- Breuer proposed the concept of 'ideas inadmissible to consciousness' (Bewusstseinsunfähig) for ideas intense enough to cause somatic phenomena but remaining outside consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p743]
- Breuer argued that the disposition to hysteria lies in an excess rather than a defect of nervous function, contrary to Janet's theory of psychological weakness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p756]
- Breuer studied and successfully treated a famous case of hysteria between 1880 and 1882, later published with Freud. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5066]
- Breuer published his observation on the treatment of hysteria jointly with Freud in 1893 and 1895. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p5066]

## Inferences

- Inference: The collaboration with Breuer on cathartic treatment represented a crucial step in Freud's development of psychoanalytic method.
- Inference: Breuer's distinction between 'splitting of the mind' and Janet's 'splitting of consciousness' reflects his view that unconscious ideation exceeds what becomes organized as a secondary conscious system.
- Inference: Although Pierre Janet may claim priority of publication, Breuer's collaboration with Freud led to the systematic development of psychoanalytic technique.
