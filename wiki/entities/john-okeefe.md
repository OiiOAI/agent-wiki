# John O'Keefe

## Summary

John O'Keefe is a neuroscientist at University College London who made the groundbreaking discovery in 1971 that neurons in the hippocampus register information about space rather than single sensory modalities. He found that the hippocampus contains a cognitive map of external space and identified the pyramidal cells as 'place cells' that process location-specific information.

## Key facts

- In 1971, O'Keefe discovered that neurons in the hippocampus of rats register information about the space surrounding the animal, a modality depending on several senses. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p292]
- O'Keefe showed the hippocampus contains a representation—a map—of external space and referred to the pyramidal cells processing place information as 'place cells.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p292]
- O'Keefe applied Kantian logic about space to explicit memory, arguing that many forms of explicit memory use spatial coordinates. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p318]
- O'Keefe studied gender differences in spatial orientation, finding women use nearby cues or landmarks while men rely more on an internalized geometric map. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p325]
