# Subthalamic Nucleus

## Summary

The subthalamic nucleus (STN) is a small diencephalic structure positioned between the thalamus and substantia nigra, holding a central position in the indirect pathway of the basal ganglia where it acts as a brake on motor systems and plays critical roles in response inhibition and decision-making under conflict.

## Key facts

- The STN receives direct cortical projections via the hyperdirect pathway and serves as an input nucleus of the basal ganglia, not merely a relay in the indirect pathway. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p302]
- The STN can be subdivided into functionally segregated territories: motor (dorsolateral), associative (intermediate), and limbic (ventromedial). [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p303]
- Bilateral STN lesions in monkeys produce hemiballismus, excessive involuntary contralateral movements, while STN overactivity characterizes Parkinson's disease pathophysiology. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p301]
- Computational models propose that the STN provides a 'hold your horses' no-go signal during decision conflict, buying time to select optimal responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p305]

## Inferences

- Inference: STN deep brain stimulation (DBS) at high frequencies (>100 Hz) improves motor symptoms in PD by disrupting pathological overactivity, though it can impair cognitive functions dependent on associative STN territories.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The exact dynamical interplay between direct, hyperdirect, and indirect cortico-basal ganglia pathways during response inhibition remains incompletely characterized.
