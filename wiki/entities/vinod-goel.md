# Goel, Vinod

## Summary

Vinod Goel is a cognitive neuroscientist who has conducted extensive research on the neural basis of planning, deductive reasoning, and creative problem solving. His work combines neuroimaging and neuropsychological approaches to understand how the prefrontal cortex supports different types of cognitive processes.

## Key facts

- Goel and Vartanian (2005) used Match Problems to investigate the neural correlates of hypothesis generation, finding that set shifts specifically implicate the right ventral lateral PFC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p417]
- Goel (1995) proposed that lateral transformations are facilitated by imprecise, ambiguous, fluid, indeterminate, or vague mental representations, while precise and concrete representations can hinder them. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p418]
- Goel (2007) has shown that the left DLPFC is involved in deductive reasoning paradigms where subjects judge whether a conclusion follows logically from given information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p416]
- Goel has proposed that the brain's architecture for complex systems is hierarchical, making it efficient to reconstitute component systems to serve multiple complex activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p426]
