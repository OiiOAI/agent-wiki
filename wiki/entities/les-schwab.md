# Les Schwab

## Summary

Leslie Schwab (1917–2007) was an American businessman who built Les Schwab Tire Centers into a连锁经营 with over 300 stores and over $1 billion in annual sales. Starting with a small tire shop in Bend, Oregon, he developed innovative practices including profit sharing and supermarket-style product selection.

## Key facts

- Leslie Schwab was born in Bend, Oregon and after WWII service, bought OK Rubber Welders, turning a $32,000-a-year business into one generating $150,000 annually. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p397]
- Schwab built the company to over 300 stores with sales exceeding $1 billion annually through innovations such as profit sharing and independence from tire manufacturers. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p397]
- Les Schwab was born in Bend, Oregon in 1917. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p420]
- After WWII service in the Air Cadet Corps, he bought OK Rubber Welders and grew it from $32,000 to $150,000 annual revenue. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p420]
- The Les Schwab chain now operates over 300 stores with sales exceeding $1 billion annually. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p420]
- Schwab succeeded despite competing against manufacturer-tied stores with cost advantages and later against Costco, Sam's Club, and Sears. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p419]
- Schwab must have had a very clever incentive structure, personnel selection system, and was described as 'an artist' in advertising. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p421]
- Munger notes Schwab had no education yet succeeded, questioning the value of business school graduates for solving such problems. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p421]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger uses Les Schwab as an example of extreme success from getting many factors right, including clever incentive structures, personnel selection, and advertising.
- Inference: Les Schwab likely rode the Japanese tire invasion wave in early competition periods, as Munger identifies this as one necessary success factor.
