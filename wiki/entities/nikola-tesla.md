# Nikola Tesla

## Summary

Nikola Tesla (1856-1943) was the inventor of the revolutionary alternating current (AC) electric motor. He used narrative fiction — particularly Goethe's Faust — to envision his breakthrough, experiencing a "flash of lightning" while reciting poetry. His method combined vision with self-reliance, leading him to defy Thomas Edison's DC consensus and pioneer the electric age.

## Key facts

- Tesla was born in 1856 on the borderlands of the Austrian Empire. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p193]
- He saw that alternating current (AC) could be conducted at high voltage over long distances, making electricity available to everyone everywhere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p193]
- Tesla rejected prototyping, stating: "We have, undoubtedly, certain finer fibers that enable us to perceive truths when logical deduction is futile". [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p194]
- In February 1882, while reciting Goethe's Faust in Budapest, Tesla had a flash of inspiration for the AC motor. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p194]
- Tesla described the moment: "The glow retreats, done is the day of toil..." — and as he uttered these words the idea came like a flash of lightning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p194]
- While imagining a mind rotating the earth while standing still, Tesla envisioned a motor that performed the same action: a magnetic field rotating while its metal casing stood still. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p195]
