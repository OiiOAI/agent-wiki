# The Black Swan

## Summary

A book by Nassim Taleb that examines highly improbable events with massive consequences. Taleb introduced the concept of the narrative fallacy and argued that human cognition is poorly equipped to anticipate and understand extreme events, making the illusion of understanding particularly dangerous in complex systems.

## Key facts

- Taleb introduced the concept of narrative fallacy to describe how our tendency to construct coherent stories about the past makes it difficult to accept the limits of our ability to predict. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p257]
- Taleb argued that our intuitive prediction abilities fail especially in the realm of rare, high-impact events (Black Swans), yet we continue to seek and believe confident explanations after such events occur. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p281]
- Taleb highlighted that the 2008 financial crisis was frequently described as 'inevitable' only after it occurred, demonstrating the narrative fallacy in action. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p260]
- Taleb coined the term 'Black Swan' for rare events with massive impact, arguing that human understanding is particularly limited in these domains where prediction markets and expert judgment perform poorly. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p281]

## Inferences

- Inference: Taleb's Black Swan concept complements Kahneman's work by emphasizing that the limits of predictability are most severe precisely in domains where people most desperately seek predictions.
