# Donnie Moore

## Summary

Donnie Moore was a pitcher for the California Angels who gave up a game-winning home run to Dave Henderson in the 1986 American League Championship Series. The memory of this pitch haunted him relentlessly, contributing to depression and ultimately to a tragic incident in July 1989 where he shot his wife before killing himself. His case exemplifies the devastating power of persistence.

## Key facts

- Donnie Moore was the California Angels pitcher who gave up the game-winning home run to Dave Henderson in the ninth inning of the fifth game of the 1986 ALCS. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p217]
- Despite teammates' reminders of games he had saved, Moore focused only on the fateful pitch, blaming himself for the team's defeat. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p217]
- Fans and media strengthened Moore's vivid recollection by talking about the incident incessantly, making it impossible for him to find relief in the passing of time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p221]
- Moore's agent commented that 'even when he was told that one pitch doesn't make a season, he couldn't get over it. That home run killed him.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p218]
