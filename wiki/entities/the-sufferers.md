# The Sufferers

## Summary

One of three post-infidelity outcomes identified by Esther Perel. Sufferers are couples who remain trapped in an endless cycle of bitterness, revenge, and self-pity after an affair. Years after the infidelity, it remains the epicenter of their relationship. They endlessly revisit the same grievances, blame each other for their agony, and may have stayed together ostensibly for the family while remaining emotionally dead to each other.

## Key facts

- In marriages of Sufferers, 'there is little room for neutrality, because the partners take the call for self-reflection as a personal attack'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p292]
- Debbie, who stayed with Marc after his extramarital exploits, 'constantly makes him feel that he is lucky she didn't kick him out, as if only he stands to lose everything they've built'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p292]
- Debbie states: 'I want to make love, but it'd be like saying everything is okay now.' They haven't had sex since the affair three years ago. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p292]
- Perel observes: 'In these highly reactive couples, whatever was broken can't be pieced back together. The relationship wears a permanent cast.'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p293]
