# Charlie Munger

## Summary

Charlie Munger is Warren Buffett's business partner and vice chairman of Berkshire Hathaway who advocates building a broad toolbox of mental models. He stresses that the key to worldly wisdom is having many models from different disciplines and connecting experience to them, warning against remembering isolated facts without a theoretical framework.

## Key facts

- Munger states: If you just remember isolated facts and try and bang 'em back, you can't really know anything. Facts don't hang together on a latticework of theory. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p111]
- Munger advocates looking out for the most powerful concepts in every discipline and understanding them so thoroughly they become part of thinking. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p110]
- The quote An idea or a fact is not worth more merely because it is easily available to you is attributed to Munger. [raw/books/productivity/How to Take Smart Notes_ One Simple Technique to Boost - Sönke Ahrens.pdf#p123]
- Munger considers Benjamin Franklin his greatest hero, sharing characteristics of self-teaching, hard work, insatiable curiosity, and patient demeanor. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p55]
- Munger estimates he uses approximately one hundred mental models for investment analysis. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p57]
- He co-founded the Munger Partnership in 1962-1975, which Buffett later analyzed as part of Berkshire Hathaway's history. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p60]
- Munger calls his approach 'sit on your ass investing,' emphasizing few transactions, lower broker costs, less nonsense, and favorable tax treatment. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p60]
- He believes a portfolio of three companies is sufficient diversification. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p60]
- Munger has never taken any course in chemistry, economics, psychology, or business, yet psychology forms a foundation of his system. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p59]
- He believes temperament (inborn tendencies) combined with hard work, intellect, and experience are all needed to become a great investor. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p61]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger's success stems from a combination of character traits (preparation, patience, discipline, objectivity) and his multidisciplinary mental models approach, rather than any single factor.
