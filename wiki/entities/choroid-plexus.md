# Choroid Plexus

## Summary

The choroid plexus is an epithelial structure that produces most of the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) bathing the brain. It appears as epithelial invaginations where the lateral, third, and fourth ventricles form, developing into a ciliated cuboidal epithelial layer that encapsulates a stromal and immune cell network and extensive capillary bed. The choroid plexus epithelial cells form tight junctions and regulate the import of water, ions, metabolites, and protein mediators into the CSF through active transport mechanisms.

## Key facts

- The choroid plexus produces most of the cerebrospinal fluid that bathes the brain, with choroid plexus epithelial cells forming tight junctions that prevent unregulated movement of molecules into the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p161]
- The choroid plexus transports fluid and solutes from serum into the CNS to generate CSF, with fenestrated capillaries allowing free passage of water and small molecules into the stromal space, followed by regulated import through transporters and channels in choroid plexus epithelium. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p161]
- Choroid plexus epithelial cells synthesize and secrete many proteins into the CSF that modulate development of neural stem cells and may regulate processes such as cortical plasticity during embryonic and postnatal brain development. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]
- The choroid plexus secretome can be altered by inflammatory signals from the periphery or within the brain, affecting neuronal function during infection and in aging, and may include microRNAs, long noncoding RNAs, and extracellular vesicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p162]
