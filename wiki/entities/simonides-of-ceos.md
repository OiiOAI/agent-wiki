# Simonides of Ceos

## Summary

Simonides of Ceos (556–468 BCE) was an ancient Greek lyric poet credited with inventing the method of loci mnemonic technique according to legend recounted by Quintilian and Cicero. The story describes how he saved himself during a banquet roof collapse by remembering where each guest had been seated, leading to his insight about associating items with locations for memory recall.

## Key facts

- According to legend, Simonides was hired by a nobleman called Scopas to declaim a poem in Scopas's honor that included references to Castor and Pollux. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]
- Scopas paid Simonides only half the agreed sum, telling him to charge the rest to Castor and Pollux. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]
- Castor and Pollux supposedly called Simonides to the door just before the roof of the banquet hall came crashing down, saving his life. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]
- Simonides was able to identify the crushed bodies by remembering where each guest had been seated at the banquet. [raw/books/neuroscience/The forgetting machine _ memory, perception, and the - Rodrigo Quian Quiroga; hoopla digital.pdf#p177]

## Inferences

- Inference: Simonides's experience at the banquet led him to realize that organized spatial positions are easier to recall than abstract information, which formed the basis of the method of loci.
