# Daniel Povinelli

## Summary

Director of the Cognitive Evolution Group and the Center for Child Studies at the University of Louisiana. Povinelli is known for his critical re-examination of chimpanzee cognition and his 'reinterpretation hypothesis' suggesting that chimpanzee social behaviors may not reflect similar psychological processes to humans.

## Key facts

- Povinelli argues that chimpanzee mental states are primarily about ecological concerns: remembering food locations, watching for predators, and tracking the alpha male. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p61]
- Povinelli proposes that for any ability humans and chimpanzees share, humans augment it with systems unique to our species. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p61]
- Povinelli found that a trained chimp cannot teach another chimp to pull a rope for a food reward, suggesting lack of intentional teaching. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p69]
