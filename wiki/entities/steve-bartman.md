# Steve Bartman

## Summary

A Chicago Cubs fan who deflected a foul ball during the 2003 NLCS Game 6, preventing left fielder Moises Alou from catching it. The Cubs subsequently lost the game and the series. Bartman became a scapegoat, exemplifying how observers blame others' bad outcomes entirely on their decision-making while ignoring the role of luck.

## Key facts

- In 2003, Steve Bartman deflected a foul ball in Game 6 of the NLCS, preventing Moises Alou from catching it. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1000-L1008]
- The Cubs led 3-0 at the time and were five outs from the World Series, but ultimately lost the game and Game 7. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1009-L1012]
- Bartman was blamed by 40,000 fans at Wrigley Field and subsequently by Cubs fans worldwide, receiving death threats and verbal abuse. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1013-L1020]
- Alex Gibney's documentary showed multiple fans reached for the ball, but Bartman was uniquely blamed despite similar behavior by others. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1020-L1030]
- The Cubs still had a 3-0 lead after the play with five outs remaining, and the subsequent collapse involved multiple factors outside Bartman's control, including an error by shortstop Alex Gonzalez. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1031-L1038]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bartman's case illustrates the human tendency to attribute outcomes entirely to decision-making while ignoring luck, even when multiple people behaved identically and the subsequent events were clearly outside the individual's control.
