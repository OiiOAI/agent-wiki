# Cass Sunstein

## Summary

Cass Sunstein is a Harvard law professor who conducted a massive study on ideological diversity in federal judicial panels with colleagues at the University of Chicago Law School. The study found that even life-tenured judges sworn to uphold the law succumb to groupthink and polarization, but a single panelist from the opposing party had 'a large disciplining effect.'

## Key facts

- Cass Sunstein's study of over 6,000 federal appeals and nearly 20,000 individual votes found that judicial voting generally followed political lines, with even life-tenured judges unable to achieve pure open-mindedness. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1350-L1360]
- The study found 'strong evidence of ideological dampening': Democrat appointees voted for plaintiffs 43% of the time overall, but just 10% when sitting with two Republican appointees. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1365-L1375]
- A single panelist from the opposing party had 'a large disciplining effect' on federal appellate panels. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1360-L1370]
- The study concluded that judges follow the human instinct of succumbing to groupthink, with like-minded judges going to extremes together. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1375-L1380]
