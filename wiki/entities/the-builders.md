# The Builders

## Summary

One of three post-infidelity outcomes identified by Esther Perel. Builders are couples who remain together because they value commitment and the life they've created. They care about each other and want to preserve the family and community. They can move past the infidelity but don't transcend it—their marriages revert to a peaceful version of the status quo without undergoing significant change.

## Key facts

- Perel describes Builders as those who 'focus on structural strengths' of their relationship, 'not looking for massive renovations; they simply want to come back to the home they know and the pillow they can rest on'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p293]
- For Builders, 'lying and deceiving are more agonizing than thrilling, and the end of the affair is simply a relief'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p294]
- Perel notes that for Builders, 'familiarity over the roller coaster of risky romantic love and sexual passion' is preferred, and 'Self-fulfillment without an ethical mooring feels hollow'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p294]
- Joanna's case illustrates the Builder experience: she reflected they 'almost divorced' but felt 'relief that I was going to stay and not destroy my family'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p294]
- Lyle, another Builder, states: 'I'd still rather look at porn and stay out of trouble than risk losing my family'. [raw/books/psychology/The State of Affairs - Esther Perel.pdf#p294]
