# Katharina

## Summary

An eighteen-year-old girl whom Freud encountered at a mountain refuge hut in the Hohe Tauern in 1893. She suffered from anxiety attacks characterized by pressure on the eyes, dizziness, crushing sensation in the chest, choking, hammering in the head, and the hallucination of a furious face. Her case illustrates how pre-sexual experiences become traumatic only later when the individual acquires understanding of sexual life ('Nachträglichkeit'), and demonstrates the mechanism of conversion through the scene of discovering her uncle and cousin in sexual intercourse.

## Key facts

- Her anxiety attacks began with pressure on the eyes, dizziness, a crushing sensation in the chest preventing breath, choking in the throat, and hammering in the head, accompanied by the hallucination of an awful man's face. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p651]
- The first attack occurred two years earlier when she discovered her uncle (her father) and cousin Franziska in sexual intercourse; the physical symptoms of choking and loss of breath appeared immediately after the discovery. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p652]
- She had earlier experiences where her uncle made sexual advances toward her when she was fourteen, including an episode where she woke to feel his body in bed beside her, experiences she did not clearly understand at the time. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p654]
- When she discovered her uncle with Franziska, she suddenly understood the earlier experiences and fended them off; following a period of 'incubation,' the symptoms of conversion set in, with vomiting as a substitute for moral and physical disgust. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p655]
- The recurring hallucinated face was later identified as her uncle's face when he was in a rage against her for having exposed his affair, threatening to do something to her. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p656]
- The splitting of consciousness was due not to conscious rejection but to ignorance—she was not yet capable of coping with sexual experiences at the time they occurred. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p657]
- Freud noted that impressions from the pre-sexual period which produced no effect on the child attain traumatic power at a later date as memories, when the individual has acquired an understanding of sexual life. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p657]
- The conversion did not occur immediately after the trauma but after an interval of incubation, which Charcot described as the 'period of psychical working out.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p658]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud later revealed that 'Katharina' was in fact the daughter, not the niece, of the landlady, and that the man she discovered was her own father, making her illness a result of sexual temptations by her father.
- Inference: The vomiting as the first hysterical symptom had abreacted, but the anxiety attacks remained and acquired a fresh content (the hallucinated face), consistent with her later reports to her aunt.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the analysis would have been more complete with hypnosis, as the patient could agree that Freud's interpretations were probably true but could not recognize them as personally experienced.
