# Emile Durkheim

## Summary

Emile Durkheim (1858–1917) was a late-nineteenth-century French sociologist widely regarded as one of the founders of modern sociology. In the context of Joseph LeDoux's 'The Deep History of Ourselves,' Durkheim is cited for his definition of suicide, which requires that death result from a positive or negative act of the victim himself, which he knows or believes will produce the intended result—death. LeDoux uses Durkheim's definition to argue that true suicide requires reflective social consciousness and is thus a social condition unique to humans, not possible for animals with purely internal physiological constraints.

## Key facts

- Durkheim proposed that suicide applies only to cases of death resulting directly or indirectly from a positive or negative act of the victim himself, which he knows or believes will produce the intended result—death. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p364]
- Durkheim argued that because this kind of conception depends on reflective social consciousness, animals with purely internal physiological constraints are incapable of true suicide. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p364]
- Durkheim insisted there were 'social facts' irreducible to facts about individuals—social facts emerge as people interact and are as real as individual mental states. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p246]
- Durkheim proposed that humans have two sets of social sentiments: first binding individuals to each other, second binding individuals to society as a whole. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p247]
- His definition of religion emphasizes unity: 'A religion is a unified system of beliefs and practices...which unite into one single moral community called a Church.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p269]
- Durkheim was born on April 15, 1858, in Epinal, France, descended from a long line of rabbis. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p48]
- He developed the concept of social facts as forces and structures that are external to, and coercive of, the individual. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p47]
- In The Rules of Sociological Method (1895), Durkheim differentiated between material and nonmaterial social facts. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p48]
- Durkheim argued that primitive societies were held together primarily by a strongly held common morality called the collective conscience. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p50]
- He argued that the source of religion was society itself, and that society comes to define certain things as religious and others as profane. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p50]
- Durkheim founded the journal L'année sociologique in 1898, which became a powerful force in the development and spread of sociological ideas. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p50]
- He died on November 15, 1917, a celebrated figure in French intellectual circles. [raw/books/sociology/现代社会学理论 - 第7版 - 美 - 乔治 - 瑞泽尔 - 后浪大学堂 - 2018.pdf#p51]
