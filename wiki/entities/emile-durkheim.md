# Emile Durkheim

## Summary

Emile Durkheim (1858–1917) was a late-nineteenth-century French sociologist widely regarded as one of the founders of modern sociology. In the context of Joseph LeDoux's 'The Deep History of Ourselves,' Durkheim is cited for his definition of suicide, which requires that death result from a positive or negative act of the victim himself, which he knows or believes will produce the intended result—death. LeDoux uses Durkheim's definition to argue that true suicide requires reflective social consciousness and is thus a social condition unique to humans, not possible for animals with purely internal physiological constraints.

## Key facts

- Durkheim proposed that suicide applies only to cases of death resulting directly or indirectly from a positive or negative act of the victim himself, which he knows or believes will produce the intended result—death. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p364]
- Durkheim argued that because this kind of conception depends on reflective social consciousness, animals with purely internal physiological constraints are incapable of true suicide.
