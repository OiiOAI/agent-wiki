# AMPA Receptor

## Summary

AMPA receptors are ionotropic glutamate receptor-channels that mediate fast excitatory synaptic transmission in the central nervous system, with permeability properties determined by RNA editing of the GluA2 subunit.

## Key facts

- AMPA receptor subunits are encoded by four separate genes (GluA1-GluA4) and form tetramers, often containing the GluA2 subunit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p279]
- Most AMPA receptor-channels are impermeable to Ca2+ because the GluA2 subunit undergoes RNA editing that replaces glutamine with arginine in the M2 pore loop. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p281]
- X-ray crystal structures show AMPA receptors have a bilobed clamshell-like ligand-binding domain that closes upon glutamate binding, triggering conformational changes that open the ion channel. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p280]
- AMPA receptors associate with TARP (transmembrane AMPA receptor regulatory protein) auxiliary subunits that enhance surface trafficking, synaptic localization, and channel open time. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p279]
- The stargazer mouse mutant lacks the TARP stargazin and shows complete loss of AMPA receptors from cerebellar granule cells, resulting in cerebellar ataxia and seizures. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p279]
