# Vestigial Expressions

## Summary

Vestigial expressions refer to Darwin's claim in 'Expression of the Emotions in Man and Animals' that smiles, frowns, and other emotional expressions were useless, vestigial movements—products of evolution that no longer serve a function, like the human tailbone and appendix and the wings of the ostrich. Darwin made this statement over a dozen times in Expression. He believed emotional expressions were compelling examples for broader arguments about evolution: if these expressions are useless in humans but shared with other animals, they must exist because they were functional in a long-gone common ancestor. Modern scientists misattribute to Darwin the claim that expressions evolved to serve a survival function, but Darwin actually said the opposite.
