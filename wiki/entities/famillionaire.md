# famillionär

## Summary

The word 'famillionär' (combining 'familiär' meaning 'familiarly' and 'Millionär' meaning 'millionaire') is Freud's primary example of condensation with composite word formation in jokes, taken from Heine's description of Hirsch-Hyacinth.

## Key facts

- Freud uses 'famillionär' (from 'familiär' + 'Millionär') as his principal example of condensation with composite word formation, demonstrating how the joke depends entirely on its verbal expression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2669]
- The joke is Heine's: Hirsch-Hyacinth claims Baron Rothschild treated him 'quite famillionairely,' meaning 'as a millionaire would treat a social inferior, in a familiar manner.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2667]
- The composite word 'famillionär' stands for the 'Millionär' portion of the omitted sentence and puts the reader in a position to infer the second sentence that has been omitted. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2669]
