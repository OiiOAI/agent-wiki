# Base-rate Problems

## Summary

Base-rate problems, exemplified by Kahneman and Tversky's 'lawyer-engineer' task, assess decision making under uncertainty. The normative response is determined by group proportions (base rates), but stereotypical descriptions trigger heuristic responses that often conflict with the logical answer. Four types exist: incongruent (conflict), congruent (consistency), neutral-heuristic, and neutral-analytic.

## Key facts

- In the classic base-rate task with 5 engineers and 995 lawyers, the normative answer is 'lawyer' based on base rates, but many people respond 'engineer' based on stereotypical beliefs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p174]
- On incongruent base-rate problems where heuristic and analytic responses conflict, only 45% accuracy was achieved, compared to 93% on congruent problems. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p204]
- The representativeness heuristic underlies the heuristic response in base-rate problems, causing neglect of statistical base-rate information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p196]
- Solving incongruent problems took longest (3553ms) compared to congruent problems (3008ms), reflecting the cognitive cost of conflict. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p204]
