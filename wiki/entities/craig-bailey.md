# Craig Bailey

## Summary

Craig Bailey is a cell biologist recruited by Kandel in 1973 to explore the structural changes accompanying the transition from short- to long-term memory. His work with Mary Chen revealed that long-term memory involves anatomical changes including growth of new synaptic connections.

## Key facts

- Bailey and Chen found that in long-term habituation, presynaptic connections among sensory and motor neurons decrease, while in long-term sensitization, sensory neurons grow new connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p225]
- A single sensory neuron has approximately 1300 presynaptic terminals contacting about 25 different target cells. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p225]
- In long-term sensitization, the number of synaptic terminals more than doubles from 1300 to 2700, and the proportion of active synapses increases from 40% to 60%. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p225]
- Bailey made the remarkable discovery that long-term memory in Aplysia endures because sensory neurons grow new axon terminals that strengthen their synaptic connections with motor neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p269]
