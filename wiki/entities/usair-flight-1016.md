# USAir Flight 1016

## Summary

USAir Flight 1016 (DC-9) crashed on July 2, 1994, near Charlotte, North Carolina, during a severe thunderstorm, killing 37 passengers and injuring 20 others. The accident occurred during a go-around after encountering a severe microburst. The investigation demonstrated how susceptible pilot situation awareness can be to information from multiple participants in the airspace, including incomplete information from ATC controllers.

## Key facts

- The accident occurred on July 2, 1994, when USAir DC-9 Flight 1016 crashed near Charlotte, North Carolina. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p120]
- The aircraft encountered a severe microburst and downburst with a change from 35-knot headwind to 26-knot tailwind with vertical velocity of 30 feet per second. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p120]
- The accident killed 37 passengers and injured the remaining 20 passengers and crew; both pilots survived. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p120]
- Charlotte ATC controllers observed lightning and discussed it but did not notify the flight crew. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p121]
- The crew received incomplete and misleading information regarding the location of windshear detected by the LLWAS system. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p121]
- The airborne windshear detection system did not signal a warning because the flaps were in transition during the go-around. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p122]
- The NTSB determined that the failure of Charlotte controllers to provide complete information contributed to the accident. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p123]
- Just before impact, the captain commanded the first officer to lower the nose, contrary to airline guidance for windshear escape. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p122]
