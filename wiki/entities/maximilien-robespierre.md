# Maximilien Robespierre

## Summary

Maximilien Robespierre (1758-1794) was a French revolutionary leader who Greene presents as Danton's main rival for power. Greene focuses on Robespierre's mastery of the 'Insinuating-Doubt' passive-aggression strategy, using the example of his attack on Georges Danton. Robespierre noticed Danton's loss of revolutionary fervor and spread rumors against him, eventually having him arrested and executed. After Danton's death, Robespierre unleashed the Great Terror, executing approximately 20,000 people in four months before being overthrown and guillotined himself in July 1794.

## Key facts

- Robespierre was Danton's main rival for power in the French Revolution. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]
- Robespierre mastered the Insinuating-Doubt Strategy, exemplified by his attack on Danton where he defended him while repeating all the charges against him. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p618]
- Robespierre spread rumors that Danton had lost his revolutionary fervor and could no longer be trusted. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]
- Robespierre packed the Committee of Public Safety with his sympathizers after Danton failed to gain enough votes. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]
- After Danton's execution, Robespierre unleashed the Great Terror, executing approximately 20,000 people in four months. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p646]
- Robespierre was overthrown in late July 1794, guillotined without trial the following morning, and the revolutionary tribunal was abolished. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p646]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene implies that Robespierre's hypersensitivity to criticism and dissent was characteristic of chronic aggressors described earlier in the chapter. His downfall came precisely because the public had 'had enough of the executions,' mirroring how chronic aggressors eventually overreach.
