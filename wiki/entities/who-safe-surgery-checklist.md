# WHO Safe Surgery Checklist

## Summary

The WHO Safe Surgery Checklist is a 19-item surgical safety instrument developed by the World Health Organization, designed to reduce surgical complications and mortality worldwide through systematic verification at three critical pause points: before anesthesia, before incision, and before leaving the operating room.

## Key facts

- The WHO Safe Surgery Checklist contains 19 checks distributed across three pause points. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p102]
- In an eight-hospital pilot study across diverse settings (high-income and low-income countries), the checklist reduced major complications by 36% and deaths by 47%. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p111]
- The checklist was developed by a WHO working group that combined elements from three prior checklists: the University of Toronto's 21-item checklist, Johns Hopkins' 18-item checklist, and Kaiser's 30-item preflight checklist. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p102-103]
- The three pause points are: before anesthesia induction, after anesthesia but before incision, and before the patient leaves the operating room. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p102]
- In the pilot study, 435 patients would have been expected to develop serious complications based on baseline data, but only 277 did—a reduction of over 150 patients from harm. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p111]

## Inferences

- Inference: The WHO Safe Surgery Checklist represents a convergence of three independent surgical checklist initiatives, suggesting the solution was convergent rather than unique to any one institution.
- Inference: The substantial reductions in both complications and deaths across radically different hospital settings (from rural Tanzania to Seattle) indicate the checklist addresses fundamental systemic failures common to all surgical environments.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The study could not definitively establish the precise mechanism by which the checklist produced improvements—communication enhancement is hypothesized but not proven.
- Uncertain: Whether the checklist's effects persist long-term without ongoing reinforcement and institutional support remains uncertain.
