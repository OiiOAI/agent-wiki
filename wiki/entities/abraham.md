# Abraham

## Summary

In Hebrew legend, Abraham was born during the reign of Nimrod, who sought to slay all newborn boys. His mother abandoned him in a cave where God sent Gabriel to give him milk from his finger. The infant Abraham independently arrived at knowledge of the One God by observing that the sun, moon, and stars that he first took for gods all proved temporary.

## Key facts

- King Nimrod read in the stars that a man would be born who would rise up against him and triumphantly give the lie to his religion, so he commanded that all male newborns be slain by midwives. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p253]
- When Abraham's mother left him in the cave, God sent Gabriel down to give him milk, making it flow from the little finger of the baby's right hand until he was ten days old. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p255]
- Abraham observed the stars and said 'These are the gods,' but when dawn came and they disappeared, he denied them. He then called the sun his god, but when it set he denied it too. Seeing the moon also disappear, he cried 'There is One who sets them all in motion.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p256]
- When Death came to Abraham, he appeared in great glory and beauty, explaining that he takes such form only for the righteous; Abraham refused to go willingly, and God removed his soul as in a dream while the archangel Michael took it up into heaven. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p279]

## Inferences

- Inference: Abraham's legend illustrates the infant exile archetype combined with the theological revelation theme—the hero discovers transcendent truth through his own experience rather than through revelation from established religious authority.
