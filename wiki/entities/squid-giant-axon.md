# Squid Giant Axon

## Summary

The squid giant axon is a large-diameter axon (up to 1 mm) in the squid mantle used historically as a model system for studying neuronal electrical properties. Its size allowed insertion of electrodes and voltage-clamp apparatus. Hodgkin and Huxley used it to characterize the ionic basis of the action potential, determining that Na+ influx causes depolarization and K+ efflux causes repolarization.

## Key facts

- The squid giant axon can reach a diameter of 1 mm, allowing experimental manipulation impossible with smaller mammalian axons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p197]
- Hodgkin and Huxley used voltage-clamp experiments on the squid giant axon to derive equations describing Na+ and K+ conductance changes during the action potential. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p205]
- The squid axon has K+ concentration of 400 mM inside and 20 mM outside; Na+ is 50 mM inside and 440 mM outside. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p181]
