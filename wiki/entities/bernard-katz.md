# Bernard Katz

## Summary

Bernard Katz (1911–2002) was a German-born British physiologist who fled Hitler's Germany in 1935 and joined A.V. Hill's laboratory at University College London. He made crucial discoveries about synaptic transmission, including establishing that acetylcholine fully accounts for all phases of the synaptic potential in muscle. He showed that neurotransmitter-gated ion channels differ from voltage-gated channels and discovered that transmitters are released in discrete packets called quanta, packaged in synaptic vesicles. Later, he demonstrated that calcium influx into the presynaptic terminal triggers vesicle fusion and neurotransmitter release. Katz shared the 1970 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Ulf von Euler and Julius Axelrod.

## Key facts

- Katz showed that acetylcholine released by motor neurons fully accounts for all phases of the synaptic potential in skeletal muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p110]
- Katz discovered that transmitter-gated ion channels respond only to specific chemical transmitters and allow both sodium and potassium ions to flow through. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p110]
- Katz proposed that neurotransmitters are released in discrete packets called quanta, each containing about five thousand molecules packaged in membrane-bound sacs called synaptic vesicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
- Katz discovered that calcium influx into presynaptic terminals triggers the molecular steps leading to neurotransmitter release. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p113]
