# Head of the Caudate Nucleus

## Summary

The head of the caudate nucleus is a subcortical brain region involved in processing reward and punishment information during decision making. Its activity is highly context-dependent, responding primarily when reward-related information is relevant to the individual's goals.

## Key facts

- The head of the caudate nucleus processes information about positive and negative outcomes during decision making. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p227]
- Reward-related information is processed in the caudate primarily when the information is relevant to one's goal. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p227]
- Caudate neuron firing is context-dependent, changing when environmental conditions change. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p227]
- The caudate shows counterfactual comparison effects, with activity dependent on both experienced outcomes and alternative possible outcomes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p230]
- Activity in the head of the caudate is sensitive to action-outcome contingency, being significantly activated when outcomes are perceived as contingent on the individual's action. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p231]
- The hemodynamic response in the head of the caudate orders parametrically according to the magnitude of reward or punishment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p233]
- Caudate activity decreases as learning progresses, reflecting that behavioral consequences provide more information early in learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p234]

## Inferences

- Inference: The caudate's sensitivity to action-outcome contingency suggests it may play an important role in learning from the consequences of one's actions.
- Inference: The caudate's tendency to respond in a counterfactual manner allows it to focus processing on relevant consequences of decisions.
