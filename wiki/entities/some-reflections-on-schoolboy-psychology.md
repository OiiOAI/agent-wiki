# Some Reflections on Schoolboy Psychology

## Summary

An essay written by Sigmund Freud for the 50th anniversary celebration of the k. k. Erzherzog-Rainer-Realgymnasium (formerly Sperlgymnasium) in Vienna, which he attended from 1865 to 1873. Freud reflects on his school days, the psychology of the student-teacher relationship, and introduces the concept of ambivalence, arguing that students transfer their childhood feelings toward their fathers onto their teachers.

- Freud introduces the concept of 'ambivalence' to describe how students feel equally inclined to love and hate their teachers, to criticize and respect them. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4351]
- Freud argues that a boy's emotional attitudes to other people are already established in the first six years of life, and later acquaintances become substitute figures for these first objects. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4351]
- Freud states that the most important imago from childhood is that of the father, and organic necessity introduces emotional ambivalence into a man's relation to his father. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4352]
- Freud concludes that schoolmasters became substitute fathers, explaining why even young teachers seemed so mature and adult to students. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4352]
