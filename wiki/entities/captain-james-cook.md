# Captain James Cook

## Summary

Captain Cook's use of psychology to get his crew to eat sauerkraut to prevent scurvy exemplifies practical application of elementary psychology—he used indirect methods and social proof to overcome resistance to unfamiliar food.

## Key facts

- Captain Cook noticed Dutch ships had less scurvy than English ships and identified sauerkraut as the cause (it contains trace vitamin C). [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p234]
- Officers were served sauerkraut first where men could observe them; eventually Captain Cook allowed men to have it one day a week, gradually extending consumption to the whole crew. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p235]
- Cook used psychology (officer example = social proof) rather than direct orders to overcome resistance; this constructive use of elementary psychology may have saved countless lives. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p235]
