# Walter A. Shewhart

## Summary

Walter A. Shewhart was a physicist and statistician who developed the Shewhart Cycle in 1939, showing how specification, production, and inspection correspond to the scientific method's hypothesis, experiment, and testing. His work 'Statistical Method from the Viewpoint of Quality Control' was edited by Deming and profoundly influenced quality management.

## Key facts

- Shewhart displayed the Shewhart Cycle in 1939 in his book 'Statistical Method from the Viewpoint of Quality Control'. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Shewhart showed that specification, production, and inspection correspond to making a hypothesis, carrying out an experiment, and testing the hypothesis. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Shewhart stated that the three steps constitute a dynamic scientific process of acquiring knowledge. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
- Shewhart's work was heavily influenced by C.I. Lewis's pragmatist philosophy as presented in 'Mind and the World Order'. [raw/books/strategy/PDSA_History._16th_Deming_Research_Seminar_Feb._2010.Moen.docx.pdf#p3]
