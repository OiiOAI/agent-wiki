# Michael Tomasello

## Summary

One of the world's foremost experts on chimpanzee and human cognition, based at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology. His comparative research demonstrated that humans possess 'shared intentionality'—the ability to coordinate actions toward shared goals—that chimpanzees lack. This ability may represent the key Rubicon crossing that enabled cumulative culture and human ultrasociality.

## Key facts

- Tomasello stated: 'It is inconceivable that you would ever see two chimpanzees carrying a log together.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p225]
- His research showed chimps and two-year-olds perform equally on physical tasks (~68%) but humans far outperform chimps on social collaboration tasks (74% vs 35%). [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p226]
- Tomasello proposes that human ultrasociality arose in two steps: first sharing intentions in small groups, then scaling up to larger groups via cultural evolution. [raw/books/philosophy/The Righteous Mind - Jonathan Haidt.pdf#p228]
