# William Griffin

## Summary

William Griffin was a 37-year-old man who committed a series of murders on June 17, 1981, in Rochester, New York, including killing his mother and a handyman, wounding his stepfather, and later shooting five people near a bank before taking nine hostages. He was the first hostage-taker in U.S. history to kill a hostage on deadline, demonstrating the 'Black Swan' principle that past experience cannot predict future extremes. FBI negotiators assumed Griffin wanted money or escape like all previous hostage-takers, but his true interest was being killed by police—a desire never previously encountered.

## Key facts

- On June 17, 1981, William Griffin killed his mother and a handyman, wounded his stepfather, and shot five bystanders before taking nine bank employees hostage. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p187]
- Griffin was the first hostage-taker in U.S. history to kill a hostage on deadline, shattering the law enforcement assumption that deadlines were psychological tools and that hostage-takers always wanted money, respect, and escape. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p188]
- FBI negotiators failed to recognize Black Swans: Griffin had already killed without monetary demands, his rambling note contained the line 'after the police take my life,' and he wanted to die by cop. [raw/books/strategy/Never Split the Difference_ Negotiating As - Voss, Chris.pdf#p190]
