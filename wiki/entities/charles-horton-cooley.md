# Charles Horton Cooley

## Summary

Charles Horton Cooley (1864-1929) was a sociologist associated with the Chicago school who received his Ph.D. from the University of Michigan in 1894. Although he spent his entire career at Michigan rather than Chicago, his theoretical perspective aligned with symbolic interactionism. Cooley is remembered for his social-psychological insights, particularly the concept of the "looking-glass self"—the understanding that people possess consciousness shaped in continuing social interaction—and the concept of "primary groups," which are intimate, face-to-face groups that play a key role in linking the individual to larger society. Like Mead, Cooley rejected behavioristic views of human beings and urged sociologists to use "sympathetic introspection."
