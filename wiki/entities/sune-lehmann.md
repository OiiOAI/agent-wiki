# Sune Lehmann

## Summary

Sune Lehmann is a professor at the Technical University of Denmark who conducted the largest scientific study to date on whether collective attention spans are shrinking. His research analyzed Twitter, Google Books, Google searches, movie tickets, and Reddit to prove that our ability to focus collectively is declining.

## Key facts

- Lehmann and colleagues found that between 2013 and 2016, the average time a topic remained in the top 50 most-discussed subjects on Twitter dropped from 17.5 hours to 11.9 hours. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p35]
- Analyzing 130 years of Google Books data, his team found that topics have been appearing and disappearing faster with each passing decade—not just since the internet. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p36-37]
- His mathematical models showed that flooding a system with more information causes faster rise and fall of topics—the key mechanism driving declining attention. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p37]
- He warns of a future where an 'upper class of people' protects their attention while others 'live more and more inside their computers, being manipulated more and more.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p39]
- He personally stopped using all social media except Twitter (checked once weekly), cancelled TV, reads newspapers instead of social feeds, and reads many more books. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p39]
- Wikipedia was the one exception to declining attention—topic focus has held steady there. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p36]

## Inferences

- Inference: Lehmann's finding that this trend predates the internet suggests the attention crisis has deeper systemic causes beyond technology alone.
