# Amy and Rachel

## Summary

Amy used Tiny Habits and Behavior Design to help her daughter Rachel, who was diagnosed with ADHD in kindergarten and described by a neuropsychologist as 'the smartest but most scattered kid he'd ever seen.' Amy found Rachel's real aspiration (keeping pace with classmates rather than getting good grades), then used the Behavior Change Masterplan to create tiny homework habits. By sixth grade, Rachel was no longer in Special Ed and eventually took advanced placement courses and graduated with honors.

## Key facts

- Rachel was described by a neuropsychologist as the smartest but most scattered kid he had ever seen. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p283]
- Amy discovered that Rachel's real aspiration was to keep pace with her classmates, not to master homework—something Amy realized was all Amy, not Rachel. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p284]
- The key breakthrough was asking Rachel: 'What if everyone else goes to fifth grade and you don't?'. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p284]
- Amy broke homework into ten-minute increments, organizing supplies, making to-do lists, and interspersing tasks with fun activity bursts. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p285]
- One habit that persisted from fourth grade: After getting home from school, Rachel would check her homework list and guess how long it would take. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p286]
- By sixth grade, Rachel was no longer in Special Ed classes and eventually took advanced placement courses and graduated with honors. [raw/books/productivity/Tiny Habits_ The Small Changes that Change Everything - BJ Fogg.pdf#p287]
