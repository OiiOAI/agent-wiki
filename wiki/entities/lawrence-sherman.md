# Lawrence Sherman

## Summary

Lawrence Sherman is a criminologist who designed the Kansas City Gun Experiment, which showed that focused patrol in crime hot spots could dramatically reduce gun violence. He later emphasized that such aggressive policing must be confined to high-crime areas to avoid civil liberties violations.

## Key facts

- Sherman designed the Kansas City Gun Experiment (1992-1993), which placed four officers in District 144—a tiny 0.64 square mile high-crime area—for focused patrol. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p173-174]
- The experiment reduced gun crimes by half in the target area while surrounding areas saw no change, validating hot-spots policing. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p174]
- Sherman emphasized that his experiment required special training and was confined to a known high-crime area—conditions often lost when the model was replicated elsewhere. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p190-191]
- Sherman argues that stop-and-search is a 'dangerous procedure' that can generate hostility to police, requiring the same caution as invasive medical procedures. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p190]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sherman's later work emphasizes that the Kansas City model was misapplied when exported to lower-crime areas and to broader populations, leading to the kind of encounters that occurred with Sandra Bland.
