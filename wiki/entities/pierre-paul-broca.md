# Pierre Paul Broca

## Summary

French neurologist who first identified specific areas of the brain concerned with language production. In 1861 he described the patient Leborgne, who could not speak but could understand language, and found a lesion in the posterior frontal lobe—now called Broca's area. His work founded neuropsychology as a science distinct from Gall's phrenology.

## Key facts

- Broca identified specific areas of the brain concerned with language in 1861. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- His patient Leborgne could not speak grammatically or create complete sentences despite having no motor deficits of tongue, mouth, or vocal cords. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- Postmortem examination showed a lesion in the posterior region of the frontal lobe, now called Broca's area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- Broca studied eight similar patients, all with lesions in the left cerebral hemisphere. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- He announced in 1864: 'Nous parlons avec l'hémisphère gauche!' (We speak with the left hemisphere!). [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p62]
- Broca founded neuropsychology by correlating clinical evidence of aphasia with brain lesions discovered post mortem rather than correlating behavior with skull bumps.
