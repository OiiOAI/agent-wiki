# Thales of Miletus

## Summary

Thales (fl. c. 585 BC), founder of the Milesian school, is conventionally regarded as the 'first philosopher' (Bertrand Russell). A dedicated observer of the natural world, he made discoveries in astronomy and used mathematics to address engineering problems. He correctly predicted an eclipse of the sun in 585 BC. He posited water as the primary principle (arch) of all things, likely derived from observing water's transitions between solid, liquid, and gaseous states and its omnipresence in living things. McGilchrist notes that Thales, like Heraclitus, believed 'all things are full of gods.'
