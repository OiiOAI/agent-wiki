# Solomon Shereshevsky

## Summary

Solomon Shereshevsky was a Russian newspaper reporter who had one of the most expansive memories ever documented. He was oblivious to his peculiar memory until his editor noticed he never took notes yet repeated verbatim instructions from meetings. He had synesthesia—every stimulus triggered every other sense. He could taste words, see music, and smell colors. He could raise his heart rate by imagining running after a train. His remarkable capabilities were studied for 30 years by Alexander Luria, who described them in 'The Mind of a Mnemonist.' However, his vivid imagination made it difficult to distinguish imagination from reality, and he eventually worked as a taxi driver in Moscow.
