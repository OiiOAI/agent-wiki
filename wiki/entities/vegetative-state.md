# Vegetative State

## Summary

A disorder of consciousness characterized by wakefulness without awareness, where patients open their eyes and have sleep-wake cycles but show no signs of conscious perception. Also called unresponsive wakefulness syndrome. The book discusses how neuroimaging has revealed that some patients diagnosed as vegetative may retain hidden awareness.

## Key facts

- Vegetative state is distinguished from minimally conscious state by absence of behavioral signs of awareness. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p203-206]
- Glucose metabolism is reduced in vegetative state, particularly in frontal and parietal regions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p224-225]
- EEG local-global testing can reveal consciousness in some patients diagnosed as vegetative. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p218-221]
- Recovery from vegetative state can occur, with some patients improving after months or years. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p228-233]
- Owen's imagination study demonstrated that some 'vegetative' patients could modulate their brain activity in response to instructions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p209-216]

## Inferences

- Inference: The term 'vegetative state' is being replaced clinically by 'unresponsive wakefulness syndrome' (UWS) due to its pejorative connotations
- Inference: Neuroimaging has 'shattered clinical consensus' by revealing that some patients presumed vegetative retain covert consciousness
