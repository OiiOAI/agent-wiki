# Peter Pronovost

## Summary

Peter Pronovost is a critical care specialist at Johns Hopkins Hospital who developed a simple checklist for central line infections that achieved dramatic results. His work demonstrated that even experienced medical professionals frequently skip basic steps and that checklists can dramatically reduce harm and costs.

## Key facts

- Pronovost created a central line infection checklist covering five steps: wash hands, clean skin with chlorhexidine, use sterile drapes, wear mask/hat/gown/gloves, and apply sterile dressing. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p35]
- Initial observation found doctors skipped at least one step in more than a third of patients when inserting central lines. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p35]
- With nurse authorization to stop doctors skipping steps, the ten-day line-infection rate went from 11 percent to zero in one year. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p36]
- Pronovost calculated that in one hospital, the checklist prevented 43 infections, 8 deaths, and saved $2 million in costs. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p36]
- Despite initial results, hospitals were slow to adopt his checklists, with physicians sometimes offended by the suggestion they needed them. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p36]
- Pronovost holds an MD and PhD in public health from Johns Hopkins and is trained in emergency medicine, anesthesiology, and critical care medicine. [raw/books/strategy/The checklist manifesto _ how to get things right - Atul Gawande.pdf#p36]

## Inferences

- Inference: Pronovost's broader checklists for pain management and ventilator care showed similar dramatic improvements, suggesting the approach was generalizable beyond central lines.
