# Thomas Huxley

## Summary

Charles Darwin's most important scientific ally and a leading biologist in his own right, who in 1883 made the famously incorrect prediction that major sea fisheries were 'inexhaustible' in relation to contemporary fishing methods.

## Key facts

- Thomas Huxley was invited to comment on whether fishers might exhaust their stocks, and in 1883 he stated that there was little reason to worry about the most important sea fisheries. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p158]
- Huxley concluded in his 1883 speech: 'I believe that it may be affirmed with confidence that, in relation to our present modes of fishing, a number of the most important sea fisheries, such as the cod fishery, the herring fishery, and the mackerel fishery, are inexhaustible.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p158]
- Huxley was spectacularly wrong in his optimism; within a few decades many of these fisheries, especially the cod, were in serious trouble. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p159]
- What sent people down the wrong path was their inability to recognize how much fishing technology would change, leading to 'our present modes of fishing' disappearing not long after Huxley's words. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p159]
