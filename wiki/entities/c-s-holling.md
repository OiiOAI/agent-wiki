# C.S. Holling

## Summary

C.S. Holling was an ecologist at the University of British Columbia who, with Gordon Baskerville, created a computer model of the spruce budworm problem. His work demonstrated how nonlinear feedback relationships in ecological systems produce complex oscillations and why spraying insecticides can paradoxically create persistent outbreak conditions.

## Key facts

- C.S. Holling of the University of British Columbia and Gordon Baskerville created a computer model showing that budworm outbreaks are controlled by nonlinear feedback relationships with predators and food supply. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p119]
- Holling coined the term 'persistent semi-outbreak conditions' to describe how forest management practices lock the system into a state where outbreaks are always on the verge of occurring. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking in Systems - Donella H. Meadows.pdf#p121]

## Inferences

- Inference: Holling's research demonstrates how well-intentioned interventions (pesticide spraying) can weaken the natural feedback loops that keep systems stable, creating worse long-term problems.
