# Spontaneous Remission

## Summary

Spontaneous remissions are cases where disease disappears without medical intervention. Langer's mother experienced a spontaneous remission of cancer, inspiring Langer's lifelong research into mind-body effects. Research suggests behavioral patterns, positive beliefs, and spiritual factors may play roles in these unexplained recoveries.

## Key facts

- Langer's mother had a radical mastectomy, then cancer spread to her pancreas with only months to live, yet the cancer vanished with no trace on subsequent scans. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p128]
- Researchers Challis and Stam reviewed spontaneous remission cases and concluded behavioral patterns and beliefs play a part in survivor outcomes. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p130]
- Kelly Ann Turner found cancer survivors shared behavioral patterns including being more positive, trusting, spiritual, and taking vitamins. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p130]
- Many cancer survivors believe in a higher power that could heal the body, suggesting thoughts affect bodies. [raw/books/psychology/The Mindful Body_ Thinking Our Way to Chro - Ellen J. Langer.pdf#p130]

## Inferences

- Inference: Spontaneous remissions may be more common than reported since many people with undiagnosed illnesses who recover spontaneously never seek medical attention.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The actual frequency of spontaneous remissions is unknown because many cases go unreported.
