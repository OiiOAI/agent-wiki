# Amaterasu Ōmikami

## Summary

The beautiful sun-goddess of Japanese Shinto tradition, whose drawing forth from a heavenly rock-dwelling is one of the most important myths already old when chronicled in the eighth century A.D. Amaterasu retired into a cave after her brother Susanowo's destructions, causing darkness to cover the world. The eight million gods assembled and, through Uzume's dance, a mirror, and a sacred rope (shimenawa), drew her forth. This represents the rescue of a reluctant hero from without.

## Key facts

- Amaterasu retired into a heavenly cave, closing the door, which caused permanent darkness—meaning the end of the universe before it had properly begun. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p163]
- The eight million gods assembled and used a mirror tied to a great tree, Uzume's dance, and merriment to draw Amaterasu from the cave. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p163]
- The shimenawa (rope of straw stretched behind her) symbolizes the graciousness of the miracle of the light's return and is the simplest sign of resurrection. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p165]
- Amaterasu is ancestress of the Japanese royal house and honored as one of the supreme divinities of Shinto. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p164]
