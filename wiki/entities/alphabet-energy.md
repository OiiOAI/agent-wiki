# Alphabet Energy

## Summary

Alphabet Energy is a Berkeley-based clean tech startup that developed thermoelectric materials to generate electricity from waste heat. The company differentiated by using silicon wafers (like computer CPUs) instead of rare elements for manufacturing, enabling small-batch production using existing semiconductor infrastructure. This allowed rapid iteration from concept to physical prototype in six weeks and a pivot after just three months of investigation when initial hypotheses were disproved.

## Key facts

- Alphabet Energy's thermoelectric material was developed over ten years by scientists at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratories using silicon wafers, enabling use of low-cost semiconductor infrastructure for manufacturing. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p188]
- CEO Matthew Sullin stated 'Our thermoelectric is the only one that can use low-cost semiconductor infrastructure for manufacturing.'. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p188]
- Alphabet can go from a product concept to holding a physical version in its hand in just six weeks from end to end. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p188]
- Alphabet hypothesized simple cycle gas turbines would be ideal applications but disproved this quickly when power companies showed low tolerance for risk as early adopters. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p189]
- Alphabet was ready to pivot after just three months of investigation because it was not weighed down by a large-batch approach. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p189]
- To date, Alphabet has raised approximately $1 million while other energy startups like BrightSource raised $291 million without delivering a watt to a single customer. [raw/books/business/The Lean Startup_ How Today's Entrepreneur - Eric Ries.pdf#p190]
