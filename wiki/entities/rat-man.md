# The Rat Man

## Summary

The Rat Man (German: Rattenmann) is the name given to one of Freud's most important obsessional neurosis cases, documented in 1909. The patient, referred to as Dr. Lorenz, was a young man aged 29 who suffered from severe obsessional symptoms including commands, prohibitions, superstitious beliefs, and death wishes. His central obsession involved the 'rat idea'—a fear that his beloved cousin and his father would suffer punishment involving rats. The case provides detailed insight into the structure of obsessional thinking, the mechanisms of distortion and displacement, the role of ambivalent love-hatred conflicts, and the techniques of psychoanalytic treatment. The Rat Man was killed in World War I in 1914.
