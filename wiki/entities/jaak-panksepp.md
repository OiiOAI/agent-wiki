# Jaak Panksepp

## Summary

Jaak Panksepp (1943-2017) was an Estonian-American neuroscientist and psychologist who pioneered the field of affective neuroscience. He was a professor at Bowling Green State University and developed the first comprehensive neurobiological theory of emotions, identifying seven core emotional operating systems (SEEKING, FEAR, RAGE, PANIC, LUST, CARE, PLAY) that are shared across all mammals.

## Key facts

- Panksepp served as a professor at Bowling Green State University where he conducted most of his research. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p6]
- He authored over 200 scientific publications and wrote this book as a comprehensive text on affective neuroscience. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p3]
- Panksepp advocated for over 25 years that animal brain research combined with psychological analysis could reveal the neural foundations of emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p21]
- He corresponded with B.F. Skinner and argued that psychology needed to integrate brain science, behavioral science, and experiential science. [raw/books/neuroscience/Affective Neuroscience_ The Foundations of Human and Animal - Panksepp, Jaak.pdf#p12]
- Panksepp proposed that the same neural circuits control both emotional behaviors in animals and emotional feelings in humans because these circuits are conserved across mammals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p186]
- Using electrical stimulation in rats, Panksepp mapped areas from which behaviors related to fear, rage, and other emotions could be elicited. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p188]
- Panksepp distinguished primary process affective states (primitive conscious feelings) from cognitively elaborated conscious feelings created through memory, attention, and language. [raw/books/neuroscience/Anxious - Joseph LeDoux.pdf#p186]
- Panksepp writes that consciousness 'is deeply enmeshed with the brain mechanisms that automatically promote action readiness', rejecting the view that consciousness arises from sensory-perceptual imagery. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p313]
- Panksepp sees consciousness as something that begins very deep in the midbrain and 'migrates' through higher regions, transforming itself through branches to the 'cerebral canopy'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p312]
- Jaak Panksepp (1943–2017) was a neuroscientist who 'more than any other, understood the origin and power of feelings', and the book is dedicated to his memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p6]
- Panksepp led the way in arguing that feelings are generated by subcortical brain systems, and he was an animal researcher — his research demonstrated that mammals feel pain, fear, panic, sorrow and similar emotions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p14]
- Panksepp's Affective Neuroscience (1998) laid out in detail evidence that subcortical brain systems responsible for regulating the 'level' of consciousness also generate a 'content' of their own. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p35]
- Panksepp identified the mesocortical-mesolimbic dopamine circuit as the brain's 'SEEKING' system — the circuit responsible for 'the most energised exploratory and search behaviours an animal is capable of exhibiting'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p34]
- Panksepp was one of three neuroscientists (alongside Antonio Damasio and Bjorn Merker) who shared Solms's view that the neuroscience of their time lacked focus on the embodied nature of lived experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p14]
- Panksepp and Solms co-founded the International Neuropsychoanalysis Society as co-chairs in 2000, at the inaugural congress held at the Royal College of Surgeons of England. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p48]
- Decorticated rats were observed to be more active and emotional than normal rats — Panksepp used this in teaching to challenge assumptions about the cortex's role in emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p57]
- Panksepp developed a taxonomy distinguishing between homeostatic, sensory, and emotional affects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
- Panksepp's research was based on deep brain stimulation studies performed on thousands of animals including pigeons, chickens, beagles, guinea pigs, rats and prairie voles. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
- His experiments aimed to determine which brain structures and circuits reliably arouse the same affective responses across individuals and across species. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
- When accused of anthropomorphism towards animals, Panksepp said he would rather plead guilty to zoomorphism towards humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
- Panksepp spent the last decades of his life studying only positive emotions due to increasing concern about ethical issues flowing from his findings about animal sentience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
- Panksepp called the primal SELF—the very source of our sentient being—the PAG, superior colliculi, and midbrain locomotor region. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p130]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether electrical stimulation actually elicits conscious emotional states in animals, as Panksepp claimed, is disputed by LeDoux and others.
- Uncertain: The specific experimental methods Panksepp used with animals are not detailed in this chunk; only general findings are reported.

## Inferences

- Inference: Panksepp's work with animals demonstrated that sentience is not unique to humans and that subcortical structures generate genuine emotional experience, forming a key empirical foundation for Solms's arguments about the brainstem as the source of consciousness.
- Inference: The illness that finally killed Panksepp might well have been caused by excessive exposure to the animals in his laboratory, especially the birds [raw/books/neuroscience/The Hidden Spring - Mark Solms.pdf#p99]
