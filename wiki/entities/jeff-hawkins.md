# Jeff Hawkins

## Summary

Jeff Hawkins is the author of 'A Thousand Brains' and the founder of Numenta, a research company dedicated to understanding the neocortex. After studying electrical engineering at MIT and working at Intel, he switched careers to neuroscience, eventually creating the Redwood Neuroscience Institute before founding Palm Computing and later Numenta. His work focuses on developing a unified theory of how the neocortex creates intelligence.

## Key facts

- Hawkins studied electrical engineering at MIT and worked at Intel before switching careers to study the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p19]
- In 1979, reading Francis Crick's essay 'Thinking About the Brain' inspired Hawkins to dedicate his life to understanding the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p19]
- Hawkins created the GridPad in 1988-1992 and founded Palm Computing in 1992, designing early handheld computers and smartphones. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p21]
- Hawkins created the Redwood Neuroscience Institute in 2002, focusing exclusively on neocortical theory with ten full-time scientists. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p21]
- Numenta was founded to develop a theory of how the neocortex works and apply brain principles to machine intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p22]
- In 2010, Hawkins discovered how neurons make predictions via dendrite spikes, and in 2016 he realized reference frames were the missing framework. [raw/books/neuroscience/A Thousand Brains _ A New Theory of Intelligence - Jeff Hawkins; Richard Dawkins; Basic Books.pdf#p56]
