# David Hubel

## Summary

David Hubel and Torsten Wiesel made the most fundamental advance in understanding cortical organization since Cajal. They discovered that visual cortex cells respond to linear contours and edges rather than spots of light, with each cell responding to a specific orientation, and that cells with similar properties are grouped into columns.

## Key facts

- Hubel and Wiesel made the astonishing discovery that once visual signals reach the cortex, cells do not respond vigorously to small spots of light but instead respond to linear contours and elongated edges. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p311]
- Most amazingly, each cell in the primary visual cortex responds only to a specific orientation of light-dark contours; cells are organized into columns based on similar orientation axes. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p311]
- Kandel states that Hubel and Wiesel's work 'stands as the most fundamental advance in our understanding of the organization of the cerebral cortex since the work of Cajal at the turn of the last century.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p311]
