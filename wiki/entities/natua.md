# Natua

## Summary

Natua was a dolphin at the Dolphin Research Center in Marathon, Florida, who participated in experiments designed by J. David Smith to test whether dolphins possess metacognition. The experiments demonstrated that Natua could monitor his own uncertainty during a pitch discrimination task.

## Key facts

- Natua was a dolphin trained to classify underwater sounds according to pitch, pressing a left paddle for low pitches and a right paddle for high pitches. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p307]
- When the sound frequency was very close to the 2,100 hertz boundary, Natua's responses became very slow and he shook his head before hesitantly swimming to one side, often the wrong one. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p307]
- Natua learned to use a third 'escape' response paddle that replaced the difficult stimulus with an easy 1,200 hertz sound earning a small reward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p307]
- Natua spontaneously decided to use the opt-out response only on difficult trials, pressing the third paddle only when the frequency was close to the 2,100 hertz reference—precisely those trials where he was likely to make an error. [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p308]

## Inferences

- Inference: The pattern of Natua's escape responses—used only on subjectively difficult trials—suggests the dolphin possesses metacognitive knowledge of his own uncertainty, essentially 'knowing what he doesn't know' [raw/books/neuroscience/Consciousness and the Brain_ Deciphering H - Stanislas Dehaene.pdf#p308].
