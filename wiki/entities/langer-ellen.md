# Ellen Langer

## Summary

Ellen Langer is a Harvard social psychologist whose research on the 'because' word demonstrates automatic compliance in humans. Her experiments with library Xerox machine queues showed that adding the word 'because' followed by any statement—even a restatement of the obvious—dramatically increased compliance rates from 60% to over 90%.

## Key facts

- Langer's experiment showed that 'Excuse me, I have five pages. May I use the Xerox machine because I'm in a rush?' produced 94% compliance. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p11]
- Without the word 'because,' the request 'Excuse me, I have five pages. May I use the Xerox machine?' produced only 60% compliance. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p11]
- Even 'because I have to make some copies?'—a restatement of the obvious—produced 93% compliance, demonstrating it was the word 'because' itself that triggered compliance, not the reason given. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p12]
