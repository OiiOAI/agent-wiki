# Frau Emmy von N.

## Summary

Frau Emmy von N. was a patient of Sigmund Freud treated in 1889-1890 for hysteria. She was approximately 40 years old, from the Baltic provinces, widowed with two daughters. Her symptoms included stammering, a peculiar 'clacking' tic, frequent terrors, animal hallucinations, and gastric pains. Freud's detailed daily notes of her treatment provided early insights into hypnotic technique, false connections, and the relationship between traumatic memories and hysterical symptoms.

## Key facts

- She was about 40 years old, from Baltic Russia, widowed with two daughters aged 16 and 14 who suffered from nervous troubles. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p585]
- Her protective formula 'Keep still! Don't say anything! Don't touch me!' was derived from experiences where animals appearing in hallucinations attacked when movements occurred. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p591]
- Her stammer originated from two events: horses bolting with her children in a carriage, and a thunderstorm where lightning struck a tree in front of her horses. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p593]
- She was treated by Freud for seven weeks in 1889 and returned for eight weeks of treatment in 1890. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p578]
- Her clacking sound originated from sitting by her ill daughter's bedside when she had wanted to keep absolutely quiet. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p589]
- She developed animal hallucinations including seeing mice, snakes, toads, and vultures during confused states. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p596]
- Frau von N.'s psychical symptoms included alterations of mood (anxiety, melancholic depression), phobias, and abulias (inhibitions of will). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p618]
- Her phobias included primary human fears (snakes, toads, thunderstorms, vermin) that were strengthened by specific traumatic experiences, and more specific phobias such as fear of sudden shocks, strangers, asylums, and being buried alive. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p618]
- Her fear of strangers derived from a period when she was being persecuted by her husband's family and saw agents of theirs in every stranger. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p618]
- Her fear of being buried alive was entirely explained by her belief that her husband was not dead when his body was carried out. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p618]
- Freud believed the persistence of her phobias required a neurotic factor—the fact that she had been living for years in a state of sexual abstinence. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p618]
- Her anorexia was an abulia determined by disgust at meals connected with childhood trauma—forced to eat cold meals under threat of punishment, and later prevented from expressing affects at meals with her brothers. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p619]
- Freud hypothesized that hysterical paralyses arise from the inaccessibility to fresh associations of ideas connected with a body part, depending on the involvement of that part in a trauma-loaded recollection with unresolved affect. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p619]
- Frau von N.'s psychical situation was characterized by two points: unresolved distressing affects attaching to traumatic experiences, and a lively memory activity bringing traumas with their affects into present-day consciousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p620]
- Her tongue-clacking and stammering resulted from the mechanism of 'putting antithetic ideas into effect'—an intention to remain still gave rise to a counterwill fear of making a noise, which was then actualized. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p621]
- The exclamation 'Emmy' during confusion reproduced her frequent states of helplessness during her daughter's treatment and functioned as a protective formula against attacks. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p623]
- Freud observed that even in somnambulism she did not have access to her full knowledge—there was an actual and a potential consciousness even in that altered state. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p626]
- She exhibited a contrast: highly suggestible for irrelevant matters but obstinately resistant to suggestions connected with her symptoms, which required personal conviction or psychical analysis. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p627]
- Freud noted that among all the intimate information she provided, there was a complete absence of the sexual element, which he found suspicious given that sexual factors are among the most frequent causes of traumatic experiences. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p630]
- Freud concluded that hysteria is compatible with an unblemished character and a well-governed mode of life, and that it would be wrong to label such a woman a 'degenerate'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p631]
- Freud disputed Janet's view that the hysterical disposition consists in abnormal restriction of the field of consciousness, noting that Frau von N. remained capable of managing a large industrial business and fulfilling her obligations despite her illness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p632]

## Inferences

- Inference: Freud's follow-up on Frau Emmy revealed that she later had a relationship with a young man who swindled her, provoking a near-psychotic reaction, and that she eventually broke off relations with both her children over financial disputes.
- Inference: Freud's earlier note that the patient 'had been living for years in a state of sexual abstinence' was corrected by Andersson (1979), who found she had many erotic affairs with doctors, possibly after treatment.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The footnote suggests Freud may have made a diagnostic error in failing to recognize Emmy von N.'s unconscious hatred of her two children, revealed only in 1935 correspondence.
- Uncertain: The exact extent to which therapeutic success was due to suggestion versus abreaction is indeterminate, as Freud combined both factors.
