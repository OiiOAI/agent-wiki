# The Iron Foundry Case Study

## Summary

A financially strapped iron foundry that lost about $3 million annually on revenues of around $60 million and was $8 million in debt. Lia DiBello's intervention using a simulation game and 'To be cast' report turned the company profitable within a month, raising on-time delivery from 37% to 86% and cutting scrap rate in half.

## Key facts

- The foundry had been losing money since 1998 and tried changing leadership frequently without success. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p231]
- DiBello discovered workers had no way to know due dates and were focused on making as many molds as possible rather than meeting schedules. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p233]
- The company was losing $3 million per year from selling castings at 98 cents per pound when it cost $1.09 per pound to make them. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p237]
- Within a month of implementing simple changes, the company was turning a profit, reaching almost $500,000 in gross profit in the third month alone. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p229]
- The 'To be cast' report answered nine key questions: customer delivery dates, cleaning dates, final preparation dates, pour dates, mold dates, core dates, tooling dates, pattern availability, and material availability. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p237]
- The company achieved 6.8 percent profit rate, exceeding the industry average of 2 percent, and has been profitable ever since. [raw/books/strategy/The Power of Intuition - Gary Klein, Ph.D.pdf#p242]

## Inferences

- Inference: The case demonstrates how intuition can identify systemic problems that conventional management approaches miss, and how simulation games can help organizations discover solutions themselves rather than having them imposed.
