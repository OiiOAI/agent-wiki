# Harry Grundfest

## Summary

Columbia University neurophysiologist (1904–1983) who mentored Kandel during a crucial period and advocated for studying the brain one cell at a time. A victim of McCarthy-era accusations, he lost NIH funding but continued his research with reduced resources.

## Key facts

- Grundfest obtained his Ph.D. in zoology and physiology at Columbia in 1930 and worked with Herbert Gasser, a Nobel laureate, at the Rockefeller Institute. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p69]
- In 1953 he was summoned to testify before the Senate McCarthy hearings and invoked the Fifth Amendment, after which he lost NIH funding for several years. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p70]
- He told Kandel that to understand mind, they needed to look at the brain one cell at a time, which became a guiding principle for Kandel's career. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p67]
- He emphasized that the nerve cell is the key to understanding how the brain works, and that cellular studies could reveal the biological basis of perception, attention, learning, and memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p72]
