# Herbert Blumer

## Summary

Herbert Blumer (1900-1987) was the central figure in the Chicago department from the 1930s into the early 1950s. He was a major exponent of symbolic interactionism developed at Chicago and coined the phrase "symbolic interactionism" in 1937. Blumer played a key role in keeping this tradition alive through his teaching at Chicago, writing essays that were instrumental in keeping symbolic interactionism vital into the 1950s. Despite this activity, the Chicago school was in decline, especially after Blumer moved in 1952 from Chicago to the University of California at Berkeley. However, the Chicago tradition has remained alive with major exponents dispersed throughout the country and the world.
