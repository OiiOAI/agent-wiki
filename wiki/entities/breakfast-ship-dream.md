# Breakfast-Ship Dream

## Summary

In Freud's breakfast-ship dream, his wife happily announced an English warship was coming, but Freud was frightened. The dream object resembled a black Etruscan toilet-set from museums, which led to thoughts of funeral boats and a quotation from Schiller about an old man sailing quietly into port. Analysis revealed that 'English' connected to 'breakfast' (breaking fast), and the cheerful memory of eating breakfast on a mail steamer concealed the gloomiest thoughts of an unknown and uncanny future. The transformation of cheerfulness into fear expressed a portion of the latent dream content.

## Key facts

- The dream-work transformed cheerfulness into fear, and this transformation was itself giving expression to a portion of the latent dream content. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1712]
- The dream object, resembling a black Etruscan toilet-set, meant a black 'toilette' (mourning dress) and made a direct reference to death. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1712]
- The word 'English' connected to 'breakfast' meaning 'breaking fast', where 'breaking' related to the shipwreck and 'fasting' connected with the black dress. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1713]
- It was precisely behind the memory of the most cheerful joie de vivre that the dream concealed the gloomiest thoughts of an unknown and uncanny future. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1713]
- The dream proves that the dream-work is at liberty to detach an affect from its connections in the dream thoughts and introduce it at any other point it chooses in the manifest dream.
