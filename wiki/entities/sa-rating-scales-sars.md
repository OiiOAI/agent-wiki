# SA Rating Scales (SARS)

## Summary

The SA Rating Scales (SARS) are four instruments developed by the Air Force Research Laboratory to measure situation awareness (SA) in operational F-15C squadrons. The first instrument required respondents to provide their personal definition of SA and rate the importance of 31 behavioral elements using a 6-point Likert scale. The other three SARS measured SA from three perspectives: self, supervisory, and peer. The self-report SARS and supervisory SARS required respondents to rate either themselves or their subordinates on each of the 31 items using a 6-point scale relative to other F-15C pilots. The peer SARS required respondents to rate other mission-ready pilots on general fighter pilot ability and SA ability and rank order them on SA ability.

## Key facts

- The SARS instruments were developed based on 31 behavioral elements of SA identified by SMEs including instructor pilots. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p146]
- Data was collected on 238 mission-ready F-15C pilots from 11 squadrons at 4 different Air Force bases. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p148]
- Cronbach's coefficient alpha for internal consistency was 0.99 for all 31 items on the supervisory SARS. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p148]
- For the supervisory SARS, average individual reliability was 0.50 and pooled supervisor ratings reliability was 0.88. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p148]
- The peer SARS showed an individual reliability of 0.60 and a combined reliability of 0.97. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p148]
- Correlation between supervisor and peer SARS was 0.89 for fighter pilot ability and 0.91 for SA ability. [raw/books/strategy/Situation awareness _ analysis and measurement - edited by Mica R_ Endsley, Daniel J_ Garland.pdf#p148]

## Inferences

- Inference: The high interrater reliability values (0.88-0.97) suggest that experienced raters can consistently judge SA across pilots, supporting the validity of observer-based SA assessment.
