# Muzafer Sherif

## Summary

Muzafer Sherif was a Turkish-born social scientist who conducted classic research on intergroup conflict using boys' summer camps. His studies demonstrated that competition between groups creates hostility and that cooperation toward common goals reduces it.

## Key facts

- Sherif's camp studies showed that simply separating boys into groups and assigning names was enough to stimulate "we vs. they" feelings. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p163]
- Competitive activities (treasure hunts, tugs-of-war, athletic contests) produced name-calling, cabin raids, and physical fights between groups. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p163]
- Bringing groups together for pleasant activities (picnics, movies, social events) produced food fights, shouting contests, and shoving matches. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p164]
- Intergroup hostility was reduced only when groups had to cooperate toward common goals that required all members to work together. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p164]

## Inferences

- Inference: Sherif's research provides the scientific foundation for the jigsaw classroom approach, showing that intergroup cooperation toward superordinate goals is essential for reducing prejudice.
