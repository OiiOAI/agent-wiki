# François-Léon Arnaud

## Summary

Dr. François-Léon Arnaud was a French psychiatrist who in 1896 presented the case of patient Louis to the Société Médico-Psychologique in Paris. He introduced the term 'déjà vu' and characterized it as a misattribution of current sensations onto the past rather than an actual memory of a similar past experience. His work helped establish déjà vu as a distinct phenomenon separate from other memory distortions.

## Key facts

- In 1896, Dr. Arnaud presented patient Louis to the Société Médico-Psychologique in Paris and introduced the term 'déjà vu' to describe inappropriate familiarity. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p125]
- Arnaud characterized déjà vu as misattribution of current sensations and experiences onto the past rather than memory for a similar past experience. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p125]
- Arnaud distinguished déjà vu by its intensity, conviction that a present experience is identical to a past one, and the feeling that one knows precisely what will going to happen next. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p125]
