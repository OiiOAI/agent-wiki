# Melanie Klein

## Summary

Melanie Klein (1882-1960) was an Austrian-British psychoanalyst who specialized in the study of infants. Greene cites her observations of babies who displayed higher levels of anxiety and greed from their first days, suckling as if attacking the breast and needing excessive coddling. Klein speculated that these 'greedy babies' might be the early manifestations of chronically aggressive adults who are born with a greater need to control everything around them. Greene uses Klein's work to suggest a possible genetic component to chronic aggression.

## Key facts

- Melanie Klein specialized in the study of infants and noticed that some babies were decidedly more anxious and greedier than others from their first days. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p603]
- Klein observed that anxious infants would suckle on the mother's breast as if attacking it, needing more coddling and attention than others. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p603]
- Klein speculated that chronically aggressive adults could be adult versions of the 'greedy baby,' born with a greater need to control everything around them. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p603]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene presents Klein's observations as suggestive but not definitive evidence of genetic factors in aggression. The speculation that 'greedy babies' become chronically aggressive adults represents a theoretical link rather than established scientific fact.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Greene notes this is speculative: 'It is possible that there is a genetic component to this.' Klein's observations are clinical, not derived from controlled studies.
