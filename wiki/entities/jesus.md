# Jesus

## Summary

Jesus was a man who lived two thousand years ago and realized divine presence—his true nature—becoming a vehicle for pure consciousness. Tolle interprets Jesus's statement 'Before Abraham was, I am' as a Zen-like declaration of presence transcending time, indicating he had moved beyond the consciousness dimension governed by time into the realm of the timeless. The 'second coming' of Christ is interpreted not as an external arrival but as a transformation of human consciousness, a shift from time to presence.

## Key facts

- Jesus was a man who lived two thousand years ago and realized divine presence, his true nature, making the statement 'Before Abraham was, I am'—not 'I already existed,' which would imply time-bound identity. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p104]
- The 'second coming' of Christ is interpreted as a transformation of human consciousness, a shift from time to presence, not the arrival of some external man or woman. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p105]
- Jesus said: 'Split a piece of wood; I am there. Lift up a stone, and you will find me there'—expressing that God-essence is present in every creature and thing, not just in one form. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p127]
- Jesus said: 'Blessed are the gentle; they shall have the earth for their possession'—referring to spiritual energy that dissolves unconscious patterns rather than opposing them. [raw/books/psychology/The Power of Now - Tolle, Eckhart.pdf#p188]

## Inferences

- Inference: Tolle treats Jesus as an exemplar of realized presence rather than primarily as a religious figure, focusing on his teachings about consciousness rather than theological content.

## Uncertainties
