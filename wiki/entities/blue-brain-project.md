# Blue Brain Project

## Summary

The Blue Brain Project, led by Henry Markram at EPFL in Switzerland in collaboration with IBM, aims to reverse-engineer the mammalian brain by creating detailed 3D computational replicas. The first goal—a complete neocortical column of a two-week-old rat with ten thousand neurons and thirty million synapses—was achieved in 2006.

## Key facts

- The project aims to simulate brains of mammals with high biological accuracy and ultimately study the emergence of biological intelligence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p379]
- A single neocortical column (NCC) of a two-week-old rat was constructed, consisting of about 10,000 neurons within dimensions of 0.5mm diameter and 1.5mm height. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p380]
- The model includes 30 million synapses in precise 3D locations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p381]
- Markram argues that the main problem in computational neuroscience is that theoreticians without profound neuroscience knowledge build models far from biological realism. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p379]
- The project uses IBM's Blue Gene/L supercomputer, described as the biggest, baddest, fastest computer in the world. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p380]

## Inferences

- Inference: The project is not an attempt to create artificial intelligence but to represent the biological system, from which insights about intelligence and consciousness may be drawn.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The ultimate goal of simulating a human brain remains far in the future and may depend on whether the rat neocortical column model proves accurate.
