# Non Vixit Dream

## Summary

Freud's 'Non Vixit' dream is one of his most extensively analyzed dreams, centered on the Latin phrase meaning 'he did not live.' The dream contained hostile and distressing feelings at the point where Freud annihilated his opponent and friend, and delight at the end regarding the possibility of revenants. Analysis revealed multiple sources: anxiety about friend Fliess's recovery from surgery, self-reproaches for not visiting him, shame about past indiscretions, and a childhood phantasy of conflict with a nephew named Josef that had been projected onto various 'Josef' figures throughout life.

## Key facts

- The exciting cause of the Non Vixit dream was that Freud heard his friend Fliess in Berlin was about to undergo an operation, and unfavorable reports made him anxious. - Freud's emotional life has always insisted that he should have an intimate friend and a hated enemy, and it has not infrequently happened that friend and enemy have come together in a single individual. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1729]
- The childhood memory or phantasy that served as an intermediate element involved two children disputing over some object, where might prevailed over right. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1729]
- The satisfaction in the dream was partly interpretable as: 'It serves you right!' regarding friend Josef P., who could not wait for the removal of another man and was himself removed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p1730]
- A major part of the dream's affect was satisfaction at surviving—infantile in origin—at being in possession of the field, as in the phantasied scene from childhood.
