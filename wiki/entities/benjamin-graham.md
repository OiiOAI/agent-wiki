# Benjamin Graham

## Summary

Benjamin Graham (1894-1976) was a British-born American investor and author who pioneered the value investing philosophy. His works 'Security Analysis' and 'The Intelligent Investor' became classics, and his concepts of intrinsic value and margin of safety profoundly influenced Warren Buffett and Charlie Munger.

## Key facts

- Graham introduced the concept of 'intrinsic value' and the wisdom of buying stocks at a discount to that value. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p193]
- Graham's classic approach involved finding stocks selling below their working capital per share during the post-Depression era. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p192]
- Graham created the concept of 'Mr. Market,' treating the stock market as a manic-depressive who offers daily prices. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p194]
- Graham operated when the world was in shell-shock from the 1930s, the worst contraction in the English-speaking world in about 600 years. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p192]
- Graham was so academic that three Columbia departments (literature, Greek and Latin classics, mathematics) invited him into Ph.D. programs and asked him to start teaching immediately. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p215]
- Graham was very preoccupied and brilliant, even looked like an academic; he died rich despite being generous, spending 30 years teaching at Columbia, and authoring the best textbooks in his field. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p215]
- Buffett says 'The Intelligent Investor' is still the best book on investing, containing the only three ideas needed: Mr. Market analogy, stock as piece of a business, and margin of safety. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p252]
- If Buffett had never learned anything new after Columbia, Berkshire would be a pale shadow—Graham's teaching was enough to make anybody rich but not to build the kind of enterprise Berkshire became. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p214]

## Inferences

- Inference: Munger is illustrating that Graham's approach was necessary but not sufficient—continuous learning and evolution beyond Graham's framework was essential for Berkshire's extraordinary success.
