# Michael Gazzaniga

## Summary

Michael Gazzaniga is a neuroscientist who, with Roger Sperry, conducted pioneering split-brain research in the 1960s-70s. Their work on patients with surgically severed cerebral hemispheres demonstrated that consciousness depends on brain neural circuits and is not associated with a separate nonphysical soul. Gazzaniga later proposed the 'verbal interpreter' theory of consciousness.

## Key facts

- Gazzaniga and Sperry studied split-brain patients and found that the left hemisphere could not explain behaviors generated by the right hemisphere, leading to confabulation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p263]
- Split-brain findings provided compelling evidence that consciousness depends on brain neural circuits, not a separate nonphysical soul as Descartes proposed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p263]
- Gazzaniga and LeDoux wrote 'The Integrated Mind' (1978), proposing that the left hemisphere's verbal system generates narratives to make sense of behaviors it didn't initiate. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p273]
- Gazzaniga proposed the 'verbal interpreter' as a theory of consciousness — the left hemisphere's interpreter system generates explanations for all brain activities. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Deep History of Ourselves_ 4 - Billion - Year Story of How - Joseph E LeDoux; Caio Da Silva Sorrentino.pdf#p271]
