# Nir Eyal

## Summary

A tech designer who studied with B.J. Fogg at Stanford's Persuasive Technology Lab and worked with major Silicon Valley companies. He wrote the controversial book 'Hooked' (a guide to building habit-forming products) and later 'Indistractable' (about overcoming distraction). His work represents the dominant Silicon Valley view that individual willpower is the primary solution to attention problems.

## Key facts

- Nir Eyal wrote 'Hooked: How to Build Habit-Forming Products,' which he described as a 'cookbook' containing 'a recipe for human behavior' with techniques for 'mind manipulation.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p160]
- In 'Hooked,' Eyal wrote that designers should get users 'to repeat behaviors for long periods, ideally for the rest of their lives,' noting 'Habits can be very good for the bottom line.'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p161]
- Eyal's later book 'Indistractable' argues that individual changes should be 'the first line of defense' against distraction and that people should look inward to understand their internal triggers. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p157]
- Eyal told Hari that turning off distractions is 'pretty simple' and criticized those who don't change their notification settings, saying 'Come on, this is basic stuff, right?'. [raw/books/productivity/Stolen Focus _ Why You Can't Pay Attention - and How to Think.pdf#p159]

## Inferences

- Inference: Eyal's trajectory from creating addiction mechanisms to selling solutions to them illustrates what critics call 'cruel optimism' — offering inadequate individual solutions to systemic problems.
