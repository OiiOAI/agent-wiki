# iPhone

## Summary

Steve Jobs' iPhone, launched in 2007, was based on the diagnosis that people would value a mobile pocket-size combination Web browser and phone. Its success came from recognizing the technology was becoming capable of providing a real portable Web-surfing device, with Jobs waiting for 'the next big thing' after returning to Apple in 1997.

## Key facts

- Steve Jobs switched Apple's development focus from a pad device to the phone when he saw Bas Ording's inertia-like rubber-band touch screen scrolling in 2005. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p139]
- Jobs initially did not want an app store, wanting only pure Apple apps on the phone, but the Apple team convinced him this was a mistake. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p140]
- When the Apple app store opened in 2008, it had 500 apps; a year later it had 50,000; by 2015 there were 2 million. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p140]
- Experts predicted the iPhone would fail because they used the PC industry analogy, assuming smartphones would evolve like PCs with clones and low margins. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p140-142]

## Inferences

- Inference: The iPhone's rocket had three stages: Web in your pocket, apps, and mobile social media—none of which Jobs fully foresaw.
