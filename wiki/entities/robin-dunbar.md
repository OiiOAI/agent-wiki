# Robin Dunbar

## Summary

Robin Dunbar is an anthropologist whose research identified natural limits on group sizes and the depth of relationships humans can maintain. His findings, known as Dunbar's number, show limits at approximately 5, 15, 50, and 150 people for different levels of relationship depth and trust.

## Key facts

- Anthropologist Robin Dunbar found fifteen to be the limit of people one person can deeply trust, with around five people known and trusted closely. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L862-L864]
- Dunbar's research identifies approximately five people for close personal relationships, fifteen for deep trust, fifty for mutual trust, and 150 for remembering capabilities. [raw/books/business/Team_Topologies.epub#L872-L876]
- Dunbar correlated neocortex size with social group size across primates and apes, finding two parallel scales where apes require a larger neocortex per given group size. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p101]
- Dunbar proposed that the limitation on social group size stems from the cognitive ability to manipulate and coordinate social relationships, not vision, memory, or emotion. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p102]
- Dunbar calculated that the human social group size from neocortex ratios is approximately 150, matching the size of traditional hunter-gatherer clans and horticultural societies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p103]
- Dunbar proposed that language evolved as a substitute for physical grooming, allowing humans to 'groom' multiple individuals simultaneously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p104]

## Inferences

- Inference: Dunbar's research supports the social brain hypothesis, which posits that cognitive demands of social living drove the evolution of larger brain sizes in primates.
