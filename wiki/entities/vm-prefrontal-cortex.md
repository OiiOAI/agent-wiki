# VM Prefrontal Cortex

## Summary

The ventromedial (VM) prefrontal cortex is critical for decision making and emotional processing. Lesions cause patients to know what is right but fail to act accordingly—manifesting declarative knowledge without behavioral change. The VM cortex serves as a trigger structure for somatic/emotional states from secondary inducers (thoughts and memories), coupling knowledge to representations of 'what it feels like' to be in a situation.

## Key facts

- VM prefrontal cortex lesions cause patients to prefer disadvantageous decks A and B in the IGT, consistent with their real-life inability to decide advantageously. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p115]
- The VM cortex receives projections from all sensory modalities and has extensive bidirectional connections with amygdala, somatosensory (SI, SII), and insular cortices. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p122]
- VM cortex serves as a convergence-divergence zone coupling memory records to effector structures for emotional response and substrates holding representations of emotional states. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p127]
- VM cortex lesions interfere with generation of somatic responses (SCRs, heart rate) from internally generated images of emotional situations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p127]
- VM cortex is a trigger structure for somatic/emotional states from secondary inducers (thoughts, memories), while the amygdala triggers responses from primary inducers. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p127]
