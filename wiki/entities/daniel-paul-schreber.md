# Daniel Paul Schreber

## Summary

Daniel Paul Schreber (1842–1911) was a German jurist who served as Senatspräsident (presiding judge) at the Oberlandesgericht in Dresden. He suffered from two episodes of severe mental illness—first in 1884–1885 (diagnosed as hypochondria) and more severely in 1893–1902 (diagnosed as dementia paranoides). His autobiography, Denkwürdigkeiten eines Nervenkranken (Memoirs of a Nerve Patient), published in 1903, provided Freud with material for his psychoanalytic study of paranoia.

## Key facts

- Daniel Paul Schreber was born in 1842 and served as Senatspräsident (presiding judge) at the Oberlandesgericht in Dresden. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p9]
- Schreber suffered from his first illness in 1884–1885, diagnosed as severe hypochondria, which he attributed to standing as a candidate for the Reichstag while Landgerichtsdirektor at Chemnitz. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p12]
- Schreber's second illness began in October 1893, following notification of his appointment as Senatspräsident, with symptoms including torturing sleeplessness. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p13]
- During his second illness, Schreber believed he was dead and decomposing, suffered from hallucinations, and attempted suicide by drowning. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p13]
- Schreber's delusional system centered on his belief that he had a mission to redeem the world and restore mankind to a lost state of bliss, contingent upon his transformation from a man into a woman. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p17]
- The original core of Schreber's delusion involved emasculation as persecution and sexual abuse rather than as a religious mission—this sexual delusion was later transformed into the Redeemer delusion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p18]
- Professor Flechsig of Leipzig was initially identified by Schreber as his chief persecutor, whom he called a 'Seelenmörder' (soul murderer). [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p14]
- Schreber published his Denkwürdigkeiten eines Nervenkranken in 1903, and in July 1902 he had succeeded in having his civil rights restored through legal action. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p15]
- Freud states that the psychoanalytic investigation of paranoia is possible because paranoics 'betray (in a distorted form, it is true) precisely those things which other neurotics keep hidden as a secret.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p9]
- Schreber served as Senatspräsident (Presiding Judge) of the Saxon Court of Appeals in Dresden. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3745]
- He published 'Denkwürdigkeiten eines Nervenkranken' (Memoirs of a Neurotic) in 1903, describing his delusions. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3745]
- Schreber experienced delusions of persecution by Professor Flechsig and later developed a belief that God demanded voluptuousness from him. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3746]
- He believed he was being transformed into a woman and felt he had become God's wife. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3747]
- His marriage was childless, which Freud linked to his psychological state. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3768]
- He fell ill at age 51, a critical period in sexual life corresponding to the male climacteric. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3758]

## Inferences

- Inference: Schreber's pre-illness character was described as intelligent and of high ethical character, which aligns with his later role as a presiding judge.
- Inference: The court judgment restoring his rights recognized his 'absolute harmlessness,' suggesting his delusions did not lead to dangerous actions.
- Inference: Schreber's claim of strict sexual morals before his illness, combined with the content of his delusions, suggests repression of homosexual impulses that emerged during his illness.
