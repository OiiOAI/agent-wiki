# Brian Hare

## Summary

Brian Hare is an evolutionary anthropologist at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig. His research demonstrates that chimpanzees and other primates have more sophisticated social cognitive abilities than previously recognized, particularly in competitive contexts. His work challenges assumptions about what mental capacities are uniquely human.

## Key facts

- Hare and colleagues at Max Planck Institute showed that chimpanzees can take the visual perspective of another when in competition for food. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p203]
- Chimpanzees spontaneously avoided food that humans were watching, using indirect routes to approach hidden food only when the human could not see them move away. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p204]
- Hare's research provides strong evidence for intentional deception in chimpanzees at least in competitive food situations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p204]
- Hare suggests that previous tests looking for theory-of-mind capacities in primates using helping tasks may have looked in the wrong place, as chimpanzees perform most skillfully in competitive cognitive tasks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Human _ the science behind what makes us unique - Michael S_ Gazzaniga.pdf#p204]
