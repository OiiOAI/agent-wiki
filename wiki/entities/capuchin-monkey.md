# Capuchin Monkey

## Summary

Capuchin monkeys (Cebus apella) are New World primates studied in comparative economics research to understand the evolutionary origins of economic preferences. Research by Santos and colleagues uses token trading methodologies to examine whether capuchins display economic behaviors analogous to humans, including price-theoretic responses and prospect theory biases.

## Key facts

- Santos and colleagues examine whether capuchins display economic behaviors analogous to humans including price-theoretic responses and prospect theory biases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p81]
- Capuchins are studied as a model primate species to explore whether behavioral economic biases have evolutionary origins shared with humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p85]
- Capuchins are native to South and Central America and evolved in different ecological niches than Old World species. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- Capuchins have extremely large brains relative to their body size and live in large social groups (up to 40 individuals in the wild). [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- Capuchins are considered 'the chimpanzee' of New World primates among primate researchers and are known for elaborate tool-use including stone hammers and anvils for opening palm nuts. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
- Capuchins are an extremely socially adept and tolerant species, maintaining only a loosely defined dominance hierarchy that permits sharing food with many group members. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroeconomics _ Decision Making and the Brain - Paul W_ Glimcher, Ernst Fehr, Colin Camerer, Antonio Rangel,.pdf#p84]
