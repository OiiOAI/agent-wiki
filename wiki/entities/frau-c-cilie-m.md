# Frau Cäcilie M.

## Summary

Frau Cäcilie M. was Freud's most severe and instructive case of hysteria, whose thirty-year illness provided extensive material for understanding hysterical conversion and symbolization. Her symptoms included violent facial neuralgia, hysterical attacks with hallucinatory reproduction of traumatic scenes, and numerous somatic phenomena symbolically linked to her emotional conflicts.

## Key facts

- Frau Cäcilie suffered from violent trigeminal neuralgia which, upon hypnotic analysis, proved to be symbolically linked to experiences of insult—the sensation of being 'slapped in the face.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p177-179]
- She developed pain in her right heel symbolically linked to fear of not 'finding herself on a right footing' with strangers at a sanatorium. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p179-180]
- The study of Frau Cäcilie M., jointly with Breuer, led directly to the publication of their Preliminary Communication. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p178]

## Inferences

- Inference: Her case demonstrated that the same neuralgia could be triggered through associative reverberations from her mental life (symbolic conversion) as well as through the original traumatic simultaneity, exactly as observed in Fräulein Elisabeth von R.
