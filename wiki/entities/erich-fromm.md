# Erich Fromm

## Summary

Erich Fromm (1900-1980) was a German-American psychoanalyst and social psychologist. Greene cites Fromm's work on the relationship between early family life and adult aggression. Fromm argued that domineering parents who repress their children's need for power and independence often create adults who like to dominate and tyrannize others. Children who were beaten often resort to physical abuse as adults, turning the enforced passivity of childhood into active control through aggression. Greene uses Fromm's ideas to illustrate environmental influences on chronic aggression.

## Key facts

- According to Erich Fromm, domineering parents who repress their children's need for power and independence often create adults who like to dominate and tyrannize others. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p604]
- Fromm observed that children who were beaten often resort to beating and physical abuse as adults, turning enforced passivity into active control. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p604]

## Inferences

- Inference: Fromm's analysis suggests that aggression can be learned as a coping mechanism—children who lacked control develop a compensatory need for total control as adults. This environmental factor combines with (or may override) genetic predispositions.
