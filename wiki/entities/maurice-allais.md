# Maurice Allais

## Summary

Maurice Allais was a French economist who won the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (1988) and gained fame for the 'Allais Paradox,' which he demonstrated at a 1952 Paris conference attended by the world's leading economists, including several future Nobel laureates. The paradox showed that even the most eminent proponents of expected utility theory made choices inconsistent with their own theory, challenging the normative foundations of rational choice.

## Key facts

- Allais designed his paradox specifically to demonstrate that the 'American school' (expected utility theory as formalized by von Neumann and Morgenstern) was contradicted by the actual choices of its most eminent proponents, including Paul Samuelson, Kenneth Arrow, and Milton Friedman. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p404]
- Allais presented his paradox at the 1952 Paris conference, and most attendees did not notice the inconsistency in their choices until Allais pointed it out at the end of the meeting—an episode Kahneman and Tversky later described as a clear example of theory-induced blindness even among the most sophisticated minds.
