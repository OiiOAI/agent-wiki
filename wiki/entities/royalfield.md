# Royalfield

## Summary

Royalfield is a disguised Fortune 500 company whose annual strategy retreat illustrates the failure mode of treating strategic planning as budgeting. The CEO focused on financial performance targets (Success Score Cards) rather than addressing fundamental strategic challenges, leading to market share losses of 30 percent.

## Key facts

- Royalfield is a Fortune 500 company where about twenty-five senior executives gather annually for strategic planning. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p257]
- Royalfield's Success Score Card specified 15 percent annual growth in earnings with a 15 percent return on equity—financial targets rather than strategic initiatives. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p282]
- Royalfield's CEO's daily experience was explaining financial results to investors, analysts, and the board, and his incentive package was framed in accounting results and stock-market returns. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p283]
- Royalfield had significant strategic issues: organized by regions while the industry had become global, and its technology had been equaled and surpassed by a competitor. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p283]
- When Rumelt raised strategic issues with the CEO, the CEO held out his hand asking him to stop, not wanting negative things about the team. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p283]
- In the years after that event, Royalfield's competitors grew faster and the company lost 30 percent market share as competitors targeted key customer segments. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p284]
- Royalfield lowered expenses so net profit margin improved, but growth rate fell behind the industry and market share dropped by 30 percent. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p284]

## Inferences

- Inference: Royalfield's case illustrates that optimizing for financial metrics without addressing competitive challenges can improve short-term profitability while destroying long-term market position.
