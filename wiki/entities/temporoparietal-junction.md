# Temporoparietal Junction (TPJ)

## Summary

The temporoparietal junction (TPJ) is located at the posterior end of the superior temporal cortex, at the junction with parietal and occipital cortex. It is prominently involved in higher order social reasoning tasks and has been described as involved in 'uniquely human social cognition'. Connectivity-based parcellation reveals that posterior TPJ connects with nodes of the social brain including anterior temporal cortex, PCC, and MPFC, while anterior TPJ interacts with areas associated with attentional control.

## Key facts

- In the human brain, a prominent locus of socially related gray matter changes is in the posterior end of the superior temporal cortex, at the junction with parietal and occipital cortex, known as the TPJ. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p188]
- TPJ gray matter changes correlate with the ability to perform recursive social reasoning rather than social network size directly. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p188]
- The TPJ has been described as involved in 'uniquely human social cognition'. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p188]
- Connectivity analysis identified the mid-STS as the macaque area with the most similar connectivity fingerprint to human TPJ. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p193]
