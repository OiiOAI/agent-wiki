# Broccoli

## Summary

Broccoli is the eighth Genius Food, a cruciferous vegetable rich in sulforaphane—a powerful genetic modulator that activates the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway. Young broccoli sprouts contain 20 to 100 times the sulforaphane-producing compounds of adult broccoli. Sulforaphane shows promise for treating or preventing cancer, autism, autoimmunity, brain inflammation, gut inflammation, and obesity.

## Key facts

- Sulforaphane is created when two compounds in broccoli, held in separate compartments of the plant's cells, unite as a result of chewing. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p311]
- Sulforaphane is the body's most potent known activator of the Nrf2 pathway, the master switch for creating powerful chemicals that mop up oxidative stress. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p311]
- Young broccoli sprouts yield 20 to 100 times the sulforaphane-producing compounds as adult broccoli. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p312]
- One pound of broccoli sprouts equals 100 pounds of adult broccoli in terms of sulforaphane-producing capacity. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p312]
- The enzyme myrosinase, which helps create sulforaphane, is destroyed by high-heat cooking, but adding mustard powder after cooking can restore this ability. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p312]
- Sulforaphane has been shown to reduce weight gain by 15 percent and visceral fat by 20 percent in mice fed an obesity-promoting diet. [raw/books/health/Genius Foods - Max Lugavere.pdf#p311]

## Inferences

- Inference: Broccoli sprouts represent a highly efficient dietary source of sulforaphane, making them a cost-effective addition to a brain-healthy diet.
