# Thalamus

## Summary

The thalamus is an egg-shaped structure constituting the dorsal portion of the diencephalon. It serves as an essential relay (not passive) between sensory receptors and the cerebral cortex, acting as a gatekeeper that enhances or prevents passage of specific information depending on behavioral state. It contains approximately 50 thalamic nuclei classified into anterior, medial, ventrolateral, and posterior groups relative to the internal medullary lamina.

## Key facts

- The thalamus contains excitatory thalamic relay cells that convey sensory input to primary sensory areas of the cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p130]
- The thalamus is classified into approximately 50 nuclei grouped into anterior, medial, ventrolateral, and posterior groups relative to the internal medullary lamina. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p130]
- The ventral posterior lateral nucleus conveys somatosensory information from the spinal cord to the primary somatosensory cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p130]
- The thalamic reticular nucleus surrounds the thalamus and is made up almost totally of inhibitory neurons that modulate relay cell responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p130]
- The thalamus receives extensive return inputs from the neocortex (feedback connections) that play a modulatory role in processing sensory information. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p131]
- The output of somatosensory information from the ventral posterior lateral nucleus is subject to four types of processing: local processing, modulation by brain stem inputs, inhibitory input from the reticular nucleus, and modulatory feedback from the neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p131]
