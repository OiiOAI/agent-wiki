# Epictetus

## Summary

Stoic philosopher and former slave whose teachings on responding to misfortune influenced Munger's approach to life's inevitable setbacks. Epictetus taught that every mischance creates an opportunity to behave well and learn something useful, with duty being not to become immersed in self-pity but to utilize each terrible blow constructively.

## Key facts

- Epictetus believed that every mischance in life, however bad, created an opportunity to behave well and to learn something useful. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p447]
- Epictetus's epitaph: 'Here lies Epictetus, a slave, maimed in body, the ultimate in poverty, and favored by the Gods.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p447]
- Munger cites Epictetus's influence on Marcus Aurelius and many others over many centuries. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p447]
