# David Chalmers

## Summary

David Chalmers is an Australian philosopher who coined the term 'hard problem' of consciousness in the early 1990s. His 1995 paper established the framework that continues to shape philosophical and scientific debate about consciousness. Chalmers has also proposed the 'meta-problem' of consciousness and defended a form of panpsychism.

## Key facts

- Chalmers coined the term 'hard problem' of consciousness in the early 1990s. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p23]
- In his 1974 paper 'What is it like to be a bat?', Thomas Nagel argued that consciousness involves subjective experience regardless of functional capacity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p21]
- Chalmers more recently introduced the 'meta-problem'—why people think there is a hard problem—which he treats as an easy problem about verbal behaviour. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p38]
- Chalmers co-authored 'The Extended Mind' with Clark, arguing that true mental circuitry could be spread across brain, body, and aspects of the material and technological world. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p163]
- Chalmers introduced the thought experiment of philosophical zombies—creatures whose behaviors perfectly match ours but lacking any inner mental life. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p124]
- Chalmers proposed the meta-problem of consciousness as the problem of explaining why we are drawn toward mind-body dualisms and tempted to posit an explanatory gap. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Experience Machine_ How Our Minds Pred - Andy Clark.pdf#p115]
