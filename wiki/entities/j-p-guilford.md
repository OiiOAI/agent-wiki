# J. P. Guilford

## Summary

J. P. Guilford was an American psychologist who, during World War II working with the U.S. Army, encountered ENIAC and became convinced that all intelligent behavior could be reduced to computer protocols. In the 1950s, he proposed that innovation was a combination of divergent thinking (randomly associating old ideas) and convergent thinking (refining results via pattern finding)—a two-step protocol called ideation that was later incorporated into design thinking and eventually generative AI.

## Key facts

- During World War II, J. P. Guilford worked with the U.S. Army and encountered ENIAC, becoming convinced that all intelligent behavior could be reduced to computer protocols. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p236]
- In the 1950s, Guilford proposed that innovation was a combo of two computer-like protocols: divergent thinking (randomly associating old ideas) and convergent thinking (refining via pattern finding). [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p236]
- This two-step protocol for computing new ideas became known as ideation, which programmers incorporated into businesses, schools, design thinking, and managerial troubleshooting. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p236]
- In the twenty-first century, programmers fulfilled Guilford's ultimate dream by coding ideation into computers, resulting in generative AI. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p236]
