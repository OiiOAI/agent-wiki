# Bed Nucleus of the Stria Terminalis (BNST)

## Summary

The BNST is a sexually dimorphic brain region in the basal forebrain that plays important roles in mating behaviors, stress responses, and emotional processing. It contains more neurons in males than females in rodents and humans, and its volume correlates with gender identity in transgender individuals.

## Key facts

- The BNST is sexually dimorphic, containing more neurons in males compared to females in both rodents and humans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1272]
- In humans, the BNST volume is smaller in male-to-female transsexuals compared to men, while female-to-male transsexuals appear to have larger BNST volumes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1281]
- Lesions of the BNST in male rodents result in deficits in male sexual behavior and can disinhibit female-type sexual receptivity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1272]
