# Amygdala

## Summary

The amygdala is the neuronal substrate for feeling emotions as opposed to their outward manifestation. It generates emotional content and, through its connectivity to cortex, thalamus, and hypothalamus, coordinates both the cognitive and motor expression components of emotional responses.

## Key facts

- Experimental activation of the amygdala by electrical stimulation or excitatory amino acids elicits states consistent with fear and distress. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p169]
- Long-term stimulation of the amygdala produces stress-related illnesses such as gastric ulcers, demonstrating its role in generating emotional-physiological states. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p169]
- Lesions of the amygdala produce Klüver-Bucy syndrome: animals become placid and emotionally unengaged, unable to recognize the context of danger. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p169]
- The amygdala disconnects from cortex may result in inability to generate emotional conditions by specific sensory modalities while other sensory inputs remain functional. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p170]
- The amygdala triggers emotion from primary inducers, which are stimuli innately pleasurable/aversive or learned to be so, automatically, quickly, and necessarily eliciting emotional response. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p124]
- Amygdala patients failed to generate SCRs when told they lost large sums of money, unlike normal subjects and VM patients who generated these responses. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p125]
- Patients with bilateral amygdala lesions, like VM patients, selected more cards from disadvantageous decks in the IGT and failed to generate anticipatory SCRs. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p125]
- The amygdala is evolutionarily designed for 'fight or flight' responses and benefits from occasional false alarms, as responding to a false alarm (rock like bear) causes no harm. [raw/books/neuroscience/Neuroscience of Decision Making - Mandel, David R.,Vartanian, Oshin.pdf#p124]
- The amygdala consists of approximately 12 nuclei, but the lateral and central nuclei are especially important in fear conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1136]
- Lesions of the amygdala prevent Pavlovian fear conditioning; animals with amygdala damage fail to learn the association between conditioned and unconditioned stimuli. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1135]
- The lateral nucleus is the input nucleus receiving information about the conditioned stimulus from the thalamus, while the central nucleus is the output region projecting to brain stem areas controlling defensive behaviors. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1136]
- Patients with damage to the amygdala fail to undergo fear conditioning and fail to recognize facial expressions of fear. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1137]
- The amygdala is also involved in positive emotions and processing of rewards; it is required for associating neutral stimuli with rewards. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1139]
- The lateral nucleus of the amygdala is the site of convergence for conditioned (tone) and unconditioned (shock) stimulus signals, receiving direct and indirect pathways from thalamus and cortex, and is the primary site of LTP induction during fear conditioning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1530]
- LTP in the lateral amygdala requires Ca2+ influx through NMDA receptors and L-type voltage-gated Ca2+ channels, triggering a biochemical cascade that enhances synaptic transmission through insertion of AMPA receptors and increased presynaptic transmitter release. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1532]
- The level of amygdala activity during encoding of emotional stories is highly correlated with long-term free recall of emotional information, implicating the amygdala in encoding and retrieval of emotionally charged memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1510]

## Inferences

- Inference: The amygdala specifically generates the emotional feeling component, distinct from the motor expression (handled by hypothalamus/brainstem) and sensory detection, representing a specialized affective processor.
- Inference: The amygdala system is a necessary prerequisite for normal development of the VM system for triggering somatic/emotional states from secondary inducers.
