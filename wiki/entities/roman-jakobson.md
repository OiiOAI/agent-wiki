# Roman Jakobson

## Summary

Roman Jakobson (1896-1982) was a Russian-American linguist and literary theorist who made foundational contributions to linguistics, including the development of distinctive feature theory and phonology. He proposed that word associations can be analyzed into two broad categories: syntagmatic (complementary sequential relationships) and paradigmatic (substitution relationships), and suggested these processes should be distributed to different cortical regions.

## Key facts

- Jakobson proposed that word associations underlying language processes can be analyzed into two broad categories: syntagmatic and paradigmatic dimensions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p331]
- Jakobson suggested that these two types of word association operations should be distributed to frontal and posterior regions of cortex, respectively. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p331]
