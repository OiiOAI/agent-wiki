# Eugen Bleuler

## Summary

Swiss psychiatrist (1857–1939) who coined the term 'ambivalence' to describe the simultaneous presence of opposing emotional attitudes towards the same object. Bleuler worked closely with Jung at the Burghölzli clinic and introduced terminology fundamental to psychoanalytic theory, including 'ambivalence' and 'complex'. Freud acknowledges Bleuler's contribution in discussing the coexistence of positive and negative transferences.

## Key facts

- Bleuler coined the term 'ambivalence' to describe the simultaneous presence of opposite feelings (such as love and hate) towards the same person. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3818]
- Freud references Bleuler's 1911 work on ambivalence and his Bern lecture on the topic delivered in 1910. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p3818]
