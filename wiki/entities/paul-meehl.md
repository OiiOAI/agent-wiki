# Paul Meehl

## Summary

A versatile American psychologist known for his work comparing clinical versus statistical prediction. His 1954 book 'Clinical vs. Statistical Prediction' reviewed 20 studies showing that mechanical formulas outperformed expert clinical judgment, a finding that triggered decades of controversy and over 200 replications. Meehl also made important contributions to clinical psychology, psychoanalysis, philosophy of science, and personality research.

## Key facts

- Paul Meehl's 1954 book 'Clinical vs. Statistical Prediction' showed that simple statistical formulas beat clinical expert predictions in approximately 20 studies across domains including academic performance, criminal recidivism, and pilot training. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p288]
- Meehl famously stated that no other controversy in social science shows as many diverse studies with uniform results pointing in the same direction. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p288]
- Meehl described the clinical vs. algorithmic debate as having a moral dimension, with statistical methods criticized as 'mechanical, atomistic, additive, picture-breaking' while clinical methods were praised as 'dynamic, global, meaningful, holistic.'. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p294]
- Kahneman used Meehl's findings to redesign the Israeli Army's interview procedures, incorporating systematic trait evaluation and formulas to improve prediction over global clinical impressions. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p297]

## Inferences

- Inference: Meehl's finding that simple formulas beat experts has not led to the widespread adoption of algorithmic decision-making, suggesting that emotional and moral resistance to reducing humans to algorithms remains powerful despite the evidence.
