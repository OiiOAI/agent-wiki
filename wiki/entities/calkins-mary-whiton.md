# Mary Whiton Calkins

## Summary

Mary Whiton Calkins (1863–1930) was a pioneering American psychologist at Wellesley College who conducted one of the first statistical studies of dream content in 1893. She was the first woman president of the American Psychological Association and developed standardized questionnaires for dream research. Her key finding was the continuity hypothesis, showing connections between waking and dreaming life.

## Key facts

- Calkins published Statistics of Dreams in 1893, using statistical principles to analyze nearly 400 dream reports from herself and a male participant. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p32]
- She found that most dreams, including vivid ones, occurred during morning sleep, with dreaming also occurring in earlier parts of the night. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p33]
- She concluded there is congruence and continuity between waking and dreaming life, a finding that foreshadowed the continuity hypothesis. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p33]
- Calkins developed standardized questionnaires to determine the frequency of visual, auditory, tactile, olfactory, and taste imagery in dreams, establishing a sensory hierarchy. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p33]
- She became the first woman president of the American Psychological Association. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p32]

## Inferences

- Inference: Calkins's methodological approach—defining variables, designing reproducible experiments, and emphasizing quantitative data—established foundational practices for dream science.
