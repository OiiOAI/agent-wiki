# Georgia Institute of Technology

## Summary

Georgia Tech was the engineering school where John Boyd earned his industrial engineering degree from 1960-1962 under the AFIT program. It was ranked among the top state engineering schools in America with a demanding academic program and high bust-out rate.

## Key facts

- About 6,488 students were enrolled at Georgia Tech in 1960, with only 20 female students and no significant protests during the 1960s. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p125]
- Tech's bust-out rate was so severe that at freshmen orientation students were told half would flunk out their first year. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p125]
- Boyd wore civilian clothes to class and no one knew he was an Air Force officer; he was simply an older student competing in class. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p126]
- Boyd's Georgia Tech transcript shows erratic grades ranging from A to D with several dropped courses, but he doubled up enough to graduate one quarter early. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p128]
- Charles E. Cooper, a 19-year-old aeronautical engineering student, tutored Boyd in thermodynamics and helped him understand entropy concepts that led to his breakthrough. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p130-133]
