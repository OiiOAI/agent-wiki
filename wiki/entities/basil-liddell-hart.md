# Basil Liddell Hart

## Summary

Basil Liddell Hart was a British military theorist whose concept of the 'Indirect Approach' heavily influenced Boyd's thinking. Boyd read most of Liddell Hart's work and his ideas bear close resemblance to Liddell Hart's arguments. Liddell Hart is considered the conceptual father of the Blitzkrieg concept that Boyd studied extensively.

## Key facts

- Boyd read at least six works by Liddell Hart including A Science of Infantery Tactics Simplified (1926), The Future of Infantery (1933), The Ghost of Napoleon (1934), The German Generals Talk (1948), and Strategy (1967). [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p62]
- Liddell Hart developed the concept of the 'Indirect Approach' which over time became elevated to a general rule and ultimately a philosophical basis for the analysis of all war at all levels. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p62]
- Liddell Hart criticized Clausewitz's three dominant theories: the theory of absolute warfare, the theory of concentrating fire against the main enemy, and the theory that the true objective in war is the enemy's armed forces. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p62]
- Liddell Hart argued that 'the most decisive victory is of no value if a nation be bled white while gaining it' and that grand strategy should discover and pierce the Achilles heel of the opposing government's power to make war. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p63]
- Liddell Hart stated that a strategist should think in terms of paralyzing, not of killing, and that it is more potent as well as more economical to disarm the enemy than to attempt his destruction by hard fighting. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p63]
- Liddell Hart asserted that 'true concentration is the product of dispersion' – an army should be distributed so its parts can combine to produce maximum concentration at one place while minimum force is used elsewhere. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p65]
- Liddell Hart is considered the conceptual father of the Blitzkrieg concept Germany developed during the Interbellum, a concept Boyd took deep interest in. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p65]
- What Liddell Hart termed the Indirect Approach, Boyd refers to as Maneuver Conflict, one of three kinds of human conflict. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p66]

## Inferences

- Inference: Boyd's parallels with Liddell Hart run deep; although Boyd conceptually expanded considerably on Liddell Hart's ideas, the underlying philosophy was shared.
