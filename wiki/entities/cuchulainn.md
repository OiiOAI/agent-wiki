# Cuchulainn

## Summary

Cuchulainn is the chief hero of the Irish Ulster Cycle whose cosmic energies would burst like an eruption, causing a characteristic 'paroxysm' or 'distortion.' His first day under arms at age four and his hero-journey to learn battle skills from Scathach exemplify the hero as warrior and lover archetypes.

## Key facts

- When Cuchulainn was four years old, he went to test the 'boy corps' of his uncle King Conchobar. Fifty boys attacked him simultaneously; he parried all with his fists, forearms, palms, and little shield, then was seized with his battle-frenzy and laid low fifty of the best. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p259]
- Cuchulainn had seven toes to each foot and seven fingers to each hand; his eyes were bright with seven pupils apiece, each glittering with seven gemlike sparkles. On either cheek he had four moles: a blue, a crimson, a green, and a yellow. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p260]
- During his paroxysm, Cuchulainn's flesh and every limb quivered; his feet, shins, and knees shifted behind him; one eye became engulfed far in his head while the other protruded upon his cheek; flakes of fire streamed from his twisted mouth. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p261]
- The Badb (Morrigan), a battle-goddess, told Cuchulainn she would become an eel and throw a noose round his feet at a certain ford; he would die when the calf of a sacred cow became a year old, though he later overcame her and lived to die another day. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p281-p282]
- Cuchulainn won from the warrior-sorceress Scathach acquaintance with her feats, marriage to her daughter without payment of a bride-price, knowledge of his future, and sexual intercourse with herself during his year of training. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p269]

## Inferences

- Inference: Cuchulainn's distinctive physical features and his explosive paroxysm represent the dangerous nature of heroic power—the divine energies within the hero that cannot be contained by ordinary human limits.
