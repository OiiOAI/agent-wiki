# Ion Channels

## Summary

Ion channels are integral membrane proteins that span the cell membrane and form aqueous pores allowing selective ion flow across the lipid bilayer. They have three essential properties: recognition and selection of specific ions, opening and closing in response to electrical, chemical, or mechanical signals, and conduction of ions across the membrane. Up to 100 million ions can pass through a single channel each second, enabling the rapid changes in membrane potential required for neuronal signaling. Ion channels are grouped into gene families including voltage-gated channels, ligand-gated channels, and mechanically gated channels.

## Key facts

- Up to 100 million ions can pass through a single channel each second, a rate comparable to the turnover rate of the fastest enzymes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p166]
- The human genome contains nine genes encoding variants of voltage-gated Na+ channels, 10 for Ca2+ channels, 80 for K+ channels, 70 for ligand-gated channels, and more than a dozen for Cl- channels. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p177]
- Ion channels have three conformational states: closed and activatable (resting), open (active), or closed and nonactivatable (inactivated or refractory). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p173]
- Voltage-gated ion channels are thought to have evolved from a common ancestral K+ channel from single-cell organisms living more than 1.4 billion years ago. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p177]
- Ion channels can be grouped into gene superfamilies whose members have similar amino acid sequences, transmembrane topology, and related functions, having evolved from common ancestral genes by duplication and divergence. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p177]
