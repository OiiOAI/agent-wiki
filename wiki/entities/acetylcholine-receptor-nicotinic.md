# Nicotinic Acetylcholine Receptor

## Summary

The nicotinic acetylcholine receptor is a pentameric ionotropic receptor-channel found at the neuromuscular junction and in neurons, composed of multiple subunits that form a cation-selective pore activated by acetylcholine binding.

## Key facts

- The muscle nicotinic ACh receptor is a pentamer composed of two α-subunits and one each of β-, γ-, and δ-subunits encoded by four distinct but related genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p264]
- Each ACh receptor has two binding sites for acetylcholine, formed at the interfaces between α-subunits and their neighboring γ- or δ-subunits; both sites must be occupied for efficient channel opening. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p264]
- The ACh receptor-channel is permeable to both Na+ and K+ with nearly equal permeability, giving a reversal potential of approximately 0 mV. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- Single-channel conductance of the ACh receptor-channel is approximately 30 pS. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- The pore diameter of the ACh receptor-channel is approximately 0.6 nm, allowing passage of hydrated Na+ and K+ but excluding anions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p261]
- The ACh receptor-channel undergoes desensitization during prolonged agonist exposure, entering a nonconducting state even while agonist remains bound. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p263]
- High-resolution X-ray crystal structures have revealed that ligand binding causes subunit tilting and twisting that opens a hydrophobic leucine gate in the middle of the M2 pore segment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p268]
