# Amyloid Precursor Protein

## Summary

Amyloid precursor protein (APP) is a transmembrane protein encoded by a gene on chromosome 21. Sequential cleavage by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase generates Aβ peptides. Mutations in APP cause autosomal dominant early-onset AD either by increasing total Aβ production (Swedish mutation at β-secretase site) or by shifting production toward the more aggregation-prone Aβ42 species. A protective APP mutation near the β-secretase site decreases Aβ production.

## Key facts

- APP is encoded by a gene on chromosome 21; triplication of this chromosome in Down syndrome leads to 50% overproduction of APP and Aβ throughout life. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- BACE1 (β-secretase) cleaves APP at the N-terminus of the Aβ sequence to generate soluble APPβ and a membrane-bound C-terminal fragment. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- γ-Secretase cleaves APP within the transmembrane domain to release Aβ peptides of varying lengths, including Aβ40 and the more aggregation-prone Aβ42. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Swedish mutations at the β-secretase cleavage site, required for Aβ formation, cause cells to secrete several-fold more Aβ peptide than wild-type APP. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- A mutation in APP adjacent to the β-secretase site protects against AD by decreasing Aβ production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- APP mutations within the Aβ sequence itself affect Aβ aggregation and clearance from the brain without altering Aβ production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- A mutation causing γ-secretase to generate a greater proportion of longer Aβ species (such as Aβ42) relative to shorter species (Aβ40) is associated with familial AD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Duplication of the APP locus alone causes autosomal dominant AD and cerebral amyloid angiopathy in the absence of Down syndrome. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- APP is a large transmembrane glycoprotein present in dendrites, cell bodies, and axons of many types of neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1388]
- APP is cleaved by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase (presenilin complex) to generate Aβ peptides of 40 or 42 amino acids. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1389]
- Mutations flanking or within the Aβ region of APP cause increased Aβ secretion, and Aβ42 nucleates more rapidly into amyloid fibrils than Aβ40. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1390]
- The APP gene lies on chromosome 21, explaining why individuals with Down syndrome (trisomy 21) develop AD pathology by age 40. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1390]

## Inferences

- Inference: APP mutations causing early-onset AD demonstrate that altered APP processing leading to increased Aβ production or aggregation is sufficient to cause the disease, establishing a direct causal relationship.
