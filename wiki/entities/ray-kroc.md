# Ray Kroc

## Summary

Ray Kroc (1902-1984) was the milkshake machine salesman who discovered the MacDonald brothers' hamburger stand in 1952 and went on to build the McDonald's empire. Gerber presents him as the consummate entrepreneur who understood that the hamburger wasn't his product—McDonald's was. He created the Business Format Franchise and the Turn-Key Revolution by building a system that could work without him.

## Key facts

- Ray Kroc was a fifty-two-year-old milkshake machine salesman when he first visited the MacDonald brothers' hamburger stand in 1952. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p86]
- Kroc convinced Mac and Jim MacDonald to let him franchise their method, and twelve years later bought them out. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p86]
- Ray Kroc suffered from one major liability: he had a huge dream and very little money, making the franchisee model essential to his strategy. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p90]
- Kroc went to work on his business, not in it, treating it like an engineer working on a pre-production prototype. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p91]
- Gerber describes Ray Kroc as a man in love with McDonald's, just as a craftsperson loves their art, devoted to perfecting his system. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p94]
