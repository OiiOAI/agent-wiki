# Parkinson Disease

## Summary

Parkinson disease is a common neurodegenerative disorder affecting approximately 3% of the population over age 65. The disease is characterized by resting tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity, and impairment in movement initiation. Pathological hallmarks include progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and accumulation of Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites. Both sporadic and familial forms exist, with multiple genes identified including SNCA, PARKIN, PINK1, and LRRK2.

## Key facts

- Parkinson disease affects approximately 3% of the population older than age 65 years. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1548]
- The pathological hallmarks are progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and accumulation of Lewy bodies and Lewy neurites. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1548]
- Mutations in SNCA encoding alpha-synuclein cause autosomal dominant Parkinson disease, with duplications and triplications also identified as causes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1548]
- PARK2, PARK6, and PARK7 encode parkin, PINK1, and DJ-1 respectively, which are involved in mitochondrial function and quality control. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1549]
- PINK1 and parkin work together to promote healthy mitochondria through a process termed mitophagy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1556]
- Dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta are particularly vulnerable due to their dependence on Ca2+ channels for rhythmic firing, causing baseline mitochondrial stress. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1550]
- Parkinson's disease typically strikes people in their fifties, affecting men more than women. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- In Parkinson's, neurons in the substantia nigra die, diminishing dopamine transmitted to the putamen. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p161]
- Parkinson's patients took longer to trace around a star, traced more slowly, and paused more frequently than controls. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p162]
- Despite impaired mirror tracing, Parkinson's patients showed normal sequence-specific learning, indicating different neural substrates for different motor skills. [raw/books/neuroscience/Permanent Present Tense _ The Unforgettable Life of the - Suzanne Corkin.pdf#p164]

## Inferences

- Inference: The putamen, affected early in Parkinson's, is necessary for mirror-tracing skill learning but not for sequence-specific motor learning.
