# Division of Neurobiology and Behavior at NYU

## Summary

The Division of Neurobiology and Behavior was founded by Eric Kandel at New York University. It brought together Alden Spencer, James Schwartz, and Kandel to merge cellular neurobiology with the study of simple behavior, pioneering the use of reductionist approaches to understand learning and memory.

## Key facts

- Kandel founded the Division of Neurobiology and Behavior at NYU, with Alden Spencer, James Schwartz, and himself forming the nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p195]
- The division was influenced by Stephen Kuffler's unified department of neurobiology at Harvard, which brought electrophysiology together with biochemistry and cell biology. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p195]
- Kandel, Spencer, and Schwartz proposed that the division examine how the nervous system produces behavior and how behavior is modified by learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p196]
- Tom Carew joined the division in 1971 and opened up studies of long-term memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p203]
