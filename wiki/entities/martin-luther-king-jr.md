# Martin Luther King Jr.

## Summary

Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968) was the central figure of the American civil rights movement, whose life story illustrates the Law of Aimlessness and the power of developing a sense of purpose. Born in Atlanta to a strict minister father with high expectations, King struggled with internal conflicts about his path until he heard an inner voice during the Montgomery bus boycott crisis that confirmed his calling to stand up for justice. His subsequent leadership of the civil rights movement, from the Birmingham campaign to his 'I Have a Dream' speech, demonstrates how purpose guides decisions and sustains courage through adversity. He was assassinated in Memphis in 1968.

## Key facts

- King was born in 1929 in Atlanta, Georgia, the son of Martin Sr., pastor of Ebenezer Baptist Church. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p437]
- His father used whippings to turn him into a 'real man' and placed high expectations on young Martin to follow his ministerial path. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p437]
- King was exceptionally sensitive and had an easygoing temperament contrasting with his father's seriousness and simple fundamentalist faith. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p438]
- He initially resisted his father's plan, studying medicine, sociology, and law at Morehouse before choosing ministry at Crozer Theological Seminary. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p439]
- In 1954 he accepted the position at Dexter Avenue Baptist Church in Montgomery, Alabama, against his father's and wife's wishes. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p440]
- In December 1955 he was chosen as president of the Montgomery Improvement Association leading the bus boycott. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p441]
- After receiving death threats and being arrested, he experienced a crisis one night and heard a voice telling him to stand up for righteousness. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p443]
- His Birmingham campaign in 1963 used children in demonstrations, provoking Bull Connor's violent response that was broadcast nationally, leading to the Civil Rights Act of 1964. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p446]
- On April 4, 1968, he was assassinated by a rifle shot while on the balcony of his motel room in Memphis, Tennessee. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p449]
