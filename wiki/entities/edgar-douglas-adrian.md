# Edgar Douglas Adrian

## Summary

British physiologist (1889–1977) who developed methods for recording action potentials from individual sensory neurons, discovering that nerve signals are all-or-none and that stimulus intensity is encoded by frequency of firing.

## Key facts

- Adrian developed methods to record action potentials from single sensory neurons on the skin, making the elementary utterances of nerve cells intelligible for the first time. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p89]
- He discovered that all action potentials generated by a single nerve cell are essentially the same shape and amplitude—the all-or-none characteristic of nerve signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p90]
- Adrian found that stimulus intensity is encoded by the frequency of action potentials—a gentle touch produces 2-3 per second while a strong pinch produces up to 100 per second. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p91]
- He shared the 1932 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Charles Sherrington for their discoveries regarding the function of neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p92]
