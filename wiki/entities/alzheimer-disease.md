# Alzheimer Disease

## Summary

Alzheimer disease is the most common cause of dementia, affecting approximately one-eighth of people over 65 years. The disease involves progressive memory loss and cognitive decline due to brain atrophy, extracellular amyloid plaques composed of amyloid-beta peptides, and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein. Genetic factors include mutations in APP, PSEN1, and PSEN2 for familial cases, and APOE variants for late-onset cases.

## Key facts

- Alzheimer disease affects approximately one-eighth of people older than 65 years, with prevalence increasing from about 2% at age 70 to more than 20% after age 80. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1568]
- The three main brain abnormalities in AD are atrophy from neuronal and synaptic loss, extracellular amyloid plaques, and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1569]
- Amyloid plaques are composed of aggregates of amyloid-beta (Aβ) peptides, particularly Aβ42, which are cleaved from amyloid precursor protein (APP) by beta and gamma secretases. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1570]
- Neurofibrillary tangles are composed of hyperphosphorylated tau protein, a microtubule-associated protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1569]
- Early-onset familial AD is caused by mutations in APP, PSEN1, or PSEN2 genes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1567]
- The epsilon 2 allele of the apolipoprotein E (APOE) gene is protective against AD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1562]
- The entorhinal cortex, hippocampus, parts of the neocortex, and the nucleus basalis are especially vulnerable in AD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1569]
- Aβ peptides are generated from amyloid precursor protein (APP) by sequential cleavage by β-secretase (BACE1) and γ-secretase. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- BACE1 is a transmembrane protein concentrated in central neuron synapses and is the neuronal β-secretase; brain cells from BACE1 knockout mice do not produce Aβ peptides. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- γ-Secretase is a multiprotein complex including presenilin-1, presenilin-2, nicastrin, Aph-1, and Pen-2 that cleaves APP within the membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- All individuals with Down syndrome (trisomy 21) who live to middle age develop AD pathology and dementia, consistent with 50% overproduction of APP and Aβ. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Duplication of just the APP locus in families causes AD and cerebral amyloid angiopathy without Down syndrome, providing strong evidence that APP overexpression alone is sufficient to cause AD. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- The first three genes identified for autosomal dominant early-onset AD encode APP, presenilin-1 (chromosome 14), and presenilin-2 (chromosome 1). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Swedish mutations at the β-secretase cleavage site increase Aβ secretion several-fold, while another APP mutation near this site protects against AD by decreasing Aβ production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Presenilin mutations generate higher proportions of the more aggregation-prone Aβ42 species relative to Aβ40. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- APP mutations can occur within the Aβ sequence itself, affecting aggregation and clearance without altering Aβ production. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Transgenic expression of mutant APP in mice produces amyloid plaques, dystrophic neurites, decreased synaptic terminal density, and impairments in spatial and episodic-like memory. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1620]
- Neurofibrillary tangles contain aggregates of hyperphosphorylated tau, a microtubule-binding protein normally soluble that is key to intracellular transport. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Tau aggregation occurs in neurofibrillary tangles in various neurodegenerative disorders including AD, frontotemporal dementia, progressive supranuclear palsy, corticobasal degeneration, and CTE. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Mutations in the tau gene cause frontotemporal dementia with Parkinsonism-17 (FTPD17) in the absence of Aβ deposition, demonstrating tau aggregation alone can cause neurodegeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Progressive symptoms of AD correlate better with the number and distribution of tangles than with amyloid plaques, and tangles appear first in entorhinal cortex and hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Aβ accumulation precedes neocortical tau pathology by approximately 15 years and appears to drive tau aggregation and spreading in the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Mice expressing both mutant APP and mutant tau develop much worse tau pathology than mice with either mutation alone. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Aβ42 injection into specific brain regions of transgenic mice expressing mutant tau increases the number of tangles in nearby neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- Aβ and tau aggregates can spread from cell to cell in a prion-like manner, possibly transsynaptically, which may be interruptible with antibodies. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1621]
- AD prevalence is vanishingly small below age 60, 1-3% between 60-70, 3-12% between 70-80, and 25-40% in those older than 85. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- APOE4 is present in about 25% of the general population but 60% of those with AD; one copy increases risk 3.7-fold and two copies 12-fold relative to APOE3/APOE3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- APOE2 decreases AD risk by approximately 40% relative to APOE3/APOE3. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- ApoE4 promotes Aβ aggregation by diminishing Aβ clearance and promoting fibrillization (ApoE4 > ApoE3 > ApoE2). [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- Rare TREM2 mutations double or triple AD risk, similar to one copy of APOE4, suggesting involvement of the innate immune system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1622]
- MRI can predict with approximately 80% accuracy which patients with MCI will develop AD based on cortical thinning and ventricular enlargement. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1623]
- Amyloid plaques can be visualized in living brains by PET using Pittsburgh compound B (PIB) or FDA-approved agents florbetapir, flutemetamol, and florbetaben. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1623]
- CSF biomarkers show decreased Aβ42 and increased total and phosphorylated tau with AD pathology and neurodegeneration. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1623]
- FDA-approved symptomatic treatments for AD include acetylcholinesterase inhibitors and the NMDA receptor antagonist memantine, both providing only modest benefit. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1624]
- Active and passive immunization against Aβ reduces Aβ levels, Aβ toxicity, and plaques in animal models; antibodies may stimulate microglial phagocytosis or decrease soluble Aβ toxicity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1625]
- Anti-tau antibodies and small molecules targeting tau aggregation are in clinical trials based on animal model evidence that they can decrease aggregated tau and improve function. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1626]
- Lifestyle factors that may decrease AD risk include high levels of education, cognitive stimulation, social engagement, regular exercise, maintaining healthy weight, and appropriate sleep. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1624]

## Inferences

- Inference: The evidence that APP duplication alone causes AD, that APP mutations cause early-onset AD through increased Aβ production or altered Aβ species ratios, and that APOE4 promotes Aβ aggregation collectively supports the amyloid hypothesis that Aβ accumulation is the primary initiating event in AD pathogenesis.
- Inference: The correlation between tangles and cognitive decline being stronger than plaques and cognition, combined with evidence that Aβ drives tau spreading, suggests a two-stage model where Aβ initiates disease and tau propagation drives neurodegeneration.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The mechanism by which APOE4 increases AD risk beyond promoting Aβ aggregation is uncertain, though possible roles in tau pathology, immune response, cholesterol metabolism, or synaptic plasticity have been proposed.
- Uncertain: The exact details of how Aβ deposition promotes transsynaptic spreading of tau aggregates remain to be worked out.
