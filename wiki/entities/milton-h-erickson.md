# Milton H. Erickson

## Summary

Milton H. Erickson (1901-1980) was an American psychiatrist and hypnotherapist who developed influential techniques in hypnosis and brief therapy. The book references him extensively as the developer of the Confusion Technique, paradoxical interventions, and the principle of utilizing the patient's own conceptual framework ('language') in therapy. Erickson's innovative approaches to inducing trance and resolving impasses significantly influenced the Brief Therapy Center's methodology.

## Key facts

- Erickson developed the Confusion Technique after an incident where bumping into someone and stating the wrong time (when it was clearly much later) created bewildering disorientation that prepared the subject for hypnotic induction. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L99-102]
- Erickson's rule 'Take what the patient is bringing you' emphasizes learning the patient's 'language' rather than imposing a therapeutic system or requiring the patient to learn new terminology. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L102-104]
- Erickson resolved a marital driving-argument impasse by suggesting one spouse drive within one block of home, then the other take over for the remainder—saving face while resolving the conflict. [raw/books/strategy/Change _ principles of problem formulation and problem - Paul Watzlawick, John H_ Weakland, Richard Fisch; foreword.epub#L101-103]
