# McDonald's

## Summary

McDonald's is presented as the exemplar of the Franchise Prototype model. Founded by Ray Kroc after observing the MacDonald brothers' hamburger stand in San Bernardino, California in 1952, it has become a $40-billion-a-year business with over 28,000 restaurants worldwide serving 43 million people daily. McDonald's is described as 'The Most Successful Small Business in the World' because it has applied the principles of the Franchise Prototype to achieve consistent, predictable results.

## Key facts

- Ray Kroc first visited the MacDonald brothers' hamburger stand in San Bernardino, California in 1952 and was amazed at how it worked like a Swiss watch. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p86]
- McDonald's has approximately 28,707 restaurants worldwide in 120 countries serving more than 43 million people every day. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p87]
- The average McDonald's restaurant produces more than $2 million in annual sales with an average 17 percent pretax net profit. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p87]
- Ray Kroc re-engineered McDonald's decades before reengineering became fashionable, treating it like Henry Ford treated the Model T. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p91]
- McDonald's created Hamburger U (University of Hamburgerology) where franchisees learned to run the system, not just make hamburgers. [raw/books/business/The E - myth revisited _ why most small businesses don't work - Michael E_ Gerber.pdf#p99]
