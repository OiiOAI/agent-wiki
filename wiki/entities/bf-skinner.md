# B.F. Skinner

## Summary

Psychology professor whose early experiments with rats and pigeons using rewards were ingenious and important. He demonstrated that prompt rewards worked better than delayed rewards and that random distribution maintained conditioned behavior longest. However, he later lost personal reputation by overclaiming incentive superpower and displaying little recognition of the rest of psychology, exemplifying the 'man-with-a-hammer' tendency.

## Key facts

- Skinner demonstrated 'repeat behavior that works' as a recurring generalized behavioral algorithm in nature. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p477]
- Skinner caused pigeons to develop strong superstitions through food rewards and demonstrated conditioning that improved autistic children. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p477]
- Munger criticizes Skinner for losing reputation by overclaiming free incentive superpower to the point of thinking he could create a human utopia with it. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p478]
- Skinner was right in his main idea: 'Incentives are superpowers,' but wrong in trying to explain everything with incentive effects. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p478]
