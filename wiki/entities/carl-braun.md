# Carl Braun

## Summary

Carl Braun was an engineer who designed oil refineries with spectacular skill and integrity. He developed a communication rule requiring that all instructions specify Who, What, Where, When, and Why. Munger cites Braun's practices as an example of psychologically sophisticated business communication.

## Key facts

- Carl Braun designed oil refineries with spectacular skill and integrity. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p595]
- Braun required that all communications specify Who was to do What, Where, When, and Why. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p595]
- If a communication omitted the explanation of why, Braun was likely to fire the sender because he knew ideas got through best when reasons were meticulously laid out. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p595]
- Braun's company is described as 'large' and 'Teutonic.'. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie's Almanack_ The Wit and Wisdo - Charles T. Munger, Peter D. Kaufman, Ed We.pdf#p595]
- Carl Braun created C.E. Braun Engineering Company which designed and built oil refineries. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p168]
- Braun looked at standard accounting and said, 'This is asinine,' then fired all his accountants and had engineers devise their own accounting system. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p168]
- Braun's communication rule required telling who, what, where, when, and why—employees could be fired for not including why twice. [raw/books/strategy/Poor Charlie’s Almanack_ Charles T_ Munger, Peter D_ Kaufman, Ed Wexler, Warren E_pdf.pdf#p168]

## Inferences

- Inference: Braun's requirement of 'why' in all communications reflects the psychological principle that explaining reasons increases understanding and compliance—Munger later cited this as part of worldly wisdom.
