# Andrew Packard

## Summary

A biologist who published a famous paper in 1972 arguing that the evolution of modern cephalopods occurred in parallel with the evolution of certain kinds of fish, with both groups evolving in response to competition.

## Key facts

- In a famous paper from 1972, Andrew Packard argued that the evolution of modern cephalopods occurred in parallel with the evolution of certain kinds of fish. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
- Packard proposed that from about 170 million years ago, some fish evolved new forms that competed with cephalopods, and cephalopods evolved in response, including developing complex behavior. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
- The hypothesis of 'accidental intelligence' in cephalopods was encouraged by Packard's view that there was a single process: the evolution of large brains by cephalopods with minor variations afterward. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
- Packard based his view on fossil evidence, which is always sketchy with soft-bodied animals, and later genetic evidence changed the picture. [raw/books/neuroscience/Other Minds_ The Octopus, the Sea, and the - Peter Godfrey - Smith.pdf#p154]
