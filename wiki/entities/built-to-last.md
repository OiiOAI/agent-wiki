# Built to Last

## Summary

A bestselling management book by Jim Collins and Jerry Porras that analyzed 18 pairs of companies to identify practices that distinguished visionary companies from their competitors. The book became a canonical text in business education despite research showing that the differences between successful and less successful companies are largely explained by luck rather than management practices.

## Key facts

- Built to Last analyzed 18 pairs of competing companies, with one being significantly more successful than the other, and claimed to identify consistent patterns of management practices associated with success. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p267]
- Kahneman critiques the book for failing to account for regression to the mean: the gap between successful and less successful companies shrinks over time because initial differences include substantial luck, which is not stable. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p268]
- Research by Rosenzweig showed that business success books continuously overstate the impact of leadership and management practices on outcomes, making their lessons rarely useful. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p267]
- The correlation between management quality and company success is estimated at approximately 0.30, meaning a better CEO leads a more successful company in only 60% of comparable pairs—a 10% improvement over random chance. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The popularity of books like Built to Last despite their limited predictive validity reflects the narrative fallacy and halo effect: coherent success stories appeal to readers' need for causal understanding while underweighting the role of luck.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The actual correlation of 0.30 between CEO quality and company success is a rough estimate, as precise measurement of management quality is difficult.
