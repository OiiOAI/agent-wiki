# Frederick Lincoln

## Summary

Frederick Lincoln had a massive stroke when he was in his mother's womb. Diagnosed with cerebral palsy at birth with paralysis on his left side, he was told he would probably never crawl, walk, or talk. At age four, he underwent CI therapy at the Taub Clinic for 21 days. Within 19 days, he developed a pincer grasp for the first time. He made remarkable progress, learning to run, play sports including baseball, and making the regular all-star baseball team. His case demonstrates that CI therapy can work for children with congenital brain damage.

## Key facts

- Frederick had a massive stroke when he was in his mother's womb; an MRI showed one-quarter of his brain was dead. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p164]
- He was diagnosed with cerebral palsy with paralysis on the left side of his body and couldn't crawl, walk, or talk until he was three. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p165]
- At age four, after conventional therapy had plateaued, he went to Taub's clinic where he wore a cast on his good arm. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p165]
- A mere nineteen days into therapy, Frederick developed a pincer grasp for the first time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p165]
- He can now run, play volleyball and baseball, and made the regular all-star baseball team (not one for handicapped children). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p166]
- At age 5-6, he played in the five-to-six-year-old division baseball and was chosen for the all-star team, with the winning RBI in three of five games. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p166]
