# Daniel M'Naghten

## Summary

A Scottish woodturner who attempted to assassinate British Prime Minister Robert Peel in 1843, mistakenly killing his secretary Edward Drummond. M'Naghten's paranoid psychosis and clear insanity led to his acquittal, prompting Parliament to formalize the legal standard for insanity that bears his name, requiring that defendants cannot distinguish right from wrong due to 'disease of the mind.'

## Key facts

- M'Naghten spent two years before the assassination wandering Europe, hearing voices, and convinced he was being spied upon and persecuted by powerful people including the Prime Minister. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p602]
- The M'Naghten rule established that a person is not criminally responsible if, at the time of the crime, they could not distinguish right from wrong due to disease of the mind. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p603]
- M'Naghten spent the rest of his life in insane asylums, reasonably well treated by the standards of the time. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p603]
