# Charles Sanders Peirce

## Summary

Charles Sanders Peirce (1839-1914) was an American philosopher and scientist who developed a comprehensive theory of signs (semiotics). His work on the trichotomy of icon, index, and symbol forms the foundation for Deacon's analysis of reference. Peirce argued that all thought is essentially semiotic, with different modes of reference (iconic, indexical, symbolic) forming a hierarchical structure where more complex forms build upon simpler ones.

## Key facts

- Peirce distinguished three categories of referential associations: icon (based on similarity), index (based on physical/temporal connection), and symbol (based on convention). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p73]
- Peirce's insight that interpretants can be of categorically different kinds and that reference itself is hierarchical in structure underlies Deacon's analysis. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p65]
