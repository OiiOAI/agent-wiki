# Johann Gottfried Herder

## Summary

German philosopher (1744-1803) who published one of the most important essays on the origins of language in 1772. Herder noticed that the 'I-thou' element in communication at the most intuitive level - the empathic force present in music - is hardly characteristic of human language, and concluded that language appears as a natural organ of reason. McGilchrist quotes Herder's observation that language can help blind us to the intrinsically synaesthetic nature of experience.

## Key facts

- Herder published one of the most important and influential essays on the origins of language in 1772. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p167]
- Herder noticed that the empathic force present in music is hardly characteristic of human language. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p167]
- Herder concluded that 'language appears as a natural organ of reason'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p167]
- Herder suggested that some of the intrinsically synaesthetic nature of experience must be caught in word-sounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p174]

## Inferences

- Inference: Herder anticipated McGilchrist's view that language is primarily an aid to a particular type of cognition rather than merely a communication tool.
