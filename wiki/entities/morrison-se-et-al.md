# Morrison SE, Saez A, Lau B, Salzman CD (2011)

## Summary

Morrison et al. (2011) conducted neurophysiological recordings in monkeys examining the time course of learning-related changes in amygdala and orbitofrontal cortex during reversal learning. They found that appetitive and aversive networks in these structures exhibit different learning rates depending on valence preference.

## Key facts

- For positive cells, changes in OFC neural activity after reversal were largely complete many trials earlier than in positive cells in the amygdala. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p42]
- For negative cells, the aversive network in the amygdala learned more rapidly than the aversive network in the OFC. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p42]
- Analysis of local field potentials suggested amygdala-to-OFC directional flow was stronger earlier during the learning process. [raw/books/neuroscience/Decision Neuroscience _ An Integrative Perspective - Jean - Claude Dreher; Léon Tremblay; Institut de sciences.pdf#p43]
