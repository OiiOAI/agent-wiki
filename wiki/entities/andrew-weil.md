# Andrew Weil, MD

## Summary

Andrew Weil, MD, is a physician and author who wrote the foreword to The Better Brain. He is a New York Times best-selling author and director of the University of Arizona Center for Integrative Medicine. He received only 30 minutes of nutrition instruction during his Harvard Medical School training (1964–68), and he argues this deficiency persists in medical education. He describes integrative medicine as making dietary modification the first item in treatment plans, often sufficient alone, and supports the use of micronutrients for mental health.

## Key facts

- Weil received only 30 minutes of nutrition instruction during his Harvard Medical School training from 1964 to 1968. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p9]
- He describes how adopting an anti-inflammatory diet and supplementing with omega-3 fatty acids for autoimmunity cases may reduce the need for immunosuppressive drugs. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p10]
- He discusses the cytokine hypothesis of depression, which posits that upregulation of inflammatory cytokines correlates with depressed mood, fatigue, decreased appetite, and social withdrawal. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Better Brain - Bonnie J. Kaplan.pdf#p11]
- He describes the growing understanding of the gut microbiome's influence on the brain, noting that gut organisms produce neurotransmitters that travel to the brain via the vagus nerve, and that microbiome composition is linked to autism, ADHD, mood disorders, and Parkinson's disease.
