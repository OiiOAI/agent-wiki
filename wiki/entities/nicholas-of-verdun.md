# Nicholas of Verdun

## Summary

A Lorraine artist of the twelfth century whose bronze Moses statuette in the Ashmolean Museum at Oxford Freud cited as supporting his interpretation of Michelangelo's Moses. Nicholas of Verdun's work shows Moses making the gesture Freud hypothesized as a preliminary stage of Michelangelo's version.

## Key facts

- Nicholas of Verdun's Moses statuette, just over 23cm high, holds the Tables of the Law in his left hand, resting them on his knee, and grasps his beard pressing strands between palm and thumb. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4344]
- Freud notes that the Moses of Nicholas of Verdun (c. 1180) shows an instant during his storm of feeling, while Michelangelo's statue in San Pietro in Vincoli depicts the calm when the storm is over. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4344]
- Nicholas of Verdun's masterpiece is considered to be the Shrine of the Three Kings in Cologne. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p4344]
