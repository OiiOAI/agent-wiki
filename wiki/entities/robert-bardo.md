# Robert Bardo

## Summary

Robert Bardo is an American convicted of murdering actress Rebecca Schaeffer in 1989. Bardo's case led to significant changes in California law regarding stalking and became a landmark case in threat assessment. He was interviewed by Gavin de Becker during his imprisonment.

## Key facts

- Robert Bardo murdered Rebecca Schaeffer on July 18, 1989, outside her apartment building. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p159]
- Schaeffer had sent Bardo a kind postcard calling his letter 'the nicest, most real letter I ever received,' which Bardo said propelled him to seek more contact with her. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p161]
- Bardo advised: 'Be careful about what you write. If you do answer fan mail, don't let it be so over-glowing. That's not the way to be with a fan, because it makes it seem like they're the only one, and that's how I felt.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p161]
- Bardo had correspondence with Mark Chapman after Chapman killed John Lennon, asking why he had done it. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p160]
- Bardo studied other assassins including Arthur Jackson and brought similar books (including Catcher in the Rye) when he killed Schaeffer. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p160]
- Bardo stated: 'I'm not a monster. On television they always want to portray me as someone frightening.' He was concerned about his public image compared to other assassins. [raw/books/psychology/The Gift of Fear - Gavin de Becker.pdf#p160]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bardo's statement that he felt 'I was the only one' after receiving what he perceived as special attention from a famous person suggests that seemingly positive fan mail responses can inadvertently encourage obsessive behavior in mentally unstable individuals.
