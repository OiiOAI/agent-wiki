# Hoover

## Summary

Hoover was a harbor seal at the Boston Aquarium who learned to produce human speech-like vocalizations. He was discovered as an orphaned pup by a fisherman in Maine and named for the vacuum cleaner due to his voracious appetite. He began talking in human-like phrases around puberty, producing sounds like 'Hey!', 'Get outa there!', and 'Hoova!' His speech was slurred but recognizable, and was produced by leaning his head back and slightly moving his tongue and lips. He died during his annual fall molt, and autopsy revealed unusual degree of calcification in his cranium, probably associated with early encephalitis.

## Key facts

- Hoover was discovered as an orphaned and sickly pup by a fisherman in Maine, who nursed him back to health and named him for the vacuum cleaner because of his voracious appetite. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p243]
- Hoover did not talk from infancy until many years later, approaching puberty, with vocalizations developing from odd but unclear sounds into reasonably clear phrases. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p244]
- He produced phrases including 'Hoova!', 'Hey!', 'Hey hey hey hey!', 'Hey you!', 'Get outa there!' and a gurgling sound like 'Yadda yadda yadda'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p244]
- His speech was produced by leaning his head back, propping his mouth open, with only slight movement of tongue and lips, using fewer muscles than normally required for similar sounds. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p245]
- Autopsy revealed Hoover had died of an infection and showed unusual degree of calcification in his cranium, probably associated with early encephalitis or other brain damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p247]
- Efforts to train other young seals to mimic speech have not produced any new talking seals, suggesting Hoover was unique. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p245]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hoover's speech may have resulted from perinatal damage to limbic-midbrain projections, allowing usually transient cortical projections to vocal control nuclei to persist into adulthood.
- Inference: The aquatic nature of seals may be significant because aquatic mammals need direct cortical control over breathing from an early age, potentially providing a prerequisite for speech-like abilities.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: No further analysis of Hoover's brain has been done, so the exact neurological basis for his speech remains unknown.
- Uncertain: Whether Hoover's speech was taught by humans or spontaneously developed cannot be confirmed.
