# Brenda Milner

## Summary

Brenda Milner is a neuropsychologist who conducted pioneering studies on patient HM, demonstrating that motor skill learning could occur independently of explicit memory. Her work established the functional and anatomical separation of implicit and explicit memory systems.

## Key facts

- Milner's studies beginning in the 1950s with amnesic patients (most famously HM) showed that motor skills could be learned and retained without conscious recollection. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- Milner coined the term 'perceptual learning' (later called priming) based on her findings with amnesic patients identifying fragmented drawings. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p191]
- Her work demonstrated that implicit and explicit memory are functionally separate and separable from one another in more than a semantical sense. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
