# Brenda Milner

## Summary

Brenda Milner is a neuropsychologist who conducted pioneering studies on patient HM, demonstrating that motor skill learning could occur independently of explicit memory. Her work established the functional and anatomical separation of implicit and explicit memory systems.

## Key facts

- Milner's studies beginning in the 1950s with amnesic patients (most famously HM) showed that motor skills could be learned and retained without conscious recollection. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- Milner coined the term 'perceptual learning' (later called priming) based on her findings with amnesic patients identifying fragmented drawings. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p191]
- Her work demonstrated that implicit and explicit memory are functionally separate and separable from one another in more than a semantical sense. [raw/books/neuroscience/I of the Vortex _ From Neurons to Self - Rodolfo Riascos Llinás.pdf#p190]
- Milner and William Scoville reported in 1957 that memory could be traced to specific regions of the brain, particularly the medial temporal lobe and hippocampus. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p129]
- Milner found that H.M. retained perfectly good short-term memory, long-term memory for events before his surgery, but could not convert new short-term memories into permanent long-term memories. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p140]
- Milner showed that H.M. could learn to trace a star in a mirror and improve over days, despite having no conscious memory of performing the task—this demonstrated implicit memory storage. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p142]
- Milner identified the roles of the hippocampus and medial temporal lobe in explicit memory and provided the first evidence of implicit memory storage. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p143]

## Inferences

- Inference: Milner's work disproved Lashley's theory of mass action, showing that memory is not distributed equally across the cortex but localized to specific structures.
