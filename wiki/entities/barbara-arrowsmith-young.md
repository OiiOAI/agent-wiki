# Barbara Arrowsmith Young

## Summary

Barbara Arrowsmith Young is a Canadian educator and the founder of the Arrowsmith School in Toronto. Born in 1951 in Toronto and raised in Peterborough, Ontario, she overcame severe multiple learning disabilities through self-designed brain exercises, demonstrating neuroplasticity before the concept was widely accepted.

## Key facts

- Barbara Arrowsmith Young was born in Toronto in 1951 and raised in Peterborough, Ontario. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p37]
- Her auditory and visual memory tested in the ninety-ninth percentile, while her frontal lobes were remarkably developed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p37]
- Her brain was 'asymmetrical,' with exceptional abilities coexisting with areas of retardation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p37]
- She had a confusing assortment of serious learning disabilities including problems with Broca's area (speech), spatial reasoning, kinesthetic perception, and understanding symbol relationships. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p37-p38]
- She reversed b, d, q, and p, read 'was' as 'saw,' and read and wrote from right to left (mirror writing). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p39]
- She lacked the capacity for spatial reasoning, causing her to misjudge space from a young age, once running into a car while playing matador at age three. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p37-p38]
- She couldn't distinguish between 'the father's brother' and 'the brother's father,' couldn't understand double negatives, and couldn't read a clock. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p38-p39]
- She understood nothing in real time, only after the fact in lag time, and had to replay simple conversations twenty times over. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p39-p40]
- Her grade-one teacher told her parents she had 'a mental block' and wouldn't learn the way others did; the term 'learning disabilities' wouldn't be widely used for another two decades. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p40]
- Her father Jack was an electrical engineer and inventor with thirty-four patents for Canadian General Electric. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- She studied child development as an undergraduate at the University of Guelph and was accepted into graduate school at the Ontario Institute for Studies in Education (OISE). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- At OISE, Joshua Cohen first understood her disabilities and designed a compensation program for her. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p41]
- She read Aleksandr Luria's books about patients with strokes and wounds who had trouble with grammar, logic, and reading clocks. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- Reading Lyova Zazetsky's diary, she thought 'He is describing my life'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p43]
- At age 28, she read Mark Rosenzweig's paper showing neuroplasticity in rats, which inspired her to believe compensation might not be the only answer. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- She designed mental exercises to strengthen her most weakened function — relating symbols to each other — including reading hundreds of clock-face cards. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- After weeks of exhausting exercises, she could read clocks faster than normal people and began for the first time to grasp grammar, math, and logic, and could understand what people were saying in real time. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p45]
- She married Joshua Cohen and in 1980 they opened the Arrowsmith School in Toronto. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- She designed exercises for various brain dysfunctions including difficulties with space, knowing where limbs were, visual disabilities, weak auditory memories, social clumsiness, and frontal lobe deficits. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p48-p49]
- Today Barbara Arrowsmith Young is sharp and funny, with no noticeable bottlenecks in her mental processes. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p53]
