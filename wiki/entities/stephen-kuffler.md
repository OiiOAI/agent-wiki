# Stephen Kuffler

## Summary

Stephen Kuffler (1918–1980) was a Hungarian-born, Viennese-trained physician-turned-physiologist who left Vienna in 1938 because he was both Jewish and a socialist. He joined John Eccles's laboratory in Sydney, Australia, during World War II alongside Bernard Katz, where his exceptional surgical skills enabled him to dissect individual muscle fibers to study synaptic input. Later immigrating to the United States, Kuffler made fundamental discoveries about dendrite properties in crayfish neurons and greatly influenced Eric Kandel's thinking about choosing simple experimental systems.

## Key facts

- Kuffler left Vienna in 1938 because he was both Jewish and a socialist. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p108]
- Kuffler amazed Eccles and Katz with his surgical skills, particularly his ability to dissect out individual muscle fibers to study the synaptic input from one motor axon to one muscle fiber. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p108]
- Kuffler's studies on crayfish dendrites showed that these structures have electrical properties and can generate signals, contradicting the prevailing view. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p120]
