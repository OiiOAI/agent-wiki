# U.S. Army Special Operations

## Summary

U.S. Army Special Operations is the elite military force that partnered with Professor Angus Fletcher to develop and test Primal Intelligence training. In the early 2000s, they observed that young recruits had high IQ scores but performed poorly at real-world decision-making, strategic planning, and leadership—they 'could solve math problems but not life problems.' Special Operations has a pipeline for producing individuals with 'big imaginations but no patience for bullshit,' zero tolerance for 'magic happy' (nice ideas that break on contact with reality). Their training methods, developed at sites like Pineland and Cape Fear, have been translated to military, corporate, academic, and elementary school settings.

## Key facts

- Young Special Operations recruits in the early 2000s underperformed at decision-making, strategic planning, and leadership despite high—even profoundly gifted—IQ scores. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p11]
- The recruit issue was deteriorating: recruits did worse in 2020 than in 2010 than in 2000, prone to violent anger, dysfunctional relationships, and pill addiction. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p11]
- Special Operations has a pipeline that produced handheld GPS, quick-clotting medical gauze, and future gear too hush-hush for public knowledge. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p9]
- The training produced Operators who 'saw the future faster, healed quicker from trauma, and chose wiser' in life-and-death situations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p10]
- Pineland is a 15,000-square-mile Special Forces training site in the Carolinas with a fictional foreign army that has invaded America, stocked with thousands of actors performing unconventional backstories. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p35]
