# Regulation 18B

## Summary

Regulation 18B was a Defense Regulation introduced in Britain during World War II that allowed for the detention without trial of individuals suspected of being a threat to national safety or defense. Originally intended for IRA suspects, it was later used to intern hundreds of British fascists, Nazi sympathizers, and others deemed security risks. Captain Archibald Ramsay was detained under this regulation for nearly four years without being charged.

## Key facts

- Regulation 18B was introduced ostensibly to deal with IRA members committing minor outrages in London. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p432]
- The regulation enabled arrest and imprisonment on suspicion without the suspension of Habeas Corpus. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p432]
- Hundreds of British subjects, many of them ex-servicemen, were arrested under this regulation in May 1940. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p431]
- Ramsay was detained under Regulation 18B for four years and four months without any charge being preferred against him. [raw/books/psychology/Willpower _ Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength - Roy F_ Baumeister, John Tierney.pdf#p434]
