# Robert Axelrod

## Summary

Robert Axelrod is an American political scientist and professor at the University of Michigan who conducted groundbreaking computer tournaments on the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma. His work demonstrated that 'nice' strategies—those that never defect first—tend to be most successful. He collaborated with W.D. Hamilton on biological applications of game theory.

## Key facts

- Axelrod ran computer tournaments where strategies were matched against each other in the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma game. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p248]
- Axelrod coined the technical meaning of 'nice' to describe strategies that never defect first. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p240]
- Axelrod collaborated with W.D. Hamilton on a joint paper published in Science in 1981 that won the Newcomb Cleveland Prize. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p253]
- Axelrod introduced the concept of 'collectively stable strategy' for strategies like Tit for Tat that are not strictly ESS but behave similarly. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p256]
- Axelrod organized a round-robin tournament where participants submitted Prisoner's Dilemma strategies, and Anatol Rapoport's simple Tit for Tat won. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p360]
- Axelrod and Hamilton's 1981 paper became a buzz phrase in evolutionary biology for bridging game theory and behavioral evolution. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p361]
