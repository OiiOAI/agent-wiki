# Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation

## Summary

Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS) is a technique that uses a paddle-shaped device with a coil of copper wire to generate a changing magnetic field that penetrates the brain and induces electric currents in neurons, causing them to fire. Invented and pioneered by Alvaro Pascual-Leone, TMS can either stimulate specific brain areas or block their function depending on intensity and frequency. High-frequency repetitive TMS (rTMS) can activate neurons so strongly they continue firing after stimulation stops, making it useful for therapeutic applications like treating depression.

## Key facts

- TMS uses a changing magnetic field that passes painlessly through the body, inducing an electric current only when it reaches neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p203]
- TMS can stimulate an area of about 0.5 cubic centimeter consisting of millions of cells in the brain map for a specific body part. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p203]
- Pascual-Leone was the first to use TMS to map the brain, determining function by firing bursts to temporarily block areas and observing which mental function was lost. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p204]
- High-frequency repetitive TMS (rTMS) can activate neurons so much they excite each other and keep firing even after the original burst has stopped, turning a brain area on therapeutically. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p204]
- rTMS was shown effective in treating severely depressed patients, with 70% who failed traditional treatments improving with fewer side effects than medication. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p204]

## Inferences

- Inference: The ability of TMS to create 'virtual lesions' by temporarily blocking brain areas suggests it could be a valuable diagnostic tool for mapping brain functions without surgery.
