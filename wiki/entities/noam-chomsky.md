# Noam Chomsky

## Summary

Noam Chomsky is a linguist whose theory of Universal Grammar and the language organ proposed that humans possess innate, species-specific knowledge of grammatical structure. His work has been central to debates about language evolution and innateness. Deacon critically examines Chomsky's theory, arguing that while grammatical acquisition is remarkable, the focus on innate grammar misses the more fundamental problem of symbolic reference.

## Key facts

- Chomsky proposed that the complexity and systematic structure of grammar cannot be learned from input, requiring innate grammatical knowledge (Universal Grammar). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p35]
- Chomsky's theory suggests that all human brains contain a built-in 'language organ' providing a blueprint for language structure. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p35]
