# Carl von Clausewitz

## Summary

Carl von Clausewitz (1780-1830) was a Prussian army officer whose book "On War" became "the bible" of U.S. Special Operations. He read Shakespeare through German Romantic influences, developed innovative military methods at the Prussian War Academy based on teams eating each other's plans, and his student Helmuth von Moltke used these methods to win wars against Austria and France.

## Key facts

- Clausewitz was a Prussian army officer born in 1780. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p116]
- He read Shakespeare and the German Romantics Goethe and Humboldt, purchasing an English copy of Shakespeare's Dramatic Works printed in 1824. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p116]
- He was captured by Napoleon at the Battle of Jena-Auerstedt in 1806 and spent ten months as a captive in France. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p116]
- At the Prussian War Academy, he installed a curriculum encouraging staff officers to use each other's exceptional characteristics to drive innovation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p117]
- His method involved splitting into teams, sharing plans without ranking them, identifying what's exceptional in each other's plans, and placing innovations in tension to produce final plans. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p117]
- His student Helmuth von Moltke used these methods to win wars in 1866 against Austria and 1870 against France with "astonishing speed". [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p118]
- Special Operators say "Clausewitz is our enemy, so we ate him" to incorporate violently different thinking. [raw/books/neuroscience/Primal Intelligence_ You Are Smarter Than - Angus Fletcher.pdf#p119]
