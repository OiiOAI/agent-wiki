# Louis Sass

## Summary

Louis Sass is a psychologist who has written extensively on the parallels between the phenomenology of schizophrenia and the culture of modernism. His works include 'The Paradoxes of Delusion: Wittgenstein, Schreber, and the Schizophrenic Mind' and 'Madness and Modernism'. He identifies core disturbances including hyperconsciousness, loss of ipseity, and 'unworlding' as fundamental to both schizophrenia and the modern condition.

## Key facts

- Sass considers the parallels between the role of detached, introspective observation in philosophy and the reports of Daniel Paul Schreber. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p539]
- Sass explores the idea that 'madness is the end-point of the trajectory consciousness follows when it separates from the body and the passions, and from the social and practical world, and turns in upon itself'. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p539]
- In 'Madness and Modernism', Sass draws parallels between the reported experiences of schizophrenics and the world picture of modernism and post-modernism. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p540]
- Sass identifies hyperconsciousness, loss of ipseity and 'unworlding' as fundamental to the experience of subjects with schizophrenia. [raw/books/neuroscience/THE MASTER AND HIS EMISSARY - Iain McGilchrist.pdf#p542]
