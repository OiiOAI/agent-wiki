# NMDA channel

## Summary

The NMDA channel is a type of ion channel in neurons that is sensitive both to voltage and to glutamate. It rarely opens even in the presence of glutamate if the local membrane potential is near its resting value; if the potential becomes less negative, glutamate will open the channel. This property means it responds to an association between presynaptic activity and postsynaptic activity. When opened, it allows passage of sodium, potassium, and calcium ions, with the calcium ions apparently initiating complex chemical reactions that alter synapse strength for days, weeks, months, or longer— possibly forming the basis for one form of memory.

## Key facts

- The NMDA channel is sensitive both to voltage and to glutamate, responding only when there is an association between presynaptic activity and postsynaptic voltage change. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p116]
- When the NMDA glutamate channel opens, it allows passage of not only sodium and potassium ions but also an appreciable amount of calcium ions (Ca++). [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p116]
- The incoming calcium ions appear to be the signal that initiates complex chains of chemical reactions that alter the strength of the synapse, probably for days, weeks, months, or even longer. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p116]
- If NMDA channels are chemically inactivated in a rat's hippocampus, the rat cannot remember where it has been. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p116]

## Inferences

- Inference: The NMDA channel's associative property (requiring both presynaptic activity and postsynaptic depolarization) provides a neural mechanism for Hebbian learning— neurons that fire together wire together.
