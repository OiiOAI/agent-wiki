# Patricia Goldman-Rakic

## Summary

Patricia Goldman-Rakic was a neuroscientist at Yale University who pioneered research on the neural basis of working memory. Her experiments demonstrated that sustained firing of prefrontal cortex neurons maintains visual spatial information during delay periods, providing key evidence that short-term memory involves continued neuronal activity.

## Key facts

- Patricia Goldman-Rakic and her colleagues at Yale conducted experiments showing that sustained firing of neurons in the prefrontal cortex is associated with short-term memory for visual spatial location. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p257]
- Her monkey experiments demonstrated that when a target stimulus was presented and then switched off during a delay, neurons maintained their firing until the monkey made its response, and if firing was not maintained the monkey was highly likely to make a mistake.
