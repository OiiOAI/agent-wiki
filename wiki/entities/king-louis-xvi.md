# King Louis XVI

## Summary

King Louis XVI (1754-1793) reigned over France from 1774 until he was deposed in 1792 and executed in 1793. Greene presents him as the antithesis of Danton—a monarch trapped by generational myopia, unable to perceive the revolutionary tide gathering beneath surface stability. Shy and uncertain compared to his grandfather Louis XV, Louis clung to the divine right of monarchy and sought reassurance through coronation rituals. He consistently misinterpreted signs of discontent as the work of godless agitators rather than legitimate grievances, and attempted to use force to preserve traditions that had already lost their meaning. His failed escape attempt in 1791 sealed his fate, and he was guillotined as 'Louis Capet' in January 1793.

## Key facts

- Louis XVI was twenty years old when he became king in 1774, and he dreaded the possibility of ascending to the throne. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p631]
- Louis sought confidence through the coronation ritual's legend of holy oil from the Holy Spirit, believing it would elevate him above mere mortals. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p631]
- Louis decided to reenact the coronation in its more original form and surround himself with imagery associating him with Roman gods. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p632]
- Louis misread signs of change as the work of godless individuals and a noisy minority, believing force could restore respect for tradition. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p649]
- The king held the Estates General at Versailles against advisers' warnings, hoping the grandeur would intimidate delegates, but they remained unimpressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p637]
- Louis attempted to flee France in June 1791 with his family but was recognized and captured, destroying his credibility. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p642]
- Secret correspondence was discovered revealing Louis had conspired with foreign powers to overthrow the revolution, sealing his fate. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p643]
- Louis was tried as a traitor, stripped of his title, and guillotined on January 21, 1793, shouting 'People, I die innocent!'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p643]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene contrasts Louis XVI with Danton to illustrate the consequences of generational myopia. Louis saw only continuity with the past; Danton saw the rising tide. Greene suggests Louis was not simply weak but trapped by a worldview that made alternative interpretations impossible.
