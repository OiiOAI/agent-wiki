# Silver Spring Monkeys

## Summary

The Silver Spring monkeys were 17 macaque monkeys in Edward Taub's laboratory at the Behavioral Biology Center in Silver Spring, Maryland. In 1981, Alex Pacheco, a cofounder of PETA, secretly photographed the monkeys and alleged they were being mistreated. This led to a police raid, the first police raid on an animal research facility in U.S. history. Taub was charged with 119 counts of animal cruelty but was ultimately exonerated. The monkeys were controversially given to PETA member Lori Lehner, disappeared, and were later returned. The controversy delayed Taub's work for years but ultimately made PETA the largest animal rights organization in the U.S.

## Key facts

- In 1981, Alex Pacheco, a 22-year-old political science student who was secretly the cofounder and president of PETA, volunteered to work in Taub's lab. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p151]
- Pacheco took photographs that appeared to show monkeys suffering, injured and neglected, and that suggested they were forced to eat from pans dirtied by their own feces. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p151]
- The Maryland statute covering cruelty to animals could be interpreted as making no exception for medical research, unlike laws in other states. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p151]
- Taub was arrested and put on trial, charged with 119 counts of animal cruelty. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p152]
- Before his trials began, two-thirds of Congress voted for a Sense of Congress resolution to stop his funding. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p152]
- By the end of Taub's first trial, 113 of the 119 charges had been dismissed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p153]
- The Maryland Court of Appeals found that the state anticruelty law was never intended to apply to researchers, and Taub was exonerated in a unanimous decision. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p154]
- Police turned the monkeys over to Lori Lehner, a PETA member, and the entire colony subsequently disappeared mysteriously. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p153]
- The monkeys appeared to have been taken on a two-thousand-mile round trip to Florida and back, leaving them physically disturbed. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p170]

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Taub claimed the photos were staged and captions fabricated; Pacheco denied this—the truth of the allegations remains disputed.
