# Golgi Tendon Organ

## Summary

The Golgi tendon organ is a sensory receptor approximately 1 mm long and 0.1 mm in diameter located at the junction between skeletal muscle fibers and tendon. Each organ is innervated by a single Ib axon and signals the tension in a muscle, with its firing rate closely correlated to total muscle force.

## Key facts

- Golgi tendon organs are slender encapsulated structures approximately 1 mm long and 0.1 mm in diameter located at the junction between skeletal muscle fibers and tendon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p852]
- Each tendon organ is innervated by a single Ib axon that branches into many fine endings inside the capsule intertwined with collagen fascicles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p852]
- Stretching of the tendon organ straightens the collagen fibers, compressing the Ib nerve endings and causing them to fire. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p852]
- Studies show that the average level of activity in the population of tendon organs in a muscle is a good index of the total force in a contracting muscle. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p852]
- In passive animals stimulation of tendon organs produces disynaptic inhibition of homonymous motor neurons, but during locomotion the same Ib fibers produce an excitatory effect because transmission in the inhibitory pathway is depressed.
