# Charles Scott Sherrington

## Summary

British physiologist (1857–1952) who studied the neural basis of reflex behavior, discovering that neurons can be inhibited as well as excited. He coined the term 'synapse' and built on Cajal's anatomical findings to link neural structure to physiology and behavior.

## Key facts

- Sherrington discovered that some nerve cells are inhibitory—they use their terminals to stop receiving cells from relaying information. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p83]
- He introduced the concept of reciprocal control, whereby inhibitory neurons select among competing reflexes to ensure a stable, coordinated response. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p84]
- Sherrington invited Cajal to give the Croonian Lecture to the Royal Society in London in 1894. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p82]
- He shared the 1932 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Edgar Adrian for their discoveries regarding the function of neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p92]
