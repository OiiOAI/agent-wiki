# Humberto Maturana

## Summary

Humberto Maturana (1928-2021) was a Chilean biologist who developed the concept of autopoiesis—the ability of systems to maintain and reproduce themselves. He collaborated with Francisco Varela on 'Autopoiesis and Cognition' (1980). Anil Seth met Maturana in January 2019 in Santiago, Chile, where they discussed these ideas in a cafe garden.

## Key facts

- Humberto Maturana was a Chilean biologist who died in May 2021 at the age of ninety-two. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p250]
- He developed the concept of autopoiesis with Francisco Varela. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p250]
- Anil Seth met Maturana in January 2019 in Santiago, Chile. [raw/books/neuroscience/Being You _ A New Science of Consciousness - Seth, Anil.pdf#p250]
- Maturana hypothesized that the nervous system operates as a closed network of interactions where every change results in changes of the same or other components, and that circular organization is the basic organization of all living systems. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p140]
- Maturana coined the term 'autopoietic system' to describe networks of production processes where each component participates in producing other components, such that the network continually 'makes itself.'. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p140]
- Maturana hypothesized that living systems are cognitive systems, and 'living as a process is a process of cognition,' fundamentally redefining the scope of cognition beyond thinking to include perception, emotion, and action. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p141]
- Maturana proposed that perception cannot be viewed as representation of external reality but must be understood as continual creation of new relationships within the neural network. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p141]
- Structural coupling refers to recurrent interactions with the environment each triggering structural changes in the system, but only those perturbations specified by the system itself trigger changes, implying organisms build their own distinctive worlds. [raw/books/strategy/ScienceStrategyWar_Osinga.pdf#p141]
