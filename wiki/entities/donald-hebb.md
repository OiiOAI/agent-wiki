# Donald Hebb

## Summary

Donald Hebb was a psychologist who proposed the fundamental mechanism of synaptic modification whereby connections between neurons are strengthened when the presynaptic and postsynaptic neurons fire synchronously. This 'Hebbian' mechanism underlies both learning and the competitive selection of neural connections during development.

## Key facts

- Hebb hypothesized that axons regularly releasing neurotransmitters in synchrony with recipient cell firing would have their synaptic connections strengthened, possibly via growth factor release. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p217]
- Though initially proposed as a mechanism for learning, the Hebbian mechanism also determines which connections 'win' in the competitive elimination process during development. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p217]
- Hebb (1949) envisioned his theory as a way to explain basic reinforced learning processes in the brain: how connections could become altered in their relative signal transduction 'strength' in response to the patterning of signals carried on these connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Many neural net computing paradigms employ Hebbian learning rules and demonstrate their effectiveness as a substrate for learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
- Hebb's general theory applies both to processes modifying synaptic strengths during learning and to those determining which synapses are preserved and which are lost during early development. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Symbolic Species - Terrence W. Deacon.pdf#p514]
