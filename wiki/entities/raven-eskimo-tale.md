# Raven (Eskimo)

## Summary

A trickster figure in Eskimo mythology who entered the belly of a whale and discovered himself in a handsome room where the soul (inua) of the whale lived. Campbell uses this tale as an example of rescue from without, illustrating how the hero's greed (breaking the heart-artery tube) caused the whale's death, but villagers then came to cut open the whale and rescue him. Raven's subsequent trick—frightening the people away with fire sticks to enjoy the feast alone—represents secondary elaboration on the basic hero-in-the-belly-of-the-whale archetype.

## Key facts

- Raven entered the belly of a whale and discovered the inua (soul) of the whale, a beautiful woman who lived in a handsome room within the animal. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p162]
- Raven broke a heart-artery in the whale's belly, causing the whale-cow to die and its body to wash ashore. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p162]
- The fire sticks motif underwent dislocation and rationalization; originally symbolic of the sex act, they became a plot device for Raven's trick to enjoy the feast alone. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p192]
