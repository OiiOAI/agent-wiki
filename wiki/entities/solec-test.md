# SOLEC Test

## Summary

The SOLEC Test (Stand On One Leg, Eyes Closed) is a balance assessment that removes visual information from the equation to isolate other sensory inputs used for balance control. It is one of two primary balance tests described in the Built to Move methodology.

## Key facts

- The SOLEC Test requires standing on one leg with eyes closed, removing visual input from balance assessment. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2041-L2047]
- By eliminating visual information, the test isolates other sensory mechanisms involved in balance. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2048-L2057]
- Balance depends on three main factors: the inner ear, sensory receptors in muscles, tendons, fascia, and joints, and eyesight. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2059-L2061]
- The SOLEC test measures how well the body's non-visual balance tools work by requiring the test-taker to stand on one leg with eyes closed. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2061-L2063]
- The test is performed standing barefoot on the floor in an open, uncluttered space. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2073-L2074]
- The test requires holding the position for twenty seconds while counting the number of times the raised foot touches the floor. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2074-L2076]
- If the test-taker feels uneasy, they should stand next to a wall or in front of a sink for safety. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2076-L2078]
- Scoring: zero touches indicates good mastery of balance, one to two touches indicates pretty good balance, three or more touches indicates balance needs work. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2080-L2085]
- The test can be taken every day as it serves as a balance practice in itself. [raw/books/health/Built_to_Move.epub#L2085-L2086]

## Inferences

- Inference: The SOLEC Test likely assesses proprioceptive and vestibular contributions to balance since it removes visual feedback.
- Inference: The SOLEC test is a simple field assessment that can be performed without special equipment, making it accessible for self-assessment at home.
