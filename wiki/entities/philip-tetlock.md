# Philip Tetlock

## Summary

Philip Tetlock is a psychologist who conducted a 10-year study evaluating 28,361 predictions from 284 self-appointed professionals, finding expert accuracy only marginally better than random chance.

## Key facts

- Philip Tetlock evaluated 28,361 predictions from 284 self-appointed professionals over ten years. [raw/books/strategy/The Art of Thinking Clearly _ Better thinking, Better - Rolf Dobelli; Nicky Griffin.pdf#p97]
- Philip Tetlock and Jennifer Lerner described the two kinds of group reasoning styles: confirmatory thought (one-sided rationalization) and exploratory thought (even-handed consideration of alternatives). [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1215-L1220]
- Complex and open-minded thought is most likely to be activated when decision makers learn prior to forming opinions that they will be accountable to an audience whose views are unknown, who is interested in accuracy, who is reasonably well-informed, and who has a legitimate reason for inquiring. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1225-L1235]
- Tetlock co-founded Heterodox Academy with Jon Haidt and others. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking_in_Bets.epub#L1390-L1395]
- Tetlock interviewed 284 experts over 20 years, collecting over 80,000 predictions, and found that expert predictions were generally worse than if people had simply assigned equal probability to all outcomes. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p282]
- Experts were worse than random chance even in their own domains of expertise, and those with the most knowledge often had the most illusory expertise, reaching the point of diminishing returns very quickly. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p283]
- Tetlock found that more famous experts made more flamboyant predictions and were invited on television more often, despite performing worse than more cautious colleagues. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p283]
- Tetlock adopted Isaiah Berlin's 'hedgehog and fox' distinction, finding that 'hedgehogs'—experts with one big theory who explained everything through a coherent framework—performed worse than 'foxes' who acknowledged multiple perspectives and uncertainty. [raw/books/strategy/Thinking, Fast and Slow (Cover Baru) - Daniel Kahneman.pdf#p283]

## Inferences

- Inference: Tetlock's finding that famous experts make worse predictions than unknown ones suggests that media and social rewards select for overconfidence rather than accuracy, creating a market for confident predictions that reliably fail.
