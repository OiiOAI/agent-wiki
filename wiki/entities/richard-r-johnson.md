# Richard R. Johnson

## Summary

Richard R. Johnson is a criminologist at the University of Toledo who conducted a groundbreaking study analyzing 452 segments from the TV show 'Cops' to test whether police officers' assumptions about demeanor cues (eye contact, smiling, speech patterns, hand movements) accurately indicate truthfulness or guilt. His research found that these cues have no reliable connection to guilt or innocence, and in some cases show the opposite pattern from what police are taught to expect.

## Key facts

- Johnson analyzed 452 segments from 'Cops' where the citizen was visible from the waist up for at least 60 seconds, categorizing interactions as involving 'innocent' or 'suspect' individuals. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p230]
- Johnson found that innocent Black individuals are actually less likely to look police in the eye than Black individuals suspected of crime, contrary to what the Reid Technique teaches. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p231]
- The study showed that innocent Black Americans smile the most, while Hispanic suspects smile the least—opposite to what police training assumes. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p231]
- His study was published as 'Race and Police Reliance on Suspicious Non-Verbal Cues' in Policing: An International Journal of Police Strategies and Management 30, no. 2 (2007). [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p230]
