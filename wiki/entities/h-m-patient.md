# H.M. (Henry Molaison)

## Summary

H.M. (Henry Molaison, 1926–2008) was a patient who developed profound anterograde amnesia following bilateral surgical removal of the hippocampus and medial temporal lobe to treat epilepsy. His case, studied from 1957 until his death, provided the first clear evidence that the medial temporal lobe is critical for forming new long-term memories while leaving working memory and old memories intact.

## Key facts

- H.M. underwent bilateral removal of the hippocampus, amygdala, and adjacent temporal cortex at age 27 in 1953 to treat intractable epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1294]
- Despite anterograde amnesia, H.M. retained normal IQ, working memory for seconds to minutes, and long-term memories from before his surgery. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1294]
- H.M. learned to trace a star in a mirror (mirror-tracing task) over several days with normal improvement, yet had no conscious memory of performing the task. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1296]
