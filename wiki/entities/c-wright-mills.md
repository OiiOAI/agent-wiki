# C. Wright Mills

## Summary

C. Wright Mills (1916-1962) was the most notable exception to the marginalization of Marxian theory in America, keeping a radical tradition alive in sociological theory. Born in Waco, Texas, he attended the University of Texas and did doctoral work at the University of Wisconsin. He spent most of his career at Columbia University from 1945 until his death. Mills was known for his combativeness and was at odds with American society and sociology. He published "White Collar" (1951), "The Power Elite" (1956), and his most theoretically sophisticated work "Character and Social Structure" (1953) with Hans Gerth. His "The Sociological Imagination" (1959) severely criticized Talcott Parsons and grand theory. Mills did not read Marx until the mid-1950s and was not a Marxist. He died in 1962, an outcast in sociology, but set the stage for the eventual acceptance of radical sociology.
