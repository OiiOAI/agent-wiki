# Tom Christie

## Summary

Tom Christie was a civilian mathematician at Eglin Air Force Base who became John Boyd's essential collaborator in developing the Energy-Maneuverability Theory. Christie performed the mathematical computations and computer work while Boyd provided the conceptual framework. Their partnership, lasting years, combined Christie's technical abilities with Boyd's fighter pilot experience and theoretical vision.

## Key facts

- Christie was a better mathematician than Boyd but Boyd insisted on understanding every equation. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p146]
- Christie performed much of the computer work on the IBM 704 using project codes borrowed from other authorized work. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p147]
- Christie lived 40 miles away in Pensacola and often worked until 2:00 AM, with Boyd calling him at home. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p147]
- Christie produced Joint Munitions Effectiveness Manuals (JMEMs) that all four services signed off on at the four-star level. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p198]
- Christie ate eleven steaks in a single sitting at a 'all you can eat' restaurant. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p204]
- Christie wrote the official secret E-M report published in May 1964. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p166]
