# Nvidia Corporation

## Summary

Nvidia was founded in 1993 by Jen-Hsun Huang, Curtis Priem, and Chris Malachowsky to become a designer of 3-D graphics chips. After an initial product failure (NV1), the company reformed its strategy to focus on 3-D graphics for desktop PCs using the SGI-based triangle rendering method. Nvidia implemented a six-month product release cycle through three overlapping development teams, investing heavily in emulation facilities to enable early driver development. By 2007, Nvidia dominated the high-performance 3-D graphics chip market and was named 'Company of the Year' by Forbes.

## Key facts

- Nvidia was founded in 1993 by Jen-Hsun Huang, Curtis Priem, and Chris Malachowsky, all with backgrounds at major technology companies. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p84]
- The company's first product, the NV1 introduced in 1995, was a commercial flop due to its quirky 3-D graphics approach. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p84]
- Nvidia invested heavily in simulation and emulation techniques, organized around formal verification of chip logic. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p84]
- Nvidia developed a unified driver architecture (UDA) that allowed all chips to use the same driver software, adapting to each chip's capabilities. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p84]
- The GeForce 256 chip had nearly 23 million transistors and achieved floating point calculation capacity of 50 gigaflops. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p85]
- During 1997–2001, Nvidia achieved 157 percent average annual gain in graphics performance. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p85]
- Rival 3dfx closed its doors in late 2000, selling patents, brands, and inventory to Nvidia. [raw/books/strategy/Good strategy, bad strategy _ the difference and why it - Richard P_ Rumelt.pdf#p85]
- Nvidia pushed aside its competition by shortening its product introduction cycle from the normal eighteen months for semiconductors to six months. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p95]
- Nvidia created three development teams, each taking eighteen months to deliver a design but each on a schedule offset by six months. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p95]
- With a new design hitting the market every six months, competitors could not keep up. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p95]
- Nvidia passed on acquiring ArtX, a small cadre of talented former Silicon Graphics engineers, which competitor ATI snapped up. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p102]
- Nvidia's graphics processors work on a different principle than Intel's x86 microprocessors, using simpler cores capable of simultaneous operations. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p179]
- An Nvidia GPU core has only 10 million transistors, and the most recent consumer GPU has 2,176 cores. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p179]
- Nvidia's most recent high-end AI chip, the A100, has 54 billion transistors and 43,000 cores. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p179]
- ARM owns processor architecture designs underlying most mobile devices and is beginning to invade Intel's cloud-processing space. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p179]
- The crux of Nvidia's strategy with ARM is to use ARM-supported designs around Nvidia cores to serve cloud-based specialty areas like encryption, image analysis, and machine learning. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p179]

## Inferences

- Inference: Nvidia's strategy demonstrates how a faster development cycle can induce competitors into less coordinated responses, creating strategic advantage through execution speed.
- Inference: Nvidia's success demonstrates how operational speed can create sustainable competitive advantage even against much larger competitors like Intel.
- Inference: Nvidia's acquisition of ARM represents a strategic coupling designed to expand from GPU-based AI into broader processor markets by combining ARM's flexible architecture designs with Nvidia's parallel processing expertise.
- Inference: The tight integration of processing and memory that Nvidia is developing could potentially overtake Intel's architecture in certain applications, representing a significant strategic bet.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Whether the Nvidia-ARM integration will successfully overtake Intel architecture in cloud computing remains to be seen, as the text describes this as a bet rather than a certainty.
