# Deborah Burke and Donald MacKay

## Summary

Deborah Burke and Donald MacKay are cognitive psychologists who developed a network model explaining why people block more often on proper names than common names. Their model proposes that proper names have a single, fragile link between person identity nodes and lexical representations, unlike common names which have multiple converging conceptual links. This model explains age-related increases in name blocking and the role of frequency of encounter.

## Key facts

- The Burke and MacKay model proposes that for common names, visual representations activate conceptual representations that converge directly on lexical representations with summed excitation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p97]
- For proper names, individual conceptual representations converge on a single person identity node that connects by a single fragile link to the lexical representation. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p98]
- Name blocking occurs most often for names of people encountered infrequently because the already fragile link between lexical and conceptual representations is weakened. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Seven Sins of Memory - Daniel L. Schacter.pdf#p99]
