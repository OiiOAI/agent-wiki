# Area Postrema

## Summary

The area postrema is a small region adjacent to the nucleus of the solitary tract along the floor of the fourth ventricle. Unlike most of the brain, it contains fenestrated capillaries that allow its neurons to sample blood contents, enabling detection of toxins and initiation of vomiting.

## Key facts

- The area postrema contains fenestrated capillaries that allow its neurons to sample the contents of the blood stream, unlike most of the brain which is protected by a blood-brain barrier. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1041]
- Neurons in the area postrema detect toxic substances in the bloodstream and activate neurons that clear the digestive tract through reversal of peristalsis, increased abdominal muscle contraction, and gag reflex activation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1041]
