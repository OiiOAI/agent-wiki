# John Hughlings Jackson

## Summary

John Hughlings Jackson (1835-1911) was an English neurologist who made foundational contributions to understanding cortical localization of motor function. Based on observations of epileptic seizures, he proposed that discrete cortical regions anterior to the central sulcus have a causal role in movement.

## Key facts

- Jackson treated patients with epileptic seizures characterized by repeated spasmodic involuntary movements resembling fragments of purposive voluntary actions. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p887]
- Jackson observed that seizures always spread to different body parts in a fixed temporal sequence (Jacksonian march), leading him to propose that paroxysmal activity spread across clusters of neurons along the central sulcus. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p887]
- Jackson's proposal that discrete cortical regions are involved in motor control was a strong argument for localization of function in the cerebral cortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p887]
