# Theodor Meynert

## Summary

Theodor Meynert (1833–1892) was Freud's former teacher and a highly esteemed neurologist in Vienna. He represented the German medical establishment's skeptical and alarmist views toward hypnotism. Meynert described hypnosis as 'an experimentally produced psychosis' and 'insanity,' and criticized the reality of hypnotic phenomena. Freud engaged extensively with and refuted Meynert's criticisms in his reviews of Obersteiner and Forel.

## Key facts

- Meynert described hypnosis as 'experimentally produced insanity' and characterized hypnotism as surrounded by a 'halo of absurdity.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p81]
- Freud stated that many high medical authorities in Vienna still exhibited alarmist or sceptical views on hypnotism, citing Meynert's remarks as an example. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p67]
- Meynert confused knowledge about hysteria and hypnotism in a paper before the Vienna Society of Medicine, distinguishing four stages of hypnosis where Charcot only distinguished three. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p87]
- Freud argued that the objections heard in Germany against hypnotism deserve attention only on account of the names of their authors, citing Meynert as a case in point. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p81]
