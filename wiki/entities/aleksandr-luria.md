# Aleksandr Luria

## Summary

Aleksandr Luria (1902-1977) was a Soviet neuropsychologist who founded the field of neuropsychology by integrating psychoanalytic methods with neurology. He developed detailed case histories of patients with brain injuries, most notably Lyova Zazetsky, and mapped which brain areas process which mental functions.

## Key facts

- Aleksandr Luria was born in 1902 and came of age in revolutionary Russia. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- He was deeply interested in psychoanalysis, corresponded with Freud, and wrote papers on free association. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- In his twenties he invented the prototype of the lie detector. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- When the Great Purges of the Stalin era began, psychoanalysis became scientia non grata and Luria was denounced. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- He delivered a public recantation, admitting to 'ideological mistakes,' then went to medical school to remove himself from view. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- He integrated aspects of psychoanalytic method and psychology into neurology, becoming the founder of neuropsychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- Oliver Sacks wrote that 'Luria's case histories can only be compared to Freud's in their precision, their vitality, their wealth and depth of detail'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- His book 'The Man with a Shattered World' was a summary of and commentary on the diary of Lyova Zazetsky. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p42]
- He observed Zazetsky over thirty years and related the location of the soldier's wound to the mental functions lost. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- Luria formed his map of the brain by working with patients like Zazetsky, observing where wounds occurred and relating location to lost mental functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
- Barbara Arrowsmith Young used Luria's work to understand which areas of the brain commonly process which mental functions. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p46]
