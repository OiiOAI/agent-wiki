# Aleksandr Romanovich Luria

## Summary

Soviet neuropsychologist (1902-1977) who pioneered the study of complex brain functions and contributed foundational work in neuropsychology. His 30-year study of the mnemonist S. exemplified his clinical approach of treating cases as 'syndromes'—examining how one psychological feature affects the entire personality structure. Luria combined clinical wisdom with scientific psychological acumen.

## Key facts

- Luria was a Soviet neuropsychologist who began studying S. in the 1920s when he was recently started in psychology. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p17]
- Luria approached the study of S. not merely to measure memory capacity but to determine what effect exceptional memory had on other aspects of personality, habits of thought, imagination, behavior, and personality development. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p14]
- Luria's approach treats this as a 'syndrome' study, analyzing how an unusually developed feature of psychic makeup produces causally related changes in the entire structure of psychic life and total personality. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p5]
- Jerome Bruner noted that Luria combined the clinical wisdom of the fine physician with the theoretical acumen of the scientific psychologist. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Mind of a Mnemonist_ A Little Book about a Vast Memory, - Aleksandr R_ Luria, Lynn Solotaroff, Jerome Bruner.pdf#p9]
