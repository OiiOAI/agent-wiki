# Oxytocin

## Summary

Oxytocin is a mammalian neuropeptide that stimulates affiliative behaviors including pair bonding and parental bonding with offspring. In mice, oxytocin is required for social recognition. In prairie voles, oxytocin released in the brain during mating stimulates pair-bond formation. Its role in human social behavior is not definitively established.

## Key facts

- Oxytocin and vasopressin stimulate mammalian affiliative behaviors such as pair bonding and parental bonding with offspring. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p92]
- In mice, oxytocin is required for social recognition, the ability to identify a familiar individual. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p92]
- Oxytocin released in the brain of female prairie voles during mating stimulates pair-bond formation. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p92]
