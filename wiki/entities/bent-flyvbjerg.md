# Bent Flyvbjerg

## Summary

Bent Flyvbjerg is a researcher Taleb cites for firm evidence that increasing the size of projects maps to poor outcomes and higher cost overruns as a proportion of total budget. He showed that bridge and tunnel projects (monolithic, cannot be broken into pieces) have markedly increasing percentage cost overruns with size, while roads built in small segments show no serious size effect. He also demonstrated that IT projects cause a large share of cost overruns.

## Key facts

- Flyvbjerg showed firm evidence that increasing the size of projects maps to poor outcomes and higher cost overruns as a proportion of total budget. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p355]
- Bridge and tunnel projects involve monolithic planning and cannot be broken up — their percentage cost overruns increase markedly with size. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p355]
- For roads built in small segments, there is no serious size effect — project managers incur only small errors and can adapt. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p355]
- Flyvbjerg showed that IT projects cause a large share of cost overruns, making them a primary focus for understanding project delays. [raw/books/strategy/Antifragile_ Things That Gain from Disorde - Taleb, Nassim Nicholas.pdf#p358]

## Inferences

- Inference: Flyvbjerg's work supports Taleb's argument that small is beautiful and less fragile — projects that can be modularized avoid the squeeze effects that plague monolithic large-scale projects.
