# Ana Belen Montes

## Summary

Ana Belen Montes was a senior intelligence analyst at the U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency who was secretly working for Cuban intelligence for nearly her entire government career (1985-2001). Nicknamed the 'Queen of Cuba,' she was one of the most damaging spies in U.S. history, receiving a medal personally from Fidel Castro.

## Key facts

- Ana Montes joined the DIA in 1985 and was recruited by Cuban intelligence after expressing support for Nicaragua's Marxist Sandinista government. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p49]
- Montes was known as the 'Queen of Cuba' inside the intelligence community due to her expertise on Cuban affairs. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p39]
- DIA counterintelligence officer Scott Carmichael interviewed Montes and found her explanation for suspicious behavior convincing enough to dismiss Reg Brown's suspicions. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p50]
- Montes was discovered in 2001 when the NSA decoded Cuban communications revealing 'Agent S' had accessed information about the SAFE computer system. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p52]
- During her career, Montes met with her handlers at least 300 times and was personally awarded a medal by Fidel Castro. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p50]
