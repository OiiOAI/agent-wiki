# Norbert Hanold

## Summary

A fictional young archaeologist created by Wilhelm Jensen for his novella 'Gradiva'. Freud analyzes Hanold as suffering from a delusional state in which he falls in love with a marble relief and later believes he encounters the woman depicted (as a ghost or living person) in Pompeii. Hanold's delusion is revealed to be a return of repressed memories of his childhood friendship with Zoe Bertgang.

## Key facts

- Norbert Hanold is a Lecturer in Archaeology who had surrendered his interest in life in exchange for an interest in the remains of classical antiquity. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2907]
- Hanold had given his attention only to women of marble or bronze, and had never paid the slightest attention to contemporary female representatives of the sex. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2909]
- Hanold's phantasies about Gradiva were revealed by Freud to be echoes of his forgotten childhood memories of Zoe Bertgang. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2925]
- Hanold permanently turned away from women, producing a personal susceptibility to delusion formation. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2938]
- Hanold's memories of his childhood relations with Zoe were repressed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2939]
- Hanold's journey to Pompeii represented a flight from his awakening erotic longing. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p2953]

## Inferences

- Inference: Hanold's condition may be described as hysterical delusion rather than paranoia, given the absence of typical paranoid features.
