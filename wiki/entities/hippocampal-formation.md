# Hippocampal Formation

## Summary

The hippocampal formation (or hippocampal system) is a medial temporal lobe structure critical for explicit (declarative) memory formation and spatial representation. It comprises the dentate gyrus, hippocampus proper (CA1, CA2, CA3), subiculum, and entorhinal cortex. Unlike the six-layered neocortex, the hippocampus is a three-layered archicortex. It receives polysensory information from perirhinal, parahippocampal, and retrosplenial cortices via the entorhinal cortex.

- The hippocampus is a three-layered cortical structure (archicortex), phylogenetically older than the six-layered neocortex. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p141]

- The hippocampal circuit flows serially from layer II of the entorhinal cortex to the dentate gyrus, then to CA3, then to CA1, and finally to the subiculum before returning to deep layers of the entorhinal cortex.
