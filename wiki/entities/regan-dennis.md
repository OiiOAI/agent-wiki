# Dennis Regan

## Summary

Dennis Regan is a Cornell University professor who conducted experiments demonstrating the overwhelming power of the reciprocity rule. His study showed that an unsolicited small favor doubled compliance regardless of whether the recipient liked the requester, proving reciprocity can override the normal influence of interpersonal attraction.

## Key facts

- In Regan's study, Joe the assistant gave some subjects an unsolicited Coke during a rest break, then later asked them to buy raffle tickets. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p25]
- Subjects who received the unsolicited Coke bought twice as many raffle tickets as those who did not, even controlling for liking. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p26]
- Regan found that for those who owed Joe a favor, liking made no difference—disliked Joe sold as many tickets as liked Joe. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p27]
- Regan conducted his experiment at Cornell University. [raw/books/psychology/Influence_ The Psychology of Persuasion - Robert B. Cialdini.pdf#p25]
