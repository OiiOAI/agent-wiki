# Gap Junction Channel

## Summary

Gap junction channels are specialized protein structures that directly connect the cytoplasm of adjacent cells, forming pores approximately 1.5 nm in diameter that allow passage of ions and small molecules.

## Key facts

- Gap junction channels are composed of a pair of hemichannels called connexons, one contributed by each apposed cell membrane. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p244]
- Each connexon is formed by six identical connexin subunits arranged symmetrically around a central pore. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p244]
- The three-dimensional structure of the human connexin 26 gap junction channel has been determined by X-ray crystallography, revealing how transmembrane α-helices assemble to form the central pore. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p246]
- Gap junction channels close in response to lowered cytoplasmic pH or elevated cytoplasmic Ca2+, with sensitivity varying among different channel isoforms. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p244]
- The N-terminal α-helix of connexin 26 may serve as the voltage gate of the channel, plugging the cytoplasmic mouth of the pore in the closed state. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p246]
