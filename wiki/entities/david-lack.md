# David Lack

## Summary

David Lack was a British evolutionary biologist and ornithologist whom Dawkins identifies as 'the chief architect of the selfish gene theory of family planning.' Lack's research on clutch size in wild birds established that each species tends to have a characteristic clutch size, not because of group-level benefits but because individuals who produce the optimal number of offspring for their circumstances rear more surviving children than those who produce too many or too few.

## Key facts

- David Lack was 'the chief architect of the selfish gene theory of family planning,' working especially on clutch size in wild birds, with theories generally applicable beyond birds to all species. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p149]
- Lack's essential point was that for any given species in any given environmental situation, there must be an optimal clutch size—where three is the optimum for swifts, any individual who tries to rear four will probably end up with fewer children than rivals who only try to rear three. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p149]
- Unlike Wynne-Edwards who asked about optimum for the group, Lack asked 'Optimal from whose point of view?' and answered: each selfish individual chooses clutch size that maximizes the number of children she rears—practicing birth control to maximize surviving offspring, not to avoid overexploiting group resources. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p149]
- According to Lack, individuals who have too many children are penalized because fewer of their children survive to adulthood—genes for having too many children are not passed on in large numbers because few children bearing those genes reach adulthood. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p150]
