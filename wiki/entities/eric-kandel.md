# Eric Kandel

## Summary

Eric Kandel is a psychiatrist and researcher who won the Nobel Prize in 2000 for demonstrating that learning produces physical changes in neurons, including alterations in synaptic connections and anatomical structures. Born in Vienna in 1929, he survived Kristallnacht and later became a major figure bridging neuroscience and psychoanalysis.

## Key facts

- Kandel was born in 1929 in Vienna and experienced Kristallnacht, which he said became 'burned into memory' more than sixty years later. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p226]
- He was the first to show that individual neurons alter their structure and strengthen synaptic connections as we learn, winning the Nobel Prize in 2000. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p223]
- Kandel demonstrated that when we form long-term memories, neurons change their anatomical shape and increase the number of synaptic connections to other neurons. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p223]
- Working with James Schwartz and geneticists, Kandel showed that for short-term memories to become long-term, a new protein must be made in the cell, involving protein kinase A moving from the neuron body into the nucleus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p225]
- Kandel, with colleagues Mary Chen and Craig Bailey, showed that when a single neuron develops long-term memory for sensitization, it might grow from 1,300 to 2,700 synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p226]
- Kandel argued that when psychotherapy changes people, 'it presumably does so through learning, by producing changes in gene expression that alter the strength of synaptic connections, and structural changes that alter the anatomical pattern of interconnections between nerve cells of the brain.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p226]
- Kandel wrote 'I cannot help but think that the experiences of my last year in Vienna helped to determine my later interests in the mind, in how people behave, the unpredictability of motivation, and the persistence of memory.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p227]
- Kandel stated, 'There is no longer any doubt that psychotherapy can result in detectable changes in the brain.'. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p238]
- Kandel wrote a memoir titled In Search of Memory dealing with his painful memories of the 1930s. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p248]
