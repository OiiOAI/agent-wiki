# Cranial Nerves

## Summary

The twelve pairs of cranial nerves (I-XII) provide sensory and motor innervation for the face, head, and neck, as well as autonomic functions for the body. They are homologous to spinal nerves but mediate more complex behaviors. Each cranial nerve may be associated with one or more functions and may overlap the physical territory of another cranial nerve.

## Key facts

- Cranial nerves I and II are associated with the forebrain and diencephalon respectively, while cranial nerves III-XII arise from the brain stem. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1071]
- The olfactory (I) nerve terminates in the olfactory bulb in the forebrain and the optic (II) nerve is associated with the diencephalon. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1072]
- The oculomotor (III), trochlear (IV), and abducens (VI) nerves control movements of the eyes, with the abducens nerve contracting the lateral rectus muscle to move the globe laterally. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1072]
- The trigeminal (V) nerve is a mixed nerve with three sensory divisions (V1 ophthalmic, V2 maxillary, V3 mandibular) that innervate the face, scalp, and oral cavity. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1072]
- The facial (VII) nerve supplies muscles of facial expression as well as the stapedius muscle, stylohyoid muscle, and posterior belly of the digastric muscle, and includes parasympathetic fibers to lacrimal and salivary glands. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1072]
- The glossopharyngeal (IX) and vagus (X) nerves are mixed but predominantly autonomic, transmitting sensory information from the pharynx, upper airway, and taste from the posterior third of the tongue. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1073]
- The spinal accessory (XI) nerve originates from motor neurons in the upper cervical spinal cord and innervates the trapezius and sternocleidomastoid muscles. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1074]
- The hypoglossal (XII) nerve is purely motor, innervating the muscles of the tongue, and when injured the tongue atrophies on that side with visible fasciculations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p1074]
