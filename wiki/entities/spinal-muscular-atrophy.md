# Spinal Muscular Atrophy

## Summary

Spinal Muscular Atrophy is a developmental motor neuron disorder caused by recessively inherited mutations in the SMN1 gene encoding survival motor neuron protein. It is characterized by weakness, wasting, loss of reflexes, and fasciculations, typically beginning in infancy with short survival. Two therapeutic approaches—antisense oligonucleotide therapy targeting SMN2 splicing and gene therapy with AAV-delivered SMN1—have achieved extraordinary clinical benefits.

## Key facts

- SMA is caused by recessive mutations in the SMN1 gene encoding survival motor neuron (SMN) protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- The SMN2 gene produces a shortened SMN protein due to alternative splicing that excludes exon 7. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- SMN protein is implicated in trafficking RNA and in the formation of complexes important for RNA splicing. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) therapy blocks hnRNPA1/A2 binding to SMN2, promoting exon 7 inclusion and increasing full-length SMN protein. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
- Gene therapy using adeno-associated virus (AAV) to deliver SMN1 cDNA dramatically augments survival in infantile SMA. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1462]
