# George Ojemann

## Summary

George Ojemann was a leading researcher at the University of Washington School of Medicine at Seattle who performed experiments on the exposed human brain during necessary brain operations. He used transient electric currents sufficiently small to inactivate small local regions near the electrode without producing permanent after-effects, and concentrated on regions of the cortex involved with language.

## Key facts

- George Ojemann pioneered experiments using transient electric currents to inactivate small local regions near an electrode without producing permanent after-effects. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p124]
- Ojemann concentrated on regions of the cortex involved with language, since he wanted to avoid damaging language areas when removing portions of a patient's cerebral cortex to relieve incurable epilepsy. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p124]
- One of Ojemann's striking results showed that stimulation at certain places on the left neocortex temporarily prevented a bilingual patient (English and Greek) from using certain English words but not Greek ones, demonstrating clear differences in the location of aspects of the two languages. [raw/books/neuroscience/The astonishing hypothesis _ the scientific search for the - Crick, Francis.pdf#p124]
