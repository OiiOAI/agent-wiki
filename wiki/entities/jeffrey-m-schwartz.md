# Jeffrey M. Schwartz

## Summary

Jeffrey M. Schwartz is a psychiatrist who developed an effective, plasticity-based treatment for obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and related conditions. Working at UCLA, he pioneered the use of brain scans (PET) to understand OCD and develop psychotherapy, demonstrating for the first time that a talking therapy could change the brain. His four-step 'Brain Lock' treatment approach has helped 80 percent of his patients improve when combined with medication.

## Key facts

- Jeffrey M. Schwartz is a psychiatrist at UCLA who developed a plasticity-based treatment for OCD. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p174]
- Schwartz used PET brain scans to understand OCD and developed a new form of psychotherapy—the first time brain scans helped doctors both understand a disorder and develop a psychotherapy for it. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p175]
- Schwartz tested the new treatment by doing brain scans on patients before and after psychotherapy, showing their brains normalized with treatment—the first demonstration that a talking therapy could change the brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p175]
- Schwartz calls OCD 'brain lock' because the orbital frontal cortex and cingulate turn on and stay on as though locked in the 'on position' together. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p176]
- Schwartz developed the Brain Lock treatment to help those with OCD and those with more everyday worries who get mentally 'sticky' and hold on to worries. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p175]
- The treatment can also help with compulsive nail biting, hair pulling, shopping, gambling, eating disorders, obsessive jealousy, substance abuse, and excessive concern about self-image. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p175]
- Schwartz teaches patients to distinguish between the universal form of OCD (worrisome thoughts and urges that intrude) and the content of an obsession. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p178]
- Schwartz found that eighty percent of his patients get better when using his method in combination with medication such as Anafranil or Prozac. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p182]
- Schwartz authored the book 'Brain Lock' containing pictures of abnormal OCD brain scans to help patients understand the condition. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p177]

## Inferences

- Inference: Schwartz's work represents a paradigm shift in psychiatry by demonstrating that talk therapy can produce measurable changes in brain structure, challenging the view that brain disorders require pharmacological intervention to achieve neurological change.
