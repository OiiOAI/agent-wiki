# Jerry Sandusky

## Summary

Jerry Sandusky was the defensive coordinator for Penn State University's football team who was convicted in 2012 of forty-five counts of child molestation involving at least eight boys over many years. His case illustrates how allegations of abuse can fail to trigger action due to ambiguity and default to truth.

## Key facts

- Jerry Sandusky founded The Second Mile charity in 1977, a recreational program for troubled boys that served as his primary source of victims. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p67]
- A 1998 complaint about Sandusky showering with a young boy was closed after interviews with Sandusky, who claimed 'nothing happened' and the boy himself denied abuse. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p68]
- Michael McQueary witnessed Sandusky in a shower with a young boy in February 2001 but the investigation did not begin until nearly a decade later. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p64]
- Sandusky was convicted of forty-five counts of child molestation and Penn State paid over $100 million in settlements to his victims. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p66]
- Sandusky founded The Second Mile, a charity for at-risk youth that served as the vehicle for his abuse. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p206]
- Sandusky's nickname was 'The Dean of Linebacker U' during his coaching career at Penn State. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p206]
- Sandusky wrote in his autobiography 'Touched' that 'Wherever I went...part of me,' reflecting on his relationship with the boys he coached. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p206]
- Sandusky was convicted in 2012 on 45 counts of child sexual abuse and sentenced to 30 to 60 years in prison. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p206]
