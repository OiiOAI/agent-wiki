# Ventral Premotor Cortex (PMv)

## Summary

The ventral premotor cortex (PMv) is the most lateral part of the premotor cortex, reciprocally connected with parietal cortex areas AIP, PF, and PFG. PMv is involved in planning motor actions of the hand based on the physical affordances of target objects. It contains neurons selective for specific grasping actions and grip types, and houses mirror neurons that discharge both during active grasping and when observing similar actions performed by others.

## Key facts

- The ventral premotor cortex (PMv) is reciprocally connected with parietal cortex areas AIP, PF, and PFG and the secondary somatosensory area. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- Electrical stimulation of PMv contains extensively overlapping circuits that control hand and mouth movements. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- Many PMv neurons fire preferentially during specific stereotypical hand actions such as grasping, holding, tearing, or manipulating objects. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- Some PMv neurons discharge only if the monkey uses a specific type of grip, such as a precision grip, whole-hand prehension, or finger prehension. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- Many PMv neurons discharge when grasping an object is executed with effectors as different as the right hand, the left hand, and even the mouth. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- PMv discharge often correlates with the goal of a motor act rather than with individual movements forming it. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p835]
- Mirror neurons, first discovered in PMv and later in parietal AIP, discharge both when the monkey actively grasps and manipulates objects and when it observes similar actions performed by another monkey or the experimenter. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
- Mirror neurons typically do not respond when a monkey simply observes a potential target object or when it observes mimed arm and hand actions without a target object. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
- Some parietal mirror neurons can differentiate the ultimate goal of similar observed actions, such as grasping and picking up food to eat versus putting it into a cup. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p839]
