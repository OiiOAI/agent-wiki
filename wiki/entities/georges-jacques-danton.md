# Georges-Jacques Danton

## Summary

Georges-Jacques Danton (1759-1794) was a leading figure of the French Revolution who Greene presents as an exemplar of someone who transcended generational constraints to sense the coming revolution. Born to a peasant family that rose to the middle class, Danton witnessed Louis XVI's coronation at age fifteen and intuited even then the emptiness of monarchy. He became a lawyer with powerful oratorical skills, joined revolutionary clubs, and foresaw the 'avalanche' of revolution. He played a key role in the August 10, 1792 uprising that ended the monarchy, later becoming Minister of Justice and de facto leader of the Republic. He created the revolutionary tribunal that initiated the Terror but later sought to end it, for which Robespierre had him executed in 1794.

## Key facts

- Danton was born in 1759 to a family of peasants whose father became a lawyer, raising them to the middle class. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p632]
- At age fifteen, Danton attended Louis XVI's coronation and intuited the emptiness of monarchy, seeing the king as merely ordinary despite his regalia. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p633]
- Danton became a lawyer with powerful oratorical skills and joined the Cordeliers Club, where he detected growing discontent with the monarchy. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p635]
- Danton turned down a higher position on the King's Council, warning that France was on the brink of revolution: 'Can't you see the avalanche coming?'. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p636]
- Danton was elected deputy prosecutor for Paris and gained control of the commune, planning the August 10, 1792 uprising that ended the monarchy. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p642]
- After the king's execution, Danton created a revolutionary tribunal that initiated the Terror, sending thousands to the guillotine. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p644]
- After his wife Gabrielle died in premature childbirth, Danton returned from Belgium and famously disinterred her body to say goodbye. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]
- Danton became less supportive of the Terror and openly worked to end it, earning him the enmity of Robespierre. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]
- Robespierre had Danton arrested and executed in March 1794 on charges of treason, though based on innuendo. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]
- Danton predicted Robespierre's downfall, shouting 'You're next!' as he was led to the guillotine. [raw/books/psychology/The Laws of Human Nature - Robert Greene.pdf#p645]

## Inferences

- Inference: Greene uses Danton as an archetype of someone who developed 'generational awareness'—the ability to sense underlying cultural shifts and act on them. Danton's swimming metaphor (letting the tide carry him) illustrates how he aligned himself with historical forces rather than fighting them.
