# Vampire Bats

## Summary

Vampire bats (Desmodus rotundus) provide a striking example of reciprocal altruism in nature. They regurgitate blood to feed unlucky roostmates who failed to feed, even among unrelated individuals. Wilkinson's research showed that bats preferentially feed 'old friends' from their own cave, demonstrating individual recognition and long-term reciprocal relationships.

## Key facts

- Vampire bats feed on blood at night, and when dawn comes some individuals may be completely empty while others have a surplus. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p271]
- Wilkinson observed 110 regurgitations, with 77 easily understood as mothers feeding children, but some involved unrelated bats. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p271]
- Bats preferentially feed 'old friends' from their own cave rather than 'new friends' from different caves (odds less than 1 in 500 against coincidence). [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p273]
- The blood-donor relationship follows the pay-off structure of a Prisoner's Dilemma game. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Selfish Gene_ 40th Anniversary edition - Richard Dawkins.pdf#p272]
