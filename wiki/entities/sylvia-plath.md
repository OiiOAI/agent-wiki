# Sylvia Plath

## Summary

Sylvia Plath (1932-1963) was an American poet and novelist who committed suicide in London in February 1963. She is used in the book as an example of how coupling—specifically the availability of carbon monoxide from town gas—influenced her suicide method.

## Key facts

- Sylvia Plath committed suicide in February 1963 by placing her head in her kitchen gas oven, using the carbon monoxide from 'town gas' that was common in British homes at the time. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p152-155]
- She had multiple risk factors for suicide: previous attempts, history of mental illness, recent divorce, living in a foreign country, and isolation from family. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p153-154]
- When Britain phased out town gas in favor of natural gas (which contains no carbon monoxide) between 1965 and 1977, suicide rates for British women aged 25-44 dropped by approximately half. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p163]

## Inferences

- Inference: Gladwell argues that had Plath lived ten years later, when natural gas had replaced town gas, she likely would not have died by suicide because her preferred method would have been unavailable.
