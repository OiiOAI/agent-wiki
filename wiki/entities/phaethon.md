# Phaëthon

## Summary

Greek hero, son of the sun god Phoebus, who demanded and received permission to drive the solar chariot. Unable to control the horses, he caused catastrophic damage to the world—burning Ethiopia black, turning Libya to desert, and forcing the Nile to hide its head—until Zeus struck him down with a thunderbolt.

## Key facts

- Phaëthon was born of a virgin in Ethiopia and taunted by playmates about his unknown father, leading him to journey to Phoebus's palace. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p109]
- Phoebus bound himself by oath to grant any proof Phaëthon desired, and the son demanded the chariot of fire itself. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p110]
- Phaëthon's wild driving scorched the constellations, evaporated the clouds, set the earth ablaze, turned Ethiopia black, and made Libya a desert. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p111]
- Zeus struck Phaëthon with a thunderbolt and the river Po received his burning body, with epitaph: 'Here Phaëthon lies: in Phoebus' car he fared. And though he greatly failed, more greatly dared.'. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p111]
