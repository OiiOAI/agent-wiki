# Alfred Maury

## Summary

Alfred Maury (1817–1892) was a French professor of history and ethics at the College de France who conducted early experimental dream research in the 1860s. He proposed that dreams operate through automatism, with associations between daytime experiences forming dream content. His self-experiments with sensory stimuli (water, smells, sounds, tickling) during sleep demonstrated that external stimuli could be incorporated into dreams.

## Key facts

- Maury proposed that dreaming operates through automatism, with actions in dreams guided by a mechanistic process reflecting acquired experiences. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p26]
- He hypothesized that unlike the waking brain, the dreaming brain does not work as a synchronized whole, allowing faculties like perception, memory, will, and judgment to fluctuate independently. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p27]
- Maury conducted experiments where his assistant applied sensory stimuli during his sleep, showing that sounds of alarm bells and smells of burning could generate coherent dream narratives. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p27]
- He argued that dreams can retrieve memories long forgotten by the conscious mind, including names, places, and events. [raw/books/neuroscience/When Brains Dream - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p27]

## Inferences

- Inference: Maury's stimulus experiments anticipated modern laboratory studies of sensory incorporation into dreams by approximately one hundred years.
