# Ramakrishna

## Summary

Nineteenth-century Hindu mystic (1836-1886) who served as priest in the newly erected temple of the Cosmic Mother at Dakshineswar, near Calcutta. He experienced a vision of the goddess who appeared as a beautiful woman bearing a child, then devoured it, returning to the Ganges.

## Key facts

- Ramakrishna (1836-1886) was priest at the Dakshineswar temple displaying the Cosmic Mother in her two aspects: terrible and benign. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p94]
- The temple image showed the goddess with four arms: upper left brandishing a bloody saber and gripping a severed head, upper right in 'fear not' gesture, lower extended in bestowal of boons. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p94]
- Ramakrishna beheld a beautiful woman ascend from the Ganges, give birth to a child, nurse it, then assume a horrible aspect and devour the infant before returning to the Ganges. [raw/books/philosophy/The Hero With a Thousand Faces (Paladin Bo - Joseph Campbell.pdf#p94]
