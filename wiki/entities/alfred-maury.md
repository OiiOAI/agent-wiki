# Alfred Maury

## Summary

Alfred Maury (1817-1892) was a French professor of history and ethics at the College of France who conducted pioneering experiments on dreams in the mid-19th century. His work included sensory stimulation experiments during sleep and the proposal that dreams could occur instantaneously.

## Key facts

- Maury proposed that dreams could occur instantaneously, an idea that persists in some circles today. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p34]
- Maury argued that during dreams, faculties like perception, memory, will, and judgment could fluctuate independently, giving rise to bizarre dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p34]
- Maury conducted experiments where his assistant applied sensory stimuli during his sleep, including water drops, scents, and feathers, to study how they influenced his dreams. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p35]
- Maury concluded that the sleeping brain uses sensory information to create relevant dreams, anticipating later findings about dream incorporation. [raw/books/neuroscience/When brains dream _ understanding the science and mystery of - Antonio Zadra.pdf#p35]
