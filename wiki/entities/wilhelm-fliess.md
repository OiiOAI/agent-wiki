# Wilhelm Fliess

## Summary

Wilhelm Fliess (1858–1928) was a Viennese otolaryngologist and close personal friend of Freud. During the early period covered by Volume I (1892–99), Freud sent Fliess a series of private 'Drafts' and letters that constitute one of the most important sources for understanding the development of psychoanalytic theory. These unpublished papers, contained in the 'Fliess Papers' section of Volume I, reveal Freud's evolving thinking on the aetiology of the neuroses, the mechanism of anxiety, melancholia, paranoia, and other topics before they appeared in published form.

## Key facts

- Freud sent a series of 'Drafts' and letters to Wilhelm Fliess between 1892 and 1897, which are reproduced in Volume I of the RSE as 'Extracts from the Fliess Papers'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p14]
- The Fliess correspondence includes Drafts labeled A through N, letters numbered from 14 to 125, and is described by Strachey as 'of such vital importance to an understanding of Freud's views' that it could not be rejected despite the general exclusion of Freud's correspondence. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p20]
- The correspondence covers topics including the aetiology of the neuroses, anxiety, melancholia, paranoia, migraine, defence mechanisms, and dream theory. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p14]
- Fliess held that the nose plays a role in the pathogenesis of headaches and migraine—not only in exceptional cases but as a rule. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p140]
- Freud reported personal experience that frequent migraine was transformed by treatment of hypertrophic erectile tissue of the nose. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p140]
- Fliess was a colleague and friend of Freud's, and Freud sent him his observations on migraine for incorporation into his work. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p136]

## Inferences

- Inference: The decision to include the Fliess correspondence while excluding other correspondence underscores the unique scientific importance of these papers for tracing Freud's intellectual development.
- Inference: The Fliess-Freud relationship was significant in Freud's early theoretical development, particularly regarding somatic factors in neurosis and the connection between nasal and genital mucosa.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: Only portions of the letters 'of definite scientific interest' are included; the full correspondence was later published in the 1950a (1887–1902) and 1985c (1887–1904) editions.
