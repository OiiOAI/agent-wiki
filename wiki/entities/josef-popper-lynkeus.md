# Josef Popper-Lynkeus

## Summary

Josef Popper (1838-1921) was an Austrian engineer by profession who became well known under the pseudonym 'Lynkeus' for his philosophical and sociological writings, particularly his schemes of social reform. His volume of short stories 'Phantasien eines Realisten' (Phantasies of a Realist) contained a story that independently anticipated Freud's concept of dream censorship.

- In Popper's story 'Träumen wie Wachen' ['Dreaming like Waking'], a man who never dreams anything nonsensical is explained as having 'order and harmony in thoughts and feelings, nor do the two struggle with each other.'. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7079]
- Freud found in Popper's explanation of dream distortion an independent confirmation of his own theory: dreams become strange when there is internal conflict and repression. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7080]
- Freud never met Popper in person, fearing that personal contact might 'spoil the happy impression' of their agreement on dream theory. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7081]

## Inferences

- Inference: Popper's ideal of internal harmony, precluding dream distortion, corresponded to what Freud considered the impossible standard of complete absence of repression.
