# F-100 Super Sabre

## Summary

The F-100 Super Sabre was the first operational aircraft to reach the speed of sound in level flight and was part of the Century Series. Known as "the Hun," it was notoriously difficult to fly with a fearsome reputation as a "lieutenant-killer" and "widow-maker" with adverse yaw problems that caused many fatal accidents.

## Key facts

- The F-100 was called the "Hun" and was the first of the Century Series aircraft, designed as a day air-superiority fighter but turned into an air-to-mud aircraft by bomber generals. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p85]
- One quarter of all F-100s ever produced were lost in accidents, earning it the reputation as a lieutenant-killer and widow-maker. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p85]
- The F-100 would "depart flight" (quit flying) with one wrong control move or moment of inattention, usually resulting in violent sixty-degree pitch-up followed by an uncontrollable spin. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p85]
- The F-100 had adverse yaw problems where at low airspeeds and high angle of attack, the down aileron produced more drag than lift, causing the aircraft to roll violently in the opposite direction. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p86]
- Boyd solved the adverse yaw problem by demonstrating that at high angle of attack and low airspeed, pilots should use rudders as primary control for both roll and turn, keeping the stick centered. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p89-90]
- Boyd's solution was written into flight manuals and taught by every instructor pilot in the Air Force, and almost overnight the number of crashes in the F-100 decreased. [raw/books/strategy/Boyd - Robert Coram.pdf#p90]
