# Elon Musk

## Summary

Elon Musk is an entrepreneur whose SpaceX venture demonstrates strategic mastery of finding the crux. After failing to buy Russian rockets in 2001, he identified that the crux of high launch costs was the lack of reusability, specifically the reentry problem. His insight was to use extra fuel to slow rocket returns rather than accept fiery reentry. SpaceX achieved 23x cost reduction per pound to orbit compared to the space shuttle.

## Key facts

- Musk tried to buy Russian rockets in 2001 but was unhappy with bargaining style and tripling prices. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p12]
- Musk identified the crux of high launch costs as reentry—he realized fuel was cheaper than vehicles and could be used to slow returns. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p12]
- SpaceX was founded in 2002 with focused, coherent policies: complete redesigns, low-cost approach, no adapting of intercontinental ballistic missiles, limited subcontractors. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p13]
- By 2018, Falcon 9's cost per pound into low-Earth orbit was twenty-three times cheaper than the old space shuttle. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p14]
- Musk argued a cheap reliable rocket is like a Honda Civic—reliable not because expensive but because simple. [raw/books/strategy/The Crux - Richard Rumelt.pdf#p13]
