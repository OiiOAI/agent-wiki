# Michael Bernstein

## Summary

Dr. Michael Bernstein is an eye surgeon who had a incapacitating stroke at age 54. After receiving standard post-stroke care with incomplete recovery, he underwent constraint-induced (CI) therapy at the Taub Clinic. He made rapid progress, regaining the ability to write, play Scrabble, and eventually return to work and play tennis three days a week. His case demonstrates that CI therapy can help even patients with significant residual disability after standard treatment.

## Key facts

- Michael Bernstein was a 54-year-old eye surgeon who played tennis six times a week when he had a stroke. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p140]
- During the stroke, the entire left side of his body became completely paralyzed, indicating significant motor cortex damage. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p141]
- After standard rehabilitation including one week of inpatient and three weeks of outpatient therapy, he still needed a cane and couldn't use his left hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p141]
- Before his stroke, he could do cataract operations with his left hand despite being right-handed; after the stroke he couldn't hold a fork or button his shirt. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p141]
- His progress with CI therapy was very rapid, with intensive six-hour days including lifting cans, washing tabletops, stretching rubber bands, and writing with his left hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p142]
- In two weeks he learned to print and then write with his afflicted left hand. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p142]
- He is now back at work running his busy office and playing tennis three days a week. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p142]
