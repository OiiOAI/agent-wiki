# James Mitchell

## Summary

James Mitchell was a staff psychologist for the Air Force's SERE (Survival, Evasion, Resistance, Escape) program who later consulted with the CIA on their 'Enhanced Interrogation' program after 9/11.

## Key facts

- Mitchell was a staff psychologist at the Air Force SERE program in Spokane, Washington, where he helped design interrogation resistance training. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p138]
- He underwent SERE training himself, including being placed in a buried 55-gallon drum filled with cold water for approximately an hour as part of the standard course. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p139]
- After 9/11, Mitchell and colleague Bruce Jessen were approached by the CIA to advise on their interrogation program, contributing techniques including sleep deprivation, walling, and waterboarding. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p140]
- Mitchell and Jessen tested waterboarding on themselves—two 40-second sessions each—before the CIA used it on detainees. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p140]
- Mitchell described waterboarding as feeling 'like I was afraid I was going to die'—not that he believed he would die. [raw/books/psychology/Talking to Strangers - Gladwell, Malcolm.pdf#p140-141]

## Inferences

- Inference: Mitchell represents the complexity of the interrogation debate—someone who believed in the effectiveness of these techniques based on extensive experience, while critics like Charles Morgan argued the stress compromised the reliability of information obtained.
