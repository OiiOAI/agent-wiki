# Glial Cells

## Summary

Glial cells are non-neuronal cells of the nervous system that are specialized to regulate various aspects of neuronal function. They play essential roles in enabling and guiding neural development, insulating axonal processes, controlling the extracellular milieu, supporting synaptic transmission, facilitating learning and memory, and modulating pathological processes. In the vertebrate nervous system, glia are divided into two major classes: macroglia (oligodendrocytes, Schwann cells, and astrocytes) and microglia. Approximately 90% of all glial cells are macroglia, with about half being myelin-producing cells and half being astrocytes.

## Key facts

- In the human brain, approximately 90% of all glial cells are macroglia, with roughly half being myelin-producing cells (oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells) and half being astrocytes. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p181]
- Microglia are the brain's resident immune cells and phagocytes but also have homeostatic functions in the healthy brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p181]
- Some glial cells have receptors for neurotransmitters and voltage-gated ion channels that enable them to communicate with one another and with neurons to support neuronal signaling. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p178]
- Glial cells and neurons develop from common neuroepithelial progenitors of the embryonic nervous system. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p181]
- Glial cells play diverse roles in neural function including forming insulating sheaths for axons, supporting synaptic signaling, producing cerebrospinal fluid, and serving immune surveillance. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p122]
- Glia are less excitable than neurons, but their membranes contain transporter proteins that facilitate the uptake of ions and remove neurotransmitter molecules from the extracellular space. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p123]
- Oligodendrocytes (CNS) and Schwann cells (PNS) produce myelin sheaths that wrap concentrically around axons for rapid conduction of electrical signals. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p139]
- Astrocytes are star-shaped glia that constitute nearly half the number of brain cells and play important roles in nourishing neurons and regulating extracellular ion and neurotransmitter concentrations. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p140]
- Microglia derive from bone marrow and serve important roles in immunological surveillance, processing antigens, and clearing debris after injury. [raw/books/neuroscience/Principles of Neural Science, Fifth Edition (Principles of - Eric R_ Kandel; James H_ Schwartz; Thomas M_ Jessell; Steven.pdf#p147]
