# Francis Ford Coppola

## Summary

Francis Ford Coppola is the acclaimed film director who directed The Godfather (1972). Initially reluctant to take on the project, Coppola used a technique called a 'prompt book' he learned studying theater at Hofstra College. His prompt book for The Godfather contained his annotations and distillations of the novel, which he considered more important than even the script.

## Key facts

- Coppola initially turned down The Godfather project, calling it 'more commercial and salacious than my own taste.'. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p100]
- Coppola used a prompt book made from a three-ring binder where he cut and pasted pages from the novel. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p101]
- He distilled each scene into five criteria: synopsis, historical context, imagery and tone, core intention, and potential pitfalls. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p102]
- Coppola considered his notebook more important than the script: 'the script was really an unnecessary document; I didn't need a script.'. [raw/books/productivity/Building a Second Brain - Tiago Forte.pdf#p102]
