# Bob Flanagan, Supermasochist

## Summary

Bob Flanagan (1952-1996) was a performance artist who lived with cystic fibrosis and developed extreme masochistic practices. Born with CF, he endured constant painful medical treatments throughout childhood, spending extended periods hospitalized. As an adult, he developed a public masochistic practice with his partner Sheree Rose, engaging in extreme self-harm that included nailing his penis to a board. His case illustrates how the brain can plastically rewire pain systems to produce pleasure, transforming trauma into erotic satisfaction.

## Key facts

- Bob Flanagan was born in 1952 with cystic fibrosis, a genetic disorder causing breathing difficulties, chronic digestive problems, and requiring constant medical intervention. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p134]
- As an infant, he was tied into the crib in the hospital so he couldn't escape and hurt himself; by age seven his confinement had turned into a love of constriction as an adult. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p136]
- As a child, he was required to endure powerful nurses and doctors who hurt him; as an adult, he voluntarily gave this power to Sheree Rose, becoming her slave for pseudomedical abuse. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p136]
- Flanagan's pleasure centers were rewired in two ways: anxiety became pleasant, and physical pain became pleasure, feeling real intense pain and it feeling good. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p137]
- He said he hurt himself 'because I am a big baby, and I want to stay that way,' suggesting the fantasy of staying the tortured baby to avoid growing up and dying prematurely. [raw/books/neuroscience/The Brain That Changes Itself - Norman Doidge.pdf#p138]

## Inferences

- Inference: Bob Flanagan's life demonstrates the extreme end of neuroplasticity's capacity to rewire fundamental brain systems like pain and pleasure in response to childhood trauma.
