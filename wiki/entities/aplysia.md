# Aplysia

## Summary

Aplysia (sea slug) is a marine mollusk used extensively as a model organism for studying the cellular and molecular mechanisms of learning and memory. The gill-withdrawal reflex of Aplysia can be sensitized, habituated, or classically conditioned, allowing detailed analysis of synaptic changes underlying implicit memory.

## Key facts

- Aplysia is used to study implicit memory storage because its simple nervous system and large neurons allow direct examination of synaptic changes during learning. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1367]
- Five spaced training sessions in Aplysia produce long-term sensitization lasting 1 or more days, while training over several days produces sensitization lasting 1 or more weeks. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1367]
- Long-term sensitization of the gill-withdrawal reflex in Aplysia involves both synaptic facilitation and growth of new synaptic connections. [raw/books/neuroscience/Eric Kandel PRINCIPLES OF NEURAL SCIENCE Sixth Edition - Eric R Kandel; John Koester; Sarah Mack; Steven Siegelbaum.pdf#p1367]
- Aplysia's brain has only about 20,000 neurons grouped into nine ganglia, compared to 100 billion neurons in the mammalian brain. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p158]
- Some cells in Aplysia's nervous system are fifty times larger than hippocampal pyramidal cells and can be seen with the naked eye, making them ideal for microelectrode recording. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p158]
- Aplysia releases copious amounts of purple ink when disturbed and was once called lepus marinus (sea hare) by ancient scholars. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p157]
- The abdominal ganglion of Aplysia contains only 2000 nerve cells but controls complex behaviors including gill withdrawal, heart rate, and egg laying. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p201]
- Aplysia neurons are large and uniquely identifiable, allowing study of biochemical identity and how individual cells differ at the molecular level. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p195]
- Aplysia are hermaphrodites that can form impressive copulating chains where each member serves as both male and female. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p200]
- Kriegstein succeeded in rearing Aplysia in the laboratory from the embryonic stage of the egg mass to adulthood, enabling cell culture studies. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p265]
- Aplysia larvae rest on red seaweed (Laurencia pacifica) and extract chemicals needed to trigger metamorphosis into juvenile slugs. [raw/books/neuroscience/In Search of Memory_ The Emergence of a Ne - Eric R. Kandel.pdf#p266]

## Inferences

- Inference: The simplicity of Aplysia's nervous system made it possible to trace the complete neural circuit controlling a simple behavior and relate changes in the reflex to changes in specific cells during learning.
