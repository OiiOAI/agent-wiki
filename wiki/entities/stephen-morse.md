# Stephen Morse

## Summary

Stephen Morse is a law professor at the University of Pennsylvania and the most formidable skeptic of the relevance of neuroscience to the legal system. He coined the term 'brain overclaim syndrome' for those who overstate neuroscience's implications for legal responsibility, famously stating 'Brains don't kill people. People kill people.'

## Key facts

- Morse believes that beyond rare excusing conditions (lack of rational or control capacity due to brain damage), neuroscience offers little that should challenge the notion of responsibility. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p614]
- Morse coined the term 'brain overclaim syndrome' for those who have been 'infected and inflamed by stunning advances in our understanding of the brain,' causing them to make moral and legal claims the new neuroscience does not entail. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p615]
- Morse criticizes neuroimaging as 'determinism du jour' with 'prettier pictures' that appears more scientific, and notes that neuroscience findings are usually merely descriptive or correlative rather than causal. [raw/books/psychology/Behave_ The Biology of Humans at Our Best and Worst - Robert M Sapolsky; Michael Goldstrom; Overdrive Inc.pdf#p615]
