# Akhenaten

## Summary

Akhenaten (reigned c. 1375–1358 BCE) was an Egyptian pharaoh of the Eighteenth Dynasty who introduced a strict monotheistic religion centered on the Aten (sun disk). He changed his name from Amenophis IV to Akhenaten and founded the new capital Akhetaten (Tell el-Amarna). His religious reform excluded magic, sorcery, and the worship of Osiris, but was rejected after his death and his memory was proscribed.

## Key facts

- Akhenaten reigned from approximately 1375 to 1358 BCE as Amenophis IV before changing his name. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7195]
- He introduced a strict monotheism centered on the Aten, the sun disk, making exclusivity a central feature of the new religion. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7196]
- Akhenaten closed temples, forbade divine service, and confiscated temple property throughout Egypt. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7196]
- He built a new royal capital at Akhetaten (Tell el-Amarna) dedicated to his god. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7196]
- The Aten religion excluded everything to do with myths, magic, and sorcery. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]
- The Aten religion maintained complete silence about Osiris and the kingdom of the dead. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]
- After Akhenaten's death, the new religion was swept away and his memory was proscribed. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7197]
- Under Akhenaten, the universal god Aten became the only god; everything told of other gods was declared deceit and lies. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7229]
- Akhenaten rejected magic and the illusion of life after death, recognizing solar radiation's energy as the source of all life on earth. [raw/books/psychology/The Revised Standard Edition of The Complete Psychological - Sigmund Freud, James Strachey , Mark Solms.pdf#p7229]
- After Akhenaten's death, the Aten religion was abolished, his capital destroyed, and his memory branded as criminal—making his reform seem 'an episode doomed to be forgotten.'. ## Inferences

- Inference: According to Freud, Akhenaten's monotheism formed the basis of the Mosaic religion through Moses, making Judaism derivative of Egyptian religious reform.
- Inference: Freud sees in Akhenaten the first 'individual in human history' to attempt systematic religious reform based on universal principles.
- Inference: Freud views Akhenaten as representing the first and perhaps clearest case of monotheist religion in human history, with the religion of Aten representing pacifism in contrast to Yahweh's more bellicose character.

## Uncertainties

- Uncertain: The extent to which Akhenaten himself composed the famous hymns to the Aten, versus court theologians.
